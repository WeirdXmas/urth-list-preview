  • 2001-02-27 women, memory, and stuffSpeculates that Nettle returned to the Whorl to pursue a writing and editing career, and questions Wolfe's treatment of women in the Long Sun and Short Sun books, citing Chenille and Mucor. Also reflects on memory and identity, praising the Soldier books' depiction of Latro's torment and slow recovery of short-term memory.
  • 2001-02-02 Horn and Silk adjusting to cramped quarters.Proposes that in Return to the Whorl Wolfe signals shifting identities through the voices of Horn and Silk, who now integrate well, with Silk the more present. Offers this as another opinion to Mr. Straight and Mr. Reilly, citing In Green's Jungles' cranky tone, Silk's voice in the later book, and verbal tics and physical tells exchanged between the two.

  • 2000-09-10 Oreb Defense, mostlyDefends Oreb's intelligence against criticism, arguing his phrases like "Not cut!" carry moral meaning and that a Long Sun scene shows him learning "God's ways" from Silk. Also admits puzzlement over Babbie and the Short Sun, and thanks Robert Borski for a word.
  • 2000-09-01 Parkroads and GrundayArgues that the story Parkroads reflects a universal human urge to place paradise and meaning elsewhere, not a Catholic theme, citing its mythologized locations past and future.   • 2000-08-22 Gurloes and the AutarchAgrees that Gurloes and the Autarch may be linked, noting Severian's descriptions of the Autarch as childlike and Gurloes as burly, and that the Autarch's supposed castration could fit a family resemblance. Grounds the idea in Severian's account of Gurloes abusing a woman and in the Autarch's flight after accession, while admitting the text is not at hand.
  • 2000-08-12 Him? Them?Proposes that Severian's plural nature links him to the Outsider, since Pas/Typhon is two-headed and one face of the Outsider is female as Silk describes. Grounds this in the Chrasmologic Writings' storm-god description and a peculiar pronoun Horn uses, raising the idea without a formal Catholic reading.
  • 2000-04-30 LB and 100 YrsAsks whether list members have already traced the parallels between The Land Across... no, between Wolfe's LB (Litany? no) and Garcia-Marquez's One Hundred Years of Solitude, such as its soapy multigenerational family structure and slap-in-the-face ending. Raises the comparison as a question, wondering if it was too obvious to mention before. The post cites no specific passages and names no particular characters.
  • 2000-04-18 just the mervrow thingProposes that "mervrow" originated as a term for Dutch sailors' sea wives on long voyages, a non-pejorative label for women who served as partners and companions. Asks for confirmation from readers, recalling an unspecified reference, rather than citing a specific source or passage from the books.
  • 2000-01-20 those darned ambiguitiesAsks whether Hyacinth was an engineered embryo like Silk and Chenille and invites discussion of minor characters, including whether all Whorl inhabitants are human.   • 1999-12-30 IAQsProposes that Palaemon, given his peculiar age, may have time-traveled himself like many other characters in The Book of the New Sun, since he was a potential alter-Sev who followed similar paths. Then asks the list whether anyone knows Charles Palliser's real name, noting his Trollope-like name, and closes with social well-wishes.
