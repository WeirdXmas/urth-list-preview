Their messages (6 with summaries)

  • 2012-11-26 What symbols mean for WolfeArgues that symbols matter only through the human commitments they represent, and that commitments continually reshape us, so free will and determinism are complementary rather than opposed. Responds to Lee Berman's remark on Severian's free will by quoting Wolfe's line that symbols invent us and using Severian's repeatedly broken guild oaths as evidence.
  • 2012-11-26 Claw = Fang?Proposes that Wolfe links the Claw and fangs through serpent imagery, connecting Quetzalcoatl and the Aztec eagle-serpent myth to the Claw's talons. Grounds the idea in the eagle-of-Jove comparison Wolfe reportedly gave for the Claw and in the biblical seraphim as flying serpents, building on David Stockhoff's remarks about Typhon as the Serpent.
  • 2012-11-25 What symbols mean for WolfeArgues that Severian freely makes the decisions that shape his path, including his compassionate choices leading to exile and the option to sell the claw, so his being chosen depends on those choices. Responds to Daniel Petersen's remark on predestination and free will, noting the poster is new to the list and has not read earlier discussions.
  • 2012-11-23 What symbols mean for WolfeArgues that Wolfe's symbols represent human life, commitments that shape it, and the life story itself, using Severian's locked graveyard gate and sky gate and his account of Thecla as a symbol of undeserved love. Grounds these claims in passages from The Book of the New Sun, including Severian's remark that symbols invent us and his question whether a symbol's force survives the death of the person who embodies it.
  • 2012-11-22 Claw = Fang?Argues that Wolfe's irony is pervasive, so the Claw-as-Fang link may be a deliberate wink, with the claw intended as the eagle of Jove. Grounds this in Severian's account of women once belonging to the torturers' guild, which Bill reads as ironic and serving to keep the brothers unattached to women.
  • 2012-11-21 Claw = Fang?Argues that thorns and serpent's fangs in the Thread's Claw/Fang question link to the Genesis curse, where the curse becomes its own cure, and by extension that any thorn evokes the serpent's fangs. Grounds this in Genesis, Moses' bronze serpent in Exodus, the crucifixion crown of thorns, and Paul's thorn in the flesh, as a reply to the thread's question.
