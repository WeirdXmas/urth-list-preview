Their messages (15 with summaries)

  • 2000-09-02 Digest whorl.v011.n034Points to Gene Wolfe's earlier answer on this list about the inhumi, quoting him that they come from Green, where they reproduce, and are only tourists on Blue. Responds to Christoph Mnemonic's claim that inhumi must feed and breed on both planets by supplying Wolfe's own statement as the authority.
  • 2000-08-10 Digest whorl.v011.n013Proposes that the plural "them" in Horn's altar-scene narration reflects the Outsider speaking with multiple paired voices at the opening of Nightside the Long Sun, possibly echoing the Trinity or two of its persons. Responds to Adam Stephanides's question about whether the word is a typo by offering this reading without quoting further passages.
  • 2000-06-11 Digest whorl.v010.n118Announces that David Langford has written a review of In Green's Jungles, based on a Tor proof, to appear in Vector, and will post it here with spoiler space. Answers Yves Menard's doubts by noting the book resolves many questions, including how Horn leaves Green and reaches the Whorl in a body the list had already suspected.
  • 2000-04-13 Digest whorl.v010.n104Reports that "inhume" and "inhumations" are professional jargon from Terry Pratchett's Discworld assassins guild, and speculates that Wolfe may be a Pratchett fan. Cites John Clute's claim that Small Gods parallels The Book of the New Sun, responding to a digest query about the word inhumations on page 126 of Horn.
  • 2000-02-07 Digest whorl.v010.n078Argues that Wolfe's denial that Pike's ghost is Quetzal may not close the question, since Wolfe said in another chat that Quetzal was not the only one of his kind on the Whorl. Responds to a quoted 1997 Q&A exchange on the list, suggesting the question should have been phrased as whether Pike's ghost belonged to Quetzal's species.
  • 2000-01-30 Digest whorl.v010.n076Corrects a claim that Patera Pike's ghost is Quetzal by citing Wolfe's 1997 answer to a list question denying it, and notes Alex David Groce's point that Covenant's ring is his wedding ring. Responds to Alga's post and the Covenant-Lena ring passage, grounding the correction in the author's own written reply rather than fresh argument.
  • 2000-01-15 Digest whorl.v010.n066Argues that Borges's "The Sect of the Phoenix" is a deliberately blurred joke rather than a text giving enough clues to solve its secret rite, and suggests the rite is ordinary sexual intercourse. Responds to Josh Levitan's claim that Borges supplies enough information, challenging it by pointing to the story's teasing physical details and anti-information.
  • 1999-10-31 Digest whorl.v010.n041Shares a news story about two women killed by lightning that struck their underwire bras, adding a later pathologist's finding that the bras conducted the bolt but would not have saved them. Responds to alga's Nabokov quotation about his mother's lightning death, joking that no modernist could invent such a real event; the post is off-topic.
  • 1999-10-14 Digest whorl.v010.n033Argues that Wolfe's offhand estimate of 1000 to 2000 years between Typhon and Severian answers a different question than the one about how far the New Sun is set in our future. Grounds this in the fault-cliff passage in The Sword of the Lictor, which exposes three miles of stacked human relics, implying a far longer geological span.
  • 1999-10-09 Digest whorl.v010.n027Suggests that the leatherskin's three jaws may resemble the three gripping segments of a drill chuck, quoting Alga's idea and joking that the author must have seen many such chucks. Responds to an earlier post that corrected the creature to six legs and puzzled over its three jaws, which that poster likened to a Big Mac or club sandwich.
  • 1999-09-15 Digest whorl.v010.n011Announces that the poster has a longer review of On Blue's Waters forthcoming in Nova Express and declines to post it to the list because the magazine would not run a previously circulated review. Replies to Alice Turner's note that Publishers Weekly carried a non-starred summary review of the novel.
  • 1997-07-13 Digest whorl.v007.n004Argues that the ghost of Pike in the Long Sun books might still be one of the inhumi, since Wolfe only ruled out Quetzal, and confesses to having many private questions about the Long Sun books. Responds to Alice Turner's and Mantis's posts, citing the answer about the accidental Mint/Marble mixup as relieving his confusion.
  • 1997-07-12 Amazing Wolfe RevelationsArgues that the monitor in the Long Sun books overrules Quetzal's choice of Green because it sees his body in a glass, which shows he was not human. Supports this by pointing to the sentence about the glass in the sickbay, which would otherwise serve no purpose, and responds to Mantis's reposting of Wolfe's answers.
  • 1997-07-04 Bloody HellCorrects the attribution of the quoted lines to Alastair Reynolds and argues that the Whorl's double-planet system echoing The Fifth Head of Cerberus is deliberate Wolfe playfulness, a narrative pun. Grounds this in the shapeshifters of Fifth Head being more convincingly human than the inhumi, and points to a cast-list and chapter 6 joke in Exodus from the Long Sun about carp barbels.
  • 1997-07-01 CanticonSupplies Eric Partridge and 1811 Dictionary of the Vulgar Tongue definitions for Long Sun cant terms abram, kate, large, nanny and nipper, answering Mantis's request for help with words missing from the OED. Then asks whether the communicative pet Tick the catachrest echoes the crest cat Tick-Tock in James H. Schmitz's story Novice.
