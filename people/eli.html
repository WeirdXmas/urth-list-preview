  • 2001-07-20 Used book storesReports that Wolfe's books, especially Soldier paperbacks and The Book of the New Sun, turn up less often in used bookstores than other authors' books. Recalls a summer SoHo Books stash of nearly new Wolfe titles, probably Tor surplus, and says the poster prefers buying new copies.
  • 2001-05-29 Digest urth.v030.n120Argues that the ending of Pullman's His Dark Materials gives Lyra and Will a decision that is too narrowed and rigged to be a real moral choice, citing the angels' instruction and the arithmetic of Dust lost through one window. Responds to Adam Stephanides by rereading the ending, mocking the balance of Dust, and suggesting a rotating window schedule, though the post concerns Pullman rather than Wolfe.
  • 2001-05-29 Digest urth.v030.n120Argues that Pullman forced the ending of his trilogy so that Lyra and Will would stay apart as he wished. Answers Alice Turner's question about who 'he' and 'they' are in her earlier post, explaining that the words refer to Pullman and to Will and Lyra.
  • 2001-05-25 Digest urth.v030.n119Argues that Pullman's His Dark Materials forces the ending of The Amber Spyglass by using a Cold Equations-style plot device to coerce Will and Lyra, rather than letting their own wisdom decide. Reasons that the Dust ecology and Specter problems are not truly absolute obstacles, so the characters could have asked whether they should open the worlds at all, responding to alga's remark about children loving the third book.
  • 2001-04-27 Some recommendations [was Re: Witches, Daemons, Bears, Democrats]Recounts reading Elinor Wylie's The Sea-Rabbit, finding it fairy-tale-like and emotionally distant, and asks whether to read it first or expect aesthetic mismatch. Then praises Hope Mirrlees's Lud-in-the-Mist for its discursive writing and fairy-fruit smuggling plot, and proposes that the land you don't return from is death and the immortals are the Dead.
