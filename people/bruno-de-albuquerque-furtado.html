Their messages (17 with summaries)

  • 2012-05-28 Lake of BirdsPoints out that Severian, in a passage told to Cyriaca, reports an old woman calling Apu-Punchau a vivimancer, a dead necromancer with power over the living, supporting the idea that Apu-Punchau moved the party through time. Replies to entonio's question by citing that scene, and mocks entonio's dismissal of textual sequence in favor of any hypothesis.
  • 2012-05-26 Lake of BirdsAgrees with David Stockhoff's idea that Severian, a reformed torturer, is a pagan moving toward Christianity, but disputes his claim that Severian raised Apu-Punchau. Argues the Cumaean instead carried the group back in time to the Apu-Punchau period, so no one raised him, unlike Severian's resurrections of other characters.
  • 2012-05-23 Lake of BirdsCorrects David Stockhoff's uncertain account of the bird-warning in the Circe story, stating that in Homer it is Hermes who warns Odysseus in the wilderness before he reaches Circe's house. Responds to Stockhoff's question about whether the transformed-king bird is canonical, placing the warning in the classical source rather than a later spinoff.
  • 2012-05-23 Lake of BirdsJokes that in Lake of Birds, Odysseus' rowing crew gets even fewer warnings than the usual three before being turned into pigs, a playful riff on Circe's transformations. Responds to entonio's remark about the warnings by adding a comic exaggeration of the Odyssean episode in Lake of Birds.
  • 2012-05-23 Lake of BirdsExplains that the poster's joke about a four-armed man being worth two men alludes to a Portuguese proverb, and admits to having misread the earlier remark as meaning armed fourfold. Replies to Marc Aramini's earlier post on the doubling of limbs in the Blue and Green books, praising the chain of analogies.
  • 2012-05-23 Lake of BirdsJokingly remarks that the list is becoming more like serious academia and thanks Jerry Friedman for an English proverb, "Forewarned is four-armed", which he had not heard before. Responds to Jerry Friedman's earlier comment about working out who is whose enemy, a playful reply with no argument about the text.
  • 2012-05-22 Lake of BirdsApologizes for having asked for evidence after entonio warned that such requests had been taken as insults, explaining that the question was sincere and aimed at understanding a connection the poster was trying to see. Responds directly to entonio's remark about how evidence requests have been received on the list, with no textual argument offered.
  • 2012-05-22 Lake of BirdsConcedes that the topic of the thread is often discussed on the list, and clarifies that the earlier remark only meant that the point is easy to forget while reading the books. Responds to entonio's rebuke about the list's archives and apologises for not having read most of them.
  • 2012-05-22 Lake of BirdsDisputes Lee Berman's theory that the Old Leech is the Cumaean in disguise, asking what textual evidence supports it and doubting the link to Ceryx. Points to the blood bat at the ziggurat and the Old Leech's blood-focused nature as vampire signs, and argues that making the Cumaean demonic conflicts with how the poster reads her.
  • 2012-05-22 Lake of BirdsArgues that the Lake of Birds is probably not a surviving reconstruction of Avernus, since the far-future Commonwealth could hardly preserve it, and that Wolfe himself made it look like Avernus as a postmodern joke. Supports this with the G.W. translator figure and the claim that names like Dorcas, Cumaean and Typhon may be the translator's word choices rather than mythological originals.
  • 2012-05-22 Lake of BirdsArgues that the Lake of Birds in Wolfe's Long Sun book is a direct reference to Lake Avernus, a volcanic crater, citing Hildegrin's description of it as a volcano's mouth. Responds to an earlier remark that such resemblances are fine but should not drive conclusions about characters, and proposes that the Garden of Endless Sleep is either an Autarch's reconstruction of the Avernus region or the place itself on a far-future Earth.
  • 2012-05-22 Lake of BirdsAdds a further Tiresias role, royal councilor to the kings of Thebes, to David Stockhoff's list, and agrees with Antonio that the Tiresias identification of the figure called FI has problems. Points out that FI shows no blindness, and that the deformed masked guide who appears when Severian is with Vodalus is reported to have dark skin, suggesting autochthons.
  • 2012-05-20 Lake of BirdsReplies to enrico in Portuguese, remarking on the unexpected language and then speculating that Father Inire's long life may echo Greek myth, proposing the blind prophet Tiresias as a strong parallel. Grounds the comparison in Tiresias being a blind Theban seer who lived long, was occasionally revived, and was the son of a nymph.
  • 2012-05-20 Lake of BirdsArgues that the Cumaean in Lake of Birds is the Sibyl of Cumae, and that her bond with Severian goes deeper than it looks, since Severian is a Christ figure and also a sun god resembling Apollo. Supports this with the Cumaean Sibyl legend of Apollo trading longevity for sex, and with the witches' tower as a guild near the torturers. Responds to Gerry and Dave Lebling's view by suggesting the witch could have passed through time via the Botanic Gardens, and closes by asking who made the Mandragora.
  • 2012-05-20 Lake of BirdsProposes that the Garden of Endless Sleep in The Shadow of the Torturer is an echo of Lake Avernus, linked to Cumae and the Cumaean, with Latin chanting heard as temporal echoes. Grounds this in quoted passages from chapters XXII through XXIV, the Hildegrin dialogue, and the dead bees and birds around the avern, plus a Wikipedia note on the lake.
  • 2012-05-20 Lake of BirdsArgues that Severian and others may have overlooked Roman citizens at Lake of Birds, that the pipe may give Juturna entry into the lake, and that the Cumaean Sibyl's age and serpentine look tie to Greek myth. Replies to David Stockhoff by citing Wikipedia on the Cumaean and Delphic Sibyls, Tithonus, Lamia and the Mandragora in the Citadel.
  • 2012-05-20 Lake of BirdsAsks whether the Lake of Birds in The Urth of the New Sun is the real Lake Avernus, linking its name meaning birdless and its role as a gate to Hades, and raises questions about its gardens and time period. Grounds the argument on the old boatman's remark about a pipe to Gyoll that keeps the lake from drying up, and suggests this hints that the future can shape the past.
