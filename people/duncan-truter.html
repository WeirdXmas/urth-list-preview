Their messages (17 with summaries)

  • 2008-10-14 "My father's mounted guard"Proposes that Severian in The Urth of the New Sun is not only Severian and Thecla but also the eunuch autarch and his predecessors, and asks whether someone else is present in the narrator. Responds to Son of Witz's explanation that Severian's mingled memories come from eating Thecla's corpse, extending that idea of mingled selves beyond her.
  • 2008-10-12 a question, some Cthulhiana, and some EG spoilers (maybe)Argues that if the character GC is one of the Elder Gods, only Nyarlathotep fits, since Cthulhu sleeps for long periods and does not take other forms. Asks whether the book is a broader Mythos work or a Lovecraft-Cthulhu one, noting that the Elder Gods would be absent in the latter, and asks whether Wolfe has given recent interviews. Responds to Thomas Bitterman's post about Evil Guest.
  • 2008-09-30 a lurker asks a questionProposes that the book Severian picks up with Thecla is The Book of the New Sun, which would explain his sense that he is already dead. Responds to Adam Thornton's request for the passage where Severian recoils from a book like a rotting corpse, suggesting it may be the book he later recoils from.
  • 2008-09-26 spoilers re: evil guest. Lovecraft-esque theories.Argues that Lovecraft's fiction shows a concern with people breeding across races and producing monstrous offspring, and suggests this is what Wolfe alludes to with the Woldercan material in An Evil Guest. Grounds this on Lovecraft's known stories and the poster's reading of the novel, and adds the point as an afterthought to his earlier questions.
  • 2008-09-26 spoilers re: evil guest. Lovecraft-esque theories.Proposes a Lovecraftian reading of An Evil Guest, arguing that Gideon Chase is the villain, that the watch links to Randolph Carter, and that the bat-like creatures are night-gaunts or Mi-Go. Grounds these claims in parallels to Lovecraft stories and asks the list to rip the theories apart.
  • 2008-06-18 Urth Digest, Vol 46, Issue 30Argues that the word "isangoma" in Wolfe's text is a real-world term for a South African shaman, not a Wolfe coinage, so the jungle hut scene must be set in Earth's past. Supports the view with the word's everyday meaning and the air/aeroplane details, responding to Jeff Wilson's claim that the hut indicates Christ existed in Urth's past.
  • 2007-11-27 chris' last name *pirate freedom*Asks whether Chris in Pirate Freedom has time-traveled a third time, noting he already did it twice, and suggests the pirate adventures fall between the 1630s and 1670s, later than Columbus. Responds to his own earlier post and an apology for garbled text, with the timeline reasoning drawn from a quick Wikipedia check.
  • 2007-11-27 Chris' last name *Pirate freedom*Asks whether Chris's unrevealed surname in Pirate Freedom could be Columbus, proposing that time travel would let an older Columbus raise his younger self. Grounds the idea in the uncertain origins of Columbus and cites a Wikipedia article on theories of his origin, inviting counter-evidence.
  • 2007-11-16 The hut in the jungleArgues that the tokoloshe of Zulu and South African folklore is a little man rather than a zombie or bear-like being, drawing on the poster's childhood in South Africa. Responds to Tony Ellis and Dave Lebling's link to Wikipedia and a web page on the tokoloshe, noting that the page's claim of zombie-like features is inaccurate.

Replies to Tony Ellis's digest message and links to a Wikipedia entry on sangomas, while admitting he cannot re-read the passage because he lent the book away.
