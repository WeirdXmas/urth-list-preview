Their messages (23 with summaries)

  • 2007-04-07 Severian's sex LifeArgues that Severian's many love affairs in The Book of the New Sun are central rather than incidental, with each woman helping him discover part of himself. Cites Thecla as the start of his path toward mercy, Dorcas as teaching him to protect the innocent, and Agia as testing his commitment to mercy and marking his change from callous to sparing.
  • 2006-09-19 RocheArgues that the counterfeit coin Vodalus gives Severian shows his chivalrous nobility to be cheap, making him a petty grifter at heart. Builds on b sharp's point by contrasting Vodalus's concealed treachery with Dr. Talos, who is an open con man whose honesty gives him a form of integrity.
  • 2006-07-31 Urth geochronologyArgues that the cooling of Urth's interior and the lack of volcanic activity need not mean Urth is geologically ancient, because intelligent beings could have accelerated the cooling. Responds to Jeffrey Lefstin's point that the text's 'perhaps a million years' and the geology imply a much older Urth, using the cooling sun as a parallel for human action causing natural-seeming effects.
  • 2006-07-27 teratoid symbolsConcedes James Crossley's point that Kim Lee Soong may be a Korean rather than Chinese name, and argues it does not clearly undercut his theory about monstrous-looking writing. Reasons that Hangul looks simpler than Chinese characters, while far-future Asian scripts may have evolved beyond both.
  • 2006-07-27 teratoid symbolsProposes that the teratoid symbols marking Father Inire's areas in The Book of the Long Sun are Chinese characters rather than radiation warnings, linking this to the Chinese-speaking past of humanity. Grounds the reading in the mirror cabinet in the presence chamber, the stated portentous and malformed senses of teratoid, and the fact that Inire, Domnina and Jonas enter the active chamber unprotected.
  • 2006-06-13 Looking for dictionary recommedationsClarifies that the poster meant the DVD edition of the Oxford English Dictionary, full or concise, is much cheaper than buying the print OED. Responds to Daniel D Jones, who had questioned the price of the single-user OED DVD, and this is a dictionary-buying exchange with no reading of Wolfe.
  • 2006-05-29 The Salamander Again (Long)Argues against Robert Borski's idea that the salamander in Thrax is Mother Pyrexia, saying the two beings are too different and the salamander seems an alien sent to hunt Sev. Grounds this in the alcalde's description of Pyrexia fearing fire and light, the salamander's indifference to flames, and the 18-year gap in which she went unnoticed, while suggesting a transformation link via the earthly salamander legend.
  • 2006-05-29 The Witches functionProposes that the Witches of the Book of the New Sun are biologists or genetic engineers, citing the Cumaean's view through a glass tabletop of an artificial landscape with crippled animals as an electron-microscope image. Supports this with the Witches' tower near the Beast Tower, Gurloes's remarks on sex with them, and Merryn's sickly beauty, while admitting the Autarch and Cumaean connections remain unexplained.
  • 2006-05-25 Hierodules and timeAsks whether Typhon also made a chess metaphor, which would make chess a stronger theme, and wonders if black's slight disadvantage from moving second in chess has significance. Responds to a reply by b sharp that credits Vodalus, with the chess idea itself as the tentative link rather than a worked-out argument.
  • 2006-05-25 Hierodules & timeCorrects b sharp's attribution of a chess remark, arguing that Vodalus rather than Typhon made the chess reference and cast himself as playing the black side. Asks b sharp or others to point to the Typhon passage if the poster is wrong.
  • 2006-05-24 Borski and cacogens (Long)Proposes that the human slaves traded to the cacogens are welcomed into heirodule society and raised in its culture and technology, possibly interbreeding. Grounds this in Master Ash's mixed human and heirodule traits and his claim that humanity is partly saved, plus a Christian reading of the slaves as the wretched admitted to a paradise.
  • 2006-05-22 Borski and cacogens (Long)Disputes the quoted claims that Father Inire and the Cumean shapeshift or cannot travel time, arguing they may simply choose not to travel and may wear masks. Points out that the cacogens do visit the House Absolute, that Inire's long service suggests longevity, and raises two questions about the Cumean's role and the human slave trade.
  • 2006-05-19 Abaia & co.Agrees that undines' sexual anatomy need not match their size, proposing that Abaia could be a Lovecraftian sea monster with pseudopods who can shed pieces like Tzadkiel. Then asks whether anyone solved the mystery of the giant walking creature in the Saltus mine, citing the clues of its steps, the man-apes' fear and Severian's remark that he now knows what it was.
  • 2006-05-18 BaldandersArgues that Wolfe's moral contrast between becoming more than human and becoming a monster can be read through where characters draw their power, with Severian and the green man using innate ability rather than devices. Responds to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's quoted remark on transhumanism, weighing the heirodules' technology and the undines' innate time travel as counterexamples.
  • 2006-05-18 BaldandersArgues that Baldanders, Typhon and the Green Man show a morality-driven physical alteration, with beautiful, perverse or alien forms reflecting Wolfe's commentary on transhumanism. Agrees with Adam Thornton's reading of Wolfe's remark that monsters were named as monsters, and extends it to the undines as a third category.
  • 2006-05-17 BaldandersArgues that Baldanders represents the punished Earth as an amoral exploiter of nature and fellow men, a would-be Typhon and a candidate for epitome of Earth contrasted with Severian. Responds to David DiGiacomo's view that Baldanders is a self-modified human, citing the heirodules bowing to Severian, Baldanders' growth from small man to bloated size, and his freak-show role.
