  • 2008-09-15 OT: AnathemAnnounces that the poster's Locus review of Neal Stephenson's Anathem is posted on his blog and that a review of An Evil Guest will follow. Replies to Matthew Keeley's thread about Anathem, sharing links without discussing Wolfe's text.
  • 2008-02-13 signing at consRecalls watching Roger Zelazny good-naturedly sign fourteen books for one young fan while being teased about it, and uses the anecdote to support Bob Miller's earlier suggestion that five books per signing may be too many. Responds to Bob Miller's remarks on fan courtesy at author signings, mentioning Zelazny, Cherryh and Martin, and makes no claim about Wolfe's work.
  • 2008-02-12 CapriconAdvises a fan that bringing copies of two or three of Wolfe's novels to a convention signing is fine, while five may be too many. Responds to Matthew Groves, who asked whether bringing his own Pirate Freedom copy or a different book to the signing would be poor etiquette, and notes that Wolfe is always gracious.
  • 2008-01-18 Senor GuzmanAsks Russell Wodell to expand on his suggestion that Guzman is a third iteration of Chris, driven to madness and possible suicide by jealousy of his younger self. Requests the reasoning and textual evidence behind the idea, responding to Wodell's original post about Guzman's disappearance as a despairing escape into time.
  • 2007-12-18 free will and piracyOffers a reading of why the elderly Ignacio weeps as his younger self leaves, arguing the tears come from relief at finally returning to Novia rather than empathy for the young man's coming ordeal. Responds to Marc Aramini's point that the older version never tries to deter the younger from a path toward murder and piracy, using the scene where the novitiate departs.
  • 2007-12-14 PF - Lesage & IgnacioQuestions whether Ignacio could have launched the betrayal given the causal risk to his earlier self, then proposes that Chris was resurrected specifically to answer the Native slave Hoodas's prayers. Grounds this in Chris's quoted sense of Christ's hand on his shoulder, and floats Hoodas as a Judas figure who, not Ignacio, betrays Lesage.
  • 2007-12-06 PF reviewQuotes a commenter's view that Wolfe turns the reader into a priest in a confessional, making them feel the difficulty of forgiving a neighbor, and says this idea illuminates every Wolfe book. Responds to Michael Straight, who quoted the commenter approvingly on Paul Witcover's review site, and the post itself is a brief agreement with that reading of Pirate Freedom.
  • 2007-12-02 Some Pirate Freedom thoughts and questionsProposes that the younger Chris in Cuba was working for or near his father, with Ignacio at the monastery acting as a mentor who shaped him, and notes Chris never reports any conversation with Ignacio. Grounds this in Novia's account of Chris calling Ignacio a second father and in the absence of monastery dialogue in Chris's telling; also raises time-travel paradox questions about who is responsible, and reports Wolfe's denial at a convention that Lesage is Chris.
  • 2007-11-30 PF reviewResponds to bill reilly's questions about Pirate Freedom's Chris: whether his monastery placement really protected him, whether his confession excuses choices he actually had, and whether Christ's message that loving him suffices is good theology. Calls the questions excellent and says he is puzzled that critics dismiss the novel as light-hearted fun, missing its weightier issues.
  • 2007-11-30 * Spoilers * PF stuffAnswers Matthew Groves's question about who the other of 'them' is in Pirate Freedom by saying it is Chris himself. Offers no evidence or argument, leaving the spoiler-marked Pirate Freedom thread to supply the context.
  • 2007-11-30 PF reviewAnnounces that the poster has published a review of Pirate Freedom, originally from his Realms of Fantasy review column, on his blog and invites readers to read it. The post contains no argument about the book itself and shares only a link and a promotional footer.
