Their messages (24 with summaries)

  • 2003-04-24 Generic ConsiderationsArgues that fact, truth and reality are ambiguous terms that should not be sharply separated, and that Wolfe's characters play different language-games with different standards of objectivity. Responds to Blattid's distinctions, citing Latro's narratives, Horn and The Fifth Head of Cerberus, and answers the question of what preceded language by saying subjects, not language, construct realities.
  • 2003-04-23 Generic ConsiderationsArgues that 'correspondence with reality' is a misleading framework for judging writing, favoring a constructed view of reality and a prism or lens approach instead. Responds to Blattid's earlier questions about what reality and correspondence mean, citing Kant, positivism, and Wolfe's protagonists who are authors, such as Horn concealing information from enemies.
  • 2003-04-23 Generic ConsiderationsArgues that biographies and other non-fiction should not be ranked on a scale of 'more or less fictional' but judged by the functions and implicit conventions of different discourses. Responds to Blattid's remark about Morris's Roosevelt biography, using the lens-not-mirror metaphor and Braudel and Proust as examples, and concludes that the two largely agree.
  • 2003-04-23 Generic ConsiderationsArgues that fetishizing the 'real' is a poor starting point for judging fiction, since fiction can convey subjective experience better than historical or anthropological accounts. Responds to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's question about whether a fictive universe can map exactly onto the real, offering the general point rather than discussing any Wolfe text.
  • 2003-04-13 Other authorsRecommends other authors to a reader seeking Wolfe-like books, naming John Crowley, Jonathan Carroll's White Apples and The Wooden Sea, Jack Vance, and Lawrence Durrell's Alexandria Quartet. Responds to Steve Case's request for an obscure book that is well-written, thought-provoking, brilliant and relatively clean, adding caveats about Vance and Durrell's demands on the reader.
  • 2002-07-30 Next time I'll just say "explain yourself"Argues that the idea of total linguistic replacement, as in Orwell's Newspeak, predates Orwell in Bentham's and the positivists' projects for neutral, precise terms. Responds to Sepia Josh and Andy by suggesting that France's official language institution lies on a continuum with Newspeak, differing in degree rather than kind.
  • 2002-07-12 Suicide & HyacinthArgues that the Long Sun narrative gives a distorted picture of Hyacinth because it comes only through Horn and Nettle, who are biased by jealousy, and that Silk's view of her must be read into the text. Grounds this in Silk's self-criticism and pride, and compares Silk and Hyacinth to Simon Magus and Helen, citing a Hölderlin line about danger and saving power.
  • 2002-07-11 SilkicideArgues that Silk's arm wounds are self-inflicted, more than Vironese mourning gashes but short of a suicide attempt, and asks whether Pig might have been involved in hyacinth's death. Replies to Blattid's reading of the wounds, noting Silk gives blood to Krait in In Green's Jungles p. 81 and citing a passage where the narrator admits an uncorrected mistake and withholds where he hid his Azoth, which suggests he is wary of readers.
  • 2002-07-11 SilkicideArgues that Silk's wounds in the Long Sun books were self-inflicted during a mourning frenzy rather than a true suicide attempt, since a real attempt would have succeeded. Grounds this in the narrator's recollection of recoiling from a knife after arriving in the Whorl, which the poster reads as circumstantial evidence the knife was used on Silk.
  • 2002-05-30 TBOTSS and colonialismReports that Silkhorn deliberately opened his veins to feed inhumi, which seemed to change their later behavior. Answers Jerry Friedman's question about why Silkhorn does not donate blood by citing Jahlee as a counter-example and admitting uncertainty about Silkhorn's intent without rereading.
  • 2002-05-01 What Abos?Asks whether Wolfe's phrase 'shadowchild' in an interview about 'A Story' by John V. Marsch should be read literally or as a slip for 'abo'. Responds to Robert Borski's quotation of the interview, extending the unreliable-narrator question one level, and adds a pedantic aside on capitalization after colons.
  • 2002-04-23 Human JPEGsAsks why uploaded minds in the Long Sun books cannot be copied into several machines at once, and why the data always leaves the mainframe during downloading.   • 2002-03-30 system of langaugeArgues for a rhetorical rather than semiotic reading of language in The Book of the Short Sun, moving from text outward to readers.
