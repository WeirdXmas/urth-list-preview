  • 1999-12-12 TBOTNS FAQArgues that a Wolfe FAQ can serve both newcomers and readers seeking substance by keeping hints separate from suggested answers, either by encrypting the answers or publishing them in a separate document.   • 1999-12-05 New Sun Mysteries FAQ?Announces that the poster has received permission from Donn Seeley to reuse his old list of mysteries in The Book of the New Sun and plans a FAQ.   • 1999-12-03 New Sun Mysteries FAQ?Proposes compiling a New Sun Mysteries FAQ by using mantis's permitted list and the gist of Seeley's earlier list of questions, without copying their prose. Grounds the plan in copyright reasoning that protects expression rather than ideas, and asks for a volunteer to merge the lists by Sunday.

  • 1999-12-02 Seeley; FAQAgrees to post Donn Seeley's old list of mysteries in Wolfe's work and proposes compiling an Urth FAQ that lists alternate solutions, most plausible first.   • 1999-12-02 Shadow of the Torturer Video Game?Suggests that a rumored Shadow of the Torturer video game is a case of people inventing things they want but cannot have, comparing it to the forger in Peace who fabricates the Necronomicon and other sought-after books. Responds to Fr. Inire's mocking remark about the rumor by offering this psychological explanation, with no textual evidence beyond the Peace parallel.

  • 1999-12-02 New Sun Mysteries FAQ?Proposes compiling a FAQ answering the deliberate mysteries in The Book of the New Sun, combining a Donn Seeley Usenet list with the list in Lexicon Urthus and adding new questions. Grounds the idea in the books' hidden puzzles, such as Severian's parents and grandparents, and asks Mantis and Donn Seeley to take part.

  • 1999-11-22 MankindArgues that readers should accept the premises of The Book of the New Sun, including the Increate's goodness and its destruction of Urth, as a secondary world rather than deconstructing them. Responds to Roy's objection about the Hieros' moral authority by comparing the Bible's God and the Foundation Trilogy, and by rejecting Borgesian death-of-the-author readings.
  • 1999-11-22 Stuff, TemptationProposes that Sev may travel back in time after the New Sun to tell the Temptation story as Conciliator, or that another version of him underwent a separate temptation. Responds to a question about the House Azure stained glass and asks whether Triskele is an aquastor cared for by the aquastor Malrubius.
  • 1999-11-19 MessianicArgues that in Wolfe's pocket universes, such as Briah and Yesod, a personal Creator who shapes Creation through pain is a fact of the setting, so objections to suffering belong to God or to Wolfe. Grounds this in Wolfe's stated intention that The Book of the New Sun meditates on how his God shapes Creation through suffering, and responds to Ori's remark about the Pancreator and His apologists.
  • 1999-11-05 True Confession?Argues that Severian's admission that he is a liar is itself evidence of his trustworthiness, since the most honest narrator knows his own faults, and that his lies probably concern trivial matters. Responds to a prior post by questioning whether Severian ever lies outright in a way that matters, and asks the list for examples of such lies in Severian's narration.
  • 1999-11-04 Fairy Tale logic? Bah!Cites Gene Wolfe's 1983 interview quoted in Lexicon Urthus, in which he calls The Book of the New Sun science fantasy with no fantasy elements, to rebut the fairy-tale-logic reading. Responds to Tony Ellis's stance by using Wolfe's own statement as evidence, and notes the possibility that Wolfe misled the interviewer or later changed his mind.
  • 1999-11-04 Severian as Marcus AureliusArgues that Severian resembles Marcus Aurelius more than Claudius, because Severian leads a charge in Citadel, unlike Claudius who only commanded armies. Asks whether Wolfe borrowed heavily from Graves's The White Goddess in There Are Doors, a side question about another book.
  • 1999-11-03 Severian as Marcus AureliusCompares Severian with Marcus Aurelius and Claudius, arguing that Severian fits Claudius less well because he leads armies and is not a bumbling fool. Grounds this in the Stoic meaning of autarchy, linking Malrubius's lecture on self-rule to Stoic passages, and in shared lameness and backing into the throne.
  • 1999-10-27 PKD a modernist?Argues that Philip K. Dick is better described as a postmodernist than a modernist, and defends him as a great artist despite being a hack. Cites Stanislaw Lem's remark that Dick brought gold from the trash, then closes by wondering whether Lem would revise his praise of Dick if he read Wolfe or Egan.
  • 1999-10-27 Severian as Marcus AureliusProposes that Marcus Aurelius forms part of the basis for Severian, a philosopher-autarch whose striving to be good sits oddly beside his past as a torturer. Grounds this in the shared philosophical tone of the New Sun books and the Meditations, and in the paradox that Antoninus allegedly ordered Christians tortured yet seemed good, asking whether Wolfe intertwined these paradoxes.
  • 1999-10-16 Aw Hell (Giants)Recalls a Boy's Life science column on the square-cube law that he remembered as scaling up an insect rather than a crab, and thanks Jim Henley for confirming the article's author and memory. Responds to Jim Henley's quoted account of Ben Bova's giant-crab-turned-T. rex article, which is a social aside off the Wolfe topic.
