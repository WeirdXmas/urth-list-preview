  • 2000-09-04 A fishy incident from OBWAsks why the fish leap onto the rock for Horn in On Blue's Waters, ruling out Mucor's control because she is journeying elsewhere, and suggests the Mother or Scylla as the agent. Grounds the question in Horn's surprise at the fish and his hinted knowledge, and floats a speculative idea that witnessed theophanies leave awakened engrams.
  • 2000-09-01 The SecretArgues that clues to the secret are planted early in the Long Sun books, suggesting Quetzal and perhaps a shadowy figure seen by Silk are inhumu, and that a god other than Scylla, possibly a piece of Pas, rides Oreb. Grounds these on Silk ministering to bitten people, Oreb perching on Silk's and Starling's shoulders, and Blue as the setting of The Night Chough, responding to Mr. Borski and agreeing with Alga.
  • 2000-08-23 Oreb-Scylla?Argues that Oreb is not possessed by Scylla despite their association, since Scylla's bloodthirsty personality clashes with Oreb's wise character, and predicts Oreb's secret will matter in the third volume. Responds to the poster's earlier evidence that Oreb sees Scylla in the lake and walks with her, and rejects the simple golden-rule explanation of the Inhumu secret while linking the Neighbors to augurs as executioners.
  • 2000-08-17 Oreb/PasProposes that Oreb, the bird in The Book of the Long Sun's Lake of the Long Sun, is a shard of Pas possessed or carrying Pas's consciousness, citing Chenille speaking as Kypris and the line about 'One does. Two.' Grounds the idea in the dialogue on pp. 91-92 and the Pas-Silk body-designing background, asking readers for thoughts.
  • 2000-08-17 Clute and other QuestionsProposes that Horn grows inhumu-like in In Green's Jungles and that Oreb's resurrection may stem from possession by a Mainframe god. Responds to Borski's rejection of Horn as inhumu, and suggests Hyacinth's gem name and the biochemical-versus-chem terms hint at hybrid beings.
  • 2000-08-10 A question, an observationAsks the list where the text shows that Hyacinth is dead or that Silk commits suicide, as many posts claim, and asks for citations. Also reports an editor's explanation that the In Green's Jungles cover was aimed at shlock readers, calling it worse than the On Blue's Waters cover.
  • 2000-08-10 Crowley Interview ExcerptsAsks Alga for the full Crowley interview, since an excerpt omitted a passage about Adam's LB=Alice scheme, and shares a personal note on Crowley's handwriting. Responds to the excerpted interview quoted in the digest and to Alga's posting of it, mixing a request with a social anecdote about the writer's habits.
  • 2000-08-06 Gene Wolfe's Pants!Reports an anecdote from a Readercon panel in which Gene Wolfe passed around his belt buckle, engraved with language certifying the wearer to perform exorcisms, and ties it to his remark about staying in the basement with demons. Offers no argument about the books; the poster only jokes that the buckle's meaning is for Freudians, and the post is social with the Terminus Est! reference as the sole book link.
  • 2000-08-06 Gene Wolfe's Pants!Reports an anecdote from Readercon where Gene Wolfe passed around his belt buckle, engraved with language certifying the wearer to perform exorcisms, and jokes about its Freudian significance. Describes the event as a light panel moment, linking it to Wolfe's remark about staying down in the basement with the demons and to a Terminus Est reference.
  • 2000-08-03 Delurk + CommentIntroduces a newcomer who reports Wolfe's remark at a Readercon panel that he preferred to stay with his demons rather than seek a therapeutic cure through writing. Speculates that for Wolfe writing is a spiritual struggle toward expressing religious faith, responding to Emrys's comment that OBW and IGJ can be seen as therapy.
