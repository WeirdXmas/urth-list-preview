Their messages (14 with summaries)

  • 2000-04-10 5HC and RevelationsSupports JJordan's reading that the brothel in The Fifth Head of Cerberus counterfeits the Church and the clone line figures as Antichrist, adding parallels to Revelation chapters 13 and 17. Cites the multi-headed beast, the bottomless pit and the wounded head healed in Revelation 13:3, and proposes that this beast is the mechanical grandfather of the clones.
  • 2000-02-23 Discussions of "Ascian"Informs the list that two recent World Wide Words mailing list editions discussed the word "Ascian" and links to the discussion. Notes that those discussions made no reference to Wolfe's books, which the poster says they criticized for omitting the connection.
  • 2000-01-24 (no subject)Proposes establishing a Gene Wolfe archive of collected papers and documents, and asks where it should be housed. Argues it should be near Wolfe in Houston or near Tor so material is more likely to reach it, responding to Jeremy Crampton's earlier suggestion.
  • 1999-11-23 mankindArgues that Wolfe deliberately planted puzzles and hints in The Book of the New Sun, so readers should work out its meaning rather than ignore the author's intent. Defends this by quoting Carlton Greene on rational explanations lurking behind the shadowy elements, and asks what Severian's relationships with Joturna, Valeria, Apheta, Doras, Katherine, the Witches and Jolenta signify.
  • 1999-11-23 Religion of the New SunArgues that Wolfe's Book of the New Sun is fiction, not speculative SF or theology, so Severian is only a fictional, non-identical Christ-figure and Christian readings of the Increate and saints are misplaced. Grounds this in Tolkien's letters on allegory, the St. Catherine of Alexandria motif, Wolfe's interview refusing to equate Severian with Jesus, and the Temptation in the Wilderness scene with Typhon.
  • 1999-11-18 Liars, TWHFReplies to a claim that Severian is a liar by arguing that Wolfe's memory and the narrative are reliable and that no clear signal of deception appears in the text. Dismisses the idea of literary solipsism, invoking thesis and antithesis and comparing the book to Bach rather than Cage, and challenges the other poster to prove the claim.
  • 1999-11-09 Lies, lies, liesArgues that Severian's claim to perfect memory is unreliable, that he believes it sincerely, and that Wolfe uses this to show memory's imperfection, drawing a parallel with Jack Vance's Cugel. Responds to Roy's list of supposed lies by saying Severian has no motive to lie in those examples, and cites the swimming quotes and the charm variation as memory errors.
  • 1999-11-08 Severian's liesArgues that Severian does not lie in The Shadow of the Torturer, defending him against charges of deception over his incomplete account of the corpse in the mausoleum and his claims about memory. Grounds the defense in the distinction between incomplete truth and lying, and in the difference between never knowing someone and not knowing who he is, including an analogy about knowing a president.
  • 1999-11-08 Troubling errorsAdmits to a slip in two earlier posts where the poster called Nessus a river rather than a centaur ferryman, joking that a perfect memory should have prevented repeating the error. The post is a self-deprecating correction, with the mix-up with Gyoll as its only grounding, and it is responding to nothing specific beyond the poster's own earlier messages.
  • 1999-11-05 Baptism vs. ResurrectionArgues that the Gyoll and its nenuphars in The Book of the New Sun symbolize death and the grave, so Severian's crossing fits the resurrection of Christ better than John's baptism of Jesus. Supports the case by citing the nenuphar roots reaching into dark water, the comparison of the necropolis roses with the nenuphars, and the Balder-like crossing of the river.
  • 1999-11-05 Severian the LiarProposes that Severian's claim to be a habitual liar refers to his past self before his resurrection from the Nessus, not to his narration. Supports the idea by pointing to a hypothesis that each mention of his unparalleled memory is followed by a contradiction of fact, as in the Roche/Drotte case, and by linking the Nessus bottom to a grave.
  • 1999-11-03 Wolfe apologeticsArgues that Wolfe's New Sun miracles have rational explanations, proposing that quantum tunneling moves the Sun and that Severian is the New Sun who becomes the fountain, so his lovemaking with Apheta brings it. Defends the alzabo's memory transfer as scientifically improbable but not irrational, citing Severian's bitten hand and the brain-eating assumption.
  • 1999-11-02 Fairy Tale logic? Bah!Argues that The Book of the New Sun and The Fifth Head of Cerberus are rooted in real scientific theory rather than fairy-tale logic, citing Typhon and Piaton's anatomy, Baldanders's continuous growth, and Father Inire's mirrors explained by quantum tunneling. Responds to a thread titled "Fairy Tale logic? Bah!" and offers a physics-based rationalization of Severian's powers, noting the speculation that Wolfe drew on a quantum-tunneling article.
  • 1999-10-29 Untrustworthy Narrators?Argues that Severian is an untrustworthy narrator whose claimed perfect memory is a red herring, citing his admission in chapter three of The Shadow of the Torturer that he suspects his own sanity. Grounds this in the inconsistency over whether Drotte or Roche sees pikes in the first chapter and in the first-page passage about memory recreating itself, linking it to Soldier of the Mist.
