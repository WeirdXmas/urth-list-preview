  • 1998-06-30 Wolfe's Women (There Are Doors) -ReplyArgues that Wolfe's point in There Are Doors is that biology, not culture, determines sex roles, since male death after intercourse in the other world reverses who leads, who pursues and who is emotional. Forwards Zvi Gilbert's rec.arts.sf.written post, which invokes Delany's Straits of Messina critique of the missionary-position model and cites Severian, Thecla, Dorcas and Green's search for Lara as evidence of a stark male-female split.

  • 1997-06-17 Wolfe's Answer re Torturers' Guild -ReplyArgues that Severian's line that all men are torturers, taken literally, makes half the Whorl torturers, and notes that Wolfe singled out Silk rather than Blood, Pike or Horn as a guild member. Responds to a earlier question about which character best fits the torturer role by quoting Severian's remark back and endorsing it. Grounds the claim in the quoted line and in Wolfe's own identification of Silk.
  • 1997-06-17 Wolfe's Answer re Torturers' Guild -ReplyArgues that Silk, not Severian, is the true member of the Order of Seekers for Truth and Penitence, since membership in a church and its antichrist counterpart are mutually exclusive. Responds to Nutria's broad reading of Wolfe's answer by calling it too loose, then asks whether Silk's pagan worship is equally anti-Christian and why Wolfe says 'Knowing Typhon as I do'.

  • 1997-03-12 There are DoorsPraises There Are Doors for its alternate-universe premise in which men die shortly after sex, and works through how that changes family, worship of women, male oppression, and technology. Grounds the argument in comparison with our own biology of childbearing and notes Wolfe kept the society similar enough to make his points.
