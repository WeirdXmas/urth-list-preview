  • 2006-10-22 Wolfe: Misogynist or Realist?Clarifies the poster's earlier remark calling Samuel Delany a misogynist, saying it was repeated hearsay and not a belief, and reflects on Delany's sexuality and his portrayal of women in Dhalgren. Admits a possibly high personal tolerance for misogyny in older male-written fiction, which the poster ignores or assumes was part of the general culture. The post does not engage Wolfe's own work in any depth.
  • 2006-10-20 Wolfe: Misogynist or Realist?Argues that Wolfe is not against women despite the rapes and tortures in his books, citing that Wolfe's loved by both atheists and Christians and that his violence is alluded to rather than detailed. Responds to Jack Redelfs's claim of over-infatuation with violence against women by comparing Wolfe to other authors and reflecting on Severian's lesson that greatness comes from killing parts of oneself.
  • 2006-10-20 Wolfe: Misogynist Or Realist?Argues that Wolfe likely includes scenes of violence against women to move readers, not out of hostility toward women, and doubts he harbors ill will toward them. Responds to Daniel D Jones by conceding a point about the purpose of powerful emotional scenes and offering a psychology-based analogy about Rorschach patterns.
  • 2006-10-20 Wolfe: Misogynist Or Realist?Argues that the misogyny debate about Wolfe turns on moral context rather than the quantity of violence against women, and that Wolfe repeatedly mistreats women while sparing other characters. Responds to Jeff Wilson's comparison with Goodkind, Martin and Tolkien by conceding some validity to the charge and naming Goodkind as the worse case, while listing framing questions such as whether violence is presented as a crime or as harmless fun.
  • 2006-10-19 Wolfe: Misogynist Or Realist?Argues that the misogyny debate about Wolfe is too narrow, since many fantasy and science fiction authors portray violence against women, and psychoanalyzing authors from their works would condemn others more harshly. Responds to Jack Redelfs by naming Goodkind and Martin as more extreme cases, noting Tolkien's absence of such material, and asking whether female authors should be judged the same way, citing Gilman's story.
  • 2006-10-19 Wolfe: Misogynist Or Realist?Argues that Gene Wolfe's treatment of female characters, such as Thecla, Hunna and Morwenna, may reflect a deliberate reader reaction rather than misogyny, while doubting evidence of misogyny is clear. Responds to b sharp by defending the distinction between misogyny and chivalry and by citing Paul and Augustine on celibacy in early Christianity.
  • 2006-10-18 Wolfe: Misogynist or Realist?Argues that Wolfe's narrow, negative portrayal of women is deliberate, a revisiting of old SF and fantasy clichés where female characters serve the hero's sexual and companionship needs. Responds to b. sharp's remark linking Old Testament identification with misogyny by arguing that early Christianity was a haven for women and that early female saints' stories do not prove misogyny.
