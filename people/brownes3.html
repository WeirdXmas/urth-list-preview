  • 1998-12-12 Digest whorl.v009.n015Offers a reading of an unclear phrase in the Long Sun books by comparing its usage to the title A Clockwork Orange, suggesting it means something mechanical that should not be. Responds to a question about whether the phrase refers to Silk being a puppet of the Whorl and his church or to his awkward carriage, offering an analogy from a familiar title rather than textual evidence.
  • 1998-10-02 the Devil in a ForestAsks whether anyone has read The Devil in a Forest, a 1978 Wolfe novel the poster found in a used bookstore and had never seen mentioned on the list. Notes the cover's description of the author as acclaimed for The Fifth Head of Cerberus and asks for opinions before reading it.
  • 1998-07-12 Cards as CurrencyArgues that a proposed card-currency explanation is unlikely because it leaves unexplained what happened to the original currency, since no other money is mentioned even by nostalgic characters. Reasons from the absence of any mention of earlier money in the text, concluding cards must have been the only currency in the Whorl.
  • 1998-07-12 Who is Auk's mother?Proposes that Auk in The Book of the Long Sun is an embryo like Chenille and Silk, and asks other readers for further clues to his parentage. Grounds the theory in Auk's staying power when injured, his engineering theophany, and his leadership of others toward Blue, and cites his memory of a mother named Lilly and the secret of his birth noted near Nightside the Long Sun page 35.
  • 1998-07-12 Cards as currencyArgues Silk's cards are not interchangeable, since Malmeta needs a specific kind and tells them apart by shape, and that Pas spread them through the ship so no faction could control the landers. Disputes the idea that cards are interchangeable, citing the Silk and Malmeta conversation and recalling the cards' differing shapes from memory.
  • 1998-06-13 Talus, Urus and BufesProposes that the snakes attacking Villus in Calde were friends of Quetzal rather than manifestations of Echidna, and that Urus's terms "bufes" and "tall asses" distinguish guard machines by whether they have treads. Grounds the inhumi reading in Quetzal's reveal as the old man, Mucor's possession of Marble, Silk's unreliability, and the inhumi's fondness for children, and asks the list for thoughts.
  • 1998-05-22 Talus derivationArgues that the word talus means a heap of rubble and that Wolfe would not borrow Talos for the name of the bulldozer-like machines, since the tracked vehicles came first and the guardian vehicles were named after them. Grounds this in the sequence of Pas's bulldozers, the later armored vehicles, and Occam's Razor, then asks how intelligent taluses are, what happens to bribed ones, and what became of the yellow taluses.
  • 1998-05-19 Derivation of talusProposes a derivation for the word "talus" in Wolfe's Nightside the Long Sun: the taluses in the painting of Pas creating the Whorl are bulldozers, and talus is named after the rubble slope a bulldozer blade leaves. Grounds this on the butter-yellow colour matching Caterpillar's brand colour and on Webster's definition of talus as a slope of accumulated rock debris.
  • 1998-05-09 False Gods and BufesAsks why Maytera Mint seems to regard Urus as wholly evil, when she sees some good in everyone else, including Eland's devotion to the tunnel gods. Grounds the question in Remora's remark in the tunnel scene about the "false gods" gathered and sacrificed, and notes the point seems unaddressed on the list.
