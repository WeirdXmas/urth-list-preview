Their messages (7 with summaries)

  • 2009-02-10 Quasi Christ?Argues that Wolfe's Book of the New Sun universe contains no other sentient species beyond human beings and Powers like the Cumaean, Inire and Tzadkiel, so no further Christ figures are needed. Responds to Joe Riley's reading of Vatican II's Lumen Gentium, which would allow other peoples to be saved through their own religions, by citing the Inhumi, the Neighbors and the Cumaean's contact on Sirius.
  • 2008-04-25 Etymology/meaning/onomatopoeic aspects of the word "Urth"Replies to Pavel Bakiš's question about translating "Urth" into Czech by suggesting a Czech pun on "země" with resonances like the Norns and language evolution. Builds on Dave Lebling's earlier post, defending the added "Ur" as a fitting ambiguity about whether readers are ancestors or descendants of Urth's people.
  • 2008-04-23 PEACE: Gift ExchangeAsks whether the gift exchange in the first chapter of Peace, in which pearls are given for toilet water, literally means toilet water, and uses it to think about Weer's family. Responds to Michael Straight's remark on the Christmas gifts and recalls the poster's own childhood jokes about eau de toilette.
  • 2007-12-20 7ANAgrees with Joel Sieh that the journal in Seven American Nights may be partly fabricated, suggesting the finale is Wolfe's trick rather than a clue to tease out. Reasons that some of the journal must be true, that the story is probably genuine at the start and later completed by another hand, and proposes looking for voice changes or plot inconsistencies, such as the police station visit, as evidence.
  • 2007-12-18 7ANAsks what evidence shows that the journal in Seven American Nights is authentic, noting that nothing in the self-writing machine episode or the finale's handwriting exchange confirms the story as factual. Refers to the poster having searched the archives first, so the question is raised as an unanswered one rather than a claim.
  • 2007-06-14 A QuestionArgues that the Soldier books and The Book of the New Sun are good entry points into Wolfe, while The Fifth Head of Cerberus suits puzzle solvers and is a weaker starting place for this reader. Grounds this in the clear, pure prose of Wolfe's sentences, citing a plain line about the sea and sun, and in Michael Moorcock's praise of The Shadow of the Torturer.
  • 2007-04-25 Severian's sex LifeArgues that Severian may not have had sex with all the women he mentions, since he describes his encounter with Thecla offhandedly, unlike his detailed accounts of other encounters. Supports this with a comparison to fishermen or golfers exaggerating, then adds that Severian's conduct would be unremarkable across most of history, so readers' reactions reflect their own culture. Responds to Michael Straight's point about male chastity in Christian cultures.
