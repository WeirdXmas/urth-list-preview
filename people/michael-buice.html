  • 2003-08-20 Father Inire's Mirrors and Wolfe PhysicsArgues that the frustrated-tunneling experiment with a prism that James Wynn linked is fully explained by classical physics, so quantum mechanics is not needed for the Father Inire mirror effect. Responds to Wynn's linked demonstration and his question about whether it has any basis in the Fish explanation, saying he already described it in an earlier post and will examine the matter once he gets his copy of Shadow & Claw back.
  • 2003-08-19 Father Inire's Mirrors and Wolfe PhysicsArgues that the quantum tunneling explanation in Father Inire's mirrors discussion has nothing to do with the EPR paradox, which concerns entanglement rather than tunneling. Corrects Tony Martinez by distinguishing the two physics concepts and illustrating tunneling with the frustrated total internal reflection of light between two prisms.
  • 2003-08-19 Father Inire's Mirrors and Wolfe PhysicsExplains that group velocity and phase velocity are distinct in wave mechanics, using a pond-ripple analogy to show why light components can seem to exceed light speed without carrying usable information. Responds to Tony Martinez's recollection of a passage in The Book of the New Sun, noting Severian's wave-mechanical descriptions of matter under quantum mechanics.
  • 2003-08-19 Father Inire's Mirrors and Wolfe PhysicsRecalls that on first reading The Book of the New Sun the poster thought Severian was straining to describe something quantum mechanical, and says a detailed answer about the fish explanation must wait since the poster's copy is out on loan.   • 2003-08-18 Whorl <> Second EmpireArgues that Wolfe blends several science-fiction traditions in the Sun universe well, since the narrators Severian and Horn are lay observers whose confusion about technology is realistic. Responds to Alice K. Turner's claim that the mixed technologies are confusing, comparing the variety to horse and buggy still sharing roads with rockets.
  • 2003-08-18 chems on Urth and a FTL WhorlArgues that with enough power a generational starship becomes unrealistic, since relativistic time dilation and Lorentz contraction could let passengers cross the distance in seconds while Urth experiences decades. Grounds this in the physics of subluminal travel, noting that the Long Sun ship's roughly 500 years of passage would be a more plausible compromise and that faster-than-light travel would break causality.
  • 2003-08-18 chems on Urth and a FTL WhorlArgues that subjective travel time could in principle be made as short as desired, but that the real obstacle is engineering acceleration, while agreeing with the quoted post that practical interstellar travel would need superluminal speed. Notes that superluminal travel breaks causality and that no satisfactory solution is known, answering the quoted post's point about speed of light and the Alastair Reynolds light-hugger trilogy.
