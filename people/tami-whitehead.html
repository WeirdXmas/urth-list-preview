Their messages (12 with summaries)

  • 2003-08-22 love applesAdds that "love apple" was also an old name for mandrake, whose fruit was thought to act as an aphrodisiac and love charm, supporting a sexual-symbolism reading of the tomatoes. Builds on James Wynn's quoted reading of the Long Sun tomato dispute between Silk and Maytera Rose as a metaphor for denied love.
  • 2003-04-26 Generic ConsiderationsSupports Blattid's claim that Wolfe's puzzles and tricks express a deep conviction in the Truth of the Text, and argues that Robert Graves held a similar, broader view of texts. Points to Graves' influence on the Soldier books, including misused Spartan and Persian history and invented myth, and rejects social construction readings of myth and history.
  • 2003-03-02 Mattachine TowerQuotes Wolfe's glossary entry for Matachin Tower in The Castle of the Otter, which describes matachins as masked sword dancers in a Spanish tradition said to derive from the Moors. Responds to Bratt Ian's post on the word's Arabic origin by supplying Wolfe's own wording, where Wolfe adds a noncommittal "Well, maybe" to the Moorish derivation.
  • 2003-01-31 DOORS: men and goddessSpeculates that in the Otherworld of Doors the men who die young are sacrificed to the goddess as an act of giving, not merely sexual release, and asks why male survivors do not turn to homosexual behavior or masturbation instead. Builds on Jeff Wilson's point about men being winnowed out early, and cites the Attis comparison raised by another list member and Latro's faun-boy episode.
  • 2003-01-28 Sign from the fish's bellyClarifies that the poster is not contesting Nutria's reading of the symbols in Jonah, only correcting a point that some had taken to mean the Ninevites saw the sign of the fish. Responds to Nutria's learned remarks on the Old and New Testaments and expresses shared interest in gematria, though it adds no reading of Wolfe's text itself.
  • 2003-01-28 Sign from the fish's bellyArgues that Jonah's anger stems from a bad attitude rather than Deuteronomy's prophecy, and points out that the Ninevites never saw the sign of the fish. Builds the case on the Book of Jonah's sequence: the fish spits him out, he preaches Nineveh's fall, pouts, and is rebuked over the gourd, linking this to the Sign-from-the-fish clue in Wolfe's work.
  • 2003-01-27 Sign from the fish's bellyAgrees with Josh Geller that fear of reprisal is a strong reason prophets resist God's call, while adding that other motives also exist and that God calls people in different ways. Responds to Josh Geller's point about Jonah's reluctance to prophesy to his enemies, and the post is a general theological reflection rather than a reading of a Wolfe text.
  • 2003-01-27 Sign from the fish's bellyArgues that Jonah's refusal of his prophetic mission stems from hatred of the Ninevites rather than the illness he claims, and that prophets generally resist God's calls. Responds to Josh Geller's point about Jonah's reluctance by citing the text of the book of Jonah and the common prophetic complaint 'Why me?'. Discusses biblical theology rather than any Wolfe book directly.
  • 2003-01-27 Herodotus and XenophonRecommends two free online sites for reading Herodotus and Xenophon's Anabasis-related narrative of Cyrus the Younger, one with commentary and lexicons, the other with plain text. Responds to Michael Straight's question about which classical works and public domain translations suit the Latro books, noting the trade-offs of each site's layout.
  • 2003-01-27 Sign from the fish's bellyArgues that the Horn sign-from-the-fish's-belly line should be read through Jonah's reluctance to serve God, and asks whether Christ might have resented his sacrifice like Jonah. Supports this with the three days and nights, Gethsemane, and wider fish symbolism such as the Jewish fish eye of mercy and the papal mitre.
