Their messages (6 with summaries)

  • 2005-08-10 Has anyone heard of C. S. Cooney?Asks whether Gene Wolfe has invented a poet, C. S. Cooney, credited in the dedication of Starwater Strains as the source of the title poem "Lady Rosemary." Notes failed searches on Google, Wikipedia and ABE Books and wonders whether Wolfe used a pseudonym.
  • 2005-08-08 A poem by Emily Dickinson - bear with me!Proposes that Emily Dickinson's poem "Presentiment" is an allusion Wolfe drew on, citing words like presentiment, shadow and lawn as echoes of The Shadow of the Torturer and A Solar Labyrinth. Grounds the idea in the poem's first lines, a quoted Wolfe sentence about presentiment, and the guess that Wolfe owns a dog-eared copy of Dickinson.
  • 2005-08-03 Mamelta is Kypris?Raises the old Crying Game theory, that Silk's partner may not be who she seems, as a possible explanation for the Silk-Hyacinth situation. Replies to don doggett's argument that Hyacinth could see Kypris, suggesting instead that Silk and Hyacinth never consummated their vows, which might explain Silk's suicidal state on the airship.
  • 2005-03-23 Definitions of 'Fascism'Quotes several scholarly definitions of fascism, including Paxton, Griffin and Umberto Eco's fourteen traits of Ur-Fascism, as a long list-based reply to a claim about Moore's view of fascism in V for Vendetta. Its grounds are the quoted definitions and Eco's text itself, with no link to Wolfe's books, so it is an off-topic contribution.
  • 2005-03-15 Damon Knight's The Man in the TreeAsks whether the name Gene Anderson in Damon Knight's The Man in the Tree, read as Gene Other Son, and its blurb about a giant prophet echo Wolfe's Peace and The Book of the New Sun. Notes Wolfe's remark that Knight grew him from a bean and the 1984 copyright, then asks what give and take exists between the two writers.
  • 2004-10-04 SulpiciusIntroduces the poster's research linking the autarch Sulpicius to Sulpicius Severus and Severian's name, and proposes that Wolfe builds a verbal joke on Pelagianism. Grounds this in the Oxford dictionary entries on Severus, Pelagian and pelagic, and asks whether Severian's allegiance to Vodalus parallels Sulpicius's Pelagian period, responding to Roy Lackey's post on Sulpicius's absence.
