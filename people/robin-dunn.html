  • 2009-07-30 Urth Digest, Vol 59, Issue 24Argues that the split infinitive is acceptable English, tracing the grammar prohibition to 19th-century grammarians and comparing it to Latinate borrowings. Cites a Wikipedia article and a usage book by Bill Walsh as sources, and concludes that only split infinitives that seriously hamper clarity should be rewritten. This is an off-topic digression in a digest thread, unrelated to Wolfe's fiction.

  • 2009-05-19 Urth Digest, Vol 57, Issue 31Jokingly calls on readers to take to the streets against SUVs and fast food and to burn neoconservatives, mixing in a reference to Giordano Bruno and a made-up Group of Seventeen.   • 2009-05-16 Urth Digest, Vol 57, Issue 13Argues that Wolfe is a friend of authoritarianism who admires a Christ-like good king, yet would not endorse socialism, since class and inequity run through his stories. Responds to an earlier poster's remarks on American class and Kipling by weighing socialism's aim of narrowing the rich-poor gap against the costs of revolution.

  • 2009-04-16 Urth Digest, Vol 56, Issue 37Jokes that a film of The Fifth Head of Cerberus would be the first choice for adaptation, noting the robot would cost a moderate amount to produce. Replies to John Watkins's suggestion that Long Sun suit a series and Fifth Head a discrete movie, and proposes a short 5 to 15 minute film.
  • 2009-04-16 Urth Digest, Vol 56, Issue 37Asks which of Wolfe's short stories would be easiest to film while still conveying his moral ambiguity and complexity on screen. Says the poster is not well read in the short work and finds it drier than the novels, so asks for group opinions.
  • 2009-04-14 Weekly blog linksSuggests that Wolfe revised The Book of the New Sun more heavily than his other works, recalling that he had all four books in draft before publishing the first. Responds to Transentient's claim that the later Short Sun and Wizard Knight books feel smoother and more coherent, offering a possible explanation based on editing time rather than disputing the comparison.
  • 2009-04-13 Urth movieProposes a film adaptation of The Book of the New Sun in four two-hour films or eight one-hour episodes, and asks readers which dominant tone the book has, such as elegiac or melancholy mixed with wonder.
