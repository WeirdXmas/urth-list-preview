  • 2015-04-27 Wolfe profile in the New YorkerShares a New Yorker profile of Gene Wolfe and notes that the article says he wrote over fifty stories, which he says undercuts his own count of over 220 short stories. Thanks Marc Aramini for the pointer and hopes Wolfe will be revered in later years.
  • 2014-10-10 barrington interviewQuestions what purpose the Eucharistic and theological imagery serves in Wolfe's books, arguing it is mostly surface ornament rather than hidden essential meaning. Responds to Daniel Otto Jack Petersen's defence of the theological imagery and his claim that Wolfe does more than entertain, citing the flower-language discussion and Severian's mother as examples.
  • 2014-10-09 barrington interviewReplies to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's remarks on the Barrington interview, arguing that Wolfe's symbols and Eucharistic images are mostly surface features rather than hidden meanings. Defends the view that Wolfe's puzzles serve the plot and that he writes philosophical and spiritual material centrally, citing the Short Sun Eucharistic images and the flower-on-the-thwarts episode in Gyoll.
  • 2014-10-07 barrington interviewArgues that Wolfe's puzzles serve the plot and characters, that symbolic readings lack relevance beyond the story, and that Wolfe is a craftsman rather than a preacher. Responds to Jeffrey Wilson's demand for who is right by proposing that the best reading explains the most events without misprision, citing the Cherry Jubilee wiki analysis and Tallman's account of the excised seventh night in Seven American Nights.
  • 2014-09-06 5HCArgues that interpreting The Fifth Head of Cerberus cannot rest on logic and truth alone, since art is grasped by intuition, and declines to argue further with Gerry Quinn's reading. Replies to Lee's quoted remark that scare quotes around logic are unhappy, defending his own use of quotes and citing Dali's melting watches and a Lynch film as examples.
  • 2014-09-04 5HCDisputes Gerry Quinn's claim that the green eyes of Sandwalker's people and the Shadow Children are explained by genetic drift, arguing that the explanation fails because green eyes would not mark the Shadow Children if the ancient humans were numerous. Points to the text's statement that there are many more of Sandwalker's people than Shadow Children, and to the Shadow Children keeping green eyes when they imitate modern humans.
  • 2014-09-03 5HCArgues that the Shadow Child took Eastwind's form after Eastwind was killed, so the twin brothers who greet the French are really one Shadow Child and Sandwalker, not interbred hybrids. Responds to Marc Aramini's claim that Sandwalker's Annese descendants are Shadow Child hybrids, rejecting interbreeding as implausible and quoting the Arrow paperback's wording about open hands.
  • 2014-09-03 5HCArgues that the repeated emphasis on green eyes in The Fifth Head of Cerberus (Marsch's eyes, the boy Victor's eyes, and the line that green is the colour of eyes for Sandwalker's people) is a deliberate clue. Grounds this in the idea that a Shadow Child imitating an Annese would need green eyes, pointing toward a single solution to the puzzle.
  • 2014-09-03 5HC - Maison du ChienProposes that Number Five and his brother David in The Fifth Head of Cerberus parallel Romulus and Remus as founders of a nation or empire. Speculates, via the Sabine women, that Maitre meant to repopulate the Ste. Croix and Ste. Anne islands with pure-human clones.
  • 2014-09-03 5HC - Maison du ChienProposes that Number Five and his brother David in The Fifth Head of Cerberus parallel Romulus and Remus as founders of a nation or empire. Raises the Sabine women abduction and the idea that Maitre meant to repopulate the islands with pure-human clones as further possibilities, offering them as speculation rather than argument.
  • 2014-09-02 5HCDisputes Gerry Quinn's claim that Victor is his own mother, arguing instead that Victor is a Shadow Child who mimics humans and that his mother was a shapeshifter. Cites Old Trenchard's marriage claim and shapeshifting description, and the illiterate cell neighbour, to argue ambiguity needs no global delusion.
  • 2014-09-01 5HoC: The Top Floor of the LibraryAsks who wrote the book about Trotsky's assassination on the library's top floor in The Fifth Head of Cerberus, and whether Five's first name Gene is confirmed in the text. Argues that Maitre's cloning shows Ste. Croix's people are Earth-human, since his genetics must come from Terrestrial textbooks and shapeshifting cannot reach the microbiological level.
  • 2014-09-01 5HCDefends a proposal that the Annese aboriginals of Sainte Anne cannot shapeshift, and that the illiterate woman in the neighbouring cell is Victor's mother, against Gerry Quinn's claim that the idea negates the whole book. Grounds the reply in Marsch's Shadow Child testimony, Victor's hint about his mother going to Sainte Croix, and the claim that subtle clues leave such a reading ambiguous without destroying the story.
  • 2014-09-01 5HCArgues that John Marsch knows he is really a shadow child mimicking a man, citing Wolfe's interview where Marsch knows he is not a real Earthman. Responds to Gerry Quinn's claim that characters are not deluded and that hidden clues are never reinforced, pointing to Marsch's jail fantasies about his mother and Wolfe's stated reason for not repeating clues.
  • 2014-09-01 5HCArgues that Victor is a Shadow Child imitating an Annese who imitates Marsch, and that the legs, scars and secret police in Ste. Croix are deliberate clues rather than incidental detail. Answers Gerry Quinn's view that the OWO gives too little information, citing Cave Canem's brothel, Annese ancestry, Shadow Children saliva and slavery in Ste. Croix as evidence.
  • 2014-08-31 5HCProposes that the human populations of both planets descend from three separate waves of settlement, with an early wave interbreeding with humans and the Shadow Children being shapeshifting aborigines who imitated the first settlers. Grounds this in the Ste. Croix underclass, the Annese 'abos' of Sandwalker's people, and details from Marsch's story and the drug the Shadow Children use.
  • 2014-08-08 Short Story 82: The God and His ManProposes that the static-like name "Isid iooo ioooeee" in Short Story 82 is a garbled form of "ICWE", with the doubled "Iooo" read as a W, and asks what the acronym might mean. Compares the garbled sound to the lyrics of Louie Louie and speculates on whether W stands for Weapon or Wolfe.
