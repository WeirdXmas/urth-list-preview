  • 2000-08-24 Plant EngineeringAsks for background on the journal Gene Wolfe edited, Plant Engineering, and whether it concerns horticulture or industrial facilities. Poses a factual question about the publication with no reading of the fiction offered, so it does not cite any passage.
  • 2000-08-10 A walk on the wild sideArgues that the story is about the need for balance, with the single-minded pursuit of logic treated as an excess, and that 20th-century scientific triumphs caused the split between logic and non-logic. Responds to Mantis's reading by asking by what means humanity 'sold' its wild side to the machines, offering the idea as a framing of the story's theme.
  • 2000-05-19 PelagianismProposes that Severian is Pelagian in the sense that he achieves salvation for himself and Urth through free will, renouncing his guild's cruelty and choosing mercy. Grounds this in the test of Yesod, which turns on personal accomplishment rather than faith, and in Severian's moral struggles in deep water during his undine colloquies.
  • 2000-03-08 Philip K. Dick influence on GWArgues that Wolfe's use of translated proper names parallels Philip K. Dick's, offering Horselover Fat in Dick's work as a counterpart to Seven Lions in Soldier of the Mist. Responds to Jeremy's question about Dick's influence on Wolfe, citing the names themselves as the evidence for the parallel rather than the door scenes Jeremy raised.
  • 1999-12-13 Typhon and PasArgues that Typhon's two-headed state after surgery reads as a deliberate, god-like display of magical technology rather than mere embarrassment, linking it to Pas's bicephalic simulation on the Whorl. Grounds this in mythic precedents like Janus and fairy-tale giants, a practical need to wait for the spare head to atrophy, and the self-image Typhon left aboard the Whorl.
  • 1999-12-02 GURPS New SunAsks whether the list's contributor, Mantis, can offer an advance chance to buy the GURPS New Sun book, and asks for any news on a reported video game based on The Shadow of the Torturer. Expresses mixed feelings about commercializing Wolfe's work while admitting eagerness to buy. The post is social and responds to Ron's question about release dates, with no reading of the text.
  • 1999-12-01 Black Holes AgainArgues that a white fountain's far end would behave like a black hole, since any mass forced through a tight passage would heat up and emit X-rays, so the difference between a black hole and a wormhole-like bridge is largely semantic. Responds to Rostrum's claim that the white fountain would be a distinct phenomenon, grounding the point in how matter ripped apart by gravity releases high-frequency radiation.
  • 1999-12-01 Black Holes, Black Chasms, and Einstein-Rosenburg BridgesArgues that the 'black chasm' at the sun's heart in The Book of the New Sun is effectively a black hole, and that the White Fountain's source fits that concept. Offers the Einstein-Rosenberg bridge as a possible model for a black hole with a ring singularity leading elsewhere, and cites the old theory that quasars are black holes' outpourings from other universes. Responds to Rostrum's question about whether anyone calls the sun's far end a black hole.
  • 1999-12-01 Dark StarProposes calling the dying Old Sun an infrared star, which would make Erebus an ice demon that feeds on heat. Builds on Mantis's earlier theory about the sun and Erebus, offering a naming suggestion and a consequence for Erebus's nature rather than new textual evidence.
  • 1999-12-01 Black Hole SunArgues that the White Fountain cannot truly cure the Old Sun's decay, since it only supplies more fuel for the worm gnawing at the star's heart. Responds to an earlier quoted point about black holes by saying the cosmic fix is temporary, which undermines the allegorical sense of the New Sun burning away corruption.
  • 1999-11-02 (no subject)Argues that Wolfe's technological wonders in The Book of the New Sun, such as Typhon's second head, should still be sought as physical explanations because plausible mechanics strengthen their symbolic and divine associations. Supports this by replying to Daniel Fusch's Typhon example and contrasting it with fairy tales and Clarke-style science fiction.
  • 1999-11-02 Technology as Magic and Metaphor (long)Argues that technology in Wolfe's books, such as Inire's mirrors, Typhon's grafted head and the heirogrammates' love-driven devices, works as both a plausible-seeming explanation and a spiritual metaphor, so scientific explanations are meant to be sought in the background. Responds to Carlton Greene's claim that Inire's mirror explanation is deliberate gibberish, citing Typhon's head graft, the thorn Severian finds, and the sin of godless exploitation of science.
  • 1999-10-29 Inca Head of Day RE: postmodernism, OTism, continent-iArgues that the autochthons of the Long Sun-era Whorl speak original Quechua, citing the title Apu-Punchau as an authentic Inca term for the sun deity. Supports this by listing further evidence: the autochthons' dress, their reed boats, and the custom of drinking mate through a metal straw, which the poster links to Peru and Bolivia. The post replies to Mitchell Bailey, who had questioned whether the poster could claim to know Quechua.

Responds to a post in which Tom Urash quotes Jordan's idea that Ultan is Borges, and the poster praises it and requests the textual evidence.

Responds to the earlier Juturna-as-time-traveller idea by citing Juturna's line that the rescue has not yet occurred and Severian's claimed loss of miracle power.
  • 1999-10-21 The Conation of Catodon; Severian's skull (long)Asks who Catodon is and what the word "conation" means in Juturna's speech about Abaia, and proposes that humans and the brides of Abaia share a common origin, linking Idas and Baldanders. Also proposes that the skull Severian finds in the Gyoll is his own, implying he drowned as a boy and was resurrected, and questions Juturna's motives for saving him.

  • 1999-10-02 Slug FestArgues that Hethor's slug is probably not the transformed Mother Pyrexia, and that Hethor simply produced it from his pocket mirror, which requires fewer assumptions. Responds to Carlton Greene's reading of the alcalde's "just like" remark by citing the text's silence on the slug's appearance, its night attack, and its absence from the torch-lit tale.
  • 1999-10-02 Jolenta stopped taking her medicineProposes that Jolenta died from her dependence on hormones and drugs used to sustain her glamour, not from the blood bat bite.   • 1999-10-01 Magnetic PolesArgues that Urth probably has little or no magnetic field because its core has solidified, so Severian never uses a magnetic compass in The Book of the New Sun. Grounds this on the absence of any compass use in the text, and concedes a point that reversed north and south would conflict with Severian's northward travel, Ascia above the equator, and the southern polar ice. Responds to Mitchell Bailey's objection that the question is irrelevant.
  • 1999-09-30 Floating IslandsArgues that the setting of The Book of the New Sun is a transformed South America, citing Lake Diuturna's resemblance to Lake Titicaca, its floating reed islands and reed boats, and a Quechua-Aymara style rivalry between shore and lake peoples. Supports this with specifics from the text and the real region, then proposes that Severian's northward journey may imply reversed magnetic poles, asking whether the sun rises in the east.
  • 1999-09-30 Hethor's Slug RevisitedArgues that the creature attacking Severian in the Magician's Academy is the transformed Mother Pyrexia rather than Hethor's giant slug, reviving a reading that Pyrexia survived near Saltus. Supports this by contrasting the slug's destrier-sized bulk and its ability to snuff out torches with Pyrexia's fire-fearing, nocturnal nature, citing passages from The Citadel of the Autarch.
  • 1999-09-29 Scylla -- from Urth to Whorl?Asks whether the sea-monster Scylla of The Book of the New Sun might be connected to Typhon's daughter Scyla in the Long Sun books, and whether Typhon's children stayed on Earth and became undersea monsters like Baldanders did. Wonders whether the monsters' appearance beneath Urth's oceans can be dated to Typhon's reign, though he cites no passages.

  • 1999-09-29 "strike" on the lead weight [Digest urth.v028.n042Jokes that the lead shot Dorcas swallowed in The Book of the New Sun was not the pyrotechnic kind described elsewhere, and questions whether a person could carry a belly full of lead shot for so long without brain damage or illness.   • 1999-09-28 Dorcas as outcast?Proposes that the word "strike" stamped on the lead shot Dorcas vomits up in The Shadow of the Torturer reflects the Greek ostracism ritual, where symbols on pottery voted for banishment. Suggests this judgment explains Dorcas's dreams of shopping while people look at her with hatred, as though she were unclean and had no right to be among them.
