Responds to Brain's 1997 message by supplying a historical and personal context rather than any reading of the novel's text.
  • 2000-09-07 Names in Soldier Series AgainArgues that Wolfe's Latro books use a deliberate strategy of translating Greek place and person names into Latin equivalents, so that Boeotia becomes Cowland and Piraeus becomes Tieup, and that this problematises translation itself. Supports the argument with examples such as Plataea's Clay, Advent/Eleusis, Thought/Athens, and the House of the Sun/Temple of Apollo, and proposes further names to trace.

  • 2000-03-31 Long Sun - worth it? etcSeconds Timothy's judgment that The Book of the Long Sun is disappointing, calling it a falling away from the earlier New Sun volumes and saying Wolfe's last good work was Soldier of the Mist and Soldier of Arete.   • 2000-03-14 Variant 5HC and also PKDArgues that Tzadkiel/Zina in Philip K. Dick's Divine Invasion parallels a helper figure in The Fifth Head of Cerberus and Severian, reading her as a memory-restoring fairy figure linked to Mnemosyne and anamnesis. Grounds this in Dick's own concept of anamnesis and his biography, and ties Zina to Angel Archer in Transmigration of Timothy Archer; it responds to Crampton's earlier Dick comparison and closes by praising Soldier books.
  • 2000-03-08 Philip K. Dick influence on GWAsks whether Wolfe's work was influenced by Philip K. Dick, proposing a parallel between a door-bribery scene in The Urth of the New Sun and a doorknob-and-nickel scene in Ubik. Invites others to name further Dick echoes, noting the Ubik scene is well known and that the parallel has not been discussed before on the list.
  • 2000-02-10 Digest urth.v028.n152Argues that Wolfe's Soldier books are his most impressive work, showing the social construction of religious belief, with gods that depend on human belief. Cites the human sacrifice scene where Mother Ge says she is still young to others, and Wolfe's Greek setting where gods are met after Marathon, and compares it to Dick, Ballard, Priest and Pratchett.
  • 2000-02-04 3 reviews of Strange Travelers from BIPShares three publisher-catalogue reviews of the Strange Travelers collection from Books in Print, noting they bear on earlier list discussions of "The Ziggurat." Quotes Library Journal, Booklist and Publishers Weekly, the last of which criticizes the misogynistic and tasteless entries, including "The Ziggurat," while praising Wolfe's prose.
  • 2000-01-23 Wolfe archiveProposes creating a Gene Wolfe archive of manuscripts, letters and documents modeled on the Philip K. Offers three personal items to start it, including an edited typescript of Soldier of Arete and a signed Disclave program with a Wolfe story, and asks whether a library or Wolfe himself would take an interest.
  • 1999-12-17 the author-functionArgues that Foucault's author-function concerns the author as a unified idea rather than the individual writer, and links this to Ultan's view of books as objects in the library. Cites Foucault's Discourse on Language, quoting that the author-function prescribes the interplay of differences, and disputes the label postmodernity while supporting Jonathan's point about the author.
  • 1999-12-15 Readerly/writerlyArgues that Foucault's notion of resistance differs from escaping domination, and explains his idea of discourse as the archive's rules for what can be said. Replies to Jonathan's earlier post and Jim's S+M metaphor, citing Orwell's Nineteen Eighty-Four and Foucault's Archaeology of Knowledge; the post does not discuss Wolfe at all.
  • 1999-12-09 Readerly and writerly textsAsks whether Barthes's readerly and writerly distinction and Foucault's essay on the author can be applied to Wolfe's work and to the list's own discussion. Raises questions about whether a writerly reading is achievable under social conditions, and whether the author returns despite attempts to erase it.
  • 1999-11-08 What is a lie?Agrees with John Clute that Severian is a liar, asks whether the list accepts that view, and proposes weighing contradictory statements in The Book of the New Sun as a scientist weighs conflicting observations. Grounds this in Severian's conflicting memories of his mother and replies to Jim Henley's proposal to state positions before examining examples.
  • 1999-11-06 Digest urth.v028.n079Argues that Severian lies frequently to the other characters even if he rarely lies to the reader, listing deceptions in the first two chapters of The Shadow of the Torturer such as his false claim to be a Vodalarius and his pretended drinking. Supports the point with page-by-page examples of lies to others, to the reader about his memory and death, and to himself, responding to a poster who found few outright lies.
  • 1999-11-06 natural theology, argument from design, Wolfe, and otherDisputes Dan's claim that natural theology argues for miracles, holding instead that it rests on the design argument (teleology). Supports the point by quoting the Encyclopedia Britannica's definition of natural theology and its account of the argument from design, citing Romans 1:20, Paley and the clock analogy.
  • 1999-11-04 Digest urth.v028.n076Explains that the phrase about seeing the Hand in nature refers to natural theology or the argument from design, a 17th-18th century European belief. Names Robert Boyle as an example and says Kant and Darwin refuted it, wondering whether Wolfe is mocking that view. It answers Dan Rabin's remark about a character who cannot see the Hand in nature.
  • 1999-11-02 TBoTNS as Science and fantasyQuotes a passage from The Citadel of the Autarch in which Ash says science and religion are the same faith, and argues this makes the science-versus-fantasy distinction for the book unstable. Recalls Tim Maroney's earlier claim that the book is fantasy because nobody would be surprised by a rainbow yielding a butterfly.
  • 1999-06-11 Wolfe News--from the Horse's MouthProposes that Gene Wolfe's manuscripts be archived in a library while he is still alive, as other science fiction authors have done. Cites a London Review of Books piece on living literary archives, mentioning Philip K. Dick, Iris Murdoch and Stephen Spender, and a report on Wolfe's workplace.
