Their messages (15 with summaries)

  • 1998-09-04 Father InireArgues that Father Inire in the House Absolute should be read as a figure in a ghost story rather than a Carrollian figure, since Thecla tells the tale to a young girl. Grounds this in the framing: the story passes through a young woman who heard it as a girl, and the Exultants themselves wonder whether Inire leads the common wealth.
  • 1998-09-04 Sev's decendantsArgues that Severian's 'descendants' in the Book of the New Sun refers metaphorically to the creatures of Urth whose genesis Severian made possible, rather than to a literal line or to Appian's castration. Grounds this in the passage about the sailors who paid with their pasts and in Severian's remark that what was conceived with Apheta was 'myself', linked to the White Fountain as a second self.
  • 1998-08-28 Eco's ticArgues that Illuminatus! is valuable for playing with ideas rather than for prose, and defends its female characters as aspects of the goddess. Contrasts Foucault's Pendulum's moral that believing in a conspiracy makes it real with Illuminatus's invented story, replying to an unnamed critic; the post is off-topic for Wolfe.
  • 1998-07-19 AutarchArgues that Severian's title as Autarch is thematic, symbolizing his self-rule as he casts off the authority of the guild, the Archon and Typhon until he follows the Increate alone. Raises free will and time-loop questions, such as whether Tra knew Severian would succeed and whether the Conciliator's return makes him succeed before he begins.
  • 1998-07-03 Severian as the ConciliatorArgues that Severian is the Conciliator because he is the New Sun, and that the New Sun is the Conciliator returning to Urth. Builds the case from the Undines, Ash's disappearance, Severian's identity as Apu-Punchau, and Urth theology in The Book of the New Sun, plus Severian's musing on the Green Man's time travel.
  • 1998-06-14 potpouriProposes that the creature awakened in the buried town is an ancient sleeping evil, likely Arioch, and speculates that Scylla of the Whorl's computer is the same as Scylla of Urth, possibly the girl in Jonas's inset tale who carries seeds. Grounds these on the Lovecraft-like mine scene, Severian's hints about the creature, and Wolfe's habit of tying unrelated characters together.
  • 1998-05-21 Sex and SeverianAsks whether Severian has a sexual relationship with his younger self, Little Severian, citing two passages where Sev recalls the child while thinking of pedophilia, and asks for the quotes. Also raises questions about Severian's cave-of-Man-Ape waking, the beast-cave image, and why his wounds recur.
  • 1998-05-18 On a rereadingArgues that Severian's career in The Book of the New Sun is a slow shedding of worldly authorities in favour of direct loyalty to the Increate, citing Malrubius's dream and the turning point before the attack on Baldanders. Adds a speculative reading of phallic imagery (the autarch's iron phallus, Terminus Est, the Claw) and rejects the theory of thirteen Severians, holding that Sev2 and Sev1 are one person with the Claw as a focus of renewal power.
  • 1998-05-18 Casting CallJokingly casts an imagined Book of the New Sun adaptation, matching Severian, Baldanders, Dr. Talos and other characters to Happy Days and Princess Bride actors. Its grounds are comic pairings of each character with a TV or film figure, plus a mock Happy Days episode summary that parodies the plot.
  • 1998-05-06 Book CoversArgues that the Maitz covers' misleading sword-and-sorcery look was a virtue, since the poster was surprised to find the Urth cycle's citadel is a ruined spaceship, and laments genre bookstores crowded with media tie-ins. Ends by asking the list for further reading suggestions, grounding the complaint in the poster's own first reading at nineteen.
  • 1998-05-05 End of Book OneDisputes an earlier reading of the soldiers at the wall, arguing they merely block the road while panic scatters the crowd, then proposes that Hethor is a fugitive wanted by the creatures in the wall. The argument rests on Talos's remark about creatures seeking a miscreant, Hethor hanging his head, and Hethor's ravings about the captain of the Quazar.
  • 1998-05-03 Katharine and the ConciliatorArgues that Katharine is a robot or artificial being rather than a real person, listing the stage beheading, the wax head, fuligin, and her unchanging appearance as evidence. Also suggests the Conciliator's tomb may hold an earlier Severian who returned from Yesod and fathered a line, making Severian his own ancestor; responds to CRCulver's theory.
  • 1998-05-02 Fav linesArgues that Severian often misreads events he narrates, so readers grasp more than he does, and offers examples: Valeria's comfort about the lost dog, and the mechanical Katherine at the elevation. Asks why Agilus wears a death mask in the rag shop and proposes that Hethor loosed a creature causing the disturbance at Nessus's gate.
  • 1998-05-01 Typhon, exultants, Miles, and RAWClarifies a position that Typhon, leader of the exultants, is a provincial general from a severed galactic-empire world who returned to Urth, explaining the exultants' strange paleness, height and ruthlessness. Also argues Jonas, Hethor and KLS are all lost sailors, and proposes that Severian's claw revived a dead soldier, opening a door for Jonas to return, answering Chris Culver and Mantis.
  • 1998-04-30 Hello, and a rehash of kimleesoongProposes that Urth is a far-future Earth reshaped after a nuclear-style cataclysm, that the Exultants descend from inbred aristocrats, and that Jonas and Kim Lee Soong are travelers from before the cataclysm. Supports these claims with parallels to Athens, Theseus and kingship, the antechamber child's account of the navigator's burial, and Jonas's own statements about his name and mission.
