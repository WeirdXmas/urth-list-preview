Their messages (16 with summaries)

  • 2003-04-25 Generic considerations revisitedReprints a long, multi-voiced reply thread about whether all fiction is fantastic, touching on Severian's possible identity with the Conciliator, Ballard and Orwell, and the mythic function of endings. Quotes a fragment on the publicity for The Shadow of the Torturer comparing it to Gormenghast. Mainly a collage of several participants' remarks rather than one argument.
  • 2003-04-23 Generic ConsiderationsArgues that The Book of the New Sun can be read as science fiction because of Severian's subjective inner development, while conceding the category is debatable and that the story could survive without its advanced technology. Responds to Blattid's genre definitions, including the dilating door example and Gernsback, and raises Ballard's claim that the present has become science-fictional.
  • 2003-04-22 ...uhh...a bit more Stranger...Continues an off-list-style debate about what counts as science fiction, asking whether Burroughs (William S.) can be SF under a broad dictionary definition and whether Heinlein's ideas are original. Responds to a quoted post on Burroughs, Heinlein and Le Guin by citing Lucian's moon voyages, Ballard's remarks and Ballard's crossover with Celine and Jarry.
  • 2003-04-19 Gray, Wolfe, HeinleinCompiles a thread comparing Alasdair Gray's Lanark with Wolfe through flawed, failing protagonists, then turns to Heinlein's rape remarks in Stranger in a Strange Land and Le Guin's treatment of rape. Quotes Malco, Allan, Blattid and Chris, and closes with an Easter greeting. Mostly social chat with no close reading of Wolfe.
  • 2003-04-16 Lanark and the Sand GardenDisputes Nastler's reading of Lanark, arguing Sludden deliberately sent Lanark to fail at Provan and that this does not cause Unthank's destruction. Answers a quoted passage where Nastler says the Thaw narrative shows a man dying from being bad at loving; also dismisses the reading-order worry.
  • 2003-04-15 A book worth buying?Passes along that Alasdair Gray's Lanark, whose author-character Nastler insists the book is about real people rather than science fiction, is available as a boxed 20th anniversary edition of all four volumes. Responds to Malco's suggestion of the book by noting Gray's objection to the SF label and giving a purchase link and price, with a remark on resale value.
  • 2003-01-25 LatroAdmits an error about a temporal inconsistency in the Latro books and thanks those who pointed it out, while recalling an allusion in the final volume he cannot check. Responds to earlier corrections without offering a new reading, so it is a brief concession rather than an argument.
  • 2003-01-23 ...belated response...Argues that an author's religious belief need not drive their creative choices, and asks religious writers whether theology or the story itself leads when they write. Cites Palm Sunday in Pa. Silk's enlightenment in The Book of the Long Sun as evidence Wolfe affirms Christian vision, while doubting that was his intent. Also asks whether Latro is Aeneas, responding to Spectacled Bear and Blattid.
  • 2003-01-19 Small g, Big GConcedes the earlier point about Williams's The Place of the Lion being mystical and worth rereading, then defends a cautious distinction between a writer's theological and fictional intentions. Responds to a quoted post praising Williams and reading the novel as angelic archetypes let loose in the world, with a mostly jokey tone about haste.
  • 2003-01-18 I wonder...Argues that an author's stated religious views should not be assumed to govern the fiction, citing Wolfe's Soldier series, where Greek gods are real but perhaps not gods. Grounds this in Wolfe's remark on the Greek gods, Lafferty's similar claim, and Lewis's Tash in The Last Battle, and reacts to a poster praising Lewis's and Williams's fiction over their nonfiction.
  • 2002-12-28 Doubled NeighboursSuggests that the doubled people in Wolfe's text recall Aristophanes' speech in the Symposium, though the source's people were round and double-faced. Responds to Tom's remark by calling the comparison a serviceable analogy if the physical details are dropped.
  • 2002-12-22 The Blue Urth TheoryDisputes the Blue Urth theory, arguing the Green Man's claim to come from his own future does not place him on Urth. Grounds this in the lack of any text saying the Whorl was the only ship, and prefers the Neighbors' words in On Blue's Waters, 'From this whorl we sprang', to HornSilkIncanto's conjecture.
  • 2002-12-18 Silk out of time / Blushas? Grushas?Argues that Silk's transcendent moment in The Book of the Long Sun is an out-of-time experience of past and possible strands rather than a stoppage of time, and that Marble's decay and Nessus's aged street show time works non-linearly. Responds to earlier posts by quoting the Pike rabbit vision in Nightside and the Duko Rigoglio chapter in In Green's Jungles, and urges close textual study of Wolfe's clues.
  • 2002-12-13 names, Blue is Urth..Proposes the Long Sun Whorl, parked at the Blue/Green system, travelled fast enough for time dilation, so its three centuries equal millions of Urth years. Explains how the lapse would let New Urth's people evolve into the Vanished People and Moon jungles into Green's giant trees, citing Stapledon and admitting the Whorl's return to Urth is unexplained.
  • 2002-12-11 Nicknames?Introduces the poster after joining the list, thanking the moderator for a nickname joke and explaining the choice in honour of Deacon Mushrat from Pogo. Describes discovering The Book of the New Sun in the mid-1980s, following the Long Sun and Short Sun books, and listing favourite non-Wolfe authors.
  • 2002-12-11 Nicknames?Asks whether newly joined members should use nicknames in the style of Vironsese names, and whether a digest is available. Mentions that the poster's existing name is an animal's name. Concerns list etiquette for a newcomer rather than any reading of Wolfe's books.
