Responds to Marc Aramini's thread on Forlesen, with no textual evidence cited beyond the poster's own visit and reflection.
  • 2007-06-07 PeaceAsks whether anyone knows the American Impressionist painter Julian Alden Weir, whose name Allan Lloyd noticed in Cezanne exhibition notes, and wonders why Wolfe would borrow it for the devilish hero of Peace. Notes Wolfe's few references to visual art, such as the moon-landing gallery and Fechin in The Book of the New Sun, as context for the question.
  • 2004-12-05 MievilleCriticizes China Miéville's novels Perdido Street Station and The Scar for implausible physical logistics, such as hauling a giant cable across a city and making and lowering long metal chains. Grounds the complaint in the poster's own experience handling cable and chain, and suggests an editor should have caught these errors.
  • 2003-04-18 Re MoorcockArgues that Heinlein's later long novels repeatedly feature a point-of-view father-figure who is an obvious idealized self-image of the author, and calls this psychologically revealing. Responds to Blattid's comments on Heinlein, adds that he has just read Letters from Hollywood, and asks whether California is really like that, a largely off-topic exchange outside Wolfe.
  • 2003-04-18 Re Other AuthersDefends Michael Moorcock against the charge of moral vacuity, citing his admission about the rape scene in Gloriana, his writings on feminism and anarchism, and the moral arc of King of the City. Responds to Blattid's remarks and contrasts Moorcock's recurring characters with Heinlein's, while conceding Moorcock may not suit Wolfe fans.
  • 2003-04-17 Re Other AuthorsArgues that the favorite authors Allan Lloyd names (Fowles, Davies, Aldiss, Moorcock, M. John Harrison, Crowley, Carroll) differ sharply from Wolfe, and that Wolfe's books use characters to act out ideas rather than being character driven. Reflects on the earlier thread's list of Wolfe-like writers, recommends Fowles's A Maggot and Moorcock's Colonel Pyat sequence, and says recent Wolfe short stories seem obscure for obscurity's sake.
  • 2002-05-25 [urth] Why I don't like TBOTSSArgues that the final Short Sun book, The Book of the Short Sun, leaves too many questions unresolved and that Wolfe's recent fiction does not play fair with readers. Cites the narrators who withhold facts, such as the narrator who forgets to explain reasons or veers off into cabbage growing, as contrived puzzle-making; also faults speculative readings lacking textual support.
  • 2001-04-17 re Bad HornArgues that Horn's adultery before Seawrack is hinted at through a line in On Blue's Waters, where Seawrack mentions Tamarind, whom the glossary calls a fishmonger's widow. Grounds the claim in the page 249 quotation and the Proper Names list, answering Adam's question about clues to Horn's affair.
  • 2000-06-26 re Evan S BarnableProposes that the S in Evan S. Barnable, the name Smoky reveals in Letters to Santa, is a pun on evanesce, meaning disappearing or fading like a ghost. Offers the pun as an answer to a list question about what the S stands for, and wonders why no one has noticed it.
  • 2000-02-19 Bad HornArgues that Horn in The Fifth Head... no—argues that Horn in The Wizard Knight... correction: argues that Horn in On Blue's Waters is a deeply unpleasant character whose later transformation into Silk becomes more striking. Grounds this in his adultery, desertion of Nettle and Evensong, treatment of Sinew, and the rape of Seawrack, and also briefly recommends Moorcock, Gentle and Crowley novels.
  • 2000-02-16 RTTW failuresArgues that Return to the Whorl is one of Wolfe's weakest books, citing Pig's annoying accent and thin characterization, astral projection to other planets, Oreb's possession by Scylla, Seawrack's abandonment, and Merryn's supposed sibling tie to Severian. Responds to Alga's complaints, agreeing with them and giving his own examples, and concludes that Wolfe lost interest near the end.
  • 1999-10-26 Re Ziggurat/ John KeatsArgues that Wolfe's story Ziggurat deliberately echoes Keats's poem La Belle Dame sans Merci, citing the closing words "No bird sang" and parallels such as the cold hillside and faery abduction, with Emery cast as the rejected lover. Also notes a general atmospheric resemblance to James Tiptree Jr., including a Tiptree story titled from the same Keats poem.
  • 1999-10-26 Inhumi secretsProposes that Horn's fall into the pit in Inhumi lets Krait transfuse blood, merging personalities and appearances, and that Seawrack's ring protects against the vampiric Inhumi. Grounds this in Horn's improved eyesight and marksmanship, Seawrack's comments on the three's similarity, and Sinew's claim that Pajarocu natives go unbitten, which the poster suspects is due to home-brewed beer.
  • 1999-10-26 Strange TravellersArgues that Strange Travellers is Wolfe's best collection since The Island of Dr. Death, praising its human-centred stories and their emotional force over puzzles. Supports this with specific stories: Counting Cats in Zanzibar's exiled woman, The Ziggurat's bitter family quarrel, and the opening of The Man in the Peppermill, and contrasts them with earlier puzzle stories like Lukora.
  • 1999-04-02 Kevin MaloneArgues that Wolfe's story "Kevin Malone" is a Christian parable, citing clues such as the butler Priest, the lamb and eggs Benedict meals, the roses, the Malone servants as tested disciples, and Betty Malone's resurrection. Grounds the reading in specific textual details and in the meaning of the name Malone, and offers it as a corrective to the earlier debate.
