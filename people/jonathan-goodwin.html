  • 2014-01-27 Typhon's Confidence (plus some other questions)Asks why Typhon seems so confident about the telepathic sea-powers he mentions to Severian, and whether he has prior knowledge of them or is bluffing. Speculates that Typhon may have ripped apart the woman with the black beans, and wonders how Jonas knows so much about Erebus, while floating a theory that Severian is lying about Piaton's nose.
  • 2010-07-03 Lives of the Great BeastsAsks why the black beans woman in The Book of the Long Sun refers to the undersea monsters, proposing they wish to be obeyed as she did and asking whether the beans were seeds that grew into them. Responds to Lee Berman's quoted message about the alzabo and the beans story, and offers the assumption that the beans metaphor stands for a black hole devouring the sun.
  • 2010-07-03 Lives of the Great BeastsArgues that "megatherian" in Wolfe's novel fits the book's pattern of extinct animal names and its actual great beasts, so an allusion to Crowley's coinage is unnecessary. Responds to David Stockhoff's suggestion that a Revelation beast link via the Lives of the Saints is a plausible literary allusion, rejecting it as too much of a stretch.
  • 2010-07-03 Lives of the Great BeastsArgues that the Lives of the Seventeen Megatherians more likely refers to self-styled great beasts who were magicians than to the powers in the sea, and questions the Crowley-based chain of reasoning. Responds to Jeff Wilson by granting that the title could fit the Group of Seventeen as magicians, while doubting they would die and noting that Severian's suggestibility and Typhon's oath suggest mind-control rather than magic.
  • 2010-07-02 Lives of the Great BeastsArgues that "megatherian" is not Wolfe's coinage but an OED word for extinct giant sloths, used in the same sense of "great beast" for the huge creatures Abaia, Erebus, Scylla and Arioch in The Book of the New Sun. Responds to Jeff Wilson's suggestion of sloth biographies and a link to a Smithsonian page by rejecting any parallel between the mentioned book and Godwin's Lives of the Necromancers.
  • 2010-07-02 Lives of the Great BeastsDisputes Jonathan Goodwin's suggestion that Wolfe's megatherian echoes Aleister Crowley's self-styled Great Beast, arguing that Crowley coined the modified form from the Apocalypse's therion. Contends the plainer Greek sense of the word fits the text of The Book of the New Sun far better than an occult source does.
  • 2010-07-02 Lives of the Great BeastsAdmits he cannot locate the passage he remembered attributing the Erebus and Abaia beasts to Jonas, and proposes they are protoplasm-like animalcules from a Solaris-type planet that Urthian xenobiologists misjudged. He grounds this in Typhon's dismissive attitude toward them and a speculative link between earlier human cycles, the hierogrammates and the megatherians.
  • 2010-07-02 Lives of the Great BeastsAsks what Wolfe intended about the nature, arrival and motives of the megatherians Abaia and Erebus in The Book of the New Sun, questioning whether they were brought to Earth as replacement fauna. Proposes that such creatures take on the characteristics of their rulers as they grow, and asks whether that reading is plausible, citing Jonas's remark to Severian about the zoetic tower.
  • 2010-04-25 TSH: Ted's identityArgues that the details about Emlyn, Ambrosius and Goldwurm still matter even if the faerie elements are unreal, since they may unconsciously reveal the fabricator's state of mind. Responds to Roy C. Lackey's quotation of Gwern Branwen, which dismisses Ted's identity speculation; concedes the approach is not his favourite but says early readers' interest should not be ignored.
  • 2010-03-27 The Sorcerer's House Questions (*Major Spoilers*)Argues that the Compiler's Note in The Sorcerer's House is a paratextual frame that should not be collapsed into Bax as its arranger, since that would make the structure incoherent. Grounds this in Genette's notion of paratext and the parallel framing blurbs about Latro and Severian's manuscripts, and agrees Bax fits a fraudulent, boastful brother while noting Wolfe's habit of making psychics both frauds and genuinely gifted.
  • 2010-03-26 The Sorcerer's House Questions (*Major Spoilers*)Argues that identifying the letter compiler as Bax, and reading the letters as Bax's plan to replace his brother, makes the puzzle of The Sorcerer's House too easy. Suggests the reading would work better if reconciled with an implied author who compiled the letters and with the psychic element, responding to Thomas Bitterman's quoted claim that nothing in the letters actually happened.
  • 2010-03-23 The Sorcerer's House Questions (*Major Spoilers*)Jokingly predicts that readers will keep stacking identity swaps onto the final letter of The Sorcerer's House, ending up in an endless chain of Bax pretending to be George pretending to be Bax. Responds to Gwern Branwen's quoted argument that Bax controls the replacement scheme and that faerie is not needed, mocking the impulse toward ever more layered readings.
  • 2010-03-23 The Sorcerer's House Questions (*Major Spoilers*)Argues that Millie's remark about a professor helping her in The Sorcerer's House carries a snide tone, not a literal sense of hand-holding, and that the professor had a sapphic interest in her. Grounds this in the context of her complaints about liberal college teaching and her letter's comments on how her looks affect people, responding to Gwern Branwen's reading of the passage on page 238.
  • 2010-03-23 The Sorcerer's House Questions (*Major Spoilers*)Says that a Women's Studies professor at Mount Holyoke who offers Millie a better grade in exchange for hand-holding is fairly offensive, though he liked The Sorcerer's House overall. Replies to Gwern Branwen's post quoting John C. Wright's blog notes on his early reading of the novel, and says he plans to write a review.
  • 2009-09-25 Hero as WerwolfArgues that the word "optional" in the Hero as Werwolf text is not a misprint and makes sense as a characteristically Wolfean touch. Supports this by quoting the OED entry on "werewolf" and its etymology, noting the earliest 1000 entry spells the word "werewolf", while replying to Mo Holkar's view that "optimal" was intended.

  • 2009-04-16 PF as YAArgues that Chris, the narrator of Pirate Freedom who later becomes a traditionalist priest in America, is meant by Wolfe to be wholly sympathetic, though the poster personally does not find him so. Asks other readers for reactions and also questions a wiki entry on Seven American Nights that says Americans could behave as horribly as the people they look down on.
  • 2009-04-15 PF as YAClarifies that by calling Pirate Freedom didactic he meant it only descriptively, noting Wolfe's interview on moral ambiguity and his serious view of fiction as moral exemplar. Asks whether Christopher's possible genetic modification carries moral significance in the book.
  • 2009-04-15 PF as YAAsks whether Pirate Freedom counts as YA fiction, given whether YA includes didacticism and sympathetic protagonists, and doubts that Wolfe intends Christopher as a sympathetic figure. Cites a Peter Wright interview in Shadows of the New Sun in which Wolfe calls There Are Doors his closest execution of an original conception, and asks how Pirate Freedom measures against that.
  • 2009-04-14 Wolfe in decline?Clarifies that the poster never called time travel in Pirate Freedom a sign of poor writing, and argues instead that its time-travel element is a Wolfean addition that adds ambiguity without being integral to the narrative. Suggests the whole pirate story may be a fantasy version of a youth spent in ordinary crime, and asks whether other readers saw it that way.
  • 2009-04-14 Weekly blog linksArgues that There Are Doors is a much better book than Wolfe's two most recent novels, and that Pandora by Holding and Castleview lack the unnecessary Wolfeanism the poster found in An Evil Guest and Pirate Freedom, which undercuts an audience-expectation explanation. Responds to Fernando Q. Gouvêa's claim that Wolfe's work varies rather than declines, agreeing with him about "The Ziggurat".
