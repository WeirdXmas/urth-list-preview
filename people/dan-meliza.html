  • 2002-04-23 Human JPEGsProposes that the gods upload fragments of themselves into people and animals, which can later recombine into a full personality, using the hologram analogy: fragments show the same image at lower fidelity. Applies this to Oreb, described as an interference pattern between Scylla and Oreb proper in a bird's limited vocabulary, and to Scylla's varying admirability depending on who she occupies.
  • 2002-03-21 Marble's prophecyDisputes Marc's suggestion that doubling chromosome number would double limb count, noting that Xenopus laevis is naturally polyploid and that chromosome counts vary widely across the genus. Argues that development is too complex for such a simple chromosome-to-limb link, drawing on biology rather than the text of the book.
  • 2001-08-24 (no subject)Dan discusses Campbell's and Frazer's views of myth as religio-political works, then argues that the detective story is a modern genre because it demands experimental, hands-on testing unlike ancient observational reason. He builds on an earlier reply by Rostrum, citing Dupin, Aristotle, Galileo and Plato, and offers no Wolfe-specific claim.
  • 2001-08-21 tolkien, platonism, mythologyArgues that myth-making, like Tolkien's sub-creation, must reflect divine reality, and that the detective story is a modern myth whose puzzles Wolfe's fiction invites readers to solve. Responds to Nigel Price's remarks on myth and the physical world, citing Severian's line to Ceryx in UotNS, Kafka's The Trial, There Are Doors, and Plato's treatment of poets.
  • 2001-07-24 Borges and heavenIdentifies the Borges work Dan had in mind as a poem titled "His End and His Beginning" from the collection In Praise of Darkness, correcting Adam Stephanides's suggestion of a Swedenborg-derived story.   • 2001-07-11 PEACE: Coldhouse expandedProposes that Weer's talk of waking, read against the Chinese garden dream, means either a higher awakening or a child's dream of his own future, and suggests Weer may fabricate events, such as the shooting of Lois or locking a worker in the coldroom, to imply bigger things. Grounds this in Borges's tale of the man who dies and a fairy tale about a boy with a magic ball of string who lives his life by pulling on it.
