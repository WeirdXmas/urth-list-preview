Their messages (6 with summaries)

  • 2007-02-16 Severian snowboardReports that the poster saw a snowboard in an Austrian shop whose design copies the Bruce Pennington cover of The Shadow of the Torturer, and jokes that Severian could have used it to slide down the Typhon statue. Shares a link to the shop and a catalogue page showing front and back views of the board.
  • 2006-11-24 New BotNS cover designArgues that a good edition of The Book of the New Sun should avoid pulpy fantasy action cover art, criticizing the Don Maitz and Fantasy Masterworks covers and praising the Arrow editions. Proposes an abstract design or a moon landing painting hanging on a medieval wall instead. Replies to Jesper Svedberg's thread on the new cover; the post offers personal taste rather than textual evidence.
  • 2006-06-08 Wolves In The WallsNotes a Neil Gaiman children's picture book, The Wolves in the Walls, and recalls that The Book of the New Sun mentions rumours of white wolves haunting the walls of the Citadel. Wonders whether Gaiman acknowledges a debt to Wolfe, his former collaborator, within the story itself.
  • 2006-06-05 Order and chaos, light and dark, ...Argues that the Shadow passage on order, darkness and the Increate recalls Kant's distinction between phenomena and noumena, and suggests parallels with gnostic and kabbalistic thought. Responds to Adrian Robert's query for Christian antecedents by offering a philosophical comparison, while admitting the assumption that the unknowable universe and the unknowable Increate are interchangeable.
  • 2006-05-17 BaldandersAsks what or who Baldanders really is, noting that the assumed link to the undines is unconfirmed and that his origins and motives remain mysterious across The Book of the New Sun. Says he is morally ambiguous and possibly a monster by the end of The Sword of the Lictor, and asks for help after searching the list and reading Solar Labyrinth.
  • 2006-03-21 Hethor, Descartes and the "paracoita"Proposes that the paracoita, the lost mechanical doll in Hethor's account in The Book of the New Sun, echoes the mechanical doll Descartes supposedly carried across the sea as his daughter Francine. Quotes a Daily Telegraph summary of the Descartes story and asks whether Wolfe intended the parallel; the post is an opening query by a new poster.
