Their messages (5 with summaries)

  • 2002-07-29 Le Esprit de EscalierArgues that Merryn may not be Severian's mother, citing her age, and floats the idea that Thais is an emasculated Severian, while also questioning whether Thais is Thecla or Thea. Grounds these on Severian's remark about the real Severian's age, the pause at Thais's introduction on the boat, and a déjà vu encounter Mantis noted in The Urth of the New Sun.
  • 2002-07-29 ThaisPoints out that Anatole France's novel Thais, the basis of the opera, contains details matching the Thais figure in Wolfe's work: an actress, an innkeeper's daughter, a Nubian slave friend tortured and crucified, and fear of aging. Suggests Wolfe may have read the novel or liked the opera, while noting the historical accuracy is doubtful.
  • 2002-07-29 Thais the countessaArgues that Thais is the countessa of the play Eschatology and Genesis, citing the scene where the countessa is attended by Praetorian Guards on the Road of Air in the House Absolute. Responds to Michael Andre-Driussi's suggestion, adding that Thais's name fits a courtesan or countessa and that she conceals her past on the boat.
  • 2002-07-27 Severian's DaughterArgues that Thais is unlikely to be Severian's daughter by Dorcas, suggesting instead she may be a grandchild, based on timing and Severian's estimate of her age. Cites the boat scene in The Urth of the New Sun, noting Thais's silence during the discussion of Severian's heirs and the unexplained exchange between Pega and Odillo.
  • 2002-07-13 Severian's DaughterProposes that Thais, the woman at the end of The Urth of the New Sun, is Severian's daughter because she resembles him: she is tall and dark-cheeked and sleeps on her stomach with her head in her arms as Severian does. Grounds this on a scene where she seems about to speak as Severian offers his blanket and is cut off by Pega, and the poster admits the evidence is thin.
