  • 2010-07-04 Lives of the Great BeastsAgrees that large intelligent sea creatures exist in the Book of the New Sun, citing Baldanders and Juturna as proof, and says doubts about Erebus and Abaia were idle speculation. Concedes that Wolfe probably intends Erebus and Abaia to exist, though their absence from the story and their size under Earth's gravity made the poster wonder whether they are distractions like the Wizard of Oz.
  • 2010-07-03 Lives of the Great BeastsAsks whether the sea monsters Erebus and Abaia really exist in The Book of the New Sun or are invented bogeymen, citing their absence from the story. Argues that their size would make them physically impossible under Earth's gravity, since blood pressure and oxygen delivery would be unworkable, and so questions whether Wolfe meant them as real characters.
  • 2010-07-03 The Politics Of Gene WolfeArgues that the Second Amendment protects an individual right to own weapons, rejecting a collective-militia reading, and that the Founders intended it so. Responds to Lee's gun-control argument by citing the Founders' own interpretation, the wartime reliance on privately owned weapons, and the Bill of Rights' purpose of protecting individual liberties from government.
  • 2010-05-11 SINGLE Gene Wolfe literature universe?Argues that whether Wolfe's stories share a single universe is a legitimate but mostly academic question, and that a shared universe would not make Wolfe unoriginal. Responds to Jeff Wilson's doubt about linkages by saying the poster personally doesn't care and finds Wolfe's development of ideas and presentation more interesting.
  • 2010-05-10 SINGLE Gene Wolfe literature universe?Argues that linking Wolfe's books in one shared universe, like Robert Howard's and Edgar Rice Burroughs's series, does not make his work cliched or reduce his originality. Responds to Jeff Wilson's claim by listing Howard and Burroughs series and Wolfe's The Sorcerer's House, An Evil Guest and Pirate's Freedom, noting such works share only a universe.
  • 2010-05-10 SINGLE Gene Wolfe literature universe?Argues that Wolfe would not be unoriginal by setting his works in a single shared universe, pointing to The Book of the New Sun and the Soldier books as examples that could occupy the same universe at different times. Responds to Jeff Wilson's suggestion that all Wolfe's stories share one timeline and that Wolfe would prefer originality to that approach.
  • 2010-05-10 SINGLE Gene Wolfe literature universe?Proposes that Gene Wolfe's stories may share a single universe, comparing his multiple series to Edgar Rice Burroughs's loosely connected worlds. Responds to Sean Ako's suggestion that all of Wolfe's works lie on one timeline, and jokingly claims an original comparison.
  • 2009-07-29 OT: split infinitive [was Re: torturing BTQ]Recounts the poster's high school English teacher explaining that split infinitives and compound verbs should be avoided because many words between their parts break the sentence's flow. Responds to Jerry Friedman's quoted discussion of the Latin-grammar origin of the split-infinitive rule, offering a different reason he was taught. The post has no connection to Wolfe's books.

  • 2009-07-20 Wolfe at ReaderconArgues that Wolfe rarely gives traditional resolutions and that Soldier of Sidon brings the Latro sequence to the closest thing to closure it needs.
