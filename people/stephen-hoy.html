  • 2020-01-27 [EXTERNAL] Re: Heinlein's Universe and The Long SunArgues that The Land Across borrows from H.G. Wells' When the Sleeper Awakens through its conveyor roads, sleeper-awakening phrasing, and a would-be ruler caught between factions, while noting the parallels go little further. Also finds allusions in An Evil Guest and TLA, and suggests cloning and personality imprinting underlie TLA's mysteries, supporting Gem and Gerry's Heinlein connections.
  • 2015-04-26 Short Story 24: The Island of Doctor Death and Other StoriesDisputes Marc's reading of Tackman Babcock's name, arguing that tackman was a Scottish and northern English lease-holder whose title derives from Old Norse taka, 'take'. Cites an 1803 Scots Magazine history of the tack and, through allusions to Moreau's Talar, proposes a theme of civilization advancing from serf to villain to tackman.
  • 2015-04-26 Short Story 172*: The Dog of the DropsProposes that the name 'Fallinwalls' in Wolfe's story 'The Dog of the Drops' is a pun on 'fall in wells', since the barbs are littered with fallen walls and water-filled foundations that travelers fall into. Responds to Gerry Quinn's quoted post, which relays Marc Aramini's idea that 'Barbs' corrupts 'Burbs' and that 'Fallinwalls' might mean 'fallen walls', by building on the story's setting rather than the etymology.

Grounds each claim in quoted passages from the chapter and in cross-references to earlier list discussion of The Third Man and The Third Policemen.

  • 2011-10-23 Home Fires questionsQuestions why Wolfe uses the term "hundred-day" in Home Fires when no matching ten-day or thousand-day units appear, and argues the term may be mere window dressing. Supports the point by checking Reflection 17's wage calculation, which gives ten noras a day and seven-day weeks, and by noting that no year equals a thousand days.

Responds to David Stockhoff's list of identity problems, citing the airport recognition, the chapter-end window and rose imagery, and the apparent ages of Skip and Martha.
