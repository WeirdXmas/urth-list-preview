Cites historian Hugues Lebailly's work on Carroll's varied subjects and a carrollmyth.com page to argue that Carroll's supposed preference for young girls is overblown.
  • 2010-06-05 Religious writers and audiencesArgues that the Christian parallels in The Lion, the Witch, and the Wardrobe, like the stone table's echo of Christ's death and resurrection, are blatant, unlike Till We Have Faces. Responds to James Wynn by agreeing that most of Lewis's series hides its Christianity but disputing that the Lion's stone table scene is easy to miss.
  • 2010-06-05 traveling northArgues that unintentional allegory is not allegory under Tolkien's definition, which stresses authorial intention, and disputes Jeff Wilson's remark that Wolfe produced allegory despite disliking it. Quotes Tolkien's Lord of the Rings introduction distinguishing reader-driven applicability from author-imposed allegory, and says the freedom of readers lies in applicability.
