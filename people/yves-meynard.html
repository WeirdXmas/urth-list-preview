  • 2006-10-16 urth Digest, Vol 26, Issue 12Argues that the story's future therapy, which murders a girl to cure a disturbed man, is meant to be read as abhorrent rather than as a triumph of progress, because Wolfe undercuts the SF expectation that technology brings good. Grounds this in the recollection that Dr. Island compares Freudianism to machines, which the poster takes as Wolfe condemning psychology that treats people as machines, and doubts Wolfe agrees with Dr. Island. Responds to Kieran Mullen's objection that the therapy is disturbing.
  • 2005-10-23 Marcus v. Franzen = Unexpected Wolfe sightingReports that Rich Puchalsky disputes Adam Stephanides' claim that the basic conceit of The Wizard Knight comes from Yves Meynard's The Book of Knights, since Wolfe began the novel earlier. Meynard grants some clear parallels, mentions his own linear-sun starship story published before Nightside the Long Sun, and jokes about the thread, responding to Adam's comment.
  • 2003-04-16 Jungle GardenArgues that the Jungle Garden scene's Isangoma speaks of animals native to South America, so the setting points to South America rather than Africa and to the jungle of movies. Supports this by quoting the Isangoma line about uakaris and margays and citing range data for each species from web sources.
  • 2002-05-05 Nitpick re: Vance and prehistoric star travelCorrects an earlier claim about Jack Vance by arguing that only Space Opera posits an ancient starfaring civilization on Earth, while Star King and the Tschai novels have aliens abduct prehistoric humans. Notes this sidesteps the problem of missing industrial remains, and doubts the same explanation would fit The Fifth Head of Cerberus.
