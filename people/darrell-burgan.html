Their messages (7 with summaries)

  • 2014-01-31 Typhon's Confidence (plus some other questions)Remarks that Wolfe mixes up his symbolism to keep readers off balance, sensing something of biblical scale in the Solar Labyrinth books without being able to pin it down. Responds to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's quotation of Wolfe about Jesus as carpenter and whip-maker, expressing enjoyment rather than building a specific argument or citing scenes.
  • 2014-01-30 Typhon's Confidence (plus some other questions)Argues that Severian is only partly a Christ figure, since he commits violence and kills almost everyone while saving the world, whereas Silk fits the Christlike role better. Builds the case on Severian's deeds in the books and on Marc Aramini's remark about the biblical parallels in Wolfe, presenting Wolfe's symbols as deliberately patchwork and open to shifting readings.
  • 2014-01-28 Typhon's Confidence (plus some other questions)Expresses the poster's view that Typhon is a fascinating character who gets too little narrative attention, appearing almost as an afterthought in The Book of the New Sun despite being central to the Short Sun books. Responds to Gerry Quinn's remark that Typhon is a powerful force of nature rather than a god, and wishes for further Wolfe writing about Typhon's world.
  • 2013-11-13 Suggestions for Podcast Episode on "Peace"Proposes a three-stage reading method for Wolfe's work: first read for enjoyment, then attend closely to words and oddities, then map characters and events with notes. Offers this as advice for the podcast's listeners, having no websites to suggest for "Peace".
  • 2013-11-13 SeawrackAsks how readers understand Seawrack's role in the Short Sun novels, noting she is important in On Blue's Waters yet her origin and place in the larger arcs are unclear. Says that grasping her seems key to the Short Sun epic and invites others' views, admitting the poster is stumped.
  • 2013-11-09 Just discovered this listAdmits a habit of suspecting that every minor detail in Wolfe's work fits a larger hidden pattern, using Mamelta from the Long Sun books as the example. Quotes a saint's biography of Mamelta of Persia and says the minor character may still belong deeply in the picture despite few pages.
  • 2013-11-08 Just discovered this listAsks whether there is any symbolic or literal relationship between Seawrack of the Short Sun and Mamelta of the Long Sun, noting Mamelta's prediction that she and Silk would become lovers and her apparent death by a giant fish. Also introduces the poster as a newcomer who has reread the New Sun books and is joining the list.
