Their messages (5 with summaries)

  • 2014-08-24 Baptism and Confirmation: Shadow of the TorturerArgues that Severian's immersion in the water and the false chrisos given by Vodalus form a baptism and confirmation that bind him to Abaia's followers, so the sacraments of Abaia are symbolically sealed despite Marco's objection. Grounds this in Severian's remarks on symbols shaping us, the parallel to Catholic confirmation as a soldier of Christ, and the necropolis ranking and Hildegrin's search.
  • 2014-08-22 Urth Digest, Vol 120, Issue 59Questions why Hildegrin and Vodalus would take an interest in a peasant woman, and notes that the dark-haired woman's grave sits higher in the necropolis than the peasants' graves. Replies to Gerry Quinn's comments in the Baldanders' dream and Baptism and Confirmation threads on Shadow of the Torturer, Chapter I, raising these points as questions about his reading.
  • 2014-08-22 Baptism and Confirmation: Shadow of the Torturer: Chapter IArgues that Severian's rescue from water by the Undine in Shadow of the Torturer is a baptism by immersion, and that his taking the Autarch's coin from Vodalus is a confirmation. Grounds this in the Latin etymology of soldier from solidus, the single chrisos Severian is paid, and the later flesh of Thecla as Eucharist, read against Eastern Orthodox sacramental order.
  • 2014-08-11 Death and Resurrection: Shadow of the Torturer Chapter IProposes that Severian's rescue from water by Juturna and the white-gowned woman's lifting from earth by Hildegrin in The Shadow of the Torturer Chapter I form symmetrical, opposed resurrections. Grounds this in the Latin senses of resurgo and the Catholic Novissima, noting that Juturna searches the future and Hildegrin the past for Severian.
  • 2014-08-01 The Beast from Severian's dreamIntroduces an Italian reader and shares a Le Scienze cover showing the winged beast of Severian's dream in The Shadow of the Torturer, identified as Baldanders and linked to the pterosaur Quetzalcoatlus northropi. Quotes the chapter XV dream passage and notes the magazine's Avril 1981 date, after the 1980 first edition, jokingly crediting the corridors of time.
