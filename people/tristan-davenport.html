Their messages (6 with summaries)

  • 2006-08-20 Why so much synchronicity?Argues that Wolfe's use of coincidence follows a genre convention, the workshop rule that coincidence may propel a plot but not resolve it, rather than flouting mainstream practice. Grounds this in a quarter of Wolfe's work and the example of Severian stumbling on Vodalus in the cemetery while still having to choose to eat the autarch's brains.
  • 2006-06-12 Attending DaedalusDisputes b sharp's reading of Peter Wright's Attending Daedalus as a political power-struggle allegory, calling it fashionable everything-is-political criticism without having read the book. Grounds the objection only in b sharp's description and compares such readings to ones applied to the New Testament.
  • 2006-06-07 single parent familiesArgues that Wolfe's fiction repeatedly lacks intact two-parent families, citing estrangement in The Book of the Long Sun, the single-parent setup in There Are Doors, and absent parents in several stories. Supports this by listing examples, including Horn and Nettle, and notes a recurring pattern of father stand-ins such as Patera Pike for Silk and Severian for Little Severian.
  • 2006-05-29 Shapeshifting Evidence and PrinciplesAsks how closely Wolfe's universe repeats across cycles, arguing from Silk's vision of Jesus riding in on an ass that the Sun series may be set in our future. Replies to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes on cycles, citing Silk's mention of French among Whorl languages as possible evidence for our epoch, and asks about an avern face on Hyacinth's ring.
  • 2006-05-05 HethorArgues that the landers coming and going in the Long Sun books are explained by a flashback from Maytera Marble rather than by Mamelta telling Silk, as Rex Racer suggested. Grounds the claim in Marble's memory of being herded onto a lander and admiring a chem soldier guarding it, placing that scene in Lake or Calde of the Long Sun.
  • 2006-04-09 Juturna (and nenuphars)Argues that the pale, glossy nenuphars signify the dying sun, since the weakened sun fails to stimulate chlorophyll, contrasting them with the green plants of a healthy sun. Grounds this in references to plant paleness throughout the New Sun books and in the nenuphars' metaphorical role in Severian's near-drowning, and asks why Urth's plants are pale while lunar plants seem green.
