Their messages (8 with summaries)

  • 2005-06-03 cthulhu mythos, BotNSArgues that Wolfe's depiction of Erebus and Abaia in The Book of the New Sun echoes August Derleth's Cthulhu stories more than Lovecraft's work. Builds the case on Derleth's recasting of the mythos as a struggle between benevolent Elder Gods and malevolent Great Old Ones, with elemental beings and a ruler of the northern wastes, and clarifies a reply to Don Doggett's Lovecraft comparison.
  • 2005-05-16 voided cross and other thingsAsks why a monitor in Maytera Rose's room shows Lake Limna with Chenille possessed by Scylla, Auk, Incus, Oreb and the fisherman when Maytera Marble finds her corpse, and whether a god possessed Rose and the shock killed her. Also recommends Robert E. Howard's Savage Tales of Solomon Kane and comments on Agila's hair and Jugs' appearance, having previously disagreed about the cross.
  • 2005-05-16 voided cross and other thingsProposes that the voided cross of the Chapter is a swastika fitting Typhon's fascist rule, and that black is studiously avoided in the text, making its rare mentions symbolic. Adds that Silk's lioness-headed staff and swordsmanship echo Solomon Kane, and notes violet eyes recurring in Kypris, Hyacinth, Maytera Marble and others, as a loose run of observations.
  • 2005-01-04 (FWD) Re: (urth) The Wizard - questions -- answers (spoiler)Argues that Garvaon's self-sacrifice in The Wizard Knight is both selfish and selfless, since his death spares him from living with shame rather than redeeming him. Responds to a quoted claim that Idnn would find the sacrifice reprehensible, agreeing the point is that Garvaon gains nothing from it and that his place in the afterlife was assured.
  • 2005-01-04 The Wizard - questions -- answers (spoiler)Argues that Sir Garvaon's murder of Gilling is a paradox, since it is dishonorable by knightly standards yet Garvaon is raised to Skai, and notes Abel also breaks the knightly code and is rewarded. Responds to Russell Wodell's question about Garvaon's motive and Idnn's guilt by offering a reading of the knightly code in the book.
  • 2005-01-04 TWK questions and answersProposes that the parasites in the Angrborn's blood tie to Kulili, a composite creature of worms, and to the Norse myth of Ymir's corpse breeding maggots from which men were formed. Supports this by citing Able's account of the worlds being made from the cast-offs of the upper beings, so giants' corpses become covered with creepy-crawlies.
  • 2005-01-04 TWK questions and answersProposes that the giantesses in The Wizard Knight are thematically linked to the undines of Urth, tracing their ancestry to the giants of winter and old night, who are from deep space. Supports this by noting that Don observed Wolfe does not paint the giantesses as inhuman or grotesque as he does the giants, and that Able calls one attractive, suggesting they may look quite different.
  • 2005-01-04 Gilling's killerSuggests that the witch-ghost's vision of giant-human unions in The Book of the Long Sun may be false, meant to incite and disgust Toug. Argues that if giants and humans do mate that way, pregnancy need not follow when the woman does not want it, responding to Seth Lombardi's remark about Gilling's size.
