Their messages (15 with summaries)

  • 2011-07-03 missing Kipling epigraphAsks why the Orb edition of The Book of the New Sun lacks the epigraph for The Citadel of the Autarch, which Wolfe said in Castle of Days he meant to use from a Kipling poem. Notes that the epigraph appears in at least one other edition and praises its fit with the earlier epigraph from Shadow.
  • 2011-05-24 Wolfe's brilliance or my denseness?Jokes that the Urth list's email-based forum feels like ancient technology clung to by the light of a dying sun, a playful nod to Wolfe's setting, while admitting a newcomer's confusion. Responds to a quoted editing note correcting an attribution to Marc Aramini, with no Wolfe textual argument offered.
  • 2011-05-23 Wolfe's brilliance or my denseness?Argues that only details Wolfe intentionally placed are worth solving, so identity puzzles such as Agia being Severian's cousin seem reader-made, while the Citadel towers as rockets is authorial. Supports this with the contrast between intended clues and ambiguous mysteries, and with an anecdote about Atwood's Handmaid's Tale name that she claimed was unintentional.
  • 2011-05-22 Wolfe's brilliance or my denseness?Argues that Wolfe's difficulty lies in oblique treatment of wonders such as space travel with mirrors, the Botanic Gardens people and Miles-as-Jonas, rather than in identity puzzles, which the poster finds mostly obvious or unsupported. Responds to Marc Aramini's remarks on identity statements, citing the Giant King's killer in The Wizard Knight and drawing on Wolfe interviews and Castle of the Otter.
  • 2011-05-22 do the Hierogrammates *care* about the megatherians?Reproduces David Stockhoff's reply arguing that no prime is intrinsically rarer than another, and that 17 seems special through psychology rather than mathematics. Adds a link to a Cognitive Daily blog entry on whether 17 is the most random number; the post has no connection to Wolfe.
  • 2011-05-21 Wolfe's brilliance or my denseness?Asks whether the Cas-Dorcas link in the New Sun books was obvious when Dorcas first appears in Shadow, or whether Wolfe set it up to be missed. Recounts missing the link until the fourth book despite the chapter titled Dorcas, and asks whether that was the poster's denseness or Wolfe's audacity.
  • 2011-05-17 the atmosphere on the bridgeJason H. follows up on his earlier puzzle about why the Water Way beside the bridge is deserted, noting that Severian reports as much traffic of coaches and drays on the western side as on the bridge itself. He says this resolves his original puzzlement about why the eastern side lacked a similar scene, citing the passage Severian describes after crossing.
  • 2011-05-17 the atmosphere on the bridgeProposes that the crowded bridge in Shadow chapter XIV does not spill onto the Water Way because the bridge sits on a higher street reached only by broken steps, so carriages cannot descend there. Grounds this in the text's description of climbing from the Water Way to the elevated bridge street, offering a parallel populated road as the answer to the deserted-waterfront puzzle.
  • 2011-05-17 the atmosphere on the bridgeAsks whether the crowded bridge scene at the start of Severian's exile in Shadow of the Torturer chapter XIV makes sense, given the near-empty Water Way and the quiet night. Points to the contrast of few passersby, a song heard a league away, and then carriages, vendors and a hundred people surrounding Severian at the guard post.
  • 2011-05-13 Ultan's grammar quipArgues that Severian's error in "Master Palaemon teaches us apprentices, mostly" lies in the placement of "mostly", not in the use of "us", and lists three possible meanings with the placement each would need. Responds to James Wynn's earlier post on the grammar, agreeing that Severian means the teaching is mostly done by Palaemon, and reads the passage as Ultan's grammar joke.
  • 2011-05-13 Ultan's grammar quipArgues that Ultan's rebuke of Severian's placement of "mostly" in the Book of the New Sun is a real grammatical fault, not mere ambiguity, since the adverb dangles away from the word it modifies. Responds to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes by granting that Severian's meaning is clear and that writers like Mark Twain use such constructions colloquially, while insisting Ultan treats it as wrong.
  • 2011-05-13 Ultan's grammar quipArgues that the grammatical slip in Severian's Ultan passage lies in the placement of "mostly" in "teaches us apprentices, mostly", not in the pronoun "us". Supports the claim with adverb-placement examples ("John eats only sushi" versus "John only eats sushi") and contrasts it with a speculative Dorcas-Baldander link.
  • 2011-05-13 Ultan's grammar quipAsks what grammatical error Severian made, the one implied by Ultan's joke "But not grammar" after Severian says Master Gurloes sent him and Master Palaemon teaches apprentices. The poster says he has a 75% confident answer and invites other readers' views on the puzzle.
  • 2011-05-12 Sundial with "multitudinous faces"?Argues that the multi-faced sundial Severian describes would not show a different time on each face as he claims, and suggests it is either deliberately configured that way or has decayed after billions of years. Adds that the contradictory times symbolically make Severian's far future obscure and unfathomable, like the archaic words. Responds to Antonin Scriabin's pictures and sundial-mechanics comments in reply to a thread on the sundial.
  • 2011-05-12 Sundial with "multitudinous faces"?Asks how a faceted sundial in the Atrium of Time with multiple faces and gnomons could work, since the text describes its multitudinous faces giving different times. Also remarks that the motto "Aspice ut aspiciar" is an appeal to the sun rather than to a human bystander.
