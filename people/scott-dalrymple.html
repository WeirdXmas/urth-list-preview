  • 1997-10-23 Wolfe.conReports that Easton Press has published The Sword of the Lictor in leather binding and that The Citadel of the Autarch is not yet out, and complains that the Sword volume is noticeably taller than the first two volumes of the set. Praises the books' beauty otherwise, and responds to a quoted earlier post about the publisher's leather-bound editions of the Book of the New Sun.
  • 1997-10-22 Wolfe.conCorrects an earlier message by stating that the poster owns a hardcover of The Fifth Head of Cerberus rather than a hardcover of Island of Dr. Death and Other Stories. Admits to having misread the original post he was answering, and says he is not sure a hardcover of the latter existed.
  • 1997-10-22 Wolfe.conAsks whether other list members own fine Wolfe collections, and lists the poster's prized items: a paperback of Operation Ares, a signed Plan(e)t Engineering, a hardcover Devil in a Forest gifted by Wolfe, and a signed Cheap Street Empires of Foliage and Flower. The post is a social note about book collecting, responding to an earlier remark that the Island of Doctor Death trade edition deserves a hardback.
  • 1997-09-16 Soldier SeriesRecounts that the poster struggled with the Soldier books on first reading until hearing an unabridged audio recording of Soldier of the Mist, which improved understanding, and mentions the tape publisher and a library copy. Also asks whether a CD-ROM game called City of Lost Children relates to a film discussed on the list.
  • 1997-07-25 Westwind (Spoilers)Disputes the sinister reading of Westwind, arguing that the ruler's personal messenger relationship with subjects is poignant and spiritual. Grounds this in the ending, where the protagonist overhears another special messenger and realizes everyone may have such a bond; also praises the story and asks about Wolfe audiotapes.
  • 1997-05-09 beating a dead horseArgues that linking the Long Sun series to the Urth books, with Typhon launching the Whorl and fashioning Pas after himself, deepens the reader's sense of Typhon's arrogance and makes the Typhon section read differently. Concedes the link offers no major insight, calling it a delicious bonus, and suggests the Catholic-like religion in Long Sun implies a shorter span between Typhon and Severian's time.
  • 1997-04-14 NessusArgues that Nessus lies in South America, meaning Buenos Aires rather than Alexandria, since the Alexandrian library no longer exists today. Cites remembered South American clues and asks whether Severian would notice Urth's supposed axial flip, since north and south are arbitrary constructs.
