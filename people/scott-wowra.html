Their messages (6 with summaries)

  • 2009-11-12 PlausibilityArgues that Able in The Wizard Knight is a teenager inside an adult body, so his limited self-reflection should not be compared with Severian's, and that he matures by the end of The Wizard. Grounds this in the idea that a teenager's writing lacks insight, and in the claim that Able is not the bully he seemed early in The Knight.
  • 2009-03-17 Seawrack/BabbieProposes that Seawrack is a Common Tongue rendering of an Ancient Greek name for the woman behind all women, perhaps the Great Mother, linking Kypris, Thelxiepeia and Echidna to Greek myth. Grounds this in quoted passages from pages 148-149 on her name, and offers Babbie evidence from page 155, where Seawrack's stump shows dried blood.
  • 2009-03-16 Seawrack's NameProposes that the name Seawrack is drawn from Syrinx, the nymph who escaped Pan by turning into reeds, citing the matching sound, Horn hearing Seawrack sing, and the nymph's seductive quality. Argues Wolfe always leaves clues in his names, so Seawrack should not be wholly alien, and answers b sharp's objection that the name is too human-sounding for Horn.
  • 2009-02-13 Red in catholicismQuestions which virtue red symbolizes in the Wizard Knight books, citing a Catholic reference definition of red vestments and arguing that Ravd is neither a martyr nor a Christ-like figure. Challenges the proposed link to Spenser's Redcrosse Knight, noting that Wolfe's knighthood tale is set in a pre-Christian world.
  • 2009-02-13 Ravd of RedhallProposes that the name Ravd of Redhall derives from the Old Norse word for red, and that Ravd is a model for Able, who parallels Percival and the Arthurian Red Knight. Grounds it in Hagland's linguistic note, Norse red-haired figures, and the Red Knight episode of the Percival story.
  • 2009-02-13 Ravd of RedhallProposes that Ravd's name derives from the Old Norse word for red, and that Ravd of Redhall is a clue directing readers to Percival and the Arthurian Red Knight as a model for Able's development. Supports this with a personal communication from Hagland, Norse and Arthurian uses of the red epithet, and the Chapters 4-6 interaction between Able and Ravd.
