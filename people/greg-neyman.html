Builds this on a numbered sequence of events and answers the earlier question about Sand, Hammerstone and the chems left hanging after the Cargo left the Whorl.
  • 1999-05-15 Digest whorl.v009.n039Asks for a plainer explanation after admitting he did not follow an earlier point about D-chirality life and robots, and reasons that terraforming robots would have only limited adaptability and might fall outside standard ethics. Responds to Mantis's e-mail about chemical configurations and whether robotic life counts as life, citing his own memory of the defining traits of life.
  • 1999-05-06 don't drink the water? don't eat the berries!!Argues that alien biochemistry would probably still supply nutrients compatible with human life, citing a proto-Earth chemistry experiment and a scene in Clarke's 2061 where a fish dies after eating human remains. Replies to Robert's claim that alien life-chemistry would be incompatible with ours, and says terraforming robots need not be self-aware, likening them to the Pathfinder probe.
  • 1999-05-04 disposable robots and the talusArgues that robots built as disposable servants, like the flotilla machines in the Long Sun books, are a cruel premise, and that a computer's obedience makes the point. Supports this by tracing the name Talus to the bronze guardian of Crete in Asimov's essay on robot history.
  • 1998-11-03 thanks ... whorl power ... airship escapeAsks the list what power source could sustain the Whorl's maintenance operations, such as fueling the Long Sun, setting aside the stardrive, and asks for ideas about Silk's daring airship escape.   • 1998-11-02 Bye Silk! ... fusion ... and Escape from the Airship ...Speculates that Silk has vanished permanently at the end of Exodus from the Long Sun, citing his earlier remark on the airship deck, and floats ideas about Silk as the positively beautiful man and about childbearing time in Lake. Asks whether Silk's escape from the airship cell was too easy, doubting the Trivis would be so careless.
  • 1998-11-01 fusion and englishAsks how the chem Prion could get all its hydrogen from the air of the Long Sun ecocylinder, given how little hydrogen air holds, and asks how the Whorl powers itself. Also asks why Quetzal in Calde worked to stop theophanies in Viron and whether the book explains this, closing a thread on the English problem.
  • 1998-10-31 Digest whorl.v009.n005Asks whether chems in The Book of the Long Sun can access their assembly information and how they could keep running, raising doubts that Maytera Marble has survived 300 years on original fuel. Also replies to Keiran about Hy and defends the idea that puns and alliteration in the text are lost in translation, citing the use of shag.
  • 1998-10-29 what's up with hy? ... plus building fusion reactorsAsks why Wolfe pairs the mild-mannered clergyman Silk with the fourteen-year-old Hy, and whether their mismatch shows that love is unaccountable or that Silk is a fool for love. Also, replying to Matthew, wonders why people never build fusion reactors from the chems' hardwired plans and why English survives a million years.
  • 1998-10-27 more questionsAsks how the Whorl gets its energy and how it propelled itself, given it sat in the blue-green system for years, and why chems with fusion packs still rely on fish oil for taluses and floaters. Grounds these questions in the Whorl as an interstellar ramjet idea and in the General Mint's azoth passing through a soldier's fusion core in Calde.
