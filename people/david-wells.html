Notes Wolfe's frequent treatment of dystopian modernism and suggests empty swimming pools could fit in Free Live Free or Peace.
  • 2008-06-30 Sev's common lineageArgues that the exultants are not truly of this Urth, reversing Mark Millman's view that they descend from off-world origins, and suggests Severian was made common to fit his role. Disputes thalassocrat's view that no effort should find a source of exultant blood for Severian, citing the books' disparagement of mating men and monsters and genetic engineering.
  • 2008-06-19 The Severian Behind Curtain #3Jokingly argues that everything in The Book of the New Sun and The Urth of the New Sun is a hallucination Severian has while drowning in the Gyoll, and mocks a franchise of recycled apprentices. Builds on a quoted passage where Severian coughs up river water and glimpses a masked figure pumping his lungs.
  • 2008-06-15 The Severian Behind Curtain #3Admits confusion about how Juturna's intervention and the Sev1/2/N divergence work, recalls an earlier self-resurrection theory, and draws a Feynman single-electron analogy in response to Adam Thornton's proposal that every character is Severian. Asks whether Wolfe intends the political factions' gains and losses under the New Sun to be unclear, or whether the ending's happiness was meant to be explicit.
  • 2000-09-05 Horn's astral Journey to NettleArgues that Horn's visit to Nettle's kitchen in On Blue's Waters must be an astral journey like those in In Green's Jungles, so Horn would appear to her in altered form. Grounds this in the earlier astral-travel scenes where Horn, Oreb and the inhumi change appearance, and in the pit scene Horn describes, while admitting it is speculation.
  • 2000-08-29 Inhumi secret againProposes that the inhumi's secret is feeding on zoanthrops, a voluntary sacrifice that would leave them mindless as Horn describes. Grounds this in the zoanthrop melee with the alzabo in Chapter XVIII of The Sword of the Lictor, and raises the alzabo parallel and the fate of the wonderful inhumi offspring.
  • 2000-08-07 [SPOILERS] In Green's Jungles, first thoughtsArgues that Horn's spirit is only partly Horn and partly Silk's, so that in Silk's body he looks more like Horn during some trips, and that Horn knows he is in Silk's body but refuses to claim Silk's legend. Adds an inhumi reading based on a passage where Horn says the inhumi will come to drink blood, feeding on hates, fears and lusts, and speculates that the inhumi's glamour was inherited by feeding on the Vanished People.
  • 2000-04-10 Digest whorl.v010.n101Disclaims any claim that Silk is two-headed and defends a speculation that Typhon was grafted onto Piaton, while doubting Typhon's stated reason for it. Responds to Adam's dismissal of the clone theory as too easy by arguing that Typhon's explanation to Severian is irrational and that he is a Satan figure who lies for kicks.
  • 2000-04-07 Digest whorl.v010.n099Proposes that Silk's body may be one of several clones grown from a special embryo, so Horn might have entered another clone and so not be really Silk. Also suggests Typhon's engineers may have prepared multiple receptacles for Pas, asking whether Silk's embryos were clones of the original Typhon body.
  • 2000-03-31 Short sun foreshadowing in ExodusAsks whether foreshadowing phrases in the last pages of Exodus from the Long Sun have been discussed, and proposes readings of four: Horn's 'close my defense' line, his detailed description of Silk and the scar, Horn's remark about sinew and a dark flitting presence, and the inhumi being no more intelligent than dogs. Grounds each in quoted passages and speculates that Horn writes a messiah identikit or foreshadows Sinew's meeting with the inhumi.
  • 2000-01-20 Inhumi secret and "which Silk?"Proposes that adrenaline-like chemicals make the inhumi humanlike, so calmer humans would dull them, and suggests Silk's special embryos may be receptacles for Mainframe gods, so several Silks could exist. Offers this as a literal variant of the 'you are what you eat' theory that fits Horn's account better, and treats the multiple-Silk idea as unsupported speculation.
  • 1999-04-12 Digest urth.v025.n005Argues that Horn's dismissal of the inhumi as no more intelligent than dogs is a serious misjudgment, since one inhumi passed as a senior clergyman on the Whorl for about thirty years. Builds on the quoted point that Horn is an unreliable, partial narrator, and warns the colonists could be surprised when they underestimate their opponent.
  • 1999-02-19 Digest urth.v022.n047Proposes reframing Jack Lyons's point about Christian imagery: theists read non-religious parallels as Platonic echoes of root ideas, while atheists read religions as borrowing from the real world. Responds to Lyons's remark that any story can be given a Christian reading, praising his paragraph, admitting an atheist leaning, and calling the difference purely metaphysical.
  • 1998-09-23 Free Live FreeArgues that Free Live Free is a one-idea novel that drags for its first few hundred pages, and that it is essentially an allegory about the properties of composite materials. Responds to David Wells's request for a review by giving a negative verdict, citing Wolfe's Book of Days remark that novels should contain many ideas.
  • 1998-09-08 (no subject)Argues that Michael Moorcock's The War Hound and the World's Pain is an anti-Name of the Rose, with a Moorcock-as-anti-Wolfe contrast between von Bek and Baskerville. Recommends Warhound as a lucid, lighter contrast to Wolfe and its sequel The City and the Autumn Stars as more baroque, noting the collected Von Bek edition.
  • 1998-01-29 Free will in BOtNSArgues that Severian's life is steered by outside forces, making free will look illusory, and sets this against the Judgment for mankind's salvation; proposes sorting Severian's near-deaths into four types, including the Apu Punchau experience. Grounds the case in specific episodes: the Undine rescuing him in Chapter 1, the Big Gun shot in the Citadel, the avern duel, and his defeat of Typhon.
  • 1997-09-10 Wolfe'sReplies to an atheist's praise of Wolfe's ambiguity by arguing that Wolfe's Severian and Silk narratives do present a gospel-driven worldview, so a Christian writer communicating his faith can count as evangelism. Responds to the claim that Wolfe is not trying to convert anyone, citing Wolfe's story The Detective of Dreams as the model of a stealthy gospel invasion against tyranny.
  • 1997-06-13 Horn's narrative biasesArgues that Horn is an unreliable narrator whose jealousy toward Silk and Hyacinth colours his account, and that his description of the inhumi as less intelligent than dogs contradicts the Quetzal material. Supports the view by pointing to Horn's possible sexual jealousy and to the homosexual relationships found in Exodus and New Sun.
  • 1997-04-22 Alien invaders of Urth?Argues that the Exultants may be a genetically improved aristocracy rather than offworld invaders, and that Typhon's Piaton-transplant suggests the Whorl left Urth in haste. Grounds this in Jonas's remark, the relative recency of Typhon's transplant in Book III chapters 25 and 26, and whether Severian actually touches Typhon when he resurrects him.
  • 1997-04-21 Typhon/exultants not Of This Urth?Asks where The Book of the New Sun implies that Typhon came from off Urth and brought the exultants with him, saying a list correspondent made the claim and he cannot find it. Requests chapter and verse, and also raises unresolved puzzles about Jonas/Miles/Hethor and Valeria and the Atrium of Time, which he has not solved after three readings.
  • 1997-04-21 Zany Outsider clarificationProposes that Typhon exists as several emergency backup forms after being cast out of Mainframe, with the Outsider as a low-power last-chance version that enlightens Silk, and that Silk is a proto-Typhon. Grounds this in Silk's superstitious leaps to conclusions, the inhumi picnic scene, and a speculative plan where gods download into engineered human receptacles grown as embryos.
  • 1997-04-14 Zany Outsider theoryProposes that the Outsider of the Long Sun books is Typhon, the god who lies outside Mainframe trying to re-enter and fulfil his plan, with Silk's worship being misplaced. Grounds the idea in the Outsider's position outside Mainframe, his enlightening of Silk, and a rumour that the Outsider predates the Whorl, which the poster suggests Typhon spread.
