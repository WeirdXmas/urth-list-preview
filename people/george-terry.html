  • 2001-04-19 Godling sizeProposes that the giant godlings of the Whorl could be large because they come from near the pole, where gravity may be weaker, citing Pig's origin in the Mountains that Look at Mountains. Responds to a post by Ratty Rote that asked whether the Whorl's centrifugal gravity lets the large godling exist and how high one must go for less gravity.
  • 2001-03-15 Mother GodspeakAnswers a question about who claimed that ignoramus was the Latin word for agnostic, naming C.H. Spurgeon as the likely source. Gives no textual evidence and does not connect the remark to any Wolfe book or passage, so it is a brief off-topic exchange.
  • 2001-02-20 more RTTW questionsProposes that Horn is a humu, the opposite of an inhumu, who embodies humanity's best virtues, and that the inhumi can be defeated by becoming humi. Responds to an earlier post questioning whether Horn is an inhumu by citing Silk's virtues, Horn's son's remark that his father was good, and a Merton quotation.
  • 2001-02-05 Digest whorl.v012.n037Argues that Horn's death and return as Silk in The Book of the Long Sun is a Christ-like resurrection and incarnation, citing Horn's self-sacrifice on Green and the offering of bread and wine to the Outsider at the Calde's palace. Responds to a trinity reading of Silk, Horn and Oreb by suggesting Horn merely incarnates Silk and that any trinity must include the Outsider.
  • 1998-12-02 Digest urth.v022.n002Introduces George Terry as a new poster and argues that Jesus likely spoke Greek, since the nearby town of Sephorris was a Hellenized trade centre.
