  • 2015-01-26 Short Story 117: Read MeAsks Mark for the specific Lewis Carroll allusion he saw in the Book of the New Sun, which Mark mentioned in his review of the short story "Read Me". Thayer says he missed it and wants Mark to point to the passage or reference behind it.
  • 2014-06-14 Swanwick and othersAsks what specific similarities connect Neal Stephenson's Anathem to The Book of the New Sun, after noting the resemblance and the naming of a language Orth. Responds to Andrew Mason's post, which says he mistook Anathem's hero for Severian and suggests the Orth name may acknowledge Wolfe's influence.
  • 2013-12-20 Five Severians - Severian-as-cloneArgues against the Occam's Razor case for a simple coffin-corpse mausoleum, holding that the hidden clone-laboratory theory should not be dismissed as too complicated. Grounds this in the book's pattern of things that look mythic but are technological, such as the castle tower as rocket and the Cumaen as alien, and in the mausoleum's odd import.
  • 2013-12-19 Five Severians - Severian-as-CloneArgues that the five coffins in the mausoleum hold Severians who have not yet risen, possibly alternate-timeline versions rather than literal clones, and that the mausoleum is temporally dislocated. Grounds this in the three closed coffins waiting on the shelf in The Shadow of the Torturer, the two Severians who left by pg. 406 of The Citadel of the Autarch, and the question of who built the mausoleum.
  • 2013-12-18 Five Severians - Severian-as-CloneArgues that the mausoleum is a time-dislocated nexus that can be entered from five parallel-universe Severians, not merely five instances on one timeline. Grounds this in the two alternate Severians named in The Citadel (406), the two open coffins in The Shadow of the Torturer (12), and the time references in the mausoleum scenes.
  • 2013-12-18 Five Severians - Severian-as-cloneArgues that the mausoleum where Severian's clone is stored is linked to time through its textual details, and asks where it is and how Severian reaches it. Cites the door sprung long ago and eternally ajar, the sleepy noon watching, the aged grass and proximity in time in The Shadow of the Torturer, pages 12-13 and 21-22, as thematic support.
  • 2013-12-18 Five Severians -- Severian-as-CloneAsks what the five coffins in Severian's mausoleum hold, and proposes that Father Inire, a possible walker of the Corridors of Time, runs a clone-storage unit shaped like a coffin. Answers etonio's doubts by admitting the flaw, arguing any mausoleum explanation faces similar difficulties, and citing Inire's Second House, whose rooms look like paintings.
  • 2013-12-17 Five Severians -- Severian-as-CloneProposes that the five coffins in the Citadel mausoleum, two empty and three closed, reflect a Severian-as-clone theory in which each clone exits into a different strand of time. Grounds this in the Citadel 'read again' passage, the clone device in The Fifth Head of Cerberus, and the cowled servants and the homunculus who calls Severian brother, while responding to Lee's autobiographical reading.
  • 2013-12-14 Five SeveriansAsks where Druissi's theory of several Severians is laid out in detail, saying the Lexicon Urthus entries are opaque about which Severians are the same person and what the five coffins mean. Responds to Larry Miller's pointer to the Lexicon Urthus Severian entry, noting the Mausoleum Builder and Dorcas passages do not explain themselves.
  • 2013-12-12 Five SeveriansAsks whether the "five Severians" theory about The Book of the New Sun is laid out in detail anywhere, and requests a book or web address. The post contains no argument, only a request for a source on the theory.
  • 2013-04-04 Thecla's "Identity"Asks whether Wolfe's Book of the New Sun alludes to 20th century history, and proposes examining that broader question before weighing the idea that Thecla is Ethel Rosenberg. Cites Neil Armstrong and Borges as confirmed references and JFK and Rosenberg as proposed ones, and asks whether anyone agrees with Borski's Navigator-as-JFK argument in Solar Labyrinth.
