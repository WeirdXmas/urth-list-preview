Their messages (5 with summaries)

  • 2009-01-19 3rd Cue's a Charm?Unpacks the compressed opening sentence of the story "Third Cue's a Charm?" into six plot-level claims about BB1, the customer NC, and the granting of a perceptual ability. Reads the title as a wake-up call about readers unconsciously filtering sensory cues, and notes that the author's view was initially misread and that someone earlier refuted Borski's original idea.
  • 2009-01-18 3rd Cue's a Charm?Rejects a scatological reading of a short story's wordplay, arguing Wolfe would not write a crude joke and that the proposed links (coot, cooties, fundament, throne, toilet) are too weak. Offers alternative readings of the nested quotation marks, the tennis-racket space-time image, the train-and-rope-skipping girl, and religious puns like staked and give no quarter.
  • 2009-01-16 3rd Cue's a Charm?Argues that Wolfe's short story Cues is about perception: a cartoonist wishes to see the cosmos only humorously, and the cosmos becomes a talking bowling ball that explains how we filter infinite cues. Grounds this in the story's long explanation of filtering, the Coalsack Nebula, and the Deneb/deneb mirror-word clue, which the poster offers as a possible hint about cycles.
  • 2009-01-16 Cue reduxArgues that Wolfe's short short story Cues is about perception: a frustrated cartoonist wishes to see only humor, and the cosmos grants the wish by appearing to him as a talking bowling ball. Grounds the reading in the cosmos's explanation of filtering perceptual cues, the Deneb/deneb wordplay, and the cosmos cutting off the man's remark.
  • 2009-01-16 Thoughts on Cues2Argues that Wolfe's story Cues is about perception, with a frustrated cartoonist choosing what to see and the cosmos, appearing as a talking bowling ball, filtering infinite cues into humor. Dismisses Borski's scatological reading and reads the name Deneb, which spelled backwards nearly gives deneb, as a hint at a cyclic universe, citing the cosmos's explanation of unconscious filtering.
