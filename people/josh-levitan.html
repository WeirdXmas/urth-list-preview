  • 2001-06-21 Fooey for Fan FicArgues that Horn's sons writing the missing chunks of The Book of the Short Sun differ from fan fiction, since they did not write from simple affection for Silk and Horn. Compares them to the Gospel writers and the Apocrypha, whose authors filled historical gaps to convey a message they believed was in the spirit of Jesus' teachings.
  • 2001-06-14 Digest whorl.v012.n141Argues that existentialism is misread when it is presented as a license for religious belief, explaining that Sartre holds people accountable for creating their own meaning and morality in an absurd universe. Responds to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's quoted claim that choosing to believe the Gospels is an existentialist move, and the post admits it is off-topic for the Long Sun list.
  • 2000-08-30 Digest whorl.v011.n032Supports Borski's objection that the inhumi's flights between Green and Blue are physically implausible, since they would need to reach escape velocity and survive re-entry. Calls Horn's in-world explanation of the flights poor science, responding to Peter Westlake's argument that the flights are colonist folklore.
  • 2000-08-10 Digest whorl.v011.n015Argues that Weer in Peace likely killed the librarian, citing the scene where she pulls a gun on him during the search for treasure and his later conversation with Gold. Also asks for the exact Wolfe interview answer on whether Weer was a multiple murderer, responding to Christopher Culver's quoted claim about Wolfe's interview remarks.
  • 2000-08-08 Digest whorl.v011.n011Quips that Horn's claim that Hide/Cuoio 'looks familiar' is disingenuous, since Cuoio looks like a son Horn used to have. Replies to Adam Stephanides' comment on Horn's disingenuousness, as a brief jab rather than an argued reading, and quotes David Wells on Horn knowing he is in Silk's body.
  • 2000-08-01 Digest whorl.v011.n006Argues that the inhumi of The Book of the Long Sun change their appearance mainly through physical means, molding their faces like clay and using makeup and wigs, with telepathy at most rounding out the illusion. Responds to Alga's claim that the inhumi are only partly shapeshifters, citing Horn's remarks about molding faces and the eyewitness account of arms turning into wings.
  • 2000-07-25 Digest whorl.v011.n001Argues that the Neighbors' reluctance to discuss their gods goes beyond the logic of letting Horn work things out, and asks how Quetzal and the Inhumi reached the Whorl. Replies to Groce's view, quoted by Adam Stephanides, citing Hide's emotional situation and the Neighbors' refusal to confirm the Mother as sea goddess; questions whether the Inhumi reprogrammed Mainframe.
  • 2000-07-24 Digest whorl.v010.n138Argues that the inhumi's secret is likely the "Do Unto Others Theory", that inhumi spirits are partly made of what they feed on, and that Horn knows he is in Silk's body but stays humble about it. Responds to Alex David Groce's inhumi point by citing Jahlee's remark about the Neighbors, then complains that In Green's Jungles gives little jungle or Whorl material.
  • 2000-07-16 Digest whorl.v010.n131Reports that the poster spent half a day searching Southern California bookstores for In Green's Jungles without finding a copy, and must now wait for it to arrive locally. Responds to a digest quote from a reader who found one copy of the book at a Penn Station bookstore in New York.
  • 2000-02-04 ZigguratArgues that Wolfe's story "The Ziggurat" reverses Tiptree's "Houston, Houston, Do You Read?", with female astronauts arriving in a primitive male past and most of them killed. Grounds this in the plot parallel and the ending where the surviving woman is left to a man, and notes that Wolfe's Tiptree reference appears in the earlier story rather than this one.
  • 2000-02-04 Digest urth.v028.n147Argues that Wolfe's story "The Ziggurat" reverses Tiptree's "Houston, Houston, Do You Read?", with female astronauts arriving in the past and killed by a primitive male. Responds to Alga's comparison by noting Wolfe alludes to Tiptree in the previous story but not in this one, though this one recalls her more.
  • 2000-01-30 replies to misc. stuff -- Ghosts, rings, etc.Argues that Horn's death and revival were literal and that Krait lied about them, suggesting the Outsider raised Horn with the Claw or Krait revived him by transfusion. Responds to Alga's point that Pike's ghost mixes genres, citing Wolfe's WindyCon remark, Daniel in the lions' den, and Seawrack's claim that Horn was dead.
  • 2000-01-22 Digest whorl.v010.n073Proposes that the people of Pajarocu have a truce with the inhumi, who avoid biting townsfolk because the humans let them load a lander with meat and slaves for their home planet. Offers this as a reply to Alga, grounding it in the staged lander trap and the risk that attacks would scare the inhumi off.
  • 2000-01-20 The Inhumi-Destroying SecretDisputes the you-are-what-you-eat and do-unto-others solutions to the inhumi secret, arguing glandular control and a Christian society are unworkable. Proposes a simple, quickly actionable secret that Krait passed to Horn, hinted at by a Borges allusion to The Sect of the Phoenix. Answers David Wells's quote by reasoning that any cure must spread through society from one person.
  • 2000-01-16 Digest whorl.v010.n068Questions whether Borges's "The Sect of the Phoenix" gives enough information to decode its secret, citing Borges's own prologue to "Artifices" claiming the story hints at an ordinary fact. Asks whether the cork, wax, gum arabic and silt named in the rite could be sexual or birth-control items, or are simply red herrings, possibly due to a bad translation.
  • 2000-01-12 Revealed at Last! What (Would Have) Killed the Inhumi !Introduces the poster's doubts about a proposed secret for the inhumi in On Blue's Waters, arguing that hibernation and the Rajan's remarks don't fit a secret that would destroy them. Grounds this in the scene where Jahlee emerges from hibernation unharmed and the Rajan sends others away before telling the secret, and suggests a warmth or sunlight dependence instead, with a Borges analogy and a relativistic Venus-return idea.
