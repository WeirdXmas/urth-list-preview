  • 2007-04-12 Don't Sell 'Em ShortArgues that filmmakers and screenwriters should not be dismissed as unable to adapt a Wolfe novel, since all stories share a core that a skilled storyteller can translate from any source. Offers no textual evidence from the books, relying instead on a general claim about storytelling and a biblical analogy about John the Baptist.
  • 2006-10-30 The Holy Trinity: Silk, Horn, ...Asks whether the Horn/Silk narrator identity puzzle in The Book of the Long Sun could be read as a trinity, proposing that Horn and Silk form two-thirds of one with a third figure such as Pig, the Outsider, or Oreb. Grounds the idea in the early Church's debates over the nature of the Son and God, and asks whether Catholic Trinity doctrine would clarify the pair.
  • 2006-10-25 _Urth_: Superflous?Agrees that The Book of the New Sun is more satisfying without The Urth of the New Sun, finding Severian's turn as the Conciliator underwhelming while praising the Hierogrammates, Apu-Punchau and the play. Responds to Jack Redelfs's claim that Urth defines where the power of the White Fountain scene came from, while also admitting the earlier scene's power has faded with familiarity.
  • 2006-10-22 _Dhalgren_: Scathing Towards Women? and a few ?'s about SevAsks whether Dhalgren is scathingly hateful toward women and, in reply to Dan'l, asks what determined Severian's success with the Claw and whether his drowning in the Gyoll made him an aquastor. Cites the Triskele healing at the end of Citadel and Silk's moralizing in Long Sun, and says the aquastor theory may explain the Claw's power.
  • 2006-10-20 Wolfe: Misogynist or Realist?Argues that Wolfe is not a misogynist, that his rapes and tortures are alluded to rather than described, and that his Bad Guys often win so readers must find moral victory elsewhere. Responds to Jack Redelfs' thread by contrasting evolution's indifference to wife-beating with Wolfe's humane treatment, citing Severian's line from The Sword of the Lictor about killing parts of oneself.
  • 2006-10-19 Wolfe: Misogynist Or Realist?Disputes Jack Redelfs's claim that society prioritizes protecting women over men, calling the male-injury statistics irrelevant and questioning which society is meant. Responds to the quoted claim about chivalry without citing any Wolfe text, so the argument stays in general social terms rather than the books.
  • 2006-10-18 Wolfe: Misogynist? and accessibilityArgues that Wolfe is a misanthrope who distrusts human institutions, so his honorable female characters like Io are hard to reconcile with chivalry, and urges newcomers to skip The Shadow of the Torturer. Responds to b sharp's claim that Wolfe's feelings about women show in his fiction, citing Latro's line that all men are wolves and Tolkien's sidelining of women.
  • 2006-10-17 Wolfe: Misogynist Or Realist?Disputes the claim that Card's fiction is sadistic and castration-heavy, saying the castrations are hard to find in the novels and the early work is what has been read. Suggests that Wolfe and Card are seen as misogynistic or misanthropic partly because both are steeped in the Old Testament.
  • 2006-10-17 Wolfe: Misogynist or Realist?Argues that Wolfe's harsh treatment of women in his fiction, including in "The Death of Doctor Island," reflects brutal realism rather than personal misogyny, since abuse of women is common in history. Supports this by appealing to the dictionary sense of offensive and to Wolfe's Catholic view that the devil rules the flesh and God the spirit.
  • 2006-10-16 Is "The Death Of Doctor Island" Offensive? Why not?Argues that "The Death of Doctor Island" is brutal and disturbing, unlike Severian's story, because a violent young man murders a girl and the doctor declares the act a therapeutic step and cures him. Responds to Jack Redelfs's point that Wolfe's books already feature an executioner by contrasting Severian, who renounces the torturers' guild in the end, with the unpunished killer; concedes the story is not offensive to him.
  • 2006-09-07 Close Reading: Torturer Chapter 1Argues that the guild masters knew of Severian's bond with Thecla and assigned him to torture her to break or disillusion him, not foreseeing the kitchen knife he slipped her. Grounds this in Severian's apparent surprise at his own act and in his loyalty to the guild as his only home, responding to a poster who questioned why he was called to assist with the Revolutionary.
  • 2006-08-20 Severian as Savior; Destruction Of Urth As Redemptive Act?Argues that Severian's destruction of Urth in The Urth of the New Sun was a justified, redemptive act that brought moral and physical renewal to humanity, citing Urth's brutal customs and the fading sun. Builds on Adam Stephanides's opposing view and points to a Severian-Jonas exchange in The Claw of the Conciliator and a Pindaros line in The Sword of Thrones-era quotation from Soldier of the Mist as support.
  • 2006-05-19 Undines AND The Skirmish AND CastleviewDisputes the claim that undines serve the Hierodules, citing Severian's description of them as loathsome or monstrous in The Claw of the Conciliator and his trust in Severian's judgment. Also questions how a small skirmish on Lake Diuturna relates to the wider war, offers a metaphorical reading, and briefly says Castleview disappointed him for lacking sympathetic characters and an Arthurian spirit.
  • 1999-01-11 Forlesen -- Thoughts?Praises the story Forlesen as a favorite and notes its subtle, Dilbert-like humor and a Wolfe-style ending that withholds a full explanation. Then lists eight open questions: whether Forlesen is a program or ghost, the status of other characters, the hitchhiker, the red book, sudden deaths, the F surnames, and shifting columns.
