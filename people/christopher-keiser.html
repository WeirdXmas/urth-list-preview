Their messages (6 with summaries)

  • 2009-07-07 PEACE: LabyrinthArgues that the word Labyrinth means "palace of the double-axe" and that this meaning, linked to the labrys as a symbol of royal power, illuminates Weer's mansion in Peace. Builds on an earlier list remark by Mantis and on Wolfe's habit of mixing up Daedalus, the Minotaur and Theseus, proposing that Weer is maker, visitor and perhaps monster of his memory maze.
  • 2009-06-30 Tang and NapoleonArgues that the hand-in-coat pose of Napoleon in the Dresden figure is probably an artistic convention rather than a hidden clue, and asks how Wolfe uses it. Grounds this in several readers finding nothing about the pose in Ludwig's biography, and offers three readings: Wolfe misread or cited the biography loosely, the pose is a red herring illustrating Den's partial truths, or Den invents his own story like Borges.
  • 2009-06-29 Tang and NapoleonProposes that Peace's 'tang' wordplay and its hidden sources may come from a Tang Dynasty story, and offers etymologies for Chang'an, Macafee and Margaret Lorn. Adds a Napoleon angle, arguing the 'hand in the coat' detail lies in Peace rather than Ludwig's biography, and floats a pun on the Necronomicon quote linking Sirius with seriousness and lunacy.
  • 2009-06-10 PEACE: the source storyClarifies that the metaphysical ideas in Bierce's story were not new with Bierce, but suggests Bierce's story was the seed for Wolfe's Peace. Notes Wolfe often credits a specific earlier story as the source of his own work.
  • 2009-06-01 PEACE: the source storyLists supposed parallels between Wolfe's PEACE and Bierce's An Occurrence at Owl Creek Bridge and The King in Yellow, including a dead narrator unaware of his death, the repeated 'all is gone' motif, and a wolves-at-Carcosa passage. Jokingly proposes Wolfe as his own grandfather who time-traveled to influence Bierce, and promises a reread for textual proof.
  • 2009-06-01 PEACE: the source storyProposes that Ambrose Bierce's story "An Inhabitant of Carcosa" is the source story for Peace, and that this supports the theory that Weer is dead and his ghost lives in the far future. Grounds the claim in Wolfe's interviews naming a root source story for his works, and in the poster's own rereading that revealed the allusion.
