  • 2003-09-09 Women; Incomprehensible ThreeArgues that Wolfe's Long Sun and Short Sun books are anti-feminist, portraying women only as idolised divinities or degraded victims, with General Mint as an unconvincing exception. Grounds this in the poster's own rereadings and in the treatment of women and rape by the 'good men', while also joking about Buddhism and the Trinity.
  • 2003-09-08 TrinityExplains the orthodox doctrine of the Trinity, that the persons are one but not the same, sharing a single substance and Godhead, and says it is standard across historic non-Protestant churches. Supports this by citing the Apostolic/Nicene Creed and noting that Nestorians shared the same view, so the matter has no real theological split; it is a reply to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's remarks on the Trinity.
  • 2003-08-02 Love the teaching advice!Praises a quoted teaching remark by Alice K. Turner about women writers avoiding first-person present and men avoiding weapons in genre classes, and asks permission to forward it to colleagues. The post is social and does not discuss any Wolfe book or reading; it ends with a link and an unrelated Martial epigram.
  • 2003-08-01 The Catherine WheelExplains the name of Catherine Wheel, linking it to the martyr St. Catherine and her wheel that turned to flowers, and recommends Matt Ruff's novel Water Gas & Electric. Points to its nun character, the Order of St. Catherine of the Burning Wheel, and to stories such as "29 Names for Snow" as worth reading.
  • 2003-08-01 Dog-headed apeExplains that the cynocephalus, or dog-headed ape, was the baboon in the ancient world, a symbol of barbarian hordes and wild lands, and that St. Christopher was a baboon converted to Christianity. Offers a reference book on the dog motif in Eurasian myth, MYTHS OF THE DOG-MEN, and says it can be cited later.
  • 2003-07-19 List of Proper NamesAsks the list for a compiled list of conclusions about the proper names and references in the Short Sun books, after noting earlier etymology lists for the Long Sun's Arab-Indian names. Says the poster admires the Blue-Green stories but struggles to keep up with the names despite knowing languages.
