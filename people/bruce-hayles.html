  • 2011-04-29 "Realistic fiction leaves out too much." - Gene WolfeQuotes Gene Wolfe's interview remarks that realism is a narrow literary genre and that the adventurous reader has already moved past it, citing Le Guin's The Dispossessed as an example. Responds to James Wynn's paraphrase that realism cannot address identity, myth or ghosts, and uses it to frame Wolfe's stated view.
  • 2009-09-12 Wolfe at Dragon*Con (AEG)Proposes that Cassie's stated trip to Woldercan to find Reis in An Evil Guest may be figurative, meaning she went to find love rather than the literal man. Responds to James Wynn's puzzle over Reis's death and whether Chase is an impersonator, offering a simpler reading of the Dragon*Con-era discussion.
  • 2009-07-21 Wolfe at ReaderconAsks whether a god forces Latro to choose between finding himself among friends or regaining his memory, and suggests his continued wandering shows that friendship without memory is not enough. Responds to James Wynn's objection that Latro's trip to Sidon after returning home feels off, since the stories' arc is supposed to be his attempt to get back and recover his past.
  • 2008-12-29 Sleeping BaldandersAgrees with Craig Brewer's reading that the phrase "You -- strike off" in Sleeping Baldanders alludes to Severian striking off people's heads. Says Severian himself seems to adopt the same interpretation, though the poster is working from memory without the book at hand.
  • 2008-12-29 Sleeping BaldandersArgues that Baldanders' shifts between beast, scientist and childlike behavior are caused by fluctuating hormones rather than a deliberate act. Grounds this in three episodes: his charge at the crowd that the cacogens burn down, his scholarly manner at his castle, and his childish fight with Severian there. Responds to Matt Teel's claim that Baldanders only pretends to be a dim giant.
  • 2008-10-03 a lurker asks a questionArgues that Severian could have become a good librarian had his circumstances differed, such as different parents and a childhood in the citadel's library, clarifying an earlier remark about him. Grounds this in the guild's practice of taking small orphans and the idea that a torturer apprentice could not also be taken by the librarians.
  • 2008-10-02 food in evil guest very minor spoilerAnswers Marc Aramini's question about the 'green goddess' in An Evil Guest by identifying it as a salad dressing, quoting the restaurant exchange between Reis and Cassie. Explains Cassie's reduced weight by her months on an island with little food, and asks whether Aramini meant something else.
  • 2008-10-01 a lurker asks a questionArgues that Severian is an intellectual who does not recognize himself as one, using Ultan's remark that he might have been a philosopher and the Cyby selection scene to suggest an alternate life as the librarian's apprentice. Grounds the reading in the text's passages on Severian's self-image as dense and his hesitation before Vodalus's pledge, citing Gwern's report of a Wolfe interview.
  • 2008-09-24 An Evil Guest - Big Questions, Major SPOILERSAsks whether Gideon engineered the final events of An Evil Guest, including Bill Reis's death, Cassie's rescue by bats, and the storm, or whether those events happened independently. Lays out the alternatives: Gideon already had his information and the rest was chance, or love redeemed Reis, or Gideon used Cassie for revenge.
  • 2007-07-25 Why did Vodalus risk...?Argues that Vodalus joins the grave robbery in the necropolis from character motives: he leads by sharing his men's risks, and his pride makes him underrate the watchmen's danger. Grounds this in the opening chapter of The Shadow of the Torturer, where Vodalus tells Thea that declining risks would look bad to the others, and in his giving up the gun and being saved by Severian, who is repaid with a false coin.
  • 2007-07-23 The Morality of the HierogrammatesArgues that the precipitous flood accompanying the New Sun has a stated physical cause, the gravitational forces of the New Sun, as Baldanders explains. Replies to Paul B.'s point about sudden tectonic shifts by quoting Baldanders in The Urth of the New Sun, chapter XLII, to clear up the supposed confusion.
  • 2007-04-03 book of the new sun questionsArgues that the Hierodules' overarching goal in The Book of the New Sun is to create better intelligences, balancing chaos and order against entropy rather than pursuing power. Grounds this in Severian's conversation with Apheta about a war against entropy, and in the Hierodules' treatment of Baldanders and Severian, while rejecting Typhon's and Baldanders' paths as undesirable.
  • 2007-04-03 book of the new sun questionsAsks a series of questions about the Book of the New Sun's endgame: whether the epitome must be an autarch, whether the autarchy was built by hierodules to stagnate the Commonwealth, and why a New Sun would change humanity's path. Also questions why hierodules would support Baldanders' knowledge-sharing if the autarchy is their project.
  • 2006-09-10 Dr. TalosArgues that Dr. Talos was made by Baldanders as his opposite, citing Talos's own remark about being all Baldanders is not, and reads Talos as artist and destroyer against Baldanders as scientist and creator. Grounds this in Talos's play interruptions, his creation and destruction of Jolenta, and his killing of flowers, and argues Baldanders needs Talos to keep him in check rather than Talos mastering him.
  • 2006-09-10 Close Reading: Torturer Chapter II: SeverianArgues that the symbols on Severian's tomb (the fountain, ship volant and rose) shape him more than Vodalus does, so Wolfe's symbol speech is a rule the reader must apply. Grounds this in the symbol passage, Severian's obsessions with time stopping and the life-giving light, and his focus on the coin Vodalus gave him.
