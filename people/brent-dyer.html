Their messages (6 with summaries)

  • 2007-09-18 Long Review Essay on Wizard KnightArgues that the honor, courage and fidelity Wolfe praises in a Gaiman interview remain valid values, and that Hemingway's quoted line condemns the misuse of such words in war rather than the words themselves. Responds to Stephen Frug's claim that the idealized medieval knightly ideal in The Wizard Knight is dangerous, citing the Hemingway passage and the Iraq war as examples of those words used for propaganda.
  • 2007-09-18 Long Review Essay on Wizard KnightArgues that Wolfe's moral outcomes are ambivalent, since bad behavior often goes unpunished in The Wizard Knight, reflecting real-life ethics. Defends Abel's acceptance of a hierarchical society by comparing it to American teenagers' lives, responding to Stephen Frug's claim that Abel's modern teenage attitudes clash with the setting.
  • 2006-10-31 The littlest apprenticesArgues that Roy Lackey's off-site nursery explanation, which reconciles Severian's two statements about children, does not fit the text, since Severian's earliest memory is of stacking pebbles in a Tower courtyard. Contends the inconsistency about breastfeeding and the taken children is deliberate, meant to make readers question Severian's motives, and cites the two mentions of nursing and the cut-off for older boys.
  • 2006-10-31 The littlest apprenticesArgues that the wet-nurse passage contradicts Severian's nursing memory, so the memory cannot be confirmed as his mother's, and that Severian lies about wet nurses to sanitize the Guild. Builds the case on the gray metal Tower walls, the forced caesareans, and the claim that no free women lived in the Tower except Guild clients.
  • 2006-10-30 Were Jolenta and Dorcas lovers?Questions whether Jolenta and Dorcas were really lovers, arguing that Wolfe's hints of lesbianism come earlier in the text and that Severian's sudden claim about the women may reflect something he cannot accept. Grounds this in the scene where Severian and Cyriaca are joined at the fountain by a pair of women, and in the timing of when the hint about Jolenta and Dorcas appears.
  • 2006-10-30 Were Jolenta and Dorcas lovers?Asks whether Dorcas and Jolenta had a sexual relationship, questioning a passage in The Sword of the Lictor where Severian claims Dorcas loved Jolenta and that he felt jealousy. Offers three theories for why Severian would imply this, including that it justifies his own behavior, that he cannot grasp filial devotion, and that Dorcas's choice at the Piteous Gate prompted it.
