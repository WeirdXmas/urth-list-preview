  • 2011-05-01 "Realistic fiction leaves out too much." - Gene WolfeArgues that Jeff Wilson's question about whether the wrongness of purposeless mistreatment of an innocent is meaningful is a real ethical issue, since moral wrong and justification are established topics in ethics. Responds to Wilson's comparison with Jackson's "The Lottery" by framing Le Guin's "The Ones Who Walk Away from Omelas" as a utilitarian society whose principles can be criticised.
  • 2011-05-01 "Realistic fiction leaves out too much." - Gene WolfeArgues that Le Guin's story 'The Ones Who Walk Away from Omelas' makes a clear moral point: even a huge social gain does not justify deliberately mistreating and depriving an innocent. Responds to Jeff Wilson's dismissive reading, which saw the story's prosperous society as having only a minor externality, by insisting the story's moral statement is undeniable whatever one thinks of it.
  • 2011-04-16 Introduction and BreathArgues that Severian is an unreliable narrator not because his memory is faulty but because he lacks perfect understanding, and that he admits to leaving out unimportant details. Grounds this in his inconsistent accounts of Thecla's death and shifting descriptions of their relationship as sexual or platonic, while noting Wolfe rarely spells things out. Responds to Daniel Petersen's quoted remark questioning the unreliable-narrator reading.
