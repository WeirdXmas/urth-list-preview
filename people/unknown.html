Their messages (7 with summaries)

  • 2023-12-15 (no subject)Argues that Babbie, the two-horned beast in Return to the Whorl, is the spirit of Horn, so that Horn and Silk are the same being and the narrator of On Blue's Waters is Silk. Grounds this in matching prophecies from Marble, the spirit-as-wind idea from Mamelta, Babbie's embrace of Hoof, and the claim that Horn left Silk's body under a great tree.
  • 2023-12-15 (no subject)Argues that John Crowley's fiction, including Aegypt, lacks the spiritual profundity and transcendence that Wolfe's books offer the poster, noting Engine Summer and Little Big as the nearest exceptions. Responds to David Stockhoff's remarks on Little Big's repetition and lack of resolution by contrasting Crowley's cleverness and metafiction with Wolfe's spiritual longing.
  • 2023-12-15 (no subject)Suggests that a passage on plant-animal hybridization may mean someone took a mandrake root and humanized it into a homunculus. Notes that the full passage holds further points of interest and that more could be said, but offers no textual quotation or evidence beyond the passage itself.
  • 2023-12-15 (no subject)Proposes that Severian's descents are symbolic time travels to civilization's low point, where he finds lasting worth such as love in the Oubliette atrium and the brown book at Nessus. Tentatively brainstorms, conceding the vertical metaphor may not fit, and asks which other descents, such as Yesod, Saltus, or the House Absolute, belong.
  • 2023-12-15 (no subject)Quotes a passage describing the Etruscan fulgurator priests who read lightning bolts as divine messages and performed purification rites at strike sites. Shares this as an informational item with no direct comment on Wolfe's books, so it offers no reading of the fiction.

  • 2023-12-15 (no subject)Quotes Wolfe's interview explaining that the Ascians were modeled on Turks and Byzantium, that the setting is South America, and that the Ascian name derives from the Classical word for people without shadows. The poster accepts the South America setting and argues that Urth's culture is alien to contemporary South America because of the long time span, citing Dan'l's point and the drinking of mate.
