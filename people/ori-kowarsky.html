  • 2006-07-27 "V.R.T." - April 24Disputes the claim that the aborigines of V.R.T. were real shapeshifters, arguing that the evidence is only circumstantial and that interviewees in the Sisterworlds are confused about the abos. Points to the Shadow Child's hallucinatory metamorphoses in "A Story" as unlike what the abos are otherwise described as capable of, and challenges the engineer-narrator's logic.
  • 2006-07-27 "V.R.T." - April 24Argues that the claim that the aborigines are shapechangers in V.R.T. rests only on hearsay, so readers should not treat Victor's mother's transformation or Cassilla's looking older as established. Supports this with physics (worm mass), the inconsistency of tool use and net-weaving, and the idea that the abo dehumanizing meme may be a political device. Replies to Tony and Daniel's objections, and says Marsch's identity with Victor is unprovable in either direction.
  • 2006-07-25 "V.R.T." - April 24Proposes that the prisoner in cell 143 of V.R.T. is John Marsch, who undergoes a double-blending of identity with the abo Victor after a bite from a Shadow child. Grounds the claim in the Shadow child's bite in "A Story", the Eastwind name link, and Marsch's entries from April 24 onward, arguing that Victor died and Marsch came to believe he was Victor.

  • 1999-11-23 MessianicQuestions Nutria's claim that the Gospel survives in garbled form in The Book of the New Sun and the Long Sun, challenging the idea that the pre-Christian world was ruled by empires.   • 1999-11-22 mankindArgues against reading The Book of the New Sun as Wolfe's own message, urging readers to take Severian's narration on its own terms and dig past the Catholic allusions. Responds to Ron Hale-Evans's claim that Wolfe is the God of Yesod and Briah and that the Increate is his puppet, and his point about accepting the book's premises. Uses the Foundation trilogy deconstruction analogy and asks about the masks of the hierodules.
  • 1999-11-20 MessianicArgues that the New Sun project of Severian and the Briah crew, with its replacement of humanity by the Green Man, cannot be justified as redemption and resembles Ushas's destruction. Replies to Alex by rejecting his reading that the text presents this as a trick, and presses that the novel's rationalist explanations leave providence unproven as theodicy.
  • 1999-11-20 MessianicArgues that the miracles in The Book of the New Sun, including the water-to-wine change, are technological tricks by the Hierodule's masters rather than evidence of divinity, and that Severian's feelings about the Increate are subjective. Responds to Alex David Groce's claim that Wolfe's text supports a theological reading, by weighing each category of divine intrusion one by one.
  • 1999-11-20 Ori hammer and tongsConcedes a point that Ori Kowarsky's hammer-and-tongs approach to the Hierogrammates may be too blunt, suggesting that Catholic readers feel a bridge to the book that non-Catholics lack. Argues that the book works equally as a critique of a holy institution committing worldly crimes and as a defense of it, depending on the reader's view of material versus metaphysical guilt.
  • 1999-11-19 MessianicArgues that Wolfe's Book of the New Sun fails to show God working through suffering, since the text lacks any link between Urth's sufferings and divine power, and that the failure produces a work about the opposite. Responds to Ron Hale-Evans by rejecting reliance on Wolfe's interviews as proof, and points to Urth of the New Sun's hints and the mirror imagery as evidence.
  • 1999-11-19 MessianicArgues that the powers behind the Whorl in The Citadel of the Autarch are not divine, citing the chapter 34 passage on engineered beings, intra-dimensional travel and breeding humans as revenge. Grounds this in the plain text and in the weakness of the solution to the dying sun, which is mass slaughter by advanced technology, and mocks the Pancreator's apologists.
  • 1999-11-08 Severian's liesProposes that Severian is a liar only if he fabricates his mystical experiences or falsely claims divine ordination for changes the alien hierodules actually control. Grounds this in a view that his raising of the dead and altering of the Sun were replicable by the hierodules, and answers earlier lists of inconsistencies by asking whether he believes in his own power.

  • 1999-04-29 Are TBOTNS and UOTNS Christian Texts?Argues that Christian elements in The Book of the New Sun and The Urth of the New Sun may be only a half-remembered cultural substrate rather than real miracles, and rejects reading Severian's story as a Christian allegory. Responds to Alex David Groce's claim that a materialist reading ignores clear Christian references, pressing him on the undines, the Hieros, Silk, and the surviving population.
  • 1999-04-28 The Urth-Saint Croix Connection Further RefinedProposes further hypotheses extending the author's Grand Unified Severian Theory: that Tzad shape-shifts through self-directed Lamarckian evolution, and that Severian's abo-modified genes drive Green Men toward becoming Hierogrammates.   • 1999-04-28 Are TBOTNS and UOTNS Christian Texts?Argues that the Christian imagery in The Book of the New Sun and The Urth of the New Sun cannot be squared with Christian prophecy, since Severian's universe lacks Christ and humanity seems to evolve beyond redemption. Builds the case on Tzad's comment about a vanished creation and the Key to the Universe passage in The Citadel, and endorses a materialist, text-based reading over Catholic readings by Gevers and Groce.
  • 1999-04-24 Deluge, Hierodules, MaterialismArgues against alga and Alex Groce that The Book of the New Sun does not favour non-material or Christian readings, citing Terminus Est, the Pelerines' tent, Malrubius and the Hieros as materialist explanations. Responds to alga's and Alex Groce's claims that Wolfe's fiction tilts toward non-material explanations, and to alga's point about Tzadkiel, offering a reasoned counter-example.
  • 1999-04-22 Speak, MemoryArgues that Severian's resurrection power rests on his biology rather than his memory, proposing that he is a genetically engineered clone of Number Five with alzabo and abo tissue, and that Erebus and Baldanders are his siblings. Builds the case from Severian's memory, the Hierodules' aquastor machine, the shared dream with Baldanders, and Jonas's account of the woman with black beans in Shadow.
  • 1999-04-22 Deluge, Hierodules, MaterialismArgues that Ori Kowarsky reads The Book of the New Sun and The Urth of the New Sun from a materialist standpoint, holding that religious elements like Tzadkiel should have scientific rationales as Terminus Est does. Responds to Christopher R. Culver's quotation about Tzadkiel as a judge figure analogous to Michael, denying any connection and calling his angelic appearance a deliberate effect staged for Severian.
  • 1999-04-21 Father Inire and the DelugeArgues that the Hierodules and their masters in The Book of the New Sun are material, fallible, alien manipulators rather than angels, rejecting the Briah-aligned religious reading of the Deluge. Grounds this in Melito's story "The Cock, the Angel and the Eagle" from Citadel, quoting the Angel's admission that God's will is unknowable and Severian's line about humanity's unforgiveable sin, and questions whether Severian's choice was right.
