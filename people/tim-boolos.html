  • 2001-02-05 RTTW Questions (Spoilers- No Spoiler Space)Asks a series of questions about the ending of Return to the Whorl, including why Silk possessed Pig, whether Patera Pike and Dr. Crane were ghosts, and where the Whorl is going.   • 2000-09-05 Horn and Sinew's enmity (part 1)Argues that Horn's hostility toward Sinew in The Book of the Short Sun reflects the difficulties of the merged Horn/Silk identity, and that the narrator's purpose in writing changes across On Blue's Waters and In Green's Jungles. Supports this by pointing to Silk's disappointment over Horn's treatment of Sinew, and by comparing the shift to Latro's undisclosed aims in Soldier of Arete revealed by Pindaros.
  • 2000-08-22 Neighbor + Inhumu + One?Corrects Alice Turner's claim that Origen's heresy concerned repentant demons, arguing his condemnation involved the pre-existence of souls and cosmic recycling instead. Compares the Inhumu's wish to become real and their harmlessness in On Blue's Waters to the wistful passages in Lewis's Screwtape Letters, and notes the poster's Eastern Orthodox ordination and wish that the list not be exclusively theological.
  • 2000-08-08 IGJ, Major Spoiler re SilkArgues that Silk was on the brink of suicide beside Hyacinth's coffin but did not actually kill himself, and that the blood came from a sacrifice like Orpine's. Supports Michael Andre-Driussi's suicide reading while offering an alternative, proposing a single transference between two despondent spirits followed by Silk's body being taken by Hari Mau.
  • 2000-08-02 *De-Lurk* with some Theories/QuestionsProposes two theories about The Book of the Long Sun and Short Sun: that the cryptic passage near the end of On Blue's Waters is a forest god of the neighbors that comforts Horn as the mother comforted Seawrack, and that Horn's spirit was loaded into Silk's body at Hyacinth's death, so the narrator of On Blue's Waters and In Green's Jungles is a fused Horn/Silk writing therapeutically. Grounds both on clues about the Hyacinth funeral scene and on the shift in narrative between the two books.
