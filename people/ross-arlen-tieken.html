Their messages (9 with summaries)

  • 2013-04-11 Thecla's "Identity"Disputes Lee Berman's citation of Marvin Harris's Pigs, Cows, Wars and Witches on pagan witch cults, arguing that Harris's economic explanation of weird beliefs is discredited and unhistorical. Notes that Harris's witch-trial examples are historically baseless and that Rice Religious Studies scholars reject his approach, then praises the list's friendly scholarly community.
  • 2013-04-11 Thecla's "Identity"Argues that the popular myth of Christianity violently destroying Germanic paganism is a Nazi-era invention, and that conversion was driven by economics, politics and cultural crisis. Responds to Ross Arlen Tieken's remarks on Wolfe's Catholicism and vegetarianism by recommending a book on the Germanization of medieval Christianity, with no direct reference to Wolfe's fiction.
  • 2013-04-10 Thecla's "Identity"Argues that Wolfe is not a traditional Catholic because he believes other gods exist, and that vegetarianism in his work marks sensitive, sacrificial figures rather than a general ideal. Grounds this in Wolfe's Texas background and the poster's fieldwork with English Neo-Pagans, distinguishing Golden Dawn ceremonial magic and Heathenry from Catholic folk practice. Responds to Lee Berman's remarks on the Eucharist, vegetarianism and Wolfe's dissatisfaction with transformation.
  • 2013-04-08 Thecla's "Identity"Says the poster does not yet know what to make of the Eucharist on Blue scene in Silk and Horn that António Pedro Marques called enigmatic, and is rereading the books to try to find an explanation. Replies to Marques's quoted list of unanswered questions about the scene's motive, timing, results and symbolism, offering no interpretation of its own.
  • 2013-04-08 Thecla's "Identity"Thanks Dan'l Danehy-Oakes for supplying the Latin of a Vulgate phrase, explaining that he had been translating backwards from the English because he lacks a copy of the Vulgate. Responds to Danehy-Oakes's remark that the feast in the forest perverts the Eucharist and that Severian purifies it, so Thecla is resurrected in him.
  • 2013-04-08 Thecla's "Identity"Agrees with Dan'l Danehy-Oakes that the forest feast perverts the Eucharist, which Severian purifies, and that Thecla seems resurrected in him. Argues that the reference to Jesus' words in John remains a valid direct allusion despite Dan'l's objection to it.
  • 2013-04-08 Thecla's "Identity"Argues that Severian's taking of Thecla's and the Autarch's bodies, and Horn/Silk's sacrifice at the altar in On Blue's Waters, enact Christian Eucharistic worship as described by Odo Casel and Gregory Dix. Responds to António Pedro Marques's query about Evola by rejecting Evola as a model and offering the Casel reading as grounds, with Severian as a member of Christ's mystical body rather than an allegorical Christ.
  • 2013-04-05 Thecla's "Identity"Argues that Wolfe shares the mystical, neo-feudal, myth-making Catholic preoccupations of modernists like Eliot, Pound and Tolkien, and defends distributism as the Catholic economic vision. Grounds this in a Tolkien essay on the Dark Ages, a Chestertonian defense of the Middle Ages, and a claim that Wolfe is a Catholic in good standing.
  • 2013-04-05 Thecla's "Identity"Argues that New Criticism suits Wolfe because he is a modernist rather than postmodernist writer, inspired by Catholic Modernist-Traditionalist authors such as Tolkien, Eliot and Pound. Supports Marc Aramini's claim that meaning is reconstruction rather than deconstruction, citing the New Critics' aim to analyze such writing and noting that modernism differs from postmodernism.
