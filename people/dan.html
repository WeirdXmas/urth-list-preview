  • 2008-03-13 Urth Digest, Vol 43, Issue 2Argues that Lesage is a time traveler who knows events in advance, and that he is Chris's son who returns to ensure his own birth, killing Valentin and betraying everyone at the end. Grounds this in Lesage's uncanny timing, the buccaneers' laughter at Chris's question, Chris's wariness, and the visitor who receives Chris's manuscript at the book's start.
  • 2008-02-13 CapriconQuestions why Gene Wolfe is absent from the Capricon XXVIII guest listings despite the convention's pirate theme and his awards, and asks whether it is oversight or snubbery. Responds to a quoted post by tgoulding05 about Wolfe's omission, and suggests a list member ask Wolfe whether Lesage is Chris's son, referring to Pirate Freedom.
  • 2008-01-05 Urth Digest, Vol 41, Issue 5Argues that Novia could avoid rape on the pirate ships because any attempt would cost the attacker his life, and that Chris's killing of Michet would deter others. Responds to Matthew Keeley's question about why Novia is not raped more often, and suggests her journey is more problematic and her story doubtful, perhaps a disguised prostitute situation.
  • 2008-01-02 Some Pirate Freedom thoughts and questionsArgues that Chris's biography in Pirate Freedom implies unrecorded history, perhaps leaving room for a sequel, and that Ignacio and Novia would never admit to piracy while living in Veracruz. Responds to Matthew Keeley's question about how the biographer knew Chris vanished while sailing alone, reasoning from Chris's stated revenge motive and the legal danger of piracy.
  • 2007-12-22 Typhon and the WhorlConcedes that the earlier message should have said Hierogrammates rather than Hieros, and clarifies that he meant the humans with faster-than-light capability. Reacts to the quoted account of Yesod, Briah and the Hierogrammates by calling the explanation simple and less satisfying than expected.
  • 2007-12-21 Typhon & the WhorlArgues that Typhon hijacked the Whorl as a living museum preserving Earth's cultural diversity, not as a personal monument or lifeboat, and proposes that Abaia and the creatures were part of a family plot against Typhon transcribed into the Whorl. Grounds this on the chem stockpile timing and the rapid decline, plus a recalled Citadel passage about procuring technology from other worlds and the Hieros' reluctance to share it.
  • 2007-12-18 Typhon & the Whorl (was Prior Race On Blue And Green)Argues that Typhon hijacked the Whorl late in his decline, after its construction was nearly complete, and that the Pas cult and its digital pantheon were not part of the ship's original design. Supports this with the one-headed mountain monument versus the two-headed Pas depictions, the timing of his decline after the solar cooling disaster, and the damage the pantheon's infighting caused aboard ship.
  • 2007-12-17 Typhon & the Whorl (was Prior Race On Blue And Green)Argues that Typhon copied himself into the Whorl's mainframe to hijack the ship, planning to return to Earth, recolonize it with worshipping peons, and restore his empire. Grounds this in Typhon's arrogance during his talk with Severian, his drives for survival and power, and the idea that the Whorl was not built as his personal vessel.
  • 2007-12-04 Some Pirate Freedom thoughts and questionsJokingly argues that reading Wolfe makes the poster feel uneducated, and that hunting for hidden connections helps, while claiming Wolfe deliberately deflects questions about his tricks like a magician. Responds to thalassocrat's remark about Wiseguy, Lesage and Chris in Pirate Freedom, where thalassocrat reported Wolfe's answer at a convention. The post does not add new textual evidence.
  • 2005-08-19 urth-urth.net Digest, Vol 12, Issue 18Agrees that the dangerous crossing to Blue would have been worth it even before humans arrived, given the massive predators on Green, and asks whether the lander belonged to Pajarocu Auk or was the one Horn ended up in on Green. Responds to a poster's remarks on the Crossing and on Jahlee's childhood, questioning which lander belongs to whom in the Long Sun books.
  • 2005-07-05 TyphonRecalls that Typhon once said Piaton's head could be removed after his own took over the body's functions, and suggests Typhon was deposed before that happened.   • 2005-06-21 urth-urth.net Digest, Vol 10, Issue 21Proposes that Severian's pale complexion comes from his long confinement in Matachin Tower, where he rarely went outside, rather than from any other cause. Responds to a quoted thread on Severian's skin colour that cites Wolfe's own Thrust interview description, offering a simple everyday explanation instead.

  • 2005-06-16 gene wolfe and cyberpunkArgues that Gene Wolfe should not be pigeonholed as a cyberpunk author, though some of his stories may carry a cyberpunk bent, and that cyberpunk's roots lie in 1970s short fiction. Notes Blade Runner's influence on anime, Wolfe's reading of Philip K. Dick, and the PKD flavor in Free Live Free and There Are Doors.
  • 2005-06-16 "There Are Doors"Replies to Dan's question about whether Klamm and Green in There Are Doors are the same man at different times, a suggestion drawn from Klamm calling Green Herr K and apologizing for it. Compares this to the older-self moment in 2001 and asks why Klamm never warns Green off or why Green fails to recognize Klamm's identity.
  • 2005-06-02 "There Are Doors" Comes To JapanAsks whether Mr. Klamm and Mr. Green in There Are Doors are the same man at two different points in time or two separate men in the same circumstances, one beginning his pursuit and the other nearing its end. Also remarks that the novel is the best Philip K. Dick-style book not written by Dick, a passing comparison with other writers.
