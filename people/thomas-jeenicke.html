Their messages (4 with summaries)

  • 2000-03-29 The Beautiful Ones, They Allways Hurt UArgues that the Beautiful Ones of Sidhe rule through deception and manipulation, that their ban on human reproduction is enforced by kidnapping newborns who are likely killed or enslaved, and that a surviving child could become a symbol that the humans' situation can be changed. Responds to a post questioning the galactic authority's passivity by citing the novel's passages about poor visitors, departure rights, and the animals' show.
  • 2000-03-15 The two TyphonsArgues that Typhon and his followers came from another planet in the same solar system rather than from Urth, and that his revival by head transplant or digitization are alternative immortality attempts. Responds to Nicholas Gevers's earlier reply, and doubts Wolfe intended a single connected series across the New Sun, Long Sun and Short Sun books, asking whether Wolfe ever said so in an interview.
  • 2000-02-24 Calvino, Wolfe and languagesArgues that the fragments in Calvino's If on a Winter's Night a Traveler are themes rather than genres, and that Wolfe is better characterized by recurring themes like memory or rebirth than by genre. Asks whether Calvino influenced Wolfe, noting that the answer depends on English translation dates and whether Wolfe knows Italian or other languages.
  • 2000-02-22 Wolfe and CalvinoArgues that Wolfe probably did not draw on Italo Calvino, finding little evidence and saying memory is not a main Calvino theme. Suggests Calvino's labyrinthine novel If on a winter's night a traveler, ending in a library, is the nearest parallel, and asks whether Wolfe is a postmodern writer.
