  • 2004-12-01 genderlessnessArgues that Dorcas in The Book of the New Sun is not a standard figure, citing the incestuous undertones of her relationship with Severian, whom she may be ancestor to and who may be her lover's father via resurrection. Responds to emily zilch's genderlessness thread, suggesting the hierogrammates' gender complexities are disguised as genre conventions, while tentatively hedging the claim.
  • 2004-12-01 One Good BookArgues that Christianity is a historical phenomenon and that transcendence cannot be known, then reads The Book of the New Sun as a gnostic ascent to salvation through knowledge, with The Book of the Long Sun and The Book of the Short Sun as epicycles around it. Responds to turin's earlier comments, and also ranks Wolfe against Joyce, Chesterton, Tolkien, Lewis and Borges while asking whether anyone has read Borski's book.
  • 2004-11-30 Wolfe and CatholicismArgues that Wolfe builds on Tolkien rather than mirroring him, with an evolutionary path toward blessedness in The Book of the New Sun and Urth of the New Sun that parallels gnostic ascent. Contends Wolfe is better called a Catholic than a Christian writer, and that the books' evolutionary dynamic embodies apocatastasis, the restoration of all souls including the damned and Satan.
  • 2004-11-30 the problem with gaiman, mieville, and pullmanArgues that Gaiman, Mieville and Pullman are locked in a Bloomian agon with Tolkien and Lewis, and that Wolfe displaces his precursors more fully than these writers can. Grounds the claim in Freudian paternal rivalry and in Wolfe's admiration for his precursor, and ranks Pullman above the others.
