Their messages (17 with summaries)

  • 1997-03-14 Doors and SunsReflects on Gene Wolfe's labyrinthine style, arguing that There Are Doors and the Long Sun books, especially Nightside, give readers pauses of lucid prose amid dense puzzles. Praises Wolfe as a tale-teller with a traditional, Chestertonian sentence style and cites specific passages like the crate opening and Viron marketplace.
  • 1997-02-24 Mind stuffProposes a taxonomy of the kinds of intelligence in the Whorl, from ordinary humans and enhanced humans to animals, inhumi, chems and electronic entities, using Horn, Silk, Oreb, Quetzal and Hammerstone as examples. Grounds the scheme in the Long Sun books, arguing that the story's central mystery is whether the Outsider is real and whether there is a divine Mind behind the suffering.
  • 1997-02-14 Finding the Wolf(e)Describes the poster's own path into Wolfe's fiction, starting with The Fifth Head of Cerberus and moving through the New Sun, the Latro books and the Long Sun series, and praises Exodus for delivering on its setup. Responds to the thread's literary tangent by listing favorite authors and noting enjoyment of sf page-turners alongside Wolfe's deeper complexity.
  • 1997-02-13 groping in the darkArgues that Wolfe's reality-probing ambiguity and carefully puzzled plots deserve more critical respect, placing him beside Nabokov, Borges, Calvino and Barthelme as a modern stylist rather than an SF writer. Grounds this in the Long Sun opening, where Silk's advance on Blood's villa is detailed and absorbing, and in readers quitting The Book of the Long Sun after a third.
  • 1997-02-11 groping in the darkArgues that Wolfe's characters begin in darkness and grope toward ambiguous knowledge, citing Silk's idolatry in the Long Sun books and Silk's misunderstandings. Builds on Jim Jordan's and Dave Lebling's comments by listing examples from "The Devil in a Forest," Latro, Green, Severian and The Fifth Head of Cerberus, and compares the theme to Nabokov's Invitation to a Beheading.
  • 1997-02-04 Long Sun timeline -- and distractionsThanks Ranjit for a timeline of the Long Sun books and says the poster will save it as a reference and study it carefully. The post contains no argument about the chronology itself, only a note of appreciation with an automated-style sign-off line.
  • 1997-02-04 Hyacinth; Kypris; Pas and moreAgrees with Dave Lebling on several Long Sun points: Silk's biological parents, the Whorl's long stay in the Blue-Green system, and Pike's ghost being Quetzal. Also praises the books on rereading, and suggests Kypris possessed Hyacinth through the glass at Blood's villa, citing his memory of Silk's conversation with Kypris.
  • 1997-01-28 Quetzal at Silk's deathAsks why Quetzal, who is present when Silk wakes after being buried, tells Silk he was thought dead while Chenille later says they knew he was alive, and whether Quetzal healed Silk with some curative substance. Grounds the question in close quotations from Calde of the Long Sun about Silk's vision, the pain in his neck, and the contradictory accounts.
  • 1997-01-25 Patera Pike's GhostAgrees with David Lebling that the figure seen after Pike's vision, flitting out the window, is probably the shape-shifting Quetzal rather than Oreb. Argues Silk's musings about ghosts and devils should not be taken seriously this early in Long Sun, and previews a later question about Quetzal's presence at Silk's near-burial.
  • 1997-01-22 Silk's standing orders?Proposes that Silk has special 'standing orders' from his frozen-embryo origin, giving him innate military skill such as expert dueling and airship flying. Grounds the idea in Hammerstone's remark about soldiers' orders in 'Lake', Silk's unschooled duel with Xiphias, the Mamelta and Mucor cases, and Silk's reflection on Sand as a cognate of himself in 'Calde'.
  • 1997-01-18 Re Typhon and fictional crosslinksArgues that Wolfe should avoid linking his own novels, so that Silk's Long Sun tale and Severian's Book of the New Sun stay independent, though resemblances like the twin planets in The Fifth Head of Cerberus are acceptable. Responds to RevJack's noting of the name Typhon, citing Cordwainer Smith's shared-universe stories as a contrast to Wolfe's approach.
  • 1997-01-17 InhumiAsks about the inhumi in the Long Sun books, tracing Quetzal's attacks on Teasel and Mattak and noting Patera Pike's remark that devils infiltrated the Whorl. Also reports that the introductory list of gods, persons and animals in Exodus hints that Horn wrote the Book of Silk, which is the Long Sun book itself.
  • 1997-01-12 the mystery of QArgues that the sudden first-person I at the end of chapter 13 of Exodus from the Long Sun reveals the boy Horn as narrator, reframing the earlier third-person telling. Responds to James Stewart by quoting the line from page 282 and noting that some readers may find the shift gimmicky.
  • 1997-01-12 the mystery of QProposes that Quetzal is not a human being, citing Mucor's 'the man who isn't there' and Oreb's 'Bad thing' as clues that Silk misreads. Echoes Patrick's remarks on buried revelations and notes the name's links to Quetzalcoatl and to the Greek root of 'trogon', meaning gnaw.
  • 1997-01-11 More Whorl MysteriesArgues that the political intrigue and war in The Book of the Long Sun amounts to futility, so that Silk's despair on the airship points to the pointlessness of history set against the personal quest for meaning. Responds to Patrick O'Leary's remarks, quoting his question and adding Hyacinth's possible possession by Kypris and Maytera Marble's lie about her past as personal sorrows.
  • 1997-01-05 more on the airshipAgrees with Rich Skrenta that Mucor's possession of Horn upset Horn's balance, so Silk was not really attempting suicide, and entertains Peter Cash's idea that Hyacinth may have been a Trivagaunti spy. Builds on both readers' suggestions to interpret the top-of-the-airship conversation between Silk and Horn in Exodus from the Long Sun, and invites further views.
  • 1996-12-30 on the airshipAsks why Silk is depressed in the airship-top conversation with Horn, whether Horn fell and Mucor saved him, and what Hycanith's apparent falsity implies. Proposes that Silk suspects corruption in Pas's plan and has despaired of the Whorl gods while keeping faith in the Outsider. Newcomer to the list seeking help after finishing Exodus.
