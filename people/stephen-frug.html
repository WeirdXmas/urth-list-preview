  • 2007-09-18 Long Review Essay on Wizard KnightExplains why the poster finds the knightly ideal troubling in The Wizard Knight, arguing it is hierarchical, rests on personal fidelity over institutions, and legitimizes violence. Grounds this in modern liberal values and a claim that the knightly ideal is conservative and historically tied to the antebellum South's slaveholding aristocracy.
  • 2007-09-18 Long Review Essay on Wizard KnightArgues that the knightly ideal of honor, courage and fidelity, which Wolfe invokes in The Wizard Knight, is troubling and dangerous in today's world, while other forms of those virtues remain valid. Clarifies his earlier Hemingway citation by tying Wolfe's Gaiman-interview phrase to the same idealized medieval code that the Hemingway quote points to.
  • 2007-09-18 Long Review Essay on Wizard KnightArgues that critics should not dismiss criticisms of The Wizard Knight merely because Wolfe wrote it, and that a critic's prima facie case shifts the burden of proof onto defenders. Responds to unnamed defenders who invoked Wolfe's unreliable narrators, citing the poster's own review and the contrast with The Book of the New Sun and The Fifth Head of Cerberus.
  • 2007-09-18 Long Review Essay on Wizard KnightArgues that The Wizard Knight is good but not among Wolfe's best, faulting its slow first half, uneven pacing and rushed ending, and tracing these to Wolfe's habit of indirection and weak adventure writing. Opens a long blog review essay, cross-posted for responses, citing Clute's remark that Wolfe never tells a straightforward tale and the praise on the book's covers.
