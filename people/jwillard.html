  • 2008-01-25 Graves etc.Argues that Wolfe's work invites source-hunting, citing the Kafkaesque torture device in The Book of the New Sun and the blind librarian Ultan as echoing Borges. Responds to Marc Aramini's claim that Wolfe would not benefit from rewriting Graves, Nabokov and others, by contending that resisting such explorations treats stories as sealed units, and ends by asking to be removed from the list.
  • 2007-06-14 A QuestionRecommends Seven American Nights as a starting point for newcomers to Wolfe, arguing its short length and lingering questions make it a good introduction. Responds to Stuart Hamm's request for a first Wolfe book to give friends, and suggests a second Island story as another option.
  • 2007-06-07 What the elm?Argues that Wolfe's allusions go beyond homage, proposing that Virgil, Dante, Augustine and Nabokov are important influences on The Book of the New Sun. Points to Charon and Nessus in the Inferno and quotes a Nabokov passage on involution and hidden plots as sounding like Wolfe.
  • 2007-06-01 The Distant Suns of Gene WolfeArgues that the editor's remark that Wolfe would struggle to find a publisher today is not a sign of partisan bias, since both political ends would reject difficult, strange religious fiction. Responds to JWillard's claim that a liberal New York publisher is to blame, saying that dumbing down of publishing and the search for easy sales explain the rejection instead.
  • 2006-09-17 RocheProposes that Roche is a spy serving higher manipulative forces, tracing his active role through Severian's revival, the House Azure transaction with Thecla, and the chrisos incantation. Grounds the theory in close readings of several chapters and in the saint's arrest and the Autarch's remark on Roche's red hair, and asks whether binary-star imagery fits.
  • 2006-09-10 Close Reading: Torturer Chapter II: SeverianWorks through numbered paragraphs of The Shadow of the Torturer chapter II, raising questions about the cypress and resurrection symbolism, the Hercules cult, Eata's hints, the spoon, hair imagery, and the boatman's tar-stained clothes. Ties these to Graves's The White Goddess quotation and to earlier passages such as the Sand Garden and the all-women guild remark, asking whether each is a pointer or a casual detail.
  • 2006-09-06 Close Reading: Torturer Chapter IReplies to a proposal for collecting chapter observations on The Shadow of the Torturer, then reviews several readers' remarks on the gate as the symbol of exile and on Vodalus's commands. Argues that Severian's words like 'perhaps' and 'seem' are unreliable, and that his allegiance to Vodalus is an affectation.
