  • 2007-12-18 The Tomb of the Unknown SeverianProposes that the tomb in Citadel XXXVIII could have been built by an original Severian who failed the test in Urth of the New Sun, went back in time, and altered events so he later succeeded. Grounds this in Severian's remarks at the end of Urth of the New Sun, including his rescue as a boy from the river by Juturna, and answers Tony Ellis's question about which Severian the tomb was built for.

  • 2007-06-28 New Wolfe Story at Subterranean PressAdmits misreading a passage in the new Subterranean Press story and explains why he first took the dog Rover to be dead, citing 'lying quietly by the side of the house, half hidden by shrubbery' and the 'God forgive me!' line.   • 2007-06-26 New Wolfe Story at Subterranean PressProposes that Julianne's name is a pun on julienne, a knife-cut of food, and reads a paragraph about Roberta's puppy and the Robinsons' hedge as containing sinister words like seized, penetrated and capture. Grounds the reading in a Wikipedia definition of julienning and in the wording of the passage, while admitting he may be overreading.
  • 2007-06-26 New Wolfe Story at Subterranean PressLists Joel Sieh's first-reading observations on the new Subterranean Press story, arguing robots are imperfect substitutes for real life and that the narrator may be a figure of death who kills Rover. Grounds these in the robot dog Robber, the girls Roberta and Julianne, the hedge imagery, and the word 'penetrated'.
  • 2007-06-15 A QuestionSays Wolfe's use of Neil Gaiman's world and characters in the stories felt somewhat gimmicky but did not spoil enjoyment, and prefers "Blueberry Jam" partly for its image of a world stuck in a traffic jam.
