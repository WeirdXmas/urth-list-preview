  • 2011-10-14 Silk's originProposes that Silk may be a biological child of Tussah's own body, with Auk and Chenille as her children, rather than a child she bought or adopted. Builds this on the mailed phrase "son not of my body", which he reads as ruling out a son from Tussah's body, and is responding to Andrew Mason's remark that Chenille is like a female Auk.
  • 2011-10-11 Return to the WhorlArgues that the secret of the Inhumi in Return to the Whorl is essentially the Golden Rule, since they feed on human cruelty and deceit and absorb those traits from their hosts. Responds to Larry Miller's earlier post by proposing that if humans were kinder, the Inhumi would be no more dangerous than a snake or other dangerous animal.
  • 2011-10-11 Typhon's NatureProposes that Mucor's genetically arising mental powers, and her birth from an embryo bought by Blood, point to Typhon being a product of genetic breeding or in-vitro rearing rather than ordinary birth. Grounds the idea in Typhon's remark that he was not born as such and in the embryo-breeding evidence around Mucor, while openly calling it conjecture.
  • 2011-09-20 The Book of the Long Sun QuestionProposes a mechanism for how Quetzal and other inhumi reached the Whorl: humans parked in the green-blue system earlier, with landers making trips down and back, letting inhumi stow away. Grounds this in the Long Sun setting and in a general Wolfe pattern where an impersonator gradually becomes the entity impersonated, especially when that leads toward God.
  • 2011-01-19 the prime calcula/his citadel and other quotesProposes that the Silk/Typhon clone question needs no grand conspiracy, only Horn and Nettle agreeing to omit the resemblance from the narrative. Offers speculation that Silk, seeing his face on Pas, realised he was a Typhon clone and asked Horn to keep it secret, while acknowledging it is not strongly grounded in the text.
  • 2010-09-14 The face of PasRelays James Wynn's suspicion that Typhon keeps his head and face because his magic would not survive cloning or uploading into a chem or mainframe. Presents this as a practical reason the face story serves as cover, hiding the true source of Pas's power; it replies to Roy C. Lackey's thread on the face of Pas.
  • 2010-07-09 Urth Digest, Vol 71, Issue 87Proposes that the monkey-like man-apes Severian meets in The Book of the New Sun may be degenerate humans from our own era, which would explain their shared traits without making them one person. Grounds this on Jonas telling Severian that mankind has changed over many long years after the man-ape incident.
  • 2010-07-07 Urth Digest, Vol 71, Issue 48Argues that Inire's disguised appearances matter only if they change the reading, and speculates Inire nudges Sev1 toward the New Sun. Replies to the thread's demand for clues, then insists that reducing the books to one correct reading discards their many interpretations, comparing this to the film Primer.
  • 2010-07-06 Urth Digest, Vol 71, Issue 41Parodies a chain of identity claims: Severian heals a girl in The Sword of the Lictor, so he must be Inire, Jesus and Lewis Carroll, with the girl possibly the Cumaean through time travel. Responds to John Watkins's post by piling on puns about the Talitha cumi phrase and noting the X-Files episode of that name.
  • 2010-06-04 Travelling North aka miscellaneous thoughts on WolfeArgues that the Urth-in-the-future idea can be squared with parallel universes sharing names like Robert and Marie, and that time is elastic so that later events, such as Frankenstein, ripple back into earlier worlds. Grounds this in the Baldanders legend, the saints' names, and a stone-in-a-pond analogy, while claiming Wolfe answers questions about Christ in The Book of the New Sun.
  • 2009-06-22 Inhumi swimming through the starsProposes that Inhumi once swam between stars because they inherited that power by feeding on undines, which originated on Blue/Green, and lost it once no undines remained. Offers this as a plausible explanation for why Inhumi no longer travel between stars and asks whether the reasoning has an obvious flaw.
