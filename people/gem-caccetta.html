  • 2021-08-26 A little bit of PeaceShares a long passage from Peace on page 6 and remarks on its Proustian length, noting the narrator's reflection on the garden as the core of the real world. Quotes the sentence comparing the Elm and America to a locket, and links it to Dante's Paradiso and the earth's place in its cosmos.
  • 2020-01-29 Wolfe & WomenSuggests that a line from Free Live Free, where Barnes says he wants women but does not like them, may reflect Wolfe's own attitude toward women. Supports the guess by quoting Barnes's remark about admiring Madame Serpentina, offered as a tentative personal reading.
  • 2020-01-27 Heinlein's Universe and The Long SunArgues that The Long Sun differs from typical generation-ship stories because its characters remember their voyage, with the Crew and educated Cargo knowing they are on a ship and merely setting it aside. Responds to Gem Caccetta's suggestion of a Heinlein homage by noting the shared two-headed mutants and 'Freight' term, and compares the Cargo's denial to ignoring global warming.
  • 2020-01-26 Heinlein's Universe and The Long SunNotes that Heinlein's novella Universe, a generation ship drifting after its purpose is forgotten and its descendants fall into superstition, shares many features with The Long Sun, including a lost mission and a young man who takes control. Lists specific parallels: the ship's degenerated religion, the two-headed mutant leader Joe-Jim, and Hugh being shown the stars in the control room.
  • 2019-09-08 Silk & the Order of the Seekers...Argues that Silk, from The Book of the Long Sun, is a member of the Order of the Seekers for Truth and Penitence, reading him as a figure of the Order in the broad sense. Grounds this in a passage from Calde of the Long Sun on augurs' black clothing hiding blood, and compares it to the torturer's fuligin cloak in the Book of the New Sun.
  • 2019-06-19 Time-traveling through the Urth listReports that the poster is reading the whole Urth list from 1996 and reflects on the list's posters, especially Marc Aramini and the late Alice K. Turner, and on reading Wolfe aloud. Replies to Marc Aramini's note by praising his posts, noting the poster's discomfort with Wolfe's treatment of women, and citing Pomeroy's book as context for Soldier of the Mist.
  • 2019-06-18 Time-traveling through the Urth listIntroduces a newcomer reading the list chronologically and remarks on the list's time-travel feel, then notes Alice K. Turner's death and her voice. Points out that Crowley's Little, Big opens with a Chesterton epigraph about women, contrasting it with Wolfe's depiction of women and Chesterton's influence on him.
