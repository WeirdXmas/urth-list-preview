  • 2001-02-26 Wolfe, Religion, and YouArgues that a secular reader can fully appreciate Wolfe's religious content, praising his genre deconstruction, ethical compassion and prose while admitting occasional clumsy metaphors. Responds to a poster who wondered what non-Christians see in Wolfe, offering his own reasons rather than citing specific scenes in detail beyond a eucharist-related quip.
  • 2001-02-11 Horn's spiritAsks when Horn's spirit left Silk's body, doubting that Remora's passage alone explains it, and whether Silk's astral form still resembled Horn aboard the ship on Ocean. Also floats a separate idea that Jahlee's attack on Nettle was prompted by her drinking blood from the amoral people of New Viron.
  • 2000-12-08 The cover of IN GREEN'S JUNGLESArgues that the Short Sun jacket pictures of naked women and badly rendered aliens drag Wolfe's books into generic genre territory, while praising the Long Sun and Free Live Free covers. Proposes alternatives such as landscapes, metaphorical images like Iain M. Banks's British paperbacks, or pure typographic design. Offers no reading of the novels themselves; the argument rests on cover-design examples and sales logic.
  • 2000-12-05 A reaction to IGJReacts to the hardcover edition of In Green's Jungles, criticizing its dust jacket and complaining that the Horn versus the Despoiling Conquerors conflict repeats too much and ends in a deus ex machina battle with Blanko's artillery. Praises the parenting theme inherited from On Blue's Waters, asks whether Wolfe's own parenting shows in Horn's experiences, and closes by asking for reading suggestions while waiting for Return to the Whorl.
  • 2000-12-05 My reaction to IN GREEN'S JUNGLESComplains that In Green's Jungles repeats the Horn versus Despoiling Conquerors conflict and that its climactic battle is deus ex machina, since Blanko's heavy artillery appears unmentioned until needed. Compares this background conflict unfavourably with the handling in On Blue's Waters and asks whether Wolfe's portrayal of Horn's parenting reflects his own.
  • 2000-11-22 Digest whorl.v012.n007Bets list members fifty cents each that the giant figure on the cover of Return to the Whorl is the Outsider, Silk, a Silk/Pas composite, Gene Wolfe himself, or a mixture of these. Offers the wager playfully, doubting Wolfe would insert himself into the book but admitting one never knows.
