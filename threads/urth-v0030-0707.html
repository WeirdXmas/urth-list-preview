"Five" in In Glory Like Their Star [Digest

2001-10-09 James Jordan #

Reports that a lecture series on English language history linked the words for "five" and "all" as basically the same root, which Jordan suggests may explain a title's wordplay. Responds to John's claim that the Proto-Indo-European roots for "five" and "all" are distinct, offering a tentative, admittedly uninformed rationale for Wolfe's etymological choice.
supports · source-and-allusion · theories: Title's five refers to etymological link with all

Hmmm. I'm no expert here at all, but I happened to be listening to a series of lectures on the History of the English Language from The Teaching Company, and the speaker said that "five" and "all" (pn and pn) were basically the same.That's what jogged my dim memory. I don't recall if that's because of the number of digits in a hand. At any rate, the real question is what Wolfe thought, given the way he likes to trade on etymologies, and this explanation is all I've come up with that begins to make sense. I guess somebody could ask Wolfe, but he'd probably just smile....

Jim Jordan
