Themes: The dying sun and the cosmos

2008-11-26 Son of Witz #

Asks where the idea that the black hole is an astroengineering product comes from, since the text only shows a black chasm in Malrubius's speech and a worm eating the sun. Cites the Sand Garden passage about Black Pits and White Fountains and the Claw of the Conciliator line about a black worm devouring the sun, and asks whether the Hierodules say they placed it.
questions · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion

Can anyone help me out with this "black hole" thing?
I see how it's inferred from the text at one point, when Aquastor Malrubius says: (Citadel, XXXI, Sand Garden) "You know of the chasms of space, which some call the Black Pits, from which no speck of matter or gleam of light ever returns. But what you have not known until now is that these chasms have their counterparts in White Fountains, from which matter and energy rejected by a higher universe flow in endless cataract into this one."

and there is the Worm eating the Sun: Claw IV, Boquet
"You, the hero who will destroy the black worm that devours the sun;" (more on that quote later)

but where does the idea that the Black Hole is the product of AstroEngineering. I don't have the Lexicon on hand, but I don't remember it giving a textual reference.

what do folks make of that?
Do the Hierodules say they put it there in Urth?

2008-11-30 Jeff Wilson replying to Son of Witz #

Recalls that the term "black hole" in Citadel's Malrubius passage on Black Pits and White Fountains traces to black beans thrown by a woman, identified as Typhon's daughter Scylla. Answers Son of Witz's question about the phrase from the Sand Garden chapter, but gives no provenance or reference for the claim.
answers · source-and-allusion · theories: Black hole term traces to Scylla's black beans

ISTR that it has been traced to the black beans thrown by a woman, and that the woman has been identified as Typhon's daughter Scylla, but I couldn't provide you with the provenance or a reference.
