multi-limbs

Themes: Colonization and the native, Ghosts, dreams and visions

2009-05-04 b sharp #

Argues that the multi-limbed creatures on Blue's Waters mark native beings rather than alterations, since four limbs are only an evolutionary by-product and arthropods show otherwise. Links this to the Inhumi, who are poor with tools, parasitic and mimicking, and suggests The Fifth Head of Cerberus could illuminate Inhumi origins and their assumption of new identities.
proposes · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: Multi-limbs mark Blue's natives rather than alterations; Inhumi connect to Shadow Children

FWIW, my impression of the message of multi-limbs on Blue's creatures is the opposite that some others get. Having four limbs is not a universal ideal or baseline (as our arthropod cousins show); it is just an evolutionary by-product of the basic earth vertebrate pattern.

So rather than seeing multi-limbs as an alteration, I see them as a way that Wolfe marks certain creatures on Blue as natives; others are are alien invaders. This could be a clear message that Blue is not Earth or Urth or Ushas.

Also, I suppose it has been mentioned before but I just re-noticed Wolfe's description of Inhumi as not being good with tools. Add this to their parasitic and mimicking nature and I think the connection to Shadow Children becomes quite powerful. This might make Fifth Head of Cerberus a very fruitful place for understanding Inhumi origins and how they think and feel as they assume a new identity.

-bsharp
