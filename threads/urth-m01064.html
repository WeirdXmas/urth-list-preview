2005-01-27 Tony Ellis #

Argues that the narrator of The Fifth Head of Cerberus is Victor, who became Marsch, and that the Annese are the illiterate underclass among the colonists rather than replacements for them. Supports this with the handwriting match between the prisoner and Victor, the officer nodding at Victor's schoolbook, and the argument that the colonists could not be aboriginals who learned to read in three generations.
proposes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Victor became Marsch; Annese are illiterate underclass not replacements; Victor's mother and Cassila are Annese passing as French refugees; Liev's postpostulate about Annese refugees

<lots!>

Well, the 'What Abos?' thread tackled this way back when, and hopefully we covered at least some of the key points. The argument starts here, if you can bear it:
http://www.urth.net/urth/archives/v0204/816.txt.shtml

I should point out that Robert Borski rendered our whole debate largely pointless by quoting Wolfe as saying the narrator of VRT is a Shadow Child. I think 'Shadow Child' was a slip of the tongue for 'abo', but I'm open to persuasion.

We're shown people who can use tools, and people who can't, so no, I don't think the Annese can have replaced the human colonists entirely. Quite aside from anything else, if every colonist is an abo, why do they think they are colonists? How can they read? How can they maintain their rich, complex culture, if they were all naked savages three generations ago?

If you want to find the Annese, you have to look among the illiterate and the marginalised: the beggars and the slaves. I believe in what I understand to be Liev's postpostulate: that after the war, some of the Annese were able to enter human society under the guise of being French refugees. Victor's mother is one, Cassila is another.

The prisoner writes with the same wretched handwriting as Victor: we see the officer look back at Victor's old schoolbook and nod to himself. This, and masses of circumstantial evidence, suggests that Victor became Marsch.
