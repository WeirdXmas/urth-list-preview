The thread: Readers debated whether Silversilk, lodged in Pig's damaged eye, moved from Pig to the narrator, directly or via the Mainframe, and whether that transfer resolves the search for Silk in The Book of the Long Sun. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes clarified that he never said the idea was unworthy of discussion, though he finds it textually unsupported and wrong to him. aramini1 argued that Silversilk will leave Pig to rejoin the mainframe, grounding this in Silk's talk with Chenille about possession, and defended speculative readings against the charge of not returning to the text. James Wynn agreed such options may be offered, and suggested the transfer coincided with Pig losing his eye, admitting he posted partly to stir debate. The thread ended without resolution, with the speculation defended but unconvinced.

  • James Wynn: transfer into Silk coincided with Pig losing his eye; speculative readings are fair to post

2005-01-09 Marc Aramini #

Argues that Silversilk, lodged in Pig's damaged eye, will leave Pig to rejoin the mainframe, so that the search for Silk in The Book of the Long Sun culminates in Silk's retrieval. Grounds this in Silk's talk with Chenille about possession leaving fragments that conjoin, and defends presenting such speculative options against criticism for not returning to the text.
proposes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Silversilk leaves Pig to rejoin the mainframe; Silk's retrieval ends the Long Sun search

I never implied that Silk or Pig would be downloading anything from eye to eye. In Book of the Long Sun, Silk speaks with Chenille about possession, and how it always leaves something behind that changes the one possessed and the fragment of the possessor that leaves, so that they become inexplicably conjoined and change each other. I was merely taking several facts:

1. Silversilk is in Pig
2. Silversilk is trapped until the eye of Pig can be repaired. 2b. It seems implied that Silversilk plans to leave Pig to rejoin mainframe, as a fragment of Pas once planned to return to mainframe. 3. The book is about the search for Silk.
3b. Silk's original spirit has been damaged so much that it is no longer whole, and requires outside support. 4. It makes sense that the book would end when silk is found, one way or another. 5. SOMETHING makes Silk whole again.

Therefore, it seems logical that both narrative strands would culminate in a retreival of Silk.

Certainly the admonishments to return to the text are unwarranted - I see the interpretive task as one of opening up a multitude of options. Without the presentation of options (whether valid ones or not), the discussion of the work becomes pretty much moot. We would then have to tow the party line, and the discussion becomes stiffled and dead. Whether you believe these options or not is irrelevant - the point is that I work both with facts from the texts and themes which I believe are extent in the text but are not always overtly stated.

You don't have to agree with me - but you shouldn't say that I am wrong to present these options to a "discussion" list. You are all capable of making your own decisions about believing the options I present or rejecting them.

Marc
