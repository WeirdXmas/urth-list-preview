Violet=Plutonium

Urth list · 12 messages · 2011-11-14 to 2011-11-14

2011urthThe Book of the New SunThe Book of the Long SunJordon FlatoLee BermanSergei SOLOVIEVGerry QuinnDavid StockhoffJames Wynn
The thread: Posters debated whether Violet, nicknamed Plutonium by Sand in The Book of the Long Sun and Exodus from the Long Sun, might be a chem, with the nickname as the key evidence. James Wynn and Lee Berman proposed and supported the chem theory, tying it to made beings and plutonium power, while Gerry Quinn cited Crane's remark about isotopes. David Stockhoff, Sergei Soloviev, and Jordon Flato pushed back, calling the evidence thin and arguing that the nickname may be slang rather than a clue. Jordon Flato then offered a 'hot stuff' reading of the word. The thread ended with no agreement, with the chem theory left unresolved.
  • James Wynn: Violet may be a chem; the endearment toward her justifies serious consideration
  • Lee Berman: Chems were built to receive scanned human souls; plutonium fits a pet name meaning 'my heart'
  • Gerry Quinn: Crane's isotope remark suggests chems have some nuclear power source
  • David Stockhoff: Case for Violet as chem is weak and would complicate the plot
  • Sergei Soloviev: Chem reading rests on thin evidence; 'Plutonium' is soldiers' argot
  • Jordon Flato: Violet may not be a chem; 'plutonium' may be a slang stand-in for 'hot stuff'

Themes: Robots, androids and artificial life, Names and their meanings, Hidden identities and parentage

2011-11-14 James Wynn replying to Lee Berman #

Proposes that Jonas in Wolfe's Long Sun-era Chelle and Jonas material is a made being whose reading of fiction could be a programmed trait, linking it to chems judging smells and to Jonas becoming Miles. Offers two routes: Miles will become Jonas through a remembered speech, or Jonas is built from Miles's biological remains that retain memory, as with Chelle's arm in Home Fires.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Miles will become Jonas; Jonas is made from Miles's remains; chems judge smells by programming

The chems also wear perfume and the original chem owner of Rose's hand used to "practice her hand". There is a practical reason for them to smell and judge good smells (the females anyway), because they have to cook. But this is a convenient way to accomplish the judging part.

Until now, I did not apply this to Jonas. It seemed unlikely that Wolfe would have thought of this method and done so little with it until the mid 90s. Now I'm not so sure. This might explain how Jonas "becomes" Miles. In truth (according to this theory), Miles *will* become Jonas. Severian's speech to Jonas will be in Jonas's subconscious and sometime after he leaves Sev, he remembers it and does not return. On the other hand, it might be simpler to propose that Jonas is made from what is left of Miles's biological parts, and as with Chelle's new arm in "Home Fires" those parts remember.

J.

2011-11-14 James Wynn replying to Sergei SOLOVIEV #

Argues that the question of whether Violet is a chem deserves serious consideration despite weak evidence, and lists what is known about her: she works at Orchids, spies for money, and Hyacinth also was a spy. Responds to Sergei Soloviev's jab about skyscrapers built on sand by stressing that Sand's use of the endearment toward only Violet justifies the speculation.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Violet is a chem

Sand? What the...!!

Seriously, though, this is more like a kwansit hut built on sand. But many assertions that There's Nothing To See Here are also not founded in granite.

The question of whether Violet is a chem was worth knocking around for these reasons:
Only Sand uses this supposed "term of endearment". He never employs it toward anyone but Violet; neither bio or chem.
We'd be morons not to knock around the idea that she is a chem, just to dispense with it. I'm not inclined to believe that she is, but I *do* believe more is going on. I always have.

I want to distinguish things we definitely know and what is not confirmation of anything (I don't do this to *prove* that Violet is a chem):

Violet works at Orchids. Violet makes extra money as a spy. Hyacinth was a spy for the Trivigaunte and has also worked at Orchids. If Violet is a chem, the people she works for know it, but this is only necessarily true if she is a chem. Her claim not to be able to read undermines this suspicion, but she could be lying. A desire for money is not evidence that she is a chem. One of the black mechanics has altered his chem so that he works for no money. There is a chem, Sard, who runs a shop. Chems want things money can buy. Since they want replacement parts, their desire is possibly greater.

2011-11-14 David Stockhoff replying to Sergei SOLOVIEV #

Argues against the idea that Violet is a chem in Exodus from the Long Sun, saying the case for it is weak and that it would complicate the plot. Supports the doubt by pointing to Violet's place as Orchid's employee and her being a spy suspect, and to her use of a non-standard name, Plutonium, for Hammerstone, while noting that her illiteracy is a fair point.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Violet is a chem; Violet is an agent of Trivigauntis

Well, not entirely. First, we are not building skyscrapers but rather imagining what they might look like and how they might be built. I don't think Violet is a chem either, but if not, why not?

Second, as an employee of Orchid's---and a big girl to boot, "second tallest" in fact---Violet is already a spy suspect before she happily gets into bed with Siyuf. Having her be a chem too probably complicates the situation too much.

Violet calling Hammerstone "sweetheart" means little in a prostitute. Calling someone you know by a completely different name---one that violates the known naming conventions, as "Plutonium" does---is a little different.

Good point about Violet being illiterate though---if she's not faking it.
