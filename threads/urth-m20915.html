Mandragora

Urth list · 7 messages · 2011-11-30 to 2011-11-30

2011urthThe Book of the New SunJerry FriedmanSergei SOLOVIEVGerry QuinnBrian LovelyDavid StockhoffLarry Miller
The thread: Readers debated what the mandragora in The Book of the New Sun is, using Wikipedia definitions of mandrake plants and sorcerer's demon-dolls. David Stockhoff and Brian argued it was a demonic doll or fetish that could serve as a spare or possessed body, linking it to the possession motifs of the Long Sun and Short Sun books. Gerry Quinn disputed this, noting Severian's conversation with the mandragora shows no possession and the homunculus says it is a preserved deformed fetus. Brian then said the Fava dream sequences had confused the poster and that the mandragora was a forgotten possible host. Jerry Friedman ended the thread by arguing the homunculus was lying about being a dead fetus, possibly as a ruse to get Severian to kill it, with no agreement reached.
  • David Stockhoff: mandragora points to possession and riding motifs in the Long Sun and Short Sun books
  • Brian: mandragora may be a demonic doll or fetish, later saying it was a forgotten possible host
  • Gerry Quinn: mandragora is a homunculus that could be a spare body, but shows no possession in the text
  • Jerry Friedman: the homunculus is lying about being a dead fetus, perhaps to get Severian to kill it

Themes: Doubles, twins and clones, Robots, androids and artificial life, Unreliable narration

2011-11-30 Gerry Quinn replying to David Stockhoff #

Argues that the mandragora in The Book of the New Sun is a homunculus-style demon doll that could serve as a spare body, linking it to the riders and ridden of the Long Sun and Short Sun books. Answers David Stockhoff's Wikipedia-based definitions by noting that Severian's conversation with the mandragora shows no possession, and that the homunculus itself claims to be a preserved deformed fetus.
disputes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Mandragora is a spare body for possession; Mandragora is a hybrid spirit like Mani; Homunculus is a preserved deformed fetus

From: David Stockhoff
Since we all agree Wikipedia is never wrong ;), I'll just stick with their definitions. Mandragora is the genus of the plant mandrake, which often has a somewhat man-shaped root. It is also the name of a type of demon. These demons are little dolls given to a sorceror by the Devil for the purpose of consulting "in time of need". They are thought to be related to the "fetish, which is nothing else than a dwelling-place made by a shaman or medicine-man for the reception of any wandering spirit who chooses to take up his abode therein."

Admittedly, Wikipedia has this last quote labeled as "citation needed", but this does sound remarkably like a spare body.

Very nice. And again, this points me to BLS/BSS, and their multitude of riders and ridden. Regardless of how much detail Wolfe had worked out by the time he finished BNS, the concept plainly was fully developed even though BNS *seems* to have little possession going on.

Except that Severian talked to the mandragora in question, and there was no indication of possession in the sense used elsewhere in the Sun series. One might postulate a hybrid spirit like Mani in Wizard Knight. But the homunculus itself claimed to be a deformed fetus, preserved artificially in a semi-living state.

As for Typhon, I think he?d find a homunculus in a jar a bit confining. That is, if he weren?t dead a couple of thousand years anyway.

- Gerry Quinn
