Themes: Names and their meanings

2002-05-23 Russell Wodell #

Asks how the name Thecla is pronounced and proposes that its resemblance to "the claw" in The Claw of the Conciliator may carry meaning, since he believes the name is Biblical and probably Hebrew. The post raises the name's sound and possible source without offering a settled reading or citing a passage.

2002-05-24 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Gives the pronunciation of the name Thecla in The Book of the New Sun, saying it sounds like "THEH-kluh" and differs from "the claw" in the initial th sound. Responds to Russell Wodell's question about the name's Biblical origin and its closeness to "claw" by noting the link has been noticed many times before.
answers · close-textual-reading

My understanding is that it is pronounced "THEH-kluh," not "THUH-klah" or "THEE-klah." (Those "th" sounds might even be different, voiced vs. unvoiced, but I can't hear it right now. The difference between "thin" and "the": yes, I think they are different, Thecla being like "thin" and "the claw" being, obviously, "the.")

But yes, I have seen the discovery of the eerie linkage between Thecla and "the claw" a number of times over the years

=mantis=
