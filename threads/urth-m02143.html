The thread: Joel and Rachel asked whether Gene Wolfe left commentary, journals or letters that would explain his fiction after his death. b sharp argued Wolfe would not want posthumous revelations, citing an interview in which he reacts sharply to questions about his legacy. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued Wolfe does not deliberately mislead but withholds so much that his books give little away, rejecting Gregg's suggestion that a journal might be a red herring. 2006-07-26 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes replying to Gregg #

Argues that Wolfe does not deliberately mislead readers but withholds so much that his books give little away, comparing him to an overly cautious reviewer who avoids spoilers. Responds to Gregg's suggestion that a Wolfe journal might be a deliberate red herring meant to mislead readers about the true story.
disputes · author-and-biography

Ummm, I can't speak for anyone else, but I don't think Wolfe deliberately misleads, just goes too far in not giving things away, something like a reviewer so cautious about spoilers that she doesn't actually tell you anything about the book/movie/whatever.

2006-07-26 Gregg replying to Joel and Rachel #

Jokes that Wolfe's journal, if it exists, would itself be a decoy, and that only a download of his brain would reveal the true meaning of his writings. Responds to Joel and Rachel's discussion of a possible Wolfe journal, hoping his notes survive his will.
chats

If Mr. Wolfe has a journal that reveals anything, much less everything, some of the readers of this mailing list will believe that the journal is something he devised to throw us off the trail of the real truth. I suggest the contents of his brain must be downloaded to find the real meaning of his writings.

Having typed that, though, I can believe that he has written notes among his papers and his notes would be fascinating to read. Let's hope his will doesn't stipulate the destruction of his notes. Still would need that brain download to understand how it all fits together.
