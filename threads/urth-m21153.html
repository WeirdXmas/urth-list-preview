2011-10-13 Marc Aramini replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that Gene Wolfe is not a deceiver like his narrators, while joking that he lies to everyone except the poster. Responds to Lee Berman's question about what the Father Inire material says about Wolfe's view of his life, writing and readers, saying Wolfe enjoys confusing careless readers but is not malicious.
answers · author-and-biography

It's been my experience he lies to everyone [except me, of course]. (That's a joke. I'm not really serious - he's a cagey and sly fellow but I think he is much more kind and trustworthy than his narrators [except for a few special sensitive topics]).

For sure he takes some small joy in confusing careless people, but I don't think it's malicious.

2011-10-13 Lee Berman #

Argues that Severian's unreliability in The Book of the New Sun stems mainly from deliberate lies told to readers, motivated by a wish to hide his family's dirty laundry. Responds to Ryan Dunn's point that Severian plays detective in his memoir, agreeing in part while noting that deception and density are hard to tell apart in the text.
supports · myth-and-structure · author-and-biography · theories: Severian's main lies are told to readers to hide family secrets

Ryan, I think we are basically in agreement. I don't think we can always tells where Severian's unreliability is due to him being deliberately deceptive or just that he is a bit dense.

Still, Severian mentions a few times in the text what a big liar he is and that used to confuse me since he seems quite honest in his dealings with the other characters in the story. I've come to conclude that his admission of guilt is because the biggest recipient of his lies are his readers. Severian finds this regrettable but necessary and I still think his primary motivation for lying to or misleading us is to avoid openly airing his family's dirty laundry.

What I find fascinating is what this might say about Gene Wolfe and how he views his own life, his writing and his readers.
