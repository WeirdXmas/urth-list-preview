Where Judas lost his boots

Urth list · 7 messages · 2010-10-14 to 2010-10-15

2010urthThe Book of the Short SunAntónio Pedro MarquesJerry FriedmanJames WynnLee Berman
The thread: Readers asked how the name 'Judastree', applied to Jahlee in Sinew's village, connects to Judas, to the boots remark, and to Horn's oath. Jerry Friedman explained the Judas tree legend, which says it blushed when Judas hanged himself on it, and tied it to Incanto's view of Jahlee's trustworthiness. António Marques offered a Portuguese idiom for a far-off place and argued that Judas carries a treason sense in the Whorl that predates Horn. James Wynn proposed that the name is a deliberate treasonous cognate because Jahlee means 'false', and asked whether the village speaks Sanskrit or Hindi. António Pedro Marques then asked whether the Vironese speak Spanish. The thread drifted into language-of-names questions without settling any of them.
  • Lee Berman: asks how the Judastree name links to Judas, boots, and Horn's oath, and offers his own names for Jahlee
  • Jerry Friedman: the tree blushed at Judas's suicide, reflecting Incanto's doubt about Jahlee's trustworthiness
  • António Marques: 'boots' is a Portuguese idiom, and Judas carries a treason sense predating Horn
  • James Wynn: Judastree is a deliberate treasonous cognate because Jahlee means 'false'
  • António Pedro Marques: the Vironese high speech is probably Spanish, so the names may draw on it

Themes: Names and their meanings, Women, sex and gender, Christ figures and messiahs

2010-10-15 António Pedro Marques replying to Lee Berman #

Explains that the 'boots' remark is a Portuguese expression for a far-off place nobody wants to visit, and argues that the name Judas in the Whorl carries a treason sense that predates Horn. Responds to Lee Berman's puzzlement over the Cercis siliquastrum tree and Jahlee, citing the folklore that the Judas tree was a fig and the possible 'Judea's tree' origin of the English name.
disputes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Judas name carries treason sense in the Whorl

'while in', I meant.

Folk lore says it was a fig tree. The english "Judas tree" may be a misconstruction of the latin/romance for "Judea's tree".

Oh, the boots thing is just one portuguese expression for 'a place far away nobody wants to have to go to from here'.

I don't get Incanto's mistrust of Jahlee at that specific point, but the relevant part is the use of the name 'Judas' in the Whorl's universe. Conjoined 'Judastree' may be evidence that he knew not what the components meant, but the idea of treason seems to be is there. Either way, if it wasn't there by Horn's time, it was there at some earlier point.
