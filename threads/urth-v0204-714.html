5HC: Is the Wolfe line necessarily doomed to failure?

Urth list · 1 messages · 2002-04-19 to 2002-04-19

2002urthDan'l Danehy-Oakes

Themes: Robots, androids and artificial life, Evolution and the future of humanity

2002-04-19 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Asks whether a passage in The Fifth Head of Cerberus, where Mr. Million's face dissolves into sparkling dots and he admits his simulation can feel emotion, shows that the Wolfe line of clones has changed. Grounds the question in the quoted lines and the poster's wording that something happened that did not happen with previous clones, linking it to Wolfe's own views on inherited traits.
questions · close-textual-reading · theology-and-religion · theories: Mr. Million's emotion shows the Wolfe clones can evolve

I'm not sure what this means, but it appears that Number Five does something no previous Gene Wolfe has done. In the conversation in which he discoveres the nature of Mr. Million, this happens (Ace ed'n, p45):

[Mr. Million] paused agian, and for an instant his
face dissolved into myriad sparkling dots, swirling
like dust motes in a sunbeam. "I am sorry. For once
you wish to listen but I do not wish to lecture. I
was told, a long time ago, just before the operation,
that my simulation - this - would be capable of
emotion in certain circumstances. Until today I had
always thought they lied."

Okay, I freely admit that I'm not sure what this is about. The preceding conversation is all about what Mr. Million is. Is it possible that no preceding generation has _asked_? Or...?

At any rate, it appears that _something_ has happened here that did not happen with the previous GW clones. Does this imply that the GWs can evolve after all? (After all, the real GW believes in some "heretical" ideas about inheriting acquired characteristics.)

--Blattid
