Baptism and Confirmation: Shadow of the Torturer

Urth list · 4 messages · 2014-08-24 to 2014-08-24

2014urthThe Book of the New SunmarcobadieGerry QuinnMarc Aramini
The thread: Readers debated whether Severian's immersion and the false chrisos from Vodalus constitute a baptism and confirmation binding him to Abaia's followers. Marcobadie argued yes, citing Severian's remarks on symbols, the soldier-of-Christ parallel, and the necropolis ranking. Marc Aramini pushed back on the theology of confirmation, arguing it is an adult acceptance of childhood-imposed beliefs and that it does not bear on Wolfe, who converted as an adult. Gerry Quinn turned to the necropolis, proposing Vodalus deliberately takes corpses from various classes. The thread ended without agreement, with the sacramental reading and the necropolis point left as separate threads of discussion.
  • marcobadie: Severian's immersion and Vodalus's chrisos form a baptism and confirmation binding him to Abaia's followers
  • Marc Aramini: confirmation is a fully adult acceptance of childhood-imposed beliefs and does not affect Wolfe's reading
  • Gerry Quinn: Vodalus may deliberately take corpses from various social classes to serve insurgent aims

Themes: Catholic faith and sacraments, Gods, angels and the Increate, Gnosticism, heresy and other theologies

2014-08-24 marcobadie #

Argues that Severian's immersion in the water and the false chrisos given by Vodalus form a baptism and confirmation that bind him to Abaia's followers, so the sacraments of Abaia are symbolically sealed despite Marco's objection. Grounds this in Severian's remarks on symbols shaping us, the parallel to Catholic confirmation as a soldier of Christ, and the necropolis ranking and Hildegrin's search.

Of course, of course, this is the essential point (and I have missed it in my post): the power bestowed to Severian is so measureless that the sacraments administered by Abaia and his followers are irrelevant.

that might be one part of it.

The meaning of baptism is rebirth (from sin).
The resurrection of Severian from water represents his admission in the ranks of the followers of Abaia. At this baptism the Undine is his godmother.

This is Severian's rambling on the power of symbols (the coin with the face of Autarch): "We believe that we invent symbols. The truth is that they invent us; we are their creatures, shaped by their hard, defining edges. When soldiers take their oath they are given a coin, an asimi stamped with the profile of the Autarch." At confirmation Catholics confirm the pledge of allegiance to Christ made at baptism by their godparents, becoming ?soldier of Christ"; and Severian confirms his baptism declaring himself a vodalarius, a ?soldier of Vodalus". The choice is sealed by the symbolic power of a false chrisos. Like for the Gild of Torturers, Severian will break this oath of allegiance.

Personally, nine years old, I receveid Confirmation Thursday and First Eucharist next Sunday. This was the tradition of the Roman Catholic Church, at the least in Italy; today also, I think.

Hildegrin is searching for Severian, with him are Vodalus and Thea, the leaders of the insurgency. Why Hildegrin would be interested in a peasant's woman? Moreover, the necropolis is organised by social rank: higher there are the masoleums of the optimates, lower the burial mounds of peasants and labourers. Severian is watching the scene behind a calcedony angel, the grave of the dark-haired woman is the grave of an armigette, at least.

Thanks for your comments.

Marco Cecchini

2014-08-24 Gerry Quinn replying to marcobadie #

Argues that Vodalus may deliberately take corpses from various social classes, linking the mourners' varied backgrounds in the necropolis to the insurgent's purposes. Grounds this in the scene where volunteers guard their dead and fear their kin's graves will be robbed, and responds to marcobadie's quoted point about Hildegrin and social rank in the cemetery.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Vodalus deliberately takes corpses from various social classes

"We're the volunteers," one of the others said. "We
come to protect our own dead."

But then of course while we saw a peasant, we did not see the man who was going to watch over his mother.

A man who had not spoken before said, "I'm going to watch over my mother. We've wasted too much time already. They could have her a league off by now."

And they split up and went in several directions, Presumably they came from a variety of social classes. All seemed to fear, correctly or not, that their kins' graves might be robbed. And indeed, perhaps Vodalus deliberately takes corpses from various walks of society.

- Gerry Quinn
