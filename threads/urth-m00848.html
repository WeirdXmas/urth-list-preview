2005-01-04 stilskin replying to harlekin #

Argues that Garvaon's sacrifice in The Wizard Knight is selfish rather than noble, so Idnn would find it offensive, and that his death in battle against Setr is what earns his raising to Skai. Responds to harlekin's paradox about Garvaon's honor and Gilling's murder, tying it to a broader theme of redemption and second chances.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Garvaon's sacrifice is selfish, not honorable

I'm not sure that Garvaon's sacrifice would have been
appreciated by Idnn had she known of it. In fact, I'm
pretty confident she would find it reprehensible and
offensive. Garvaon's motivation is selfish. He
redeems himself in battling Setr. Had he died in
almost any other way, I doubt he would have been
raised to Skai. One interesting theme that runs
throughout the Wizard Knight is that of redemption, of
second (and sometimes even third and fourth) chances.

Paul

http://www.sff.net/people/stilskin

2005-01-04 harlekin #

Argues that Sir Garvaon's murder of Gilling is a paradox, since it is dishonorable by knightly standards yet Garvaon is raised to Skai, and notes Abel also breaks the knightly code and is rewarded. Responds to Russell Wodell's question about Garvaon's motive and Idnn's guilt by offering a reading of the knightly code in the book.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Garvaon murdered Gilling for Idnn

This is a paradox. Garvaon sacrifices his knightly honor to benefit Idnn, something not even Able is willing to do. Don

His murder of
Gilling, in knightly terms, is a deeply selfish and
dishonorable act -- thus it's interesting that he is
"raised" to Skai. But then again, Abel himself goes
against the knightly code many times, and is rewarded
for it.

Paul
