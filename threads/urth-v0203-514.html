Blue ain't Urth

2002-03-29 Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that Blue cannot be Urth, citing two textual arguments from In Green's Jungles, p. 349: the Neighbors were made from Blue's dust, not Urth's, and Silkhorn's familiar constellations were absent from the sky over the Bear Tower. Presents these as a firm textual commitment, supporting the case with direct quotation from the novel.

or Ushas, either.

There are two separate arguments against it, both on page 349 of IGJ. Too much to quote here, but the gist is:

1) The Neighbors were created by the Outsider from the dust of Blue, just as humans had been created from the dust of Mankind's old Short Sun Whorl. "Besides, the dust of that whorl can scarcely be identical to the dust of this one." It's pretty hard to get a more definite textual commitment in Wolfe's writings.

2) The constellations Silkhorn was used to seeing on Blue were not to be found in the night sky of Urth, when he stayed in the Bear Tower there.

-Roy
