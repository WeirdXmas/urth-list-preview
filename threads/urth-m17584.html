2011-01-06 Jerry Friedman replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that the Inhumi's reptilian appearance does not imply reptilian behavior or evolution, and that their external fertilization in Return to the Whorl does not conflict with a reptile-like look. Responds to Lee Berman's comparison of the Inhumi to Earth reptiles and fish, rejecting the idea that they must be more primitive than reptiles.
disputes · genre-and-craft · theories: Inhumi evolution need not follow Earth reptiles

I'm not going to try to check whether I've said this when you made similar statements--I'm just going to point out that it's not the inhumi's nature that's reptilian; it's their appearance. They're nothing like Earth reptiles in their behavior. And I see no discrepancy between reptilian appearance and external fertilization. I see no reason evolution on Blue-Green should have gone the same way it did on Earth, no reason we have to adjust our ideas about them when we learn about their fertilization or even if they have some mysterious essential link to trees and vines.

2011-01-06 Lee Berman #

Proposes that the Inhumi of Return to the Whorl sit at ever more primitive origins, moving from vampire to reptile to fish or vine or shadow, and suggests the Shadow-born aboriginals may share that origin. Grounds this in the Gondwanaland point (predating primates), the external fertilization revelation at the end of Return to the Whorl, and the Fomalhaut connection.
proposes · myth-and-structure · close-textual-reading · theories: Inhumi have progressively more primitive origins; Shadow-born aboriginal children share Inhumi origins

Well, if it was just "Africa" that might hint at the evolutionary origin of primates, including humanity. But Gondwanaland predates the evolution of primates (I had to look that up to be sure. Did Wolfe?)

I do like the idea of a faerie explanation. But perhaps Wolfe is suggesting that such things predate even primates. Sorry for repeating but perhaps as they are something as basic as the Shadow which must be formed when God creates both Light and Objects. Again, shadows are dark, imitative and indeterminate in size, shape and number.

And again I'd like to make the comparison to Inhumi. First we think they are just vampires. A variation of a human being. Then we find they are scaly, clawed and reptilian in nature. But then, the big revelation at the end of RttW..they have external fertilization. So they are more primitive than reptiles. (maybe fish even? heh@ Fomalhaut= Inhumi's Mouth).

Or maybe their basic structure is a parasitic vine. Or perhaps something even more basic and primitive than that, by extrapolation....perhaps simply a..shadow?

I can't say it is clear and obvious but I get the impression Wolfe keeps suggesting ever more primitive origins for Inhumi. If so, perhaps the same for abo/shadow kids.
