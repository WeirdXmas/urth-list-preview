2009-02-12 Joe Smulkstis #

Asks whether a reviewer's comparison of Richard K. Morgan's work with Gene Wolfe's Book of the New Sun is accurate, noting that Morgan is clearly mining Wolfe territory. Says the Kovacs novels did not strike him as Wolfean, while describing Wolfe's blend of gritty realism and remote future fantasy as the subgenre's model.
questions · genre-and-craft · theories: Morgan's Kovacs novels are not Wolfean

But for me the best example of this subgenre can be found in the work of Gene Wolfe, beginning with his Book of the New Sun novels, fantasy novels set in a remote but very imaginable future that have the gritty realism of the best science fiction. Richard K. Morgan is clearly mining Wolfe territory, and that is fine territory indeed.

Has anyone here read the book and, if so, is the reviewer's comparison with Wolfe accurate?

The Kovac's novels didn't strike me as at all Wolfean.

Muskrat

2009-02-13 Rob Thornton replying to Lane Haygood #

Compares Richard K. Morgan to cyberpunk and names Paul Park, John Crowley and Ford Madox Ford as writers close to Wolfe in prose style or unreliable narration. Responds to Lane Haygood's doubt that Morgan's novel will feel Wolfean, offering a list of comparable authors without developing an argument about the texts.
chats

Richard K. Morgan reminded me of cyberpunk. The closest authors to Wolfe in my mind (in terms of prose style) are Paul Park and John Crowley, though I don't think that anyone in F/SF can beat Wolfe in creating unreliable narratives. The only non-SF author that comes to mind is Ford Madox Ford ("The Good Soldier").

rob t.
