Inire's "Mirrors"

Themes: The dying sun and the cosmos, Genre: science fiction, fantasy, both, Mirrors, pictures and art

1999-11-01 Nicholas Gevers #

Argues that Inire's account of how his mirrors work is deliberate gibberish by Wolfe, meant as a rhetorical conceit rather than plausible faster-than-light science like Anderson's or Asimov's. Adds that Typhon's usurping of Piaton's body fits an alchemical reading of flesh made perfect and immortal, defending mantis's view.
proposes · genre-and-craft · allegory-and-typology · theories: Inire's mirror technology is deliberate gibberish; Typhon's usurping of Piaton is alchemical transmutation

I'm glad to see that my "Fairy Tale Logic" posting
ended our dry patch; I'm also gratified that mantis
agrees with me in part, a rare event. But to take up
the cudgel again: I still contend that Inire's
description of the working of his mirrors is
intentional gibberish on Wolfe's part, to the extent
that he wishes it to be read as gibberish, in contrast
with all the intended-to-be-plausible explanations of
FTL technology in SF. An ideas-driven writer like Poul
Anderson or Isaac Asimov desires his FTL conception to
be believed, to be given the benefit of a very large
doubt; but for a style-driven writer like Jack Vance
or Wolfe, an FTL-exposition is a CONCEIT, a rhetorical
contrivance, serving a metaphorical or humorous
purpose. Inire's mirrors should be interrogated on
that basis.

Incidentally, in defense of mantis' view of Typhon as
alchemical symbol: the point of alchemy for many was
the conversion of their base flesh into perfect matter
blessed with immortality. Typhon's aim in usurping
Piaton's body is also extended life.

1999-11-02 Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that Inire's account of how his mirrors work is deliberate gibberish by Wolfe, meant to be read as a rhetorical conceit rather than plausible faster-than-light science. Contrasts style-driven writers like Vance and Wolfe with idea-driven SF writers like Anderson and Asimov, and mocks the scientific basis of the white fountain and the angel-born remedy for the dying sun.
