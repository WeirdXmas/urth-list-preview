Sphinx: Mother's mother

2003-05-09 Marc Aramini #

Asks whether the Sphinx riddle about a daughter who gives birth to her mother, answered by night and day, could explain the line "I am your mother, and your mother's mother." Admits the riddle does not seem to fit the line, though the two seem similar, and says he remains confused.
questions · source-and-allusion · theories: Sphinx riddle answer night and day explains the line

I was thinking about "I am your mother, and your mother's mother". One riddle of the sphinx was "Which daughter gives birth to her mother in turn?" or something like that, and the answer was night and day. Pretty banal, but could that explain the line? It doesn't seem to for me, but it seems similar somehow. I remain confused by it.

Marc Aramini

2003-05-09 James Jordan #

Proposes that Latro's daily memory lapses give him a fuller alternation of day and night than other people have, and asks which god he offended and whether that god is connected to the sphinx. Responds to Marc Aramini's question about the line "I am your mother, and your mother's mother" by linking it to the sphinx riddle whose answer is night and day.
proposes · myth-and-structure · source-and-allusion · theories: Latro's daily amnesia alternates day and night; Latro's condition is set by an offended god

Could it be this: Latro's lapses of memory between each day provide for him a much fuller alternation of day and night than for other people. I don't remember very much from the books. Which god did Latro offend? Someone connected with the sphinx? This god/goddess would be the author of his alternating condition.
