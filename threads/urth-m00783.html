Sinew's parentage

Urth list · 14 messages · 2005-02-21 to 2005-02-24

2005urthThe Book of the Short SunPhilip Loguinovmournings gloryStone OxJames WynnChris PDan'l Danehy-OakesRoy C. Lackey
The thread: Readers debated who fathered Sinew in Wolfe's Long Sun books, with James Wynn proposing Marrow as father and later Silk, and Chris and Roy C. Lackey backing Marrow based on the text. Dispute centered on whether the Calde's Palace conception passage (pg 430 or 384) is in the text, with arguments over edition page numbers and Amazon search screenshots. Mournings glory offered a roman-a-clef reading tied to Wolfe's son, and Philip Loguinov argued Horn is an unreliable narrator who doesn't know. The thread ended in impasse with no proof offered, and Wynn left the question open.
  • James Wynn: Sinew is Marrow's son, later argued Silk fits better and conception may have been outside the Whorl
  • Chris: Horn doubted his paternity, and the Horn-Marrow-Nettle episode supports Marrow
  • mournings glory: Sinew's name fits butchery terms, Marrow may point to another crimelord, ending is a roman-a-clef clue
  • Roy C. Lackey: Sinew's parentage is stated in Exodus from the Long Sun at page 384
  • Philip Loguinov: Horn is an unreliable narrator and does not know when Sinew was conceived
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: Horn, Hoof, Hide and Sinew are animal parts a paper and ink maker would value

Themes: Unreliable narration, Fathers, mothers and children, Names and their meanings

2005-02-21 James Wynn #

Proposes that Sinew in the Long Sun books is Marrow's son, citing the name fit with Marrow, Marrow's bailout of Horn and Nettle, and Horn's unexplained resentment of Sinew. Follows up on Kieran's post by pointing to a textual parallel: Marrow tossing his cane into Horn's boat mirrors Sinew tossing his knife as Horn left Lizard Island.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Sinew is Marrow's son

In case Kieran's post doesn't "take" I'll offer this one.

Is Sinew be Marrow's son?

Consider:
1) Sinew doesn't "quite" fit with Horn's name. Not the way Hoof and Hide do. But it does work with Marrow.

2) Marrow bailed out Horn and Nettle when their farming venture failed, so they could get a fresh start. He didn't sell them into slavery as he did with others. What did Marrow get in return?

3) When Horn leaves New Viron in his boat, Marrow tosses his cane into the boat _just as Sinew tossed his knife_ as Horn left Lizard Island. I believe the text makes this precise comparison.

4) Horn harbors a distinct resentment for Sinew that is not explainable by any of Sinew's actions.

Well?

~ Crush

2005-02-21 Chris P replying to James Wynn #

Argues that Horn strongly suspected he was not Sinew's father, reading his ambivalence toward Sinew in The Book of the Long Sun as a sign of doubt about paternity. Supports the idea of Marrow's paternity from the Horn, Marrow and Nettle episode, while admitting no passage settles who Sinew's father is.
supports · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Marrow is Sinew's father

This isn't as good as a reasoned argument for the subject, but for what it's worth I got the same impression fairly directly - that is, the scenario with Horn/Marrow/Nettle had taken place, and that Horn strongly suspected he was not Sinew's father. I almost want to say "knew", but there's a sort of deep ambivalence in Horn about his own relationship with Sinew that gave me the impression that he had some doubts that he might have been Sinew's father after all.

I don't know that there's anything there that establishes beyond a doubt who Sinew's father is. It shouldn't matter, ultimately, and I don't think this is something Horn needs to discover because he knows it from the beginning; this isn't the only thread of the book which brings out a tension between knowing the right way to behave/live and actually living that way.

2005-02-22 mournings glory replying to James Wynn #

Disputes James Wynn's claim that Sinew is Marrow's son, arguing that the name Sinew fits a logical subset of butchery terms and that Marrow may instead point to another crimelord. Suggests the ending of Sinew is a roman-a-clef clue to Wolfe's estranged son, a reading recognizable to family and friends.
disputes · puzzle-solving · source-and-allusion · theories: Sinew is not Marrow's son; Marrow alludes to crimelord Blood; Sinew's ending encodes Wolfe's estranged son Matthew

Not sure I agree. Most cultures that process their own meat (as opposed to shopping at a supermarket) consider horns, hooves, hides (skin) and sinews (tendons) to be inedible; all, however, can be rendered down into gelatin. So "sinew" at least for me seems part of a logical subset of possible filial names.

"Marrow," on the other hand, may be meant to suggest another crimelord in another Viron; i.e., Blood.

The -ew ending of Sinew may be a Roman-a-clef-type-clue that he represents Wolfe's estranged son Matthew--something at least family members and close friends would recognize.

2005-02-23 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes replying to James Wynn #

Proposes that the names Horn, Hoof, Hide and Sinew, which Wolfe gives to the Long Sun and Short Sun characters, share a meaning: they are animal parts not usually eaten, and parts a maker of pen, ink and paper would value. Offers this word-association as a possible link to the parentage discussion in the thread, answering James Wynn's quoted remarks about page numbers and a passage.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Horn, Hoof, Hide and Sinew names refer to writing materials

Well, Horn, Hoof, Hide and Sinew are all not-usually-eatable parts of an animal (not considered "meat"). They're also all parts that would be considered important by a maker of pen, ink, and paper.

--Dan'l

But that doesn't surprise me in the least even if Sinew is not Horn's natural son, for reasons I'll be better able to explain if I can decide -- to the degree of certainty that Roy has -- who Sinew's bio-dad is.

"... Within that point of light [the Whorl] Sinew had been conceived, in all probability, in the Calde's Palace. It did not seem possible."

In all probability. It did not seem possible. Especially by wolfe standards, this doesn't seem like very good evidence to place Sinew's conception.
