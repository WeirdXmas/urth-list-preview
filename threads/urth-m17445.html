Wolfe and Gaiman

The thread: The thread began with whether calling Neil Gaiman a comics author is apt, with Son of Witz defending comics as a distinct visual art with its own craft problems and António Pedro Marques agreeing. Lee Berman pushed back, proposing "visual arts" instead and arguing Wolfe's immaterial elements such as Neighbors, Yesod and the Whorl Gods resist visual rendering. Marc Aramini questioned whether Wolfe's deliberate ambiguities can survive film without forcing a firm reading. Jeff Wilson argued the Naviscaput could be drawn convincingly by emphasizing its ship-like shape and hiding its limbs, citing Miyazaki. The posts end on the Naviscaput depiction question without a clear resolution.
  • Son of Witz: comics are a distinct visual art with their own craft problems, so calling Gaiman a comics author is apt
  • António Pedro Marques: agrees comics are a separate visual art form with craft problems unlike prose
  • Lee Berman: prefer "visual arts" to "comics"; Wolfe's immaterial elements resist visual rendering and the Naviscaput would look silly
  • Marc Aramini: Wolfe's ambiguities cannot be filmed without forcing an interpretation; asks whether descriptive contradictions are intentional
  • Jeff Wilson: the Naviscaput can be depicted convincingly by dropping anthropic features and stressing its ship-like shape

Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Suffering and sacrifice

2011-01-04 Son of Witz replying to António Pedro Marques #

Argues that calling Neil Gaiman a comics author is apt because comics are a distinct visual art form with craft problems unlike those of painting, film or the novel. Responds to Lee Berman's dismissal of comics as a minor art by drawing on the poster's own experience making comics pages.
disputes · genre-and-craft

I don't want to start ranting about comics, but calling him a "comics author" is very appropriate in that it highlights the particular problems of the craft. Comics are a distinct visual art form, with problems and solutions that are specific to it's form. The probelms of a painter, a film maker, or a novelist are very very different than the problems facing someone trying to tell a story in a serial juxtaposition of images into a complicated arrangement of sub-compositions. I've made a lot of comics pages, let me tell you that it busts my brain as much or more than trying to do computer programming. It can be very simple if your form and content work that way, sure, but to do something complicated...

2011-01-04 Lee Berman #

Proposes replacing the word "comics" with "visual arts" in the thread's praise of Gaiman, noting Gaiman's works reaching the big screen. Suggests Wolfe's fiction may resist film adaptation and argues Wolfe does not look down on work that translates well to visual media.
proposes

I'd like to suggest the adjective "comics" be changed to "visual arts" or something like that. Neil has had more than one of his works transferred (through an obviously very expensive process) to the big screen. After 40 years we may need to accept that Wolfe's work just isn't seen as amenable to this process.

I think it would be quite a mistake to think that Wolfe looks down his nose at authorial work which translates well to visual.

2011-01-04 Lee Berman replying to Lee Berman #

Clarifies that his objection to calling Wolfe and Gaiman differently was about breadth rather than elitism, and that visual accessibility is a general way to characterize their differences. Argues that Wolfe's immaterial or indeterminate elements such as Neighbors, Yesod, Brook Madregot and the Whorl Gods resist visual rendering, and that the Naviscaput would look silly in any drawing, responding to Antonio and Witz.
disputes · genre-and-craft

Quite the contrary! I unfortunately trend toward the mission of puncturing pomposity and elitism in the arts. A bit of artistic dignity is fine but as it increases it gradually morphs into those two balloons I always want to pop. I apologize and try to keep this failing of mine under control.

My objection was only in breadth not elitism of terms. Visual accessibility is just a general way I would characterize the differences between Wolfe's and Gaiman's work.

As Witz suggests, even the more prosaic aspects of Wolfe's work can be difficult to render in visual form. So how do you convey the idea of immaterial or indeterminite elements like Neighbors or Yesod or Brook Madregot or Whorl Gods? I mean, c'mon, the Naviscaput would end up looking awfully silly no matter who drew him...

2011-01-04 Jeff Wilson replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that a naviscaput, a huge sea creature, could be depicted convincingly if its anthropic features like hands and shoulders are dropped and its ship-like shape, wave distortion and limbs hidden under wreckage are emphasized. Offers as support the claim that several Miyazaki films portray similar giant creatures without looking silly.
proposes · genre-and-craft

A naviscaput needn't look any sillier than any other scary ship plying the waves; the key would be to avoid the anthropic elements like giant hands and shoulders and concentrate on the the ship-like structure above the waterline, the deformation of the waves and currents from the sheer bulk of the creature, and conceal the exact appearance of any limbs with muck, trash, and accumulated wreckage. Now that I think of it, several Miyazaki films have very unsilly execution of similar giant baddies.
