Name of the _Whorl_

Whorl list · 4 messages · 1998-05-14 to 1998-05-15

1998whorlThe Book of the Long SunThe Book of the New SunPeter WestlakeMichael Straight
The thread: Readers asked why the starship in the Long Sun books is called the Whorl. Michael Straight argued it names the spinning world the travelers inhabit, citing passages where 'whorl' stands where 'world' would be expected. Peter Westlake first asked the question and speculated about a link to a similarly named computer in another series, then proposed that the name draws on the cloud whorls of a typhoon, tying it to Typhon. Straight offered a tentative answer that Typhon named his artificial world for its spin, while conceding he might have been answering a different question about Wolfe's choice. The thread ended with the two broadly converging on the spinning-world explanation, without a settled answer on Wolfe's intent.
  • Michael Straight: Whorl names the spinning starship that is the travelers' whole world, supported by 'whorl' used for 'world' in the text
  • Michael Straight: Typhon named his artificial world for its spinning motion and the societies on it, though he may be answering a different question
  • Peter Westlake: Opened by asking why the ship is called Whorl and floated a speculative link to a computer of the same name, framed as unlikely
  • Peter Westlake: The name may come from the ship and from typhoon cloud whorls, tying it to Typhon and his empire

Themes: Names and their meanings, Buildings, cities and labyrinths, The dying sun and the cosmos

1998-05-14 Michael Straight #

Explains that the ship's name Whorl comes from the spinning starship being the travelers' entire world, which they call the Whorl. Supports this by citing passages where characters say whorl where world would be expected, such as the most beautiful woman in the whole Whorl and the Outsider existing outside the Whorl.
answers · close-textual-reading · theories: Whorl is the travelers' name for their spinning starship world

For the travelers, Starcrosser is their whole World, but since it spins, they call it the Whorl.

There are lots of passages where people use the same syntax as if they were going to say "world" but say "whorl" instead. Stuff like, "She's the most beautiful woman in the whole Whorl!" or "Unlike Pas and Scylla and the other gods, the Outsider exists outside the Whorl."

Oh my. "Two-headed **pho*"! I love it! Someone really needs to confront Wolfe with that one.

-Rostrum

1998-05-14 Peter Westlake #

Asks why the Whorl, a hollowed-out asteroid starship in the Long Sun books, has that name, and floats a speculative link to a computer of a similar name in another series. Frames the idea as improbable, with jokes about a two-headed owner and a tea-making scene involving Silk and Pike's ghost.
questions · theories: Whorl's name links to a computer in another series

Is there any special reason for the _Whorl_'s name?
It doesn't seem like an obvious choice for a hollowed-out asteroid.

The only idea I have about this is so infinitely improbable that I really hope someone will tell me it can't be true ... it's a starship run by computers, so I wonder if it's related to the ship's computer with the very similar name in a series almost, but not quite, entirely unlike Wolfe? On a ship owned by the two-headed **pho*?

Was Silk making a really hot cup of tea when Pike's ghost appeared?

-- Spectacled Bear, aghast at finding how well this hangs together, except for the Cup of Tea, which was a Joke.

1998-05-15 Michael Straight #

Offers a tentative answer to why the Whorl is called that, suggesting Typhon named his artificial world for its spinning motion and the societies built on it. Replies to Peter Westlake's question about the name, admitting that it may have been answering a different question about Wolfe's choice rather than Typhon's.
answers · close-textual-reading · theories: Typhon named the Whorl for its spinning motion

Hmm. Maybe I was answering the question "Why did Wolfe name it 'Whorl' than why did Typhon name it 'Whorl'. But maybe not. Typhon knew he was creating an artificial world, complete with artificial societies and religions, and it whorls around, so thus 'Whorl'.

-Rostrum

1998-05-15 Peter Westlake #

Suggests that the name Whorl for the world may derive from the ship's name and from the whorls of cloud around a typhoon, linking it to Typhon and his empire. Responds to Rostrum's remark on the travelers' use of 'whorl' where 'world' would be expected, building on Peter Westlake's earlier point about the word's usage in the Long Sun books.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Whorl named after typhoon cloud whorls; Whorl linked to Typhon and his empire

That's a natural confusion arising from the name, though, surely? I wondered why the ship had been called "Whorl" in the first place. Of course, you get whorls of cloud around a typhoon. Maybe that's it: an offshoot of Typhon and his empire.

Spectacled bear.
