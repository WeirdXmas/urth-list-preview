Sev: Satisfactory Conciliator?

Urth list · 2 messages · 1999-11-23 to 1999-11-24

1999urthThe Book of the New SunNicholas GeversJames Jordan

Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Christ figures and messiahs

1999-11-23 Nicholas Gevers #

Argues that Canog, as propagandist, shaped Severian's prison account into a persuasive prophetic book that turns a few random miracles into the legend of the Conciliator. Grounds this in the parallel with the Gospels and Wolfe's apparent point about writing outlasting mere action.
proposes · author-and-biography · myth-and-structure · theories: Canog's book shaped Severian's legend into prophecy

With regard to the question of how satisfactory Sev's
career in the Age of the Monarch is as the basis for
the legend of the Conciliator: one mustn't forget
Canog's role as propagandist. His BOTNS, based on
Sev's account of the future given in prison, might be
a very skilful work of prophetic literature--as
persuasive as the Gospels have been, perhaps. By this
means, a few random miracles could acquire great
resonance--a comment by Wolfe on the power of the
written word, which records, and supersedes by
outlasting, mere actions.
