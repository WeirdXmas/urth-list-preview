The thread: Posters debated how The Fifth Head of Cerberus is structured and whether it has a single solution like a detective novel. Maru Dubshinki argued the book is a Möbius band whose true ending lies at 719 degrees, past a fake ending at 359 degrees, citing Gödel, Escher, Bach and asking how a reader can be sure they reached the real solution. Jim Raylor agreed the book escapes the whodunnit problem because the Möbius structure has no fixed last page, offering no textual evidence and withholding his solution. Nathan Spears complained that Raylor's reply was unclear and asked posters to proofread, offering no reading of the novel. The thread ended in an unresolved exchange with no agreement on the book's ending.

2005-02-02 Maru Dubshinki replying to Jim Raylor #

Argues that The Fifth Head of Cerberus is shaped like a Möbius band, so its true ending lies 719 degrees along the loop, past a tonic-like fake ending at 359 degrees. Responds to Jim Raylor's detective-novel analogy, citing Gödel, Escher, Bach for the idea of a tonic that fails to return to the home key, and asks how a reader can be sure they have reached the real solution.
proposes · myth-and-structure · puzzle-solving · theories: The Fifth Head of Cerberus is shaped like a Möbius band; Real ending lies 719 degrees along the loop; A tonic fake ending occurs at 359 degrees

In a moebius loop, you must go through 720 degrees to return from whence uou began, because one loop is 360 degrees, and you are flipped on the first loop, it takes a second. So the last degree is located precisely 719 degrees from the begining degree. There is a fake ending, when you reach 359 degrees, a tonic which fools you perhaps but doesn't return you to the key you began in (read 'Godel Escher Bach:...' if that makes no sense to you.). What makes you so sure you found the home key, the skeleton key, teh real solution?

~Maru

2005-02-03 Jim Raylor #

Argues that The Fifth Head of Cerberus gets around the usual whodunnit problem because its Möbius-band structure means there is no fixed last page to reveal the ending. Offers no specific evidence from the text and withholds the solution, linking to an outside page and playfully citing The Prisoner.
proposes · genre-and-craft

A detective novel in which the first few pages have
been removed by a sharp edge?
No you are holding it upside down.
When GW wrote 5HC he got around the usual problem with
whodunnits.
Once you reach the final pages you know what happened
and (if it is good) you will never re-read it again
without knowing the ending.
But what is the last page in a novel shaped like a
moebius band?
You find it by a process of eliminaton and relaxation.
I don't want to spoil it for others.
