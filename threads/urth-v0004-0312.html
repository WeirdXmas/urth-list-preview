Lucius' language

Urth list · 1 messages · 1997-09-17 to 1997-09-17

1997urthThe Book of the New Sunxury

Themes: Books, libraries and writing

1997-09-17 xury #

Asks how Lucius can read the Greek journal entries when he barely speaks Greek, and notes Wolfe's choice of archaic Latin as Lucius's written language. Grounds this in a Soldier of Arete scene where a character remarks on the Roman's letter A shaped like an ox's head, and adds a side question about Hethor calling Severian the red-clawed master.
questions · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading

The introduction to "Soldier of the Mist" says that archaic Latin is the written language of Lucius, and Greek is used is some places where others have written in the journal (ie Eurykles and Pindaros). However this brings up the subject of how Lucius reads these entries when he cannot read Greek, he barely speaks it, accordingly to himself. The way Wolfe has set up this whole language conception is interesting because in "Soldier of Arete" Lucius meets someone who comments that the Roman writes his letter A like " A ox's head." Which, in fact, was how the Egyptians and Semites wrote their A. But, Wolfe does say to us that Lucius uses Archaic Latin and not Latin as we know it now (Which developed around 114 AD). Oh, and Lucius' friend who took him home were Crimson Men, of whom Lucius was not of nationality, he just knew their language.
Xury

PS Why is Severian called the red-clawed master by Hethor? (One example is at The Claw of the Conciliator, ch 8, p288 of The Shadow & The Claw.)
