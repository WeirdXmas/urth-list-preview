DOORS: Tina's origins

Urth list · 3 messages · 2003-01-27 to 2003-01-28

2003urthRoy C. LackeyPeter Westlake
The thread: Spectacled Bear asked whether Tina in There Are Doors was built by someone in the story's world rather than being magic, citing Green's remark about robots and answering Mantis's magic-doll idea with pages 228 and 270, then asked how Tina knows about Daddies. Roy C. Lackey argued that children in the Otherworld were given dolls and had no daddy, so Tina's limited doll memory could not hold knowledge of a father, citing her calling Klamm "Papa" in the limousine. Spectacled Bear replied that Klamm may be Green's father and tied this to the Attis legend and death-and-rebirth cycles, raising the problem that Lara's lovers cannot all be Attis reincarnations if Green and Klamm coexist. The thread ends with the Attis reading left open and no consensus reached.

  • Roy C. Lackey: Tina's Papa remark cannot come from an Otherworld doll's memory since children there had no daddies
  • Mantis: Tina may be a magic doll (mentioned only as the suggestion being answered)

Themes: Robots, androids and artificial life, Fathers, mothers and children, Myth and folklore sources

2003-01-27 Peter Westlake #

Proposes that Tina in DOORS may have been built by someone in the story's world rather than being magic, citing Green's lunch remark about robots. Answers Mantis's magic-doll suggestion by quoting pages 228 and 270, then asks how Tina knows about Daddies, a question the poster says has not been addressed before.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Tina was built by someone in the story's world

The lady in the haberdasher's called Tina a "magic doll", and in a world with a real goddess that has to be a possibility, as Mantis suggested. But at lunch with Lora, Green talks about how Tina isn't really magic, and that Heathkit will sell you a robot and the Air Force have planes that can fly and fight and land themselves. "I couldn't build her, and I don't know anybody who could. But somebody here might be able to, if we put our minds to it." (p270)

Maybe they did?

I know it doesn't work if the time differential is constant. I know artificial intelligence isn't as easy as it seemed, and it never seemed very easy - though from the point of view of a mechanical engineer, in the late Eighties, it might seem possible enough to allow willing suspension of disbelief. I can believe it, for a story.

But the really interesting question about Tina, that I have not seen addressed before, is: how does she know about Daddies? For his birthday, "Didn't Mama and Daddy give you anything?" (p228) Green hasn't seen his father for years, but Tina says his father still loves him. "I know about Daddies, though. And you don't." And so on. Where in the world - in which world - did she learn about Daddies?

Spectacled Bear.

2003-01-28 Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that Tina, the doll-like child in There Are Doors, cannot have learned about fathers from her original Otherworld life, since children there were given dolls and had no daddy. Grounds this on the passage where Tina calls Klamm "Papa" in the limousine and on the idea that a mechanical doll's limited memory would not hold recollections of a person the child could never know. Responds to Spectacled Bear's question about how Tina knows about Daddies.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Tina's memories come from a doll with limited memory; Tina's origins lie in the Otherworld dolls

And just to confuse things further; when they were in the limo Tina said to Klamm, speaking of Green: "He needs your help, Papa." Klamm matter-of-factly answers her: "Then he shall haff it. Whatever I can give." (300) Why did Tina call him "Papa"?

Almost by definition, children in the Otherworld to whom the dolls were given wouldn't have a daddy. Given the necessarily limited amount of memory available in a mechanical doll, I don't think it would be expended on a person the child could never know.

-Roy

2003-01-28 Peter Westlake #

Speculates that Tina calls Klamm "Papa" because Klamm may be Green's father, linking this to the Attis legend of a son of Cybele and death-and-rebirth cycles. Raises the problem that if Lara's lovers are all Attis reincarnated, Green and Klamm cannot coexist, and questions why Green recalls a sea-captain life.
proposes · source-and-allusion · myth-and-structure · theories: Klamm is Green's father; Lara's lovers are Attis reincarnated; Green's sea-captain memories are a previous life

I think she simply recognized him as such - but was it only because he was acting in a helpful and fatherly manner, or is he really Green's father? Some versions of the legend of Attis have him being the son of Cybele as part of a cycle of death and rebirth. This fits with Green having memories of Billy Hurst's life come back to him. But if all Lara's lovers are Attis reincarnated, it's hard to see how there can be two of them around at the same time in Green and Klamm. But if they aren't (and the story really doesn't seem to be constructed that way), why does Green feel that he is remembering a previous life as a sea captain?

That's right. Whereas if it was manufactured here, the manufacturers might well not be in on the secret of the existence of the Otherworld, and would program the doll for life in our world.

Spectacled Bear.
