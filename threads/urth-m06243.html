2008-06-18 Duncan Truter #

Argues that the word "isangoma" in Wolfe's text is a real-world term for a South African shaman, not a Wolfe coinage, so the jungle hut scene must be set in Earth's past. Supports the view with the word's everyday meaning and the air/aeroplane details, responding to Jeff Wilson's claim that the hut indicates Christ existed in Urth's past.
disputes · source-and-allusion · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Isangoma scene is set in Earth's past

I get the urth list in a daily digest so I'll quote what I'm replying to. the quotes may be a bit out.
"

IIRC we established that there is no picture of the original moon landing that matches the composition described. It's either a mistake on Wolfe's part, a painting (perhaps by Michael Collins or Alan Bean) rather than a photograph, or a hint that Urth and Earth have divergent histories.
"
I don't know about you lot, but the word "Isangoma" is a totally everyday word to me that means shaman/witch doctor
(http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangoma). It's not a Wolfe word, it's a real life word from now. Wolfe must have intended this scene to be in our past (early/mid 1900s, if I recall the air/aeroplane correctly). I think Isangoma is too specific to not mean what I think it means.

Duncan
