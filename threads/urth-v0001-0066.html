Apu's Eclipse

Urth list · 5 messages · 1997-05-18 to 1997-05-20

1997urthThe Book of the New SunReuschMichael Andre-DriussiJoel PriddyDerek Bell
The thread: Readers asked what caused the delayed sunrise and wrong-season stars at Apu Punchau's eclipse in The Book of the New Sun. Joel Priddy proposed either Severian's time magic or the Hierodules' ship reflecting a different sky, and doubted the scene answers itself. m.driussi and others argued the arriving hierodule ship is the vessel that opens The Urth of the New Sun rather than the time craft over Baldanders' tower, and Derek Bell explained that eclipses let wrong-season stars show. Reusch questioned whether the sequel confirmed Tzadkiel's ship as the eclipsing object, and Priddy later asked why Tzadkiel's ship would cause it, offering readings about protection, theological presence, or a plot point. The thread drifted toward consensus that Tzadkiel made the eclipse, with the symbolic meaning left open.
  • Joel Priddy: eclipse caused by Severian's time magic or the Hierodules' reflecting ship, and the answer is not self-contained
  • m.driussi: the arriving ship is the vessel opening The Urth of the New Sun, not the time craft
  • Reusch: asks whether the sequel confirmed Tzadkiel's ship as the eclipsing object, from memory
  • Derek Bell: the ship is the one from The Urth of the New Sun, and wrong-season stars show only during eclipses
  • Joel Priddy: asks why Tzadkiel's ship causes the eclipse and offers three symbolic readings

Themes: Time loops and causality, Gods, angels and the Increate, The dying sun and the cosmos

1997-05-18 Joel Priddy #

Proposes two explanations for the delayed sunrise and wrong-season stars at Apu Punchau's eclipse in The Book of the New Sun: Severian working time magic, or the Hierodules' ship reflecting a different sky. Grounds both in the scene's details, the Afterword's rejected causes, and the Hierodules' knowledge of Severian's stories, while doubting that the answer is self-contained.
proposes · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Severian caused the eclipse with time magic; The Hierodules' reflective ship caused the eclipse

alga,
Thanks for the pointer towards Peter Carey. I haven't read him, but there's a copy of Tristan Smith just sitting on the library shelf, waiting for me.
BTW, I very much enjoyed History of Hell. Write lots more, okay?

Tusk,
ditto re: the Pastel City. Turns out that the local university library has a copy. I wonder if my old card will still work?

Okay, I will now proceed to distance myself from any
non-Wolfe-writer threads until I've heard from on-high that it's permitted on this list. Back to the matters at hand. I thought I'd do a little thinking-out-loud on the subject of Apu Panchau's eclipse.

----------SPOILERS-------------

--> Okay, we know that Severian's life is spared by the fact that the sunrise is delayed one morning when he is held by the autocthons.
--> During this extended night Severian sees stars from the wrong season. Stars he'd expect to see for that season where he in his native time, but this is presumably before the Urth's axis-shift. --> When the sun does appear, it has already risen a considerable distance.
--> We are told in the afterword that the plausible cause is an eclipse of some sort, having discounted halting the Urth's rotation as too drastic, and mass-hypnosis as too pat.
--> Severian has a certain amount of control of time and space which seems to manifest itself most potently when he feels himself to be in dire need.
--> Severian has three friends, the Hierodules, who live backwards through time, and own a spaceship. The Hierodules are attentive listeners, and have drawn conclusions from Severians past stories, including the one that led them to find him among the autocthons.

Two conclusions that seem plausible from this are:
(1)Severian worked some sorta time-hoodoo, but made a mistake because the fact of the axis-shift hadn't quite sunk into his head yet.

Maybe he took the whole village through time, maybe he just opened up a passage in the Corridors of Time to looked in on the night sky of a different era (can other people see into such openings? Could Severian see the Green Man's passage?)
Severian may or may not have enough power to accomplish such a task. He had enough power to get to this point in the past in the first place, but he seems to be of limited power several decades later when the villagers again threaten his life. I would definitely try to confine thoughts on Severian as the source of the eclipse to things he could have done right at that moment, as opposed to thinking about what he might go back in time, after the fact, to set up. For one reason, I think at any later point Severian would not have made the seasonal-astronomy mistake. For a second reason, it's a little too "Bill and Ted's Bogus Journey."

or

(2)The Hierodules ship is highly reflective, so that when they position it between the village and the sun, it reflects the night sky from a different angle, thereby showing a different season's sky.

Are there any previous descriptions of the Hierodule ship (and would it necessarily be the same ship they'd have in the remote past?) or any other evidence that they might have a reflective ship? Or a ship that could make itself reflective if needed (and it certainly would be a nice feature on a space craft)? Would light sails be as reflective as would be needed to produce the described eclipse? If they were, I think Severian wouldn't have described them as being fuligin and silver, but as mis-matched pieces of night sky. But then, maybe that's what he meant (I'd read it as the sails being fuligin on one side, and silver on the other).

The thing that gets me about this is: if Wolfe bothers to tease us with this event in the Afterward, I can't help but feel that the solution must point towards some other unstated plot point. Therefore, I find the two above conclusions suspect because they're so self-contained.

Anyone have any thoughts?

-cephalothorax-

1997-05-19 Derek Bell #

Agrees with the quoted poster that the hierodule's ship is the vessel that opens The Urth of the New Sun, and explains that the stars wrong for the season are visible only during eclipses when the Sun's glare no longer drowns them. Replies to the quoted post by Mantis and also addresses Tercel with a friendly aside about astronomy.
supports · close-textual-reading · theories: hierodule's ship is the vessel opening Urth of the New Sun

I'd assumed it was that vessel too - the stars that are wrong for the season are wrong because they are normally drowned in the glare of the Sun - they wouldn't be visible for another few months in the future or past, except during eclipses.

1997-05-20 Joel Priddy #

Asks why Tzadkiel's ship causes the eclipse in Apu's time, offers three possible readings: a statement that Severian remains under Tzadkiel's protection, a sign of theological presence in the far past, or a hidden plot point. Builds on the thread's consensus that Tzadkiel made the eclipse, and admits his astronomical ignorance while asking for an explanation of the symbolism.
questions · close-textual-reading · theories: Tzadkiel's ship causes Apu's eclipse; Ship's eclipse signals Severian's continued protection

Okay, consensus seems to be... Tzadkeil as eclipse-maker. Makes plenty sense to me, and I appreciate the more astronomy-savvy amongst us filling me in on the whole
you-can-see-stars-you-wouldn't-normally-see-during-an-eclipse thing.
So, just to plumb the depths of my ingnorance a little further... Why is Tzadkiel's ship causing eclipses? We know that it doesn't usually come any further in-system than the ring of Dis. Is it to make a dramatic statement of the fact that Severian is still under Tzadkiel's protective wing (so to speak)? To illustrate the fact that it, and all it's theological implications, is still a presence in this far-remote past? Or does it refer to some specific or implied plot point I'm missing out on?

-cephalothorax-
