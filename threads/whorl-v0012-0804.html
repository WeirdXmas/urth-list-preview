A Mania for Conversations

Whorl list · 4 messages · 2001-06-10 to 2001-06-11

2001whorlThe Book of the Long SunSteve StricklandMichael Straight
The thread: Michael Straight proposed that Wolfe's elliptical style comes from a preference for writing conversations over describing actions, linking this to Horn's remark about a mania for recording conversations in The Book of the Long Sun. Steve Strickland disputed this, arguing the remark is a self-deprecating joke by Wolfe answering critics who found the Long Sun books too dialogue-heavy, with the blame placed on the narrator Horn. Straight later jokingly agreed with Strickland, saying he only grasped the point once Wolfe pointed it out, but offered no new evidence. The thread ended in agreement by joke rather than resolution of the ellipsis question.

  • Steve Strickland: Horn's remark is Wolfe's self-deprecating joke answering critics of too much dialogue
  • Michael Straight: later jokingly concedes Strickland's reading, citing Wolfe's own pointing-out, without new evidence

Themes: Unreliable narration, Books, libraries and writing, Humour in Wolfe

2001-06-10 Michael Straight #

Proposes that Gene Wolfe's elliptical style comes from a preference for writing conversations over describing actions, linking this to Horn's remark about a mania for recording conversations in The Book of the Long Sun. Suggests that narrative gaps occur when a conversation is interrupted by an event, which is then skipped in favour of another conversation that only alludes to it.
proposes · close-textual-reading · genre-and-craft · theories: Wolfe's ellipsis stems from preferring conversation to action

Horn's statement about having a mania for recording coversations struck me as a key insight into Wolfe's writing. Could it be that Wolfe's eliptical style is largely based on a preference for writing conversations rather than describing actions? I'd have to do some more reading with this idea in mind, but I have the impression that quite a lot of the gaps in Wolfe's narratives come when a conversation is interrupted by some event, and we skip the event for another conversation in which someone may or may not describe or allude to the event itself.

-Rostrum

2001-06-10 Steve Strickland #

Argues that Horn's remark about a mania for recording conversations is a self-deprecating joke by Wolfe answering critics who said his Long Sun books had too much dialogue and too little description. Grounds this in the assumption that the later Long Sun volumes drew complaints about narrative sections, reading the remark as a playful nod to those critics.
disputes · author-and-biography · theories: Horn's conversation remark is Wolfe's joke at critics

I took Horn's statement as a bit of self-deprecating humor on the part of Mr. Wolfe. No doubt as a result of the extensive narrative sections in the latter volumes of Long Sun, Wolfe received more than a few comments like, "Can't you write anything but dialogue?" or "Can't you pepper the dialogue with a little more description?" (And I'm sure he received less critical observations regarding the same.)

As I see it, Horn's statement is Wolfe's playful nod to the critics (while playfully placing the blame on the narrator-character, Horn.)

Very cute.
