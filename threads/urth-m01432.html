2005-03-24 David DiGiacomo replying to Dayton Williams #

Recommends that a newcomer read The Urth of the New Sun first and then consult two reference works, Lexicon Urthus and Solar Labyrinth, noting the first is out of print and can be obtained by interlibrary loan. Responds to Dayton Williams's request for a FAQ on The Book of the New Sun, offering reading advice instead of a FAQ.
answers

1. Read "The Urth of the New Sun", if you haven't already.

2. Read the following:
Michael Andre-Driussi, "Lexicon Urthus"
Robert Borski, "Solar Labyrinth"

The first one is out of print. I was able to get both through inter-library loan with no problem.

2005-03-28 Adam Stephanides replying to Dayton Williams #

Advises a newcomer to search the list archives and recommends Lexicon Urthus with its errata pamphlets, while warning that Castle of Days and Borski's Solar Labyrinth are of limited use. Responds to Dayton Williams's request for a FAQ by noting none exists and arguing that no reference work will reveal what The Book of the New Sun is really about.
answers

[snip]

Did you investigate the archives? While their size can be overwhelming, there's a lot of useful stuff there, much of it by people who no longer post here (such as the authors of the two books David DiGiacomo recommended). Unfortunately, there's no FAQ dealing with BotNS as far as I know.

As for the suggestions so far, _Castle of Days_ won't be of much help. I haven't read _Solar Labyrinth_, but based upon Borski's former postings to this list, I'd take his theories with a grain of salt (that's only my opinion, though). _Lexicon Urthus_ (and the three pamphlets of "Additions, Errata, &cetera" if you can get hold of them) compile a lot of information about BotNS into an easy-to-read format, but won't tell you "what it's all about." Nor will anything else. Despite all the intelligent people who have been studying BotNS for two decades, "what it's really about" remains a mystery, and the same is true of Wolfe's other books.

--Adam
