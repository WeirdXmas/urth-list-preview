Musings on the Hierodule fieldguide

Urth list · 4 messages · 1998-09-02 to 1998-09-05

1998urthThe Book of the New SunCharles DyeC R CulverWilliam H. Ansley
The thread: The thread began with CRCulver speculating that Father Inire resembles Toth or an ibis, asking about his lifespan after Severian sailed to Yesod, and wondering whether the fantassins on the time saucer subdued Baldanders. The discussion quickly turned to a joke comparing Inire to Lewis Carroll, which a poster read as an accusation of child abuse. William Ansley clarified that Dodgson did not molest children and that the joke was not meant literally. Charles Dye argued Inire is unlike Carroll in manner yet shares mirrors and little girls with him, and pointed to 'pleasance' as a further Carrollian link. Ansley agreed a Carroll connection exists in the Book of the New Sun and explained he had flagged a possible misreading, so the thread ended on a shared, mostly amicable reading of the Carroll link.
  • CRCulver: Inire resembles Toth or an ibis; questions his lifespan and whether the fantassins defeated Baldanders
  • William H. Ansley: Carroll joke was not literal; Dodgson did not molest children, but a Carroll link exists in the text
  • Charles Dye: Inire is unlike Carroll in manner, yet mirrors and little girls link them; 'pleasance' is a Carrollian hint

Themes: Mirrors, pictures and art, Names and their meanings, Time loops and causality

1998-09-02 C R Culver #

Speculates that Father Inire, the hunched Hierodule, resembles Toth or an ibis, and jokes about his conduct with exultantesses. Raises questions about Inire's lifespan after Severian sailed to Yesod, and asks whether the fantassins aboard the time saucer are the ones who subdued Baldanders at the House Absolute.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · close-textual-reading · theories: Father Inire resembles Toth as an ibis totem; Fantassins on the time saucer match those who subdued Baldanders

<???--Father Inire. He's a hunched little guy, a Daedalus or Hephaestus or Toth, and since Toth is the most ancient I tend to think of Inire as being something like Toth's totem, the ibis, a bent neck little bird hobbling around, finding treasures in the muck. (Of course, he's also Lewdis Carnal, Victorian child pornographer, hunched over his primitive camera obscena. <g>)>

Happily, Inire seems to be just enamoured by little exultantesses, and there is no evidence of his actually molesting them. Boy, wouldn't that be an embarassment to the reign of the Autarch Severian.
I'm sure Inire has a longer lifespan than other Hierodules, considering he easily surivived ~2 chiliads as grand vizier (but what happened to him after Severian sailed to Yesod???)
Mantis compares Inire to a bird, but I believe the monkey comparison is used more than once in the ol' manuscripts

<There are more Hierodules onboard the time saucer. Dr. Talos wants them to come out and blast/scare the natives.>

Do you mean the so-called "fantassins?" Fantassination is one of the least- discussed aspects of Hierodulic technology, and I wish Wolfe would share more. Perhaps the fantassins about the Ship of the Hierodules are the same who tried to subdue Baldanders at the House Absolute.

1998-09-04 Charles Dye #

Argues that Father Inire in 'Father Inire's Mirrors' is unlike Lewis Carroll in manner, since he takes Domnina away, frightens her and speaks general relativity, yet mirrors and little girls still link them. Responds to William Ansley's quoted joke about Dodgson by pointing to the coy drop of the word 'pleasance' as a further Carrollian link.
proposes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Father Inire is modelled on Lewis Carroll

In fact, Inire seems decidedly un-Carrollian in the "Father Inire's Mirrors" episode. He takes Domnina away without her parents' permission; he frightens her; his explanations seem to confuse rather than educate. He speaks general relativity, rather than charming nonsense.

Still, the connection is definitely there. Mirrors and little girls. And notice how coyly Father Inire drops the name "pleasance?"

1998-09-05 William H. Ansley #

Agrees that a Carroll connection exists in The Book of the New Sun, citing the Cat episode and Father Inire's mirrors, and explains that he had flagged a possible misreading of an earlier joke about Lewis Carroll. Responds to Charles Dye's remarks on Inire's un-Carrollian behavior and mirrors and little girls, and to the list's exchange over Christopher Culver's post.
supports

I certainly don't deny the Carroll connection, I noticed it myself on my first reading of TBotNS and especially "The Cat".

Even though I was quite sure that Christopher R. Culver <[email]> was just joining in on mantis's joke about Lewis Carroll and that everyone reading the list would understand this, I thought there was just the tiniest possibility for confusion, which was why I spoke up (thus revealing a hypersensitivity to possibly harmful misunderstandings that Charles Dodgson himself could probably have appreciated).

(BTW, I accept your apology mantis, but no real offense was taken.)

William Ansley
