The Green Man and Agia

Urth list · 3 messages · 1999-06-01 to 1999-06-02

1999urthThe Book of the New SunNicholas GeversPeter StephensonPeter Westlake
The thread: Nicholas Gevers argued that the Green Man's prophecy that Severian will find Agia above ground is deliberately ambiguous, suggesting the woman he seeks underground may be Agia rather than the dead Thecla, citing the search in the caverns and Thecla's alzabo resurrection within Severian. Peter Stephenson replied that the Green Man is a fraud at predicting personal histories and that no one in the book has genuine supernatural sight, listing time-travellers and magic mirrors as the only true foreseers. Peter Westlake then asked how the Vatic Fountain works, whether it truly prophesies or only shows images readers interpret afterward, and whether Severian projects his own foreknowledge onto it. The thread ends with the positions stated but no resolution.
  • Nicholas Gevers: the Green Man's prophecy is a deliberate ambiguity, and the woman underground may be Agia, not Thecla
  • Peter Stephenson: the Green Man is a fraud and no genuine prophets exist in the book apart from time-travel and mirrors
  • Peter Westlake: the Vatic Fountain may show images that only make sense in hindsight, and Severian may be projecting foreknowledge onto it

Themes: Gods, angels and the Increate, Memory and forgetting, Free will and choice

1999-06-01 Nicholas Gevers #

Argues that the Green Man's prophecy about Severian finding Agia above ground is deliberately ambiguous, reading it as a warning that the woman Severian seeks underground is Agia rather than the dead Thecla. Grounds this in the sequence after the showman's tent, where Severian searches the caverns for Thecla and Agia waits outside, and in the alzabo resurrection of Thecla within Severian.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Green Man's prophecy warns Agia is the woman Severian seeks; Thecla is resurrected through alzabo ritual within Severian

I was a little surprised to read of people stumped by the precise (or deeper) meaning of the green man's prophecy of Severian finding Agia above ground. I've thought all along that there is a typical Wolfean ambiguity here: soon after the scene in the showman's tent, Severian searches for Thecla underground; as a genuine prophet, the green man is a) warning Severian that the woman he will in fact be searching for is not the dead Thecla, but Agia (the true author of "Thecla's letter"), who is still alive (unburied, above ground) and will be waiting in ambush for Severian outside the caverns (above ground); and b) that the woman he will believe he is searching for, Thecla, after proving not to be in the caverns (below ground) will be resurrected through the alzabo ritual as part of Severian (again, alive, above ground).

1999-06-02 Peter Stephenson #

Questions whether Wolfe's Book of the New Sun contains any genuine prophets, arguing the green man is a fraud at predicting personal histories and that no one has supernatural sight. Responds to Nicholas Gevers's claim that the green man's prophecy about Agia is a genuine Wolfean ambiguity, citing Severian's search for Thecla and listing time-travellers and magic mirrors as the only foreseeing figures.
questions · close-textual-reading · theories: The green man is a fraud as a prophet

I don't want to drag this out into a pointless argument over points of view (viewless argument over views of points?) [I was arguing the green man, although he comes from the future, is a fraud when it comes to `predicting' people's personal histories], but... are there really genuine prophets in the Book? There are people who've seen the future, and can report on their own experience there; there are even beings who live backward in time; there are people with magic mirrors who can see things which may or not be there. But it doesn't seem to me there is anyone with supernatural sight. It would seem to violate the `no magic' rule. I'd be fascinated to hear a counterexample.

corncrake

1999-06-02 Peter Westlake #

Asks how the Vatic Fountain in The Book of the New Sun works, whether it truly prophesies Severian's future or only shows images that readers must interpret afterward. Raises whether it would work for anyone else, and whether Severian projects his own foreknowledge onto it, in reply to a quoted argument that the green man is no genuine prophet.
questions · theories: Severian projects his own foreknowledge onto the Vatic Fountain

...

Not a person, but what about the Vatic Fountain? It shows Severian all sorts of things from his future. How does it work? Does it just show lots of things and let you interpret them after the fact, or can it really prophesy? Would it work for anyone else? If not, how does it know who's looking at it? Or is Severian projecting his own foreknowledge of the future (if any) onto it?

SBear.
