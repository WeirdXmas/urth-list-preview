Themes: Robots, androids and artificial life

2001-06-27 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that the soulist reading of Maytera Rose's absorption into Maytera Marble is more fundamental to Wolfe than the materialist reading, though the two can coexist. Responds to Paul C Duggan's remarks on the alzabo feast and Westwind, citing the Kafkaesque milieu of "Detective of Dreams" and noting that Wolfe's stories are not technological problem-solving.
disputes · theology-and-religion · genre-and-craft · theories: Maytera Rose's soul resides in Maytera Marble; Soulist reading is more fundamental than materialist reading; Soulist and materialist readings are not mutually exclusive

Is it just me, or would Vodalian Alzabo Feast make a great name for a band?

Oh, dear... "shot down" is just too harsh. "Disagreed
with," please.

Or if I did shot you down I apologize.

H'mmm. Some clarity here: I don't for a moment suppose
that the two readings are mutually exclusive. What I do
believe is that many materialists _do_ think they are.
(This is somewhat analogous to the way many conservative Christians believe that the account in Genesis and the
Standard Model are mutually exclusive.)

In particular: I would say that the soulist approach is
more fundamental to a clear reading of Wolfe than is the materialist approach, but that Wolfe -- partially because he _is_ an engineer/tech writer at heart -- also likes to provide engineerish mechanisms by which the soulish
events occur.

Yes, a favorite Wolfe short of mine (after "Detective of Dreams"). But I should say it's really, really important to keep in mind that the milieu of that story bears the
same kind of relationship to our world that the milieu
of Kafka's novels does, not the kind of relationship a
standard SF universe does. The Lupiverse, I should say,
is somewhere in between.

That, and the fact that his stories aren't generally about problems to be solved by technological means.

--Blattid
