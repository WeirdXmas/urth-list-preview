2013-09-09 Marc Aramini replying to Gerry Quinn #

Agrees with Gerry Quinn that foreign-language covers of Wolfe's books are often better than the English ones, and praises the Spanish title Puertas over There Are Doors. Mentions that Japanese covers have interesting elements, though they are not accurate to the books, and states a preference for covers that avoid landscape imagery.
supports · genre-and-craft

I agree in general. Puertas is so much better than "there are doors". Have you seen the Japanese covers? While not accurate there is some interesting stuff going on in those ones. I favor the covers that don't go for landscape, though.

2013-09-09 Marc Aramini replying to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Comments that the poster could imagine ending up with a clumsy rustic cover like those on Robert Jordan books, and argues that literary mainstream book design is generally far better than genre covers. Responds to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's quoted note about a Donaldson-derived cover, and cites Abercrombie's gilt papyrus-look covers as a lucky exception.
chats

I can imagine writing something and then winding up with one of those awful rustic Jordan covers where the people aren't even proportioned like people. Literary main stream book design is vastly superior to most genre covers, in general. Abercrombie got lucky with his papyrus looking gilt covers for the first law trilogy.

> One thing I loved about the Merril Collection was its extensive holdings of Gene Wolfe material, including many of the limited/special editions produced by small presses. As I was sifting through some photos recently, I thought I'd post a few here, for those readers and collectors interested in the bibliographic side of Wolfe's work.
>
> ... I took the time, while I was at the collection, to check out some of the hard-to-find (and now incredibly expensive) printings of some of Wolfe's shorter fiction, such as this stunning embroidered and illustrated copy of Empires of Foliage and Flower (Cheap Street, 1987):
>
> ... Hidden in the basement of the Lillian H. Smith branch is the Merril Collection's store room, which contains many shelves of uncatalogued foreign language materials, including many foreign language Wolfe books donated by Wolfe himself, who has visited and spoken at the Merril Collection several times. I found these very interesting for the different cover art they used - I've noticed that there is almost no consistency between how different cover artists depict Severian and Silk, in particular (check out Hoof&Hide's great Wolfe cover art gallery for more examples of this).
