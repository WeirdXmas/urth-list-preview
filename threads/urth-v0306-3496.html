(TBOTN) Utter speculation

Urth list · 9 messages · 2003-06-19 to 2003-06-20

2003urthThe Book of the New SunMatthew MalthouseAndy RobertsonCraig BrewerJames WynnDan'l Danehy-OakesMichael Andre-DriussiJames Jordan
The thread: Readers speculated about the purpose of the Wall of Nessus and the Old Port in The Book of the New Sun, with the thread mostly drifting into imaginative theories rather than textual resolution. Craig Brewer proposed the Wall was a launch pad for the Whorl, while Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued it was built to contain hazardous wreckage and James Jordan floated a blast shield idea. Michael Andre-Driussi suggested the city crawled into an older structure like a hermit crab, and James Wynn questioned whether the Old Port implied a New Port. Matthew Malthouse offered a killing-ground theory and then argued that 'old' need not imply a 'new' port. The discussion ended without consensus, with several contributors admitting their ideas lacked direct textual support.
  • Craig Brewer: the Wall of Nessus was a launch base for a vessel like the Whorl
  • Michael Andre-Driussi: the Wall predates the city, which later crawled into it; the Old Port was a shuttle port
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: the Wall was built to contain hazardous wreckage from a crash
  • James Jordan: the walls muffle launch noise and the great walls may be blast shields
  • James Wynn: an Old Port implies a later New Port, so the citadel was probably not a Whorl launch site
  • matthew.malthouse: the open ground is a killing ground and 'old' need not imply a 'new' port

Themes: The dying sun and the cosmos, Deep time and ruins, Power, rulers and politics

2003-06-19 Craig Brewer #

Speculates that the Wall of Nessus was originally a huge launch pad or base for a cylindrical vessel like the Whorl, rather than a city wall enclosing an open space. Grounds the idea in the wall's enormous height and circular span and in Baldander's claim in Shadow that the enclosed space was not for Nessus' expansion, while admitting the launch idea seems impractical.
proposes · genre-and-craft · theories: Wall of Nessus was a launch pad for the Whorl

I've been thinking about the Wall of Nessus and
Baldander's assertion (ch. 35 of Shadow) that the open
space inside the walls wasn't for Nessus' expansion.
But something about the sheer size and height of the
walls made me wonder how far off it would be to
suggest that Nessus wasn't always a city but that it
was initially (possibly) one huge space pad. Assuming
that the walls are circular, then would it be possible
that what we have is some kind of base for a huge
cylinder, like...the Whorl?

Now this would be a rather insane (and, as far as I
can tell) rather impossible way to build and "launch"
a ship as big as the Whorl (which seems to be too
massive to ever be "launched" from a planet).
Still...why is the wall miles high and spread so far
around the city?

If someone can easily disabuse me of this idea, please
do so since it's captured my easily distracted
imagination.

Craig

2003-06-19 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Speculates that the Wall of Nessus and its enclosed space predate the city, which later crawled into the walls like a hermit crab, and that the Old Port and citadel were a shuttle port and dumping ground. Offers several skiffy explanations for the structure's purpose and notes the gigantism typical of the dying-sun fictions, responding to Craig's idea that Nessus began as a space pad.
proposes · myth-and-structure · theories: Nessus walls predate the city; Nessus was a shuttle port; Wall of Nessus as failed sky-elevator

Sorry, no diabusing from me! I'll just spur you on -- you haven't gone far enough yet! (Well except for the Whorl lifting off from Urth; I'll agree that seems unlikely.)

Fact: the Walls pre-date the city.

Fact: the city has been crawling upstream for a long time, so it has presumably entered the walls and taken up residence like a hermit crab moving into a found shell.

Fact: the citadel pre-dates the city, and the earliest name we have for it is the Old Port, iirc. So it probably was one of the ports from which people and materials where shuttled up to the Whorl (and that was relatively late in history -- the city was . . . approaching, but not there yet? The necropolis was for the military people based at the Old Port -- it was a military point); it was definitely a dumping ground for old worthless ships.

As for the why, who knows? Gene Wolfe might not even know. It is a Big Dumb Object that is an Enigma, with echos of the Tower of Babel and skiffy notions like a failed sky-elevator, or a blast-containment area for ships with radioactive exhaust, or a simple terrarium-type structure (an early Trantor-style "world building" module?) that never got its Buckydome roof, or just a super "particle accelerator." IIRC it is a feature of the dying sun fictions (if not even the gothic before it?) to have gigantism like this.

2003-06-19 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that the Wall around Nessus was built to contain hazardous wreckage from a crash, not to launch the Whorl, and asks whether the Wall is closed to the Sea. Grounds this on Nessus predating Typhon and the Whorl, the Towers already standing in Typhon's time, and Aldiss's Hothouse as a model for Short Sun's giant biology.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · source-and-allusion · theories: Wall contains hazardous materials from a crash; Nessus predates Typhon and the Whorl; Short Sun relates to Aldiss's Hothouse

While I like much of your and Mantis's) ideas here, I'm pretty sure that Nessus is more than a thousand years old and so predates Typhon and the _Whorl_. (In fact, I think it's
_much_ more, but I can't recall a cite at present to prove it.)

I suspect that the Wall was originally built to contain damage in the event of a crash (something Mantis tosses off below). It would be miles high to limit the escape of hazardous materials (radioactives, alien life spores, etc.) on the wind.

Pondering: There are a few things of BDO scale in the NS books. Some of them are alive. Is there any connection between them and the nonliving ones? Could the Wall (despite my, I think, convincing theory above) have anything to do with them? Probably not; a wall isn't necessary to keep them "out" since they can't walk abroad on land.

Question: Do we know that the Walls form a closed ... circle, or whatever? Or are they open to the Sea? Or extend out into it? I don't recall Sev. mentioning passsing through them as he comes up the river on _Samru_, but my memory could well be faulty in this. Anyone recall such a thing?

H'mmm. If they aren't fully closed, that blows off the "containment" theory, doesn't it?

The Citidel was clearly the old spaceport, yeah. I mean, that's why the Towers are old spaceships, neh?

Umm, no. The towers were already towers in Typhon's time, if I'm not totally mistaken; they take Sev to the Matachin in UotNS, don't they? I'm pretty sure it's not a port anymore at that point. H'mm: does anyone recall whether the city is surrounding the Citadel yet at that point? Or if there's any indication?

Yes ... Aside from Vance, the other major "dying Earth" work would be Aldiss' _Hothouse_, which has its share of biological gigantism -- the world-
banyan, the spiders that web the Earth to the Moon ...

Hey, now. It occurs to me, going waaaaaaaaaaay back to certain theories about trees. Could _Short Sun_ (and especially Green) be related to Aldiss' book in a way similar to _New Sun_'s relationship to Vance's?

--Blattid

2003-06-19 James Jordan #

Argues that the enormous buildings and monuments in the dying-sun setting reflect a decaying culture, citing Lewis Mumford's view that great monuments mark a strong culture's final phase. Also suggests the great wall may be a huge blast shield, offering the idea informally.
proposes · author-and-biography · theories: Giant buildings signal a dying culture

IIRC it is a feature of the dying

I believe it's Lewis Mumford who points out that the building of huge buildings and monuments is an aspect of the last and dying phase of a strong culture. An active and growing culture does not take the time to build such things to its own glory. Another version of Parkinson's Law: "The efficiency of an organization is in inverse proportion to the depth of carpet pile in its headquarters' offices."
But I'm inclined to think the wall is a huge blast shield of some sort.

FWIW

2003-06-20 Matthew Malthouse #

Proposes that the open ground inside the Wall of Nessus is a killing ground meant to give defenders an unobstructed field of fire, and that Nessus was once smaller with the Citadel as a spaceport to its north. Reasons from the Citadel's role as a point of entry for returning travelers and from the Autarchs' wish to keep the populace away from the fortifications, though admits no direct textual support.
proposes · myth-and-structure · theories: Open space inside Nessus wall is a killing ground; Citadel was once a spaceport north of Nessus; Wall of Nessus centred on the Citadel

I can think of two mundane reasons for the space inside the wall.

First to enclose farming land or other resources. That actually doesn't stand up very well because a technology that could build the wall is unlikely to be menaced by a purely ground based attack to the concept of "wall" is redundant unless there was more too it (speculations about forcefield domes have been made here in the past).

The second, and I think more logical potential reason is that the open space within the wall is a killing ground - a space left bare to provide the forces within the wall an unobstructed field of fire.

Such can be seen around fortifications built in our history but usually outside the walls so why a killing ground _inside_ the walls of Nessus.

Nessus - or rather the Citadel - is a space port; we might infer an important one. If it's the point of entry for cacogens or humans returning from the empire it's an obvious danger zone and one might well want to keep affective guns trained on it. Thus Nessus isn't "within" the wall but "outside it, the wall enclosing the rest of the world.

There's nothing I can recall to directly support this but consider that Nessus once much smaller; had the space port / Citadel to it's north while now the Citadel is in the southern inhabited reaches of the city. My impression was always that the circle formed by the wall was centered on the Citadel - again, no recalled evidence - and that the presence of Nessus, the city, was actually unrelated to the existence of the wall.

If that's credible then one should ask why the prohibition remains long after it ceased to be meaningful. One reason is the inertia such things have, law become custom and the Commonwealth seems riddled with such. Another is that as Nessus grew from a seaport to a great metropolis, the largest concentration of people and thus trouble in the realm, subsequent Autarchs might have appreciated the wisdom of not having the rabble wash up right to the foot of the fortifications.

Oh, and as for the Whorl I'm fairly sure Marble describes being ferried up to it with an implication that it was constructed in orbit.

Matthew
