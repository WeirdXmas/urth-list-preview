Major new Wolfe story online

Urth list · 8 messages · 2002-05-07 to 2002-05-07

2002urthNicholas GeversAdam StephanidesAndy RobertsonJosh GellerDan'l Danehy-OakesMatthew Davis
The thread: Gene Wolfe's new story "Under Hill" was posted online, and the thread turned quickly from news to interpretation. Andy Robertson called it minor, arguing its theme was that life beats peace and finding no depths, while Adam Stephanides noted the glass-mountain princess motif and admitted he saw no hidden depths on first reading. Josh Geller explained "Under Hill" as an old euphemism for Faerie and the land of the Dead, citing John Crowley's Little, Big, then disputed Robertson's claim that the Sidhe come from confused burial mounds, arguing for older religious roots in Icelandic sagas. The exchange ended without any reply to Geller's counterpoint, so it stopped at Robertson's unanswered assertion.
  • Andy Robertson: the story is minor, its theme is life over peace
  • Adam Stephanides: sees the glass-mountain motif but no hidden depths
  • Josh Geller: Under Hill means Faerie and the dead; fairies stem from older religion than burial mounds

Notes that a new Wolfe story draws on the princess-on-a-glass-mountain motif, linking it to Kai Lung and to a novel titled The Glass Mountain by Leonard Wolf. Wonders whether the motif appears in a genuine fairy tale or medieval romance, and admits no hidden depths were visible on first reading.

2002-05-07 Josh Geller #

Argues that fairies living under the hill come from older religious practices than Celtic burial mounds, citing Icelandic sagas as the best source and noting that hills or mountains aligned to the sky also served. Responds to Andy Robertson's claim that the Sidhe derive from confused Celtic burial mounds, adding that kings and priests buried there were believed to return.
disputes · source-and-allusion

They are not just Celtic. In fact, the best source I know for information on these ancient religious/magickal practices is the Icelandic sagas.

Also it's not always an artificial mound. Often it is a natural hill or mountain. What matters is that it have the proper alignment to the Sky, basically. Kings and priests and so on are buried in these places, from which they are sometimes held to have returned (see my post with the subject "Mound Dwellers").
