1998-06-02 Robert Borski #

Jokingly reports eye-color genes for characters, listing Phaedria as violet and Celestine Etienne as blue-violet, and suggests violet should be charted as a variant of blue like Mendel's peas. Mocks his own vision with contacts, signing off as a fictional optician of a one-eyed tribe; the post is social wordplay rather than a textual argument.
chats

mantis, my bug-eyed friend:

I see an E, then everything else is bleary.

Damn it, these contacts make me feel like I'm double-pupiled.

Guess that's why I forget to include the following IGenes:

Phaedria: violet.
Celestine Etienne: blue-violet.

Technically, I think that while violet is a variant of blue we need to chart them like Mendel's peas.

Dr. Cyclops
Optician of the One-Eyed Flying Purple-People Eaters (a lost abo tribe of Ste. Anne)

1998-06-03 Mark Millman #

Argues that Phaedria's violet eyes may be the product of cosmetic surgery, since the text says she has had procedures to make her more valuable and that her eyes were targeted. Responds to Robert Borski's joke about charting eye colour like Mendel's peas by saying the eyes' origin is uncertain but still possibly significant.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Phaedria's violet eyes are probably not natural

But, Dr. Wynken, we know that Phaedria has
had cosmetic surgery and other procedures
in order to make her more attractive and/or
valuable, and that Wolfe specifically mentions
that her eyes have been targeted for attentions
of this type. We can't be sure that her violet
eyes are natural (though that's far from saying
that they therefore can't be relevant or significant).

Mark Millman
