Which Wolfe Should I Read Next?

Urth list · 6 messages · 2014-11-11 to 2014-11-12

2014urthAllan AndersonDan'l Danehy-OakesAntonin ScriabinDaniel Petersen
The thread: Antonin Scriabin asked which unread Wolfe novels list members favored, then said he would read There Are Doors next on Daniel Petersen's advice, citing his fondness for Castleview and Free Live Free, and asked whether Operation Ares is as bad as reported. Daniel Petersen replied that There Are Doors is minor but strange and rewarding, The Devil in a Forest is solid and tight though not his favourite, and Operation Ares is worth reading only by completists and is an unrealised apprentice work useful for tracing Wolfe's growth. Allan Anderson added a brief personal note that he has read Home Fires and Pirate Freedom and returns to Pirate Freedom often. The thread stayed at the level of recommendations and personal taste, with no real disagreement and no decisive conclusion.
  • Antonin Scriabin: wants recommendations and plans to read There Are Doors next
  • Daniel Otto Jack Petersen: There Are Doors is minor but strange and rewarding; Operation Ares only for completists
  • Daniel Otto Jack Petersen: The Devil in a Forest is solid and tight but not a personal favourite
  • Allan Anderson: Pirate Freedom is a book he returns to and holds dear

Themes: Books, libraries and writing

2014-11-11 Daniel Petersen replying to Antonin Scriabin #

Offers a brief evaluation of Wolfe's novel The Devil in a Forest, calling it solid and tight in pace and style despite being odd for Wolfe and not a personal favourite due to subject matter. Responds to Antonin Scriabin's recommendation list, dismissing Ares as an unrealised apprentice novel and noting its value for tracing Wolfe's artistic growth.
supports

I thought Ares was a particularly unrealised apprentice novel. I might get more out of it someday when I want to delve even deeper into how Wolfe grew as an artist.

Devil is not one of my faves per se, but it was a very satisfying read, a very solid novel, if quite odd for Wolfe in some ways. (Yet quite Wolfean in others.) I think it's not one of my faves simply out of preference as to subject matter. It might actually be one of his tightest written in terms of pace and style and perhaps even plot.
