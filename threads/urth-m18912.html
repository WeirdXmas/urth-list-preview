The thread: Readers debated what grammatical error Severian commits in 'Master Palaemon teaches us apprentices, mostly' and why Ultan answers 'But not grammar.' Jason H. opened the puzzle and argued the fault lies in the placement of 'mostly' rather than 'us,' using adverb-placement examples. David Stockhoff proposed the misplaced 'mostly' as the target of Ultan's jab, and James Wynn countered that the problem is ambiguity or style, not grammar, and that 'us apprentices' is also unclear. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes defended the hanging adverb as colloquial, citing Mark Twain, while conceding ambiguity, and Jason H. held that Ultan treats it as a real fault. The thread ended without consensus, with Stockhoff testing placements against Ultan's quip.
  • Jason H: The error is the misplaced 'mostly' (dangling adverb), a real fault Ultan rebukes
  • David Stockhoff: Ultan's jab targets the misplaced 'mostly'; later argues the joke works only on some readings
  • James Wynn: Not a grammar error but ambiguity or style, with 'us apprentices' also unclear
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: Hanging adverb means 'mostly teaches,' colloquial like Twain, though technically ambiguous

Themes: Stories within stories, Books, libraries and writing, Unreliable narration

2011-05-13 James Wynn replying to David Stockhoff #

Argues that the quip in Ultan's line "Master Palaemon teaches us apprentices, mostly" is a style problem rather than a grammatical one, since the sentence stacks two direct objects. Responds to David Stockhoff's reading of the misplaced "mostly" by explaining why readers blame "us apprentices" and why people swap "us" for "we" for clarity.
disputes · close-textual-reading

For the same reason, I always thought the problem was with "us apprentices". But this is really a style issue rather than a grammatical one. Perhaps it was a pet-peeve of style guide writers when Wolfe went to college. The problem is that it uses two direct objects (which could be argued to be a grammatical error, I suppose). Thus, it is cumbersome and confusing for the reason David gave. So, it is for clarity's sake, rather than ignorance, that people tend to replace the "us" with "we" which is technically incorrect. One should avoid the pattern entirely. If "us" needs to be qualified and the speaker needs to clarify his membership to "us", there are lots of ways to speak and write the thought more clearly.

2011-05-13 Jason H replying to James Wynn #

Argues that the grammatical slip in Severian's Ultan passage lies in the placement of "mostly" in "teaches us apprentices, mostly", not in the pronoun "us". Supports the claim with adverb-placement examples ("John eats only sushi" versus "John only eats sushi") and contrasts it with a speculative Dorcas-Baldander link.
proposes · close-textual-reading · genre-and-craft · theories: Ultan's grammar complaint targets misplaced "mostly"

I agree that Severian's grammatical error occurs in "Master Palaemon teaches us apprentices, mostly", but I have a different take.

There's nothing inherently wrong with the "us apprentices", grammatically or stylistically. People often misuse "us" vs. "we" in this setting (as James says), so our suspicions here are raised, but Severian's use is correct. So why does Ultan imply that he didn't learn grammar?

I think that the problem is the placement of "mostly" (although for a different reason from what David says). It needs to be adjacent to the word it modifies. The correct statement is "Master Palaemon mostly teaches us apprentices." That is, the main thing that Palaemon does is teach: he "mostly teaches". In contrast, "teaches us apprentices, mostly" really means that he teaches different kinds of people, and most of them are apprentices (but e.g. there might be some journeymen that he also teaches). I don't think this is what Severian means.

Adverb placement is a common but subtle issue. It's the different between saying "John eats only sushi" (correct), and "John only eats sushi" (commonly used, but wrong: it implies that John does nothing but eat; he doesn't sleep, or work, etc.).

To me, this little "grammar puzzle" Wolfe sets up is more interesting than speculating on whether Severian's great-aunt might actually be Baldander's cousin. But I realize I might be in the minority here. :)

-Jason

2011-05-13 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes replying to Jason H #

Argues that Severian's sentence 'Master Palaemon teaches us apprentices, mostly' uses a hanging adverb meaning the same as 'mostly teaches', and compares it to Mark Twain's usage in Huck Finn. Responds to Jason H.'s grammatical argument that 'mostly' must sit next to the verb, while conceding the sentence is technically ambiguous.
disputes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion

If Severian meant that, he would probably say "Master Palaemon teaches mostly us apprentices" -- or, more properly, "mainly." The hanging adverb, separated by a comma from the rest of the sentence, is used by Mark Twain (especially in the voice of Huck Finn) exactly as Wolfe-through-Severian uses it here: to modify the verb, but to have that modification come as an afterthought. The sentence as provided is perhaps technically ambiguous, but the meaning is clear: it is the same as if the "mostly" had been placed before "teaches."

2011-05-13 Jason H replying to James Wynn #

Argues that Severian's error in "Master Palaemon teaches us apprentices, mostly" lies in the placement of "mostly", not in the use of "us", and lists three possible meanings with the placement each would need. Responds to James Wynn's earlier post on the grammar, agreeing that Severian means the teaching is mostly done by Palaemon, and reads the passage as Ultan's grammar joke.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Ultan's grammar quip is about the placement of "mostly"

James,

Good ear, and I think I now agree with you as to what Sev meant. But I wouldn't say "the placement of 'mostly' is ambiguous". If we want to be really strict, there are right and wrong placements, depending on the meaning of the sentence.

Here are three possible meanings:

1. Most of the people Palaemon teaches are apprentices.
2. Most of what Palaemon does is teach.
3. Most of the teaching is done by Palaemon.

I still think that to a strict grammarian like Ultan, Sev's statement is only correct if he means (1). (Which he doesn't, hence the error.)

If Sev had meant (2) (as I originally thought), he should have said "Master Palaemon mostly teaches us apprentices."

If Sev had meant (3) (which, I agree with James, is what he really does mean), he should have said something like "Mostly Master Palaemon teaches us apprentices." (Although I guess "Master Palaelmon mostly teaches..." works here too.)

Is this splitting hairs in the most absurd way? Of course! I'm just a mortal trying to get the Master Librarian's little joke.

-Jason
