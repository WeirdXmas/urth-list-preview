Father Inire

1998urthThe Book of the New SunJ. Schultz

Themes: Ghosts, dreams and visions, Stories within stories

1998-09-04 J. Schultz #

Argues that Father Inire in the House Absolute should be read as a figure in a ghost story rather than a Carrollian figure, since Thecla tells the tale to a young girl. Grounds this in the framing: the story passes through a young woman who heard it as a girl, and the Exultants themselves wonder whether Inire leads the common wealth.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Thecla tells Father Inire story as ghost story

Remember that this story is told by a young woman, who heard it as a young girl, who heard it from a young girl, about a mysterious figure. Father Inire's role in the HA is not even understood by the Exultants, who wonder if FI is the true leader of the common wealth. Thecla's purpose in telling the story is most likely akin to telling a ghost story. ("Whooo, the scary monkey-man who lives in the walls of the House Absolute!)
