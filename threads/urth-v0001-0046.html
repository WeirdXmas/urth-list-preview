'Spinning Jenny' DePlatta" were deliberate or a typo introduced in the Broken Mirrors reprint, and asked who the "Captain Mirk" entry refers to. m.driussi defended the small press against typo complaints, citing its affordable Wolfe reprints, but could not confirm the dates since he lacked the Cheap Street edition. 1997-05-14 James Jordan #

Asks whether the original Cheap Street edition of the Bibliomen vignette "Gertrude S. 'Spinning Jenny' DePlatta" has sequential or contradictory dates, suspecting the circular dating may be deliberate or a typo in the new Broken Mirrors edition. Also asks whether the "Captain Mirk" in the new edition refers to anyone, after noting the "Seaman" entry is Neil Gaiman.
questions · close-textual-reading

Mantis, or whoever:

I have the new Broken Mirrors Bibliomen. "Broken Mirrors" is right, since there are typos in everything they do!
In "Gertrude S. 'Spinning Jenny' DePlatta," the dates are contradictory in my edition. Now, since circularity is the theme of this vignette, I can see that this might be deliberate. Or it might be a typo. I can't trust my edition. So, what I wanna know from anybody who has the original Cheap Street edition is this: Are the dates sequential in your edition, or not? BTW, I got that the new "Seaman" is Neal Gaiman, but is "Captain Mirk" anyone?

Nutria

1997-05-14 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Responds to a complaint about typos in a Broken Mirrors edition by listing reasons to cut the small press some slack, including its affordable Wolfe reprints and its move toward a magazine. Notes that the poster lacks the Cheap Street edition of the story and that it is not in the Weird Tales Wolfe issue, so cannot help the questioner.
informs

Reply: Item #8567332 from [email]@INET01#

Nutria,

Re: Broken Mirrors and typos. While not denying what you wrote, we are to cut some slack for BM because:

(1) it really is a micropress;

(2) it provides affordable Gene Wolfe stuff for the masses (BIBLIOMEN and EMPIRES OF FOLIAGE AND FLOWER were both originally Cheap Street books);

(3) Gene Wolfe gave Bryan Cholfin a standing ovation when Cholfin won a World Fantasy Award in 1995; and

(4) BM has gotten out of the book business to focus on its magazine, "CRANK!"

As for the story in question, since I don't have the Cheap Street edition I was hoping against hope that the story would be found in the special Wolfe issue of "Weird Tales"--alas, it is not, so I can't help you.

=mantis=
