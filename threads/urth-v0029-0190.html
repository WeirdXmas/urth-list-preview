The thread: Carlton Greene asked whether Plant Engineering, the journal Gene Wolfe edited, concerned horticulture or industrial facilities. Kevin J. Maroney replied that it was an industrial-facilities trade magazine, where Wolfe had been a staff writer after working as a food engineer, and pointed to a reprinted robotics article and a tallus-design lecture in Exodus from the Long Sun as signs of that background. A later post from Greene said the industrial sense had been assumed from Wolfe's engineering descriptions, and added that Dorcas and her flowers show Wolfe also knows plant matter well. The exchange was brief and factual, and the question was settled rather than debated.

2000-08-24 Kevin J. Maroney #

Informs the list that Plant Engineering is an industrial-facilities trade magazine, not horticulture, and that Wolfe once worked as a staff writer for it after serving as a food engineer. Supports the point by citing a reprinted robotics vocabulary article and a tallus-design lecture in Exodus from the Long Sun as traces of his engineering background.
informs · author-and-biography

Industrial facilities. Before he retired to become a full-time writer in the 1980s, Wolfe was a "food engineer" for Proctor and Gamble and then a staff writer for PE. One of Wolfe's PE articles--a vocabulary for robotics--is reprinted in his Boskone book, _Plan[e]t Engineering_; his background shows through in things like the lecture about tallus design in _Exodus from the Long Sun_.

_Plant Engineering Magazine_'s home page is at
<http://www.manufacturing.net/magazine/planteng/>.
