The thread: Dan Rabin and Dan Schmidt argued the third-person Whorl sections were narrated by the Homecoming protagonist, while Adam Stephanides repeatedly disputed this, arguing the Book of the Short Sun editors wrote them, citing the Afterword, Hoof's opening narration, and the editors' naming of the protagonist as Silk. Stephanides also disputed Schmidt's reading that the editors wrote only the final few Whorl chapters, using the Afterword's preceding paragraph about what Horn left written. Jeffrey Meyers reported that the Horn-gives-an-eye-to-Pig point is stated in a late chapter and speculated Horn may have donated his own eye earlier, replying to Stephanides' claim that the transfer is never stated. The thread stayed contested, with Stephanides making most of the posts and no clear resolution in the cards.

2001-02-08 Adam Stephanides #

Corrects Dan Rabin's claim that the third-person Whorl sections are narrated by the Homecoming protagonist, arguing instead that the editors of the Book of the Short Sun wrote them. Cites the Afterword, Hoof's opening narration, and the editors' eventual naming of the protagonist as Silk to question why the editors keep the protagonist's identity ambiguous.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Whorl sections written by the editors Hoof, Hide and wives; Editors withhold that Horn is dead; Whorl protagonist is Silk, not Horn

Not quite. The third-person sections are not written by the Homecoming-protagonist, but by the editors of BoTSS, Hoof, Hide and their wives, as stated in the Afterword. Which raises the question: why do the editors keep up the ambiguity over the protagonist's identity until almost the end? One possibility is that they don't want to admit that Horn is dead, but there are a couple of problems with this: apparently Hide and Hoof never did believe that the protagonist was Horn (see the start of Hoof's narration on p. 337); and when the editors finally do name the protagonist, they unambiguously call him Silk. Another case where the editors create a mystery and I don't see why: it's clear that "Horn" gives an eye to Pig, but as far as I can recall this is never stated.

--Adam

2001-02-08 Adam Stephanides #

Argues that the third-person sections of Return to the Whorl were written by the editors of the Book of the Short Sun, not by the protagonist, and corrects Dan Schmidt's reading. Supports this by quoting the Afterword passage, which says the editors re-created the protagonist's sojourns in Old Viron and the West Pole from his spoken accounts.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Editors of the Book of the Short Sun wrote the third-person sections

No: the editors write: "He [the protagonist] left us no written record of his brief sojourns in Old Viron and the West Pole, but he spoke of them often. In this volume we have re-created them to the best of our ability, based upon these conversations." (p. 409)

--Adam

Reports that the Horn-gives-an-eye-to-Pig point is explicitly stated in a late chapter of Return to the Whorl, and speculates Horn may have donated his own eye earlier, as hinted by a passing reference to him having one eye. Replies to Adam Stephanides, who said the transfer is never stated and suspected the editors invented the mystery; the poster recalls the recovery room section prompting the same question.
answers · close-textual-reading · theories: Horn donated his own eye to Pig

I wondered if "Horn" had donated his own eye well before he is described, in passing (I can't find the page # now), as having one eye, or maybe as observing someone out of his one good eye. Someone else may be able to locate the page number.

2001-02-08 (date estimated) Adam Stephanides #

Argues that the third-person sections of Return to the Whorl were probably not written by Horn, citing the editors' Old Viron territory and the Afterword's list of proper names. Responds to Dan Schmidt's claim that the Afterword says the editors wrote only the last few Whorl chapters, using the Afterword's preceding paragraph about what Horn left written as evidence.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Editors wrote the third-person sections of Return to the Whorl

I think that when the editors say "Old Viron," they are including its territory as well (we are told in the list of "Proper Names in the Text" that Endroad is subject to Viron). In any case "Horn" (I will leave off the question marks in the future) and company arrive in Old Viron proper in Chapter Ten, long before the end of Horn's account. I guess it's possible that Horn had written the third-person sections up to that point and the editors continued the third-person narrative in Horn's style and interpolated sections throughout the remainder of Horn's account. But I very much doubt it. For one thing, in the preceding paragraph the editors tell us what Horn did leave a written account of, and they don't say anything there about the Whorl.

--Adam
