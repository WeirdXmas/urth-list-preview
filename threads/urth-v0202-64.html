Themes: Humour in Wolfe, Names and their meanings

2002-02-03 Marc Aramini #

Proposes that the narrator Horn's name carries sexual connotations, including phallic meaning, and that his fall into the island pit could figure him as an impregnating phallus. Responds to Joe's request for more Freudian readings, citing "Hornbus, you whore" in Nightside the Long Sun and admitting the pit may be coincidental.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Horn as phallus lost in the pit; Pit hybridizes man and tree to impregnate

Hey Joe. If you wanted another Fruedian association in Wolfe, then the name of the narrator Horn offers a few. I'm sure we've discussed the Shakespearian connotation of horns and cuckholdry, and the Horn as male genitals from "Hornbus, you whore" of Nightside the long sun (which, I believe, if you will forgive my crudity, invokes fellatio). Here is one more that might be just coincidental: Horn falls in that big pit on the island. If you buy my theory that this recreates the vanished people by hybridizing man and tree, then Horn acts as an impregnating phallus "lost in the big woods". Unlike naming the taverns "the cock" and "the bush", I am not willing to say that this Horn in a pit is intentional on Wolfe's part. I just recall being traumatized by the description of Jolenta's private parts as an unhatched chick or something in The Claw of the Conciliator in the fifth grade or so. That's a pretty scary description to a young boy. And then there is that whole iron dingus scene in the Claw that always catches me by surprise.
Please forgive the crudity of this post.
Marc Aramini

2002-02-03 James Jordan #

Suggests that Horn's fall into the pit on the island in Long Sun may echo a sexual image, recalling a Fifth Head of Cerberus story about two stones and a bush tied to procreation. Responds to Marc Aramini's phallic reading of Horn by saying Wolfe does use geography this way, though the poster admits the memory is vague and asks a Fifth Head expert to confirm it.
