The thread: A short exchange on the Urth list started with David Stockhoff correcting an earlier claim that Buenos Aires does not sit on an estuary, and proposing that Nessus was a native settlement where the Gyoll meets the sea. He asked about the origin of the name Nessus and where to find text files of The Book of the New Sun. brunians disputed the mythological side, saying Cheiron, not Nessus, trains Achilles, and that Nessus is the centaur who tricks Heracles' wife with a poisoned shirt. Son of Witz answered the text file question with a torrent and search tip, and also agreed with Stockhoff's guess about Urth's timescale. The thread ended without further debate, with the classical correction left standing.
  • David Stockhoff: Nessus was likely a native settlement at the Gyoll's mouth, supported by a history of Spanish founding and native attacks
  • brunians: Cheiron trains Achilles and Nessus is the centaur behind Heracles' poisoned shirt, so the earlier identification was wrong
  • Son of Witz: Text files come from a torrent, and Urth's future lasts no more than about 100,000 years

2008-12-01 David Stockhoff #

Corrects an earlier claim, saying Buenos Aires does sit on the Rio de la Plata estuary, and argues Nessus was likely a native settlement where the Gyoll meets the sea. Supports this with a Wikipedia history of Spanish founding and native attacks, then asks about the origins of the name Nessus and where to get TBotNS textfiles.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Nessus was a native settlement at the Gyoll mouth

To all:

I have to step back from my statement that Buenos Aires is not on the Rio de la Plate. It IS in fact on the estuary of that river---an estuary that is so wide at that point that it may as well be a sea. So yes, Nessus was originally probably a native settlement by the sea/where Gyoll meets the sea, and Buenos Aires has already moved north from where it was "founded" by Spaniards. However, the Wiki doesn't mention any previous settlement at all:

Seaman Juan D?az de Sol?s
<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_D%C3%ADaz_de_Sol%C3%ADs>, navigating in the name of Spain <http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain>, was the first European to reach the R?o de la Plata
<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%ADo_de_la_Plata> in 1516. His expedition was cut short when he was killed, supposedly during an attack by the native Charr?a <http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charr%C3%BAa> tribe in what is now Uruguay <http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uruguay>.

The city of Buenos Aires was first established as /Ciudad de Nuestra Se?ora Santa Mar?a del Buen Ayre/^[7]
<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buenos_Aires#cite_note-Short_history-6> (literally "City of Our Lady Saint Mary of the Fair Winds") on February 2, 1536 by a Spanish expedition led by Pedro de Mendoza
<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_de_Mendoza>. The city founded by Mendoza was located in what is today the San Telmo
<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Telmo> district of Buenos Aires, south of the city centre.

More attacks by the indigenous peoples forced the settlers away, and in 1541 the site was abandoned. A second (and permanent) settlement was established in 1580 by Juan de Garay
<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_de_Garay>, who arrived by sailing down the Paran? River <http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paran%C3%A1_River> from Asunci?n <http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asunci%C3%B3n> (now the capital of Paraguay <http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraguay>). Although upon the refounding, the city itself was named Holy Trinity <http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Buenos_Aires> (Spanish <http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_language>: /Santisima Trinidad/) and only the port was still called Buenos Aires, over the next two centuries the port's name won out.

So, whatever. Does anyone know of any origins/meanings for the name "Nessus"?

Also, my guess as to the futurity of Urth is no more than 100,000 years. My 2 cents.

2008-12-01 Josh Geller replying to Lane Haygood #

Corrects Lane Haygood's identification by stating that the centaur Cheiron, not Nessus, trains Achilles, and that Nessus is the centaur who advises Heracles' wife to make a poisoned shirt from his skin. Grounds the correction in the classical myth of the shirt that burns Heracles and leads to his death and apotheosis.
disputes · source-and-allusion

That is Cheiron. Nessus is the one who instructs Heracles wife to skin him and make a shirt from the skin, which poisons/burns Heracles' skin when he puts it on, precipitating Heracles suicide/apotheosis.
