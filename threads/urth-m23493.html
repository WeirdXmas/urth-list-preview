The thread: Posters debated where the Inhumi come from and how they reach Blue, centering on whether they are space-faring beings. Lee Berman and Andrew Mason argued the Inhumi are plant-derived or from Green and that their flight claims are lies or unreliable, while David Stockhoff, nate jarvis and Daniel Petersen tested the hard-SF physics of interplanetary travel, re-entry and meteor glow. Antonio Pedro Marques and Jerry Friedman accepted that some travel happens but questioned Jahlee's knowledge of burn-up, and Gerry Quinn argued frozen bodies would not glow. The thread drifted into meteor physics and mythic parallels such as Yggdrasil and legend, without reaching agreement on whether the Inhumi truly fly.
  • Lee Berman: Inhumi are plant-derived stowaways whose claims of flight and burn-up are deliberate lies
  • David Stockhoff: Hard-SF space travel is implausible, but Inhumi may share plant ancestry with Green's vines
  • nate jarvis: Inhumi flight may be a rumor they spread themselves; questions physical feasibility
  • Andrew Mason: Official account of interstellar travel is false and Inhumi come from Green
  • Gerry Quinn: Inhumi travel between worlds but frozen bodies would not glow as meteors
  • Jerry Friedman: Inhumi remains would likely break up; Jahlee is probably lying about burn-up

Themes: Unreliable narration, Myth and folklore sources, Robots, androids and artificial life

2012-11-26 Lee Berman #

Argues that the Inhumi's secret in The Book of the Short Sun is a lie like their other statements, but that it must still hide something of substance tied to their reliance on being spaceship stowaways. Grounds this in the Inhumi's constant deception, their astral travel producing their true forms, and their treatment of humans as cattle, while rejecting Antonin Scriabin's biological-oddity explanation and the claim of wings-only interplanetary flight.
proposes · close-textual-reading · allegory-and-typology · theories: Inhumi's secret is a lie hiding stowaway reliance; Inhumi deceive humans like herded cattle

With regard to the astral projection sort of travel, I agree with those who think that has more to do with the less-than-substantial Neighbors' influence than Inhumi. FWIW, this sort of travel seems to produce the "real essence" of the travellers or something like that. So gods and inhumi who travel that way end up showing their "true" form, whatever that may mean. The Inhumi end up as hyper-sexualized humans which is probably worth some discussion.

I'll leave aside the debate on whether all predatory humans rely on stealth and emphasize the point that all Inhumi that we encounter are relentless liars and deceivers. It seems completely a part of their nature. Likely this is metaphorically related to the Serpent, Satan, The Great Deceiver, etc., not to mention Jahi/Lilith, the temptress, demoness etc.

We could go so far as to say that the Secret of the Inhumi is a lie just like everything else the Inhumi say. But I think good writing requires that there is something of substance to this secret that we are supposed to figure out.

As you point out earlier, nate, this secret is supposed to be something which could ruin the Inhumi race if revealed. Your idea about consent is along these lines, and I appreciate that.

Yes, quite a bit is said about the Inhumi travelling across the void from Green to Blue when the planets are in conjunction. In previous discussions about that we've agree that the planets cannot be close enough to share an atmosphere and not collide so the Inhumi must be travelling through the void.

My position has been that multi-cellular organisms cannot possibly survive such a trip without exploded cell membranes. Perhaps most damaging to this story is the impossible premise that biological creatures could somehow achieve escape velocity purely on the power of wings. Still, I may be in the minority in disbelieving the lies of the Inhumi, but it leaves me feeling as frustrated as when I hear real world masses believing the lies of some demogogue.

So, I think that is a lie to cover up the inhumi reliance on being spaceship stowaways. I feel it has to have some important significance like that. Likewise, Antonin Scriabin's thought that the Inhumi mating habits could be just a biological oddity doesn't ring true to me. Why would such a thing be revealed at the very end of the story by Juganu? There are more lies going on here I think, meant to protect the Inhumi race.

These are like the lies told by Quetzal to get some of the Whorl residents to go to Green. As Krait tells Horn, the Inhumi think of humans as cattle. They are more (socially) intelligent than humans and will use lies and deception to herd us where they want us, just as we use fences, whips and nose rings to herd cows where we want them.

2012-11-27 Lee Berman replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that Wolfe answers the Inhumi questions clearly: the flight claim rests only on the Inhumi's own words, while Krait's hitching a ride onto Horn's boat and then the lander shows the Inhumi's skill is in stowing away rather than technology. Grounds this in Krait's manipulation, lies, flattery, and his remark that humans are cattle, and notes the names Horn, Sinew, Hoof and Hide.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Inhumi lie about flying through space; Inhumi are skilled at hitching rides, not technology

Yes, I think these are exactly the questions Gene Wolfe expects us to ask. But I differ from some others in finding that he provides unequivocally likely answers.

The first question boils down to: "Do the Inhumi really overcome impossible biological challenges to fly through space or do they lie about it?"

obvious answer. Yet, we are still tempted to believe the lie, despite a dearth of evidence for the flight except the words of the Inhumi. (to be fair, I find one bit of evidence: the inhumi ability to stay alive for years buried underground; but this does not address the cellular fluid pressure nor escape velocity questions).

Wolfe also gives us the answer to the second question, the one you post above regarding the Inhumi using technology or surreptitiously hitching a ride. Inhumi are explicitly described as poor at using technology but brilliant at hitching rides.

We don't know how Quetzal got onto The Whorl but we are shown in exquisite detail how Krait wrangles a ride onto Horn's boat and then onto the lander. Not only does Wolfe show Krait using manipulation, lies, flattery, forced oaths, impersonation, exploitation of family etc., but he makes it seem easy and natural for Krait to use these stratagems to tie Horn into knots. Plus he has Krait admit he thinks of humans as cattle. Unintelligent, easily herded food. (Horn, Sinew, Hoof, Hide etc. are probably not coincidental names here).

2012-11-27 David Stockhoff replying to Lee Berman #

Asks whether the narrator in the Long Sun books ever suggests inhumi descend from plants, then argues they may be related to the vines of Green and proposes a plausible way plant-descended beings could cross space. Lists six dangers and challenges of interstellar travel, such as vacuum, radiation, reentry, and dormancy, to support the idea as a stretch but worth making.
proposes · close-textual-reading · genre-and-craft · theories: Inhumi are descended from the vines of Green

Does anyone recall a passage in which the narrator suggests inhumi are derived from plants? (Please excuse it if I've brought it up before.)

I'd have to look at it again to confirm it (and that it was meant in a literal sense), but it could make for an interesting twist to this discussion. I believe the inhumi are related to the vines of Green. Plants could traverse space given sufficient external hardness, but that is insufficient by itself. There are at least three dangers and three challenges in traversing space:

(1) Vacuum rupture
(2) Radiation
(3) Heat/cold variation

(4) Navigation/propulsion

(5) Reentry/landing
(6) Food/air

I'm pretty sure there are Earth seeds that could handle the first two or three for a period. The second three are probably unsolvable except by (1) setting a trajectory, (2) either large "wings" or a very hard, nonflammable exterior, and (3) dormancy.

A winged, intelligent descendent of plants capable of going dormant could conceivably carry this out (aside from firing seeds across the abyss). It's a stretch, but one worth making. Anything else is unlikely even by SF standards.

Asks how the Neighbors could have shipped inhumi from Green to Blue across space, listing the physical obstacles to interstellar travel such as vacuum, radiation, navigation and reentry. questions · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Inhumi spread rumors of space flight on Blue

If the Neighbors sent/brought the inhumi to the Whorl to judge what humans are like based on how the inhumi parasitizing them behaved, then how did they do so? If the Neighbors on Green were shipping inhumi to Bue as part of their war between Green and Blue, what was their method of shipping?

Climbing a mountain--even one that pokes up out of the atmosphere like our own Olympus Mons--and then flapping your wings really really hard to fly through vacuum just seems silly. 2012-11-28 David Stockhoff replying to entonio #

Argues against the idea that the inhumi, or inhunu, are space-faring aliens on Blue, rejecting the space-flight theory in the setting of Wolfe's Blue's Waters. Responds to entonio's point by offering three reasons to set aside hard-SF speculation, and notes that winged flight between planets would need an ether Wolfe never proposes.
disputes · close-textual-reading · genre-and-craft · theories: Inhunu are capable of interstellar space flight

You're absolutely correct: We're not talking about hard SF here. Some basic reasons to give the inhunu space flight theory a break are (1) they are aliens whose physiology we have only the smallest clues about (2) we do indeed keep expanding our understanding of the limits of earthly life (although to be fair bacteria don't quite count) (3) as you suggest, we know they have the powers of glamour and inhumation, so why not? Cthulhu-like "flight" between "the stars"? (Tzadkiel seems to do it.) I've argued for the first two; the last bothers me because I'm not really sure I accept the shapechanging powers.

Still, winged flight between planets requires ether to swim in. Wolfe does not propose an ether. He makes allusions to Lovecraft but does not resurrect the whole Lovecraftian pseudoscientific context for winged flight. So even just arguing from literary suspension of disbelief is problematic.
