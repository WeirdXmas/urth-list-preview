The thread: Stockhoff asked whether incest themes in The Book of the New Sun come from the twinned sexes of the gnostic God, arguing Valeria is Severian's sister and his matings mirror a Keymaster fitting keys to birth universes, and linked this to Crowley's Endless Things. Wynn agreed Gnostic models such as Norea as wife and sister could apply, but warned against reading Valeria as a sister, citing Wolfe's bending of kin terms in The Short Sun. Wilson supported the sibling-spouse reading and extended it to Old Testament parallels like Abraham, Sarah and the Pharaoh and the Song of Songs. The thread stayed on the sibling-spouse pattern across sources; no firm conclusion about Valeria was reached, and the posts remained in disagreement over whether a gnostic framework is needed.
  • David Stockhoff: Valeria is Severian's sister, and his matings reflect a gnostic twinned-sex cosmology
  • James Wynn: Gnostic models like Norea can fit, but do not assume Valeria is a sister given Wolfe's loose kin terms
  • Jeff Wilson: Sibling-spouse motifs also appear in Old Testament sources such as Abraham, Sarah and the Song of Songs

Well, for starters, the Gnostic Christ does not save anyone individually. He saves mankind as a whole through the example of his enlightenment. This is closer to the salvation of Silk and Severian than Jesus. Additionally "the Christ" is merely the male half of God (although even that term has a somewhat different meaning). With the female half they form the syzygy of their particular Aeon. This is Severian and Thecla. Silk and Hyacinth are a retelling of the gnostic story of Simon Magus and Helena. Helena was the Ennoia who had fallen into the lower aeons and become trapped in matter. "The Father", manifesting himself as Simon Magus, "The Son", finds her in a brothel suffering abuse, and rescues her. There's also Ophis, the serpent, mixed up in the story. It would difficult, but take fewer words, to detail the ways that New Sun and Long Son are inconsistant with what we know of the teachings of guys like Tatian and Valentinus or the group founded on their teachings known as the Severians.

J

2010-06-04 James Wynn replying to David Stockhoff #

Argues that incest motifs in Wolfe's work can draw on Gnostic models, citing Norea as wife and sister of Seth in some texts and as a hostile wife of Noah in others. Warns against concluding Valeria is Severian's sister, pointing to Wolfe's habit of bending kin terms, such as the Rajan calling Jahlee both daughter and sister in The Book of the Short Sun.
disputes · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: Valeria is Severian's sister

There's nothing inconsistent about incest between the important characters of Gnosticism (the influence of Egyptian philosphy and myth ensures that). Norea was said in some writings to be the wife and sister of Seth, while simultaneously half of a syzygy. That said, who Norea was depends on who you are reading. For others, she is the wife of Noah (a hostile relationship there --like Sev and Valeria-- since she burned down his ark three times). The problem with saying "Valeria must be Severian's sister" is that Wolfe so happily plays with the definitions of terms. In The Book of the Short Sun, the Rajan calls Jahlee (the inhuma bearing, I believe, Chenille's soul) his daughter and his sister. So you're not just dealing with the twisted and unintuitive world of Gnosticism, but also the twisted worlds of Wolfe where little is as it seems.

J.

Argues that incest and sibling-spouse motifs in Wolfe appear in the Old Testament too, citing Abraham, Sarah and the Pharaoh and the Song of Songs as parallels to Gnostic material. Responds to James Wynn's point that Gnostic figures such as Norea were both wife and sister, extending the comparison beyond Gnosticism to biblical sources.
supports · source-and-allusion · theology-and-religion

It's not just Wolfe and Gnosticism; the regular OT has Abraham, his "sister" Sarah, and the Pharaoah, plus some "my wife, my sister" action in the Song of Songs.
