Heinlein's Universe and The Long Sun

The thread: Participants first debated whether Wolfe's The Long Sun echoes Heinlein's novella Universe, with Gem Caccetta and Gerry Quinn arguing the Crew and educated Cargo remember they are on a ship, unlike Heinlein's forgetful descendants. Frederick Norwood then argued The Land Across satirizes Heinlein, which Dan'l Danehy-Oakes and Ab de Vos disputed as Heinlein's politics were debated. The discussion ended with Norwood asking to move the Heinlein talk to another thread and returning to Wolfe, and de Vos arguing Wolfe is the subtler political writer who is no ideologist.
  • Gem Caccetta: The Long Sun differs from generation-ship stories because its characters remember their voyage and merely set it aside
  • Gerry Quinn: The Crew and Mainframe know the ship's situation, so the descendants are not fully forgetful
  • Frederick Hudson Norwood: The Land Across satirizes Heinlein, with Grafton as an inverted version of his learning hero
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: Heinlein never advocated military dictatorship and Farnham's Freehold is a failed novel
  • Ab de Vos: Heinlein grew more conservative with age, and Wolfe is the subtler political writer
  • Marc Aramini: The Rolling Roads are symbols tracking Graton's training, not Heinlein references

Themes: Power, rulers and politics, Colonization and the native, Time loops and causality

Argues that The Land Across borrows from H.G. Wells' When the Sleeper Awakens through its conveyor roads, sleeper-awakening phrasing, and a would-be ruler caught between factions, while noting the parallels go little further. Also finds allusions in An Evil Guest and TLA, and suggests cloning and personality imprinting underlie TLA's mysteries, supporting Gem and Gerry's Heinlein connections.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: The Land Across draws on Wells' When the Sleeper Awakens; Cloning and personality imprinting underlie The Land Across

Appreciating the Heinlein connections noted by Gem and Gerry; a reminder that RAH is still relevant in the 21st century, as Christopher Nuttall might put it.

The interesting bit about the conveyor belt roads of Heinlein's The Roads Must Roll is that it has a precedent, and a much better fit with TLA, in H.G.Wells' When the Sleeper Awakens (1899). Wells' title recalls a noticeable sentence in TLA Chapter One "Now it seems to me that I must have been asleep a long time before I got into bed" followed by several "awakenings" throughout TLA.

Note that Wells and Wolfe each relate the struggle of a potential ruler of a dystopian society who gets caught up in a struggle between opposing factions. I don't think the parallels go much beyond this. It's a lot like Wolfe's choice of Baskin-Robbins as an allusion to Andromeda (Messier-31 Flavors) in An Evil Guest, or the allusion to Boris Badenov in a conversation at a cafe in TLA, "I don't trust that conductor. Why is he so short?" to draw attention to Papa Zenon's lack of stature.

Aramini's Black-Red-White trichotomy helps us think about a lot of TLA's mysteries, although I suspect there is a lot of cloning going on along with the imprinting of personalities. Imprinting is found in Home Fires, TLA, A Borrowed Man. There's cloning/imprinting of some sort in A Borrowed Man, and I think something similar is happening in The Land Across.

- Stephen

The interesting bit about the conveyor belt roads of Heinlein's The Roads Must Roll is that it has a precedent, and a much better fit with TLA, in H.G.Wells' When the Sleeper Awakens (1899). Wells' title recalls a noticeable sentence in TLA Chapter One "Now it seems to me that I must have been asleep a long time before I got into bed" followed by several "awakenings" throughout TLA.

Note that Wells and Wolfe each relate the struggle of a potential ruler of a dystopian society who gets caught up in a struggle between opposing factions. I don't think the parallels go much beyond this. It's a lot like Wolfe's choice of Baskin-Robbins as an allusion to Andromeda (Messier-31 Flavors) in An Evil Guest, or the allusion to Boris Badenov in a conversation at a cafe in TLA, "I don't trust that conductor. Why is he so short?" to draw attention to Papa Zenon's lack of stature.

Aramini's Black-Red-White trichotomy helps us think about a lot of TLA's mysteries, although I suspect there is a lot of cloning going on along with the imprinting of personalities. Imprinting is found in Home Fires, TLA, A Borrowed Man. There's cloning/imprinting of some sort in A Borrowed Man, and I think something similar is happening in The Land Across.
