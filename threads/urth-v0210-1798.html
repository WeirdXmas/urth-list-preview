Wolfe excerpt online

Urth list · 4 messages · 2002-10-03 to 2002-10-07

2002urthSteve StricklandDan'l Danehy-OakesRon CrownChristopher Culver
The thread: Ron Crown announced that an excerpt from The Wizard Knight was posted online ahead of the Fall 2002 Conjunctions issue, and Christopher Culver found its language juvenile while expecting Wolfe's usual trickiness to hold interest, noting this would be his first Wolfe for a younger audience. Steve Strickland asked when the excerpt would appear in print and how to quickly learn Norse mythology before reading American Gods. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes advised skipping the Norse study and suggested children's books on gods and heroes instead, steering the thread away from Wolfe. The thread ended with the Norse question answered and no real debate about the excerpt.
  • Ron Crown: announces the online excerpt and praises the issue
  • Christopher Culver: excerpt reads as juvenile, but Wolfe's trickiness should still hold interest
  • Steve Strickland: asks publication date and how to learn Norse myth quickly
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: no need to study Norse myth first; try children's books, avoid Marvel's Thor

Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Myth and folklore sources

2002-10-04 Christopher Culver #

Remarks that the excerpt of The Wizard Knight posted online reads as juvenile in its language, though the poster expects Wolfe's usual trickiness to hold interest. Notes never having read The Devil in a Forest, so this would be the first Wolfe writing for a younger audience, and mentions being a student at the university's Madrid campus.
chats

I must say that the language looks somewhat juvenile, but I get the feeling that there will enough standard Wolfe trickiness to keep me interested. I've never read THE DEVIL IN A FOREST, so I've never seen Wolfe write to a younger audience before.

Small world. I'm a freshman at SLU's Madrid campus.

Christopher Culver <[email]>

2002-10-07 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Advises a reader not to bother learning Norse mythology before reading American Gods, but suggests children's library books on gods and heroes if he wants to, and warns against Marvel's Thor comic. Responds to Steve Strickland's question about the quickest way to learn Norse myth, with no connection to Wolfe's fiction.
answers

Actually, you really don't, but if you really want to, ... h'mmm. Check out the mythology section of a good children's library, there are always good books of Gods And Heroes there. But stay far, far away from Marvel Comics's THOR.

--Blattid
