The thread: Posters debated whether Severian could be Typhon's son through time travel rather than cloning, with Catherine (Bird of the Wood, a time-travelling Contessa) as the mother set adrift on the Brook Madrigot. Son of Witz proposed the idea and James Wynn supported it, while Andrew Mason argued Catherine's name is a deliberate clue tied to Ouen and Dorcas. Jeff Wilson defended the story's manipulated protagonist as valid fiction, and Adam Thornton's joking claim that every character is a reincarnation of Severian drew sharp rebuttal from James Wynn. The thread ended with Son of Witz agreeing that the House Azure story contains cloning, so the clone reading gained support but no firm resolution was reached.
  • Son of Witz: Severian could be Typhon's son via a time-travelling Catherine, not cloning
  • James Wynn: Clone solution over-determines the story; supports time-travel link and Brook Madrigot
  • Andrew Mason: Catherine's identity is a deliberate clue tied to Ouen and Dorcas
  • Jeff Wilson: Manipulated Severian remains a valid story; explanations judged by entertainment value
  • Adam Thornton: Joking claim that all characters are reincarnations of Severian
  • Lee Berman: Time travel explains recurring figures in Long Sun better than cloning

Themes: Time loops and causality, Doubles, twins and clones, Fathers, mothers and children

2010-12-21 James Wynn replying to Andrew Mason #

Argues that Severian's bond with Dorcas runs through Ouen, suggesting Catherine is hidden in plain view or is less important than expected, and rejects the clone solution as over-determining the story. Responds to Andrew Mason's question about how Severian connects to Dorcas by citing Wolfe's recurring false family ties, such as the Rajan's sons and Number Five's father, and by weighing the Hierogrammates' role.
proposes · myth-and-structure · close-textual-reading · theories: Dorcas is Severian's mother through Ouen; Catherine is hidden in plain view; Severian is a clone and was designed for his role

Severian knew Dorcas before he knew Ouen. They might have even been lovers before he met Ouen.
So why bring up Ouen at all? Well, appearance suggests they /are/ connected. But another answer to your question might be that Dorcas is the /only/ one who is important, and Ouen is her son. And in some sense by reuniting the two of them, Severian is reuniting himself to Dorcas. Again, that's an off-hand supposition. It is worth noting that false familiar ties is a staple of Wolfe. The Rajan calls Hoof, Hide, and Sinew his sons. He warns Hide that Jahlee (in soul travel and bearing the soul of Chenille) could be his "sister or aunt". Resurrected Horn accepts an inhumu bearing Sinew's soul as his son. Number Five calls Matre his father. This is all very mythological in that gods become the children and relatives of each other as told by mythographers although historically they were unrelated.

I take it differently. I take Catherine's absence as evidence of a feint. EITHER:
She has been sitting in plain view all the time (for example, she's Dorcas or Jolenta -- I'm not saying that's the case, I'm saying that's the sort of thing I expect if she's been in plain view). This is common for Wolfe. I remember getting to the end of "Peace" and say "Wha???" Then I thought about it for two days and realized most of my biggest questions had already been answered.

OR

For some reason, she's not really as important as we naturally thought she would be.

In what way? Remember that the myths are very detailed. And if Severian fulfills them, I expect a detailed fulfillment.

I agree it complicates matters regarding Ouen. But Time-traveling is worse in some ways. You put your finger on probably the most unsavory aspect of the Clone Solution. It makes the story not just Deterministic, but Super-Deterministic. Severian is not just destined to be the New Sun. He was existentially designed for it. That's probably the aspect of the The Book of the New Sun that I like the least: The entire race of the Hierogrammates. But Time-traveling over-determines, and /incestuates/ the story in the same way. And even when I understand that the H's are always behind the curtain --nudging Severian like NASA nudging Voyager around Jupiter, and even rebooting him when he catastrophically dies-- I'm still left with the feeling of something going on just beyond my field of vision. I'm not so attached to the Clone Solution. It's just the simplest path to get there. If there were evidence of a more complicated solution --if it answered an open question for me-- I'd probably go for that solution.

2010-12-21 James Wynn replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that Wolfe repeatedly destroys and refabricates identities in the Long Sun books, citing Pas through Jerboa and Sand, Chenille through Jahlee, and Horn and Silk, and asks what a self is beyond memory. Grounds this in the Horn-Greenbuck father-son link and compares sonship and fatherhood in the Christian sense, including Philo's Son of God.
proposes · theology-and-religion · myth-and-structure · theories: Horn and the Not Horn are father and son

Well, he /was/ a greenbuck. I don't know his name or whether such concepts are valid for Neighbors. The Not Horn becomes the father of the Not Sinew. But in a sense, they were father and son. The connection was there.

I think Wolfe is, again, exploring the nature of identity. He destroys and refabricates Pas through Jerboa and Sand (and Oreb, says I), he refabricates Chenille through Jahlee, refabricates Horn in the pit and then through Silk, he refabricates Silk in a variety of ways. I think he refabs several other characters throughout the books but lets not get into that. He obliterates these identities and restores them to see how they react like some evil scientist in his tower.

What what am I beyond my memories? What are we to God? I could see this being a very important personal question to Wolfe. Additionally, familial titles are a key theological/mythological issue. The Christ is called The Son of God (a concept systematically described by the early first century Jewish philosopher Philo), but it is a mistake to think of him as God Jr. By the same token we call God our Father; Roman Catholics make free use of the terms Father, Mother, Sister, Brother; Evangelical Christians call each other Brother and Sister. What are the values of such relationships (real or titular) to one stripped of physicalities such as memory.

In short, Wolfe has many angles. He is accomplishing many different things.

2010-12-21 Jeff Wilson replying to James Wynn #

Argues that Severian's struggle remains a valid story even though he is manipulated throughout the Book of the New Sun, since the layers of manipulation are part of a staged play. Responds to James Wynn's claim that time travel and the clone solution make Severian super-deterministic, and suggests explanations can be judged by how entertaining they are as fiction.
disputes · author-and-biography · theories: Severian's world is a play staged for unseen audiences

It's still a a struggle for Severian, though...and as it gradually becomes apparent he is being manipulated, and the manipulation crescendos into being part of an entire manipulated world, and the manipulated world crescendos into a play being put on for the benefit of forces yet more mysterious, and so on...it's still a valid story of how Sev handles all this. And I think the meta-meta-metafictional subtext is useful because we really can say how likely a given explanation is based at least somewhat on how successfully it makes an entertaining story, because we represent the mysterious audience.
