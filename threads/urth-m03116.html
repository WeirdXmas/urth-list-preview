Close Reading: Torturer Chapter 1

Urth list · 4 messages · 2006-09-07 to 2006-09-09

2006urthThe Book of the New SunDaniel D JonesDavid DuffyChris MulderJack Redelfs
The thread: Readers debated why the guild assigned Severian to torture Thecla in the first chapter of The Shadow of the Torturer. Jack Redelfs argued the masters knew of his bond with Thecla and meant to break or disillusion him, which the kitchen knife did not anticipate. Daniel D Jones offered a related motive: a loyalty test to see whether Severian could be trusted with guild work, and Chris Mulder agreed and argued he failed it. David Duffy shifted to a separate reading, treating the epigram about the shortest watch and the pistol as clues that Severian is the watchman. The thread ended with the loyalty-test reading mostly accepted and Duffy's symbolic reading standing apart from it.
  • Jack Redelfs: guild masters assigned Thecla to break Severian's bond with her, not foreseeing the knife
  • Daniel D Jones: the assignment was a loyalty test to see if he could be trusted with guild work
  • Chris Mulder: the Thecla scene was a guild loyalty test that Severian failed
  • David Duffy: the epigram and pistol point to Severian being the watchman, with the pistol as a symbol

Themes: Justice, torture and mercy, Power, rulers and politics, Love and loss

2006-09-07 Jack Redelfs #

Argues that the guild masters knew of Severian's bond with Thecla and assigned him to torture her to break or disillusion him, not foreseeing the kitchen knife he slipped her. Grounds this in Severian's apparent surprise at his own act and in his loyalty to the guild as his only home, responding to a poster who questioned why he was called to assist with the Revolutionary.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Masters assigned Severian to torture Thecla deliberately

The masters well knew about Severian's relationship with Thecla, in general if not in detail. I think they required him to torture Thecla to, shall we say "break him," or disillusion him of ever becoming close to a client again. I don't think they could of foreseen that he would have slipped her a kitchen knife, as indeed Severian seemed surprised himself. I think the masters trusted that in the end Severian's loyalty to the guild would win over whatever inner conflict he felt. He was indeed loyal to the guild ? it was the only home or family he had ever known.

2006-09-08 Daniel D Jones replying to Jack Redelfs #

Proposes that the guild assigned Severian to interrogate Thecla as a loyalty test, so that his performance would show whether he could be trusted with guild work. Responds to Jack Redelfs's suggestion that the masters wanted him to break his bond with Thecla, offering a related but different motive.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian's interrogation of Thecla was a guild loyalty test

Or perhaps it was a test to SEE if his loyalty to the guild would hold. If he got through this, he could be trusted with anything. If not, then he obviously wasn't cut out for a life in the guild, and the guild found this out early rather than late.

2006-09-08 Chris Mulder replying to Daniel D Jones #

Argues that the scene with Thecla in the first chapter of The Shadow of the Torturer was a loyalty test set by the guild for Severian, which he failed. Agrees with Daniel D Jones's reading of the chapter and reasons that the guild would want to learn early whether an apprentice could be trusted.

2006-09-09 David Duffy replying to Daniel D Jones #

Argues that the epigram about the shortest watch is a clue that Severian is the watchman, and reads the pistol as a symbol of something precious, uncommon and destructive. Points to the act of giving a pistol to another to care for the woman you love, and links it to van Vogt-style descriptions of ravening energies.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian is the watchman holding the shortest watch

Not actually in Chapter 1, and previously discussed on the list -- the epigram. Severian is the watchman holding the shortest watch?

The pistol as a theme or symbol: something so precious, uncommon and completely destructive. (Later, bringing in VanVogtian descriptions of ravening energies ;)) Giving a pistol to another to care for the woman you love.

David Duffy.
