1999-12-17 Paul C Duggan #

Points out that the same sentence about symbols inventing us appears in both Vonnegut's and Wolfe's texts, then jokes that the two versions look very different. Asks whether Vonnegut did this on purpose and quips that his Severian lies less than Wolfe's, replying to an earlier post about Kilgore Trout's imagined book.
chats

Well, when Vonnegut writes

"We believe that we invent symbols. The truth is that they invent us; we are their creatures, shaped by their hard, defining edges."

it's certainly much different than Wolfe's

"We believe that we invent symbols. The truth is that they invent us; we are their creatures, shaped by their hard, defining edges."

You can clearly see the difference. Alot of other identical texts are pretty different too. Do you think Vonnegut did that intentionally? Vonnegut's Severian lies alot less than Wolfe's does too.

:-)
Paul
