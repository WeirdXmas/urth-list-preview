The thread: Readers debated the Urth/Whorl plot around the inhumi and the eight-legged companion in Horn's story. John Roberson proposed that willing human sacrifice would instill selflessness and keep inhumi from reverting to vermin, a view Adam Stephanides called circular because the sacrifice must be unselfish yet would itself prevent it. akt argued the creature with Horn is Babbie, sent by Mucor, and rejected the Scylla-in-Oreb reading from The Night Chough as unsupported. Alex David Groce held that the Scylla foreshadowing in the Short Sun books remains unpaid, with an Oreb payoff would be a surprise. Kevin J. Maroney, who had read The Night Chough differently, offered only a light exchange with alga. The thread ended without agreement, with several competing readings left open.
  • John Roberson: willing human sacrifice would make inhumi selfless and prevent reversion to beasts
  • Adam Stephanides: Roberson's argument is circular, since sacrifice must be unselfish but its result would stop it
  • akt: the eight-legged companion is Babbie sent by Mucor, not Scylla or Neighbors
  • Alex David Groce: Scylla foreshadowing in the Short Sun books is still unresolved, and an Oreb payoff would be surprising
  • Kevin J. Maroney: reads The Night Chough differently and plans to reread it

Themes: Stories within stories, Beasts and the animal in us, Suffering and sacrifice

2000-08-24 Roberson, John #

Proposes that the inhumi's secret is that humans offering themselves willingly would instill selflessness, so inhumi feeding only on animals would lose sentience and revert to croc-like vermin. Answers Spectacled Bear's question about Horn's claim that the inhumi could be reduced to beasts, arguing this conflicts with Incanto's view that their developing souls must not be killed.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Willing human sacrifice makes inhumi lose sentience; Inhumi feeding only on animals revert to croc vermin

My thoughts on this go as follows:

If humans offer themselves willingly to the inhumu, who then develop something of their soul/spirit of not harming others and being self-sacrificing, then those inhumu will quit feeding on other humans. Feeding from only non-sentient animals, they would lose their sentience and revert to blood-sucking croc vermin. While this would get rid of the inhumu problem, it would be somewhat contrary to Incanto's ethical stance, since he believes the inhumu spirits to be developing "souls" and to do something that would revert them back to beasts would be "killing" their human-like spirits.

-Ram

2000-08-24 Alice Turner #

Argues that the eight-legged creature accompanying Horn in The Book of the Long Sun is Babbie, sent by Mucor as his companion and guardian, not Scylla or Neighbors. Disputes the Scylla-in-Oreb reading from The Night Chough, calling it unnecessary and noting that the five Long Sun books offer no evidence for it.
disputes · puzzle-solving · theories: The eight-legged creature is Babbie sent by Mucor

Said William:

Not so; we'd all been counting eyes, but you got there first.

Mr. Borski:

This may well be true in "The Night Chough," which only you and mantis seem to have read. But there's not a tweak of evidence for it in the five books we have all read. Scylla has apparently been there (for the story), done that and skedaddled.

Oh, don't forget me, Robert! It's my favorite Position, and I think with William that your tortured explanations are completely *unnecessary.* The 8-legged thingie is Babbie, whom Mucor has sent to be Horn's companion and guardian. That's why he jumps and swims. Why do you need to drag Neighbors or some anonymous critter into what seems perfectly obvious? It's the hus, the whole hus, nothing but the hus.

-alga

2000-08-28 Adam Stephanides #

Argues that John Roberson's proposal, that self-sacrificing humans could make the inhumi lose their intelligence, is circular, since the sacrifice must be unselfish yet its result would itself prevent it. Replies to Roberson's Whorl post by mocking the argument as a looping chain of conditional steps that ends unfinished with an ellipsis.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Self-sacrifice would make the inhumi lose their intelligence

And if humans offered themselves knowing that it would result in the inhumi losing their intelligence, they would not be acting in the spirit of not harming others, so the inhumi would not imbibe this spirit, so they wouldn't stop feeding on humans, so they wouldn't lose their intelligence, so the self-sacrificing humans wouldn't be harming them, so...

--Adam
