Crowley, inhumi

1997whorlKieran Cleary

1997-05-26 Kieran Cleary #

Recommends several John Crowley novels after noting Gene Wolfe's praise of Crowley, and proposes that 'inhumi' may be a generic term like 'cacogen' for non-human beings of extrasolar origin. Responds to an earlier post quoting a question about whether the inhumi on Whorl came from Blue or Green, offering a wider reading of the term instead.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Inhumi is a generic term for non-humans

I must say that I'm gratified that Gene Wolfe recommends John Crowley. I just interrupted my reread of the New Sun books as I got my hands on Crowley's latest (and there aren't many books which can do that). I'd recommend the following by Crowley:

Engine Summer
Little, Big
Aegypt
Love & Sleep

Great! Maybe the term "inhumi" is more like "cacogen", i.e. generic term for non-humans of extrasolar origin, or maybe just non-human which leaves it a bit open-ended.

Lasrach
