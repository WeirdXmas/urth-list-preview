Themes: Gnosticism, heresy and other theologies

2000-07-03 David Duffy #

Suggests that a Gnostic Wolfe would welcome Judas as hero, and recalls the finale where the character acts from Nature rather than Duty. Responds to alga's view of Judas as a necessary betrayer, citing the finale's Nature-versus-Duty remark and parallels between the endings of LB and B and a scene where Adar and Nod inhale dry leaves.
supports · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: Judas as hero of the book; Judas as a Gnostic hero

As a good Gnostic and fan of heterodoxies, JC would be happy to have Judas as hero, wouldn't he? I was fuzzily thinking as part of the hero, based on my distant memory of the finale. Rereading it he says he acts from Nature rather than Duty. As LB ends with a Parliament, so too does B I guess.

On rereading TD, Neither-nor hibernates and Adar and Nod burn and inhale a few old dry leaves together ("hungrily" ;)).
