Sapphire Star

Urth list · 4 messages · 2008-12-29 to 2008-12-31

2008urthThe Book of the New SunDavid StockhoffJohn WatkinsSon of Witz
The thread: The thread asked what the sapphire blood-stone and the Claw's blue-and-red colouring signify in The Book of the New Sun. David Stockhoff argued the sapphire carries heavenly and virtue meanings from medieval lapidaries rather than healing, and that the colour clashes with Severian's black iconography, possibly pointing to a black hole. John Watkins supported the colour reading with Byzantine Christ and Mary iconography, with red as divinity and blue as humanity. Son of Witz proposed the Claw symbolizes Severian's star and the Avern echoes him as New Sun, citing argent robes. Stockhoff later argued the blue link is iconic and symbolic rather than a doppler blue-shift effect, while allowing religious significance; the thread ends there without clear resolution.
  • David Stockhoff: sapphire signifies heaven and virtue via medieval lapidaries, not healing; colour may link to a black hole
  • John Watkins: Byzantine blue-over-red iconography frames the Claw's colours as divine and human
  • Son of Witz: the Claw symbolizes Severian's star and the Avern echoes him as New Sun
  • David Stockhoff: the blue link is iconic and symbolic, not a doppler blue-shift effect

Themes: Light, darkness and colour, Names and their meanings, The dying sun and the cosmos

2008-12-29 David Stockhoff #

Argues that the Claw's colors and the sapphire symbolism Wolfe chose for Severian's blood-stone do not match Severian's black iconography, and that Wolfe chose the sapphire for its heavenly meanings rather than healing. Quotes medieval lapidary sources, including Marbodeus on sapphire as heaven and virtue, and suggests the color might instead link to a black hole.
disputes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: the Claw's sapphire symbolizes heaven rather than healing

That does point up something germane, however. The Claw's colors are blue and white on the outside (sky, sun) and red and black (green??) on the inside (thorn, blood). None of these has anything to do with Severian's iconography (black). And indeed Severian believes the Claw is separate from himself until he is shown otherwise.

Presumably sapphire is connected to healing in some reality-based tradition. But I don't find any evidence of it. I did find the following:

" Symbolic precious stones by the early Christian artists have been alluded to by several ancient writers. Conspicuous among these was Marbodeus, Bishop of Rennes, who wrote (as before mentioned) at the commencement of the twelfth century.

The blue-coloured Sapphire is an emblem of heaven, virtue, truth, constancy, heavenly love, and contemplation. [NOT HEALING!!!]

"The azure light of Sapphire's stone

Resembles that celestial throne,

A symbol of each simple heart

That grasps in hope the better part,

Whose life each holy deed combines,

And in the light of virtue shines."

In his "Commentary," Marbodeus says, "The Sapphire is of the colour of the sky. It signifies, then, that while they be yet on earth, set their affections on things above, and despise things terrestrial." "

Makes sense for the New Sun, don't it? And Wolfe, who must have done at least a little research into Byzantine icons and early Christian art, hid Severian's blood inside a blue stone rather than go for the obvious carbuncle (blood of christ) or the imperial "sardius":

"The Sardius, with its purple red,

Sets forth their merit who have bled,

The martyr-band, now blest above,

That agoniz'd for Jesu's love."

Eesh.

I wonder, has anyone considered that Severian's iconic color is literally the color of a black hole?

2008-12-29 John Watkins replying to David Stockhoff #

Argues that colour symbolism in Byzantine art offers a frame for the blue-and-red layering of the Claw, with red as divinity and blue as humanity. Builds on David Stockhoff's observation about the Claw's colours by citing the Christ and Mary iconography, where blue over red and red over blue express divine and human relations.
supports · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: Claw's blue-over-red layering echoes Byzantine divine-human iconography

Another point that may be of interest regarding red within or beneath blue.

In Byzantine art, red is the color of divinity, blue the color of humanity. Christ is therefore often shown with blue over red--the divine "putting on" the human form. (Mary, in contrast, wears red over blue, the human taken up by divine grace).
