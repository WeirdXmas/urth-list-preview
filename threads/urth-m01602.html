2005-10-27 Marc Aramini #

Defends The Book of the New Sun against critics who fault Wolfe for focusing on Severian as a torturer, praising its characterization, dialogue, atmosphere and prose. Responds to the linked blog discussion, saying Severian's repentance moves him and that Hethor is a well-drawn character unfairly criticized.
supports · genre-and-craft

After looking over the discussion on the latter blog, I am a bit amazed to see how preoccupied with the idea of Severian as a Torturer these critics are, and how quick to castigate Wolfe for his authorial choices (Wolfe isn't a great author because he stoops to fetishism? what? what about James Joyce?) What about characterization and dialogue and atmosphere - all, IMO, unparalleled in TBOTNS. The beauty of the langauge alone in many of the scenes screams out for admiration.

I, for one, always wanted Severian to be crueler. Severian's repentance always brings a tear of regret to my eye. Throw that girl off the cliff! Kill Agia, torture her, too. And Hethor was a neat character, too. Why are they ragging on him as some kind of undesirable? Can't ever please some people.

Marc Aramini

2005-10-27 Marc Aramini replying to aramini1 #

Disputes a blog's dismissal of Wolfe as a second-rate, one-trick writer, arguing that the Long Sun, Short Sun and New Sun books differ completely in narrative voice. Points out that the narrators of New Sun, Latro and Peace are closest in capacity for violence and distance from reality, while Long Sun and The Wizard Knight differ from them.
disputes · genre-and-craft

I was constantly amazed at the classification of Wolfe as a second rate, one trick pony writer on that blog. second rate? ;(

Only to third rate readers. Long Sun, Short Sun, and New Sun are completely distinct in voice. If anything, the narrators of New Sun, Latro, and Peace are the closest in terms of capacity for violence/distance from reality, but Long Sun and TWK are completely different.

Arghh.

Marc Aramini
