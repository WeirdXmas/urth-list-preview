Father Inire - Hethor

Urth list · 14 messages · 2011-10-19 to 2011-10-21

2011urthThe Book of the New SunRyan DunnGerry QuinnDavid StockhoffJeff WilsonAndrew Mason
The thread: Participants debated whether the enigmatic figure Isangoma in Wolfe's New Sun is Father Inire in disguise, rather than Dorcas. Andrew Mason proposed this identification, citing Isangoma's mysterious origins, his South American look, Inire's known disguises, and the jungle guide's resemblance to Isangoma in The Citadel of the Autarch. David Stockhoff supported it, while Ryan Dunn and Gerry Quinn backed Mason's broader claim that Wolfe hides key clues in plain sight. Jeff Wilson questioned the jungle guide's features and disputed Mason's reading that Isangoma can see the tokoloshe, though Mason later argued from the Urth episode that Inire can see Severian and Agia. The thread ended without resolution, with the disagreement about Inire's perception and identity still open.
  • Andrew Mason: Isangoma is Father Inire in disguise, and Inire guides Severian throughout the series
  • David Stockhoff: Isangoma resembles an older, clothed Inire and shares a shamanic quality with him
  • Ryan Dunn: Wolfe plants contextual clues that careful readers must notice to solve mysteries
  • Gerry Quinn: Wolfe hides all important plot elements in plain sight, so readers miss them only through inattention
  • Jeff Wilson: Severian never remarks on the autochthons' features, and Isangoma likely senses tokoloshe rather than seeing them
  • Mason (on Urth episode): Inire has eyes and can perceive Severian and Agia despite his blindness

Themes: Masks, disguise and shapeshifting, Hidden identities and parentage, Doubles, twins and clones

2011-10-19 Ryan Dunn replying to Gerry Quinn #

Argues that Wolfe plants contextual clues that readers must notice to solve mysteries, citing Talos's glamor on Jolenta and Severian's recognition of the Badger as examples. Responds to Andrew Mason's view that Wolfe hides things in plain sight, and points to the Pinakotheken moon man image, where Severian reports something but misreads it.
supports · close-textual-reading · theories: Wolfe plants clues Severian fails to notice

I also think he presents contextual clues, thens expects the reader to participate in solving mysteries.

For instance, Talos casting a glamor on Jolenta, Severian making a narrative point to recognizing the Badger... I think that is Wolfe sort of telling the reader to be on the look out for other like situations where Severian happens to not notice.

He shows us in the Pinakotheken with the moon man image that Severian can observe and report something but totally misinterpret or simply get it wrong.

These sorts of situational clues I think can go a long way toward looking the right way for riddles and demystifications.

I just haven't gotten there yet. :)

2011-10-19 Andrew Mason #

Argues that the character Isangoma is most plausibly Father Inire in disguise, rather than Isangoma being Dorcas or Inire being Dorcas. Grounds this in Isangoma being a mystery with no other story roots, his puzzling African name and South American look, and Inire's known disguises, including one appearance described as resembling Isangoma.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Isangoma is Father Inire in disguise

It seems to me that the Isangoma-Inire theory has more going for it than the Isangoma-Dorcas or Inire-Dorcas theory. The difference is that we have quite an elaborate backstory for Dorcas - though, to be sure, not a complete one - and know more or less what she was doing in the place where Severian found her, so identifying her with another character would subvert what we think we know. Isangoma, by contrast, is a mystery, with no other roots in the story, and has some definitely puzzling features - why does he have an African name but look South American? Why can he see Severian and Agia while Marie cannot? This, I think, makes it not implausible that he is someone else in diguise; and since Inire is known to be disguised on occasion, and on one of his appearances is described as looking like Isangoma, he is a likely candidate.

Of course, it doesn't have to be so. The problem with reading Wolfe is that we know some things are hidden beneath the surface, but for every proposed theory about them there are also rival theories which are equally possible. Nevertheless it seems to me that this one is relatively reasonable.

2011-10-20 Andrew Mason replying to Andrew Mason #

Argues that Father Inire tracks and guides Severian throughout the New Sun books, working with the Autarch toward Severian's killing the Autarch and succeeding him, and proposes that Inire may be the disguised autochthon at the masked ball in The Sword of the Lictor. Grounds this in Rudesind in the House Absolute saying Inire told him what to say, Rudesind guiding Severian to the Autarch, and the aquastor Paeon's report to the Autarch after the House Azure visit.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Inire guides Severian to kill the Autarch and succeed him; Inire is the autochthon disguised at the masked ball; Paeon told the Autarch of Severian's significance

.

Because he is telling us about his expericence as it happened at the time, and he didn't know it was Inire then?

I think there is a definite plotline involving Inire, which is a mystery as it unfolds, but is fairly clear by the end. Inire is tracking and guiding Severian, which becomes clear, if it wasn't earlier, with Rudesind in the House Absolute, who actually _says_ quite openly that Inire told him what to say. We can also tell that Inire is in cahoots with the Autarch, since Rudesind guides Severian to the Autarch. What we don't know at that point is to what end they are guiding him and why. In the end we discover this: the purpose is that he should kill the Autarch and become his successor; and the plot began when the aquastor Paeon told the Autarch of his significance, on the night when he visited the House Azure.

I do feel that, if Inire appears in three volumes, it would be good if he were in the fourth as well, so I have tried to find him in SOTL. The only possibility I can see is the autochthon disguised as an autochthon who comes to the masked ball - another covered face. Perhaps Severian has misinterpreted his disguise?

2011-10-21 Andrew Mason replying to Andrew Mason #

Argues that Father Inire, through Isangoma's words and his own later behavior, can see Severian and Agia despite his blindness. Grounds the case in Robert's reply that Inire has eyes and in the chanter knowing Severian was leaving, quoting these exchanges from the Urth episode.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Father Inire can see Severian and Agia

Actually, this is rather less clear than I imagined, but I still think he can. Isangoma says 'The tokoloshe, Preceptress, the tokoloshe come'. Marie says 'I think he's lost his mind,' and Robert replies 'He has eyes, Marie, and you don't.' After this Robert looks at Severian and Agia, and says 'As Isangoma says, the tokoloshe are here.' So he, at least, clearly thinks Isangoma can see them.

Later when Severian leaves, while Isangoma is chanting, he says 'The chant continued, but the chanter knew we were going; his singsong held a note of triumph'.
