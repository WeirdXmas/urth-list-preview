Woodhenge without abos

Urth list · 4 messages · 2002-05-20 to 2002-05-22

2002urthJerry FriedmanAdam StephanidesDavid DuffyJosh Geller
The thread: The thread asked whether the Short Sun books depict colonists on Viron and Urth erasing evidence of native peoples, such as the Annese and inhumi. David Duffy argued that colonists do destroy evidence, leaving museums with distorted records, and that Casilla's remarks about nonhuman abilities reflect a slave-holding viewpoint. Jerry Friedman questioned whether the fairies were meant and suggested the handover reads as escapism. Adam Stephanides disputed the existence of the Annese, citing the book's own doubts and the missing physical traces, while Josh Geller offered real-world examples from Costa Rica and Argentina. The thread ended with the sides still divided over the evidence in the text.
  • David Duffy: colonists destroy native evidence, so museum records are distorted; Casilla's remarks reflect a slave-holding viewpoint
  • Jerry Friedman: questions whether the fairies are meant and suggests the handover is escapist
  • Adam Stephanides: missing Annese traces on Urth are suspicious and the book itself doubts the natives existed
  • Josh Geller: real-world denial of native numbers in Costa Rica and Argentina shows how evidence is erased

Themes: Colonization and the native, Power, rulers and politics, Slavery and service

2002-05-20 David Duffy #

Argues that the Short Sun books depict colonists destroying evidence of native peoples, so that museums hold distorted records, and notes an honourable handover of power in the story. Asks what the inhumi were like when they mirrored the Neighbours, and says the remarks about Casilla's appearance reflect a slave-holding viewpoint rather than evidence of nonhuman abilities.
proposes · myth-and-structure · theories: Inhumi mirror human nature in Short Sun

There is little doubt that there were abos. It is more the melancholy fact, that just as on our world, all evidence of their occupation is intentionally (and/or incidentally) destroyed by colonists, so that even the museums are filled with lies. One thing about Short Sun is that there is an honourable handover of sorts (speaking of which, if the inhumi are an echo or mirror of human nature now, what were they like when they mirrored the Neighbours?).

I agree that there is no evidence that Casilla has nonhuman abilities -- the remarks re appearance are those of a slave-holding MCP.

David Duffy.

2002-05-22 Adam Stephanides #

Argues that the absence of any physical evidence for the Annese on Urth is suspicious, since colonists elsewhere destroy evidence of natives but later colonists here openly discuss the Annese. Responds to David Duffy's remark that the natives existed by citing the book's own doubts, the French records passage, and Marsch's view that the war does not explain the missing traces.
disputes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion · theories: The Annese never existed as a people on Urth

Well, the books themselves contain doubts. And I still haven't seen a convincing explanation of why, if we are to take the Annese's existence for granted, there is not one single piece of solid evidence for it: why every piece of evidence proves doubtful upon close examination.

As Jerry Friedman said, there are certainly many cases on our world of indigenous groups having been wiped out, but I don't recall any case in which the invaders/colonists sought to wipe out all evidence of such groups having existed.

But the colonists' present-day descendants don't seem to be making any attempt to cover up the Annese's existence; they will freely talk, not only about the Annese themselves, but about the humans exterminating the Annese. Mrs. Blount saw nothing wrong in her father killing abos; presumably, neither did her parents. And there's no indication the Annese government would have intervened. So why cover it up?

The more I think about it, the more suspicious the absence of any physical traces is. The war merely caused "the destruction of the records of the first French landing parties" (157, Ace pb), not the destruction of all skeletons or artifacts that might have been collected by the French. Marsch himself, who interprets all evidence as favorably as possible, doesn't regard the war as explaining the absence of physical traces. I'm not saying this proves that the Annese never existed, just that the absence of physical evidence is indeed not easy to explain away.

--Adam

2002-05-22 Josh Geller #

Argues that in Costa Rica and Argentina, present-day locals deny that native peoples were ever numerous, offering a real-world case of erasing evidence of indigenous groups. Responds to Adam Stephanides's claim that no colonists ever sought to wipe out all trace of a native people, using national examples rather than Wolfe's books.
informs

In Costa Rica and in Argentina, if you ask the current locals what happened to the Indians, they will say "There were never too many Indians here".
