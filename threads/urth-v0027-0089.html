Android Souls, Re: (urth) Re: Antechamber, Jonas, navigator

Urth list · 1 messages · 1999-06-12 to 1999-06-12

1999urthThe Book of the New SunMitchell A. Bailey

Themes: Robots, androids and artificial life

1999-06-12 Mitchell A. Bailey #

Argues that Wolfe's non-human characters, including the android Jonas and Mr. Million in The Fifth Head of Cerberus, are treated as having souls and as capable of gaining humanity. Supports a earlier poster's point by quoting Severian's remark about something that had no humanity finding it, and by citing Number Five's questioning of Mr. Million about whether he has a soul.
supports · close-textual-reading

... Ascribing
Who was it who wrote earlier that Wolfe regularly explores the boundaries of what constitutes humanity? " If it's possible for someone to lose his humanity, surely it must be possible for something that once had none to find it". Severian referred specifically to Jonas in that remark (IV, ch. X). Or look in 5HC, in "Fifth Head" where Number Five attempts to question Mr. Million regarding whether he has a soul or just some very sophisticated programming capable of "simulating" a soul for the observer. Mr. Million pleads ignorance, citing the lack of a frame of reference from which he could explore the matter.
