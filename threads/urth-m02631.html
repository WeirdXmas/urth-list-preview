2006-05-26 b sharp #

Proposes that the ugly hierodule masks of F, B and O in The Book of the New Sun represent an insect, a lamprey and a human leper, with Famulimus as the leper. Supports this by noting the triangular heads of earthly praying mantises, which resemble Thecla's face, and asks whether the mantis imagery is deliberate symbolism by Wolfe.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · close-textual-reading · theories: Hierodule masks represent insect, lamprey and leper; Famulimus is the leper hierodule

I'm pretty sure the ugly Hierodule masks of F, B and O are an insect, a lamprey (fish) and a human leper. I think Famulimus is the leper.

yep, earthly hierodules, uh praying mantises, have very triangular heads. So does Thecla (face anyway). Perhaps Michael Andre-Driussi also?

Praying Mantises are pretty scary looking up close but helpful to us because they eat a bunch of nasty, harmful bugs. Plus they pray! Deliberate symbolism by Wolfe?

2006-05-26 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes replying to b sharp #

Confirms b sharp's identification of the ugly hierodule masks of F, B and O as an insect, a lamprey and a leper, with Famulimus as the leper. Adds that the faces beneath a second layer of masks were exceptionally beautiful, and recalls Severian saying they resemble the walking statue in the garden of the House Absolute.
supports · close-textual-reading

This is correct. And there were exceptionally beautiful human faces -- to the point, as I recall, that they were no longer quite human -- underneath the second layer of masks. Severian, if I recall correctly, remarks that they appear to him to be what the walking statue in the garden of the House Absolute was based on.

--Dan'l
