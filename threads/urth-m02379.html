Thecla/Hethor

Urth list · 7 messages · 2006-05-05 to 2006-05-06

2006urthThe Book of the New SunMacronautmournings gloryMatthew Kingpthwndxrclzpb sharpMaru Dubshinki
The thread: Participants in this Urth thread debated whether Hethor could have travelled backward in time as a crewman on Tzadkiel's ship and whether the unnamed little gray man there might be Purn. Macronaut and mournings glory argued for the Purn link, with mournings glory citing a gray-haired little man in Urth's 'The Battles' chapter. Matthew King floated Hethor living backward in time, while b sharp kept the Thecla/Hethor/Appian connection and joked about cannibalized shipmates. A side debate over which book editions should be canonical for citations was split between those wanting page references and maru dubshinki, who argued the text is identical across editions. The thread ended without resolution, drifting into the citation question.
  • Macronaut: Hethor could be a time-traveling crewman on Tzadkiel's ship, and the gray man is Purn
  • mournings glory: Urth Chapter XIII's gray-haired little man links Hethor to Purn
  • Matthew King: Hethor may live backward in time, reflected in his familiarity with Severian
  • b sharp: Thecla, Hethor and Appian are connected; 'no shipmates' hints Hethor ate his crew
  • pthwndxrclzp: Asks which editions count as canonical for citations, favouring the SF Book Club edition
  • maru dubshinki: Edition choice does not matter since the text is identical and citations use book, chapter and verse

Themes: Time loops and causality, Ghosts, dreams and visions, Books, libraries and writing

2006-05-05 Macronaut replying to Matthew King #

Argues that Hethor could have travelled backward in time as a crewman on Tzadkiel's ship without special powers, and suggests the unnamed little gray man on the ship may be Purn. Bases this on Hethor's talk of feeding dying pets and Purn feeding the apports, most of which die, and cites the need for a wiki with page citations.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Hethor was a crewman on Tzadkiel's ship; Purn is the little gray man; Hethor and Purn are connected

Hethor wouldn't need special powers to move backward
in time if he had been a crewman on Tzadkiel's ship, a
la Gunnie/Burgundofara. Speaking of which, where is
the little gray man on the ship mentioned?

I don't know about shapeshifting, but the Hethor-Purn
connection is hard to ignore -- Hethor's talk about
feeding the "dying pets," and Purn feeding the apports
and noting that most of them die. So Purn seems a
likely candidate to me. Unless the gray man was
working in the apport zoo too.

This wiki thing is sounding better all the time,
especially if entries included page citations.

Macronaut

Grounds the idea in the introduction to Hethor and the paracoita, and notes the poster cannot recall their last meeting.

2006-05-05 b sharp #

Maintains a proposed connection between Thecla, Hethor and possibly Appian while conceding that the phrase "no shipmates" is cryptic. Responds to Matthew's reading that it means "they are not my mates," noting that Americans rarely use "mate" alone for friend, and floats the idea that Hethor killed and ate his shipmates.
disputes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Thecla, Hethor and Appian are connected; Hethor killed and ate his shipmates

Matthew, thanks for the correct text submission. I should have waited until my books were in front of me before posting. And I had considered your interpretation of "no shipmates" to mean "they are not my mates".

By interesting coincidence I was just discussing with a friend about how Americans don't use the word "mate" by itself to mean friend, usually. So I still consider the phrase "no shipmates" to be a bit cryptic.

Perhaps somewhere in the archives it was suggested that it meant Hethor killed his shipmates (and ate them or fed them to his pets?)

Either way, I'll still stick to my interpretation on the Thecla, Hethor and maybe Appian connection, for now.

2006-05-05 mournings glory replying to Macronaut #

Supports Macronaut's suggestion that Purn is linked to Hethor, arguing that a little gray-haired man in Urth's 'The Battles' chapter is a clue to that connection. Quotes Urth Chapter XIII, paragraph 3, where a little man with gray hair like Hethor's delivers the leader's ultimatum, and calls the point one not raised before.
supports · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: The little gray man in Urth resembles Hethor

URTH, Chapter XIII, "The Battles," paragraph 3:

"Again my semblance stepped forward. Having relieved myself of something of the contempt I felt for him, I was able to note his dragging foot and the arrogant angle at which he poised his head. The leader spoke again, and a little man with dirty gray hair like Hethor's told me, 'He desires you to do likewise. If you do not, we will kill you.'"

Excellent point, and one that I don't believe has been mentioned before.

2006-05-06 Maru Dubshinki replying to pthwndxrclzp #

Argues that the choice of physical edition of Wolfe's New Sun books does not matter, since the text is the same across printings and citations use book, chapter and verse rather than page numbers. Responds to pthwndxrclzp's question about which editions count as canonical, offering no textual evidence beyond the general claim that the wording does not vary.
disputes

Hmm. I don't really know. Does it actually matter? I mean, they are simply different physical editions, and so far as I know, the actual text doesn't vary (and varying texts, in the abstract sense, is the usual bone of contention), which would seem to be more important. It doesn't seem to matter even for citations, since everybody in the list seems to go Book, Chapter, and verse, rather than page numbers.

~maru
