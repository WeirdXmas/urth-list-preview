John C.Wright on Wolfe' "Unreliable Moral Narrative"

Urth list · 8 messages · 2011-11-23 to 2011-11-28

2011urthLee BermanGwern BranwenDavid StockhoffJames WynnJeff WilsonDan'l Danehy-Oakes
The thread: Thread centers on John C. Wright's essay about Gene Wolfe's unreliable moral narration. Gwern Branwen and Lee Berman both support Wright's analysis, which Berman praised for its clarity while disliking its socially conservative, anti-abortion stance. Berman noted Wright's admission that most Wolfe readers already see the social critique in narrators who casually accept prostitution, slavery and torture. Branwen extended the idea across Wolfe's work, citing The Fifth Head of Cerberus and An Evil Guest. David Stockhoff then argued that Skip's sexual history with Chelle's mother is left to the reader's imagination rather than to moral evasion. The thread ends with the discussion moving from the essay's ideas to a close reading of one narrator's silences, with no clear resolution.
  • Gwern Branwen: Unreliable moral narration recurs across many Wolfe works, including The Fifth Head of Cerberus and An Evil Guest
  • Lee Berman: Wright's analysis is clear and useful, though his anti-abortion stance is distracting
  • David Stockhoff: Skip's sex with Chelle's mother is left to the reader's imagination because the narrator withholds it

Themes: Unreliable narration, Power, rulers and politics, Justice, torture and mercy
