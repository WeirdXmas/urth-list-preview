Themes: Gnosticism, heresy and other theologies

2002-02-11 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Argues that Harold Bloom admires David Lindsay and Crowley because their work is openly Gnostic, so he would likely dislike Wolfe's more ambiguous, semi-Gnostic The Book of the New Sun. Grounds this in Bloom's apparent enthusiasm for A Voyage to Arcturus, the only Lindsay novel he is thought to mention, and then adds a bibliographic plug for Sirius Fiction booklets on Wolfe and Crowley.
disputes · theology-and-religion · theories: Bloom would dislike Wolfe because Wolfe is only semi-Gnostic

My strong impression is that Bloom enjoys Lindsay and Crowley so very much because their work (well . . . A VOYAGE TO ARCTURUS, let us say, since I've never seen Bloom mention any other Lindsay but I would bet that he has read at least a few others, if not all of them) is so unambiguously Gnostic.

As such, he would probably not like Gene Wolfe's fiction, even ambiguously semi-Gnostic works such as TBOTNS.
