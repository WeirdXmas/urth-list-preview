The thread: Thread opened by MBS 808 asking which younger writers might be compared with Gene Wolfe, with questions about VanderMeer, Erikson and Gentle. Matthew Weber disputed that Erikson matches Wolfe, calling him standard epic fantasy with more moral ambiguity than Tolkien. Other posters offered recommendations: cilluff1 (VanderMeer, Chiang, Carroll, Mieville), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (Fowler, Gaiman, Mieville, Willis), and Phoebe Davis, who named Tim Powers as the closest match. Dan'l then backed Powers, and matthew.malthouse exchanged tastes in other writers such as Zindell and Reynolds. The thread ended as a list of recommendations with no shared reading of Wolfe.
  • MBS 808: asks which younger writers compare with Wolfe
  • Matthew Weber: Erikson is engaging but falls short of Wolfe
  • cilluff1: recommends VanderMeer, Chiang, Carroll and Mieville
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: lists post-Gibson writers sharing Wolfe's virtues, then champions Tim Powers
  • Phoebe Davis: Tim Powers is the closest match for density and Catholic themes

2003-02-28 Matthew Weber #

Argues that Steven Erikson, whom the poster is currently reading, is engaging but falls short of Gene Wolfe, calling his work fairly standard epic fantasy with more moral ambiguity than Tolkien. Responds to a list member's question asking whether Erikson or Mary Gentle might compare with Wolfe as a writer of the younger generation.
disputes

I'm reading Steven Erikson right now, and I'm having a great time with him, but I wouldn't put him anywhere near GW's level as a writer. His world-building and plotting are impressive, and the books are very engaging & entertaining, but when all's said & done they're fairly standard epic fantasy (though with more moral ambiguity than your standard Tolkien knockoff).

2003-02-28 Phoebe Davis #

Recommends other writers who might compare with Gene Wolfe, naming Tim Powers as the closest match for density, elegance and Catholic themes, along with Jonathan Carroll, Dan Simmons, James Blaylock, China Mieville, Neil Gaiman, Pat Murphy and Sherri Tepper. Responds to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's question about younger writers, citing Powers's Declare and the Last Call series as examples.
answers

Enjoy Gentle will track down Erickson. Jonathan Carroll is a treasure as is Dan Simmons and James Blaylock. I thought King Rat by Mieville was a better read if not as dense as Perdido St. Certainly Neal Gaiman, Pat Murphy, would include Sherri Tepper's early work.

The one writer that I've ever felt compares with Wolfe on several levels is Tim Powers. Powers is Roman Catholic, "Declare" is his latest and probably the most emblematic of that. I believe Nick mentions that book in his Washington Post article on GW. Powers' writing style while not identical to Wolfe is elegant and dense. When I owned my own SF bookstore, I must have sold 40 copies of "Last Call" in hardcover. Last Call was followed by Expiration Date and Earthquake Weather.

Phoebe

2003-02-28 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Recommends Tim Powers as a writer comparable to Wolfe and suggests On Stranger Tides as a starting point, praising its density and pirate adventure. Responds to Phoebe's remark that Powers compares with Wolfe, adding that Powers is his own writer rather than a descendant of Philip K. Dick, with Charles Williams as a possible predecessor.
supports

Aaargh. I can't _believe_ I left Tim out of my list. I even had my copy of "Earthquake Weather" in my pocket when I was=20 making it.=20

Last Call / Expiration Date / Earthquake Weather is undoubtedly Tim's masterpiece to date, but I'd suggest ON STRANGER TIDES as possbily the best starting place. It isn't quite as dense as=20 the trilogy, but dense enough to challenge (well, at least to challenge anyone who doesn't swallow Wolfe neat), stands alone, and is a ripping good pirate story. (Surely there isn't anyone out there immune to the lure of the Pirates of the Caribbean...)

Of course if Tim is anyone's "progeny" it's Philip K. Dick's, but even that's too much to claim. He's definitely of his own making. And if he has a predecessor, it isn't Dick or Wolfe, but Charles Williams...

--Blattid
