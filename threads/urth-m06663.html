The thread: The thread began as a joke about Silk being Calde, but drifted into 2008 election talk, with Paul B comparing Obama's image to Silk among the Vironese. Dave Tallman drew out the parallel by listing how the Vironese hopes in Silk went badly. John Watkins, Gwern Branwen and brunians argued over whether Wolfe's interview remarks on politics, Christianity and Bush made him a serious political thinker, with Watkins calling the interviews silly. Most later posts were social chat about politics and bumper stickers rather than readings of the books, and the thread faded in 2010 with t-shirt and sticker jokes.
  • Dave Tallman: Silk's Vironese hopes went badly, and the new Calde may fare better
  • Gwern Branwen: Wolfe is serious about politics and Christianity, and his interviews show it
  • John Watkins: Wolfe's interview remarks are provocative for effect and sillier than his political thinking in Long Sun
  • brunians: Wolfe is a profound political philosopher even for a reader who dislikes Bush
  • Matthew Keeley: Wolfe seems disaffected with Bush, and reading across the spectrum brings one closer to truth
  • Paul B: Obama's image resembles Silk's among the Vironese, later sharing a Silk is Calde sticker

Themes: Power, rulers and politics, Myth and folklore sources, Books, libraries and writing

2008-11-05 Dave Tallman replying to Paul B #

Argues that the Vironese hopes placed in Silk in The Book of the Long Sun went badly, listing five outcomes: false expectations of miracles, a corrupt council, hostile foreign allies, a hostile new world, and Silk's falling popularity. Responds to Paul B.'s comparison of Silk to Obama by drawing that parallel out and ending with a hope that the new Calde fares better.
supports · close-textual-reading

Indeed. Things didn't work out quite as well as the Vironese hoped in the case of Silk, however:

1) People expected miracles and the favor of the gods when Silk himself had lost faith.
2) It was soon business as usual for the corrupt Ayuntamiento. 2) Silk reached out for allies in a neighboring country, only to find they had a hostile agenda of their own.
3) Many who followed his lead went to a greener world and found the environment quite hostile.
4) Silk was far less popular on leaving office than when he started.

I hope things work out better for this Calde...

2008-11-05 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes replying to Bruce Hayles #

Reflects on the 2008 US presidential election, arguing that Obama's victory and the serious candidacies of Clinton and Palin make America's myth that anyone can become President a little truer. Off-topic social commentary; it claims Wolfe would appreciate this and notes remaining barriers for gay, Muslim or atheist candidates.
chats

One thing about this election that I think GW would appreciate.

A nation lives by its myths about itself. One of America's myths about itself
is that "anybody can grow up to be President."

This year, we made that a little bit truer. Not just with the election of Obama,
but with the taking-seriously of the candidacies of Hillary Clinton and Sarah
Palin, we eliminated the hidden truth: "...as long as they're a white male."

There are still barriers. An openly gay, Muslim, or atheistic person still probably can't be President. But today our myth about ourselves is just a little bit truer than it was yesterday.

Every nation has myths about itself, but America actually seems to lurch, a little backwards, a little forwards, towards making its myths about itself true.

No. He's quite serious about it.

No. Some are, but many aren't. For example, I just tried googling some quotes from an interview by Nick Gevers and by Melissa Mia Hall, and no dice.

It's a pity, because some of the material is really good. For example, Gene's essay "Books in The Book of the New Sun" has a lot of interesting material on the Ultan scene. I quote the end:

"...In writing the legends, I have supposed facts and old stories to have become confused with others, a rascally technique which has earned me at least one vehement accusation of plagiarism.* Thus, for example, the custom of an academic thesis is confounded with the legend of Theseus in "The Tale of the Student and His Son.' The fourth book, as the astute reader will have guessed long ago, is _The Book of the New Sun_ itself. And now we are come again to the notion of recurrence, of the library folded in upon itself like the Klein bottle. This notion of recurrence seems to me to circle our Earth (you may spell it as you like) like the Midgard serpent which clasps its tail in its mouth and is brother to the wolf Fenris. For the library of Master Ultan is in _The Book of the New Sun_, and _The Book of the New Sun_ is in his library. And you are the readers of that book."

Some of his essays like "Nor the Summers as Golden: Writing Multivolume Works" are just intrinsically interesting. I would quote the ending from that as well, since it was an very eloquent one, but it appears I forgot to make a copy. :(
