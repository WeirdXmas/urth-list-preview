1998-05-03 Alice Turner #

Asks whether Severian dies at several points in The Book of the New Sun, beginning with his drowning, and defends the young Severian against charges of being a jerk. Also asks readers to identify the paintings in Rudesin's pinakotheka, suggesting one is Neil Armstrong on the moon and another resembles Rossetti or Burne-Jones.
questions · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian dies at several points in the story

I got so muddled by all the talk of numerical Sevs that I went back to Shadow to check---and I'm still muddled. Doesn't anyone but me think he actually dies at various points, and the first is when he drowns? How can you talk of Sev1 as being such a jerk--he's just a kid, and how do we know what he would turn out like? Triskele, Vodalus, Valeria and Thecla are still to come in his hasty adolescence.

But enough of that. Re Rudesin's pictures: The one he is cleaning is obviously of Neil Armstrong on the moon. Does anyone recognize the others? "...a dancer whose wings seemed leeches, a silent-looking woman who gripped a double-bladed dagger and sat beneath a mortuary mask." Fantastic realism reigns in the pinakotheka, not the "drippers and spitters" currently popular in the House Absolute.

That second one seems sort of late Victorian, Rosetti or Burne-Jones, doesn't it? Anyone hear a bell ring? Of course, they could all be science fiction art, covers of magazine issues Wolfe has appeared in---that would be a good joke! (And a real possibility.)

-alga-

1998-05-08 C R Culver #

Suggests that a silent woman with a double-bladed dagger beneath a mortuary mask in Rudesin's pictures may recall Lady Macbeth, possibly through an old painting or a classical Greek play. Promises to post later the connections he has found between Macbeth and The Book of the New Sun, though he has not had time yet.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Rudesin's silent woman recalls Lady Macbeth

< a silent-looking woman who gripped a double-bladed dagger and sat beneath a mortuary mask.>

I don't know why Lady Macbeth comes to mind. I would say it is either an old painting of her, and there are many, or perhaps something from a classical Greek play.
Sooner or later, I shall post the connections I've found between Macbeth and the BotNS. But, I've not had much time lately.
