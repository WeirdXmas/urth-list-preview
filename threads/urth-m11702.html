Ships and Empires

The thread: Posters debated whether the Autarch's empire can really be interstellar, given that ships only near light speed would take years to reach nearby stars. David Stockhoff argued that historical empires managed distance and that Wolfe simply omits such details, while Andrew Mason called the reach exaggerated, citing the Whorl's travel times and Vodalus's confirmation of the empire's extent. Gerry Quinn held that the books show FTL communication through the mirrors, leaving the empire's extent uncertain and the Whorl story inconsistent. 2010-12-11 David Stockhoff replying to Andrew Mason #

Argues that interstellar empires need not be ruled through constant travel, citing historical empires such as Persia, Egypt under the Sultans, and the Roman Empire to show how distance was handled. Responds to Andrew Mason's objection that a land four years away cannot really be ruled, and suggests Wolfe omits such details because they are not worth exploring.
disputes · genre-and-craft

Doesn't it all depend on the kind of empire you are thinking of? There are rigid empires and flexible ones; personal ones (like Napoleon's and Alexander's) and more structured ones. These handle distance differently.

Consider how long it took British ships to reach India.

Consider that when Peter the Great died, it took about 5 years for this news to reach Paris.

Consider that the Persian Empire consisted of satrapies which had to be reconquered every few decades even under Cyrus.

Egypt under the Sultans was independent for decades but showed up as Turkish on maps long after the British co-opted it, because the Bey was nominally loyal to the Sultan.

When parts of Central Asia became independent in the 1880s, it took the Chinese 10 years to take it back. Each year their army campaigned, stopped to plant crops, overwintered, and moved on again. No one in the West took any notice except the British and the Russians.

But communications in the Roman Empire were comparatively rapid due to technology and the Middle Sea, and so we naturally relate to that kind of highly structured empire.

If your ships are constantly going out among the stars, you will have ships reaching the provinces as often as you have ships going out. If you have an elite in place in that world or a little king who depends on your support (say, blast pistols delivered annually), you don't need to go out there personally even every four years.

Even if every planet is relatively autonomous, it can still be part of an empire, if the empire is a nominal one or a mercantile one. I think this is why Wolfe doesn't go into the details---it's not worth it. Typhon's empire could have lasted only a century (like the American Empire will) and still stand as a high mark in human interstellar power.

2010-12-12 Lee Berman replying to Andrew Mason #

Argues that Book of the New Sun characters echo 1960s TV: Jolenta resembles Ginger from Gilligan's Island, and Hethor avoids Jonas as Dr. Smith avoids the Robot. Grounds this in Lost in Space and Hethor's avoidance of Jonas in the tunnel, on the path, and in the antechamber, as an aside to Andrew Mason's question about Jonas's family.
proposes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Jolenta modeled on Ginger from Gilligan's Island; Hethor is a Dr. Smith analog; Jonas is a Robot analog from Lost in Space

Gene Wolfe spent the decade before writing BotNS watching silly US TV shows with his kids as my parents did with me. The impact of 60's TV shows cannot be underestimated. I've previously identified Jolenta as modeled after Ginger from Gilligan's Island.

A classic book was converted to a classic movie in 1960- Swiss Family Robinson. This story in turn was converted to a cheesy, low-budget SF tv show called Lost In Space, featuring the Robinson Family, the nefarious, conniving Dr. Smith and their noble Robot servitor (Warning! Warning! Danger Will Robinson!).

It is quite apparent that Hethor has been avoiding Jonas since their first meeting. He hides in the tunnel through the Wall of Nessus, avoids him on the path to the House Absolute and avoids him in the antechamber. As a Dr. Smith analog, Hethor does not want to be recognized by his shipmate, the Robot-analog Jonas.

;- )
