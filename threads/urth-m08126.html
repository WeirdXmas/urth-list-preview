Cloak and Baldric

Urth list · 7 messages · 2009-02-17 to 2009-02-17

2009urthThe Book of the New SunGwern BranwenChris PJosh GellerSon of Witz
The thread: Readers asked how Severian's large sword on a baldric could work with a cloak, since the baldric seemed to pin one arm. Son of Witz raised the costuming question and whether a swordsman-like figure could exist if the sword is mainly cargo. Brunians argued Severian is not a duellist, could disassemble his staff-like weapon quickly, and would be deterred by his look and office. Gwern Branwen proposed a slit in the cloak so the sword hangs outside it, and Chris P agreed the issue is practical carriage, since the sword is built for executions. The thread ended in a practical consensus on carriage rather than a resolved dispute about defense.
  • Brunians: Severian is no duellist, so the large sword is not for combat and his staff-like weapon is easily managed
  • Son of Witz: the baldric over a cloak would constrict movement and pin an arm, raising the costuming problem
  • Gwern Branwen: a slit in the cloak lets the baldric and sword pass so the sword hangs outside
  • Chris P: the sword is built for executions, so the question is practical carriage rather than defense

Themes: Justice, torture and mercy, Power, rulers and politics, Robots, androids and artificial life

2009-02-17 Son of Witz #

Asks how Severian's large sword on a baldric would work with a cloak, since the baldric would seem to constrict the cloak and trap one arm. Frames it as a practical costuming question prompted by drawing Severian, and invites insight from people with historical recreation or Ren Fair experience.
questions

No, it's not a new subgenre, it's a costuming question.

So, Severian's got this huge sword on a baldric, slung over his (left) shoulder most of the time. Too big to hang from a sword belt.

But how does that work with a cloak? It would have to be worn OVER the cloak, but, unless the cloak was pushed back over the shoulders, the baldric would essentially trap his arms in by holding the cloak tight around his torso. It seems he could have one side free, at least, but the other would be constricted.

Or am I completely wrong? Am I mistaken, or is this not really much of a problem for a wandering swordsman?

Are any of you guys Ren Fair or Historic Recreation types? any insight here?

I ask because I was drawing Severian, and this suddenly struck me as a real problem.

/Devil in the details

~Witz

2009-02-17 Chris P replying to Son of Witz #

Argues that Severian's sword is built for executions rather than quick fighting, so carrying it is a matter of cargo: how to hold it without hampering movement. Responds to Witz's point that the sword's awkwardness would still be an objection, agreeing that the issue is practical carriage rather than defense.
disputes · close-textual-reading

The sword itself is very explicitly not designed for that kind of fighting. It is designed for executions; the balance and size guarantee that, no matter how you contrive to carry that thing, you're not going to get a quick graceful reaction with it. So yeah, I would say it becomes a cargo issue - the task is to carry it in a way that doesn't overly interfere with your movement.
