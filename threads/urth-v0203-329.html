2002-03-12 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Argues that industrial pollution plays little part in Wolfe's Commonwealth, with the poisoned water coming from its large population rather than residue of earlier ages. Answers G Willickers's question about evidence of Earth's pollution by pointing to M. John Harrison's Virconium novel The Pastel City and Miyazaki's film Nausicaa: Valley of the Wind as far-future pollution works.
answers

Industrial pollution doesn't seem to play a large part in the Commonwealth. The poisoning of the water seems more a result of the city's large population than any residue of previous ages.

(Pollution plays a more significant role in M. John Harrison's far future, but not "dying sun" series: Virconium. THE PASTEL CITY is one novel. IIRC, there are deserts of metallic rust, pools of corrosive agents, etc. Which, in turn, reminds me of Miyazaki's manga/anime film "Nausicaa: Valley of the Wind," which is very much about pollution lingering into the far future, changing society and the landscape.)
