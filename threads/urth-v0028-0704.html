2000-01-19 Dave Lebling #

Argues that Baldanders' scorn for the hierodules' short lives embodies the sin of seeking immortality, a theme drawn from C. S. Lewis, with Typhon embodying space colonization. Answers Talarican's question about Baldanders' tone by citing the "like dogs" remark at Castle Baldanders and noting that Wolfe's space colonies seem unhappy.
answers · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion · theories: Baldanders embodies the sin of seeking immortality; Typhon embodies the sin of space colonization; Hierodules' humility echoes C. S. Lewis

This one at least seems fairly clear. Baldanders' goal is, on more than one occasion, stated to be eternal life. Famulimus and Barbatus have "awesome cosmic power" but very short lifespans. Baldanders thinks they've completely missed the point.

Recall that there is a certain strain of C. S. Lewis in the hierodules' attitudes: Lewis believed (or at least wrote, in _Out of the Silent Planet_) that space colonization and the desire for immortality were sins. Baldanders embodies one of these sins, Typhon both; the cacogens neither. Their humility is certainly presented as a positive thing, contrasting with Baldanders' creepy Dr. Frankenstein-ish experiments (not unlike those in _That Hideous Strength_) and later, Typhon's equally nasty attempt at eternal life.

Have you ever noticed that none of Wolfe's space colonies seem to be very happy places?
(Then again, I suppose none of Wolfe's _places_ seem to be very happy.)

Dave Lebling
(aka vizcacha)

2000-01-19 Michael Straight #

Argues that C. S. Lewis did not hold space colonization or immortality to be sinful in themselves, but thought both disastrous because sin corrupts humanity. Responds to David Lebling's claim that Lewis regarded colonization and the desire for immortality as sins, reasoning that colonization would spread corruption and immortality would let evil grow without limit.
disputes · theology-and-religion

I don't think Lewis thought colonization and immortality were themselves sinful. It would be more accurate to say he thought either one would be disasterous because of sin's corruption of humanity. To colonize the universe would be to spread corruption to possibly untainted planets. And since death provides a chronological limit to how bad a person can become, immortality would make it possible for someone to become more and more evil forever.

-Rostrum
