Themes: Stories within stories, Time loops and causality

2000-10-20 Dave Lebling #

Relays Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's suggestion that the Chinese tale in the novel parallels the overall story, offering it as an explanation for Olivia's voice over the intercom. Argues that, as in the fable, Weer lives out his life and then is woken to relive it wiser, and asks what else the tale could mean.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · close-textual-reading · theories: Weer lives or dreams his life and is woken to relive it

From: "Dan'l Danehy-Oakes" <[email]>

Has the gang of three looked into the relationship of the Chinese tale to the overall story? It seems to me that that story (sorry, I'm at work, details will be sketchy) provides a possible explanation for Olivia's voice over the intercom. That is, just as in the fable, Weer lives or dreams his entire life, seeing the consequences of the choices he makes, and then at the end Olivia wakes him to live it all over again as a wiser man. I don't see the point of including that story unless it speaks to something else in the novel, as all the other included tales do. What is its relevance to the novel, unless it is that?

-- Dave Lebling
(aka vizcacha)
