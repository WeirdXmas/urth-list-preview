Great Wolfe Sentences

Urth list · 1 messages · 1999-07-21 to 1999-07-21

1999urthNicholas Gevers

1999-07-21 Nicholas Gevers #

Proposes a thread collecting sentences by Wolfe readers consider quintessentially Wolfean, and offers one from Nightside the Long Sun as a first example. Cites the sentence about the shade rising and a dulcet breeze declaring the day's heat empty lies, calling it pure Wolfean poetry.
proposes · close-textual-reading

It's occurred to me that in the two months remaining until ON BLUE'S WATERS appears and the floodgates of Wolfean discussion are once more opened, it might be diverting for people to indicate sentences by Wolfe that they consider quintessentially Wolfean, especially brilliant, especially paradoxical, or whatever. This was done quite entertainingly with Vance sentences on the Vance BBS list. Here's an initial contribution (from NIGHTSIDE THE LONG SUN, p. 323): "The shade had risen until no sliver of gold remained, and a dulcet breeze whispered soothingly that the dust and heat of vanquished day had been but empty lies." Pure Wolfean poetry.
