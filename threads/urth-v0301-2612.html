The thread: Readers argued over whether paraphrasing Typhon's words inside quotation marks was misleading, starting from maa32's reading that the Conciliator and the New Sun are equated so Typhon would be deterred by Severian's kneeling. Matthew Malthouse called the paraphrase inside quotes unfortunate and, if knowing, duplicitous, and rejected Marc's quoted words as false textual support. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes and Chris argued that deception needs intent and that Marc believed what he posted, while Andrew Bollen defended the quotes as reported speech and suggested flagging paraphrases. maa32 suggested agreeing to disagree, and the thread ended in impasse over the ethics of quotation rather than the scene itself.
  • maa32: Conciliator and New Sun are equated, so Typhon would be deterred by Severian's kneeling; admits paraphrasing and suggests agreeing to disagree
  • matthew.malthouse: Paraphrase in quotation marks misrepresents the text and is duplicitous if knowing
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: Deception requires intent to pass a paraphrase off as a quote; asks to avoid namecalling; proposes a quasi-quote mark
  • Chris: Calling it duplicitous assumes intent; quotes were inaccurate but Marc believed what he posted
  • Andrew Bollen: Quoting a paraphrase is not dishonest since it includes reported speech; flagging paraphrases would help

Themes: Christ figures and messiahs, Power, rulers and politics, Books, libraries and writing

2003-01-06 Marc Aramini #

Argues that in the Typhon scene the Conciliator and the New Sun are equated, so Typhon recognizes Severian as both and would be deterred by his kneeling. Grounds this in the scene's wording and the logic of Typhon tempting a savior figure, and responds to Roy's fixed reading by admitting paraphrasing and suggesting agreement to disagree.
disputes · close-textual-reading · allegory-and-typology · theories: Typhon recognizes Severian as both Conciliator and New Sun

Roy has a very fixed idea of the Typhon scene in mind - and I admit I am guilty (all the time) of paraphrasing. Even in quotes. We should agree to disagree about this scene - I can't read it without thinking that the Conciliator and the New Sun are equated, no matter how many times I look at it, and that Typhon of course recognizes that Severian is both the Conciliator, and that he is the New Sun, who will kneel before him if Severian does so. I just can't read it any other way. What's the point of having him tempt the "savior" figure here if he doesn't understand the importance of deterring him? Isn't there some kind of allegorical resonance here? Doesn't Satan know what's up?

No big deal.

Marc Aramini

2003-01-06 Matthew Malthouse #

Argues that a critic's paraphrase of Typhon's words, presented in quotation marks, misrepresents the text because no such wording exists in the book. Responds to Andrew Bollen's defence of Marc, insisting that quotation marks conventionally signal direct citation and so the passage gave a false impression of textual support.
disputes · close-textual-reading

Marc ascribed to Typhon words that are not in the text.

The use of quotation marks is conventionally to indicate a direct citation.

Unless you propose that we abandon the conventions what was presented to us was an interpretation apparently textually supported but for which in reality no such text exists.

Matthew
