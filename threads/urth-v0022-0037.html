Time Spans and the Conciliator

Urth list · 1 messages · 1998-12-16 to 1998-12-16

1998urthThe Book of the New SunDave Lebling

Themes: Christ figures and messiahs

1998-12-16 Dave Lebling #

Questions whether Agia's remark that the Conciliator may have lived thirty thousand years ago points to Jesus rather than Severian, and argues the time span between Typhon and the book is implausibly long. Asks why Severian would learn about the Conciliator from Agia if the book he gave Thecla already recounts it, suggesting the original Book stopped before the Claw.
questions · chronology-and-plot-logic · allegory-and-typology · theories: Agia's Conciliator remark originally refers to Jesus; The Book of the New Sun originally ended before the Claw

Something I noticed in the chapter of _Shadow_ dealing with the destruction of the Pelerine's altar. Agia is telling Sev about the Conciliator. She remarks that he may have lived thirty thousand years ago.

I have two wonderings about this.

First, I wonder if the original intent of this passage was to refer to Jesus, not Severian-as-Conciliator? As usual, it seems wrong for the span between the age of Typhon and TBoNS to be that long, given other evidence, though to me they seem emotionally correct, I must admit. (I've mounted this hobby-horse before, though, so I'll be quiet about it).

Second, why is Agia telling Severian about all this (and Severian finds it to be news) if the fourth book he brought to Thecla is _The Book of the New Sun_? Wouldn't he know all this stuff already? Hmm. Well, perhaps the original _Book_ only deals with the time up to the Conciliator's disappearance, and makes no mention of the Claw, a later addition like the pieces of the True Cross.

-- Dave Lebling
