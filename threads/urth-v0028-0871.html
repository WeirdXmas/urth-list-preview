The Beautiful Ones, They Allways Hurt U

Urth list · 1 messages · 2000-03-29 to 2000-03-29

2000urthThomas Jeenicke

Themes: Power, rulers and politics, Slavery and service

2000-03-29 Thomas Jeenicke #

Argues that the Beautiful Ones of Sidhe rule through deception and manipulation, that their ban on human reproduction is enforced by kidnapping newborns who are likely killed or enslaved, and that a surviving child could become a symbol that the humans' situation can be changed. Responds to a post questioning the galactic authority's passivity by citing the novel's passages about poor visitors, departure rights, and the animals' show.
proposes · close-textual-reading · allegory-and-typology · theories: Beautiful Ones kidnap and enslave human newborns; a surviving child becomes a symbol of resistance

Talarican wrote (digest urth v028.n169.2):

It must be considered that most people coming to Sidhe are very poor (p.92: "People still come, though, mostly the ones that are too poor to go anyplace else"), and authorities (galactic or otherwise) usually don't care much about that kind of peolple. Furthermore, the Beautiful Ones try not to get too much attention from otherworldly authorities: if somebody on another world ask about a human on Sidhe, that human is allowed to leave (p.93). Obviously, the Beautiful Ones fear that somebody with power could take an interest in their world and their'world's situation after all. The traders probably still come because there is still profit to make. And if a trader leaves the ship, it was his choice and he has to take the consequence (so they think, maybe).
I guess the strength of the Beautiful Ones is deception and manipulation. They look noble, but are not at all. Their manipulative skills must be great, though they aren't described. The humans on Sidhe being underdogs even before they came to the planet) don't think they can do much against the situation, and they don't work together! That working together can solve problems, is what the animals' show try to teach (p.95/96).

I think that it si forbidden to reproduce and, if it happens anyway, the newborns are kidnapped. My guess is that they are either killed then or raised as slaves - all humans on Sidhe are practically slaves. Why the Beautiful Ones were afraid of babies born on Sidhe right from the start, is difficult to say. But now, that the rule is implemented, it is clear, what a surviving newborn will be. The human will probably not be able to work wonders, but he will be a saviour insofar he is a symbol that there is a way to defend oneself against the Beautiful Ones (esp. if people are working together), that the miserable situation of the humans on Sidhe is not fate (not precribed by the "planets"), but can be changed.

Thomas
