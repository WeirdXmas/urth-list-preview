2000-12-20 Mr Mnemonic #

Recommends Philip K. Dick's Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep as an astounding book that others have loved, noting it inspired the film Blade Runner. Acknowledges that Dick's style differs from Wolfe's and invites discussion while the list waits, with a note on a reprinted edition title.
chats

Not a movie, but a nother non-wolfe-related reccomendation ^-^

Do Androids Deam of Electric Sheep, by Phillip K. Dick is an astounding book. While the style of writing does differ from that of Wolfe's, just about everyone that I've given this book to has said they loved it. I would assume that any of you scifi fiends out there like me have already read and discussed it, but I'm also sure there are some out there that haven't. This book was the inspiration for the movie "Blade Runner", and makes a lovely conversation piece if anyone cares to rant whilst we wait.

-Mnemonic

P.S. If anyone wants to get a copy, I believe it has now been reprinted under the title "Blade Runner (Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep?)"

2000-12-21 Michael Straight #

Recommends other Philip K. Dick novels, praising The Man in the High Castle and The Three Stigmata of Palmer Eldritch as better than Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep. Notes that Dick reportedly consulted the I Ching while writing High Castle, letting its readings decide plot turns, and also names Ubik and Martian Timeslip.
chats

Dick's _The Man in the High Castle_, and _The Three Stigmata of Palmer Eldritch_ are even better, in my opinion. _High Castle_ is just plain beautiful. The book features the I Ching, and Dick supposedly actually used it when writing the book: at points where the characters consult the I Ching, he consulted it and used whatever came up to help determine how the plot would go.

_Ubik_ and _Martian Timeslip_ are also good.

-Rostrum
