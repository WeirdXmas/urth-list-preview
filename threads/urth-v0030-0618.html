2001-08-07 Binah #

Proposes that the narrator of Return to the Whorl, apart from the editors, is a composite soul of Horn and Silk, with Silk predominant at the close. Grounds this in the Rose-and-Marble merger and a Blade Runner-style ghost-in-the-machine reading of Marble and her daughter, asking whether it fits.
proposes · genre-and-craft · theories: Return to the Whorl narrator is a composite of Horn and Silk; Silk asserts predominance at the close of Return to the Whorl

Sorry if this is a tired old subject, but I'm new to the list.

I assumed that the narrator of Return to the world ( aside from the editors) was an amalgam of the minds and souls of Horn and Silk- similar to the combination of Rose and Marble after Rose's physical death. I understand that Marble is a synthetic being, but I feel that Wolfe advances a kind of "Blade Runner/ A.I." type ghost in the machine philosophy with Marble and her daughter. Couldn't the narrator be a composite like soul with Silk asserting predominance at the close of Return?
thanks,
aadair

2001-08-08 Michael Straight #

Argues that the narrator of Return to the Whorl is a merger of Silk and Horn, with Silk's soul having died into despair and Horn's spirit joining him until Silk heals and Horn's spirit may finally die. Responds to Michael Straight's post by agreeing with his reading and pointing to Horn's hybrid corn lecture at the start of On Blue's Waters as foreshadowing.
supports · close-textual-reading · theories: Return to the Whorl narrator is merged Silk and Horn; Silk's soul died and Horn's spirit joined him; Horn's spirit dies once Silk heals

Welcome! You may not be aware that there is also a list specifically for Wolfe's Long Sun and Short Sun series. It was established as the books were coming out to keep people in the urth list from seeing spoilers. Now that the series is complete, I imagine it may get folded into the "urth" list, but for now most of the discussion about the Short Sun books has been on this list:

[email]

I'm "crossposting" this to that mailing list; if you'd like to continue the discussion, it might be better to restrict it to the "whorl" list.

This is controversial, of course, but my take is similar to yours. That Silk's soul had "died" - i.e. that he was overwhelmed by despair, and that Horn's spirit joined Silk's in some way long enough for Silk to heal, and that Horn's spirit possibly finally dies once Silk has healed (i.e. once he can admit to himself that he is Silk and that Hyacinth is dead).

Note how Horn's lecture on the benefits of hybrid corn at the very beginning of On Blue's Waters seems to forshadow this.

-Rostrum
