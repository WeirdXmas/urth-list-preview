Themes: Power, rulers and politics

2010-09-28 Andrew Mason #

Questions whether the word 'rule himself' means only owing fealty to no one else, arguing it also implies self-control and not ruling only for one's own interests. Clarifies his earlier point that Ymar and successors called themselves autarchs while Typhon and earlier rulers were called monarchs, though Commonwealth people applied 'autarch' loosely to them.
disputes · close-textual-reading

?

As I understand it, 'rule himself' doesn't just mean 'owe fealty to no one else', but 'control, discipline himself', 'not rule only in his own interests', etc. But I'll have to check the passage to be sure of that.

However, I don't think that matters to my original point. All I meant is that Ymar and his successors called themselves autarchs, that Typhon (and apparently earlier rulers) did not call themselves autarchs, but monarchs, but that people in the Commonwealth sometimes used 'autarch' for earlier rulers, because that was the term they were used to. This can be so whether or not there is any principled basis for the distinction.
