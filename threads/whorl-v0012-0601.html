The thread: Readers debated which figure in Wolfe's Sun books plays Dante's Virgil, and whether Dante's structure maps onto the series. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued for Krait or Sinew in the Long Sun books, while James Jordan argued for Oreb in the Short Sun books and later countered with the two boys anticipating Hoof and Hide, conceding that his earlier Krait remark referred to the rest of the novel rather than Dante. Adam Stephanides then proposed matching specific Long Sun episodes to specific Divine Comedy episodes rather than seeking a full parallel. The thread ends without consensus, with Jordan still favoring Oreb and the Dante mapping left as a set of local comparisons.
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: Krait or Sinew is Virgil in the Long Sun books, paralleling the three beasts of Inferno
  • James Jordan: Oreb is Virgil in the Short Sun books, mapping Blue, Green and Whorl onto Limbo, Inferno and Purgatorio
  • James Jordan: the two boys Horn meets anticipate Hoof and Hide, echoing Odysseus meeting his son
  • Adam Stephanides: read specific Long Sun episodes against specific Dante episodes rather than seeking a full parallel

Themes: Myth and folklore sources, Christ figures and messiahs, Fathers, mothers and children

2001-04-12 James Jordan #

Proposes that Oreb, not Horn, is the Virgil figure in the Short Sun books, since Oreb is ridden by Scylla and Scylla leaves him, mapping Blue, Green and Whorl onto Limbo, Inferno and Purgatorio. Grounds this in Horn's despair in On Blue's Waters, the Abandon hope inscription, and the way Seawrack and Nettle both serve as Beatrice, and suggests Mantis could write on the Dante and Odyssey allusions.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · source-and-allusion · theories: Oreb is Virgil in the Short Sun books; Blue, Green and Whorl map onto Dante's realms; Seawrack or Nettle is Beatrice

The logical candidate for Virgil is Oreb, especially since Oreb, while at one level a figure of the Holy Spirit, is throughout this time ridden by Scylla, a political and pagan type of character. True, Oreb is not left behind at the end, but Scylla has left him (right?). I wonder if more could be done with this. I recall that when Horn fell into the pit, in OBW, he says that this was the end of all his happiness. "Abandon hope, all ye who enter here"? Entrance into Inferno? The rest of Blue would be Limbus Patrum, Green would be Inferno proper, and Whorl would be Purgatorio.
Beatrice would seem to be both Seawrack and Nettle, in that Horn and later the Narrator frequently think of both of them in idealized terms. The real-life Beatrice, for those who may not know, was a young girl that Dante fell in love with, though he and she were married. Nothing happened, of course, this being courtly-love. And then she died very young. She became his ideal and inspiration, and the departed literary-Beatrice conducts Dante through Paradisio after his journey with the "good pagan" Virgil through Inferno and Purgatorio.
I suppose Mantis needs to produce a chapbook on literary allusions and underpinnings of the SS books, showing the Odyssey and Divina Commedia sequences.
Interesting. Wonder if Wolfe really intended it....?

2001-04-18 Adam Stephanides #

Proposes reading Long Sun episodes against specific episodes of Dante's Divine Comedy rather than seeking a full parallel. Compares Krait's rescue of Horn from the pit to Virgil leading Dante out of the dark wood and climbing Satan's body, and compares the demon-guided ditch crossing to Horn and Sinew boarding the inhumi-controlled lander.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · source-and-allusion · theories: Krait's rescue of Horn echoes Inferno's opening and Satan's pit

I think it might be more productive, instead of seeking a blow-by-blow correspondence with the Divine Comedy a la ULYSSES, to look for correspondences between specific episodes, not necessarily in order. Thus, Krait's rescue of Horn from the pit resembles the beginning of the Inferno, where Virgil leads Dante out of the dark wood. But it also resembles Dante and Virgil's climbing up Satan's body out of the lowest circle of Hell: Horn in the pit is at a low point, both physically and psychologically; the lowest circle of Hell was for traitors, and Horn becomes a traitor to his family, whatever the pressure he was under; and being lifted out of a pit by an inhumu is similar to climbing out of a pit using Satan as a ladder.

Similarly, in Cantos XXI-XXIII Dante and Virgil have to cross a series of ditches, and find that the bridge across one of them is shattered. A demon falsely tells theml there is another bridge further on, and convinces them to accept as guides to it a group of demons, who later turn on them. This is reminiscent of Horn and Sinew's boarding the lander which they think will take them to Whorl, but which is controlled by inhumi and aimed at Green.

--Adam
