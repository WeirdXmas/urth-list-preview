Severian as the Outsider?

Urth list · 3 messages · 2008-01-03 to 2008-01-03

The thread: Thomas Gamble proposed that Severian might be the Outsider, pointing to his unique status as a god outside the Whorl and to a dark visitor linked to the Claw, and asked what that would imply about Typhon and Silk. Matthew Keeley offered a tentative reading of a Wolfe interview answer saying Severian is a form of the Outsider rather than the reverse, taking it as a Catholic-style claim about being made in the Outsider's image. James B. Jordan supported that reading and identified the Outsider with the biblical Creator, adding that each person is made in God's image. The thread stayed on the interview reading and the theology, and ended without resolving Gamble's plot questions.

2008-01-03 thomas gamble #

Proposes that Severian may be the Outsider, citing his status as the only god outside the Whorl and his "dark" epithet, then linking a dark visitor with a glowing blue stone in Horn's hallucinations to the Claw. Asks what it would imply: that Typhon recognized Severian, and that Severian's visit to Silk on Sun Street had some purpose tied to Pas's future plans.
proposes · puzzle-solving · allegory-and-typology · theories: Severian is the Outsider

This may well have been covered, and if so I apologize for re- treading over old ground, but my question is; can a legitimate claim be made (even a decently founded speculation!) that Severian is the Outsider? First, he is marked as the only god existing outside the Whorl (makes sense- no reason for Sevvy to be in Typhon's seed-ship), is referenced as "dark" (stretching, i know). These two points alone, of course, do not make a for any kind of argument. However, when we get to Horn/Silk's journey in SS, and Horn's near-starvation/ hallucinations in the crude trap his son made, he is visited by a dark character (whom he believes to be the Outsider), wielding a glowing blue stone (the parallel seems too strong for this to be anything but the Claw).
If Severian is indeed the Outsider it raises some questions that perhaps you could shed some light on for me. First, it would mean that Typhon recognized and included Sev in his canon, albeit in an indirect way. This seems peculiar, but I suppose that I wouldn't preclude him for having some strange respect for the New Sun. Secondly, it means that Severian enlightens Silk on Sun Street, but to what end? To prevent Pas' future plans from coming to fruition?

Argues that the Outsider is the Creator-God of the Bible and that Severian, like everyone, is made in His image, in a special way. Agrees with Matthew Keeley's Catholic reading of Wolfe's interview answer that Severian is a form of the Outsider, adding that Wolfe would say each person is special.
supports · theology-and-religion · theories: The Outsider is the Creator-God of the Bible

You are not wrong. The Outsider is the Creator-God of the Bible, "outside" in every respect. Severian, like all of us, is made in His image, and in Sev's case, in a special way. But then, each of us is special, Wolfe would say.

2008-01-03 Matthew Keeley replying to thomas gamble #

Quotes Wolfe's interview answer that Severian is a form of the Outsider rather than the reverse, and reads it as a Catholic-style claim that Severian is made in the Outsider's image. Offers this as a tentative interpretation of Wolfe's own words, responding to Thomas Gamble's question about whether Severian is the Outsider.
informs · author-and-biography · theology-and-religion · theories: Severian was created in the Outsider's image

Actually, Wolfe discussed this issue in an old interview: http://www.urth.net/whorl/archives/v0006/0350.shtml

To quote:
"15. "Is the Outsider a form of Severian?" No. Severian is a form of" the Outsider.""

I tend to interpret that as a Catholic answer, something along the lines of "Severian was created in the image and likeness of the Outsider." I could be wrong though.

-Matt
