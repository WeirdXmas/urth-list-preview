Themes: Love and loss, Death and resurrection

2002-09-27 Joshua A. Solomon #

Argues that Severian's and Jonas's stories share one pattern: both seek love, find only shadows of it in women, then rise into space to receive divine love and are resurrected. Grounds this in Wolfe's stated view of The Book of the New Sun as a young man's search for love and in the parallel of Jonas's semi-prosthetic woman and his final ascent.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · theology-and-religion · theories: Jonas repeats Severian's search for divine love

Remember that Wolfe has claimed TBOTNS is a story of a young man (Severian) searching for love. The women he desires offer, at best, (platonic) shadows of the divine love that he really needs. At the end of TBOTNS, he blasts into space/heaven to get himself a piece of that divine love (although, of course, he already has it, as is demonstrated by his resurrections).

Jonas does a riff on the same theme. He desires a semi-prosthetic woman, remains unsatisfied, then steps into space/heaven to get himself something better. He gets (at least semi-) resurrected too.
