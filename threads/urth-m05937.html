What's So Great About Ushas?

Urth list · 5 messages · 2008-07-18 to 2008-07-18

2008urthThe Book of the New SunDave TallmanPaul BDan'l Danehy-Oakes
The thread: Readers argued over whether the Hierogrammates' decision to let Ushas destroy Urth was defensible. Dave Tallman disputed Paul B's claim that Ushas's gravitational effects are minor, citing the bells and tidal destruction in Baldanders's text, and asked how the white fountain could emit gravitational waves. He defended a deontological view and suggested the New Sun may be the lesser evil compared with Ragnarok. Paul B countered that the Hierogrammates cannot be seen as untainted because they chose an evil course, and dismissed the idea that panic would make a Ragnarok-era evacuation useless. Paul B also clarified that he holds a deontological morality without demanding brutal punishment, while objecting only to people calling themselves angels. The thread ends with the two sides still divided over the moral verdict, with no resolution.
  • Dave Tallman: Ushas's effects are severe and the Hierogrammates' choice was the lesser evil, defended on deontological grounds
  • Paul B: The Hierogrammates are tainted by choosing an evil course, and evacuation objections are weak
  • Paul B: Holds a deontological morality without demanding brutal punishment; objects to calling oneself an angel

Themes: The dying sun and the cosmos, Gods, angels and the Increate, Power, rulers and politics

2008-07-18 Dave Tallman #

Disputes Paul B's reading that Ushas's gravitational effects are minor, arguing that Baldanders's text says the bells toll the death of continents and that tidal forces cause the destruction. Asks how the spinning white fountain emits gravitational waves, whether it resembles a quasar, and whether the New Sun is the lesser of two evils compared with Ragnarok, while defending a deontological moral position.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theology-and-religion

I'm sorry, this is dead wrong. The actual phrase was "[the bells] toll the deaths of continents". Baldanders knew perfectly well what would happen, and he explains that "waves of gravity" are the cause.

We had different interpretations of the same text, it seems. Baldanders also said "... a white fountain has been drawn across the void for millennia. It spins as it flies, and in its motion it emits waves of gravitation.... These waves are too slight to render us giddy. Yet Ocean feels them and breeds new tides and fresh currents. ... Bells feel them the same way. Like Ocean, their mass is delicately poised. ... The force that made your bells sound has warmed the cold heart of Urth once more. Now they toll the death of continents."

I read that the gravitational forces were weak, and so mistakenly concluded that the primary effects on Urth were caused by heating. I see now that Jeff Wilson is correct and that tidal forces caused by gravitation will cause dramatic changes in sea level and also distort the earth's crust, bringing volcanos and earthquakes.

I still don't understand the bit about the fountain spinning and why this would cause it to emit "waves of gravitation." Is the white fountain supposed to be something like a quasar?

I've actually been advocating for putting them on an equal moral footing with humans, not demonizing them outright. That comes later! Killing the vast majority of a planet's population of your moral equals reflects badly on you. As I've insisted, a responsible moral framework (deontology) doesn't much care what the outcome of your actions is.

Even deontologists don't apply that standard absolutely. I've read that Kant argued it is always wrong to lie, even to a murderer asking for the location of his victim, but most say that the consequences of telling the truth require a lie in this case. The alternative to the New Sun was the Ragnarok future, in which the whole solar system dies and a pitiful remnant of humanity is evacuated. Is that the lesser of two evils?

2008-07-18 Paul B replying to Dave Tallman #

Argues that the Hierogrammates cannot be considered untainted because they chose an evil course of action, whether or not it was the lesser evil. Rejects the Ragnarok-evacuation alternative by saying speculation that panic would make evacuation useless is weak, since ordinary evacuations accepted panic over mass death.
disputes · theology-and-religion

That's not a relevant question. The heart of the matter is that an evil course of action was chosen, lesser or not. Thus, it's impossible to consider the Hierogrammates to be untainted.

As far as other possible courses of action, speculation about evacuation not being useful due to panic is weak at best. In more mundane evacuations panic was seen as an acceptable alternative to mass death.

2008-07-18 Paul B replying to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Responds to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes by saying he holds Rorschach's deontological morality but does not demand that everyone be moral or that every wrong be brutally punished. Offers a hypothetical about humans killing aliens to uplift survivors, calling such acts immoral without necessarily calling the actors evil, and objects only to them calling themselves angels.
disputes

Well, the only two Rorschachs I know are the psychologist and the Watchman. (Trailer recently came out - topical, hey?) If we're talking about the latter, as far as morality goes, sure I do - he's a posterboy deontologist. I just don't couple that with the expectation that everyone be moral in everything they do, and with the demand that all wrongs be brutally punished.

For example, if futuristic humans kill a bunch of aliens in order to uplift the survivors, I wouldn't be surprised, even though I'd think their action immoral and wrong. They'd have to have a pretty solid track record of doing shady things before I'd call them evil, even. I just wouldn't stand for their calling themselves angels, either.

Paul
