The thread: Posters tried to pin down the secret of the inhumi in Wolfe's Long Sun books. Lee Berman proposed that they cannot cross space unaided and must stowaway as parasites on human ships, citing a Nick Gevers interview and the examples of Quetzal and Krait. Marc Aramini argued the inhumi are a vestigial, parasitic remnant of humanity that cannot breed without fresh human blood, and offered a tongue-in-cheek alternative to Berman's stowaway idea. A third post, from Daniel Petersen, clarified that an earlier reference concerned libertarian free will rather than the political party, and asked whether Wolfe's political libertarianism matters to that question. The thread ends without resolution and drifts toward a side issue.
  • Lee Berman: inhumi are stowaway parasites who cannot travel space unaided
  • Marc Aramini: inhumi are a vestigial human parasite that needs fresh human blood to breed
  • Daniel Petersen: the libertarianism reference concerns free will, and Wolfe's political libertarianism is irrelevant to it

Themes: Beasts and the animal in us, Free will and choice, Power, rulers and politics

Wolfe's answer does not (to me) address nate's concern. Horn is clear that the secret of the Inhumi must be something which, if revealed, would threaten their entire race. I'm not sure if Wolfe is being deliberately unclear or whether he had forgotten this aspect of it with this answer.

My own answer tries (weakly) to springboard from Wolfe's answer. Those humans who prey on other humans generally rely on stealth and deception. Especially if we buy into Daniel Petersen's ideas regarding Wolfe's libertarianism, humans who prey upon other humans are to be considered parasites. One way of being a parasite is to be a stowaway.

Apologies for rehashing a previous discussion but I think that's the inhumi secret which, if revealed, would threaten their species. A parasitic species needs to be able to travel. Stuck in one place, they would quickly deplete their hosts and be left without food. The secret is that inhumi cannot, as they claim, fly through space. Given Wolfe's awareness of scientific detail, I don't think the impossibility of unassisted biological space travel is something that escapes his attention.

So, the inhumi are, by nature, a species which specializes in imitating a host and getting rides to other planets on their spacecraft. We see this practice in two of the best known inhumi, Quetzal and Krait.? 2012-11-26 Daniel Petersen replying to Lee Berman #

Clarifies that an earlier reference to Daniel Petersen's ideas on Wolfe's libertarianism concerned the analytic philosophy concept of libertarian free will, not the political party. Notes Wolfe's self-described political libertarianism but argues it is not relevant to a discussion of free will versus determinism, and asks whether that is so.
disputes · close-textual-reading

Especially if we buy into Daniel Petersen's ideas regarding Wolfe's libertarianism, humans who prey upon other humans are to be considered parasites.

Forgive me for being pedantic and patronising, but I just want to make sure everyone understands I was talking about the concept of libertarian freewill in analytic philosophy. I was not at all referencing the political position known as Libertarian (as distinct from Republican, Democrat, etc.). Wolfe happens to be self-professedly the latter, but that's not immediately relevant to a philosophical discussion about freewill vs. determinism. (Is it?)

-DOJP
