The Changeling in Orbit

Urth list · 1 messages · 1997-07-15 to 1997-07-15

1997urthRon Crown

Themes: Hidden identities and parentage, Unreliable narration

1997-07-15 Ron Crown #

Reports that Damon Knight, in his preface to a reprint of "Changeling" in an Orbit best-of collection, quoted Wolfe's whispered remark "The old man is dead, you know" as a clue to the story. Asks whether the old man is the changeling Peter Palmer/Palmieri, suggesting a PEACE-like scenario, or the father of Peter's boyhood playmate whom Palmer visits.
questions · close-textual-reading · author-and-biography · theories: The old man is the changeling Peter Palmer/Palmieri; The old man is the father of Peter's boyhood playmate

Nutria, you're in good company in your puzzlement over "Changeling;" when Damon Knight reprinted "Changeling" in a best-of Orbit collection from the first ten volumes; he prefaced it with a letter to Wolfe written when he bought the story in which he confesses that he "can't sort it out into any one consistent, linear, daylight-logic pattern." According to Knight, Wolfe revealed the secret years later when he whispered to him "The old man is dead, you know."

I'm not sure if this clears things up for me, though; is the "old man" the "Changeling" (Peter Palmer/Palmieri) in which case we have a PEACE-like scenario or is the old man Wolfe is referring to the father of Peter's boyhood playmate that Palmer/Palmieri visits (if I've got the details right).

I'd be interested in seeing your questions.
