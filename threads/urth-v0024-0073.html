Themes: Memory and forgetting, Myth and folklore sources

1999-03-18 Tony Ellis #

Corrects a earlier remark that the Moses passage is Deuteronomy, noting the title was "the Compassionating" for God, and argues that Marie's Bible differs because the text was garbled over retellings. Leans toward the explanation that the hut people are from Severian's time but "chant-caught", and suggests Marie's reading reflects her Urthian mind or a faulty book.
proposes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion · theories: Hut people are from Severian's time, chant-caught; Marie's Bible reading reflects Urthian perception or a corrupted book

(Just to pedantically set the record straight, Moses is only referred to as he\him. The title you're remembering, "the Compassionating", referred to God.)

I used to think that the differences were just a consequence of what Severian wrote being translated back into English, but in fact there are some big chunks from the original that are just plain missing. It reads more like one of the stories from Wonders of Urth and Sky that has been retold so many times almost all the original sense has gone.

Yes, I'm leaning more and more towards Explanation Two, that the people in the hut are actually from Severian's time, but "chant-caught". Perhaps Marie reads Deuteronomy the way she does because that's the way her Urthian mind perceives the text, or perhaps she's reading verbatim, and it's the book itself that is at fault.
