The thread: The thread asks whether Urth can literally be Green, as Ushas is Blue, in Wolfe's Urth/Lune and Blue/Green Whorl systems. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argues the two are separate physical systems linked only symbolically, and Jordon Flato and David Stockhoff reject the literal identity as flattening the thematic resonance between the worlds. Lee Berman concedes the theory needs many contortions but says trying to support Marc Aramini's theory was more stimulating than refuting it. Jeff Wilson and Gerry Quinn then debate whether a black hole could produce the needed lensing and sky effects, with Quinn judging it physically implausible. The thread drifts into physics and ends without agreement.
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: Blue/Green Whorl and Urth/Lune are separate physical systems; any link is thematic or symbolic
  • Jordon Flato: Literal Green-Urth reading flattens thematic resonance; metaphorical reading is more interesting
  • DAVID STOCKHOFF: Literal Urth-Green equation strains the text and is an obsession
  • Lee Berman: Theory needs many contortions, but supporting Aramini's theory stimulated more thinking than refuting it
  • Jeff Wilson: A single black hole cannot produce the lensing; a ring of black holes might but would be conspicuous
  • Gerry Quinn: Seeing one's own star system from afar is physically infeasible within a galaxy

Themes: The dying sun and the cosmos, Gods, angels and the Increate, Deep time and ruins

2011-01-11 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that the Blue/Green Whorl system and the Urth/Lune system are separate physical systems, with any link between them purely thematic or symbolic. Grounds this in the fact that Urth's red sun is visible from Blue, so the two must be different systems unless Ushas and Lune travel across the stars and back in time.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading

Look. I'm sorry, but any connection between the Blue/Green(/Whorl) system and the Urth/Lune(/Ushas) system is purely thematic/symbolic -- NOT physical. Urth's sun, still red, is visible in the skies of Blue. Unless you want to propose that Ushas and Lune, in their distant, post-Severian, future, are transported (a) across the sea of stars and (b) backwards in time, they're different systems.

There's all sorts of good thematic/symbolic stuff going on, of course, and StCroix/SteAnne can also be brought in here, but please don't confuse this hermeneutic level with that of the fictive "reality."

2011-01-11 Jordon Flato replying to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that treating Urth as literally Green, as Ushas is Blue, flattens the thematic resonance between the two worlds and requires too much contortion, so the poster rejects the idea. Responds to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's analogy comparing Urth's greenness to Moby Dick being God, saying the metaphorical reading is more interesting than the literal one.
disputes · theology-and-religion · theories: Urth is literally Green

This is it for me. The interest in the resonance between Urth/Green and Ushas/Blue (my preference there) is pretty much flattened if we are asked to pretzel our understanding in order to make it literally true. The amount of gymnastics required to make it work are, to me, astounding...and yet it adds little to the thematic resonance which is already there, in existence, and pretty clear (to me, to me, of course). The equation doesn't balance. If I felt some really dramatic metaphoric or symbolic resonance was served by having them be literally the same, I might be more inclined to go with it where it leads. At this point, it actually flattens that resonance for me, and so I reject it.

2011-01-11 Jeff Wilson replying to Gerry Quinn #

Argues that a single black hole cannot produce the lensing effect needed for the described sky, since it does not close light paths outside its event horizon, while a ring of black holes might but would be conspicuous. Responds to Gerry Quinn's point about a giant black hole filling the sky by noting that negative curvature of space and time would work easily but implies interference from the Yesodis.
disputes · theology-and-religion · theories: Yesodis interfere with the cosmos from above

A single black hole could not do it; by definition, it does not close the path of light outside its event horizon. Several in a ring might do it , but they would be very noticeable.

If you allow negative curvature of space and time, you can do it easily, but that implies interference from above by the Yesodis.
