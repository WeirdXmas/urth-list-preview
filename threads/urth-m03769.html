Seven American Nights question

Urth list · 6 messages · 2007-07-15 to 2007-07-16

2007urthGwern BranwenDan'l Danehy-OakesKurt SchwindaaronRuss Allbery
The thread: Aaron asked why the ocean is yellow in the opening of Seven American Nights and why the American objects when Nadan points it out. Kurt Schwind backed Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's view that it is industrial pollution, offering no textual evidence. Aaron disputed this, arguing the yellow comes from river sediment and citing research, while noting the captain's oil-like phrase. Gwern Branwen agreed the cause is soil erosion, linking the captain's 'bleeding to death' remark to it and citing Jared Diamond and the Yellow River. Russ Allbery countered that erosion has a real American precedent in the Dust Bowl, and the thread ends with the erosion reading standing against the pollution reading.
  • Kurt Schwind: yellow sea is industrial pollution, poisonous waste, though unsure why yellow
  • Aaron: yellow comes from river sediment erosion, not oil, citing research
  • Gwern Branwen: soil erosion explains it, captain's bleeding remark refers to erosion
  • Russ Allbery: erosion is plausible in America, citing the Dust Bowl
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: suggested the yellow ocean is industrial pollution (cited by Kurt Schwind)

Themes: Time loops and causality, Deep time and ruins

2007-07-16 aaron replying to Kurt Schwind #

Argues that the yellow sea in Seven American Nights comes from river-borne sediment rather than oil pollution, despite the captain's line about the country bleeding to death. Responds to Kurt Schwind's earlier thread by citing research sources that confirm the erosion explanation and noting the captain's phrase had suggested crude oil.
disputes · source-and-allusion · theories: Yellow sea results from river sediment erosion, not oil

Thanks for everyone's input on this. I had always assumed that the yellow color came from oil or gasoline etc. leaking into the sea. The only thing that, to me, disputes the pollution theory is the fact that the captain tells Nadan: "It's his country...bleeding to death." That is why I assumed crude oil "bleeding" from the earth. But, after doing a little research, I found several sources that confirm the erosion theory. It seems that dirt carried into the ocean from rivers turns the water yellow (in the worst cases) or green (not quite as bad). Again, thanks to all.

Aaron Singleton

2007-07-16 Russ Allbery replying to Gwern Branwen #

Argues that soil erosion has a real precedent in the United States, pointing to the Dust Bowl as an example of massive erosion caused by poor farming and land use. Responds to Gwern Branwen's remark that soil erosion sounds more like a foreign or ancient problem than an American one, and offers a NASA Earth Observatory page on the Dust Bowl as supporting information.
disputes · source-and-allusion

Ah, but see the Dust Bowl. Soil erosion has happened before in the US on a massive scale due to poor agricultural practices or use of land for things it just can't sustain.

See http://earthobservatory.nasa.gov/Study/DustBowl/ for more information, and there are many other interesting pages via Google.
