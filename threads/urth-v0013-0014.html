Fifth head times 2 brothers

Urth list · 1 messages · 1998-06-09 to 1998-06-09

1998urthNeal Smith

Themes: Doubles, twins and clones, Beasts and the animal in us

1998-06-09 Neal Smith #

Argues that the Annese race and humanity in The Fifth Head of Cerberus were once one people, using the middle novella's twin brothers Eastwind and Sandwalker as a metaphor for two separated races. Grounds this in the textual clues already discussed and the parallel of one brother's death leaving the other uncertain of his identity, while admitting the migration's timing is unclear.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · close-textual-reading · theories: Annese and humans share a common origin; Eastwind and Sandwalker metaphor for the two races

I agree with Tony that the existine Annese race was an offshoot of the human race (or vice versa) at one time. In addition to the direct clues, which have been mentioned several times, I also think it is stated metaphorically, in the middle novella.

The two brothers (Eastwind and Sandwalker) were separated at birth, and came together later. They were convinced that they would switch bodies, then one was killed, leaving the remaining uncertain which one he had been originally. While this does not strike me as plausible in the story (there should be too many physical clues as to which he was), it is a good metaphor for the two races. That they were brothers, long separated, indicates another piece of evidence that the two races came from the same source, though long since separated. One of the brothers dying off and the other being uncertain of identity echoes the same issues with respect to the two races.

So, in addition to the clues presented over and over again, I think the metaphor indicates also that the reader was meant to think of the two races as linked. When in time the migration should be placed, however, is not clear to me.

Neal Smith
