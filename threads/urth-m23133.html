The Perils of Partnership

The thread: Fred Kiesche received three replacement copies of Gene Wolfe short story collections from an Amazon Marketplace seller, all ex-library books with stickers and wrapping, and complained the seller did not disclose this. Jeff Wilson and Matthew Weber argued that ex-library copies listed as used can be acceptable and that defaced copies are fair condition at best, while Antonio Pedro Marques advised checking vendor ratings and leaving feedback. The thread stayed entirely on used-book buying practice and Amazon sellers, with Kiesche offering the copies away, and ended without any discussion of Wolfe's fiction.
  • Fred Kiesche: undisclosed ex-library copies were a bad buy and he wanted to give the books away
  • Jeff Wilson: ex-library copies are acceptable for used listings unless sold as collectible
  • Matthew Weber: defaced books should not be graded good or better; advised a poor seller rating
  • António Pedro Marques: vendors are usually honest; check ratings weighted by review count and leave feedback

2012-03-22 Matthew Weber replying to António Pedro Marques #

Advises a fellow poster to give a poor seller rating on Amazon Marketplace after receiving a misdescribed book, and to revise it only if the seller pays return postage and a refund. Reflects on how open marketplaces let anyone sell withdrawn library books, muddying the trade for experienced booksellers; the post is off-topic from Wolfe and responds to Fred-directed complaint by Antonio Pedro Marques.
chats

Fred, there are Amazon Marketplace vendors that pull this crap all the time--listing books in "good" or "very good" condition when they've been defaced with library stamps, written in, etc. I suggest being a complete pain in the ass and giving the seller a "poor" rating (and giving specific reasons why the seller earned that rating). Quite often the seller will contact you and ask that you revise the rating, which you should only do on condition that they pay for return postage and a refund.

On the one hand, it's good that Amazon Marketplace has allowed all kinds of booksellers to piggyback on Amazon's infrastructure and keep their businesses going. The downside is that any schmuck with a stack of withdrawn library books can go online and sell them, and in so doing muddy the waters for experienced bookmen who know their trade and are trying to do a conscientious job of describing what they're selling. I've been where you are several times, and it's extremely annoying.
