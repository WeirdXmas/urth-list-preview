Themes: Hidden identities and parentage, Time loops and causality

1999-03-17 Tony Ellis #

Argues that the Jungle Garden couple in The Shadow of the Torturer and The Claw of the Conciliator may be brother and sister who are chant-caught people from Severian's own era, dating them to the 1930s or later. Responds to Roy's question about the husband/brother discrepancy by offering the chant-caught explanation, then says it hopes the couple's identity is never explained.
proposes · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Jungle Garden couple are chant-caught people from Severian's time; Couple are brother and sister, not husband and wife; Couple date from the 1930s or later

The latter, I suspect. But, don't forget that there are actually two explanations for what the couple are doing in the Jungle Garden in the first place: Explanation One, that the twisty turny paths of the Garden twist through time as well as space, and Explanation Two that these are actually people from Severian's own time, who are simply "chant-caught". If Explanation Two is the right one, then the couple really could be brother and sister, and just think that they are husband and wife - in which case Sev (and Wolfe) are still right on both counts.

Nice idea, but still too long ago to pass my "mail plane" argument. We need to be thinking 1930s or later, I say. And maybe not much later: studying art in Paris, 3-prop mail planes and taking quinine-water for malaria all have a pretty pre-60's feel about them.

This is lots of fun, but in a way I hope we -don't- find out who the couple are. I've always loved this episode for its haunting strangeness: we're in a world so far in the future we can barely imagine it, and here in a hut in the botanic gardens are two people from our own time talking about Paris and reading Deuteronomy. Do we want this explained away?

1999-03-17 Peter Stephenson #

Argues that the Robert and Marie episode in the botanic garden hut should stay unexplained, since the book's strangeness is intact, and raises the language problem of how Severian understands them. Responds to Tony Ellis's hope that the couple's identity is never revealed, citing the passage's Deuteronomy quotation and the mismatch between the reading and the biblical source.
disputes · close-textual-reading

It's not even quite Deuteronomy. The original biblical quotation is about Moses, who isn't mentioned by name in what they're reading --- I forget the term used, it's some generic SF grand-sounding title.

This brings up the question of language. Severian can understand them, and doesn't speak English, French or Zulu; it seems the language used by the three is the local one, with the added touch that the bible comes out a bit weird. Everything in this passage adds weirdness by the bucketload. I don't think we need to worry too much about it being explained away.

corncrake
