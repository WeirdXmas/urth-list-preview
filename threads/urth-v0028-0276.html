NORTH/SOUTH

Urth list · 6 messages · 1999-10-27 to 1999-10-28

1999urthThe Book of the New SunMatthew MalthouseGreene, CarltonAlex David GroceUrash, TomJames Jordan
The thread: Posters debated whether Blind Ultan in The Book of the New Sun is modeled on Jorge Luis Borges, a claim Jim Jordan argued from Borges's blind librarianship, the Aleph-like all-books image, and Buenos Aires. Tom Urash welcomed the idea enthusiastically without new evidence, and Carlton Greene asked for textual support. Alex David Groce went further, reading the apprentice Cyby as Wolfe's self-portrait, and Matthew Malthouse countered that this self-casting might show hubris. The thread ended with the Borges identification largely accepted by its advocates and Groce's and Malthouse's readings of Cyby left as contrasting positions rather than resolved.
  • Jim Jordan: Blind Ultan is Borges, shown by a list of parallels
  • Tom Urash: Delighted by the Ultan-Borges idea, adds no evidence
  • Carlton Greene: Asks for textual evidence of the Borges portrait
  • Alex David Groce: Cyby is Wolfe as humble apprentice to a master
  • Matthew Malthouse: Wolfe casting himself as Cyby may show hubris

Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Names and their meanings, Mirrors, pictures and art

1999-10-27 James Jordan #

Argues that Ultan in The Book of the New Sun is Borges, citing his role as a blind librarian, a book containing all books like the Aleph, and the city of Buenos Aires. Responds to Carlton's request for support for the Ultan-Borges connection, offering a list of parallels rather than a fuller argument.
answers · source-and-allusion · theories: Ultan is Borges

Well, I'd read Eco first, so I was set up for it.
He's a librarian.
He's blind.
One of the books contains all books, Borges's Aleph.
The city is Buenos Aires.

jbjordan

1999-10-27 Alex David Groce #

Proposes that Cyby in The Book of the New Sun stands for Wolfe himself as a humble apprentice to a master of the archives, reading the character as an authorial self-portrait. Grounds this in a half-remembered list comment that Cyby is described as resembling what Wolfe might have looked at while writing, and closes with John 8:32.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · author-and-biography · theories: Cyby represents Wolfe as humble apprentice

And someone (I forget who) on here has pointed out that Cyby is described as roughly what Wolfe might have looked at when he wrote BOTNS, so we have Wolfe as the humble apprentice to the master of the archives.

1999-10-28 Matthew Malthouse #

Proposes that Wolfe may be showing hubris in casting himself as Cyby, the apprentice, sitting at the feet of Borges as Blind Ultan in The Book of the New Sun. Grounds the idea in Ultan's vast learning and library, and in Cyby's continued capacity to read, learn and inherit the master's mantle, responding to Tom Urash's quoting of Jim Jordan's Borges-Ultan idea.
proposes · author-and-biography · allegory-and-typology · theories: Wolfe figures himself as Cyby sitting at Borges's feet

Reading this I was suddenly struck by the posibility of hubris on Wolfe's part.

Sure, Cyby as apprentice to Ultan; Ultan knowing exactly where to lay hands on so many books, and knows much of their contents: so learned, so wise. Wolfe (as Cyby) figuratively sitting at the feet of Borges (as Ultan).

But Cyby still has eyes to read, and learn and grow. And any apprentice can hope to inherit the master's mantle....

Matthew
