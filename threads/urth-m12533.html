2010-12-29 Jordon Flato #

Asks how a world that never knew Jesus could preserve a Saint Catherine myth in the Torturers' elevation rite, and argues that Christ's influence might survive in diluted form on Urth. Grounds the question in the Seekers of Truth and Penitence ritual and a 'Christ-like' reading of Severian, and invites answers.
questions · theology-and-religion · source-and-allusion · theories: Saint Catherine myth survives from Christian Earth

So, not to pick a scab, but Witz and I were chowing down burgers today, and talking about our favorite subject, BotNS. We both are more amenable to the idea of Severan being a 'Christ like' figure or 'instantation of the Logos' than many on this board, to be sure, so we're apt to look at the books through that lens.

With that in mind, for those who feel that these books indicate a world that never knew Jesus, how do we get the myth of Saint Catherine that is so important to the Order of the Seekers of Truth and Penitence? Sure, the name may have simply been a translation choice by the fictional Gene Wolfe, but the ritual of elevation clearly lays out the actual story of Saint Catherine, at least in some guise. How do you have a Saint Catherine if there is no Christianity? What mental gymnastics do we need to put ourselves through to posit a world where such an event could be orally transmitted (or otherwise) down through the ages to end up as the rites of the Guild of Torturers without that event having some basis in far far far distant truth?

Certainly our world today is considered 'fallen' by some, and many Christians. Yet our world decidedly DID know Jesus. So why is it impossible to think that the influence of Jesus on Urth would be so far gone and diluted as to be only extant in the most second hand of myth and hearsay? It seems possible to me, and in fact partially supported textually. Of course, it could be an earlier instantiation of Briah, in which the life of Christ and Earth was slightly different, leading to the Urth we see in New Sun, but it can't have been so different that Jesus didn't spawn a movement which engendered saints the likes of Saint Catherine, said saint's stories surviving the aeon's to end up as the elevation rights of the guild of torturers.

Perhaps this has already been answered in the pages of this list. Anyone care to take a crack at that?

2010-12-29 James Wynn replying to Jordon Flato #

Argues that St Catherine is a myth in the Urth books' world, comparing saint-names to names like Thor or Freya, and that Jesus is no more meetable than Cinderella. Responds to Jordon Flato's question about whether Severian can be read as a Christ-like figure in a world that never knew Jesus, offering a brief link-based construct rather than a detailed textual argument.
proposes · theology-and-religion · theories: Jesus never existed in Briah's world; St Catherine is a myth

Well, I'm not inclined to squint at the stories to see if they are theologically correct, but I did take a stab at this here: http://lists.urth.net/pipermail/urth-urth.net/2010-December/019620.html

In short, I would say, in the construct I've offered above, that St Catherine is a myth. Naming people after saints is equivalent to naming your child Thor or Freya. You could no more go back in Time and meet Jesus than you could go back in Time and meet Cinderella. The same with Jesus.

2010-12-29 James Wynn replying to António Pedro Marques #

Argues that the Christian Church in Green's world need not reflect a real Jesus, since religions can mimic a true faith and then diverge for unrelated reasons, pointing to Zeus, Thor and Balder as examples. Replies to António Pedro Marques's claim that myths must have real sources, using Wolfe's imitation-leads-to-genuity idea from the Long Sun books, C.S. Lewis's account of pagan parallels, and the claim that Jesus was unnecessary, comparing it to Hercules or Dionysus.
disputes · myth-and-structure · theology-and-religion · theories: Briah's religions mimic Christianity without a real Jesus

There's not much discussion of religion in Green's world, but assuming his is ours there is a Christian Church. If there were no equivalent in Lara's world, their world would be vastly different or huge rationalizations would be necessary in Lara's world to keep the two in alignment. But you don't imagine there was ever a Zeus do you? Who had his sinews cut and battled Typhon? A Thor who went to the underworld to bring back Balder? In There Are Doors people spoke the same languages. Based on Wolfe's example of imitation-leads-to-genuity (Long Sun) any religion in Lara's world would tend to mimic the _true_ religion and thus, by little changes along the way, the world's align. They would have rationale for the changes, but the changes would actually occur for unrelated reasons (I can give several examples of this in _New Sun_ and other stories. But the most obvious example would be CS Lewis' claims about why ancient religions bore similarities to Christianity.

It could. But it is totally unnecessary for the to have been a Jesus at all. Anymore than it is necessary for there to have actually been a Hercules or Dionysus in order for people to keep their feasts.

2010-12-29 Son of Witz replying to James Wynn #

Argues that the healing aspect of God shows up as a truth beyond any one myth, so every creation could produce a saviour carrying similar messages, including Christ echoes in Wolfe's Urth. Replies to James Wynn, who says St Catherine is a myth and Jesus could not be met in time, granting that the echoes may exist in a Christless creation but insisting the archetypal truth is truer than its myths.
disputes · theology-and-religion · allegory-and-typology · theories: Christ-like saviours recur in every creation; Healing aspect of God truer than its myths

We're asking, "How does the essence of the myth come through?" It is NOT just the name, it is the same story.

If there is a metaphysical TRUTH in a myth, perhaps this truth is an archetypal base for the instantiated story. At a one level, we have a deluge and the legends it inspired. On another, we have the idea of a healing aspect of God that enters physical time and flesh that instantiates In different avatars.

So, as you, Wynn, lay out, there might be all these clear echoes of christ in a christ-less creation because he existed in another creation. That's fair enough to suggest. What I, and I think Jordon feel is that this above mentioned healing aspect of God a force is TRUER than the myths it inspired. Thus any creation or emmanation of God's would have the potential to manifest a saviour that carried similar messages.
