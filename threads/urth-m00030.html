2004-12-03 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes replying to turin #

Argues that the decline in Tolkien's universe was present from the beginning, not added later, by tracing Morgoth's path from cooperating with the Valar in building Ea, to feigning cooperation, to open rebellion. Responds to turin's claim that Tolkien's world is not in decline from the start, offering the inner corruption of Morgoth as the counterpoint.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Tolkien's decline was present from the beginning in Morgoth's heart

Counterargument: At first Morgoth cooperates with the Valar in the work of building up Ea, then he feigns cooperation, and finally rebels. I suggest that the decline was present from the beginning, but that at first it was only in Morgoth's heart.

--Dan'l

2004-12-03 Chris P replying to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that Tolkien's Silmarillion cosmology shows decline from the start, citing the Valar's incarnation in Ea and the making of the dwarves as signs. Responds to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes by saying the early singing may have been free of decline, but that this matters little, since Genesis has a similar pre-Fall period.
disputes · theology-and-religion · theories: Tolkien's universe declines from the beginning

To go along with the counterargument: you also have the insistence of the Valar to incarnate themselves (or whatever term you wish to use) in Ea, and the creation of the dwarves, etc.

Perhaps in the early stages of the singing, however, things weren't in decline... I'm not sure this matters that much, though. For example one could similarly say there was a period between "Let there be light" and the fall from the Garden where the Biblical earth was not in decline as well.
