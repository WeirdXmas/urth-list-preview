2002-10-28 Nicholas Gevers #

Praises the first six chapters of The Wizard Knight read from galleys, calling the prose straightforward yet rich in implied complexity and the Ravd inquisition and Disiri meeting well handled. Compares the narrator Able's educated voice favorably with Hoof's style in Return to the Whorl and suggests the book is top-tier young adult writing.
supports · genre-and-craft

From galleys, I've now read the first six chapters of
KNIGHT, the full advance excerpt in CONJUNCTIONS 39
(the piece on the website is only Chapter One). It
really is superb stuff--the language is
straightforward, but the implied complexities build
rapidly, and the inquisitorial progress of Sir Ravd
and the meeting with Disiri are brilliantly handled.
Fortunately, the style is nothing like that of, say,
Hoof in RETURN TO THE WHORL; the narrator, Able, is
educated, eloquent, and wise, for all his adolescence.
This may be YA writing, but if so, it's in the very
top range. Alice, take note.

--Nick Gevers.
