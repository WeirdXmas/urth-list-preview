rape of Seawrack

2001whorlThe Book of the Short SunDavid DiGiacomo

Themes: Fathers, mothers and children, Unreliable narration

2001-04-19 David DiGiacomo #

Questions whether calling Seawrack's encounter with Horn a rape fits, since she obeyed the Mother's orders and seemed emotionally passive, and asks how far Krait's account of her condition can be trusted. Also finds the scene overdone and less affecting than the offstage rape of Mora, which the poster compares directly.
questions · close-textual-reading · theories: Seawrack's encounter is another manipulation of Horn by the Mother

1. It seems a little odd to refer to Seawrack as being raped, when she was quite willing to have sex with Horn (or have her arm chewed off for that matter) on instructions from the Mother. The incident seems like another manipulation of Horn, and Seawrack seems inhuman in her obedience and lack of emotion.

2. To what extent can we trust Krait's description of Seawrack's condition?

3. I found the "rape of Seawrack" too overdone and incomprehensible to be very affecting. As I've mentioned before, the offstage rape of Mora was more disturbing.
