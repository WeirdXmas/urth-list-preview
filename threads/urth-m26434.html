Adaptations?

Urth list · 14 messages · 2015-04-29 to 2015-04-30

2015urthThe Book of the New SunNorwood, Frederick HudsonaaronMatthew KingLee BermanAntónio Pedro MarquesGerry QuinnDavid StockhoffLarry MillerDan'l Danehy-OakesAndreas Johansson
The thread: The thread opened with Andreas Johansson, David Stockhoff and others asking whether Wolfe's work had been adapted into graphic novels, theatre, film or games, and imagining Moebius or Jodorowsky-style versions. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes reported one known completed adaptation, a radio play of The Tree Is My Hat with Neil Gaiman. Stockhoff and António Pedro Marques argued against adapting Wolfe at all, preferring unknown creators to famous names, and Marques said acceptable adaptations change only surfaces, as The Hobbit film did. Later posts shifted to speculative pitches from larry miller, Matthew King, and Norwood, who also argued Wolfe's language is essential, and the thread ended in playful wish-casting rather than resolution.
  • António Pedro Marques: reluctant to see Wolfe adapted; unacceptable changes for no reason, unknown creators preferred
  • David Stockhoff: opposes adapting Wolfe, Tolkien adaptable but Wolfe not
  • Norwood, Frederick Hudson: Wolfe's language is essential, so film or comic adaptations unwelcome
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: reports radio play of The Tree Is My Hat with Neil Gaiman
  • larry miller: proposes Coppola-style film of The Book of the New Sun with costumes as sets
  • Matthew King: proposes Latro films set inside the memory palace

Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Memory and forgetting, Mirrors, pictures and art

2015-04-29 David Stockhoff replying to Andreas Johansson #

Asks whether any Wolfe adaptations exist, such as graphic novels or theatre, and imagines a Moebius-style Urth of the New Sun graphic novel or a 3D Nessus. Replies to Andreas Johansson's Moebius remark, mentions an unfinished film of The Island of Doctor Death, and asks which Lafferty book to start with.
questions

Hi Andreas
I agree that Moebius would have been a great illustrator for BOTNS. I can see the panels very clearly in my mind. There was a film project underway for The Island of Doctor Death and Other Stories, but I haven't heard any updates about it for a long while. Someone else will know.

Hi,
First of all, a great thanks to all of you out there debating and analyzing the big and the small of all things Wolfean. It's severely enlightening, both for understanding Wolfe in many more layers, and literature in general. Now then, a question that arose recently as I began the BOTNS for I think a fourth time, while at the same time delving deep into Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim - how wonderful wouldn't a "sand box" type of videogame, set in Severian's Urth? - so my question was, has there been any kind of adaptations of any of Gene Wolfe's works? Graphic novels, theatre productions, short movies, anything? Given his massive output (more than fifty short stories, hehe), you'd think that somewhere, someone would've picked up the challenge.? Of course, much of what makes Wolfe special is an inherent trait of the medium that would be lost in a visual adaptation that loses the 1st person perspective... but his worlds are so bloody vivid and beautiful that they would be a feast for any graphic artist to dig into, even if 70% of the in-text layers would be lost. Imagine a Moebius-style graphic novel based on Urth of the New Sun... or as mentioned moving freely through a 3d-rendered version of Nessus or Viron. Oh by the way, which entry-point to Lafferty would you fellows suggest? Haven't read anything by him but thought it might be time.

yrs,
Andreas Johansson

Asks whether any adaptations of Wolfe's works exist, such as graphic novels, theatre, short films or a videogame set in Severian's Urth, and imagines a Moebius-style comic or 3D world of Nessus or Viron. Closes with an unrelated question about which Lafferty book to start with.
questions

Hi,

First of all, a great thanks to all of you out there debating and analyzing the big and the small of all things Wolfean. It's severely enlightening, both for understanding Wolfe in many more layers, and literature in general. Now then, a question that arose recently as I began the BOTNS for I think a fourth time, while at the same time delving deep into Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim - how wonderful wouldn't a "sand box" type of videogame, set in Severian's Urth? - so my question was, has there been any kind of adaptations of any of Gene Wolfe's works? Graphic novels, theatre productions, short movies, anything? Given his massive output (more than fifty short stories, hehe), you'd think that somewhere, someone would've picked up the challenge.

Of course, much of what makes Wolfe special is an inherent trait of the medium that would be lost in a visual adaptation that loses the 1st person perspective... but his worlds are so bloody vivid and beautiful that they would be a feast for any graphic artist to dig into, even if 70% of the in-text layers would be lost. Imagine a Moebius-style graphic novel based on Urth of the New Sun... or as mentioned moving freely through a 3d-rendered version of Nessus or Viron.

Oh by the way, which entry-point to Lafferty would you fellows suggest? Haven't read anything by him but thought it might be time.

disputes

2015-04-30 Larry Miller replying to Gerry Quinn #

Proposes that a film adaptation of The Book of the New Sun should use Coppola's approach from Bram Stoker's Dracula, letting costumes serve as the sets and the sets shape the lighting. Grounds this in Wolfe being inspired by a costume party to create Severian, the many masked characters, and the book's stage-play feel. Replies to Gerry Quinn and aaron's earlier talk of adaptations by directors.
proposes · author-and-biography · genre-and-craft

Francis Ford Coppola said that for Bram Stokers Dracula (1992) he set out to make a film where "the costumes were the sets and the sets acted as the lighting." I think this would probably be the best approach in a film adaptation of book of the New Sun. I think that would be quite appropriate considering wolf was inspired by a costume party to create Severian, the number of characters who wear masks, and would overall add to that "stage play" feeling the book has.

Proposes a film adaptation of the first two Latro books that is set entirely inside Latro's memory palace, with him touching objects to recover memory segments out of chronological order. Reply to Larry Miller's thread on adaptations, arguing this structure keeps the books' disjointed narrative and might suit a video game better.
proposes · genre-and-craft

I've toyed with the idea of a film adaptation of the first two Soldier books that takes place entirely in Latro's memory palace. He wanders the palace touching items, recovering segments of memory. Traduttore, traditore.

This approach might actually work better as a video game.
