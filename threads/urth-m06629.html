The thread: The thread began with James Wynn arguing that the wooden puppet and triple-jawed leatherskin in On Blue's Waters allude to Pinocchio, and Nigel Price playfully proposed that Wolfe drew on the Gerry Anderson puppet show Stingray. Matthew Keeley added that puppets recur in Wolfe, citing Strange Birds and Stromboli from The Toy Theater, and Price said the joke was starting to seem worth taking seriously. The thread then drifted into off-topic chat about Lovecraft and silent film adaptations, Vampyr, San Francisco memories and local venues, with no return to Wolfe's text. It ended in drift rather than agreement or resolution.
  • James Wynn: the puppet and leatherskin passages allude to Pinocchio
  • Nigel Price: playful suggestion that Wolfe drew on Stingray, grounded in marionette imagery
  • Matthew Keeley: puppets and puppeteers recur in Wolfe, including Stromboli in Strange Birds

2008-11-03 James Wynn replying to Nigel Price #

Argues that Horn's childhood memory of a string-moved wooden puppet in On Blue's Waters, and the triple-jawed leatherskin that attacks him soon after, are obvious allusions to Pinocchio. Responds to Nigel Price's dismissal of puppetry in Wolfe by quoting the passage at length and linking the leatherskin to Pinocchio's dogfish.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: The triple-jawed leatherskin alludes to Pinocchio's dogfish; Horn's wooden puppet memory alludes to Pinocchio

In The Book of the Short Sun, Horn remembers the following from his childhood:

"I once had a toy, a little wooden man in a blue coat who was moved by strings. When I played with him, I made him walk and bow, and spoke for him. I practiced until I thought myself very clever. One day I saw my mother holding the two sticks that held his strings, and my little wooden man saluting my youngest sister much more cleverly than I could have made him do it, and laughing with his head thrown back, then mourning with his face in his hands. I never spoke of it to my mother, but I was angry and ashamed." On Blue's Waters, pg. 158

Soon he after he was assaulted by the triple-jawed leatherskin---an obvious reference to Pinnochio's Dog-fish.

J.

2008-11-03 Nigel Price #

Playfully proposes that Gene Wolfe may have drawn on the Gerry Anderson puppet show Stingray, suggesting parallels such as Seawrack and Marina and the Storm God attack. Grounds the joke in Severian's dream of a toy theatre with marionette versions of himself and Baldander, and in the undersea cities of AEG and the Urth books.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Wolfe drew on Stingray for undersea imagery

As I was writing about evil undersea cities and civilisations in AEG and the Urth Cycle, one part of my mind facetiously wandered and wondered whether Gene Wolfe had ever watched the Gerry Anderson puppet show "Stingray" in the 1960s. As I thought whimsically about it, the parallels scattered through his books became stronger and stranger. Was Seawrack inspired by Marina? Was the idea of the US Navy attacking the Storm God inspired by the sight of Troy Tempest and the WASPs attacking Titan and his aquaphibian minions?

No, of course not, it's a silly idea. It's not as if Wolfe ever refers to puppetry in his stories.

Hang on there, wait a minute...

What about that dream Severian has of the toy theatre and the marionette versions of himself and Baldander?

Goodness! If I'm right, anything can happen in the next half hour...

Nigel (in a playful mood)

2008-11-03 Nigel Price replying to Nigel Price #

Speculates whether Gene Wolfe heard Gerry Anderson speak at the 1987 Brighton WorldCon, and whether Ray Harryhausen's six-limbed octopus inspired Wolfe's submarine squid deities. Notes that Harryhausen cut the octopus to six limbs for budget and time reasons, and jokingly says whimsical notions keep distracting the poster from a serious analysis of An Evil Guest.
chats · theories: Harryhausen's octopus inspired Wolfe's submarine squid deities

Weird but true: Gerry Anderson and Ray Harryhausen were both speakers at the 1987 WorldCon in Brighton, England, which Gene and Rosemary Wolfe also attended.

Did Wolfe hear Gerry Anderson talk about his work?

Then again, for anyone interested in creating submarine squid deities, Harryhausen's amusing account of animating the six-limbed giant octopus in "It came from beneath the sea" might well have been inspirational. (The octopus only had six limbs because Harryhausen was on a tight budget and an even tighter timetable. Animating extra limbs takes time, and time is money!)

Nigel (who is trying to write a serious analysis of AEG - coming soon - but keeps getting distracted by whimsical notions)

2008-11-04 Adam Thornton replying to Son of Witz #

Argues that Lovecraft's monsters are usually less frightening than the build-up leading to them, so a silent Call of Cthulhu film failing at its monster may reflect a flaw in the source. Names "The Color Out of Space" and "The Dreams in the Witch-House" as exceptions that keep the reveal effective.
proposes · genre-and-craft

I haven't seen it, but one could argue the novella suffers the same flaw. Well, *most* of Lovecraft does. The reveal is much less scary than the build-up, most of the time. There are of course exceptions. "The Color Out of Space" and "The Dreams In the Witch-House" are two exceptions that leap to *my* mind.

Adam

I miss living there so much. The Le Video owner was my landlord. But when they closed their other biz, Stamp Francisco, right below us, an Aerobics Studio opened up.  They promised it would be quite yoga classes, but every day, all day you could hear techno music pounding through the walls. Gung ch gung ch gung ch gung ch gung ch gung ch, etc.  and you'd be on the toilet and hear the spinning instructor yelling "3 - 4 you can do it. 5-6 don't stop now. A little harder now"  Argh. we fought them for 4 months then finally bailed on that scene. That lovely apartment sat empty for at least a year before someone was willing to live with that noise.  Finally, the studio closed and it's a quiet stationary shop now.  I LOVED living there, but I spent too much money on all the tempting restaurants.  and there used to be at least 3 used book shops in a 2 block radius.  dangerous
