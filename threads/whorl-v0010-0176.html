SEAWRACK'S MISSING ARM

1999-11-15 Bill Carmichael #

Argues that Babbie tore off Seawrack's missing arm during a struggle aboard the boat while Horn slept, after the Mother sent her there. Grounds this in the blood Horn finds on the deck, Seawrack's gills, her forgotten past, and the Mother's ban on raw fish, answering William Ansley's question about the arm.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Babbie tore off Seawrack's arm; Seawrack's original form was nonhuman; The Mother sent Seawrack to the boat

In response to William Ansley's question about Seawrack's missing arm: I came away from the book pretty sure that Babbie tore it off, sometime before Horn became aware of Seawrack's existence.

I can't remember the exact passage, but at one point Horn recalls the blood that he found on the deck, and connects it with Seawrack. My interpretation was that the Mother had sent Seawrack to the boat once already, and she arrived while Horn was asleep and Babbie was keeping watch. A struggle ensued, and Seawrack came away from it with an arm missing. The next time she comes to the boat, she comes with jewelry.

As far as being her able to swim well without the arm, well, she's got gills. And there seem to be hints throughout the book that she is a "creature of habit" in a radical sense...the Mother's mandate that she not be allowed to eat raw fish, her own statements to the effect that she is engaged in a deliberate process of "forgetting" her life prior to Horn, etc. I think it's pretty safe to assume that Seawrack's original form wasn't anything remotely like human, and she's able in some way to compensate for the missing "arm". If the arm was only a recent acquisition for her, she probably wouldn't miss it much after it was gone.

1999-11-16 Kieran Cleary #

Proposes that Seawrack's human form, like the jewelry she brings the boat, is a calculated peace offering rather than her original shape. Builds on Bill Carmichael's remark about her missing arm by quoting OBW page 155, where the dried blood on the stump reveals Babbie's role in the struggle.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Seawrack's human form is a calculated peace offering

The relevent line from OBW is:
"I noticed the dried blood among the folds of the skin on the stump that had been her right arm, and understood why she had been so afraid of Babbie, and whose blood had stained the deck at the bow" [p.155]

The Mother has some knowledge of humanity, certainly. Thing is, if Horn had been any of the other outcasts heading for the lander, Seawrack would have faced a life of fetching gold from the sea floor.

It may be that Seawrack's not unattractive human form is as much a calculated peace offering as the jewelry.
