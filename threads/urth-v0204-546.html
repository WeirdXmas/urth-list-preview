The thread: Participants debated whether Lune has near-Urth gravity and whether Blue, Green and Lune could share Earth's size, using Green's apparent distance and size in The Book of the Long Sun and The Book of the Short Sun as evidence. Maa32 and Roy C. Lackey argued the texts do not support Earth-like scale and that Blue cannot be Urth, while Mark Millman questioned the 30,000-league figure as unreliable second-hand narration. Roy C. Lackey granted a possible atmospheric magnification effect for Green's appearance, and the thread then turned to an apparent oddity with trees in Return to the Whorl, where Michael Straight proposed that Silkhorn's path-finding ability explains Pig's failure to touch them. The thread ends with the tree question unresolved and no agreement on the planetary scale.
  • maa32: Lune cannot be near-Urth gravity since Green at 30,000 leagues is smaller than Lune
  • Mark Millman: the 30,000-league figure is unreliable because characters lie and make mistakes
  • Roy C. Lackey: Short Sun gives no support for Earth-like Lune, and Blue is not Urth
  • Michael Straight: Pig's inability to touch trees comes from Silkhorn's path-finding Neighbor gift

Themes: The dying sun and the cosmos, Deep time and ruins, Unreliable narration

2002-04-06 Marc Aramini #

Disputes the recent idea that Lune has near-Urth gravity, arguing that Green, seen at 30,000 leagues, is still smaller than Lune, so Green cannot be much larger and Lune cannot be that close. Builds on Roy's quotation from The Book of the Short Sun, chapter 17, where Green looks bigger and brighter than Blue's moon, and says they are saying the same thing.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Lune has near-Urth gravity; Green cannot be much bigger than Lune

Roy, no big deal, but there IS a scene when he actually is looking at the moon and commenting:
"Green came up, bigger and brighter than we ever see it on Blue. Or want to, either." (pg 714 in SFBC BOTSS, chapter 17)

He is talking about Lune here. We are saying the same thing. I'm kind of intrigued by the recent idea that Lune could have near urth gravity. But doesn't the fact that at 30,000 leagues Green is still not as big as Lune indicate that Green can't be much bigger? I mean, Lune would have to be really damned close if Green really were larger at all, and it can't be THAT close.

Marc Aramini

2002-04-07 Mark Millman #

Argues that the 30,000-league figure for Green's distance from Blue in The Book of the Long Sun may be unreliable, since the characters make mistakes and lie and the figure comes second-hand. Responds to Marc's acceptance of that figure by pointing to the Whorl's shorter scales and to Juganu's ambiguous claim "We are everywhere".
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Green's 30,000-league distance may be unreliable

Marc,

I'm afraid that your uncritical acceptance
of the 30,000-league figure for Green's
distance from Blue may be a bit dangerous;
after all, Wolfe's characters make mistakes,
conceal the truth, and lie outright all the
time. This figure is given at second hand,
produced by a culture that's had only one
generation of planetside life--the estimate
may be influenced by scales within the
Whorl, where the skylands are only twice as
far away as the Long Sun, and city lights
in those regions are evidently visible at
night.

Likewise Juganu's comment, "We are every-
where": we don't know whether it's a self-
aggrandizing boast, a calculated statement
meant to dishearten his listeners, or an
unthinking assumption. Is Juganu even
aware of Urth before Horn enlists his help
to travel there?

Mark Millman
(Nacre)

2002-04-08 Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that the Short Sun text gives no support for Lune having Earth-like gravity, and that Blue, Green and Lune cannot all be about the same size without absurd consequences, so Blue is not Urth. Responds to Marc's observation about Green looking bigger than on Blue, granting a possible atmospheric magnifying effect near the horizon, and closes by noting an oddity with trees in Return to the Whorl where Pig cannot touch a tree near Silkhorn.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Blue is Urth; Blue is the size of Lune; Lune has near-Urth gravity

Date: Saturday, April 06, 2002 10:41 PM

moon and commenting:
"Green came up, bigger and brighter than we ever see it on Blue. Or want to, either." (pg 714 in SFBC BOTSS, chapter 17)

He is talking about Lune here. We are saying the same thing. I'm kind of intrigued by the recent idea that Lune could have near urth gravity.<<

Please don't get started on that! As mantis has already hinted, if there is any evidence to support that idea, it's extratextual. No scene, directly or indirectly, takes place on Lune in the Urth Cycle, and there's not a word in the text about Lune's gravity.

Lune indicate that Green can't be much bigger? I mean, Lune would have to be
really damned close if Green really were larger at all, and it can't be THAT close.<<

I could offer the feeble excuse that, on the occasion you cited above, Lune had just risen and therefore Urth's atmosphere magnified Hoof's view of it, just as Moon looks bigger near the horizon than at zenith on Earth, and Hoof had limited occasions to observe relative sizes, but I don't know that that would be sufficient reason for his blanket statements that Lune appears larger than Green.

Silkhorn's statement, cited earlier, that implied there wasn't much difference in size between Blue and Green, coupled with these recent arguments that seem to indicate that Green must be close in size to Lune, leads to the conclusion that all three bodies are about the same size. Add to that my observations about the problems a much higher gravity on Blue would present for the inhumi, and where does that lead to?: That Blue and Green are about the same size and have about the same gravity, and both correspond to the size and gravity of Lune. That Blue is therefore the size of Lune, which means that Blue can't possibly be Urth. That Silkhorn & Co. woke up on Urth and just didn't notice that they weighed six times as much as they were used to.

No, I can't account for the apparent discrepancies in the text, or the apparent violations of the laws of physics. I just don't see any good reason to believe that Blue is Urth/Ushas.

I can't endorse your theories about trees/inhumi/Neighbors/people either, but I will concede that there _is_ something odd going on wrt trees in the SS series--I just don't know what. Here's one you haven't mentioned before. Near the end of the second chapter of RTTW, Pig is seized by both fear and wonder when he discovers that he is in the midst of a wood but can't touch a tree or be touched by one. It is his proximity to Silkhorn that seems to account for it, but he doesn't know that.

-Roy

2002-04-08 Michael Straight #

Proposes that Pig's inability to touch the trees in Return to the Whorl comes from following Silkhorn, whose Neighbor-given ability finds paths through bushes. Replies to Roy C. Lackey's passage on Pig's fear and wonder in the wood, offering trees moving aside for Horn as an alternative but favoring Horn's extraordinary perception of the one clear path.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Horn follows paths via Neighbor ability; Trees move to clear a path for Horn; Horn perceives the one clear path

Could this be that he's following Silkhorn, who is using his Neighbor-given ability to find paths through bushes?

Although it's possible that this works because the trees are moving to make a path for Horn, the way it's described in the text it seems more like some sort of extraordinary perception Horn has that enables him to see the one clear path that most people can't find.

-Rostrum
