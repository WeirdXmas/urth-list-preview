2000-06-16 Alice Turner #

Argues that Dr. Crane, not a divine being, secretly masterminded the populist movement in The Book of the Long Sun that puts Silk in the Calde's seat and saddles him with Hy. Responds to Alex David Groce's question about who controls the book's action, offering the claim as a challenge to prove wrong rather than supporting it with evidence.
proposes · puzzle-solving · theories: Dr. Crane masterminded the populist movement in Long Sun

No, Alex. And since I think of you and Adam as the two bright young things of the list, and since you've both got a month or so to wait until you get your grubby little paws on IGJ, I will set you the assignment to prove me wrong.

The "action" of LS, the populist political movement that eventually catapults Silk into the Calde's seat, and saddles him with Hy, as well, was masterminded and manipulated by--until circumstances decreed otherwise:

ready?

Dr. Crane

for reasons that you two can either figure out or repudiate.

(heh, heh, heh!!!)

-alga

2000-06-19 Alex David Groce #

Agrees that Crane, though his first meeting with Silk seems accidental, later manipulates events and drives the political developments in the Long Sun books, and offers to post evidence. Responds to Alga's revelation of the mastermind by explaining that he had ruled Crane out only for the initial encounter, and notes that prophecy runs even stronger in Long Sun, citing a prophetic dream in Lake of the Long Sun.
supports · close-textual-reading · theories: Crane manipulates the political events in Long Sun

(In response to Alga's revelation of the mastermind in Long Sun):

Aha. I ruled out Crane because I thought you were speaking more generally--his -initial- meeting with Silk is "accidental" (as accidental as anything is--the theme of prophecy*, all true, I'm beginning to think, runs even stronger in LS than in NS), but after that he certainly does manipulate things and is behind the political events. I can post my version of the evidence for this if anybody isn't convinced, although I thought that part was common agreement on the list.

Can I remain a bright young thing five years from now when I'm STILL a grad student?

* like the prophetic dream in Chap. 4 of Lake that I'd completely forgotten.
