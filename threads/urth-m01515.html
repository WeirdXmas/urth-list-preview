2005-11-04 Mike Sakasegawa #

Asks whether the lack of contractions in the prose portions of Arthur's letter to Ben in The Wizard Knight carries any significance, while his dialogue is full of them. Notes the contrast as a stylistic oddity noticed on a first reading of the book.
questions · genre-and-craft

Hello all. I'm relatively new to the list--I posted briefly as Hitsuji a couple of years ago and then dropped out. Anyway, I recently finished reading The Wizard and had a question.

As far as I noticed, Arthur never once uses a contraction in the prose portions of his letter to Ben. It seemed odd to me, because his dialogue uses as many contractions as you would normally see in a 20th century American teenager's speech, but I was unable to spot a single instance of a contraction in the prose. I was wondering if anyone thought there was any significance to that.

-Mike

2005-11-06 Mike Sakasegawa replying to Bob Miller #

Argues that the absence of contractions in The Wizard Knight's first-person narration is odd given the characterization, and says the poster does not recall the same absence in Wolfe's other works. Responds to Bob Miller's joke about a teacher penalizing contractions, defending contractions as legitimate grammar and promising further investigation.
proposes · genre-and-craft

Contractions are a perfectly legitimate grammatical construction, and given the first person perspective and the characterization evident in the narration, it struck me as odd that there were no contractions to be found. Since it stood out to me so much in TWK, I feel like I would have noticed if they had also been absent from the other Wolfe works that I've read, but I don't recall that being the case. I'll investigate further when I get the chance.

-Mike
