The thread: Readers discussed an interview clipping in which Gene Wolfe assigns each Free Live Free border figure a besetting sin: Serpentina to pride, Candy to gluttony, Stubb to envy, while denying he wrote allegory. maa32 asked whether authorial intent is decisive for interpretation. James Jordan accepted Wolfe's remarks as intent, noting his uncertain memory, and linked Serpentina's pride to Satan and witchcraft while finding Stubb's envy hard to see. Michael Straight disputed the neat mapping, arguing the sins overlap so Serpentina may show avarice too and Stubb pride rather than envy. The thread stayed within three posts and ended without resolution.
  • maa32: asks whether authorial intent is decisive or a fallacy in reading the sins
  • James Jordan: accepts Wolfe's stated sins as intent, links Serpentina's pride to Satan, doubts Stubb's envy

2001-04-20 Marc Aramini #

Quotes a Wolfe interview in which he assigns each Free Live Free figure a besetting sin, Madame Serpentina to pride, Candy to gluttony and Stubb to envy, while denying he wrote allegory. Responds to rostrum's citation of it by asking whether authorial intent is a fallacy or a decisive factor in interpretation.
questions · author-and-biography · allegory-and-typology

to which rostrum replied:
Here is a quote from an online interview accessible through Paul Duggan's web sight.

"GW: I suppose yeah. Yes. Somewhat the same concern. I tried to give the four borders, I tried to give each sort of a besetting sin. Madame Serpantina, it's pride. Candy, it is gluttony. Stub, I forget now. Osgood Barnes, sexuality of course.

JJ: And you drew them somewhat from the _________

GW: Envy, envy I think is Stubbs. And I wasn't trying to write allegory. I wasn't saying he was a personification of envy. I wanted to show men and women who were actually beset by these sins. "

I don't mean to beat a dead horse, but once again we need to consider the position of authorial intent: fallacy or definitively influential in interpretation?
Marc Aramini

2001-04-20 James Jordan #

Reports Gene Wolfe's own interview remarks assigning a besetting sin to each border character in Free Live Free, and accepts them as authorial intent while noting Wolfe's uncertain memory. Responds to a quoted interview clipping by linking Serpentina's pride to Satan and her witchcraft, and finding Stubb's envy harder to see.
supports · author-and-biography · allegory-and-typology · theories: Serpentina's pride reflects Satan's sin

Gosh, and that was my interview, and I'd forgotten. Well, I'm all for authorial intent, though as you can see, Wolfe was a bit unsure in his memory. But since all these sins overlap one another, I can go either way. Serpentina certainly is proud and haughty, and pride is traditionally the sin of Satan, so her involvement with witchcraft makes sense. Stubb as envious is a bit harder for me to see, but Wolfe wasn't entirely sure.

JBJordan

2001-04-20 Michael Straight #

Argues that the deadly sins in Free Live Free overlap, so Serpentina's temptation by power may reveal Avarice as well as Pride, and Stubb's may show Pride rather than Envy. Responds to maa32's quote of Wolfe denying allegory, citing Serpentina's humbled speech on human power as correcting her Pride and questioning Envy in Stubb's temptation.
disputes · allegory-and-typology · close-textual-reading · theories: Stubb's temptation shows Pride rather than Envy; Serpentina's temptation reveals Avarice as well as Pride; Serpentina's speech corrects her Pride

I think that bit about personification is important. In real life, Envy and Pride and Lust and Avarice are often all interrelated, so any attempt to create a character beset by Envy is likely to involve the others as well.

So it's possible Wolfe was thinking of Envy for Stubb and Pride for Serpentina, but that he ended up writing scenes where we see more of Serpentina's Avarice (or whatever you call her desire for Free's power) and Stubb's Pride.

If you look at what the characters learn from their temptation scene, Serpentina is tempted with the offer of power, but she ends up humbled and gives that speech about how little humans deserve more power or longer life, thus it corrects her Pride. But I have a harder time seeing the Envy in Stubb's temptation.

-Rostrum
