Lamarck, Wolfe and Exultants

Urth list · 12 messages · 2010-08-05 to 2010-08-06

2010urthThe Book of the New SunLane HaygoodRyan DunnDavid StockhoffJames WynnJeff WilsonJack SmithDavid Duffy
The thread: Ryan Dunn opened by asking whether the Long Sun exultants' height could be Lamarckian inheritance, and the thread quickly split between genetic engineering, natural selection, and environmental explanations. Jack Smith argued Darwinian sexual selection could produce tall exultants, while Jeff Wilson countered that extreme artificial selection and a hospitable world would be needed, and also that Commonwealth nutrition stunts stature. Jack Smith and David Stockhoff then pushed the dying-sun nutrition argument alongside an exultant mother to explain Severian's height. James Wynn's Lamarckian claims drew skeptical replies from Wilson and Lane Haygood, and David Duffy's late reply disputed Haygood's evolutionary psychology comparison. The thread ended without resolution, with the Lamarck question left mostly unsettled.
  • Ryan Dunn: exultant height may be Lamarckian acquired traits, later asks whether ordinary folk would be shorter
  • Jeff Wilson: exultants need genetic modification plus growth treatment, and poor nutrition explains shorter common folk
  • Jack Smith: Darwinian sexual selection suffices, and dying-sun nutrition plus an exultant mother explains Severian
  • James Wynn: Lamarckian inheritance is returning, citing grandparental famine study
  • Lane Haygood: Lamarckism revives as pseudoscience in evolutionary psychology, nature-nurture unresolvable
  • David Duffy: epigenetic inheritance is documented and Wolfe treats heredity seriously

Themes: Gnosticism, heresy and other theologies, Fathers, mothers and children, Power, rulers and politics

2010-08-05 Ryan Dunn #

Asks whether the Exultants in Wolfe's Long Sun books are a product of Lamarckian inheritance, suggesting their lingering height could be acquired traits passed to offspring. Grounds the idea in a quoted Wolfe interview remark that he has never been convinced Lamarck was wrong, and speculates that original Exultants were engineered to be taller and more idealized.
proposes · author-and-biography · puzzle-solving · theories: Exultants are a product of Lamarckian inheritance

Wolfe is quoted as thus...

"I didn't know that anybody was working in the Lamarckian area, today to be honest with you. I think somebody should be because I have never been convinced that Lamarck was wrong."

Lamarckism states that acquired traits are inherited.

This led me to a question that may have been covered...

Are exultants a product of Lamarckism? It could explain the lingering height as a physical character trait, and might take less time than Darwinian theology to see an affect.

If so, were the original exultants genetically modified to be taller, more idealized, and more hierogrammatesque, and their offspring through a few iterations thus took on some of those genetically mutated characteristics into their own genes?

...ryan

2010-08-05 Jeff Wilson replying to Ryan Dunn #

Argues that exultant height mixes genetic modification with individual growth treatment, making Ultan, Thea, Thecla and Vodalus descendants of older supermen stock aided by royal jelly. Responds to Ryan Dunn's Lamarckism question by noting inheritance of acquired traits predates Lamarck, citing Darwin's gemmules and Gurloes' remark on Thecla's 'high blood' in Shadow, Chapter VII.
proposes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Exultants are genetically modified supermen; Exultant height from royal jelly and growth treatments; Exultant families lost access to genetic modification

Yes, but "Lamarckism" is not a product of Lamarck himself. Inheritance of acquired characteristics is an ancient idea in one form or another, and Darwin allowed something like it in his idea that somatic tissues generated heritable "gemmules" in response to the development or disuse of various organs.

Wikipedia says Lamark's contributions were that inheritance of new traits is facilitated by an alchemical process that creates new complexity, and that existing traits could be adapted to the environment. This might be what Wolfe means when the BOTNS says that the continents shape new peoples into the form of the old.

Exultant height seems to be a mixture of effects. Exultants' bastards are commonly believed to be taller than the common folk, which would be plausible if they were genetically modified "supermen". This prevents commoners from posing as exultants, and lets the bastards be readily identified for elimination or co-option before they are fully of age to make real trouble. Ultan is almost certainly one of these supermen, given his impressive mental, physical, and charismatic traits other than his blindness, and Thea and Thecla are likewise gifted with pleasing voices and faces, deep thinking, and quickness if not strength.

However, there is also talk of "royal jelly" being used to make them taller still, and this has been speculated to be growth hormones or even the actual glands taken from the khaibits, who don't have to be particularly tall to serve their purpose. This might mean that some of the exultant families were ennobled after the art of genetic modification was lost or banned or simply withheld, and have to resort to individual growth treatments as they have not yet intermarried enough with the older "supermen" stock. Vodalus may be an example of this kind, as he is described as very tall but slender, and only a little taller than Thea.

I've just now come across Master Gurloes' comment on Thecla's ancestry in SHADOW, Chapter VII that seems to confirm this: "'Not just an armiger family. High blood.'"

2010-08-05 Jack Smith replying to Ryan Dunn #

Argues that exultants could arise from Darwinian evolution alone, with no genetic engineering, through sexual selection for height over ten or twenty generations. Supports this with the heights Wolfe gives for Severian, Thecla and male exultants, compared with the tallest NBA and WNBA players and the poster's personal sighting of David Robinson's sons.
proposes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Exultants result from Darwinian evolution alone

Exultants could result from Darwinian evolution alone, without any genetic engineering. According to Wolfe, Severian is about 6' or 6'1", and Thecla is a head taller (around 7' or 7'1"). Male exultants seem to be about 7'6" to 8'. Such heights are uncommon for us, but still within the range of the possible. The tallest NBA player is 7'6" and the tallest WNBA player is 7'2".

Imagine a planet colonized by NBA and WNBA players. Given 10 or 20 generations and sexual selection in favor of height, you could get a population where the average height could be 7' to 8'.

I used to live near David Robinson (7'1"), the center for the NBA Spurs. Once I was walking past his house and he and his two sons (about 10 or 12 years old) were outside his gate. The sons were taller than me.

The post mainly brings in outside science rather than discussing any Wolfe text.
supports

http://www.springerlink.com/content/x61m87x016213823/

Obviously, if a trait can be acquired across generations based on experiences occurring before the second generation was even conceived, this could --over time-- have an evolutionary effect.

2010-08-05 Lane Haygood replying to Jeff Wilson #

Argues that Lamarckism is reviving in evolutionary psychology as pseudoscience, fed by myths of inherited sin. Replies to Jeff Wilson's note on flexible phenotypes, saying nature-versus-nurture questions stay unresolved because falsifying experiments are unethical.
disputes · theories: Modern Lamarckism is pseudoscience

Lamarckism is making a comeback in the pseudoscience of evolutionary psychology.

It's still just as much crap as Lamarckism is, but the spectre of heritable acquired traits just will not die from the popular consciousness, probably because the ideas of "corruption of blood" and the "sins of the father" are so prevalent within our mythologies. It breaks down along the same inscrutable fault lines as the "nature versus nurture" debate about how we, and other organisms, learn our behavior. How much is instinct? How much is conditioned?

As best I can tell, the answer is "a mix of both" in unknown ratios because the kinds of experiments needed to falsify a Lamarckian hypothesis are deeply, deeply unethical. The way one feels toward evolutionary psychology (and related doctrines) tends to have more to do with how one feels about a range of philosophical issues, from one's stance on the mind, to the metaphysics of souls, to base factionalism. So I recognize my own rejection of it as calumny reflects my own particular biases against such concepts as "human nature," a materialist account of consciousness, or phenotypic properties being causally-linked to genetic properties.

But I think I have darn good reasons to find such concepts objectionable, so via the transitive property of bad ideas, modern Lamarckisms are vile calumny.

LH

2010-08-05 Jeff Wilson replying to Jack Smith #

Argues that exultant height could not arise from Darwinian evolution alone, since it would need extreme artificial selection, genetic screening, and a hospitable world. Responds to Jack Smith's proposal of NBA and WNBA colonists breeding over 10 or 20 generations, raising counter-pressures like short colonists and tribute-based class division.
disputes · genre-and-craft

The initial population would involve such extreme artificial selection from across a huge pool that I would hesitate to call it Darwinian evolution alone. you'd also need genetic screening at the least to make a go of it to avoid reinforcing the pathological sources of height, and it would need to be a pretty hospitable world to make sure big appetites did not have trouble being fed long enough to reach sexual maturity and survive mate selection.

There could also be counter pressure from smaller colonists who were un chosen and get stuck mating with the poor and the equally short, unless the cagers practice wasteful culling. Maybe you could get two groups of the few talls and the many shorties who avoid intermariage, presuming the talls retain some advantage they can leverage to offload their greater support requirements onto the shorties in the form of tribute.

But breeding for tallness and prettiness and good loud voices, without lots of residual variation, is hard to work out, esp when human sexual selection has always been bent in favor of wealth and power.

proposes · close-textual-reading · genre-and-craft · theories: Severian's mother is probably an exultant; Urth's common folk are shorter from poor nutrition

I presume you are thinking about humans here. There is plenty of evidence of epigenetic inheritance, including transmission of behaviours, in other species. Furthermore, the mechanisms, eg genetic imprinting, are well understood mechanistically (sections of DNA are marked by addition of methyl groups to cytosines).

I think Wolfe has thought deeply about this, starting from _tFHoC_, _tDoDI_, to the chems, possession, and Horn-Pas-Silk. Specifically, the chems have souls and an interest in right thought and action, but their *minds* can be completely controlled by others, and they are aware of this. A la Lem, you will know when you have a true artificial intelligence when it demonstrates laziness, duplicity, self interest, and a tendency to do other things, such as contributing to news groups, when it should be working.
