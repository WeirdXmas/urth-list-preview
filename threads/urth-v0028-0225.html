The Commonwealth: North or South

Urth list · 8 messages · 1999-10-25 to 1999-10-27

1999urthThe Book of the New SunMark MillmanMitchell A. BaileyInire's biggest fanJim HenleyRoy C. LackeyRedhaven1James JordanDaniel Fusch
The thread: Redhaven1 opened the question of whether the Commonwealth in the New Sun lies in South America, proposing that past magnetic pole reversal could put it in the United States or Europe. Mitchell Bailey questioned whether Urth has a significant magnetic field at all, and Mark Millman argued that a reversal would only swap compass labels without moving the geographic poles. Fr. Inire claimed poles really do flip and cited magnetic rock strata, while Jim Henley floated flip-and-flip-back and meteorite explanations. Roy Lackey and Jim Jordan both held for the Southern Hemisphere and South America, citing textual evidence such as the Autarch passage and Borges allusions. The thread ended with the South American reading reaffirmed by its defenders and the pole-reversal ideas left unresolved.
  • Redhaven1: Commonwealth may be in US or Europe if poles reversed in the past
  • Mitchell A. Bailey: Urth has too weak a magnetic field for compasses to matter
  • mark millman: a pole reversal would only swap compass labels; doubts South America setting
  • Jim Henley: poles may have flipped twice, or a meteorite explains the land west of the Andes
  • Fr.Inire: Earth's poles really flip, shown by alternating magnetic particles in rock
  • Roy C. Lackey: Southern Hemisphere settled by the Autarch's correction about the Ascians

Themes: The dying sun and the cosmos, Deep time and ruins, Myth and folklore sources

1999-10-25 Redhaven1 #

Proposes that the Commonwealth in The Book of the New Sun may lie in the United States or Europe rather than South America, if Earth's magnetic poles reversed in the far past. Grounds it in the book's placement of the Commonwealth south and the Ascians north, plus a Waterworld plot where an old map's north means south.
proposes · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Commonwealth lies in US or Europe after pole reversal

I'm new to this list, so I apologize if this has already been discussed. In TBOTNS the Commonwealth is always refered to as being in the south and the Ascians are from the north, giving rise to the speculations that the Commonwealth might be in present-day South America. But I seem to remember reading somewhere that scientists believe that the Earth's magnetic poles had reversed sometime in the past (perhaps more than once). Has anyone else heard of this? In the movie 'Waterworld' the heroes finally found the legendary 'Dry-land' by realizing that when an ancient map said 'North', it actually meant 'South' because the north pole was now the magnetic south. If TBOTNS is far enough in the future, couldn't it be possible that this had happened in its past and then the Commonwealth could possibly be in the US or maybe Europe? Again, sorry if this theory has already been put forward.

-Steve

1999-10-26 Mitchell A. Bailey #

Argues that Urth, having solidified nearly through, is unlikely to have a significant magnetic field, so magnetic compasses and magnetic poles are moot in the New Sun books. Grounds this in planetary physics about molten cores and weaker fields on near-solid bodies, and speculates that ancient underground shelters may have been built against a magnetic-reversing meteorite impact.
proposes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Urth has no significant magnetic field; Underground shelters built against meteorite-caused magnetic reversal

It has, including a rather recent iteration which should be in the latest volume.

Since I've taken an interest in the 'magnetic' aspect of the issue, I'll repeat that Urth, having been said to have solidified nearly through, is unlikely to have a significant magnetic field. This is because a planetary magnetic field is believed to be generated by the convection and shear of a conductive more-or-less liquid core. E.g. the Sun, Earth's core of molten nickel-iron, Jupiter's theoretical outer core of highly compressed 'metallic' hydrogen. Bodies which are believed to be nearly solid, such as the Moon, Mercury, Mars, have correspondingly weaker magnetic fields. Therefore magnetic compasses and the magnetic poles, which may have been utterly forgotten perhaps ages before Severian's time to judge by their absence from the text, evidently are nonexistentent and moot.

BTW, I believe the phenomenon of planetary magnetic field reversal has been attributed in theory to major meteorite impacts massive enough to jar the circulating fluid. I can't think of any phenomenon, present or legendary, in UNS most likely explainable in terms of a major impact other than perhaps mass extinctions. Or maybe the immemorially ancient underground facilities such as those which eons later became the House Absolute and the basement of the Old Citadel were survival shelters against just such a disaster prior to the first stellar empire.

1999-10-26 Jim Henley #

Proposes two ideas about the Urth's geography: that the poles may have flipped and then flipped back before the magnetic field was lost, so people in Severian's time keep a reversed sense of north. Also suggests a major meteorite impact, without any pole shift, could explain where the extra land west of the Andes came from.
proposes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Urth's poles flipped back before the magnetic field was lost; Major meteorite impact created extra land west of the Andes

I'm not a Northern Hemisphere Partisan (NHP?) - far from it - but two things struck me on reading your very informative post:

1) If poles do flip, AND Urth has lost its magnetic field, might they have flipped back before the loss? Then people in Sev's time might retain the flipped sense of North.

2) Major meteoric impact _without_ pole shifting. Near as I understand the geography of Urth _and_ present-day South America, there simply ain't room to fit all Urth's points of interest west of the present-day Andes. Perhaps a major meteoric impact would start to explain where all that extra land came from.

Best,

Jim

1999-10-27 Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that the Commonwealth lies in the Southern Hemisphere, settling the question by citing a passage in The Citadel of the Autarch where the autarch corrects Severian about where the Ascians live. Builds on points earlier made by Mitchell Bailey and Mark Millman, and quotes the flier dialogue just before the crash as decisive.
supports · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: The Commonwealth lies in the Southern Hemisphere

I thought the question of whether the Commonwealth was in the Southern Hemisphere or not was settled. It is in the Southern Hemisphere, and there's no doubt about it. In addition to the points made by Mitchell Bailey and the observations of Mark Millman, it is clearly stated in chapter XXV of CITADEL, while the old autarch and Sev are in the flier, just before the crash. Sev tells the autarch what little he knows of the Ascians, that "...they live to the north in the hot countries, and that they're..." The autarch corrects him: "Half what you said of them was incorrect. They do not come from the hot countries of the north [i.e., near the equator], but from the continent that lies across the equator." Sorry, alga, but there it is.

Roy

1999-10-27 James Jordan #

Argues that the Commonwealth lies in South America, citing Blind Ultan as Borges and Nessus as a contraction of Buenos Aires, and suggests Alga's Mediterranean reading may be salvaged. Supports the South American location by noting the Spanish culture of Viron and the Ascians coming from the north, which the poster sees as settling the question.
proposes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: The Commonwealth is in South America; Blind Ultan is Borges; Nessus is a contraction of Buenos Aires; Viron Spanish culture from Pas and Typhon's people

The Slime (Alga) suggested long ago that because of the clearly Byzantine character of the Commonwealth, we are really in the Mediterranean. She made a valiant case for it, but it did not persuade many (any?), and she may not hold to it any longer. (Alga?)
Alga's thesis might be salvaged if we are in a physical SA location, near Buenos Aires, but the cultural geography is Mediterrean-esque. Doesn't the Long Sun somewhere say that the Spanish culture of Viron came from people living near to Pas/Typhon? That might be another indication of South America.
My own thinking started by noticing that Blind Ultan is Borges, and that Nessus looks like a contraction of Buenos Aires (as Meeya Meefla in Cordwainer Smith is Miami, Fla.). But the Ascians being from the north, the hot lands, and also from a continent north of the hot lands, seems to settle the question.

Nutria
