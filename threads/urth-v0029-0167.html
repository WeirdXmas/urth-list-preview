Sev's direct denial of good memory

Urth list · 1 messages · 2000-08-16 to 2000-08-16

2000urthThe Book of the New SunA Aramini

Themes: Memory and forgetting, Unreliable narration

2000-08-16 A Aramini #

Argues that Severian's denial in The Citadel of the Autarch that anyone could memorize thousands of Ascian tags shows he cannot imagine good but imperfect memory. Grounds this in his repeated claims to perfect recall, so Severian is condemned as an unreliable narrator.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian's memory claims make him an unreliable narrator

One of my favorite lines in the whole Book of the New Sun (I'm sure this has been commented on before, but I haven't had the chance to peruse the old archives) is in The Citadel of the Autarch in Chapter V: The Lazaret. An inane conversation with Loyal to the Group of Seventeen hides a wonderful statement by Severian.
They are talking about how the Ascians only speak with correct thought:

"'The rest have thousands- I suppose actually tens or hundreds of thousands - of those tags memorized.'

'That's impossible,' I said"

Even though it at first appears that Severian is denying that the society and its practices are possible, he is directly denying that anyone's memory could be that good.
What a great scene. In my mind, this is very condemning of Severian as a narrator, since he claims over and over to remember all those thousands of lines of his book that he will perfectly copy on the ship. He says several times that he cannot conceive of a memory that is less than perfect, yet here he is presented with simply good memory and denies that it can exist.
