Lune & Urth

Urth list · 6 messages · 2001-03-03 to 2001-03-08

2001urthThe Book of the New SunJeff WilsonTony EllisDavid DiGiacomo
The thread: Participants debated whether the Long and Short Sun books' Blue and Green are Urth and Lune, a question that turns on whether the worlds could be reached and how Urth stays normal. David DiGiacomo disputed the identification, arguing that travel between them would be impossible if they already occupied those worlds and questioning Urth's dim red sun. Jeff Wilson proposed that the Whorl returned to Urth and that Lune's gravity and atmosphere were altered by the same forces seen in the New Sun, citing the moon's changing distance. Tony Ellis objected that the black beans are a separate matter from the black hole, tied instead to Erebus's arrival on Urth. Wilson then offered an inverted Jack and the Beanstalk reading, and the thread ended with the positions unreconciled.
  • David DiGiacomo: Blue and Green cannot be Urth and Lune, since travel between them would be impossible
  • Jeff Wilson: the Whorl returned to Urth and gravity-altering forces changed Lune
  • Tony Ellis: the black beans concern Erebus's arrival on Urth, not the black hole and sun
  • Jeff Wilson: the black beans invert Jack and the Beanstalk, with a vinculum to Abbadon

Themes: The dying sun and the cosmos, Deep time and ruins, Stories within stories

2001-03-03 David DiGiacomo #

Argues that Blue and Green of the Long and Short Sun books cannot be Urth and Lune, because the travel between them would be impossible if they already occupied those worlds. Notes Green's gravity resembles Blue's and questions how Urth stays normal under a dim red Old Sun, also asking where the White Fountain is.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: Blue and Green are Urth and Lune

This has been discussed on the Whorl list. It doesn't really hold up. How could they travel to Urth if they were already there? Also, the gravity on Green seems similar to that of Blue.

What seems odd to me is that the Old Sun is so obviously dim and red, but conditions on Urth are more or less normal. I thought that even a change in the Sun's output too small to notice by eye would lead to catastrophic changes on Earth/Urth.

And while we're at it... is the White Fountain inside the Old Sun, orbiting Urth, or just hanging around?

2001-03-04 Jeff Wilson #

Argues that the Whorl could have returned to Urth, perhaps through Typhon's plan, and that Lune's gravity and atmosphere could have been altered by the same world-changing forces seen in the Book of the New Sun. Grounds this in gravity-altering technology in the text, the moon's movement from 250,000 to 150,000 miles, and oddities Severian does not remark on, such as dwindling apprentices.
proposes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Whorl returned to Urth via Typhon's plan; Lune's gravity altered by world-changing forces; Whorl plunged into sun revitalizing it

Urth-normal gravity is would be expected if Lune's atmosphere was to be retained without some sort of container. The current moon's gravity is not strong enough to retain air. BOTNS includes several examples of gravity-altering technology, and a force powerful enough to move the moon from today's 250,000 miles distance to 150,000 in the Autarchy could also add extra mass to it, increasing gravity "naturally." As for how the Whorl can leave Urth and travel to Urth, it could be a cosmic accident like in Planet Of The Apes (unlikely), or it could be a plot by the duplicitous Typhon. Typhon could have planned the Whorl as a contingency to restore his dynasty if were to have been deposed before it's arrival at the end of its circular course.

There may well have been, but the various other world-altering changes may have countered some of the effect and left the Commonwealth with no obvious differences that show in the text. There are so many oddities that Severian does not remark upon directly but only emerge by close reading that any number of others could be accommodated. For example, crop yields could be horribly low, vitamin deficiencies rampant, etc. but Sev does not mention them since from his POV that's the way things are. The vastly dwindling number of apprentices near his age might be a clue, though.

It's my opinion that it likely plunged into the sun, revitalizing it before it and the black beans cancel out.

Disputes Jeff Wilson's suggestion that the black hole plunged into the sun and cancelled out the black beans, arguing that the beans are a separate matter. Argues the black beans' story is instead how Erebus and his companions came to Urth, so they should not be equated with the black hole gnawing the sun.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Black beans are the story of Erebus's arrival on Urth

Pedant that I am, I have to point out that the black beans don't equate to the black hole gnawing away at the sun. The story of the black beans is the story of how Erebus and co. came to Urth.

2001-03-08 Jeff Wilson #

Proposes an inverted allusion in which the black beans and black hole of Wolfe's Urth works reverse the Jack and the Beanstalk tale, with a vinculum reaching down to Abbadon so its creatures can take the earth's golden solar energy. Grounds the idea in the earlier quoted exchange about the black beans and the sun, and offers the treasures Jack steals as a parallel to lost earthly assets like metal.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Black beans reverse Jack and the Beanstalk

More thinking reveals what might be a sort of inverted literary allusion in the black beans/black hole matter. (I've pointed out numerous
examples of inverted parallels and analogies in GW's work in times previous to the Wolfe fans on GEnie.) In a reversal of the Jack and the Beanstalk story, the black beans/hole grow a vinculum not up into the clouds, but "down" to the Abbadon universe, allowing the bad creatures there to make off with the earth's gold, i.e. the energy output required for the sun to show its natural yellow color when warming the earth. I suppose that the other treasures that Jack makes off with in the original
tale could be the other lost assets of the earth, like the supposed shortage of metal, but I haven't had to time to take it much farther, if I haven't stretched the comparison too far already.
