1999-10-17 Alice Turner #

Argues that Horn in The Book of the Long Sun, like Matthew among the gospel writers, invents details he did not witness, and that Horn is a frail vessel without help from Mainframe. Disputes Ratty's view that the Holy Spirit explains such results, citing Matthew's invented Magi and Osiris-linked flight to Egypt, and calls the Mainframe computer model clever despite Wolfe's age.
disputes · source-and-allusion · allegory-and-typology · theories: Horn invents events as Matthew did; Mainframe did not help Horn; Horn is a frail vessel like Paul; Matthew invented Magi and Osiris ties

How do you know that? I think Matthew gets it very wrong, and on purpose. I think he totally invents the ties to Zoroastrianism (the Magi) and Osiris (the flight to Egypt) for the very understandable reason that he wants to emphasize the afterlife (there being no real Jewish connection, and that of the Persians and the Egyptians being strong and powerful). There is absolutely no evidence for Herod's slaughter of baby boys. Matthew is a fabulator, much like Wolfe. The other gospeleers are quite different.

I do *not* think that Horn had help from Mainframe. Horn is clearly, at first anyway, before "merging," a "frail vessel." The analogy is with Paul, about whom Christian factions are still fighting. I must say, I think the computer model is downright brilliant, especially considering Wolfe's age; not to be unPC, but people over 60 have a hard time with computers, generally.

-alga

1999-10-17 Alex David Groce #

Argues that Wolfe probably does not think the Gospel writer Matthew invented events, so The Book of Long Sun should be given the benefit of the doubt that its Book of Silk matches the real events. Suggests other actors may have helped compose Silk's book in a Holy Spirit role and draws a Paul analogy, citing Galatians and a possible eye loss, as a fruitful comparison.
proposes · theology-and-religion · source-and-allusion · theories: Book of Silk matches events in Short Sun; Other actors composed The Book of Silk

What Nutria and I are saying is that whatever the truth value of "Matthew invented some stuff" is, it's unlikely that WOLFE thinks he did, which is an argument for giving THE BOOK OF THE LONG SUN a huge benefit of the doubt as to whether it matches up with the "real" events (never mind the fact that we're all discussing events that, of course, are purely fictional--the question is if the fiction is that "The Book of Silk" tells what really happened to Silk). Which also brings in the possible notion of other actors than Horn and Nettle in the composition of "The Book of Silk," acting in a Holy Spirit-type role, although that's more debateable. Much as Wolfe plays with -coy- narrators who don't tell everything that's going on, I'd be surprised if it turns out that "The Book of Silk's" version of events doesn't actually match up with what people say in Short Sun--meanings may change, but the events I'll bet stay the same.

Hmm... The Paul analogy might be really fruitful--isn't Paul's illness mentioned in Galatians sometimes supposed to have been a vision problem or even the _loss of an eye_?
