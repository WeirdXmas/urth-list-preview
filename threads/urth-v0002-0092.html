Themes: Ghosts, dreams and visions, Memory and forgetting

1997-06-12 John Bishop #

Reports that the poster realized partway through Peace that the house was non-physical and a memory, and at the end that the narrator was already dead, using a Chinese youth story as the clue. Also asks where the murders come from and admits uncertainty about the puzzle in There Are Doors.
informs · puzzle-solving · theories: The house in Peace is the narrator's memory; The narrator of Peace was already dead

It was about three-quarters of the way through the book
that I suddenly realized that the house was non-physical, and was essentially his memory ("What house has all the significant rooms from your life in it, in unchangable form?").

It was at the end that I figured out he was already dead. The reference to the story of the Chinese youth was the clue.

But where do you get murders from?

I like the puzzle aspects of Wolfe's work, if I get them, but sometimes I don't (e.g., what's really going on in There_are_Doors? Is the guy just a psychotic who isn't taking his medicine?).

-John
