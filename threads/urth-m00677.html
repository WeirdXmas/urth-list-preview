5HC : Utopias and tests

Urth list · 5 messages · 2005-02-03 to 2005-02-04

2005urthJim RaylorChris PMaru DubshinkiIorwerth Thomas
The thread: Jim Raylor argued that The Fifth Head of Cerberus is logically decidable within an axiomatic structure of literature and art, so that simulating its conditioning agents is unnecessary. Iorwerth Thomas questioned what that structure is, suggested it might be Gödel-incomplete, and said the novel is too big to be a proposition at all. Maru Dubshinki countered that Gödel's encoded integers are far larger in information than the novel, defining proposition as theorem, and later argued the book splits into three parts transforming one proposition. Chris held that the book's information is largely implicit and that a fiction cannot readily be a proposition like Gödel's formal proofs. The thread ended without resolution, with the propositional framing itself left contested rather than settled.
  • Jim Raylor: the novel is logically decidable within an axiomatic structure of literature and art
  • Iorwerth Thomas: the axiomatic structure is unclear and the novel is too big to be a proposition
  • Maru Dubshinki: size is no bar to being a proposition, since Gödel's encodings are far larger; the book is three parts transforming one proposition
  • Chris: the book's information is mostly implicit, and a fiction cannot readily be a formal proposition

Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Genre: science fiction, fantasy, both

Argues that The Fifth Head of Cerberus is a logically decidable proposition within the axiomatic structure of literature and art, and that a full simulation of conditioning agents is unnecessary once a problem-solving pattern is established. Draws on Skinner's account of private mental language and Turing's abstraction of finite-state devices, and links to a Prisoner fan site.

In private conversation, Skinner referred to his own
internal processes as any of us do (I feel, I
remember, I believe, I think etc) though if pushed he
could rephrase himself in more behaviouristic terms.

Turing abstracted by considering simple devices with a
finite number of states, from this he came upon the
basis of universal computing devices and an

I maintain 5HC is a logically and operationally
decidable proposition within the axiomatic structure
of literature and art.

A full simulation of all the conditioning agents is
NOT required to reinforce the problem-solving
behaviour once the pattern is established.

2005-02-04 Chris P replying to Maru Dubshinki #

Argues that the information in The Fifth Head of Cerberus is largely implicit in the text rather than limited to its explicit words, and that a fiction cannot readily be a proposition like Godel's formal proofs. Responds to Maru Dubshinki's claim about the encoded information in Godel proofs, questioning the comparison and noting that the book's elements are hard to define as propositions would be.
disputes · genre-and-craft

The information "in" 5HoC is not at all limited to the explicit data (the words written on the page); the vast majority is implicated by the text. Which is not to say that Godel's work is entirely explicit. But we are talking about two very different ways of writing or using language - one is tailored to try and be as explicit and rigorous as possible, the other not so much.

I suspect there is also an inherent problem with calling a work of fiction a 'proposition'; I have some doubts that something can be both at the same time, because even a false proposition is not exactly fictional.

Also, if 5HoC were intended as a proposition, it would be difficult to explain why the relations between its elements are so hard to define. Unless one means to suggest that it's just a really, really lousy proposition.
