<div>Hello,</div>
<br>
<div>Despite all the effort spent analyzing many of the embedded stories in Peace, </div>
<div>there seems to have been relatively little written in this forum about the </div>
<div>banshee story.  I think that the participants in this story can be identified </div>
<div>with some of the main characters in Peace, and that this story answers the </div>
<div>question of "what went wrong?" with Wolfe's relationship to Margaret.  Roy </div>
<div>Lackey gave much of the same answer to this question already, without relying </div>
<div>on the hints hidden in the banshee tale, in </div>
<div><a href="http://www.urth.net/urth/archives/v0030/0104.html" EUDORA=AUTOURL>http://www.urth.net/urth/archives/v0030/0104.html</div> <div>but the banshee story fills in a few more of the details.</div> <br>
<div>My identification in the story is:</div>
<div>Den Weer is Jack,</div>
<div>Margaret Lorn is Molly,</div>
<div>Carl Lorn is Molly's father,</div>
<div>Julian Smart is the banshee.</div>
<br>
<div>Let's review the banshee story.  </div>
<br>
<div>Molly's father owns a big farm and Molly is his only child, so whoever </div>
<div>marries her inherits the farm.  Jack and Molly are in love, but Molly's </div>
<div>father doesn't want Molly to marry Jack, "because Jack had nothing to bring </div>
<div>to the wedding but his own two hands and a smile."</div> <br>
<div>In the "what went wrong?" speech, Weer says "I was
intelligent and </div>
<div>industrious; Margaret and I loved one another deeply." But when he comes </div>
<div>back from college, the Depression has wiped out all of his fortune and made </div>
<div>jobs very hard to find.  How is he to make a living?</div> <br>
<div>One possibility would be for Weer to marry Margaret and move to the Lorn's </div>
<div>farm.   The Lorns likely still have their farm, since they wouldn't have had </div>
<div>large bank loans, because the farm has been in their family for years and </div>
<div>Carl Lorn is naturally frugal. Even though the income from the farm has been </div>
<div>greatly reduced, they can at least grow enough food to feed themselves.  It </div>
<div>would be reasonable for Weer to help on the farm if he is to marry Margaret; </div>
<div>she is the Lorn's only child, and so will inherit the farm. Later in the </div>
<div>book, the farmer delivering potatoes to the factory says "When I was a boy, </div>
<div>there wasn't anything anybody could have that was better'n a farm.  If a </div>
<div>doctor or a banker could marry a widow that had a good one, they'd stop what </div>
<div>they was doing and work it."  This farmer is ten or twenty years older than </div>
<div>Weer, but taking this and the banshee story together, I conclude that when </div>
<div>Weer was about to graduate, he and Margaret proposed that they get married </div>
<div>and that Den move out to the farm and help work
it.
</div>
<div>                                &nb</div>
<div>                                &nb</div>
<div>                                &nb</div>
<div>                                &nb</div>
<div>
</div>
<br>
<div> From the banshee story, we see that Carl Lorn didn't want Weer to marry </div>
<div>Margaret.  He disapproved of selling the egg, and he's tight with money </div>
<div>("only Daddy never gets nothing at the store if he can find something else </div>
<div>that'll do") so Weer has two strikes against him, being Olivia's nephew and </div>
<div>being penniless.  In the banshee story, Molly's father tells Jack he'd have </div>
<div>to spend the whole night in the barn haunted by the banshee and not be thrown </div>
<div>out; in Weer's real life, Carl probably told Weer that he wouldn't let </div>
<div>Margaret marry him unless he could support her.  So Weer has to take the job </div>
<div>at the factory (the real-life parallel of the barn); unfortunately, the </div>
<div>coldhouse prank kills somebody, and Julius blackmails him into staying at the </div>
<div>factory until Julius dies.  In this scenario, it doesn't even matter whether </div>
<div>Margaret knows about the coldhouse prank.  Weer can't marry her unless he </div>
<div>finds another job which pays better, and he can't leave this job because of </div>
<div>Julius' blackmail.  Eventually, Margaret gets fed up with Weer's inaction and </div>
<div>marries Mr. Price (whom we suspect, from his surname, has sufficient money).  </div>
<br>
<div>There are some interesting differences between the banshees in Peace and the </div>
<div>banshees in Irish folklore.  Looking on the web, it seems that in Irish </div>
<div>folklore, the banshee is a woman sidhe who wails before somebody is going to </div>
<div>die (and most important families have an associated banshee that predicts </div>
<div>deaths in that family).  In some versions of the legend, if you catch the </div>
<div>banshee, she has to tell you who is going to die.  However, she only </div>
<div>foretells deaths, unlike Wolfe's banshee, who causes them. Note that Wolfe </div>
<div>is playing off the folklore banshee: when his banshee is caught, she does </div>
<div>tell Jack "who's to be born," although one might suspect that she's lying out </div>
<div>of spite.</div>
<br>
<div>Wolfe's banshees are ghosts (quite fittingly for Peace) who are "the spirits </div>
<div>of midwives that have killed the baby because someone gave gold to them to do </div>
<div>it that it might not inherit."  This gives grounds for identifying the </div>
<div>banshee as Julius: in </div>
<div><a href="http://urth.net/urth/archives/v0205/1043.txt.shtml" EUDORA=AUTOURL>http://urth.net/urth/archives/v0205/1043.txt.shtml, </div>
<div>I speculated that Julius may have killed Mr. Tilly so as to inherit the carny </div>
<div>business, which, while not an exact parallel, is certainly similar.  </div>
<br>
<div>Jack stays in the barn haunted by the banshee three nights. The first two </div>
<div>times, the banshee forces him to speak the name of someone whom she later </div>
<div>kills, first "the meanest man [Jack] can think of, a man that robbed </div>
<div>everybody and never gave poor folk a penny," and second, "a real old lady </div>
<div>that was goin' to die anyway."  The third time, he defeats the banshee.  If </div>
<div>we identify the banshee with Julius, this is reminiscent of Weer's comment </div>
<div>that he recalls uncle Julius clearly "only at three stages of his life."  The </div>
<div>first stage is when Julius tells the story of Mr. Tilly.  The second stage is </div>
<div>at aunt Olivia's funeral.  And the last is when Julius is an old man and the </div>
<div>president.  This seems a little strange, because Den lived with Julius for </div>
<div>quite a while, and this period doesn't seem included in either of these </div>
<div>stages.  However, if we take these three stages as
corresponding to the three </div>
<div>nights in the banshee story, then we are led to suspect that Julius killed </div>
<div>both Mr. Tilly and Olivia for their inheritances.  We know (since Wolfe has </div>
<div>confirmed it) that it was Peacock who actually ran Olivia over in his car, </div>
<div>but there are at least two reasons to suspect that Peacock might have </div>
<div>conspired with Julius: first, Julius Smart and Professor Peacock were good </div>
<div>friends, and second, there's the mysterious letter from Peacock to Julius </div>
<div>that Weer is unable to read.</div>
<br>
<div>Is there any more evidence for this correspondence between the banshee story </div>
<div>and Weer's life?  In the other stories in Peace that we know have parallels </div>
<div>(the princess and her suitors, and the marid), there is a correspondence </div>
<div>hidden in the characters' names.  The link from Molly to Mary to Margaret </div>
<div>seems obvious, but I don't see how to get from Jack to Den. Any ideas?</div>
