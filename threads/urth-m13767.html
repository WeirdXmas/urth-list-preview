2010-07-21 Lee Berman #

Argues that Severian dies and is resurrected several times as an eidolon, and that his healing and water-breathing powers came only when he became an eidolon or aquastor aboard Tzadkiel's ship. Grounds this in the Sanguinary Fields, where no dead copy of Severian is mentioned, and in the puzzle of why his powers never showed earlier, such as during torturer work.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian dies and is resurrected as an eidolon several times; Severian's powers were bestowed when he became an eidolon on the ship

I think Severian dies and is resurrected as an eidolon several times (probably five).

Oh, yes, I'd forgotten that line. Thanks Tony. It does seem rather explicit. A shame it can obliterate a nice theory. With Severian's death underwater or on the battlefield we can imagine a dead body surreptitiously replaced by an eidolon as a possibility. But not on the Sanguinary Fields. Somehow a dead copy of Severian's body would have to be mentioned by Dorcas or Hildegrin later. That was surely self-resurrection.

So perhaps all of Severian's power, resurrection, healing, water breathing were bestowed when he became an eidolon/aquastor on the Ship. And his powers are at least nascent as long as the White Fountain is within range.

Makes me wonder why Severian never had any expression of his power before Triskele. As a torturer there would be no shortage of injuries and dead bodies around. I expect it would be noticed if they started healing and coming back to life. Perhaps it was seeing the image of his dead mother in the grave which triggered his abilities into action.

2010-07-21 Jeff Wilson replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that if the original Severian survived to the Ship, the skull from Gyoll cannot be his mortal remains, and proposes it symbolizes an earlier time-loop iteration instead. Grounds this in Severian's resurrection and healing in the early volumes, and in the idea that aquastors are unliving projections that need not breathe, reconsidering how the Ship eidolon might have been made and injured.
proposes · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Severian's Gyoll skull symbolizes an earlier time-loop iteration; Ship Severian is an eidolon made from an injured copy

If the original, all-meat Severian lasted until the Ship, the skull from Gyoll can't be his, nor a symbol for his mortal remains since he didn't leave any. I'd taken it to symbolize the previous time-loop iteration that he supposes did die, the way the multiple slots in the mausoleum do.

As was his recovery from the ray at the jailers' keep.

This seems out of sequence, he demonstrates resurrection and healing during the first four volumes.

I'd like to take credit for an idea that seems to have caught on, but when I said aquastors presumably could be turned to breathe water, I meant that as unliving projected images they needn't be dependent on air or any other necessities of life. Indeed, Aquastor Triskele is heard even after his physical form has dissipated. They can take advantage of cartoon conceits, since they are effectively 3D cartoons.

Of course, the level of fidelity needed to make an aquastor that *can* eat, breathe, etc. and eventually accumulate enough mass to continue independently after the projection stops might further allow alterations in its bodyily plan, but there is no sign that this is ever done, even when one would think it would be greatly helpful to do so, like restoring Severian's leg.

Though this could mean that when Sev is eidoloned after his fall on the ship, they either chose to reproduce him at a time when he received considerable but not fatal injuries, chose a time when he had received otherwise mortal injuries but applied sufficient life-saving arts before he awoke, or they chose a time before he was injured in the fall for maximum fidelity of reproduction and then carefully monitored and beat him while unconscious to provide a convincing amount of injury for when he awoke.

Or perhaps those traumas were perceived as just and did not arouse in him any feeling of sympathy. Which would make for an interesting interpretation of his feelings for Thecla and the nature of her trauma.

2010-07-21 Jerry Friedman replying to Jeff Wilson #

Relays Jeff Wilson's argument that Severian's indifference to the torture victims he processes reflects a failure of compassion rather than a judgment of justice. Grounds this in Severian's training to see clients as sellers see buyers, comparing their words to mouse squeaks, while noting Triskele's suffering does arouse his compassion.
informs · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian's indifference stems from compassion not justice

From: Jeff Wilson <[email]>

I suspect it's more about compassion than justice. Severian has been trained to think of those people as "clients", the way sellers (not just Agia and Agilus) think of buyers. Just as their words convey no more meaning than the squeaking of mice, their suffering doesn't arouse compassion in him. Triskele's suffering does.

Jerry Friedman

2010-07-21 Lee Berman replying to Lee Berman #

Proposes that Severian's powers of resurrection, healing and water-breathing were bestowed when he became an eidolon or aquastor on the Ship, with the White Fountain's nearness giving them force. Grounds this in the ship travelling outside time, the Fountain being closer to Urth than in Typhon's era, and the claim that the Fountain is an aspect of Severian himself.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theology-and-religion · theories: Severian's powers bestowed when he became an eidolon; White Fountain is an aspect of Severian; Ship travels outside of time

Or holograms? Wolfe does invoke them in the first few chapters and eidolons can shatter and become silver shards or something like that. Anyway, I guess they gain real substance by eating and drinking and adding read atoms to their bodies or something like that (say B, F and o?).

So perhaps all of Severian's power, resurrection, healing, water breathing were bestowed when he became an eidolon/aquastor on the Ship. And his powers are at least nascent as long as the White Fountain is within range.

I think we are meant to understand that the Ship is travelling outside of time and temporal sequencing there has little meaning, relative to Urth.

During the first four volumes the White Fountain is in existence and hurtling toward Urth. In fact it is much closer to Urth than when Severian takes conscious control of his power back in Typhon's era.

We are told the White Fountain is an aspect of Severian himself. So, when he was born it was under a star which was already a part of him and it had the potential to convey divine power to him throughout most of his life, conscious of it or not. (perhaps this is an allusion to another famous birth star?).
