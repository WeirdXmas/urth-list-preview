1997-09-10 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Asks newt and alga whether Cordwainer Smith's work sits nearer to C.S. Lewis's unambiguous Christian mode than to Wolfe's, and places Chesterton's The Man Who Was Thursday at the Wolfe end of a spectrum. Responds to newt's point about Lewis's unambiguous Christian mode and grumbles about typos in Vance's Planet of Adventure omnibus before getting to the question.
questions · genre-and-craft

Reply: Item #6279912 from [email]@INET02#

Rostrum,

These days I hold my breath and hope that more typos aren't added. <cue: ranting about omnibus of Vance's PLANET OF ADVENTURE> Your mention of it makes me think fleetingly of submitting a list of clear typos (nevermind those two stealthies), but nah, that would only make me more crazy.

newt,

Interesting point, re: the unambiguous/beat-reader-over-head with Christianity mode of C.S. Lewis. Can we take this a little further? My personal sense of Cordwainer Smith is that his work is somewhat closer to the mode of Lewis than the mode of Wolfe (I readily admit I prefer Smith over Lewis). Would you agree? (How about you, alga?) Not to denigrate any of these authors, just arranging them in a spectrum. (The Chesterton of THE MAN WHO WAS THURSDAY to my mind is definitely at the Wolfe end of the spectrum.)

=mantis=

1997-09-11 James Jordan #

Asks for locations of typos in the Long Sun books and comments that Cordwainer Smith is unambiguous but sometimes difficult, thematically close to Wolfe yet stylistically different. Responds to Mantis's remark about typos and C.S. Lewis, and to Jim Jordan's comparison of Smith and Lewis, by requesting a list of errors rather than offering evidence.
questions

Could you or someone post the places where the typos in Long Sun are located?

Jumping in, I myself much prefer Smith to Lewis, and agree that he is not ambiguous (though sometimes difficult). Thematically he is (was) very close to Wolfe, but stylistically quite different, no?

Nutricious
