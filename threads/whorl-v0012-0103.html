2001whorlThe Book of the Short SunTimothy Reilly

2001-02-01 Timothy Reilly #

Argues that Horn in In Green's Jungles delivers sententious, didactic speeches about life and family that read like empty clichés rather than wisdom. Responds to Michael Straight's complaint about Horn's disquisitions, admitting he may be misreading them and has not yet reread the book.
disputes · close-textual-reading

Michael Straight asked of IGJ:

I confess that this is one of the things I disliked about IGJ - a certain sententiousness from Horn is his disquisitions about life, family relationships et al. I couldn't help thinking that it was meant to be wise counsel from Wolfe, though the effect for me was more like Polonius's advice to Hamlet ie vacuous cliches presented as insightful. Maybe I'm wrong in interpreting Horn's wise words in this fashion, and I've yeat to reread IGJ so it might not irritate in the same way in future. But in short, I did think it was didactic and I didn't like it.

Tim
