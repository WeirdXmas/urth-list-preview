Wolfe's Long Sun mentioned on Fox TV program this week

Urth list · 9 messages · 2012-05-02 to 2012-05-02

2012urthThe Book of the Long SunMatthew KeeleyDavid StockhoffFred KiescheDan'l Danehy-OakesAntonin ScriabinDaniel Petersen
The thread: Readers reported that the Fox TV series Fringe briefly mentioned Wolfe's Long Sun books in a bookshop scene, and Daniel Petersen asked for a detailed account of the context. Fred Kiesche and Matthew Keeley shared the mention and a link, and Kiesche mostly expressed delight and plans to buy the DVDs. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes proposed that the geek-stereotype reading explained the choice, citing a line about a girl who likes science fiction. Petersen countered that the reference was a deliberate nod to Wolfe fans rather than mere stereotyping, citing Mark Aramini's remark about cuneiform. The thread ended with the sides still apart and no settled reading of the reference.
  • Fred Kiesche: delighted at the mention and plans to order the DVDs
  • Matthew Keeley: shared the link and pointed back to an earlier list discussion
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: the mention marks a character as a geek, picking the geekiest book
  • Daniel Petersen: a deliberate nod to Wolfe fans, not mere geek stereotyping
  • Daniel Petersen: asked for context from the episode and the blog source

Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Genre: science fiction, fantasy, both

2012-05-02 Daniel Petersen replying to DAVID STOCKHOFF #

Reports that a Fox TV episode of Fringe briefly mentions Lake of the Long Sun, transcribing the bookshop dialogue where a bookseller praises Wolfe and offers the Solar Cycle. Guesses the reference is a nod to the sf community that does not bear on the plot, and says it made the poster's week.
informs

Sorry if this had already been flagged up on the board. If it did, I missed it somehow and I just got notified of it in Wolfe google alerts today. Anyway, THANK YOU very much Matthew Keeley for hunting up that clip (and Frank for asking friends)! Turns out it's *Lake* of the Long Sun that's mentioned, not *Litany* (as the Hoof & Hide blog had said). I got so fanboy excited when I saw this, I immediately transcribed it:

*Bookseller:* Can I help you?

*Man:* I hope so. The lady here is looking for a book called *Lake of the Long Sun* by Gene Wolfe. Do you know him?

*Bookseller:* Are you kidding me? I worship him! And a girl as beautiful as you who likes Sci Fi is a girl after my own heart. I?m Ed by the way.

*Woman:* Olivia. Sorry, taken.

*Bookseller:* All the best ones are. Story of my life. Ok, I?ve got the entire Solar Cycle, first edition. You want signed or unsigned?

And from there they ask him if he knows about a cuneiform symbol that they show him on a piece of paper and the clip ends.

So my guess is that this was a simple nod from the series writers to the s.f. community - maybe a little test of how erudite the bookseller's (and their audience's) knowledge is of one of the truly great works of modern s.f. (the Solar Cycle) - and that the reference doesn't really have anything to do with the plot. Still, NICE TOUCH! And how cool is it that out of all the authors of s.f. they chose Wolfe, and that they chose a volume from *Long* Sun rather than the more obvious *New* Sun, and that he refers to the ENTIRE series, the Solar Cycle? And that the dude says he worships him, ha! Amazing.

That made my week.

-DOJP
