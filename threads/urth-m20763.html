Secrets and Domes

Urth list · 6 messages · 2011-11-28 to 2011-11-29

2011urthThe Book of the Short SunBrian LovelyAntónio Pedro MarquesDavid StockhoffJames Wynn
The thread: Readers asked what the broken glass dome in Wolfe's Blue's Waters and Seawrack is, how it was broken, and why Seawrack is troubled to be near it. Brian proposed that the inhumi are a hybrid of humanity and Whorl colonists and suggested the dome might be a ruined greenhouse. James Wynn pressed for a theory of its one-way light and its small trees, while David Stockhoff speculated that a large animal or inhumi slaves broke it and that it is a violated Neighbor nursery. Brian later wondered whether the ring was a cylinder rather than a dome and asked whether others had theorized the ruin. The thread ended without consensus, with the questions left open.
  • James Wynn: the dome needs a theory of its builders, its one-way light, and its trees
  • David Stockhoff: a large animal or inhumi slaves broke it; it is a violated Neighbor nursery
  • Brian: the inhumi are a Whorl-human hybrid and the dome may be a ruined greenhouse
  • Brian: the ring may be a cylinder whose light effect works both ways

Themes: The dying sun and the cosmos, Evolution and the future of humanity, Colonization and the native

2011-11-28 David Stockhoff replying to James Wynn #

Speculates that a large animal or inhumi slaves broke the glass dome, and that Seawrack avoids it because it is a violated Neighbor nursery. Answers James Wynn's quoted questions from Brian by noting that the dome may be hard to explain if the Neighbors left long ago, and that Seawrack, though friendly with them, knows enough about them.
answers · puzzle-solving · theories: The broken dome was smashed by a large animal or inhumi slaves; Seawrack avoids the dome because it is a violated Neighbor nursery

I don't think these are terribly difficult questions. I'd guess:

(1) A big animal ran into it. Or inhumi slaves smashed it to expose the small trees inside. Depending on how long it's been since the Neighbors left, it could be harder to explain why such a glass structure might still be standing.

(2) Because she knows it's a violated Neighbor nursery and she is not a Neighbor but knows enough about them, even if she may be on friendly terms with them.

2011-11-29 Brian Lovely replying to Brian #

Brian argues that the broken glass ring in Blue's Waters might be a cylinder rather than a dome, and that its one-way light effect could work both ways, while still believing the ruin matters to the larger puzzle. He responds to Gerry Quinn's points about hybrid humans and the ruined greenhouse dome, and asks whether others have theorized about the ruin.
proposes · puzzle-solving · theories: Inhumi are a hybrid branch of humanity; Ruined glass ring is a cylinder not a dome

Good point, Gerry, after I sent it, I thought about how curved pieces of a broken structure with a circular footprint could also be a cylinder, not a dome. ...and the one-way glass could be arranged to let out as well as in. Still, I think it is signifigant, and I don't recall seeing any theories about how the ruin fits into the greater puzzle.

humanity, > and the colonists from the Whorl are the pure strain hybridizing through blood > to make a more vigorous strain? Marc has ideas along somewhat similar lines, I think. But really, I don?t see any strong reason to believe that the creatures of Green and Blue are anything other than alien species that evolved there. [Of course, one wouldn?t expect truly alien planets to have compatible ecosystems, but that?s something SF writers get a free pass on, same as FTL. Planets are like tropical islands during the great age of European sea exploration.] > Also, does anyone else see the ruined ring of glass that lets light through in > one direction but blocks it in the other as a ruined greenhouse dome? That?s one reading of it, though I didn?t see anything about it blocking light in any direction. It seems to amplify light: a possibility that occurred to me is that it transmits light from adjacent dimensions. Against that is that when Horn first saw the motion of the bush where the greenbuck was, he thought the glass might have helped him. But he later saw motion in another bush without it, and after that saw the greenbuck. Glass that amplifies light might be used in a greenhouse, or a solar power station, or an observatory, or for other purposes. - Gerry Quinn
