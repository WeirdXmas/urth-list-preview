Inhumi Secret

Whorl list · 4 messages · 2000-08-09 to 2000-08-10

2000whorlThe Book of the Long SunThe Book of the Short SunWilliam H. AnsleyJames JordanAdam Stephanides
The thread: Jim Jordan proposed that the Long Sun inhumi secret is that humans must be willing to die for the inhumi, citing the Outsider's eucharist with Horn and a triptych reading of Severian, Silk and Horn as growth toward Christlikeness, with a Cordwainer Smith comparison. William Ansley agreed the die-for-them idea seems right but doubted it could work as a credible threat, since the inhumi already know what humans can do, and argued Wolfe will not state the secret outright. Adam Stephanides objected that the idea fails to explain inhumi blood drinking, Krait's mother drinking from Sinew, and the Neighbor's remark, and quoted On Blue's Waters saying applying the secret would revert the inhumi to unintelligent predators. Jordan conceded the proposed secret may be a technique rather than a maturation, and agreed the secret likely stays unstated, leaving the guessing game open.
  • Jim Jordan: humans must be willing to die for the inhumi, a Christ-like secret Horn will act on by giving his life for Jahlee
  • William Ansley: the die-for-them idea seems right but is not a credible threat, and Wolfe will not reveal the secret directly
  • Adam Stephanides: the die-for-them secret fails to explain the blood drinking and the Neighbor's remark, and On Blue's Waters suggests humans would refuse it
  • Jim Jordan (later): the secret may be a technique rather than something humans mature into, and it will likely stay unstated

Themes: Christ figures and messiahs, Suffering and sacrifice, Names and their meanings

2000-08-09 James Jordan #

Proposes that the secret in the Long Sun books is that humans must be willing to die for the inhumi, as Christ died for enemies, and predicts Horn will give his life for Jahlee. Grounds this in the Outsider's eucharist with Horn and in a triptych reading of Severian, Silk and Horn as progressive growth toward Christlikeness, with a comparison to Cordwainer Smith's Casher O'Neill stories.
proposes · theology-and-religion · allegory-and-typology · theories: the secret is that men must die for the inhumi; Horn gives his life for Jahlee; Severian, Silk and Horn form a triptych of Christlike growth

I submit for your consideration that the secret is that men must die for the inhumi. They must learn to die for each other, but in this narrative, also to die for the inhumi. It's not just the golden rule, but being willing to lay down your life for your enemies, as Jesus did. I submit that the eucharist the Outsider leads Horn to perform indicates that this will turn out to be the answer. That's the meaning of the eucharist, for by eating the death of Jesus into ourselves, we become able to die for our enemies as He died for His.
That's why the secret is so hard. Humans will find this the hardest thing of all to do. It's harder than loving each other, because it's easy to love the lovable. Or, it's the toughest kind of love.
When humans become willing to die for their enemies, and inhumi acquire this characteristic from them, the inhumi will be willing to die rather than destroy humans. This will "destroy" the inhumi, though I suspect it will not annihilate them but transform them somehow.
I bet that Horn gives his life for Jahlee before it's all over. I also bet that when it's all over, the Memoirs of Severian, the Book of Silk, and the Book of Horn will be seen as a progression. Severian never really learns much about love, and certainly never dies for anyone. He does grow and mature, but not as much as Silk. Silk makes much more progress, but Horn will make the most. The triptich of their lives will make one thematic narrative of personal growth and maturation into Christlikeness. I cannot help but think of the Casher O'Neill stories of Cordwainer Smith, though I don't know if these influenced Wolfe at all. They deal with politics, spirituality, and religion progressively, as "On the Gem Planet" is a "mirror for princes"; "On the Wind Planet" is about conversion; and "On the Sand Planet" is about the application of that conversion back into the world. In a very broad way, Wolfe's three narratives follow the same kinds of themes: in the real world, apart from the real world (on the whorl), and back into the real world. Just a thought.
But I'll bet I'm right about the secret.

Patera Nutria

2000-08-09 William H. Ansley #

Agrees the idea that men must die for the inhumi seems right but doubts Horn could use it as a credible threat, since the inhumi already understand what humans can do and no one would believe it. Argues Wolfe will not directly reveal the inhumi secret in the final book, so the guessing game can continue, citing the reading of In Green's Jungles.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Men must die for the inhumi

Nutria,

You know, I really like this idea. It seems somehow very right. The only problem with it is that I really find it hard to see how Horn could have used it as a credible threat against the inhumi. They seem to have a pretty realistic idea of what humans are capable of, so I don't know why they would be at all worried about Horn telling this fact to anyone else. And way would anyone else believe it?

Frankly, after reading IGJ, I no longer believe that Wolfe has any intention of directly revealing the secret of the inhumi in the last book, so we will probably be able to keep up this guessing game indefinitely.

William Ansley

2000-08-09 Adam Stephanides #

Disputes Jim Jordan's proposal that the secret of the inhumi is that humans must die for them, arguing it fails to explain the inhumi's blood drinking, Krait's mother drinking from Sinew, or the Neighbor's remark. Grounds the objection in a recalled passage from On Blue's Waters where Horn says applying the secret would return the inhumi to unintelligent predators, so humans would refuse it.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: the secret is men dying for the inhumi

I don't think that this is the secret, for a couple of reasons. Fr one thing, it doesn't fit several of the things we know about the secret: it doesn't explain why the inhumi drink human blood, it doesn't have anything to do with Krait's mother drinking from Sinew, and it doesn't explain the Neighbor's remark on p. 21.

For another thing, iirc (I don't have the book with me) in OBW Horn says explicitly that if humans could use the secret it would turn the inhumi back into the unintelligent predators they originally were. So if humans did come to "love the inhumi," they would presumably refuse to apply the secret (since the inhumi have no wish to lose their intelligence).

--Adam

2000-08-10 James Jordan #

Concedes that the proposed secret Horn holds against the inhumi may not be the secret itself, arguing it would have to be a technique rather than something humans mature into. Agrees with the earlier reply that the secret will not be stated outright in the text, responding to Nutria's idea and Jim Jordan's objection about the inhumi's realistic view of humans.
supports · close-textual-reading

Yeah, you're probably right on both counts. That's what happens when you stay up too late and get inspired to write a post.
I'm still inclined to think what I wrote may turn out to be a theme in the book, but perhaps not the secret. If the secret is something that Horn could pull out of the hat and apply instantly any time he wants, then it is not something that humans have to mature into, but a technique of some sort. And I agree that we're not going to be TOLD the secret.
