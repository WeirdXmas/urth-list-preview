1997-06-02 Ron Crown #

Argues that the gammadion in The Book of the Long Sun is a religious symbol that has been downgraded into a tool, unlike the T in Huxley's Brave New World, and that it is used to work the Windows of revelation. Responds to an earlier post comparing the gammadion to Huxley's T and to screwdrivers, rejecting the simple equivalence and noting that only priests and Silk seem to carry one.
disputes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion · theories: the gammadion is a downgraded religious symbol used as a tool

But the difference is that the T symbol was not originally part of a state religion but becomes such as part of Huxley's ironic comment on the divinisation of the material (or vice versa?) while, assuming that there is some connection between our Earth (which may be assuming a lot) and the WHORL/URTH, a symbol which was originally religious continues to function in something of a quasi-religious or downgraded religious function. Aren't priests the only ones who carry the gammadion? For that matter, is anyone besides Silk mentioned as having one? Furthermore, the gammadion does function as a tool for working on the 'Windows', the sources of 'revelation' whether you regard that revelation as true or false or (better I think) truth which conceals another truth? And I don't think that Wolfe uses language in a sort of simple equivalence such as gammadion = screwdriver and nothing more; after all, he could have invented some science fictinal gobbledygook for futuristic tools like they do on Star Trek (induced phase decoupler or some such nonsense).

mantis, thanks for reminding me of "Urth-Man Extraordinary"; I've been meaning to buy a copy from Chris Drumm (who has all the Galactic Central bibs available, I think) but wasn't aware that it included secondary items as well. Eventually, I'd like to obtain copies of the 'artifacts' of course; but for right now I'm satisfied with bibliographic references. As it happens, I've got all three of the Vector items you mentioned.

Ron Crown

1997-06-02 Matthew Freestone #

Informs readers that the fanzine The Book of Gold appears to have only two issues and offers to photocopy them for anyone interested. Describes issue one's contents: a review with a glossary, a chapter-by-chapter analysis of Soldier of the Mist, and a short piece on names in The Book of the New Sun.
informs

The consensus seems to be that there are just the two issues of 'The Book of Gold'. I have copies of both which I can photocopy if anyone is desperate to see them.
No. 1 contains mainly 'Soldier of the Mist'-related material. There's a review with a glossary that covers some gaps in the book's own list, and there's a column of chapter-by-chapter analysis of what exactly is going on in SOTM.
