On dream travellers

Urth list · 7 messages · 2011-02-12 to 2011-02-15

The thread: Lee Berman and Andrew Mason debated whether dream travellers and eidolons in Wolfe's fiction can eat, digest, and become substantial, with Berman using Severian's digestion and Barbatus's account of matter as bound energy to argue they cannot fully become part of the world. Berman later proposed a half-physical, half-spiritual existence reconciling James Wynn and Roy Lackey, and linked the bleeding angel episode to the need to eat and breathe. James Wynn offered a computer-process model of astral and corporeal bodies to explain the rules of dream-travel. David Duffy compared it to real-world astral travel, then drifted to other astral-travel fiction, and the thread ended without resolution. A side exchange on Ares and Aphrodite was a separate tangent.

  • James Wynn: dream-travel works like a parent-child computer process, with the astral body as parent and the corporeal body as dependent child

2011-02-12 Lee Berman replying to Andrew Mason #

Argues that eidolons and dream travellers can eat and digest, citing Barbatus's explanation that matter is bound energy and that a visitor becomes substantial by consuming matter over time. Uses Severian's digestion as the key distinction: digesting is what makes a traveller real, so dream travellers cannot fully become part of the world they enter.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theology-and-religion · theories: Severian becomes real by digesting matter

Yes, Barbatus explains that:

"What you call eidolons are not ghosts, but beings maintained in existence by some external energy source. What you call matter is all, in actuality, merely bound energy. The only difference is that some is held in material form by its own energy.

and

"...when you cam here, you had lived a long time...During all that time you breathed ate, and drank matter that was not unstable, converting it to your body's use. Thus it became a substantial body".

So, yes, eidolons can eat and digest. Severian digesting is actually essential to him becoming "real".

Dream travellers may be able to produce deadly swords and viable sperm but they can't eat and digest and become part of the world/time/universe they have travelled to.

2011-02-13 Lee Berman replying to Andrew Mason #

Argues that beings met in dream travel in the Long Sun books have a half-physical, half-spiritual existence, reconciling James Wynn's and Roy Lackey's positions on whether they are solid. Grounds this in folklore, noting that poltergeists throw objects yet must have some physical presence, and questions how Lackey's ghost model explains their vulnerability to physical weapons.
proposes · myth-and-structure · theories: Dream-travel beings have a half in, half out existence

There seems to be a bit of talking past each other here. James seems to be saying that beings in dream travel take on a new form of existence, where some physical laws apply and others don't.

Roy seems to be saying beings in dream travel are pure, unsubstantial ghosts or spirits because they can walk through walls. But I don't see how he explains why such beings can be harmed by physical weapons.

(I still think the answer lies in folktales/mythology etc. How can a poltergeist throw things around if it has no physical existence? Same for other mythological-type beings- they must have some physical presence in our world or we'd be unable to know anything about them. Yet they don't follow the physical laws of our universe either. Perhaps a "half in-half out" sort of existence.)

2011-02-14 James Wynn replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that dream-travel in The Book of the New Sun works like a computer system in which the astral body is the parent process and the corporeal body the dependent child, explaining why killing the astral body is catastrophic. Offers this model as an account of the rules and limits of dream-travel, notes that the Rajan can afford to be casual because he can return at will, and so responds to Lee Berman's suggestion about eidolons needing food.
proposes · myth-and-structure · theories: Astral body is parent process during dream-travel; Corporeal body is dependent child process during dream-travel; Killing astral body is catastrophic, corporeal death is not

Another difference is that the eidolon becomes an independent branch of the original. From that moment, there are two of the original. This is not the case for the dream-traveler.

In IT terms, the astral body at first SEEMS to be a "client" of the corporeal body (this is the relationship Roy seems to be driving at). When the soul "returns" to the corporeal, the astral body and any child processes it has created (weapons, people, etc) are destroyed. This is efficient since it would other cause a lot of useless corpses to be left around.

It is actually more complex than that, and somewhat to the contrary. The astral body is not supported by the corporeal form during dream-travel. It is robust and self-sustaining (without the NEED to take food). During dream-travel it appears that actually the astral body temporarily becomes the parent process and the corporeal form the child process (the unstable dependent process). I say this because:

* If the corporeal body dies, the astral body can continue to run on its own without debility.

* If the astral body dies, this is a catastrophic crash: You are left with the astral corpse undestroyed (a "zombie" process) and the soul cannot "reboot" on the corporeal side. The soul on the astral side is claimed by the Increate.

When the soul moves to astral form, the "system" erases the version on the corporeal. This prevents identity confusion when the soul is re-integrated. When the soul successful returns to corporeal, it is re-written to the corporeal form before destroying the astral. This is why killing the astral body is catastrophic but killing the corporeal body is not.

Not that Wolfe was likely thinking in these terms, but the model is, I think, an accurate representation of the rules and limitations.

Additionally, I think the Rajan could afford to be non-chalant about being "not really here" during dream-travel because he could return at any time. This is not the case for anyone traveling with him. They could be marooned at the will of the Rajan and they could not bug-out at the first hint of danger.
