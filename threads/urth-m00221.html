Sulpicius

Urth list · 5 messages · 2004-10-04 to 2004-10-05

2004urthThe Book of the New SunSeth Lombardimournings gloryChris PRoy C. Lackey
The thread: Posters on the Sulpicius thread debated whether the Autarch carries subsumed memories of earlier Autarchs, and whether Severian is himself one of them. Mournings glory argued that the library room was kept for Sulpicius's return and that memories channel back through successors, and Seth Lombardi floated Ymar requesting many minds to serve his subjects. Roy C. Lackey disputed that Severian holds all past Autarchs' memories, arguing the 'first Severian' on page 270 was replaced and does not count. Chris offered two opposed readings: first that Severian is himself a subsumed Autarch whose degraded memories cause déjà vu, then that he was autarch more than once, citing a childhood tomb he later learns is his own. The thread ends in unresolved disagreement with no consensus reached.
  • mournings glory: library kept for Sulpicius's return, memories channel back through successors
  • Seth Lombardi: Autarch may carry more subsumed personalities than Autarchs; Ymar may have requested many minds
  • Roy C. Lackey: the 'first Severian' was replaced and does not count as a prior autarch
  • Chris: Severian is himself a subsumed Autarch whose memories are degraded copies, later revised to say he was autarch more than once

Themes: Memory and forgetting, Time loops and causality, Names and their meanings

2004-10-04 mournings glory #

Proposes that Sulpicius ordered the library room kept for his return and that his memories, passed to successors, could channel back into it, citing Thecla's dominance over Severian's body and narrative. Builds on Jeff Wilson's suggestion about the order and the memory-transfer, adding that Severian's visit to the library foreshadows both Sulpicius's return and Severian's rise to the Phoenix Throne.
supports · myth-and-structure · close-textual-reading · theories: Sulpicius channels himself through his successor to return to the library; Severian's library visit foreshadows Sulpicius's return

<Could it be that Sulpicius ordered that the room be kept for him until he came back, as long as necessary, and they have simply followed the order, since no other Autarch has bothered to withdraw it? Or perhaps it foreshadows the memory-transfer, since Sulpicius live on in his successors?>

Agree, but with some modification.

Perhaps Sulpicius, knowing what lays ahead of him (he will be eaten by his successor, though his memories will survive), has merely sought to ensure that one of the places he likes most in the world will survive and that he, via his new host, will still be able to visit it and savor his favorite books post-corporally. Thecla, after all, "returns" to the House Absolute and at times her presence is so dominant she takes over the narrative as well as Severian's body language (iirc he's mistaken for a woman a number of times). Sulpicius may thus be hoping he'll be able to return to the library and even be recognized as Sulpicius if he can channel himself through strongly enough. Ultan's riff about how this is not yet happened may speak to the ill-fated poignancy of this notion. Nevertheless, his 300-year-old-edict remains still operational.

There's also a nice bit of irony here: Severian, as Sulpicius's future host, *has* come to the library, foreshadowing both Sulpicius's return and Severian's own rise to the Phoenix Throne. Plus the entire notion of the Autarchy as a huge collective volume of personalities, with each chapter representing a different Autarch's life.

2004-10-05 Roy C. Lackey replying to mournings glory #

Asks which earlier autarch Severian was, and disputes the claim that Severian carries memories of all past autarchs. Argues that the 'first Severian' scenario on page 270 of The Citadel of the Autarch does not count, because that Severian was replaced and the present Severian lacks his memories of becoming autarch.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: The first Severian was replaced by our Severian; Severian does not contain memories of becoming autarch

Okay, I'll bite. Which of the previous autarchs was Severian, and when?

If you're referring to the "first Severian" scenario outlined by Sev on p.270 of CITADEL, that doesn't count. That Severian was replaced at some point by our Sev, and history was rewritten, so to speak. Sev _did not_ contain the memory of that "first Severian" becoming autarch and receiving the memories of Appian and the rest.

-Roy

2004-10-05 Chris P replying to mournings glory #

Argues that Severian himself is among the subsumed Autarchs, so his memories reach him as degraded copies of copies, which explains his sense of déjà vu and presentiments. Grounds this in the alzabo's deeper effect on Severian's memory and the drowning scene at the story's opening, and extends it to suggest his memory was never perfect.
proposes · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Severian is one of the subsumed Autarchs; Severian's memory was never perfect; Appian's memories reach Severian as copies of copies

There's a lot more to be troubled about in this. Because at least one of the previous Autarchs bouncing around in there is Severian himself, who at that point would have already been through all of the events described in the New Sun and also have (within his own memories) the echoes of all the other autarchs.

This is not necessarily a complete snake-eating-its-tale scenario, however. We know that Severian's memory causes him to get a deeper effect from the alzabo. My guess is that everyone else, having a normal human memory, would not receive or transmit a precise copy of the memories picked up from their predecessor. As a result, what ends up coming to Severian by way of Appian is a copy of a copy of a copy of a copy... what he perceives of the traces of his own memory in this is faded and confusing; events which he knows have already happened take on the appearance of having been through them more than once (case in point is the scene at the beginning where he almost drowns, and his later reflections on it), and events which haven't happened yet seem to him to be presentiments of what's to come.

Another possibility that has occurred to me is that perhaps his memory has never been perfect at all. He has perhaps been given the illusion of perfect memory by having received an infinite number of very-faded copies. For this time travelling thing involves an infinite regress - however many copies of his own memories he receives from Appian, he ends up transmitting that number plus one to one of his predecessors earlier on in the timeline. An infinite number of imperfect copies laid on top of each other may appear to have perfect clarity, and yet contain errors...

Just dabbling with ideas a bit,
Civet

2004-10-05 Chris P replying to mournings glory #

Argues that Severian was autarch more than once, citing the tomb Severian plays in as a child, which he later learns is his own tomb as an autarch. Responds to mournings glory and Roy's point that the 'first Severian' was replaced, suggesting Severian only conjectured an earlier Severian from his echoed memories, and rejects theories that Ymar was Severian or his clone.
disputes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian was autarch more than once; Severian conjectured an earlier Severian from echoed memories; Ymar was Severian

Well, a couple things. The first is the tomb that Severian plays in as a child, which he later finds out is his own (from one of his many lives as the Conciliator in and out of time). I believe that somewhere it's stated that this is the tomb of an autarch. Secondly, I think Severian himself makes the conjecture that he's been autarch more than once, in UotNS, though I don't have a copy to go looking.

Not to be confused with whoever's theory it was that Ymar was actually Severian, which I never really bought into (or the theory that he was Severian's clone).

The only place that the passing from Citadel you're referring to comes into play is that I think that perhaps Severian comes up with the *idea* of there having been another Severian from the fact that he had these echoed memories, and had no way at hand to explain them otherwise.
