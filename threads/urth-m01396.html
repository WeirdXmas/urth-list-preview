2005-03-22 Tony Ellis #

Argues that a utilitarian justification of mass killing by counting future generations applies equally to Ozymandias in Watchmen and to Severian's slaughter in the New Sun. Responds to Crush's quoted claim by pointing out that the same arithmetic could justify any slaughter and that the ratio of saved to slain matters when comparing the two. The post is an off-topic comparison of a comic and Wolfe's novel.
disputes

If what counts is the number of descendants the survivors of a mass slaughter might one day have, then mass slaughter on any scale, no matter how horrifically vast, can always be justified as the killing of the few. We tend to measure these things in terms of actual people killed.

And what about all the generations who *will* be born on Urth if the New Sun doesn't come? Not to mention all those born on all the other worlds to which humanity is transplanted after Urth dies. Don't they count?

Both Ozymandias' and Severian's mass-murders of innocents will in theory ensure the preservation of future generations. But if you're going to differentiate between their achievements - especially in the same breath as you use the word 'fascist' - then the ratio of those saved to those slaughtered seems significant.

2005-03-22 James Wynn replying to Tony Ellis #

Argues that unborn generations cannot be weighed against the real deaths Severian's plan causes, since the Hierodules have actually lived those numbers, and that humanity has few generations left without the New Sun. Responds to Tony Ellis's claim that unborn descendants justify mass slaughter, noting that transplanting humanity to the stars is no simple task, as The Short Sun shows.
disputes · theology-and-religion · theories: Severian's mass killing is justified by the New Sun; Severian's choices outweigh Ozymandias's plan; Humanity has few generations without the New Sun

Hm. You are aware that for the Hierodules those unborn numbers have actually lived, right? It is not supposition for them as it would be for you and me and Hitler and Stalin and Ozymandias. There is simply no comparison.

Who's to say humanity would have been transplanted? There is only one ship off the planet and no one else has interstellar capabilities. As The Short Sun shows, transplanting humanity into the stars is no simple task. I'd say many fewer generations are available to humanity without the New Sun.

All fascists are not mass murderers. In fact, the fascists are probably not among the top 2 or 3 mass murderers. So I don't know why the use of the term fascist in this case is anymore jarring than using more mundane terms like "socialist", "atheist", or "collectivist". But since you believe the ratios are significant, I hope I have shown that Severian does okay against Ozymandias...especially since Ozy could not be assured that his plan would save a single life while Severian could know the benefits of his actions and the costs of his inactions into the distant mists of Time.

~ Crush
