Themes: Gods, angels and the Increate

1999-03-18 Alice Turner #

Argues that Wolfe deliberately invokes angels in "Tracking Song" by equating Oz, Sugarland and Heaven, and suggests that Little Tib will soon die. Responds to William's objection that Nitty is a real person by calling Nitty an angel too, and cites the Shaggy Man and Dorothy living in Oz forever.
disputes · theology-and-religion · source-and-allusion · theories: Wolfe invokes angels in Tracking Song; Oz equals Sugarland and Heaven; Little Tib will soon die; Nitty is an angel

William,

I think you've done a great job, and that you should polish it up and publish it somewhere. About the wings: This is a peculiar thing that Wolfe does sometimes---look at the very end of "Tracking Song." Through it makes me squirm, I think that he really is invoking angels in both places. (I don't mind when they're like Tzadkiel, now why is that?) Or, to put it another way, Oz = Sugarland = The Big Rock Candy Mountain = The Great Good Place = Heaven. They certainly aren't the Gump's palm-frond wings! Though I feel cheered at the suggestion!

This suggests that Little Tib is not long for this world, and I feel that this is true. Your argument against this view is that Nitty is a real person. Yes, but he is a sort of angel too, and my own feeling is that both of them will be beyond the rainbow soon. And remember that the Shaggy Man and Dorothy and Betsy and Trot and a few other earth people do live in Oz, probably forever, since one is immortal there. I don't think Baum was necessarily a Christian, but Wolfe is, and the conclusion is obvious.

Gosh, if I thought I had a chance of going to Oz when I died, I'd try to be a better person....

-alga
