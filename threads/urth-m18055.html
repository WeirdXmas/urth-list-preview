2011-01-16 Lee Berman #

Argues that the cloisonne locket of Dorcas in Ouen's possession was not a self-portrait by Dorcas's kin but was pawned by the Boatman, citing Ouen's own account of paying the pledge and the Boatman's indifference to possessions. Responds to Jeff Wilson's inference that another piece must exist, and suggests the portrait may be by the artist Fechin from a Wolfe story.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: The Boatman pawned Dorcas's locket and Ouen redeemed it; Fechin may have painted the locket portrait

Hm..interesting. Self-portrait. Dorcas is artistic, as we know from her role in set design with Dr. Talos' troupe. Still, I think there might be an even better artist than her in the family.

With regard to the locket with Dorcas' likeness, we actually do know quite a bit about what happened to it. The Boatman is quite the opposite from obsessive about keeping material things as he tells Severian in describing the loft he currently sleeps in:

When we hear from Ouen on the subject:

"My father had a locket with her likeness. Once when I was twenty or so I came to see him and found out he'd pledged it. I'd come into a bit of money.....and I went to the pawnbroker's and paid the pledge and took it. I still wear it, sieur."

There is a BotNS story about a famous artist who draws likenesses of the people he cares about and who has trouble hanging on to material possessions. The artist is quite a charismatic ladies man as well as being a trickster and a liar. Perhaps it was (monkey- armed) Fechin who drew that portrait.

2011-01-16 Jeff Wilson replying to Lee Berman #

Concedes a point about a single cloisonne portrait and argues that a commissioned likeness by a known artist would cost more than a pawnbroker's modest loan. Notes that Ouen's father may be misremembering the artist's name, suggesting Rudesinde rather than Fechin, based on the description of the boy artist in the story. Responds to Lee Berman's quotation from Ouen about the locket.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Rudesinde, not Fechin, is the boy artist

Whoops! I guess there's only one cloisonne. Still, if a cloisonne of a person of no particular interest to the pawnbroker is worth "a bit of money", having one of done of a specific person could be a considerable expense, especially if the image was supplied by a known artist like Fechin. (Though I think Casdoe's dad is misrembering Rudesinde as "Fechin" when he talks about the scampy, long-limbed boy artist.)
