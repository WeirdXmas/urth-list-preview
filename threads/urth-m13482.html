Fortunate Cloud

The thread: Readers asked whether the name Fortunate Cloud, the ship in Jonas's story, sounds Korean or Chinese and what it signals about naming in Jonas's time. John Watkins explained that Kim is a common Korean surname homonymous with the word for gold, and questioned how many Korean names lack Chinese cognates. Gerry Quinn argued the name sounded more Chinese than Korean, possibly misheard, and linked it to Jonas's remark about modern Chinese-like names. Mr Thalassocrat proposed the lucky cloud as a traditional Chinese image, backed only by a blog link about Beijing Olympics design. The thread ended with the Chinese reading gaining support, though with little textual argument.
  • John Watkins: Kim is Korean and homonymous with gold, so Korean-Chinese naming overlaps
  • Gerry Quinn: the name sounds Chinese rather than Korean and may be a mishearing of JONAS
  • Mr Thalassocrat: the lucky cloud is a traditional Chinese image, as seen at the Beijing Olympics
  • Antonio Pedro Marques: questioned the Korean reading of the name

2010-07-12 John Watkins replying to António Pedro Marques #

Explains that Kim is the most common Korean family name and that it is homonymous with the Korean word for gold, noting its Chinese counterpart Jin. Responds to Antonio Pedro Marques's remark that he does not know Korean, offering linguistic background on a name in Fortunate Cloud, while questioning how many Korean names lack Chinese cognates.
informs · source-and-allusion

"Kim" is the most common family name in Korea. If one wanted to pick an iconic Korean name, it would be "Kim," with "Park" as the probable runner-up. It also means "gold," or rather is homonymous with the Korean word for "gold."
Now, "Kim" is also a Chinese name, but the Chinese name is sometimes Anglicized "Jin." I'm not sure how many Korean names there are that have no Chinese equivalent or cognate.
