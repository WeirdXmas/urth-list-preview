The thread: Matthew Keeley proposed that many Castleview characters map onto Arthurian figures, including Viviane, Dr. von Madadh, and the Excalibur scabbard. Michael Straight answered that everyone of European ancestry descends from Arthur through a small ancient gene pool, an idea he attributed to Wolfe. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes disputed the Excalibur cross as ordinary sword design and offered Odin's eye-for-wisdom trade as a counterpoint to the Green Man idea. Keeley accepted the bloodline idea and later linked Odin's eye trade to Silk and Pig and his ravens to the Long Sun choughs. Mo Holkar separately argued that Rex von Madadh is Sally's dead dog or a fairy, based on Gaelic word meanings. The thread ended without resolution and drifted into Odin parallels.

  • Michael Straight: all European-descended characters descend from Arthur, so they each enact Arthurian roles
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: Excalibur's cross shape is ordinary sword design, and Odin's eye trade counters the Green Man idea
  • Mo Holkar: Rex von Madadh embodies Sally's dead dog Rexy or is a fairy posing as one

2007-06-19 Michael Straight replying to Matthew Keeley #

Argues that in Castleview everyone of European ancestry descends from Arthur, so the characters Wrangler, Will Shields and others each enact pieces of the Arthurian role. Grounds this in an idea the poster attributes to Wolfe about a small ancient gene pool and generations of mixing, and presents it as an answer to Matthew Keeley's observation about the many Arthurian figures.
answers · myth-and-structure · author-and-biography · theories: Everyone human in Castleview is a true descendant of Arthur

You may have seen this in the archives, but one of the ideas in the book (I believe this is straight from Wolfe), is that the gene pool in Arthur's time was so small and there's been enough generations of mixing so that today, almost anyone with European ancestors is a descendant of Arthur. So while the faerie folk seem be looking for the "true" descendant of Arthur (it's not clear whether they realize this or not), in fact, everyone human in the story is equally a true descendant of Arthur. And so several of the characters end up acting out various pieces of that role.

Rostrum

2007-06-19 Matthew Keeley #

Argues that many Castleview characters map onto Arthurian figures: Viviane as Lady of the Lake and Morgan, Dr. von Madadh as a Merlin-Mordred hybrid, and the Excalibur scabbard as a circle closing the myth. Supports these with Malory passages, the epigraph, and the Green Knight and Odin references tied to The Wizard Knight.
proposes · source-and-allusion · allegory-and-typology · theories: Viviane combines several Arthurian women; Dr. von Madadh as Merlin-Mordred hybrid; Scabbard retains healing powers on the boat to Avalon; Will Shields as Arthur's lupine survival

Hi,

I just finished Castleview last night for the first time, and though I know there's been a lot said on this particular book recently, I hope I've got something to add to the conversation. (Note: In addition to Castleview spoilers, there's a spoiler for The Wizard Knight).

1.There seems to be a lot of discussion about whether Wrangler / Arthur Dunstan or Will Shields is the "real" Arthur; this got me thinking about just how many characters in Castleview seem to represent either part of an Arthurian figure or several Arthurian figures.

a. Viviane Morgan seems to be the Lady of the Lake, Morgan le Fay, Morgause (in Malory I believe it's Morgause, not Morgan, who sleeps with Arthur), and Vivian / Nimue, the enchantress who seals Merlin under a hill or tree.

b. Dr. von Madadh kills Shields at the end of the novel, suggesting an affinity with Mordred, but throughout much of the novel he seems to be a Merlin-figure; extremely knowledgeable constantly vanishing and reappearing (plus, he has a beard, though I think that's pushing it).The comment at the end of the book that he was "the best of them" also seems suggestive to me. Earlier in the novel, Fee tells us that God dislikes his group, of which we know the Doctor is a part. In those stories, then, Merlin would be the best of an evil brood. Fee's comment suggested devils to me, and in some stories, Merlin is in fact the devil's son. The fact that the doctor's first name is "Rex" suggests a connection with Mordred to me, as Mordred surely wanted to be the king (I believe he's even crowned in some versions of the Arthurian legend?). Anyone know the etymology of "Madadh?"

2. I wonder if there's a Jack Vance influence to this story? Vance had published at least a few of his Lyonesse books before Wolfe published Castleview, and I wonder if Wolfe's references to Lyonesse in the text constituted another one of his homages to Vance.

3. Some of the previous posts on the ending of Castleview suggest that Excalibur's scabbard will be useless to Will because it only protects against blood loss. I've always taken the epigraph a little less literally; I tend to think the scabbard would still have healing powers; otherwise why would it be on the boat to Avalon? I actually took that piece of the ending as a bit of lupine brilliance; it suggests how Arthur / Will might somehow survive AND it ties up a loose end of the Arthurian story, and in fact creates a very nice "circle" to the myth (Fairly early in Malory, Morgan le Fay steals the scabbard from Arthur, after that, we never hear of it again). I thought that the scabbard's presence in the boat was one of the most beautiful of Wolfe's inventions.

4. What are we to make of Excalibur's resemblance to a cross (Mercedes at first thinks it's a jeweled cross)? How does that fit in with the pagan aspects of the ending? Furthermore, upon what leather-bound book is the tip of the sword resting? Malory? The Bible? The missing diary of the Wrangler's ancestor?

5. Also about the ending: Wolfe really seems to like the legend of the Green Man or Green Knight; in The Wizard Knight Able tells us that he WAS the Green Knight who Gawain decapitated. Maybe Wolfe is trying to take an essentially pagan symbol and Christianize it, as it is his wont to do? The Green Man could perhaps be read as a Christ figure, though so presumably could the king who slays him, as he too will die and rise again. A little earlier in the text, Phyllis Sun sees Odin leading the Wild Hunt down the streets of Castleview; the narrator takes pains to describe his missing eye, which, if I recall correctly, he gave up so that he could give knowledge to humanity; a Christian take on Odin seems like something Wolfe would enjoy.

Any thoughts?

-Matt

2007-06-19 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes replying to Matthew Keeley #

Argues that Excalibur's cross-like shape comes from ordinary sword design, since a sword guard often looks like a cross and knights once planted swords in the ground to pray. Offers Odin's trade of his eye to Mimir for wisdom as a counterpoint to the Green Man idea, noting Odin wanted wisdom to avert Ragnarok rather than to give it away.
disputes · myth-and-structure · source-and-allusion · theories: Excalibur's cross shape is ordinary sword design; Wolfe Christianizes the pagan Green Man

The guard of a sword is often shaped like that, and knights (at least in legends) would often stab the sword into the ground to get a cross to pray by.

I don't know much about the Green Man, but Odin gives his eye to Mimir in payment for wisdom, not so he can give his wisdom away. He is looking for a way to avert Ragnarok.

2007-06-20 Mo Holkar / UKG replying to Matthew Keeley #

Argues that Rex von Madadh embodies the spirit of Sally's dead dog Rexy or is a fairy posing as it, based on the Gaelic meaning of "madadh" as "dog". Supports this with the scene where he appears muddy-footed at the door, his card, his claim to work for the Daoine Institute, and his red-gold beard linked to the Gaelic word for fox.
proposes · source-and-allusion · myth-and-structure · theories: Rex von Madadh is Rexy's spirit or a fairy disguise; Von Madadh's red-gold beard links him to Dr Talos as fox

"Madadh" is Gaelic for "dog". So Rex von Madadh ~= king of the dogs.

Von Madadh must in some sense embody the spirit of Sally's dead dog Rexy, or is a fay posing as it. Rexy was run over the previous year, but reappears during the night. When Sally calls for Rexy, von Madadh appears at the door, with muddy feet, and hands her a card the colour of bone. He claims to work for the Daoine Institute -- the daoine sidhe are the good fairies in Celtic myth.

On another note, von Madadh's beard is described as red-gold, and a fox in Gaelic is just called "madadh ruadh", "red dog" -- shades of the foxy Dr Talos?

Mo
