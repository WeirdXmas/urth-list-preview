Lackey and Maclean Jessica argued that Severian's resurrections recreate the dead in his own mind, so figures like Zama and the uhlan lack self-knowledge, with Lackey supporting this through Dorcas's memory recovery and Maclean adding the Miles-and-Jonas parallel. Maclean also floated a speculative reading of the Bible's book order and Wolfe's mixed myths as versions of one event. Michael Dale then proposed that the cyborg Jonas absorbed biological parts from the dead crewmate Miles, and tied Jonas's behaviour to Severian's interactions with Miles and to resurrection shock. The thread ends with the theories standing side by side rather than any agreement.

  • Michael Dale: Jonas absorbed parts of the dead Miles after a ship crash; confusion stems from resurrection shock

Themes: Death and resurrection, Memory and forgetting, Robots, androids and artificial life

Argues that when Severian resurrects the dead, he recreates them in his own mind, so revived figures like Zama and the uhlan lack self-knowledge because he knows nothing of them. Supports this with Severian's own post-avern confusion, Dorcas's gradual memory recovery, and the unconscious revival of Dorcas and Triskele, noting a prior argument about the assassin who killed Valeria.

2007-04-23 Maclean Jessica #

Proposes that Severian's resurrections recreate the dead person in his own mind, so Zama and the uhlan know little of themselves while Miles carries Jonas in. Also argues that the title "Eschatology and Genesis" in Talos's play suggests the Bible might run Revelation before Genesis in a cycle, and that Wolfe's mixed myths may be versions of one event. Grounds these in the resurrection scenes and the Bible's order of books.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · myth-and-structure · theories: Severian's resurrections recreate the dead person in his mind; Jonas hitched a ride inside Miles; Eschatology and Genesis suggests Revelation precedes Genesis in a cycle; Wolfe's myths are versions of one event

Hello all

As a new member, I apologise if this subject is old hat- I did trawl through the archives but couldn't find a reference. I was thinking about Severian resurrecting the uhlan, Zama, and Miles. It seems to me that when Severian raises the dead he is recreating the person in his mind somehow- therefore when he resurrects Zama and the uhlan, because he doesn't know anything about them, they do not appear to know much about themselves.

When Sev raises Miles, he had Jonas in his mind at the time... which I think allowed Jonas to hitch a ride, as it were.

Also, regarding Dr Talos's play "Eschatology and Genesis" - the title is a direct reference to the Bible, which starts with Genesis and ends in Revelations (which is a form of eschatology). To me it suggests that the Bible itself could be started with Revelations (the eschatology) first, then on to Genesis and the start of the next cycle, which is an interesting take. Of course Wolfe is too clever to merely incorporate one "world's beginning" tradition hence the incorporation of the Sumerian and Persian elements. I wonder if this is Wolfe's way of saying that all those legends, myths etc are different versions of the same event...

Thoughts, comments? :-)

2007-05-03 Michael Dale replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Proposes that the cyborg Jonas, after a ship crash, took biological parts from the dead crewmate Miles, so Jonas absorbed part of Miles rather than the reverse. Responds to Roy C. Lackey's discussion of Severian's resurrections of Zama and the uhlan, suggesting Severian's interactions with Miles may shape Jonas's behaviour and that the confusion stems from resurrection shock.
proposes · puzzle-solving · theories: Jonas absorbed part of Miles after crash; Miles recruited to a spacefaring ship; Severian's interactions with Miles affect Jonas; Confusion in revived people is resurrection shock

As for Miles and Jonas...

The way I see it is that Miles in the near future is recruited to work on a space faring ship... one that might be a bit framilar as in tBotNS, if not that ship itself...

Something happens to this ship in the distant future/past while Miles is on it and it crashes and/or malfunctions forcing the Cyborg Jonas to aquire biological parts until a more sutable replacement is avaliable. Miles a dead crewmate just so happened to be avaliable.

Thus... I do think Jonas took on a part of Miles when he did this... not the other way around...

Perhaps the interactions Severain makes with Miles... actually effects Jonas's behavior...

Otherwise the rest of the confussion is due to ressurection shock as stated before.

-Michael
