Themes: Gods, angels and the Increate

1997-02-25 Alice Turner #

Disputes Alice Turner's claim that Viron's godlessness explains why Kypris and Tartaros chose it, arguing that Trivigaunte also lacks theophanies. Cites the Trivigaunte Fliers' responses and a feminist heresy there, and proposes that Pas and his company were meant to be gods of the whole Whorl.
disputes · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: Trivigaunte also lacks theophanies, so Viron is not special; Pas and company were meant to be gods of the Whorl

Heh, heh. I hate There Are Doors too, Patrick.

Kipling's Jungle Book is one of the right-out-front sources for the Brown Book in NS, but I can't see much of a connection to LS, and I know (or used to know) my Kipling pretty well. The story mantis mentions is, I think "The City of Endless Night" (not at home to check). "Baa, Baa Black Sheep" is, I understand, bleakly autobiographical. It's a fine, wrenching, realistic story, but how does it relate to Wolfe's work?

Dave, they talk to her, but she doesn't talk back. Sort of like Owen Glendower summoning spirits from the vasty deep in Henry IV. I think the gods were turned off everywhere till Mamelta turned Echidna, Scylla and Tartaros back on. 'Cept for cunning Kypris, who hid. And Pas, who is dead.

Nice rundown, Henry, with especially good observations on Oreb.

I remember that Heinlein two-headed character, Joe-Jim or something like that---he played chess with himself. He wasn't a tyrant...but your title is too good to resist!

Er, um, I don't think this argument will wash. The only other city we actually know of, Trivigaunte, doesn't get theophanies either. And, judging from the Fliers' responses, the Trivs are operating under a surprising (to the Fliers) feminist heresy by chucking out the other gods. I think Pas & Co. were originally programmed to be the gods of the whole Whorl. And that Trivigaunte gets away with it precisely because the gods have been deactivated for more than a generation.

-alga-
