Themes: Love and loss, Myth and folklore sources

2003-04-29 Che Monro #

Quotes Robert Graves's passage from The White Goddess on the poet's love for Cerridwen, whose love comes at the price of life, and says it is strikingly similar to the themes of Wolfe's There Are Doors. Offers the parallel without developing an argument or citing any scene from the novel.
informs · myth-and-structure

...For [the poet] there is no other woman but Cerridwen and he desires=20 one thing above all else in the world: her love. As Blodeuwedd, she will=20 gladly give him her love, but at only one price: his life. She will exact=20 payment punctually and bloodily. Other women, other goddesses, are=20 kinder-seeming. They sell their love at a reasonable rate--sometimes a man= =20
may even have it for the asking. But not Cerridwen: for with her love goes= =20
wisdom.... (Robert Graves)

Startlingly similar to the themes of There Are Doors, of course...
