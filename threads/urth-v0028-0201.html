Aw Hell (Giants)

Urth list · 4 messages · 1999-10-15 to 1999-10-17

The thread: The thread began as a debate over whether giants in water, like Abaia, could breathe under square-cube limits on lung surface area. Fr.Inire argued evolved giants would gain more alveoli to absorb oxygen, while Jim Henley asked how large a giant could grow before lungs fail and recalled a Ben Bova Boys' Life column about a giant crab becoming a T. rex. Jon Camfield argued giant undines are physically possible, citing whales and dinosaurs, and suggested alternative respiration such as smaller alveoli, larger lungs, or gills. Much of the exchange drifted into off-topic chat about the Bova column and its author, and the thread ended without resolution.
  • Fr.Inire: square-cube lung objection is answered by evolved giants gaining more alveoli
  • Jim Henley: asks the size limit where lung surface can no longer supply oxygen
  • Ron Hale-Evans: recalls the column scaled up an insect, not a crab
  • Jon Camfield: giant undines are possible; suggests smaller alveoli, larger lungs, or gills

1999-10-15 Jim Henley #

Asks how large a giant could grow before lung surface area can no longer supply oxygen to the enlarged body, as a follow-up to a square-cube law discussion. Recalls a Ben Bova Boys' Life column where designing a giant crab under the square-cube law ended up producing a Tyrannosaurus Rex.
questions

I was talking giants and the Square-Cube Law last night with my Amber (RPG) group and offered the oh-so-clever observation that one could get around it by living, giant-sized in the water, oh yes I did. But one of my pals said the real problem with the SCL is in the lungs - you pass a point where the surface area of your enlarged lungs can't absorb enough oxygen for all the cells in your enlarged body.

Well darn! But I wonder; we didn't get into the math - I mean, Bill's from art school - but can anyone put a number on the size where lung limitations come into play?

I can't help remembering the old Ben Bova science columns that ran in Boy's Life back in my scouting days. (This was more than, oh, ten years ago. And I do mean "more.") One month, by way of teaching the SCL, he set out to design a giant crab. By the end of the article, after all the compromises and efficiencies were accounted for, the "giant crab" turned out to be - Tyrannosaurous Rex!

Best,

Jim

1999-10-17 Jon Camfield #

Argues that giant undines like Abaia are physically possible despite the square-cube limits on oxygen uptake, citing whales and dinosaurs as proof that large animals exist. Responds to a quoted objection about lung capacity by suggesting alternative respiratory methods, such as smaller alveoli or larger lungs or gills, and asks whether Abaia breathes at all.
disputes · genre-and-craft

The problem is that the ability to get oxygen from the air (or the water, for that matter) is based on amount surface, so it scales much slower than volume (volume scales in cubes, surface area in squares).

OTOH, we have seen giants such as the sperm whale and the brontosaurus rex alive and well on earth--so the undines are certainly within reach size-wize (I'd give them T. Rex-level proportions from what I remember--anyone have better estimates?).

And, who said Abaia breathes, in the first place?

Also, as Ron pointed out, it may be entirely possible that some other method has evolved--smaller alevoli (diminishing returns as the space between cells approaches the size of O2), bigger, more expansive lungs/gills, etc. etc. etc.
