The thread: Fred Kiesche opened the thread by noting parallels between Wolfe's Book of the New Sun and Jack Vance's The Dying Earth and M. John Harrison's Viriconium, all from Pocket's Timescape line, offering no detailed evidence. Idoru X supported the comparison by arguing the tetralogy began as Wolfe's own Dying Earth-style story, citing Wolfe's stated excitement over Vance's book. Kiesche then asked for essays or interviews where Wolfe discusses those origins, mentioning rereading "Castle of the Otter" as a starting point. The thread stayed a short, friendly request for sources with no resolution yet.

2006-10-21 Fred Kiesche #

Observes that re-reading The Book of the New Sun reveals parallels with Jack Vance's The Dying Earth and M. John Harrison's Viriconium tales, noting all three appeared from Pocket's Timescape line around the same time. Offers no detailed evidence, only the shared publisher and the recent trade paperback reissues as context.
proposes · source-and-allusion

Greetings:

In re-reading the tetralogy after (mumble mumble)
years, I was struck by something I did not notice when
I first read them: The parallels with "The Dying
Earth" by Jack Vance and the "Viriconium" tales fo M.
John Harrison.

Even odder, given that all three were published by
Pocket in the Timescape line around the same time. And
now all three are available as trade paperbacks!

2006-10-21 Los Arboles Del Frio replying to Fred Kiesche #

Argues that Wolfe's Book of the New Sun tetralogy began as his own attempt at a Dying Earth story, supporting Fred Kiesche's observed parallels with Jack Vance. Grounds the claim in Wolfe's own statements about how excited he was by Vance's The Dying Earth, adding context to the comparison.
supports · author-and-biography · theories: Book of the New Sun began as a Dying Earth

Wolfe has talked plenty of times about how excited he felt by The Dying Earth. The Tetralogy started as his attempt to write his own version of a Dying Earth.
