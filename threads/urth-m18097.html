The thread: Readers asked why Quetzal's plan in The Fifth Head of Cerberus and Return to the Whorl ends as it does, and whether Jahlee's killing and the mortality of inhumi and angels should be read literally. James Wynn argued Quetzal would simply hope to survive long enough to launch the ship, challenging Andrew Mason's view that Quetzal miscalculated the effect on Green's inhumi society. Mason argued Quetzal wanted a thriving human colony on Green and that Wolfe's Short Sun remark points beyond the food-source explanation, working through possibilities such as misjudging Remora. Lee Berman connected the posters' ideas toward the core of the mystery and raised the remora image and the question of whether spiritual beings can die. The thread ended with several open possibilities rather than agreement.
  • Lee Berman: Jahlee's death and angel mortality may be meant unrealistically, and the posters' combined ideas approach the core mystery
  • James Wynn: Quetzal would simply hope to survive long enough to launch the ship, so miscalculation is an unlikely motive
  • Andrew Mason: Quetzal wanted a thriving human colony on Green, and Short Sun hints at more than the food-source reason
  • Antonio Pedro Marques: Questioned how solidary the inhumi are and whether Quetzal could rely on Remora

Themes: Colonization and the native, Robots, androids and artificial life, Power, rulers and politics

2011-01-16 Lee Berman #

Questions whether the killing of Jahlee by Horn at the end of Return to the Whorl is meant to be taken literally, and suggests that the mortality of inhumi, shadow children and angels is unreal. Refers to the angel in The Book of the New Sun who bleeds to death and surprises her kinsman, and asks whether spiritual beings can die a physical death.
questions · close-textual-reading · theories: Death of inhumi and shadow children is not literal

At the end of RttW we are supposed to believe that Horn killed Jahlee for feasting on Nettle (iirc) simply with a number of kicks. Or are we really supposed to believe that?

For me, questioning the reality of our sort of death for such creatures as inhumi and shadow children/abos clarifies a lot mystery. Thus for demons. And for angels? In BotNS there is a story about an angel bleeding to death who surprises her kinsman who didn't think it possible.

Is it?

(i.e. can spiritual beings die a physical death?)

2011-01-17 Andrew Mason replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that Quetzal wanted a thriving human colony on Green, and that Wolfe's remark that Short Sun explains why suggests more than the food-source reason given in the Long Sun 'Defense'. Works through possibilities such as Quetzal misjudging Remora or the monitor, or not expecting to revert to his natural appearance, and asks what inhumi society was like before humans arrived.
proposes · author-and-biography · close-textual-reading · theories: Quetzal miscalculated the impact on Green

Wolfe has said that Quetzal wanted a thriving human colony on Green. So that was certainly his preferred destination. (Interestingly, he also said we would have to read _Short Sun_ to find out why. One might think that the 'Defense' at the end of _Long Sun_ tells us enough - that he wanted a food source for his people; this may suggest there is more to it than that.)

How could he ever think the people would follow his

Well, as you say, people (Remora) did. Presumably he knew Remora well enough to bank on this. He may not have taken the monitor into account. Alternatively, perhaps he didn't know he would revert to his natural apperarance when he died.

That's possible, and indeed Wolfe's words may suggest it, depending how strongly you interpret 'thriving'. Do we know what inhumi society on Green was like before the humans arrived there? Quetzal may have miscalculated what the impact would be.
