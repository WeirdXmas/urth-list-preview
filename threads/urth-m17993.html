Themes: Fathers, mothers and children, Hidden identities and parentage

2011-01-14 Andrew Mason #

Argues that the absence of children in Silk's and Severian's relationships in Wolfe's books is a deliberate pattern, not just Valeria's infertility. Cites Silk's children in Gaon, the 'tall woman' with Apu-Punchau, Severian's mention of 'my descendants', the basket and Dorcas scene, and suggests Severian may have fathered a child with Burgundofara.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Severian had a child with Burgundofara; Severian's descendants include much of the Commonwealth

If it were just Valeria, one could just say that Valeria was unable to have children. (Silk had no children in twenty years with Hyacinth, although he - or his body - produced at least two children in Gaon shortly after her death.)

However, it isn't just Valeria. He had no children with the other woman he lived with over a long period of time, 'the tall woman', Apu-Punchau's wife/partner. (I take it she was that, since when he moved into the shaman's house, she moved with him.) At least I assume he didn't; with Valeria the lack of children is specifically mentioned; with the tall woman we have to assume that if there were any he would mention them, though as this is Severian you can never tell.

You might just suppose Severian is unable to have children - but he refers near the end of UOTNS to 'my descendants'. The possibility that he had a child with Dorcas seems to be offered and then torn away from us. (When he sees her near the end of COTA she has a basket, and there are repeated references to babies in baskets. But when, a few chapters later, we find out how long he has been away, it becomes clear that if she has a child it can't possibly have been born yet.)

I think there is a considerable amount of evidence that he may have had a child with Burgundofara - from whom he and a large part of the population of the Commonwealth are no doubt descended.

2011-01-16 Jeff Wilson replying to Andrew Mason #

Argues that Severian and Valeria have no children not because Valeria is infertile but because one or more of Severian, Valeria, or Inire vetoed it, given the technology available. Responds to Andrew Mason's quoted reply to Lee Berman, which suggested Valeria simply could not have children.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian and Valeria's childlessness was a deliberate veto

She does come from a dwindling family, but given the technology demonstrably available, the only real reason Sev + Valeria don't have children is that one or more of Sev, Valeria, or Inire exercised a veto.
