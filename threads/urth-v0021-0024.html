Jayne's Adduction

Urth list · 1 messages · 1998-10-30 to 1998-10-30

1998urthPaul C Duggan

1998-10-30 Paul C Duggan #

Disputes the claim that illiterate people lack gods-seeing, arguing that self-conscious illiterates such as the Yanomamo see spirits. Asks whether Jaynes's hypothesis needs a biochemical brain shift, and raises Owen Barfield's stages of participation as a possible influence on C.S. Lewis. Grounds this in Ritchie's Spirit of the Rainforest on Yanomamo demons.
disputes · source-and-allusion · theories: Literacy does not explain loss of seeing gods; Jaynes's hypothesis requires biochemical brain shift

I'm not sure what the problem is. There really are alot of illiterates who are self-conscious *and see gods*. The Yanomamo are quite self-consious and yet, Spirit of the Rainforest (Mark Andrew Ritchie) portrays the demons that haunt every nook and cranny of their world.

Doesn't Jayne's hypothesis argue for some biochemical shift in brain physiology (recent evolution) to account for the move towards self-consciouness?

Anybody read any Owen Barfield? He also talks about the move from "Original Participation" (seeing Gods, being one with the universe) to atomism and them back to "Final Participation". I wonder if he was influential on Lewis' view of ancient man's capacities?
