2002-03-17 Andy Robertson #

Argues that Wolfe's blue/green sister-world patterns come from internally generated mythic imperatives, and asks whether The Fifth Head of Cerberus shares a universe with the New Sun books. Replies to Nick Gevers's SF SITE interview, quoting the poster's own question about Ushas/Lune and Urth/Lune and Wolfe's answer, 'I don't know.'
proposes · myth-and-structure · author-and-biography · theories: Wolfe's world-pairing patterns follow internally generated mythic imperatives; The Fifth Head of Cerberus shares a universe with the New Sun books

You have some cheek, Mr Gevers!!

As I thought - Wolfe is obeying internally generated mythic imperatives in creating these patterns. He does not know if Ushas/Lune is Blue/Green, and he can't tell us.

Andy R

[Trying again:] Can your readers usefully view The Fifth Head of Cerberus as being set in the same science-fictional universe as New Sun, Long Sun, and Short Sun? Why does Fifth Head's pattern of blue/green sister worlds recur so tantalisingly in Urth/Lune, Blue/Green?

[Wolfe]
I don't know.

2002-03-18 Nigel Price #

Quotes the SF Site interview in which Wolfe responds to a question about the M. R. James homage in "One-Two-Three For Me", and jokes about the poster's own supposed role in spotting it. Admits someone else first noticed the James homage, and cites a 2000 Urth thread on the subject as a comic aside.
chats · source-and-allusion · theories: One-Two-Three For Me homages M. R. James

From the interview - which I enjoyed very much, though I laughed perhaps more than I learned...

NG: You're very good at parody...Many of the stories in "Strange Travelers" appear to be homages on some level, some obvious...but others more fugitively so (...M R James in "One-Two-Three For Me")...

GW: I'm amazed that you saw M. R. James in "One-Two-Three For Me." You're very perceptive.

[NG: Actually, this was pointed out by somebody else, whose name presently escapes me...]
