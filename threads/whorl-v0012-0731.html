Destroying Inhumi

Whorl list · 6 messages · 2001-04-25 to 2001-04-26

2001whorlThe Book of the Short SunKevin J. MaroneyDan'l Danehy-OakesAdam StephanidesJames Jordan
The thread: Readers debated how the inhumi in The Book of the Long Sun might be destroyed. James Jordan argued for a religious, Eucharistic transformation of the inhumi into true humans, grounded in the two eucharistic scenes and the name 'inhumi' as meaning those not raised in Christ. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes disputed this, arguing that the Eucharist does not work like a blood bank and that the solution must relate to the 'Love One Another' secret, which rules out mass slaughter. Kevin J. Maroney proposed killing all of Green's humans and sentient inhumi so non-sentient inhumi could not cross to Blue, which Adam Stephanides disputed as physically implausible and without motive. The thread ended in impasse, with Jordan defending his religious reading and critics rejecting the violent proposals.
  • James Jordan: destruction means religious transformation of inhumi into true humans via Eucharistic process
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: Eucharist makes sacrifice present in eternal time; solution must fit the 'Love One Another' secret, ruling out mass killing
  • Kevin J. Maroney: kill Green's humans and sentient inhumi so non-sentient inhumi cannot cross to Blue
  • Adam Stephanides: non-sentient inhumi lack motive to fly to Blue given abundant prey on Green

Themes: Suffering and sacrifice, Catholic faith and sacraments, Power, rulers and politics

2001-04-25 James Jordan #

Argues that destroying the inhumi in The Book of the Long Sun means transforming them into true humans through a religious, Eucharistic process rather than a military one. Grounds this in the two eucharistic scenes, the Christian idea of dying to evil and being reborn, and the name 'inhumi' as meaning those not raised in Christ, while answering a earlier suggestion about Christlike blood donation.
proposes · theology-and-religion · allegory-and-typology · theories: destroying the inhumi is a religious rather than military act; transforming inhumi into humans means destroying what makes them inhumi

The Secret, it seems, is a weapon that can destroy the inhumi, but one that may be and likely is too heavy to wield.
I'd like to go back to something someone suggested, which is that when men are good, and thus Christlike, then such good people will voluntarily give blood as needed to help inhumi become good, as Christ did for humans. Practically, such blood could be deposited in blood banks, analogous to Christ's deposit of His blood in the Eucharist.
This has not satisfied because transforming the inhumi does not seem to be a weapon to destroy them. But is this the right understanding? SS is clearly a religious as well as a political and personal novel. Twice we have eucharistic scenes. That's no accident: In Christian thought the Eucharist is eating and drinking Christ's death into oneself, so that one can die to one's own evil and be born anew, over and over again. I suggest that to transform the inhumi into true humans is precisely to destroy inhumi, to destroy what makes them IN-humi.
Pay close attention to the name Wolfe has given them: We humans are usually inhumi, unless we "die" in Christ and are "raised" to a new life. In short, I suspect that destroying the inhumi has a religious meaning, not a military one.
Grist for the mill.

2001-04-26 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues against the claim that the Eucharist deposits blood in a bank, explaining that Catholic sacrament makes the sacrifice present in eternal time, and that Agape-driven humans would sanctify inhumi. Responds to an earlier post's reading of Horn's blood-weapon by proposing the inhumi are 'in-Earth' via the root humus, and argues humanity's lack of love makes the weapon unusable.
disputes · theology-and-religion · source-and-allusion · theories: Inhumi become human in spirit when fed blood; Love one another is the only destroying weapon; Inhumi derives from humus, meaning in-Earth

PatNutRit:

No, no, a _thousand_ times no! Horn says clearly that it will not, that it will reduce them to mindless beasts. (Of course, you might consider that a form of "destruction.") So far, "love each other" is the _only_ suggestion that has any plausible hope of doing this.

Not the Catholic (& therefore Lupine) understanding of the Eucharist. In the Eucharist, the Sacrifice of the Messiah is -- not reenacted, but made present, in a sense which transcends time and space: thus, the blood which the wine becomes is not blood "deposited" in some mystical blood bank, but actually bled at that (eternal, universal, non-temporal) moment. This is, btw, analogous to the original Passover, in which the message is "God did so-and-so for us" -- not "for our ancestors," but "for us"; in eating the Passover, a Jew of this day and age is mystically present at the original Exodus. All the People of Israel are present at Sinai.

A somewhat subtler objection is "if the secret will reduce the inhumi to mindless beasts, the Love One Another hypothesis won't work _because_ humans will voluntarily give their blood to the inhumi." Ahhh, but the inhumi to whom they give their blood become human (at least in spirit); so the only pure inhumi are, precisely, the mindless beasts. This, of course, is true even as Horn speaks: Krait, Jahlee, Fava, etc., are in fact human in spirit. But they represent danger _because_ the kind of human they have become is not a being of Agape; and the "weapon" is "too heavy to weild" _because_ humans, _not_ loving each other, will not voluntarily give their blood to the inhumi anyway. It is "too heavy to weild:" it is not practical (in the Fallen World, in which Blue, Green, Urth, and the _Whorl_ all inhere) to suppose that humans will as-a-race become Agape-driven, and so they cannot even lift the "sword" that would destroy the inhumi _as a threat_.

Right -- the "agape feast" is brought into extreme foreground.

Clearly you're not of the "once for all" persuasion. ;*)

My own understanding is a bit different from the "over and over again" you cite here -- Catholicism, as I understand it, teaches not that the Eucharist is rebirth (that happens at Baptism), but of the process by which we are gradually sanctified, con-formed to the Image of Christ. By analogy, repeated feeding from Agapistic humans would gradually sanctify the inhumi, and con-form them to Agape.

Ummmm. I _think_ the root of the word is not "human (ex _homo_)" but "humus." The prefix "in" is ambiguous, and can mean "not" or simply "in." Thus, the inhumi are "not-Earthly," and, at the same time, thay are the "in-Earth," the Buried, in at least the sense of "hidden," but becoming horrifically literal in the marketplace of Gaon.

There we are in complete agreement 8*)

Blat, blat,

--Blattid/Dan'l

2001-04-26 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that the proposed method of destroying the inhumi by slaughtering the sentient Green humans and inhumi conflicts with the Long Sun clue that the solution must relate to the Secret of "Love One Another." Responds to Kevin's suggestion by noting that a mass killing of Green's humans would not fit that criterion, and mentions that inviting Green's humans to Blue and then killing the inhumi would be unverifiable.
disputes · puzzle-solving

In response to my:

This is true -- but I'm not sure it's relevant. While "Love One Another" may not be The Secret, it clearly must be related to The Secret; slaughtering the entire human population of Green would _not_ fit this criterion. (As an alternative, what about inviting the entire human population of Green to come live on Blue, and then genociding the sentient inhumi...? But no; that simply couldn't be done with any measure of certainty.)

And more likely that it wouldn't occur to them to do so.

--Blattid

2001-04-26 James Jordan #

Agrees with Blattid that the blood of the inhumi works analogously to the Eucharist, while noting that transubstantiation has several versions and that the basic idea is the same. Argues that becoming humi or reverting to animal reflects daily dying to sin and rebirth from baptism, and points out that 'inhumi' also suggests 'inhumane'.
supports · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: Inhumi blood works like Eucharistic transubstantiation

Well sure. That's being more precise than I was being, but I think the analogy fits either way. As you probably know there is more than one version of "transubstantiation" in RC thought today, but whether Wolfe would agree completely with the particulars you set out or not, the basic notion is the same.

Agree. Some inhumi become humi, others revert to animals.

Either way. I think ultimately it is pretty much the same thing. We must "die" to sin daily, and be "raised" daily, so being "born anew" daily stems from the Baptismal beginning.

Well, Wolfe has his pregnant puns, and surely "inhumi" also connotes "inhumane."

I think we are in essential agreement.

2001-04-26 Kevin J. Maroney #

Proposes that destroying the inhumi could be achieved by traveling to Green and killing all its humans and sentient inhumi, since non-sentient inhumi might then be unable to cross to Blue. Grounds this on the claim that the Neighbors met the inhumi only after reaching Green, so the inhumi may have needed something from them to bridge the planets, while admitting the reasoning is uncertain.
proposes · myth-and-structure · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Non-sentient inhumi cannot pass to Blue; Inhumi stranded on Green without Neighbors' gift

"Travel to Green; kill all the humans and sentient inhumi there" might also do it. That would be a very heavy weapon indeed, because it would require mass slaughter of sentient, innocent, beings, including the children of the inhumi.

It is possible that non-sentient inhumi cannot pass to Blue. If I understand correctly, the Neighbors didn't encounter the inhumi until the Neighbors came to Green, so it is possible that the inhumi could not bridge the gap between the planets until they had acquired something from the Neighbors; if the inhumi all were devolved to beasts, then perhaps they would once again be stranded on Green.

But I feel I am standing on sand, and wish that I had some bedrock on which to build this interpretation.

2001-04-26 Adam Stephanides #

Argues that non-sentient inhumi may well fly from Green to Blue, but lack any reason to do so because Green offers plenty of non-intelligent prey and they have no interest in intelligent prey or knowledge of prey on Blue. Responds to Kevin J. Maroney's suggestion that inhumi could not cross to Blue until they acquired something from the Neighbors, granting the physical implausibility of the flight.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Inhumi lack motive rather than ability to reach Blue

Not that they couldn't bridge the gap (I think the inhumi do fly from Green to Blue, despite the physical implausibility), but that they had no reason to, since they had plenty of non-intelligent prey on Green and no desire for intelligent prey (nor inkling that any prey existed on Blue, intelligent or not). Devolved inhumi would presumably be in the same position.

--Adam
