The thread: The thread opened with Larry Miller asking whether Chenille is possessed by Kypris and admitting confusion about when she is herself. Lee Berman argued the blurring is deliberate, since Chenille is a prostitute and Kypris a sex goddess, citing a Wolfe interview. António Pedro Marques and Gerry Quinn traded views on spoilers and first readings, with Quinn noting re-reading often pays more. Late in the month the thread drifted to a ring's stone colour in Return to the Whorl: Berman and Miller proposed cosmological and resurrection readings, and Sergei Soloviev disputed the resurrection idea by pointing out that Horn received the ring only after leaving the pit. Miller conceded the sequence made his idea doubtful, so it ended without agreement.
  • Larry Miller: asks whether Chenille is possessed by Kypris and later proposes the ring enabled Horn's resurrection
  • Lee Berman: Chenille/Kypris confusion is deliberate, as prostitute versus sex goddess; later proposes black hole versus white fountain reading of the ring
  • António Pedro Marques: approach Wolfe by leaving things to one's reasoning and expecting the unexpected
  • Gerry Quinn: spoilers matter less because rereading is often richer than a first read
  • Sergei Soloviev: the ring could not have revived Horn in the pit since he received it after release
  • Andrew Mason: off-topic remarks on red hair and a greeting

Themes: Death and resurrection, Ghosts, dreams and visions, The dying sun and the cosmos

2011-11-08 Lee Berman replying to larry miller #

Argues that the confusion between Chenille and Kypris in Wolfe's Latro-era or Long Sun material is deliberate, since Chenille is a prostitute and Kypris is a sex goddess rather than a love goddess. Supports this by citing an interview in which Wolfe insists Kypris/Aphrodite/Venus was 'a sex goddess' and recalls another Chenille possession to consider.
supports · author-and-biography · close-textual-reading · theories: Chenille and Kypris identity confusion is intentional

Iirc, there is also another Chenille possession to consider. But in regard to Kypris, the identity confusion is surely intentional. Chenille is a prostitute and in one interview, Wolfe makes a very stern admonition to those who think Kypris/Aphrodtite/Venus was a love goddess. He states: "She was a sex goddess".

2011-11-08 Gerry Quinn replying to António Pedro Marques #

Argues that spoilers matter less for Wolfe because a second reading is often richer than the first, so a reader who will only read once might be better off knowing more. Responds to António Pedro Marques's remark that expecting the unexpected is the best way to approach Wolfe, agreeing about first-read enjoyment while qualifying it.
supports

From: Ant?nio Pedro Marques

It?s probably best for one?s own enjoyment to avoid reading too much about a book before one?s own first read. Although it depends, perhaps, on whether one intends to read it more than once. Arguably, the second read of Wolfe is better than the first, so a reader who will only read once might be better off spoiled! - Gerry Quinn

2011-11-27 Lee Berman replying to larry miller #

Proposes that the change in the ring's stone color in Return to the Whorl, from dark to light, may mirror a black hole versus white fountain dichotomy, with dark as an entrance for energy or spirit and light as an exit. Responds to Sergei Soloviev's quoted passage about Oreb's ring in chapter nine, offering a cosmological reading of the stone's colour shift.
proposes · myth-and-structure · theories: Oreb's ring color change mirrors black hole and white fountain dichotomy

defines an entrance for energy or spirit and light reflects an exit.

Proposes that the ring, which a character sees a Neighbor inside, allowed Horn's resurrection in the pit and that the Neighbor leaves the ring to possess Horn. proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: The ring enabled Horn's resurrection in the pit; A Neighbor leaves the ring to possess Horn

Just a quick thought. Has anyone ever considered that the ring may be what allowed Horns resurrection in the pit? This may be what was held to Horns forehead. IIRC there is a moment where a character looks inside the ring and sees a Neighbor. Maybe its this Neighbor that leaves the ring and possesses Horn? The color change could reflect an activation of sorts. Or a deactivation depending on how you look at it.
