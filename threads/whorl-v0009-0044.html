Theophanies, Silk's end

Whorl list · 4 messages · 1998-11-02 to 1998-11-04

1998whorlThe Book of the Long SunMatthew MalthouseAlice TurnerKieran ClearyMichael Straight
The thread: Readers debated whether Silk's sudden disappearance at the end of Exodus is a satisfying or unresolved ending, and whether Silk will return in Mainframe as a Silk/Pas amalgam. Kieran Cleary argued Silk may never have chosen the amalgam, since his downloaded personality could have been grafted onto an incomplete Pas persona, though he relied on a hazy recollection rather than a cited passage. Alice Turner rejected the amalgam as too obviously a demonic offer of world control and said Exodus leaves too much open, including Hy's allegiance and the taluses. Michael Straight defended the abrupt close as an ascension-like ending and read the talus and Hy details as sufficient. Matthew Malthouse challenged the view that Quetzal is benign, suggesting the humans may be a food supply for the vampires of Green. The thread ends without resolution, with the ending judged both open and deliberately co
  • Kieran Cleary: Silk may not have chosen the Silk/Pas amalgam; his personality may have been grafted onto incomplete Pas
  • Alice Turner: the amalgam is too obviously demonic; Exodus leaves too much unresolved; Quetzal seems benign
  • Michael Straight: the abrupt disappearance is a satisfying ascension-like close; Silk may have been meant as Pas's host
  • Matthew Malthouse: Quetzal only seems benign to the humans; he may be feeding them to the vampires of Green

Themes: Christ figures and messiahs, Gods, angels and the Increate, Robots, androids and artificial life

1998-11-04 Alice Turner #

Disputes the proposal that Silk will reappear in Mainframe as a Silk/Pas amalgam, arguing the idea is too obviously a demonic offer of world control and that Silk is a beautiful man. Notes that Exodus leaves too much unresolved, citing Silk's altered demeanor, Hy's allegiance, and the taluses under construction, and suggests Quetzal's benign nature and the destroyed Pas program bear on the other gods' fate.
disputes · close-textual-reading · genre-and-craft · theories: Silk and Pas merge into an amalgam in Mainframe

Greg, I don't think it's "explained," but does it need to be? Once you've had a look at Echidna and Scylla in action, wouldn't you do your best to keep them off-screen as well? Plus there is the destruction of the Pas program, which may have loosed the others to do their worst. Quetzal, despite his need for a meal beyond beef tea once is a while, generally seems to be a benign figure.

From Rostrum:

If you want to make a bet of it, I'll take it. I haven't got much of an argument except that, speaking editorially, too much is unresolved at the end of Exodus. There is the new murkiness in Silk's disposition and demeanor that someone spoke of earlier. There is the question of Hy's allegiance. There are the taluses under construction. It is true that we might not meet Silk again directly (I will be sorry for that) but we will have to hear the end of his tale. I reject the Silk/Pas amalgam because it's so baldly a variant of the demonic offer of world control, and Silk really is a "beautiful man."

-alga

1998-11-04 Michael Straight #

Argues that Silk's sudden disappearance at the end of Exodus is an artistically satisfying close, comparing it to Christ's ascension in Acts 1 and a possible Second Coming from Mainframe. Rebuts Alice Turner's complaint that Silk's story is left unresolved by noting that the talus construction details and Hy's fate can be read as sufficient, and floats a theory that Silk was meant to be Pas's host body.
disputes · allegory-and-typology · theology-and-religion · theories: Silk's disappearance mirrors Christ's ascension in Acts; Silk was meant to be host body for Pas; Silk returns as Second Coming from Mainframe

Editorially speaking, I think that what you're expressing is properly a criticism of Exodus, rather than a good reason to continue Silk's story in the Short Sun books. Unless Silk is the central character of the Short Sun books. It seems sloppy to write 4 books about a character and then have the resolution of his story take place in another set of books that center on other characters.

I think that criticism can be rebutted, that Silk's sudden disappearance can be seen as an artistically satisfying ending to the series, that the details of talus construction are not foreshadowing but the explanation of where these strange beasts come from, that we might still see more of Hy without necessarily giving Silk center stage again.

Silk's disapearance fits in well with the Christ imagery. It's straight out of Acts chapter 1. Silk is victorious and his followers think he's about to lead them to a new kingdom, and then he disappears leaving them to remember him as best they can and work things out on their own. Thus setting the stage for his Second Coming from the glory of Mainframe...

Yes it is, but I'm also of the camp that, if this happened/happens, it wouldn't be something Silk had a choice about.

I am perhaps still too enamored of the theory that Silk was originally intended to be the host body for Pas when the Starcrosser reached landfall, the Piaton that Typhon's "head" would be grafted onto.

From which might lead the (for me irresistable) irony of a crippled Pas program which needs a new "head" personality from Silk to run it and make it strong enough to win back Mainframe from Echidna and the kids.

-Rostrum

1998-11-04 Matthew Malthouse #

Argues that Quetzal only seems benign to the human protagonists of the Long Sun books, since his motives for wanting the Plan of Paz completed are never explained. Suggests he may be sending humans as a food supply to his relatives, the vampires of Green, and responds to Alice Turner's remark that Quetzal seems benign.
proposes · myth-and-structure · theories: Quetzal is sending humans as food to the vampires

Quetzal appears benign from the point of view of the human protagonists. But his motives are not explained to them, or to us. Why does he want to see the completion of the Plan of Paz? It's outcome would be to send humans to either Blue or Green - the one to a fragile saftey, the other to what is seemingly the home of the vampires. It looked to me as if he was seeking to send a new food supply to his relatives. Not so benign really.

Matthew
