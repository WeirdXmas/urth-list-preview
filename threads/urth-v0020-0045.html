'Westwind'

Urth list · 3 messages · 1998-10-16 to 1998-10-16

1998urthDan ParmenterAlex David GroceMichael Straight
The thread: Dan Parmenter opened by proposing that 'Westwind' is about people praying to God by radio and asked whether he had read it correctly, offering no textual evidence. Michael Straight argued instead that the leader is a dictator who uses the Westwind idea to keep followers quiet by making each believe they alone are special, citing the leader's luxury against the followers' squalor. Alex David Groce disputed this, arguing the God reading is the intended and more natural one and citing Wolfe's introduction to Storeys from the Old Hotel, where Wolfe says the prayer-by-radio reading was his aim. The thread ends with the two readings opposed and no further exchange recorded.
  • Dan Parmenter: people pray to God by radio; asks for confirmation
  • Michael Straight: leader is a dictator using Westwind belief for social control
  • Alex David Groce: God reading is intended; dictator reading does not fit the story and conflicts with Wolfe's own statement

Themes: Gods, angels and the Increate, Power, rulers and politics, Catholic faith and sacraments

1998-10-16 Michael Straight #

Proposes that the leader in 'Westwind' is a dictator who uses the Westwind idea as a tool of social control, making each follower believe they alone are special. Grounds this in the leader's luxurious living against his followers' squalor and the question of why each believer is told they are the only Westwind, asking whether that belief would discourage rebellion.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Westwind leader is a dictator using social control

That's one interpretation. Do you think it's possible that the leader is actually a dictator who uses the "Westwind" business as a means of social control? Why does he lead each of his followers to believe that they are the only "Westwind"? Why does he live in luxurys (as seen on TV) when his followers live in squallor?

If you believe you are the emperor's secret favorite, his spy, wouldn't that make you less likely to be part of any rebellion?

-Rostrum

1998-10-16 Alex David Groce #

Disputes the reading that the leader in 'Westwind' is a dictator using the radio business for social control, arguing the God interpretation is the intended and more natural one. Cites Wolfe's introduction to Storeys from the Old Hotel, where Wolfe says the prayer-by-radio reading is what he aimed at, and says the dictator reading does not fit the story's feel.
disputes · author-and-biography · theories: Westwind is about talking to God by radio

Wolfe says in the intro. to Storeys from the Old Hotel that the your interpretation is what he was aiming at. Doesn't mean other interpretations can't hold water, of course, but Wolfe was aiming at the God version. Actually, I think that's the more natural interpretation, as well--for me at least, the dictator version only began to exist for me after I heard someone else explain the story that way. It doesn't fit the feel of the story, in my opinion.
