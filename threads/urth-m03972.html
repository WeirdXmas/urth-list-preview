Book of the New Sun Audiobooks?

Urth list · 13 messages · 2007-06-14 to 2007-06-15

2007urthThe Book of the New SunTim BryantMatthew KingRex LycanthrosaurusSteve TaylorDon DoggettFred KiescheaaronStuart HammDavid DiGiacomo
The thread: Readers questioned whether an eBay listing of Book of the New Sun audiobooks was genuine, whether it was digitized from old tapes, and how two CDs could hold fifty hours of narration. David DiGiacomo, Matthew King and aaron explained that compressed spoken-word MP3 files could fit that much audio, while Rex Lycanthrosaurus checked the seller's figures and found a mismatch with the capacity arithmetic. Tim Bryant reported that the listing had been flagged for suspected copyright infringement and had likely been removed, urging others to report the seller's remaining items. Steve Taylor speculated that the recordings might be text-to-speech. The thread ended without verifying the product, drifting into side chatter and remaining unresolved.
  • Recursive Loop: doubts the audiobook files are genuine or legitimately digitized
  • Rex Lycanthrosaurus: disputes the CD capacity math and finds the seller's figures inconsistent
  • David DiGiacomo: explains that compressed spoken-word audio can fit fifty hours on two disks
  • Matthew King: says aggressive MP3 compression for speech makes the running time plausible
  • Tim Bryant: reports a copyright-infringement flag and urges reporting the seller's listings
  • Steve Taylor: guesses the recordings were made with text-to-speech software

Themes: Books, libraries and writing

2007-06-14 Matthew King replying to Rex Lycanthrosaurus #

Explains that MP3 compression can be tuned heavily for spoken word, so a one-hour recording can shrink to about 6 MB and fit over 100 hours on a CD. Responds to Rex Lycanthrosaurus's arithmetic on the audiobook's running time by offering a technical explanation for how so much audio fits on two disks.
informs

mp3 compression is very tunable, and you can get especially low file sizes when working with spoken word recordings.

Case in point, I just now finished preparing an mp3 recording of an hour long class. The final size was 6 MB.

At that level of compression, you can get over 100 hours on a CD.

Matthew

informs

I also just emailed the person to ask about the CDs. And yes, it is possible that they would fit on 2 CDs. You see, MP3's are 1/10th the size of regular WAV. files found on regular music CD's. In other words, a regular CD can hold up to 800 minutes of MP3 audio while with WAV's you only get 80 mins. At any rate, we will soon see if they are legit, although I am very doubtful.

2007-06-15 Steve Taylor replying to Tim Bryant #

Speculates that the audiobook recordings of Wolfe's New Sun books were made by text-to-speech software, recalling a computer-generated Lovecraft CD a friend owned. Offers this as a guess about the audiobooks' origin rather than as established fact, in reply to Tim Bryant's question about where they came from.
proposes · theories: New Sun audiobooks are computer-generated text-to-speech

As to the origin of the audiobooks, a wild guess:

A friend showed me a CD he'd bought from somewhere or other of readings of H.P. Lovecraft which were entirely computer generated - text fed into a text-to-speech program. The results were just about as awful as you'd imagine. I can see this as being a great assistive technology for blind people who are sick of the relatively restricted pool of available audiobooks, but I can't imagine anyone listening to it for any other reason.

Unless they're a *really* big Doctor Who fan and just plain like the sound of Daleks talking.

Anyway - just guesswork on my part.

Steve
