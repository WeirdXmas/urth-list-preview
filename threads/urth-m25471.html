Innocents Aboard

Urth list · 4 messages · 2014-07-14 to 2014-07-15

The thread: Thread on "Innocents Aboard" and "The Friendship Light" began with Antonin Scriabin asking whether any demon or vampire spares victims' heads, then proposed the winged green-eyed creatures are aliens who know what they are, citing the flickering Coleman lantern and abduction imagery. Daniel Otto Jack Petersen suggested an avenging angel or mythic beast such as a gryphon or sphinx, citing cherubim and seraphim. Tony Ellis argued the 'friends' are Lovecraft's Fungi from Yuggoth, who steal brains, with the lamp a pun on their moth-like form, and pointed to The Whisperer in Darkness for the head-sparing question. The thread ended with competing identifications and no consensus.
  • Antonin Scriabin: creatures are aliens, and Ty knows what they are
  • Daniel Otto Jack Petersen: an avenging angel or mythic beast such as a gryphon or sphinx
  • Tony Ellis: the friends are Lovecraft's Fungi from Yuggoth

Themes: Beasts and the animal in us, Myth and folklore sources, Ghosts, dreams and visions

2014-07-14 Antonin Scriabin #

Asks whether any mythological demon or vampire spares its victims' heads, comparing the behaviour in "The Friendship Light" to cats that leave snake and mouse heads uneaten. Also thanks a list member for a 2001 Star Child quip after checking the wiki for clues to the story.
questions · source-and-allusion

Working my way through this collection, and checked the Wiki for clues pertaining to "The Friendship Light". If one of you threw in the quip about the Star Child from 2001, thanks for the laugh!

As for the story itself, is there a type of demon / vampire from any mythology that spurns the heads of its victims? This behavior reminds me of certain cats I've known, who will leave snake and mouse heads untouched and eat a good portion of the rest.

2014-07-15 Antonin Scriabin replying to Daniel Otto Jack Petersen #

Proposes that the winged, green-eyed creatures in the story "Innocents Aboard" are aliens rather than demons, and that Ty knows what they are. Supports this by pointing to the Coleman lantern that flickers with signal frequencies and to American alien-abduction and mutilated-cow imagery, replying to Daniel Otto Jack Petersen's idea of an avenging angel.
proposes · close-textual-reading · myth-and-structure · theories: The creatures in Innocents Aboard are aliens

Here it is:

"You took him, as you promised, whole and entire. I have no grounds for complaint upon that score, or indeed upon any. And I feel certain he met his well-deserved death firmly convinced that he was in the grip of demons, or some such thing, which I find enormously satisfying."

We get very little (even by Wolfe standards) description of the maybe-demons. All we really know is they have wings and green eyes. I'm wondering if they aren't some kind of alien ... Ty seems to think it is somewhat funny that his brother-in-law will have thought he was being attacked by demons. Can we take this to mean there is no supernatural element at all? Ty clearly has a good idea of what they are, and he doesn't think they're demons.

I wonder also about the Coleman lantern at the end, strung up in a tree to flicker when "signal frequencies" change. It's not uncommon in American-myth for alien spaceships (or the beings themselves) to mess with electronics and gadgets of all kinds, when close.

Alien abduction classically is "whole and entire" as well, but this doesn't mesh with the disemboweling of Nicollete or the headless cat, unless these are "mutilated cow" events.

2014-07-15 Daniel Petersen replying to Antonin Scriabin #

Speculates about the identity of the creature in Wolfe's story The Friendship Light, proposing that it may be an avenging angel or a mythic beast such as a gryphon or sphinx. Responds to Antonin Scriabin's question about a head-sparing demon by citing the cherubim and seraphim of Hebrew scripture.
proposes · puzzle-solving · source-and-allusion · theories: The creature is an avenging angel; The creature is a gryphon or sphinx

One of my favourite stories by Wolfe: I've often wondered about the creature. As I recall, some character says, laughingly, that its victim probably *thought* it was a devil or demon - implying that this wasn't at all the case.

I wondered if it was some sort of avenging angel (think of the monstrous seraphim and cherubim of Hebrew scripture - Wolfe references the latter to great effect in another story in this collection, as he also does in 'Quadrifons' from Long and Short Suns).

I also wondered if it was a gryphon or sphinx or some other being from mythology.

(I've also never been able to figure out what in the world is a 'friendship light' is.)

-DOJP

2014-07-15 Tony Ellis replying to Antonin Scriabin #

Argues that the 'friends' in the story The Friendship Light are the Fungi from Yuggoth from Lovecraft, who steal human brains, and that their attraction to a lamp is a pun on their moth-like description. Responds to Antonin Scriabin's question about demons or vampires that spurn victims' heads, pointing to Lovecraft's The Whisperer in Darkness as the source.
proposes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: The friends in The Friendship Light are Lovecraft's Fungi from Yuggoth

Antonin Scriabin wrote (re: The Friendship Light)

The 'friends' are HP Lovecraft's Fungi from Yuggoth, who steal human brains. You can read about them in The Whisperer in Darkness.

The fact that they're attracted by a lamp is a Wolfean pun on the fact that the Fungi are described as moth-like.
