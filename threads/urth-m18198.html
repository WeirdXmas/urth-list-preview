Themes: Fathers, mothers and children, Hidden identities and parentage

2011-01-18 Andrew Mason #

Argues that Auk was sent to protect Chenille rather than Hyacinth, so his grabbing Hyacinth is a mistake about which woman Tartaros meant. Grounds this in Tartaros's words 'a woman is vital', Chenille's disappearance, and Auk's later remark that every man must bring a woman, and suggests Auk may be Tartaros's son.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Auk is a son of Tartaros; Tartaros meant Chenille, not Hyacinth; Auk grabbed Hyacinth by mistake

Possibly Echidna has been spying on Tartaros, and knows that he has chosen Auk to help him carry out the plan of Pas. What tbhis doesn't explain, of course, is why she confuses Villus with Auk.

Tartaros says 'Go to the woman. You must protect her. A woman is vital. This is not'. Auk looks around for Chenille, but she has disappeared. He then runs out and grabs Hyacinth, since he has been told that a woman is vital, and she is the only woman to hand. It's not mistaken identity as such, though in a way he has taken her in mistake for someone else.

I think it's fairly clear that Tartaros does mean Chenille. His words show that while he has a particular woman in mind, she is important becuase she is a woman. This makes sense if it's Chenille - Auk is going to start a new colony, so needs a potential partner with whom he can have children. Later, when recruiting his old mates for the expedition, Auk says that every man must bring a woman and every woman a man.

He may be a son of Tartaros (by sperm donation). When Tartaros leaves him, he weeps because he has never had anyone to think of as a father.
