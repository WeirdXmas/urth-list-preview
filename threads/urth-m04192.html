2007-03-07 Sarah Dorrance-Minch replying to Adam Thornton #

Argues that a midnight-blue dinner jacket looks blacker than black, a colour-perception point tangential to the Fuligin thread. Responds to Adam Thornton's link to a news item by describing the Edwardian dinner suit's navy, woven with black threads, and by noting that optical illusions need no high technology, ending with a quotation about the eye in dark times.

Adam Thornton <[email]> wrote: No, for real:

http://news.rpi.edu/update.do?artcenterkey=1956

Interesting that it looks dark blue. The original "tuxedo-style" dinner suit was midnight blue; a navy so dark that it could almost be black (probably navy blue with black threads in the warp or weft, since they didn't have nonreflective coating in the Edwardian era...) The point was to wear a dinner jacket that made black look faded by comparison. Apparently some optical illusions don't need high technology in the right lighting conditions.
