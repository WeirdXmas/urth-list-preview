At risk of getting in over my head . . .

Urth list · 5 messages · 2008-10-21 to 2008-10-23

2008urththalassocratMatthew KingEric Ortlund
The thread: Eric Ortlund asked how Patera Quetzal reached the Whorl, given Wolfe's remark that he was a late arrival and that no major inhumi characters were involved, and whether the inhumi's poor survival of the Blue-to-Green crossing allows any textual clue. Ortlund then proposed that the Neighbors physically ferry inhumi by spaceship from Green, grounded in the breeding process and SilkHorn's account in Return to the Whorl, and disputed Mr Thalassocrat's claim that inhumi never fly from Green to Blue. Thalassocrat made a closely similar ferrying proposal, tied to SilkHorn's conversation with Vadsig and the idea that astral transport would destroy an inhumu's nature. In his final post he argued the inhumi could only fly between Green and Blue near conjunction via a Hohmann transfer, which he said Wolfe intended. The thread stayed a two-person speculative exchange with no sign of agreement.
  • Eric Ortlund: Neighbors ferry inhumi by spaceship from Green, with a domed building on Green's mountain as the launch point
  • Mr Thalassocrat: inhumi reproduce only on Green and are physically ferried to the Whorl by Neighbor spaceship
  • Mr Thalassocrat: inhumi could only fly Green to Blue near conjunction via a Hohmann transfer, which Wolfe intended

Themes: Beasts and the animal in us, The dying sun and the cosmos, Robots, androids and artificial life

2008-10-21 Eric Ortlund #

Asks how Patera Quetzal got onto the Whorl, given Wolfe's interview remark that he was a late arrival and that no major Book of the New Sun characters were inhumi. Considers whether Quetzal was on Urth unnoticed, notes that the inhumi barely survive the Blue-to-Green crossing, and asks whether any textual clues exist.
questions · puzzle-solving · author-and-biography · theories: Quetzal was on Urth before the Whorl launched; Quetzal boarded the Whorl after its launch

I re-read BLS and BNS last summer and found it deeply satisfying, especially after trolling this mailing list from its very beginning for clues. One thing still bothers me, which I hesitate to raise, because it's related to the whole idea that Blue and Green are Urth and Lune or some variety thereof, and I simply don't want to get into that (I don't buy it, but it's way too complex and has already been discussed a lot). Namely: how did Patera Quetzal get into the Whorl? My first thought was that Quetzal/the inhumi were present on Urth without Severian knowing; but, in an interview with Wolfe where he fielded questions from readers, Wolfe said that none of the major characters in BNS were inhumi, and that Quetzal was a "late arrival" on the Whorl - which I take to mean (perhaps incorrectly) that he somehow got on board after it was launched. But how? Are there any clues in the books that anyone knows of? There's talk in the book about the inhumi barely being able to survive the crossing from Blue to Green . . . and Quetzal's been on the Lander for a long time. And, if I remember correctly, he's not the only inhumu on the whorl. Anyone care to clarify things for me?

2008-10-22 Eric Ortlund replying to Mr Thalassocrat #

Proposes that the Neighbors transport inhumi physically by spaceship from Green to the Whorl and their new world, with a low domed building on Green's mountain as the ship and a Neighbor-naut as the source of SilkHorn's information. Grounds this in the Neighbors' breeding process and in SilkHorn's account in Return to the Whorl, then questions Mr Thalassocrat's claim that inhumi never fly from Green to Blue by citing their arrival in waves every few years.

On Wed, Oct 22, 2008 at 2:16 AM, Matthew King <[email]>

On Tue, Oct 21, 2008 at 8:51 AM, Eric Ortlund

IIRC, the Neighbors intentionally infected the Whorl, the idea being
that the behavior of the inhumi would provide insight into the human
condition.

As SilkHorn explains to Vadsig in RTTW, while they are sailing from Dorp to New Viron.

There's a lot to think about in this. Just reviewing the conclusions I came to and seeing how they look now on reflection ....

Inhumi can only breed on Green. Just drinking a human's blood doesn't turn a primal inhumu into a self-aware being: only its children. The Neighbors must have taken primal inhumi from Green to the Whorl, gotten them to drink human blood, taken them back to Green to breed and then selected some of the offspring to re-introduce to the Whorl.

The Neighbors also took inhumi with them to their new world (also according to SilkHorn), and it must be the same kind of process, except on-going (as the infestation of the Whorl may not have been). Neighbors monitor themselves periodically by letting inhumi take some of their blood, transporting them back to Green to breed, and examining the nature of the offspring.

Everything suggests that the transportation of the inhumi must be *physical*, not astral travelling. An astrally-transported inhumu is no longer an inhumu. The Neighbors need space ships to do it. I believe that the low, domed building SilkHorn sees on top of the great mountain on Green is such a spaceship, and that the Neighbor he meets there is a Neighbor-naut, the source of his information about the inhumu infestation of the Whorl etc. The Neighbors select inhumi for transportation to the Whorl and their new world by choosing from those who manage to fly to the top of the mountain - the strongest-willed, the ones who care about having sentient children and so on.

Jahlee was once one of these. She never flew from Green to Blue; no inhumu does - that's a lie they tell themselves. Any inhumu on Blue came there from Green on Auk's captured lander, or on a lander from the Whorl (as with Fava, I think), or was transported there by the Neighbors. I don't know which of these methods Jahlee used.

Mr. Thallassocrat: Thanks for the very thoughtful response. There is indeed something very deep going on here. I'm reminded of when SilkHorn's son asks him why he doesn't just flat out tell him what's going on - and SilkHorn replies that he trying to get his son to think for himself, to draw important conlcusions from scant evidence - and isn't that what Wolfe is pushing us to do as readers, not just with his texts but our own lives?

Anyway, I'm thankful for your thoughts, especially the
Neighbor-Naut, which, while not knowing if it is correct, would certainly explain things. But about inhumi flying from Green to Blue - I wonder if that's a lie, because as I remember, inhumi come in waves to Green every three years or so when the two planets are close together. So I *think* that checks out. But obviously the whole issue is a huge one.

I'm delightfully stunned by the massive invention of the BSS series - the massive machinations which Horn gets caught up in.

2008-10-23 thalassocrat replying to Eric Ortlund #

Argues that inhumi could only fly between the planets Green and Blue near conjunction, using a Hohmann transfer, and that Wolfe intended this. Grounds the claim in the inhumi arriving on Green every few years and in the landers' limited thrust, and notes that this timing leaves the inhumi threat on Blue under control by the end of Return to the Whorl.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Inhumi travel between Green and Blue only near conjunction; Wolfe intended Hohmann transfer travel for landers

But about inhumi flying from Green to Blue -
I wonder if that's a lie, because as I remember, inhumi come in waves to Green every three years or so when the two planets are close together. So I *think* that checks out. But obviously the whole issue is a huge one.

Realistically, landers could only travel between Green and Blue at around conjunction, also. They seem to be rocket powered craft subject to the standard orbital mechanics & not capable of pulling more than a few gee, for limited periods.

So I think a journey between Green and Blue would require a Hohmann transfer or whatever, at some point around conjunction. And I think Wolfe intended that to be the case.

But obviously it's possible to read things differently - I just find this explanation satisfying and consistent. One reason: it means that the inhumi problem on Blue is pretty much under control by the end of RTTW. Their ranks have been decimated, and there won't be any more coming from Green.
