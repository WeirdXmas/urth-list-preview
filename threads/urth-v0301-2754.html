2003-01-21 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Disputes Adam Stephanides's claim that Green lacks intelligence in There Are Doors, arguing instead that Green's color vocabulary comes from the appliances and computers he sells. Ties this to Lara as a techno-goddess and to Green's later absorption of a furniture lexicon after his rehabilitation, with the seaman's desk as Love breaking into his new world.
disputes · close-textual-reading · allegory-and-typology · theories: Green's color vocabulary comes from his appliance sales job; Lara is a techno-goddess; Love pursues Green through furnishings and desks

Respectfully I disagree. The intriguing "Viridian" passage at the beginning of the book might at first seem like a Wolfean lexical exercise -- "Typical," one might say. Erudite. But I find it very interesting that Green has this color vocabulary because of the appliances and computers that he sells: these are words used by the manufacturers and has picked them up.

This fact, that he is a computer/gizmo salesman, feeds well into my growing sense of Lara the techno-goddess.

Notice what happens when Green has been forcefully rehabilitated: he is put into Furnishings, a retro-world, a non-electronic world. He starts selling a lot, and reading up: he learns a new lexicon, this time related to furniture. You know what happens next: Love crashes through his new world in the form of a seaman's desk!

No matter where he is, White Goddess Love is going to ferret him out.

=mantis=

2003-01-26 Adam Stephanides #

Clarifies that the protagonists of the Green's door-novel, presumably Green in There Are Doors and Ivan Green's kin, share one basic personality type rather than being identical in every respect. Responds to Michael Andre-Driussi's objection by softening an earlier overstatement, describing them as competent, despondent but normal, and fundamentally decent, and contrasts them with the protagonists of The Fifth Head of Cerberus.
disputes · close-textual-reading

on 1/21/03 11:18 AM, Michael Andre-Driussi at [email]

[snip]

I'm not quite sure what you're disagreeing with. But if it was the assertion in my final paragraph, I probably expressed myself more strongly than I'd meant to. I didn't intend to say that the protagonists were identical in all, or even most, respects. What I meant was that they all seem to me to be the same basic personality type: what I think of as "idealized middle American male" (even when they're not American). They're not intellectuals, but they know how things work, and how to do things, without boasting about it. They have a tendency to despondency, but are otherwise psychologically "normal" (unlike, say, the various protagonists of 5HC). And they're fundamentally decent, without being priggish.

--Adam
