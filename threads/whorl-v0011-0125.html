Timeframe of S/N Sun books; Oreb possessed

Whorl list · 1 messages · 2000-08-18 to 2000-08-18

2000whorlThe Book of the Long SunRobert Borski

Themes: Gods, angels and the Increate, Ghosts, dreams and visions

2000-08-18 Robert Borski #

Argues that Oreb in The Book of the Short Sun is intermittently possessed by Scylla, citing a dream report in On Blue's Waters and an observed arm-like motion in In Green's Jungles. Credits Nick Gevers with first linking a scene in Green to the swimming scene in The Shadow of the Torturer, and compares Oreb to Chenille and Tick being ridden by gods.
supports · close-textual-reading · allegory-and-typology · theories: Oreb is ridden by Scylla at times; Scene in Green occurs during Shadow swimming scene

Kevin Maroney having written:

<Someone here--Robert Borski, I think--pegged the scene towards the end of _Green_ as taking place during the swimming scene in chapter 1 of _Shadow of the Torturer_, and I suspect he's right.>

Actually, it was the very astute Nick Gevers, who alluded to the above almost before anyone else had even read the book. I say: nice job, mate.

<I don't know that it is "old hat," but then again I don't remember anybody else besides Robert Borski (and lately myself) following this. We have been saying that Oreb is ridden by a god, sometimes, but not all the time; just as Chenille is (sometimes ridden by Kypris); just as Tick is (sometimes ridden by Sphigx).

<Then again, we have read "The Night Chough" (collected in THE CROW: SHATTERED LIVES AND BROKEN DREAMS), and you probably have not.>

There are actually scenes in both Short Sun books that seem to indicate Oreb has been possessed by Scylla.

The first comes in OBW, very near the end, where Horn is ruefully cataloguing the many things he has been unable to write about in detail, including "Nothing about my dream of an angry and vindictive Scylla who talked like Oreb, the dream that woke me screaming and so terrified Brother and Sister: "Windows! Windows! Windows!" (p. 380)

[Nontangential associated question: could Brother's real name be Bricco?]

The second comes in IGJ, during Horn's second oneiric transport to Green, and regards Oreb's newly transformed form:

"'Bird go?'" he croaked doubtfully. (His voice was exactly as it had always been, though he had come to seem a sort of clumsy, feathered dwarf, and I had seen undulant arms wrestling with the flagstone as well as his now-armlike wings.)" (p. 208)

The "undulant arms" are almost certainly Scylla's or I'm a nittimonk's uncle.

Robert Borski
