2002-07-16 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that the Trivigaunti culture in the Long Sun and Short Sun books satirizes extreme patriarchal male dominance rather than feminism, and that the Gaonese, Grandecivitan, Dorpish and New Vironese cultures use satirical rhetoric to address why human societies fail. Rejects the claim that Wolfe intends satire of Indian, Italian, Dutch or Hispanic cultures, citing Swift's Houyhnhnms as a parallel and noting the Ascians as the one clear satire.
disputes · close-textual-reading · genre-and-craft · theories: Trivigaunti culture satirizes extreme patriarchalism, not feminism; Wolfe uses real cultures as templates without satirizing them

This is most=20 blatantly obvious in New Viron and Dorp -- which is, perhaps, why=20 you don't mention Dutch and Hispanic culture as targets for satire? -- but the roots of war and unrest in Gaon and the Grandecivitan colony towns are as universal as the corruption of Dorp and the strong-man-ism of New Viron.
