The thread: Posters on the Urth list playfully matched Gene Wolfe's stories to Christian liturgical feasts and calendar holidays, as an extension of an earlier thread. Michael Straight offered picks such as "Detective of Dreams" for Epiphany and "Westwind" for Whitsunday, and agreed with Kevin J. Maroney's Easter choice of "Seven American Nights" mostly for jokes. Mark Millman joked that "Peritonitis" suits Mother's Day and questioned whether holidays were open, since Wolfe may already use Father's Day in the Book of Days. Alex David Groce backed Easter for "Seven American Nights" for its resurrection theme and egg motif, citing the story's Easter procession and the Communion ending of "Detective of Dreams". The thread stayed light and ended without any settled assignments.
  • Michael Straight: playful feast assignments, agrees Seven American Nights fits Easter, jokes over textual evidence
  • Mark Millman: jokes Peritonitis for Mother's Day, doubts holidays are open since Book of Days may use Father's Day
  • Alex David Groce: Seven American Nights for Easter via resurrection and egg motif, Silhouette or Peace for Halloween

Themes: Death and resurrection, Christ figures and messiahs, Humour in Wolfe

Playfully extends a thread assigning Wolfe's stories to Christian liturgical feasts, proposing "Detective of Dreams" for Epiphany, "Westwind" for Whitsunday, and "The Packerhaus Method" or "Peritonitis" for Ash Wednesday. Maroney's earlier Easter pick of "Seven American Nights" and agrees with it, offering jokes about unsuitable stories like "All the Hues of Hell" and "Peace" rather than textual evidence.

1999-01-12 Alex David Groce #

Proposes matching Wolfe's stories to church holidays, assigning Seven American Nights to Easter for its resurrection theme and egg motif, and Silhouette or Peace to Halloween. Grounds the picks in the story's Easter procession, the Communion ending of Detective of Dreams, and the poster's mother reading Peace as a ghost story.
proposes · close-textual-reading · genre-and-craft · theories: Seven American Nights is an Easter story

Yes, only Father's Day. "Seven American Nights" is definitely the way to go for Easter--it has an Easter procession, of course, but I think the theme is definitely resurrection (whether a diabolic parody of resurrection or the real thing is another matter...) Anyway, it's an egg story, hence Easter.

Detective of Dreams was only suggested because it does end with Communion, after all.

Hmmm... I think if it wasn't already taken (holiday, not story) that "Sillhoutte" is sufficiently creepy for Halloween (PEACE would also do--when my mother read it, I told her a very important thing was to figure out what genre it was... A long time later, she said "OH! It's a ghost story.")
