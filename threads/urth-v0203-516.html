Themes: Unreliable narration

2002-03-29 Marc Aramini #

Argues that Severian's remark in The Sword of the Lictor about the Conciliator being dressed like anyone else takes on an ironic tone once his later re-enactment of the Conciliator's life is known. Grounds this in the narrator's habit of missing the obvious, citing a later line in which Severian asks whether he is not Horn, which the poster sees as a further example.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian's remarks gain irony from his later role as the Conciliator

in my immediately previous post I said something about the tone of the statement that the dust could "scarcely" be the same dust. I mean that there is some ambiguity in that formulation - like when Severian says, at the beginning of Sword of the Lictor, Gee, just think, if the conciliator were here, he would be in constume just like anyone else! How neat! That statement takes on an ironic cast when he know he will re-enact the life of the conciliator (or that he probably is the conciliator, I suppose) Let's face it, Wolfe's narrator's make a living off not seeing the obvious. For example, 1000 pages later, "You mean I'm not Horn?"
Marc Aramini
