Prehistoric Starcrossers

Urth list · 8 messages · 2002-04-26 to 2002-05-01

2002urthFilipe SilvaJerry FriedmanAdam StephanidesJeff WilsonTony Ellis
The thread: Readers asked how a prehistoric starcrosser could exist in Wolfe's Urth-era setting without an industrial society, which should have left archaeological remnants. Tony Ellis argued the story hints at such ships, suggesting a crushed ship buried in rock, while Jeff Wilson pressed that industrial traces like mines, mills and refineries are missing and that Wolfe deliberately makes the archaeological record a theme. Jerry Friedman joined the skeptical side with alliteration jokes and references to Atlantis and Mu. Filipe Silva then proposed astral or ether travel, which Wilson rejected for lacking textual support, and Adam Stephanides ended the debate by citing page 125 of the OWO to show the story describes physical starcrossers. The thread closed on the text rather than agreement.
  • Jeff Wilson: an industrial civilization would leave traces, so Wolfe must be making the archaeological record part of the story
  • Tony Ellis: the story hints at a prehistoric starcrosser and a lone buried wreck could explain the missing evidence
  • Jerry Friedman: doubts a starcrosser could exist without industry and finds the Atlantis/Mu comparisons unconvincing
  • Filipe Silva: prehistoric peoples traveled by leaving their bodies, through ether, as in esoteric and Mayan lore
  • Adam Stephanides: the text explicitly says the Shadow Children's ancestors came in a physical starcrosser, against astral travel

Themes: Myth and folklore sources, Colonization and the native, Deep time and ruins

2002-04-26 Tony Ellis #

Argues that the prehistoric starcrossers left hints in the story itself, pointing to the Old Wise One's mention of a starcrosser and his poetic recollection of the splashdown. Responds to Jeff Wilson's question about how ancient societies could travel the stars without leaving evidence, citing the alliterative language and suggesting a remnant ship buried in sedimentary rock.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Old Wise One quotes an ancient epic about the starcrosser; Prehistoric starcrosser survives as a buried wreck

They certainly leave a hint in the story. The Old Wise One talks of a "starcrosser", and later he seems to be remembering the actual splashdown itself: "'Like a spark from the echoless vault of emptiness,' the Old Wise One continued, 'the shining shape slipped steaming into the sea.'"

Notice the poetic, alliterative language the Old Wise One uses here, and nowhere else. It's as if he's quoting some epic oral poem along the lines of Homer. Another clue, I think, that there's a link between the ancient civilisations of Earth and the Annese.

As for the archaeological record, who says they didn't leave a trace there? We're talking about what may be the crushed and rusted remains of only a single spaceship, trapped between two layers of sedimentary rock somewhere on a big empty planet. Easy to miss.

2002-04-28 Jeff Wilson #

Argues that Wolfe deliberately brings the archaeological record into the story, so readers must consider what survives and what does not, citing Marsch's recurring presence across the three novellas. Responds to Tony Ellis's objection that no industrial society would leave no remnants, supporting the point by noting that two main characters debate how to interpret the archaeological evidence and asking whether grasses and animal tissue could have built the starcrossing civilization.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Wolfe intends the archaeological record to be debated within the story

I try to do the same, but since Wolfe brings the archaeological record into the story, you have to really think about what he's trying say. There is no mere mention and dismissal of lack of evidence, but a repeated visitation of the subject, with the character of Marsch entering all three novellas, and two of the main characters holding forth on the proper interpretation of the archaeological record, including what does and does not endure. Or are we supposed to think grasses and animal tissue formed the structures of the original starcrossing civilization?

Rather than that, I think that it may be possible to explain the origin of the abos and shadow children via the time shifting themes of 5HC, but that would be another post.

2002-04-28 Jeff Wilson #

Disputes Filipe Silva's claim that prehistoric peoples travelled between stars by Jinas astral travel, arguing the idea is absent from the story. Grounds this in the story's abos depending on physical living bodies for influence, and asks whether Jinas travel would undercut the refinement process by letting old races escape purification.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Jinas travel contradicts the story's physical-body dependence

This I've heard of, but it is not specifically mentioned in the story, and it would contradict the dependency the abos have on their physical bodies. "A Story" has ghosts and the Wise Old One, but they are ultimately dependent on abos with physical, living bodies for influence.

But doesn't Jinas travel confound this process of catclysmic refinement? It lets the old, unimproved races escape their purification by traveling to other planets, where they endure to late mingle with and contaminate the successive civilizations' "higher virtues."
