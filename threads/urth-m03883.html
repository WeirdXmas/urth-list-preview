Vance and Shea

2007-06-04 Yves Meynard #

Calls Michael Shea's Nifft books baroque fantasy and finds his sequel to The Eyes of the Overworld amusing, as it starts as a convincing pastiche before Shea's own style breaks through. Answers Aaron's question about Shea and notes Vance's influence on Wolfe despite Vance's cynical view of religion.
answers

Shea's a very interesting writer. The books about Nifft are impressive pieces of baroque fantasy, and the sequel to _The Eyes of the Overworld_ is quite amusing. It starts off as a very convincing pastiche, but then Shea's own writerly nature comes seeping through the cracks and the book becomes very Nifft-like at the end -- not that I'm complaining.

I reread Vance pretty regularly. He was one of my first great literary loves, and the only one who's kept all his glamour as I've aged. Amusing to think the influence he's had on Wolfe when you consider Vance's cynical take on religion.

Yves Meynard

Speculates that Wolfe may have been influenced by Jack Vance's The Dying Earth before his conversion to Catholicism, and wonders what Wolfe would think of Vance now. Recalls Wolfe saying in Castle of the Otter that he read The Dying Earth on its 1950 release, and otherwise reflects on Vance's lasting appeal and plans to buy Shea's Nifft books.

Perhaps Wolfe was influenced by Vance before he converted to Catholicism. If Wolfe only discovered Vance today, I wonder what his opinion would be?
