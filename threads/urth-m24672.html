2013-05-03 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Posts a link and quotes a passage defining madregot as supernatural revelation, vision, clairvoyance, telepathy, miracle and healing, a term that appears in Wolfe's Book of the New Sun. The poster offers no commentary of their own, leaving the quoted definition to speak for the term's relevance to Wolfe's usage.
informs · source-and-allusion

http://tinyurl.com/cgy2tel

"The term madregot comprises the various forms of super?natural revelation, vision, clairvoyance, telepathy, miracle, healing, release from the physical, and the like.

"The relation to all these madregot (though they may also be the basis for 'miracles') is like that of the Talmud: an attitude of respect and deference, together with a certain suspicion and disdain.

Thus, although madregot were considered valuable means, they were never felt to be the end in themselves."

...

"The madregot are as marvelous as any other spiritual gifts, capable of bringing much benefit and grace.

"But if they are not used correctly, they can become the very opposite."

2013-05-03 Lee Berman replying to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that the name Brook Madregot fits a character who serves only as a conduit, citing Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's quoted definition of madregot as supernatural revelation and gifts. Also suspects Tzadkiel's lingering laughter as Severian leaves, and says Severian's misuse of Brook Madregot lands him in Apu Punchau's time.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theology-and-religion · theories: Brook Madregot serves only as a conduit

Thanks, Dan'l. Very informative. It would appear Wolfe chose well for the name Brook Madregot. It would appear to serve no purpose except as a conduit. Tinkerbell Tzadkiel there is very helpful to Severian, but her lingering laughter as Severian departs always makes me feel a bit suspicious.

Hm, yes. Severian's wanton abuse of Brook Madregot lands him in the time of Apu Punchau, and a lifestyle quite lacking in grace and refinement.
