Latro and the Persian army

Urth list · 4 messages · 2003-06-13 to 2003-06-16

2003urthAndrew BollenA. Bin TalalJames WynnChris P
The thread: Readers asked where Latro fits in the Persian army at Plataea, given his injury and his supposed offense to Demeter, and whether the Spartans' fight near a shrine could match his story. Chris proposed either a cursed Eleusis episode or service in Mardonius' elite guard, and the Wynns backed the bodyguard idea, citing Praetorian-style foreign guards and Latro's Zoroastrian knowledge. A. Bin Talal rejected the Demeter-curse reading as unsupported by the text and floated a Roman-republic foreshadowing. Andrew Bollen then argued from the Thermopylae vision that Latro commanded Romans near the king in the Immortals' absence. The thread stayed on speculative reconstruction and did not settle on a single answer.
  • Chris: Latro may have been cursed at Eleusis or served in Mardonius' elite guard at Plataea
  • A. Bin Talal: No textual hints support a Demeter curse; Latro foreshadows the Roman republic
  • The Wynns: Latro likely served in the Persian contingent near Mardonius, as an Immortal officer
  • Andrew Bollen: Latro commanded Romans near the king in the Immortals' absence, per the Thermopylae vision

Themes: Memory and forgetting, Power, rulers and politics, War and soldiers

2003-06-13 Chris P #

Asks whether Latro's injury and offense to Demeter can fit the battle of Plataea, given Herodotus' account of the Spartans fighting near the shrine while the Persian contingents split. Raises two explanations: Latro was cursed at Eleusis, a different shrine sacked earlier, or he was among Mardonius' elite guard, citing Herodotus' description of the battle.
questions · source-and-allusion · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Latro was cursed at Eleusis before Plataea; Latro fought among Mardonius' bodyguard at Plataea

I've finally finished reading Herodotus this week, which was rewarding in and of itself, but somehow I couldn't help but keep my eye out for things concerning those historical characters which also appeared in Latro's story. And while this hasn't exactly inspired me to construct any bizarre theories about the story, there was one thing that struck me as curious that I wondered if anyone had an "obvious" explanation for.

Essentially, the assumption I made from the story is that Latro's injury and his offense to Demeter occurred during the battle of Plataea. Having read Herodotus' vague description of the action there, however, he provides some specifics that make this difficult to reconcile. The Greek army had split into two divisions, according to his account - the Spartans, who were engaged near the shrine of Demeter, and the Athenians who were some distance away. The Persian army also split, and it's stated that native Persians led by Mardonius pursued the Spartans, while the foreign contingent coming along after fought the Athenians & co. This implies that for Latro to have been fighting near the shrine of Demeter during the battle of Plataea, he must have been in the Persian contingent; not only that, he would have to have been in the leading elements among the Immortals and Mardonius' hand-picked bodyguard, since the rout began before the full Persian contingent was able to engage.

It's not impossible to resolve this, but it does seem to make a difference how you choose to explain it.

One of the first things that occurred to me was that Latro may have acquired his curse at a different shrine of Demeter - the Persians had sacked the one at Eleusis earlier, whereas at Plataea the shrine remained supposedly untouched. If this were the case then Latro may have lost his memory quite some time before the battle of Plataea where he received his head injury (which would then be incidental and misleading).

Another possibility is that Latro may really have been close to Mardonius. It doesn't seem unreasonable that he would be accorded some honor if he really was the one to kill Leonidas. And Latro's vague memory places him somewhere not too far away from Xerxes during the battle of Thermopylae, so perhaps he had a position of honor even back then. That, however, makes him a prominent enough figure that it seems unusual that no one thought to mention him in the various histories.

Any thoughts?

Chris

Then proposes that Latro foreshadows the Roman republic, arguing Nike follows him as she follows the Greeks, while noting Herodotus mentions no Roman contingent in the Persian army.

just like Latro.

2003-06-15 James Wynn #

Quotes Chris's argument that Latro must have served in the Persian contingent near Mardonius at Plataea, given the rout and his possible role in Leonidas's death. Responds to Crush, who agrees Latro was likely an Immortal officer, citing foreigners in bodyguard units like the Praetorian Guard and Latro's knowledge of Zoroastrianism as evidence he served in Persia.
supports · chronology-and-plot-logic · source-and-allusion · theories: Latro was an Immortal officer; Latro served in Persia before Egypt; Latro was near Mardonius at Plataea

Chris postulates:
...for Latro to have been fighting near the shrine of Demeter during the battle of Plataea, he must have been in the Persian contingent; not only that, he would have to have been in the leading elements among the Immortals and Mardonius' hand-picked bodyguard, since the rout began before the full Persian contingent was able to engage...Another possibility is that Latro may really have been close to Mardonius. It doesn't seem unreasonable that he would be accorded some honor if he really was the one to kill Leonidas. And Latro's vague memory places him somewhere not too far away from Xerxes during the battle of Thermopylae, so perhaps he had a position of honor even back then. That, however, makes him a prominent enough figure that it seems unusual that no one thought to mention him in the various histories.

Crush responds:
You have solved the puzzle yourself. Latro was an Immortal -- probably an officer among them on a tactical level. He could be merely an officer in the Persian military, but it is unlikely that a foreigner would be permitted such a distinction. Foreigners were, however, preferred among bodyguard units as they considered less corruptible (Wolfe himself states in the introduction) -- consider Rome's Praetorian Guard. How this particular Latin won a place with the Immortals is not explained, but Latro's knowledge of Zoroastrianism strongly suggests he spent some significant time in Persia (as opposed to being mustered in Egypt which the text suggests he went to first). I don't believe any of the Immortals were mentioned in Herodotus by name.

The quote from Herodotus at the beginning of SotM is from the battle of Plataea when Mardonius was killed. I would think this is where the story begins.
-- Crush

2003-06-16 Andrew Bollen #

Argues that Latro's Thermopylae vision shows him commanding a group of Romans placed near the king in the Immortals' absence, and that this explains how he could hold a post among the Persian forces. Grounds this in recalled details of the vision, including Romans fronting Leonidas's charge and Latro slaying Leonidas, and extends it to a likely Plataea role.
supports · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Latro commanded Romans in place of the Immortals; Latro personally slays Leonidas at Thermopylae

Doesn't this get kind of explained in Latro's vision at Thermopylae? I don't have time to search out the quote, but as I recall, we learn from this that Latro is captain of a group of Romans, which has been given a position of honor near the King, in the absence for whatever reason of the Immortals. They front the desperate charge by Leonidas' band; I think we can probably infer that Latro personally slays Leonidas.

It's not a stretch to imagine Mardonius granting the Romans an Immortals-like position on the field at Plataea, given this.
