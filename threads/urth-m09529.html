giant herb islands

Urth list · 5 messages · 2009-05-19 to 2009-05-19

2009urthThe Book of the Long SunMarc AraminiJames JordanJordon Flato
The thread: Marc Aramini argued that the floating islands on Blue's Waters are giant herbs rather than trees, and that the pit Horn falls into may be the maw of such a plant, citing a storm passage in chapter 6 where Horn and Babbie discover they had been walking on a giant herb. Jordon Flato questioned whether Aramini had confused the lake-and-monster island with the Pit Island, noting Horn does not yet have Seawrack then, and later argued from Horn's own wording that the floating-plant island is too alien to be repeated unremarked. Aramini partly conceded confusion, saying he was away from his books, and continued to claim the islands are partly giant trees. The thread ended without resolution, drifting toward a comparison with Life of Pi.
  • Marc Aramini: the blue islands are giant herbs, possibly partly trees, and the pit may be a plant's maw
  • Jordon Flato: Aramini confused the lake island with the Pit Island, and Horn's description makes the plant island too distinctive to be recurring

Themes: Ghosts, dreams and visions, Names and their meanings

2009-05-19 Marc Aramini #

Argues that the floating islands on Blue's Waters are giant herbs rather than trees, and that the pit Horn falls into may be the maw of one such herb. Cites a passage in chapter 6 of On Blue's Waters where a storm reveals the islands to be giant herbs that Horn and Babbie had walked on believing it to be ground.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Island of the pit is a giant herb; Pit Horn falls into is a plant maw

a few weeks ago I made the claim the island of the pit on blue was composed of trees. I am always travelling and so did not have access to my books until now. On page 161 of oBW, chapter 6, the storm shakes up the islands and reveals to Horn that they are actually composed of &quot;giant herbs&quot; that are not trees or ferns but have the nature of them, and that Babbie and he walked on the branches and leaves believing them to be ground.

Just a bit more hinting that the pit Horn falls in might be the maw of one such ocean growing herb.

2009-05-19 Jordon Flato replying to Marc Aramini #

Questions whether Marc Aramini has mixed up two different islands in On Blue's Waters, arguing that the giant-herb island with the lake and monster that attacks Babbie and Horn is not the Pit Island. Points out that Horn does not yet have Seawrack at that point, and recalls the Pit Island as a more standard island.
questions · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic

Aren't you confusing two different Islands? The one you are talking about is the one with the lake which contains the monster that attacks Babbie and He. He doesn't even have Seawrack at that point. The Pit Island I remember as more of a standard Island.

Is that incorrect?

2009-05-19 Marc Aramini replying to Marc Aramini #

Argues that the islands on Blue are partly made of giant trees, so the island with the pit that Horn falls into may also be vegetable, citing a storm passage on page 161 of On Blue's Waters where giant herbs are revealed. Responds to Jordon Flato's objection that he is confusing the lake island with the pit island, and says he was away from his books.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Pit island is a giant ocean-growing herb

I merely believe it is evidence that some of the land mass of the islands are composed of giant trees.? Whether or not it is that one, there are islands made up of trees on Blue, which means that the other island chain with the pit might be equally vegetable in nature, whether it is the same one Horn sees or not. ?

2009-05-19 Jordon Flato replying to Marc Aramini #

Argues that Horn's description of the floating-plant island is so alien that he would notice when later islands are the same kind, pushing back on Marc Aramini's claim that the blue islands are giant trees. Grounds the view in Horn's own wording about the island, then shifts to asking whether anyone has read Life of Pi, which also has a floating-plant island.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Floating island is not composed of giant trees

It just seems to me that Horn gives a very 'alien' description of the Island that is made out of floating plants then he gives for any other Island. It seems clear he would, after that point, be able to notice the difference.

In any event, it is an interesting subject!

Has any else read Life of Pi? I'm always struck by how that book also has an Island made up of floating plants....
