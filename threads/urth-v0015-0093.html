1998-07-02 mary whalen #

Proposes that the technology in Tracking Song forms a progression of mink, min and man, with the endieva rod, the Min cyborgs and the Great Sleigh's terraforming power as stages. Links this to the Greek kybernetes (helmsman) behind 'cybernetic' and to KUL, helmsman of the Egg in Hellish Hues, asking why the name ends in L and whether the idea is overreaching.
proposes · source-and-allusion · puzzle-solving · theories: Technology progresses mink-min-man in Tracking Song; KUL linked to kybernetes helmsman

This is Sean Whalen (prion).

Just an odd little thought about technology in the story Tracking Song. Cim, who's like a mink, has a mysterious rod (endieva) that probably uses electricity or poison (that's how little we know about it). Min are cyborgs (or anti-cyborgs) who possess excellent but old (from our future, though) technology. The men in the Great Sleigh have very powerful technology, and are able to terraform an entire planet by raising its temperature. Wouldn't this make the chart of the progression of technology read: mink-min-man? Progression of knowledge by variation of letters?

Oh, this reminds me. Since I used the word cybernetic organism just now, I remembered the discussion on the story Hellish Hues. Kybernetes (from which cybernetic comes) is Greek for helmsman, and now relates to robots. This reminds me of the helmsman of the Egg in the story, KUL. They seem to fit together, and in Greek there is no difference between the letters u and y (so kybernetes is the same as kubernetes). Anyone know why there's an L at the end? Anyone think I'm reaching too far?

1998-07-07 mary whalen #

Argues that Cutthroat's opening remark about someone coming for him foreshadows his death at the end of Tracking Song, and that he is not an animal-person disguised in Sleigher clothing. Cites his memories of parties, his use of equipment, his unfamiliarity with local flora and fauna, and his calling the coat 'the captain's coat' as evidence.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Cutthroat's opening recording foreshadows his death; Cutthroat is not an animal-person disguised as a Sleigher

This is Sean Whalen (prion).

I recently reread Tracking Song and have seen some points that I don't think have been addressed earlier.

First, at the very beginning of the story, Cutthroat says that he's using the device to record his words so that if someone comes for him and he's dead, they'll understand what happened to him, and "I feel that someone may, though I do not know why." This seems to be a precognition of what happens at the end of the story, and perhaps shows that he did die at the end.

Second, I'm fairly sure that he's not an animal-person dressed in a Sleigher's clothes, as he remembers parties, etc., knows how to use some of his equipment, and isn't familiar with the plant and animal life in the area.

I don't know how I missed this before, but Cutthroat clearly calls the insulated clothing he's wearing "the captain's coat."

1998-07-08 mary whalen #

Proposes that the folktale "How the Rabbit Lost His Tail" parallels Tracking Song, with the rabbit as Cutthroat and his powerful brother as someone on the Great Sleigh, and the recording device as a call for help. Grounds this in the tale's pattern of the rabbit calling his brother from trouble, and speculates the clothes-changing may show whether Cutthroat is human.
proposes · myth-and-structure · source-and-allusion · theories: Cutthroat parallels the rabbit in the folktale; Great Sleigh speeds up to rescue Cutthroat; Clothes-changing shows Cutthroat may be human-headed animal

This is Sean Whalen (prion).

In the same book, there is a story called "How the Rabbit Lost His Tail." In it, the rabbit has a brother who has amazing powers. The rabbit goes west to find adventure, and his brother says he will come to help him when he calls, even when he is busy. The rabbit keeps meeting people, being foolish, and changing clothes with the people he meets. Everytime this happens he calls his brother who takes care of it.

This seems to relate to Tracking Song, too. If the rabbit is like Cutthroat and his brother is someone on the Great Sleigh, the recording/transmitting device is the means by which he lets the sled know if he's in trouble. Perhaps the GS does increase its speed so that it can reach Cutthroat sooner. I am not sure exactly how the clothes-changing relates to this, but it may indicate if Cutthroat's a human or not (or a human head on an animal's body).

1998-07-14 mary whalen #

Proposes that every animal tribe in "Tracking Song" is an anthropomorphic creature with a living counterpart and a larger Ice Age analogue in North America, listing guesses for each tribe such as Wiggikki as wolf/dire wolf. Grounds the guesses in the story's descriptions and in a comparison of the Min with Native American bear stories, while admitting uncertainty about several tribes and Cutthroat.
proposes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Animal tribes are anthropomorphic versions of Ice Age beasts; The Min are bears from Native American bear legends

This is Sean Whalen (prion).

Doesn't anyone want to discuss "Tracking Song"? Oh, well.

Well, anyway, after I recently reread it it seemed to me that every mammal is an anthropomorphic creature that has a living cognate as well as an analogous Ice Age creature of larger form, both living in North America.

Tribe: current animal/Ice Age animal

Wiggikki: wolf/dire wolf
Lenizee: deer/?
Nashhwonk: moose/?
Ketin(cha): lion(ess)/cave lion(ess)
Pamigaka: boar/?
Pummanga: ?gopher/?
Mimmunka: lynx/smilodon (sabre-toothed tiger)
(Cim's t.): mink/giant mink
(vampires): bat/?
Min: ?bear/?cave bear
(snow monkey variety 1): ?rabbit, chipmunk, mouse/?
(snow monkey variety 2): squirrel/?
(tall thin woman): ?/?
(bear-like thing): ?/?
Cutthroat: ?man, rabbit

I think the Min are probably bears because of the similarity they have with the Native American bear stories I've read (a bear with half it's body made of flint, living in a cave, causing trouble for the hero). I'm not completely sure, though.

Cutthroat may be a native man, a man from the Great Sleigh, or some man whose head has been attatched to a rabbit man's body. I'm really not sure.

Pummanga never appears in the story, so this is only a guess.

Cutthroat says that there are several varieties of the snow monkeys, with big fluffy tails. I think they're just as human as the other animal tribes, just smaller and less intelligent.

Be alert for my weapon analysis!

By the way, are all of my letters getting through and being sent? I've had several that haven't been answered by anyone. Am I doing something wrong? Am I just sending too many?
