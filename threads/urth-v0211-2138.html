The Claw

Urth list · 3 messages · 2002-11-18 to 2002-11-18

2002urthThe Book of the New SunDan'l Danehy-OakesRoy C. Lackey
The thread: Roy C. Lackey and Dan'l Danehy-Oakes debated how Severian's Claw heals, on 2002-11-18. Lackey argued the Claw cannot simply reverse time to an earlier healthy state, citing Herena's birth-defect arm and the failure to restore Jonas and Jolenta. Danehy-Oakes proposed the Claw works only on natural persons, so artificial figures like Jonas and Jolenta cannot be restored. Lackey countered that a wooden-legged man would be restorable under time-reversal, so artificiality does not fit that model. The exchange stayed between these two posters and ended without resolution.
  • Roy C. Lackey: the Claw cannot be a simple time-reversal, since Herena's congenital arm had no healthy earlier state and Jonas and Jolenta were not restored
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: the Claw heals only natural persons, so the failures with Jonas and Jolenta are explained by their artificial natures
  • Roy C. Lackey: an artificiality explanation conflicts with time-reversal, since a wooden-legged man would be restorable under that model

Themes: Robots, androids and artificial life, Time loops and causality, Suffering and sacrifice

2002-11-18 Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that the Claw cannot work by reversing time to before an injury, since Herena's withered arm was a birth defect with no earlier healthy state. Supports this with the cases of Jonas, who could not be restored even to his pre-zapped condition, and Jolenta, whom Severian knew both before and after her bite.

indicates where the vanished people have gone - someWHEN else.<<

A quibble about the Claw: it has been suggested often before that the Claw (or Sev) worked by reaching back in time to a point before whatever malady it was being used to remedy had come about. Restoring Dorcas to life by returning her body to a time when she was still alive; healing Declan's swollen abdomen by returning his body to a time before he had appendicitis. But this doesn't work for Herena. She had "a withered arm no larger than an infant's". In other words, it was a birth defect. There had never been a time when her arm had been well and whole.

It also didn't work for Jonas. Sev couldn't even restore him to the condition he had been in before he got zapped, much less reach back in time to restore him to the way he was before his ship crashed. Didn't work for Jolenta, either, though Sev had known her both before she was bitten and before she was Jolenta.

-Roy

2002-11-18 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Proposes that Severian's healing power works only on natural persons and not on artificial ones, suggesting Jonas and Jolenta fail to be restored because both are artificial. Grounds this in the shared artificial nature of Jonas (the chem-like figure) and Jolenta, who began as a natural person before Doctor Talos enhanced her.

2002-11-18 Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that the Claw's failure to restore Jonas and Jolenta cannot be explained by time-reversal alone, since a man with a wooden leg would be restorable if the Claw worked by reaching back in time. Responds to Dan'l's view that artificiality explains the failures by questioning whether the prosthetic objection fits the claim that the Claw reverses time.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: the Claw works by reversing time to an earlier point; artificiality explains why the Claw failed on Jonas and Jolenta

Dan'l quoted:

it seems to me that the common factor between Jonas and Jolenta -- aside from their names beginning with the syllable "Jo" -- would consist in their both having an artificial nature (though Jolenta _begins_ as a natural person, Doctor T. artifically enhances her ... sort of the Pamela Anderson of posthistory).

I, too, recall such discussion, and the artificiality factor may be valid for the failures. What I was addressing, however, was the contention that the Claw worked by reaching back in time to a point before the pending problem. If the Claw's power was a function of time, why should the presence of prosthetics present a problem? Would it not work for a man with a wooden leg who was wounded woefully by a weapon?

-Roy
