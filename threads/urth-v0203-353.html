Green, possible worldship

Urth list · 1 messages · 2002-03-14 to 2002-03-14

2002urthThe Book of the New SunMichael Andre-Driussi

Themes: The dying sun and the cosmos

2002-03-14 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Proposes that Green in The Book of the Long Sun may be a worldship, smaller than Yesod and somewhere between a starship and a worldship, rather than a terraformed world. Grounds this in Paul Park's Starbridge Chronicles, where the planet Paradise moves erratically and is later revealed to be a moon with a space drive, and in Wolfe's large ships such as Yesod and the Ship's shadow at Apu-Punchau.
proposes · close-textual-reading · genre-and-craft · theories: Green is a smaller worldship

Another possibility that I don't think we ever discussed around here is that Green might be so far removed from "nature" that, rather than thinking of it as a world or a terraformed world, we might think of it as a worldship ala Yesod, perhaps a smaller version . . . somewhere between starship Tzadkiel and worldship Yesod.

Because, for example, in reading Paul Park's "Starbridge Chronicles," there was enough orbital information for me to figure out that the planet of the romance was one of the outer gas giants of our solar system. Yet the movement of the world called "Paradise" was so erratic as to be a worldship. (This was confirmed in an interview with Park: Paradise is the Moon fitted out with a space drive, tooling around the system.)

In Wolfe's case we have clear indications of ships being surprisingly big: the miracle of Apu-Punchau produced by the Ship's shadow, the worldship Yesod, etc.

If Green is a worldship, then we could remove many of the constraints we have on it, but those same constraints provide the bricks with which we build and test theories about Green.
