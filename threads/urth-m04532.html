2008-04-24 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes replying to Pavel Bakič #

Argues that the name Urth carries several layers of meaning: Urd, the eldest Norn, a variant Wyrd meaning fate, and the destined world, with Lune's name suggesting Luna and lunacy. Responds to Pavel Bakič's Czech translation question by tracing the Triple Goddess pattern, where Urth moves from Mother to Crone and back to Maiden as Ushas. The post offers name-based associations and mythic parallels rather than direct textual quotation.
proposes · source-and-allusion · myth-and-structure · theories: Urth is the destined world; Urth cycles through Triple Goddess phases

Pavel,

There's a fair amount packed into the name. Urth or Urdr or Urd is the name of the eldest of the three Norns, the Norse equivalent of the Fates. The names of the other two, Skuld and Verthandi, are given to Mars and Venus in the New Sun epoch.

But there's more: another variant on the name of Urd is Wurd, or Wyrd, which means, simply, fate or destiny. Urth is in some sense the destined world.

And, as long as we're poking at names, let's not forget Lune, whose name is of course a variation of Luna, Moon, but which also suggests to an English speaker a Loon, or Lunatic, a madman (or woman).

The Moon itself, because of its phases, has traditionally been taken to hold in itself all three phases of the Triple Goddess, of whom the Fates/Norns are one variation/version, not to be confused with the Furies and the Graces, who are also incarnations of the Triple Goddess (indeed, the nine Muses may be also, depending on whom you ask).

The Three are Maiden, Mother, and Crone, and one of the significances of "Urth" is that Mother Earth is no longer a Mother, she is now a Crone; she is barren. Her function is to cut the thread that Skuld spins and Verthandi dyes.

When Urth becomes Ushas, she becomes the Maiden once again.

2008-04-24 Ron Crown replying to Pavel Bakič #

Argues that a Czech rendering of "Urth" built on "Urzem" would reverse Wolfe's intent, since Ur in German signals antiquity and distance in time. Grounds this in the German edition keeping "Urth", the French "Teur", and the Christ/Apollo sun-son wordplay that resists translation into French.
disputes · source-and-allusion · genre-and-craft

Pavel,

If Ur means in Czech what it does in German, viz., "a long time ago," "originally," then the connotation of "Urzeme" is directly the opposite of what Wolfe was trying to achieve with "Urth" by distancing from our customary term "Earth" to underscore the distance in time from us. Translation is always tricky and I've long wondered how the unusual words that Wolfe used in the Book of the New Sun would be translated. I've got both German and French editions of the Book but haven't managed to work all the way through them yet. The German edition does use "Urth," however, while the French uses "Teur" which does something similar to "Terre" that "Urth" does to "Earth."

A lot of Wolfe's nuances would, I think, be virtually impossible to convey in another language, for example, how do you convey the Christ/Apollo connotations of the New Sun/Son? German at least has a degree of assonance (Sonne/Sohn) but French (fils, soleil)? Ron Crown

2008-04-24 Gwern Branwen replying to Pavel Bakič #

Argues that Urth is either an obscure English variant of Earth or a transliteration of the Norse norn Urd, depending on whether Wolfe intended the Norse reference. Answers Pavel Bakič's Czech translation question: the OED shows nothing special, so translators should either hunt for a literary Earth-word or keep Urth as transliteration.

2008-04-24 Dave Lebling replying to Pavel Bakič #

Argues that Wolfe's spelling of Urth works in English only because English spelling and pronunciation diverge, so a Czech rendering needs a pun or allusion rather than simply adding "ur" to "zem". Suggests Czech resonances, future language evolution, Slavic gods of the Earth-Mars-Venus triad, and the present/gift pun as models, while finding "Urzem" acceptable for Wolfe's ambiguity about ancestry. Replies to Pavel Bakič's request for native-speaker connotations of "Urth" in Czech.
answers · source-and-allusion · author-and-biography

Pavel,

Wolfe's substitution of a "U" for "EA" in English works well because English spelling and English pronunciation are so dissimilar. I don't know if the opportunity exists in Czech; my limited knowledge of it indicates words are mostly pronounced just as they are spelled. The best opportunity would seem to be a pun of some sort on "zem?"; just adding "ur" to it seems too unsubtle.

Is there a Czech word similar to "zem?" that has interesting resonances, the way "Urth" suggests the Norns, Norse mythology in general, language evolution (a future English-speaker might spell "Earth" as "Urth" -- so this is a joke about pulp SF's take on future language evolution), etc? How would one expect Czech to evolve in a million years?

Is there a set of old Slavic gods or goddesses associated with the Earth-Mars-Venus triad? Is there an allusive pun to be made like the "present"="gift" pun Wolfe makes? (I know people once made much of the fact that in German "gift"="poison", for example.)

I think the addition of "Ur" (if Czech speakers would "get it") isn't bad at all, as it goes along with Wolfe's caginess about whether we are the ancestors or the descendants of the people of Urth.

-- Dave Lebling

2008-04-24 Andy Robertson replying to Pavel Bakič #

Adds to the discussion of the word Urth by arguing that a pun links Verdandi, Skuld and Urd, the three norns, to the double meaning of English "present" via Vodalus's remark that Mars was called "The Gift." Asks whether this wordplay can be carried into other languages, responding to Pavel Bakič's Czech translation question about Urth.
supports · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Verdandi pun on English present

To add to what's been already stated: there's another pun in the Urth/Skuld/Verdandi triad of names. We learn from Vodalus in the wood that Verdandi (Mars) was called "The Gift" by the dawn-men who first went there. But gift=present and Verdandi is the norn of the present, as Skuld of the future and Urd of the past. So the pun is on the double meaning of "present" in English. I wonder if this can be carried over to other languages?

Briah is the second of the four cabalistic worlds, philosophically the one that relates to the instantiated Platonic forms, the world of creation and immanence. So Severian traveling along the Madrigot represents him traveling through levels of spiritual development in existence as well as through his own subjective experience of time. He's the flowing back of spiritual completeness from beyond Yesod that bridges Briah with Malkhut.
