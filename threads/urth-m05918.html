What's So Great About Ushas?

Urth list · 3 messages · 2008-07-17 to 2008-07-18

2008urthThe Book of the New SunJeff WilsonDave TallmanPaul B
The thread: Readers debated whether the Hierogrammates' stellar engineering in Wolfe's Ushas was an evil plan or an imperfect act with unintended results. Dave Tallman argued the white and black hole effects on Urth were mainly thermal and sea-level changes, that the Hierogrammates were imperfect rather than absolutely evil, and that mass evacuation was impractical. Jeff Wilson countered that a white fountain of moon-like mass would raise violent tides without greatly altering Urth's orbit, citing the tidal force equation. Paul B disputed Tallman's reading that Baldanders only expected the bells to ring, citing the phrase about the bells tolling continents, and argued the Hierogrammates knew the outcome and deserve harsh moral judgment. The thread ended with the positions still apart and no clear agreement.
  • Dave Tallman: Hierogrammates were imperfect, not evil; effects on Urth were mainly thermal and sea-level changes
  • Jeff Wilson: a moon-mass white fountain would raise violent tides, so gravitational effects were not negligible
  • Paul B: Baldanders knew the outcome; the Hierogrammates deserve harsh moral judgment while still being moral equals

Themes: The dying sun and the cosmos, Gods, angels and the Increate, Justice, torture and mercy

2008-07-17 Dave Tallman #

Disputes Paul B's claim that the Hierogrammates' stellar engineering reflects an evil plan, arguing instead that white and black hole effects on Urth were mainly thermal and sea-level changes. Supports the view of the Hierogrammates as imperfect rather than absolutely evil, and questions whether mass evacuation of Ushas would have been practical given panic and trust in cacogens.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: Stellar engineering was an economic sanction against Typhon; Hierogrammates were imperfect but well-meaning

Even considering the physical implications of your suggestion, you make a preposterous claim here without any evidence. Presumably a white hole is the only way to remove the black one. What would it matter how much time passes? The physical anomalies produced by the New Sun don't care, unless the black hole got a lot bigger after a few hundred years. Given that it's just had the sun to feed on, which it didn't even consume, it's unlikely.

Incidentally, it's curious that the introduction of the black hole didn't have similar effects on Urth as that of the white one.

As for the suggestion that Hierogrammates went to stellar engineering just to depose Typhon, it sounds like swatting a fly with a Buick.

There are two possible mechanisms for the white hole to disrupt earth: thermal and gravitational. I believe the gravitational effects were practically nothing: Baldanders only expected them to ring bells. He predicted the Urth would have problems due to rising sea levels (ice plus energy gives water) and to the heat causing shifts in the earth's crust. The latter is hard to understand, but I suppose some unstable faults could be triggered by changes in heat.

If the gravitational effects were really severe for either the white or the black hole, then the Earth's orbit would be perturbed. We have no hint that this was the case, and if it were it would cause even more severe problems for Ushas.

Near Typhon's time, the Sun hadn't lost that much heat, so the sea level hadn't dropped much due to increased ice at the poles. People would not have settled in the new low-lying areas. (It's interesteing that the Citadel was usable in Typhon's time. Was the wall perhaps a sea-wall, and the river changed course to flow through later?)

The stellar engineering to depose Typhon was huge in its technological power but subtle in its immediate effects. It might be thought of as an economic sanction. Coming in with guns blazing would wipe out more innocents in the short term.

Is there a problem with this, even if true? I know some religions that consider everyone to be a sinner.

It allows us to consider the Hierogrammates as imperfect but doing the best they could, like the rest of us, instead of absolutely good or absolutely evil, as we have tended to go in this discussion.

You seem to conveniently forget that Hierogrammates do possess foresight, in addition to tech that allows creation of black holes. These two alone should suggest they can do pretty much any "mundane" thing (here, building large ship(s) - [if Typhon could do it, they could too] and sending it to point Urth) at pretty much any time, no?

I'll allow that they could get together a lot of ships, especially if they only needed to get people off-planet temporarily and then dropped them back when Ushas had settled down. But there's still the issue of panic, and of people's willingness to go on the say-so of cacogens. I don't think it would have been as easy as you think.

2008-07-17 Jeff Wilson replying to Dave Tallman #

Argues that the White Fountain, if near Urth and of moon-like mass, would raise violent tides without greatly changing Urth's orbit, since tidal force falls off with the inverse cube of distance and grows with the object's size. Responds to Dave Tallman's claim that gravitational effects were practically nothing by citing the Wikipedia tidal force equation and noting Lune's existing perturbation of Urth's orbit.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: White Fountain would produce violent tides without altering Urth's orbit

Urth's orbit is already perturbed by the process of moving of Lune closer to it. The White Fountain could be of similar mass to the moon, which produces the majority of tides on the earth, and approach much more closely to create much more violent tides, but still not alter the overall orbit of the earth as much, because it is only about 1/80 of the earth's mass, like the moon is.

While direct gravitational force drops off as the inverse square of distance and irrespective of size, the derived tidal forces drop off with the inverse cube of distance, making distance much more important, but increase with in proportion to the size of the object, making bells tinkle, mountains buckle, and planets sunder. You can see for your self here

http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidal_force

where the final equation plainly has a total of 3 powers of R (distance radius) in the denominator, and delta r (the radius of the far object) in the numerator.

2008-07-18 Paul B replying to Dave Tallman #

Disputes Dave Tallman's claim that Baldanders only expected the bells to ring, citing the phrase that the bells toll the deaths of continents and Baldanders' explanation of waves of gravity. Argues that Baldanders knew the outcome and that a deontological framework judges the Hierogrammates' killing of most of a planet's people harshly, while still treating them as moral equals.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theology-and-religion · theories: Baldanders knew the bells would kill continents

I'm sorry, this is dead wrong. The actual phrase was "[the bells] toll the deaths of continents". Baldanders knew perfectly well what would happen, and he explains that "waves of gravity" are the cause.

I've actually been advocating for putting them on an equal moral footing with humans, not demonizing them outright. That comes later! Killing the vast majority of a planet's population of your moral equals reflects badly on you. As I've insisted, a responsible moral framework (deontology) doesn't much care what the outcome of your actions is.

Paul
