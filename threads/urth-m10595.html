The thread: The thread began with James Wynn and David Stockhoff discussing whether Silk and Horn in The Book of the Long Sun are one person or several, and whether the Dorcas riddle is an easy case compared with harder Wolfean riddles. Stockhoff then argued that hard riddles turn on relations between universes, so the Hieromakers, Jesus and Severian might be cognate figures across a restarting cosmos. Most of the debate between Stockhoff and António Marques concerned whether Yesod is the same universe as Briah, whether time is a property of each universe, and whether universes branch into possible futures. Marques argued that possible futures are just unknown rooms and that Yesod is simply higher than Briah, but the exchange ended without agreement, with Marques later questioning why he was asked to explain a claim he disbelieved.
  • James Wynn: Silk may be a clone, epitome or possessor body with Horn's memories also present; Tzadkiel traveled in Briah in the past so light from the White Fou
  • David Stockhoff: Hard riddles concern relations between universes; universes may branch and Yesod may be subspace or outside time; Ash and the Green Man represe
  • António Pedro Marques: Time is a property of each universe, so there are no possible futures across universes; Yesod is higher than Briah in the plain sense; As
  • Lee Berman: The Dorcas riddle is an easy standard riddle that readers solve, unlike harder Wolfean riddles such as the one Stockhoff raised

Themes: Doubles, twins and clones, The dying sun and the cosmos, Deep time and ruins

2010-08-12 James Wynn replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that Silk in The Book of the Long Sun is not a one-to-one mapping of a single person but may be a clone, epitome, or another body's possessor, with Horn's memories also present. Grounds the claim in Silk's suicide before Hyacinth's casket, the dream-traveling figure communing with the staff face, and Wolfe's concern with identity.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Silk is a psychic clone or cognate; Silk and Horn share memories and body

If it is a Wolfe novel, it is almost certainly not a 1 to 1 mapping. I the other fellow might have been a phyiscal or psychic clone. Or software epitome that was created long ago. It might be a potential future version that will never enter into the story. Or it might have been someone else in possession of the same body. Or it might have been an impostor who didn't realize that he was a psychic clone. Much of Wolfe's writing career has been picking away at the foundations of Identity.

This is an excellent example. I'm not convinced most people do understand what is going on. Silk is dead. He slashed his wrists and killed himself in front of Hyacinth's casket. Silk's body and memories are present. But Horn's memories are present as well. Wolfe would say they are both truly there. And then there is that "other" who communes with the face on his staff and can initiate dream-travels across Time and Space. Yeah, Silk is there. But I contend he not the way people seem to think he is.

Surely he is as much a "cognate" as a race of beings in a universe whose history and individuals are almost the same as another race of beings in another universe.

Wolfe is not just adding a dash of doubt. He is probing what it means to be the "I am". The cognate race is as much humanity as Jahlee was the Rajan's sister. As much as Silk was Tussah's son.

2010-08-12 David Stockhoff replying to James Wynn #

Argues that the hard-to-solve Wolfean riddles turn on relations between universes, so the Hieromakers, Jesus and Severian may be cognate or even the same figure across iterations of a restarting cosmos. Replies to James Wynn's point that Silk and Horn are not one-to-one mappings, accepting that identity is the book's concern while raising Yesod as possibly subspace.
proposes · theology-and-religion · myth-and-structure · theories: Hieromakers, Jesus and Severian are the same figure across universes; Yesod is subspace

That's exactly right. If the difference ever goes beyond just a shadowy past or a disguise and an assumed name, then it's always something outside the bounds of ordinary reality, and therefore one requiring assembly by the reader. And it's always a key to the narrative.

In this case, the relation between one universe and another is unknowable and almost magical; therefore, the relation between men of one creation and men of another is indefinable.

I personally have a hard time seeing the difference between a universe that is perpetually restarted in time and one that is infintely reborn under closely guided and identical conditions. In such a context, one can conclude that the race who made the Hieros were human beings like ourselves with our physiology and history---but whether they are in "our" future is unknowable. Whether they were in our iteration or a 99.9999999% identical one, past or future, is unknowable.

And this is why it is satisfying to consider which universe holds the Hieromakers, which holds Jesus, and which Severian, and the relation among them, and wonder if all three aren't the same---because if they aren't at least "cognate," then none of it matters.

Side topic---is Yesod merely what we know of as subspace?

2010-08-12 David Stockhoff replying to António Pedro Marques #

Argues that the Conciliator and the Hieromakers may belong to different possible futures within Briah, and that Yesod lies outside time so Aphet knows Severian is unborn without any calculation. Responds to António Pedro Marques's claim that the Hieros' makers were human and that Urth is a parallel rather than our future, defending the multiverse reading against his view.
disputes · theology-and-religion · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Conciliator and Hieromakers in different possible futures; Yesod is outside time, not before Briah; Briahs are all blooms of possible futures

Possibly. Or, depending on exactly how the universes relate, maybe the Conciliator is in one possible future, along with Master Ash and so on with other sub-possibilities, and the Hieromakers are in another. Both futures happened somewhere, and one future transcended Briah entirely. Their creatures bring Severian to Yesod, where the lack of time as we know it allows them to meet.

Again, I am not sure the explanation of universes as "blooming" (or any other metaphor that has been used) requires that we ignore the fact that all possible futures exist until they are no longer possible. And they are all Briah.

Jeff mentioned (IIRC) that for narrative reasons Yesod's being before Severian was born in Briah doesn't mean that Yesod is before Briah. That's obviously true but it's not mechanically helpful in terms of how the multiverses are constructed.

Yesod may be before Briah only in the sense that it is in some way outside time and therefore is the way it was at its birth. All the Briahs have fallen to time and are therefore always after Yesod.

Alternatively, it is simply on a plane where time is negligible, and the Ship's travels back and forth easily pass any Yesodi time point.

These seem the same, but in one, all the Briahs are more or less the same (blooms), and in the other, Severian's Briah runs alongside Yesod, which makes no sense to me. I prefer the first, which fits the description of Yesod as "higher."

Aphet's remark, therefore, may not have required her to check the universal clock, or take up the study of Severian's life, or run a time conversion to know. She knew Severian was unborn in Briah no matter how far or fast the Ship moved, because she's in Paradise and he's from Out There.
