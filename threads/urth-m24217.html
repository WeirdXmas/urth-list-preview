New Lafferty collection from Centipede Press

Urth list · 15 messages · 2013-12-02 to 2014-02-02

20132014urthThe Book of the New SunMatthew KingAndy RobertsonGwern BranwenFred KiescheDan'l Danehy-OakesAntonin ScriabinDaniel Petersen
The thread: Readers discussed the announced Centipede Press Lafferty collection, the first volume of the complete stories. Antonin Scriabin welcomed it as affordable access to hard-to-find stories while disliking the cover, and Daniel Otto Jack Petersen announced it and quoted Wolfe praising Lafferty as a genius. Fred Kiesche mocked editor John Pelan and explained a grievance over a press that took advance payments without delivering books, which drifted off-topic from Wolfe. Christopher Simon asked about Lafferty's supposed history of Rome, and Gwern Branwen and Petersen found it hard to separate standard history from embellishment. After the book arrived, Matthew King and Scriabin reported typos, missing running headers, and confusing prices, and the thread ended on physical-book complaints rather than the fiction.
  • Antonin Scriabin: welcomes the collection for affordable access but dislikes the cover and then reports typos and confusing prices
  • Daniel Otto Jack Petersen: announces the Lafferty Library and cites Wolfe's praise of Lafferty as most original writer
  • Fred Kiesche: mocks the editor and objects to Pelan's unfulfilled advance-payment orders
  • Gwern Branwen: reports the Rome history reads like nonfiction told novelistically, blurring fact and invention
  • Matthew King: confirms the physical quality but lists page-18 typesetting errors and missing running headers
  • Christopher Simon: asks others about Lafferty's history of Rome after a bookstore encounter

Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Myth and folklore sources, Genre: science fiction, fantasy, both

2013-12-02 Antonin Scriabin replying to Daniel Otto Jack Petersen #

Welcomes the announced Centipede Press Lafferty collection, saying reading R. A. Lafferty is rewarding and that the complete-stories volumes will give affordable access to stories otherwise hard to find. Responds to Daniel Otto Jack Petersen's announcement, adding a dislike of the cover design and hope for good binding quality.

Explains the grievance against a publisher named Pelan: he ran a press that took advance payment for books, including a Clifford D. Simak story anthology, and never delivered them. 2013-12-02 Daniel Petersen #

Announces Centipede Press's first volume of R. A. Lafferty's complete stories, the Lafferty Library, and links to a blog discussing its contents. Also shares a blog post quoting Wolfe's essay praising Lafferty as a genius and 'our most original writer'.
informs

I know this list occasionally gets used for Lafferty news as well (rightly so, since Wolfe considers Lafferty and actual and true genius), so I just wanted to make sure folks new Centipede Press is putting out the first fine volume of Lafferty's complete stories (they're calling it the Lafferty Library). They say each volume will feature 'a guest introduction by a notable author in the field of fantastic fiction' (the first is by noted Wolfe fan Michael Swanwick). I hope they'll get Wolfe to do one!

http://www.centipedepress.com/sf/manmademodels.html

I discuss the pros and cons of the particular table of contents they've revealed on my Lafferty blog:

http://antsofgodarequeerfish.blogspot.co.uk/2013/12/complete-lafferty-library-volume-1.html

Also, in case you've never seen where Wolfe calls Lafferty 'our most original writer' (he calls him a genius in a different essay), a few years ago I copied out Wolfe's essay from Castle of Days here:

http://antsofgodarequeerfish.blogspot.co.uk/2011/10/its-great-to-be-young-and-in-danger.html

Take care.

2013-12-02 Antonin Scriabin replying to Daniel Otto Jack Petersen #

Recounts meeting a stranger in a used bookstore who was browsing a Lafferty collection, Strange Doings, and mentions her favorite is a supposed history of Rome by Lafferty. Asks whether anyone has read that Rome history and what they think of it.

I met a Lafferty fan in the flesh the other day. I was at a used bookstore and saw a woman paging through Strange Doings, then she placed it back on the shelf. She mentioned her favorite was a history of Rome written by Lafferty. Has anyone read it? Thoughts?

2014-02-01 Matthew King replying to Antonin Scriabin #

Reports that the poster's Centipede Press Lafferty collection has typesetting errors on page 18, including a misspelled title character name and a missing word, and lacks running headers. Responds to Antonin Scriabin's earlier praise of the physical volume by confirming the quality while listing these flaws and expressing disappointment.
informs

I received mine as well. The physical quality is as Antonin described.

I was displeased to come upon three errors on the second page of the first story (18). The name of the titular character is twice given as Jan instead of Jon, and there's a word missing from a sentence immediately after that. Very disappointing in a volume priced for collectors.

Moreover, the pages have no headers; there is text, there are page numbers at the bottom, but that is all. In this kind of a collection of short stories, I expect to see the title of each story at the top of the appropriate pages.

It's Lafferty, so I still plan to buy every volume they publish, but my goodwill for their effort is diminished. Makes me wish I had had $70K in hand when his estate was up for sale.
