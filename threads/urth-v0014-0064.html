1998-06-20 adam louis stephanides #

Argues that the essential matter of The Book of the New Sun is Severian's personality rather than its setting, so The Urth of the New Sun is complementary and does not change the book's basic meaning. Responds to Spectacled Bear's claim that puzzles in BotNS should be answered within BotNS, and agrees in principle while weighing novel evidence against interview evidence.
supports · genre-and-craft · theories: Severian's personality is the essential matter of BotNS

I neglected to say that I basically do agree that the answers to interpretative questions about a work of fiction should ideally be deducible from that work itself. Thus I prefer evidence from the novels to evidence from interviews, and in interpreting BotNS I would prefer evidence from BotNS to evidence from _Urth_.

I was thinking more about the relationship between BotNS and _Urth_, and came to the conclusion that this problem is not as serious as it might be, because BotNS and _Urth_ are basically complementary. What I mean by this is that, IMO, the essential "matter" of BotNS is Severian's personality, as revealed both by the tale he tells and by the way he tells it; the setting of Urth and Briah-Yesod, while of great interest, is essentially background material. Thus, while _Urth_ may contain information about the New Sun cosmos which contradicts the best inferences we could have drawn from BotNS, this doesn't change the basic meaning of BotNS. Conversely, _Urth_ is essentially about this cosmos, and doesn't tell us much that's new about Severian's personality (which may be one reason why some people find it a disappointment).

--Adam
