A Little Dotty? M*MILLION artifact of character set

Urth list · 1 messages · 1999-12-22 to 1999-12-22

1999urthMitch Bailey

1999-12-22 Mitch Bailey #

Explains that the poster meant the plate's dot as a mid-height bullet, which Number Five realizes is a multiplication sign rather than a period, so the plate reads M*MILLION. Responds to Dan's suggestion that it reads M. Million, and notes the asterisk stands in for the bullet because email text cannot show one; also apologizes for an HTML mishap.
answers · close-textual-reading · theories: The plate reads M*MILLION with a mid-height multiplication dot

Dan,
"
Hmmm, I always assumed the plate read: M . Million

Sometimes a dot is used to indicate multiplication. "Monsieur" is often abbreviated to "M."
"

You're right, of course. I would actually have preferred to use a "bullet" dot floating at mid-line height for this, but of course the email character set wouldn't support it, so far as I know. But that's how _I_ envisioned what Number Five saw, with his hungover revelation proceeding when he realizes that the dot is not a period but a dot at half-height in the line of text.
I tried to approximate it with an asterisk, which of course is also used as a multiplication sign, esp. in most programming languages. BTW I apologize to all for my mishap last couple of posts; I didn't realize my new email software defaulted to HTML.
