Themes: Gnosticism, heresy and other theologies

2001-05-01 John Bishop #

Lays out a five-point account of what 'Gnostic point of view' means: hidden knowledge for the literate, coded truth in fables, a demiurge creator, the body as imperfect, and moral inversion with a world made by Satan. Notes that point 1 and most of point 2 are shared across Gnostic sects while point 5 belongs to the Bogomils, and closes with a remark on 'good' villains.
proposes · theology-and-religion

I think "Gnostic point of view" means the following:

1) The literal "gnosis": the idea that since most
people are illiterate or semi-literate peasants
and artificers, they can't understand abstractions
and complexities, and so are told the truths of
religion in the form of pretty stories about people
and fish and so on. But for those who can understand, there is a real knowledge beyond the pretty fables.

2) The coding: that real knowledge is encoded in the
pretty fables for any perceptive, alert reader to see, because it was put there on purpose for that reason.

3) The knowledge itself, part 1: that the God of the
Testament is just another created being of great
power, a demiurge, and the real creator is behind
and above him; part 2: that the world of the senses
is therefore not real, not the world created by God
in the stories, but something less; we live in an
imitation world created by an imitation God (the
demiurge).

4) Body issues: that therefore the body is imperfect
like the world it belongs to and to be "risen above", cue chastity, mortification and so on.

5) Moral inversion: that the demiurge is evil, or at least non-good, we therefore live in a world created by
Satan.

Anyway, that's Gnosticsm and some associated ideas, off the top of my head. All of the shades of Gnosticism would have point 1 and most point 2, but the others are less shared. Point 5 is the heresy of the Bogomils, for example.

Re "good" villians: the Draka in fiction and Rome in actual fact, with the Vikings coming in second place because even their romaticized image admits that they were a bunch of thieving, raping thugs.

-John Bishop

2001-05-01 Kevin J. Maroney #

Adds to John Bishop's summary of a Gnostic viewpoint by stating the missing element: every person holds a spark of divine breath that, through knowledge and self-abnegation, can rejoin the greater God beyond the created world. Offers this as a general account of the doctrine, without tying it to a specific passage of Wolfe's books.
supports · theology-and-religion

Good summary, with one thing omitted: That every person contains within himself (or herself) the spark of the divine breath. The point of "the knowledge" and the self-abnegation is to allow that spark to rejoin the greater God outside the created world. Direct contact with the divine is both possible and difficult.
