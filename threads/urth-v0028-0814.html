2000-02-23 Carlos Martinho #

Reports that the Portuguese dictionary lists the word "áscio" for a person living in the torrid zone where the noon sun casts no shadows, tracing it to Latin "ascios" as a possible source for Wolfe's Ascian. Offers this as a find from a separate thread on the name's origin, supplying a dictionary definition rather than an interpretation of the books.
informs · source-and-allusion

I went there and the discussion prompted me to look
for a similar word in a Portuguese dictionary (since
Portuguese is a Latin-derived language, it seemed a
good try).

So, here it is: in Portuguese there's the word
"áscio", meaning "He who lives in the torrid zone,
where, at noon, the there's no shadows produced by the
Sun". The given root is the Latin word "ascios".

2000-02-23 Ouroboros The Worm #

Informs the list that two recent World Wide Words mailing list editions discussed the word "Ascian" and links to the discussion. Notes that those discussions made no reference to Wolfe's books, which the poster says they criticized for omitting the connection.
informs · source-and-allusion

The last two editions on the World Wide Words mailing list (http://www.quinion.com/words/) have included discussions of the word "Ascian". (http://www.quinion.com/words/qa/qa-asc1.htm.)

No reference to Gene Wolfe's works, but I chastised them for that.

-Ouroboros
