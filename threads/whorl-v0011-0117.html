2000-08-16 Alice Turner #

Argues that the last sentence of The Book of the Long Sun shows Horn protecting Jahlee, paralleling his failed attempt to save the cold Fava by lying apart. Disputes the opposing argument about the ending, calling it unsupported by textual evidence and Wolfean snake-charming, and says that argument loses the book's whole meaning.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Horn protects Jahlee at the ending

Furthermore, with that beautiful last sentence, Horn is protecting Jahlee in the best way he can. As he tried, unsuccessfuly to protect poor cold-blooded Fava in the wintry night, nearly killing himself too as she inadvertently leached away his warmth. Fava's life could have been saved if she had lain between two humans (or Babbie, or any other warmblooded creature), but Horn could not take that chance and had to try alone. Just as he buried her alone. The entire point and meaning of this book is lost in the argument, and there's not a shred of evidence for it other than the usual Wolfean snake-charming. I really feel offended by it.

-alga
