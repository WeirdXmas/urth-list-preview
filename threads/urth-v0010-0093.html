Bruce Pennington covers

Urth list · 4 messages · 1998-05-06 to 1998-05-06

1998urthThe Book of the New SunTony EllisKieran ClearyPeter StephensonPeter Westlake
The thread: Tony Ellis opened by championing Bruce Pennington's original British covers for The Book of the New Sun over the American Don Maitz covers, citing the Claw and Shadow covers as especially strong. Peter Stephenson agreed, praising the Shadow cover's cloaked figure and the sense of age and decay, and noted seeing the Claw cover in an Italian edition. Kieran Cleary also preferred the UK cover art to Maitz while finding the UK Long Sun covers weak, though praising the stained-glass motif, and disliked clear protagonist faces on covers. Peter Westlake agreed the UK covers fail where they do not match readers' mental pictures, praised the stained-glass windows linking the first two Long Sun books, and regretted that the third and fourth spines repeat the second. The thread stayed friendly and largely in agreement, ending without any real dispute.

  • Peter Stephenson: Pennington's Shadow cover captures age, decay and ambiguity
  • Kieran Cleary: UK covers beat Maitz, but UK Long Sun covers are weak; dislikes clear protagonist faces
  • Peter Westlake: UK covers fail where they miss readers' mental pictures; stained glass links Long Sun books, later spines repeat

1998-05-06 Tony Ellis #

Champions Bruce Pennington's original British covers for The Book of the New Sun over the American Don Maitz covers, arguing they convey the romance, mystery and ruinous beauty of Severian's world. Responds to Dan Parmenter's remark on Maitz and the covers by citing the Claw and Shadow covers as the most outstanding and offering to scan them.
supports

06/05/98
09:40 Bruce Pennington covers

Whenever I read praise for the Maitz covers I feel this evangelical urge to champion our own original British covers (not to be confused with the various naff covers of the later reprints). Thank you giving me the chance to preach, Dan!

The artist is Bruce Pennington, and for my money his covers evoke the romance, the mystery, the great age and the ruinous beauty of Severian's world like nothing else. The covers for Claw and Shadow are the most outstanding, but they all convey - through tone and colour as much as through what is depicted - exactly what the reader is in for.

Dan, I'll be happy to scan the covers for you. And anyone else who has never seen them will be doing themselves a favour if they take a peek too.

Agreed. SF fans always assume the moral high ground in the great SF versus Literature conflict, but in fact there's an awful lot of inverted snobbery in there. And the only thing that's worse than a snob is an inverted snob.

1998-05-06 Peter Stephenson #

Agrees with Tony Ellis that Bruce Pennington's covers for The Book of the New Sun capture the mood of Severian's world, singling out the Shadow cover's cloaked figure below a ruined hill. Supports the praise by describing the age, decay and ambiguity the image conveys, and notes seeing the Claw cover in an Italian edition.
supports

I have to agree with this. The cover for Shadow, showing a mysterious figure (Severian) below a cluttered hill, has haunted me ever since I first bought it. There's a sense of age and decay, mixed with a half familiarity of seeing our own past; the figure, totally enfolded in his cloak, is at once a focus and slightly apart from the world. It's full of all the right sorts of ambiguity.

By the way, I noticed that the cover of Claw in the Italian translation was the one from this series. I wonder how far they've spread.

pws

1998-05-06 Kieran Cleary #

Compares the UK cover art for Wolfe's books favorably with the Maitz covers, while finding the UK Long Sun covers weak and praising the stained-glass motif. Also remarks on dislike of clear protagonist faces on covers and suggests Victoria's motet as music for a film of the Urth sequence.
chats

I have to agree with Tony: the covers for the UK version are (IMHO) far better than the Maitz covers. On the other hand, the covers for the UK version of the Long Sun series are very poor indeed, compared to the covers I've seen for Lake and Exodus on P.Duggan's website. A nice touch on the UK covers, though, is the embedding of stained glass windows in the otherwise naturalistic landscapes. Good conception, poor execution. Plus, I don't like to see clear depictions of protagonists' faces on covers (this is turning into a bit of a rant..better stop).

While I'm on tangential and subjective opinions, though a film of the Urth sequence would have to be a travesty, it would also *have* to feature T. L. de Victoria's "Ascendens Christus in altum" as mood music.

Been away for a while. I have to say that a stuffed-to-overflowing Urth mail folder is a nice treat.

1998-05-06 Peter Westlake #

Agrees that the UK covers are poor where they fail to match readers' mental pictures, and praises the stained glass windows that join across the first two Long Sun books. Notes disappointment that the spines of the third and fourth books repeat the second rather than continuing the picture, and that Calde carries the same cover as Lake because it is subtitled Volume Two.
supports

Me either, especially when they don't look like my mental picture, which they invariably never do, especially in this case.

The stained glass windows are very nice, and I liked the way the first two joined together to make a bigger picture; I was looking forward to the third and fourth books, and was very disappointed when the spines did not continue to build up but were duplicates of the second. Of course, Calde had the subtitle "Volume Two of the Book of the Long Sun", so I suppose it's only fair that it should have the same cover as Lake!

Hello, by the way.

Peter.

Peter Westlake, Harlequin Ltd, Barrington Hall, Barrington, Cambs CB2 5RG.
