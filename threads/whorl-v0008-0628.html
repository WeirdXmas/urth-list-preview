1998-05-15 Michael Straight #

Explains the two-headed 'pho' joke Alice Turner asked about by pointing to Zaphod Beeblebrox of Douglas Adams' Hitchhiker's Guide, a two-headed President who is a figurehead for hidden rulers. Draws the parallel by describing Zaphod's discovery that he secretly rewired his own brain, and floats whether Typhon is a pawn of the Undines in The Book of the New Sun.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Typhon is a pawn of the Undines

Two-headed Zaphod (Beeblebrox), President of the entire Galaxy (Universe?), but really just figure-heads for the dark and mysterious actual rulers (is Typhon really a pawn of the Undines?). From Douglas Adams' _Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy_ books.

And...in a spoiler for HHGTG...

Zaphod discovers that someone has messed with his brain to make him do stuff he normally wouldn't and later discovers that someone was himself, rewiring his own brains so he could become President without his real motives being discovered. It's not quite one head betraying the other, but...

-Rostrum

1998-05-15 Peter Westlake #

Suggests that Typhon's name echoes Zaphod Beeblebrox from Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy, and links the ship's computer Eddy to Typhon's later starship in the New Sun books. Replies to Alice Turner's request for an explanation of an out-of-it allusion, offering a loose name-and-source comparison while admitting uncertainty about which book came first.
proposes · source-and-allusion

Ty-pho-n or Za-pho-d Beeblebrox. Zaphod's starship "Heart of Gold" in the Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy has a ship's computer called Eddy.

Of course, Typhon didn't start out in a starship in tBotNS, and I don't know whether tBotNS or HHGG was published first. The _Whorl_ is long after Eddy the computer, of course.

-- Spectacled bear.

Peter Westlake, Harlequin Ltd, Barrington Hall, Barrington, Cambs CB2 5RG.

1998-05-15 Kieran Mullen #

Proposes two reasons for the name of the Whorl: a pun on "world" in keeping with Wolfe's wordplay, and the whirlpool-like look of a cylinder seen end-on from its surface. Responds to Peter Westlake's question about why the hollowed asteroid is so named, and asks the list whether anyone knows the Whorl's aspect ratio.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Whorl name puns on world; Whorl name evokes a whirlpool

I thought there were at least two reasons:

(1) The resonance with the word "world". Wolfe likes wordplay.

(2) If you are living on the surface of a cylinder and look down it, far away it will look like a whirlpool (I think). Does anyone know what the aspect ratio of the Whorl is?

Kieran Mullen
