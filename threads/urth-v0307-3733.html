Proposed Gene Wolfe Symposium

Urth list · 27 messages · 2003-07-26 to 2003-08-01

2003urthSheila HerndonGreg WardenWilliam H. AnsleyJosh GellerLisa Schaffer-DoggettAndy RobertsonJames WynnChris PBrandon MasonDan'l Danehy-Oakes
The thread: Josh Geller proposed a Gene Wolfe symposium in the Bay Area, sometimes framed as a paid week with the author and at other times as a weekend, and asked list members to pledge money and interest. Lisa Schaffer-Doggett and Dan'l Danehy-Oakes raised doubts that Wolfe would want to take part, and Dan'l argued he would prefer to spend his time on the Latro continuation. Other readers, including Brandon Mason from Kentucky and silk tree, expressed interest, while silk tree proposed a recurring format featuring obscure authors such as Crowley. The thread stalled amid an unrelated dispute over an insulting remark Josh made about East San Jose and a racial slur, with William Ansley and Andy Robertson demanding a public apology. By August the plan remained unsettled, with Josh still awaiting Wolfe's reply.
  • Josh Geller: organizer proposing a informal, low-cost symposium centered on Wolfe's own wishes
  • Lisa Schaffer-Doggett: supportive in principle but doubts Wolfe would attend, wants ground rules and a convention pairing
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: doubts the event will happen since Wolfe would see it as fan adulation
  • William Ansley: says Josh owes the list a public apology, which makes the event less likely
  • Andy Robertson: demands an apology to the list for an ethnic insult
  • silk tree: suggests a recurring symposium with different authors, including Crowley

Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Fathers, mothers and children, Time loops and causality

2003-07-26 Josh Geller #

Proposes a symposium where readers pay to spend a week with Gene Wolfe in an informal, fun format rather than a paid writers' workshop, and asks list members to state willingness to pay about $200 for in-person time or $50 for a live electronic link. Responds to Don's remarks about Wolfe's respect among readers and about workshops, and asks someone who knows Wolfe to put the idea to him.
proposes

Hey Don,

How much would you pay to hang out with Gene Wolfe for a week and talk about whatever he wanted to talk about? I am really getting away from the workshop concept: since it is not conceivable that we will have enough money to offer any writer a reasonable fee, I want this to be much more fun than it is work for the writer. Few people will turn down an all-expenses paid vacation. If it is actually an attempt to get them to work for free or cheap, many people will resent the attempt and not want to have anything to do with it.

By the way, I am discussing this idea with some of my friends and associates, and the feeling seems to be that it is an idea with some general potential.

Here is what I would like to happen right now: people on this list who would be willing to pay approximately $200 to hang out with Gene Wolfe for a week should sound off. People who are willing to pay approximately $50 to participate in the electronic real-time link that will be set up during each session should also sound off, and would someone who knows Gene Wolfe personally please ask him what he thinks of the idea and pass that on to the list?

Anyone who has any suggestions that do not involve doing something completely different in Chicago or somewhere else should also please speak and not hold their peace.

Or we can return to wrangling over minutiae. That is always an option.

J.

2003-07-26 Josh Geller #

Proposes that all sessions of a Gene Wolfe symposium be recorded, transcribed, marketed and published, with profits split between the author and the organizing entity. Argues this would help Wolfe and other writers such as Crowley and Patricia Anthony gain respect that they are denied because they are classed as SF writers.
proposes

Here's a detail that might make this more attractive as a general proposal:

Everything in the sessions will be recorded, transcribed, marketed and (if possible) published, with any profits to be split 50/50 between the author and the entity doing the symposiums.

There are a lot of writers who don't get anything like the respect they deserve because they have been classified as SF writers, and most SF fans don't like them for whatever reason. Wolfe, Crowley and Patricia Anthony are the three that leap to mind; I am sure that there are others.
