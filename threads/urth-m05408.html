AEG: Cassie in love

Urth list · 7 messages · 2008-12-24 to 2009-01-04

20082009urthDave TallmanMo Holkar / UKGDavid Stockhoff
The thread: Thread asks whether Cassie in An Evil Guest truly loves Bill Reis or only feigns love while remaining loyal to Chase, given her last words on the hopper. Dave Tallman argues her love is sincere, that her travel with Chase could serve glamour, saving Bill, or killing Chase, and that her feelings survive because Bill is dead. Mo Holkar counters that her journey is deliberately open and a reward, not a setup for killing Chase, and later that Fairyland as land of death denies her personal vendettas. Tallman disputes that Chase opposes Cthulhu, citing the Death's Visitors and the line 'Trust those who love you'. Exchanges stay sharp but unresolved, with Stockhoff siding with Holkar on Fairyland as land of death.
  • Dave Tallman: Cassie's love for Bill is sincere; Fairyland is dangerous adventure, not bliss; Chase is not on Cthulhu's side
  • Mo Holkar: Cassie's journey with Chase is a deliberately open reward; Fairyland is the land of death where vendettas are denied
  • David Stockhoff: Supports Holkar that Fairyland is the land of death; Wolfe's Fair Folk are never nice

Themes: Hidden identities and parentage, Ghosts, dreams and visions, Love and loss

2008-12-24 Dave Tallman replying to Mo Holkar / UKG #

Argues that Cassie's love for Bill in An Evil Guest is sincere even though she goes to Chase, and offers three possible reasons she might seek him out: restoring her glamour, travelling back in time to save Bill, or killing Chase. Responds to Mo Holkar's claim that her final words on the hopper undercut the sincerity of her love, and says her feelings survive because Bill is dead.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Cassie goes to Chase to restore her glamour; Cassie goes to Chase to save Bill through time travel; Cassie goes to Chase to kill him

As you say, she has no audience here. I have to believe the love is real. Even if she does ultimately end up with Chase, it doesn't undercut the sincerity of her feelings. Bill is dead, after all.

But I wonder if she isn't going to Chase for some other reason. There are three things he might do for her:
1) Help get her glamour back. She says that's one of the reasons she is going.
2) Help her travel in time, or get a message back in time, to save Bill's life.
3) Die, if she suspects Chase of having some hand in Bill's death.

2008-12-24 Mo Holkar / UKG #

Asks whether Cassie genuinely loves Reis or only feigns love while staying true to Chase all along, given her acting and lying. Grounds the question in her public declarations of love and her last words on the hopper, which may undercut any sincerity because she speaks them on her way to Chase.
questions · close-textual-reading · theories: Cassie only feigns love for Reis and stays true to Chase

Hi all,

This has been playing on my mind for some time now, and I haven't seen anyone else post about it yet, so:

Does Cassie genuinely love Reis / Rosenquist? We assume she's in a love triangle with him and Chase, but it seems to me at least equally possible that she's true to Chase all along and is only ever feigning being in love with Reis.

Cassie says that she loves him, and demonstrates it sufficiently well to convince him and others. But we know that she is both a talented actress and also a habitual and fluent liar.

Her last words on the hopper, when no-one is listening, might be testimony to a genuine love. But as she utters them while on her way to join Chase, that would seem to undercut any real sincerity implied.

Thoughts?

best wishes,

Mo

2009-01-02 Dave Tallman replying to Mo Holkar / UKG #

Argues that the fairyland in An Evil Guest is not a land of happily-ever-after bliss but a place of dangerous adventure, pointing to Cassie being told to go armed and to avoid talking to the fish. Responds to Mo Holkar's view that Cassie's journey is an unchanging reward, citing Wolfe's other Fair Folk stories as parallels.
disputes · genre-and-craft · close-textual-reading · theories: Fairyland in AEG is dangerous, not happily-ever-after

This is not the Fairyland of "happily ever after," but a land of dangerous adventures. Cassie is advised to go armed and not to talk to the fish. Wolfe's Fair Folk are never all that nice, whether in Castleview, the Wizard Knight, "No Planets Strike," "The Magic Animal," or AEG.

2009-01-02 Mo Holkar / UKG replying to Dave Tallman #

Argues that Cassie's reasons for travelling with Chase stay deliberately open in An Evil Guest, and that in a Woldercan-as-Fairyland reading her journey is a reward rather than a setup for a sharp resolution like killing Chase. Responds to Dave Tallman's list of three possible motives, mainly her glamour, saving Bill, and dying, by reasoning from the book's own framing and the fairy-tale model.
proposes · myth-and-structure · theories: Woldercan is Fairyland; Cassie's journey is her reward

It's interesting that her motivation for travelling is left so very open. (By necessity, since we couldn't tust what she might say about it, but even so.)

In the Woldercan-as-Fairyland model, I guess the journey is her reward for coming through the trials of the story, and she can expect unchanging bliss rather than any kind of further sharp resolution such as killing Chase -- that sort of thing doesn't happen within Fairyland.

best wishes,

Mo

2009-01-04 Dave Tallman replying to Mo Holkar / UKG #

Disputes Mo Holkar's vendetta reading of An Evil Guest by arguing that Gideon Chase is not on the side of Chtulhu. Cites the winged Death's Visitors who aid Cassie's transformation and warn her about the Squid God, the p. 235 line 'Trust those who love you', and Reis's own responsibility for his troubles.
disputes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Gideon Chase is not allied with Chtulhu; Death's Visitors are Chase's allies; Reis brought his troubles on himself

Mortals can kill other mortals in Fairyland; Hwan Lee shot Jose Balanco in "Castleview." Mortals can also kill monsters there; Able killed Grengarm in "The Knight."

On the other hand, I don't really believe the vendetta theory. My assessment is that Gideon Chase isn't on the side of Chtulhu. The winged Death's Visitors seem to be his allies (one appears to assist in Cassie's transformation on p. 43: "Behind him: great, dark wings.") The creatures warn Cassie about the Squid God and save her life. They tell her "Trust those who love you... Only them" (p. 235). That seems to mean to trust only them, along with Bill Reis and Gideon Chase.

Reis seems to have brought his troubles on himself. Chase didn't make him set the Navy on the Storm King. His only responsibility is that Reis wouldn't have loved Cassie (and wanted to attack those who endangered her) without Chase's help. Cassie surely cannot want revenge for that.
