The thread: Posters debated whether the Latro series could end with a fourth book and whether Wolfe's Sun books left room for a sequel or backstory, with John Watkins doubting a fourth Latro book and brunians suggesting Palestine as a setting for a more benevolent God. The thread then turned to whether Cyrus was seen as Messiah in the period of the Soldier books, which Mo Holkar disputed, arguing Cyrus was anointed in Jewish texts but not the Isaiah-style Messiah, a position brunians partly accepted by conceding differing meanings of moshiach. Early side debates on whether the Sun clues were enough to derive Severian's time travel and on a posthumous 'Silmarillion' were mostly jokes or assertions without resolution. The thread ended with the Cyrus question largely unresolved rather than agreed.
  • James Wynn: clues in the Sun books did not make Severian's time travel derivable
  • John Watkins: Wolfe too coy to have Latro meet the One True God directly; doubts a fourth Latro book
  • brunians: Palestine under Persian rule could offer Latro a more benevolent God; Cyrus was seen as messiah
  • Mo Holkar: Cyrus was anointed in Jewish texts but had faded as a Messiah by Soldier's setting
  • b sharp: a posthumous Silmarillion-style book would be finished by Roy Wolfe, said playfully

Themes: Christ figures and messiahs, Gods, angels and the Increate, Myth and folklore sources

2009-04-03 James Wynn replying to O'Donnell, Tim (BOSI) #

Argues that clues in the original tetralogy did not make Severian's time travel as Conciliator and Apu Punchau derivable, since readers would have had to pick the right jumping-off points and the right conclusion. Responds to O'Donnell's quoted claim that the answers were in the text, comparing that to Osama bin Laden being 'right there'; also says The Urth of the New Sun was necessary and a Silmarillion of the New Sun would not likely appear.
disputes · puzzle-solving · theories: Severian travels through time to become Conciliator and Apu Punchau

IROSFMichael also says something here that drives me bonkers:

"With the mysteries laid bare, it becomes apparent that enough clues were provided for such major mysteries to be solved in the original tetrology. That Severian would travel through time to become both the Conciliator and Apu Punchau, rather than being a reincarnation, avatar, or descendant. And that the world would drown with the coming of the New Sun."

Well, yes, there are enough clues to have jumped to those conclusions. All the reader had to do was to accurately identify the jumping off points as such --while excluding the the non-jumping off points--- AND jump to the *correct* conclusion to the exclusion of the other potential conclusions.

When Wolfe says all the answers are "in the text" it is like saying that "Osama Bin Laden is 'right there' in Central Asia so why don't you go out and get him?"

Still, something that Wolfe's argument with Hartwell does make clear is that Wolfe had a very clear understanding of what was going on in his story, even the elements kept quite obscure. That's comforting, at least.

I agree with Michael that The Urth of the New Sun *was* necessary. I also think a Simarillion of the New Sun would greatly increase the general enjoyment and popularity of the books. But I don't think that will happen. Wolfe has said "the answers never last, but the mysteries never die".

J.

2009-04-04 Mo Holkar / UKG replying to brunians #

Argues that the Messiah concept in the Soldier setting of Wolfe's books should be read through Jewish rather than Christian understandings, noting that Cyrus was honoured as a messiah in Isaiah 45 while others expected a future Davidic Messiah. Responds to brunians' quoted remarks on Isaiah by setting aside the Christian reading as irrelevant to the historical period, while allowing it may matter for Wolfe's books.
disputes · source-and-allusion · theology-and-religion · theories: Soldier-setting Jews looked back to Cyrus as messiah

I've been ignoring the Christian interpretation, as irrelevant to the historical period under consideration. Although of course it may be relevant to the books, as this is Wolfe we're talking about. Perhaps someone who knows more than me about it can comment.

Absolutely. But within Judaism itself there seem to have been variant Messiah understandings -- for example, the stipulation in Isaiah 11 that the awaited Messiah would be Jewish and of the line of David, irreconcilable with the acclamation of Cyrus as Messiah in Isaiah 45. It is a complex issue, particularly when we don't know exactly when the relevant texts were written, or (as in the case of Isaiah) suspect they were cobbled together from several authors over a lengthy period.

My point really was, though, that while many Jews of the Soldier setting would have been looking back to Cyrus as the good old Messiah that was, we can expect that plenty of others would have been looking forward to the next one.
