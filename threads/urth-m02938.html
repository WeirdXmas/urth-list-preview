2006-10-21 Fred Kiesche #

Shares a NASA photograph of Apollo 17 astronaut Jack Schmitt at the lunar lander and suggests Wolfe may have had a later Artemis mission in mind rather than the one described in the book. Supports the point with a planetarium calculation of how visible Antarctica and Australia would be from the Moon at that moment.

<http://www.hq.nasa.gov/alsj/a17/as17-134-20384HR.jpg>

Description: AS17-134-20384. EVA-1 at the LM. This is
an excellent portrait of Jack with the U.S. flag and
the Earth. We get a good view of Jack's chest-mounted
RCU and the camera bracket. Using planetarium program
Starry Night Deluxe, we see that, had cloud cover over
the southwestern Pacific been lighter, the Antarctica
would have been visible at the left and Australia
would have been coming into view over the top. Four
hours later, Earth's rotation would bring Australia to
center stage. Scan courtesy NASA Johnson.

2006-10-23 b sharp replying to Fred Kiesche #

Argues that the moon picture in the Commonwealth's art gallery is a painting rather than a photograph, so it could draw on several photo or video sources or a direct view. Responds to Fred Kiesche's point about possible photo sources and repeats the poster's Fechin-Inire connection theory about the artist's access to Earth.
disputes · puzzle-solving · theories: Fechin-Inire connection to the moon picture's artist

Regarding the multiple possible photo sources for the moon picture- given the baseline technology base of The Commonwealth I have operated under the assumption this was a painting not a photo hanging in the art gallery. A painting can be inspired by multiple photos, a video source or perhaps even a direct view.

Of course the mysterious artist would have needed access to Earth and/or its artifacts...(yes, I'm still promoting my Fechin-Inire connection ;-) )
