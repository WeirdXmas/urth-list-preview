1998urthThe Book of the New SunDavid Duffy

Themes: Catholic faith and sacraments, Justice, torture and mercy

1998-04-25 David Duffy #

Argues that Wolfe's Urth books reflect a medieval Christian view that suffering and death in this world are transitory, so the drowning at the end of The Urth of the New Sun and the torture and execution of criminals are not as dreadful as they seem. Also suggests Tirant Lo Blanc resonates with The Book of the New Sun, while admitting he cannot be specific since he read it long ago.
proposes · theology-and-religion · source-and-allusion · theories: Medieval Christian view of transitory suffering underlies the New Sun

Regarding _Acturus_ (I am a fan I guess), I think a link is what I think of as the medieval Christian viewpoint that suffering and death in this world are transitory -- so it's not so dreadful that much of Urth's population was drowned at the end of UotNS, or indeed that criminals must be tortured then executed in payment for their acts.

I doubt it is a direct influence, but _Tirant Lo Blanc_ has resonances with BotNS to me (a long time since I read the former unfortunately, so I can't be more specific).
