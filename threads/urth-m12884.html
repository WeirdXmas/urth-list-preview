The Politics Of Gene Wolfe

Urth list · 5 messages · 2010-07-02 to 2010-07-03

2010urthSunandaGerry QuinnDavid StockhoffJames Wynn
The thread: Readers debated whether Gene Wolfe's politics can be read from his fiction, starting from Sunanda's suggestion of Operation Ares. James Wynn argued Wolfe is neither clearly conservative nor libertarian, citing The Devil in a Forest's critique of Robin Hood's redistribution and the wide spread of dystopian sentiment beyond any party. Wynn then pressed David Stockhoff's claim that depicting evil agents reveals nothing about an author's beliefs, asking what a heroic-agent novel would imply. Gerry Quinn countered that favouring armed citizens in Viron need not mean approving of arms everywhere. The thread ended with no agreement and the interpretive question left open.
  • James Wynn: Wolfe's politics are not clearly conservative or libertarian; fiction's anti-government or revolutionary tones are common across camps
  • James Wynn: if depicting evil agents reveals nothing, neither should portraying heroic agents
  • Gerry Quinn: a reader can support arming citizens in Viron without endorsing it everywhere
  • David Stockhoff: depicting evil government agents says nothing about an author's beliefs

Themes: Power, rulers and politics, Justice, torture and mercy

Responds to Sunanda's suggestion of Operation Ares and disputes readings of Starwater Strains as anti-government, noting dystopian and revolutionary sentiments are widespread outside any party.

initialbelief has hardened:

Not an outlier, I think. "The Devil in a Forest" is fine unflattering deconstruction of Robin Hood as a hero who "robs the rich to give to the poor".

For the most part, however, the examples given from "Starwater Strains" are seeing what one expects to see. You don't have to be a conservative/libertarian to imagine a dystopian government (ex. George Orwell). You don't need to be a Tea Partyer to wish well a revolution that will bring down the "big important gangs with suits and guns". That sounds like the communist/anarchist G-20 protesters in Toronto.
