2000-01-17 Nicholas Gevers #

Suggests that a cartoon link to the story "1-2-3" is interesting but inconclusive, jokingly proposing that Rocky was reimagined by Wolfe as a hairy cocaine dealer. Points to the story "Fish Story" as a close parallel, citing its frame-narrated ghost-story form, phone motif and animal noises in the dark, and noting Jonathan's earlier observation.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Rocky reconceived as a hirsute cocaine dealer

The cartoon connection to "1-2-3" is interesting if
inconclusive; maybe Rocky was reconceived by Wolfe as
a hirsute cocaine dealer. A story whose frame-narrated
ghost-story format strongly recalls "1-2-3" is "Fish
Story", published in F&SF last October and available
for download from northern light. The resemblances
include the phone motif and animal noises in the dark,
as Jonathan has already noted elsewhere.

2000-01-18 Jonathan Laidlow #

Supports the view that the final scene of a campfire tale is deliberately ambiguous, suggesting the noise may come from the mysterious summoned figure or from a mere animal. Quotes William H. Ansley's reply, which concedes that the earlier post missed this ambivalence and links it to the tale's closing warning about a folk evil, the boogeyman.
supports · close-textual-reading · theories: final figure may be an animal, not a ghost

William H. Ansley wrote

I think you're correct to point this out - I failed to suggest in my original post that there is an ambivalence about this final scene: it *could* be the mysterious figure summoned by the noise, or it could just be an animal which ties in with the warning at the end of the tale. Either way the campfire participants have been reminded (and scared) of some folk evil (the boogeyman) which they must guard against.

Jonathan

> I think the most salient feature of this story is that it is a ghost story.
> Not just that, it is a ghost story being told around a camp fire. It is

> be in working order after being buried in the ground for 800 years or more.
