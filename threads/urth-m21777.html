Horn's abilities

Urth list · 5 messages · 2011-10-26 to 2011-10-26

2011urthThe Book of the Long SunThe Book of the New SunAntónio Pedro MarquesJames WynnAndrew MasonDaniel Petersen
The thread: Readers debated whether the Oreb Silk sees flying from the window in Silk's room is Pike's astral companion rather than the real Oreb, and whether Horn's astral travel explains it. James Wynn argued the astral reading is most likely, citing the limited number of night choughs in the books to rebut Andrew Mason's point that Silk never saw Oreb and Pike together. Mason countered that the vague dating and the blue-world characters' unclear sense of time undercut the timeline argument, and that the figure leaving the window is not clearly Oreb. Mason also proposed that Horn's astral travel resembles the aquastors of the Book of the New Sun, grounded in Malrubius's remark that matter is a dance that can still be touched. The thread ends without resolution, with the disagreement over the sequence of events left open.
  • James Wynn: the flying Oreb is most likely Pike's astral companion, since few night choughs exist in the books
  • Andrew Mason: the timeline argument is weak because dating is vague and time sense unclear; the window figure is not clearly Oreb
  • Andrew Mason: Horn's astral travel resembles the New Sun aquastors, with different sources and abilities

Themes: Ghosts, dreams and visions, Time loops and causality, Robots, androids and artificial life

2011-10-26 Andrew Mason #

Proposes that Horn's astral travel in the Long Sun books resembles the aquastors of the Book of the New Sun, though with different sources and abilities. Grounds this in Malrubius's remark that a dance of particles can still be touched, because all matter is such a dance, which the poster extends to astral travellers.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Horn's astral travel resembles aquastors

I think it has something in common with an aquastor. It's clearly not exactly the same; its source is different, and so are the precise abilities of the travellers. But Malrubius says that though he is a dance of particles, it's still possible to touch him, because all matter is a dance of particles - and I think that works for astral travellers as well.

2011-10-26 Andrew Mason replying to Andrew Mason #

Disputes the claim that Horn's dream-travel explains the astral Oreb, arguing the book's vague dating and the blue-world characters' unclear sense of time weaken the timeline argument. Points to the sequence in which Silk sees a flying Oreb before Pike and notes the figure seen leaving the window is not clearly Oreb.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: Astral Oreb is not clearly Oreb

I'm not wholly convinced by that, because of the vagueness of dates in the _Sun_ universe generally. GW in one of the appendices to _New Sun_ complains that the people of that age have a strangely vague conception of time; and there's something in OBW which suggests people on Blue aren't entirely clear about how long the year is.

Oreb appears _before_ Pike. Silk wakes up to see 'Oreb' flyibng out to the window, then, if I remember rightly, talks to Mucor for a while, then goes downstairs and talks to the real (contemporary) Oreb, then hears the bed creaking, comes upstairs again, and sees PIke. PIke and Oreb aren't together; and given that Silk only saw him flying out of the window, it;s not clear that it is Oreb at all.
