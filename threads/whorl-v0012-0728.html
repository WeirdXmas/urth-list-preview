Crocagators

Whorl list · 6 messages · 2001-04-25 to 2001-04-27

2001whorlThe Book of the Short SunDan'l Danehy-OakesWilliam H. AnsleyMark Millman
The thread: The thread asked how dangerous the inhumi (crocodile-like creatures) of Wolfe's Urth-related Long Sun and Short Sun books really are to humans. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued they pose little threat, since inhumi deaths are rare, the creatures are intelligent and can pass as human, and sentient inhumi might willingly give blood, comparing this to Octavia Butler's "Bloodchild". Mark Millman agreed the human population is not threatened soon, noting inhumi do not preferentially hunt humans, but insisted sentient inhumi are a real danger, citing Teazel's attack and General Oosik's son being drained. William Ansley contended that mindless inhumi would be worse than crocodiles because they can fly and squeeze through openings, and that past feedings on the Neighbors were accidental. The thread ended without resolution, with Millman conceding that deaths are rare but holding that attacks can be let
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: inhumi are not a serious threat to humans, and sentient inhumi could be harmless through voluntary blood-giving
  • mark millman: sentient inhumi are a real danger despite rare attacks, since they can drain and kill
  • William Ansley: mindless inhumi would be a greater threat than crocodiles because they fly and fit through small openings

Themes: Beasts and the animal in us, Slavery and service, Robots, androids and artificial life

2001-04-25 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that the inhumi in The Book of the Long Sun are not as dangerous to humans as feared, questioning whether the Bluvian scenes show a people facing predators. Grounds this in the rarity of inhumi deaths in the books, the inhumi's intelligence and ability to pass as human, and the Golden Rule theory of the Secret, responding to the Wombat's comparison of crocodilians to wolves.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Inhumi are less dangerous than humans fear

Which was pretty much my point. Assuming per argumentam the "Golden Rule" theory of the Secret, _and_ that the human colonists somehow manage to implement it, then the inhumi become "something we can live with," either as benevolent sentients or as dangerous but mindless giant leeches.

In fact, there's some doubt in my mind as to how many
people are actually killed by the inhumi in the situation which actually holds on Blue at the time of Horn's journey -- I don't recall many examples of someone actually dying from inhumi attacks [excepting the attack on the wedding, as a very special special case]. Do the inhumi, like wolves, bears, sharks, snakes, crocodilians... suffer from
exaggerated human fears as to the extent of the danger they pose?

I somewhat doubt it, on the grounds that the inhumi (at
least in the form we know) are far smarter than wolves,
bears, etc., and able to "pass" as humans: a combination that makes one inhumu potentially far more dangerous in
the long term than a good-sized pack of wolves. But the
Bluvian scenes in SHORT simply don't seem to reflect a
people in imminent danger of being wiped out by predators.

I dunno. There's _something_ weird missing here, what it is ain't exactly clear...

--Blattid

2001-04-25 Mark Millman #

Argues that the inhumi of Blue are unlike natural predators because they do not preferentially hunt humans, and notes Fava's care not to kill Salica when feeding. Responds to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's remark on how many people the inhumi actually kill, agreeing the human population is not threatened soon and suggesting that the baby Sinew surviving an attack may be a clue.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: The inhumi may not be simple predators

At the risk of being obvious, allow me to point out that, unlike the inhumi, wolves, bears, sharks, snakes, crocodilians, and the like do not preferentially target humans as their prey.

Also, as I recall (and I'm afraid that I don't have the book here for reference, so we'll have to depend on my recollection), Fava had to be very careful not to kill Salica when she fed from the old woman. If Fava hadn't been living with the family at the time, she very likely would have been less careful. (If anyone is likely to die from the inhumi's depredations, it would be the old, the very young, and the sick; curiously, though, even the baby Sinew was not killed when he was attacked. I wonder whether this may be a clue; thank you for bringing it up, Dan'l.) I do have to agree with Dan'l, though; the inhumi aren't going to kill off Blue's human population any time soon.

Nacre

2001-04-25 William H. Ansley #

Argues that mindless inhumi in The Book of the Long Sun would be a greater threat than crocodiles because they can fly and squeeze through small openings. Responds to Mark Millman's comparison by reasoning from the text that inhumi fed on the Neighbors by accident rather than targeting humans.
disputes · close-textual-reading

I don't think that "dumb" inhumi would preferentially target humans as their host. As I read the text, it was just an accident that the inhumi first fed upon the Neighbors and became sentient.

I think "mindless giant leeches" *that can fly* would be a much greater threat that crocodiles. It is easy to stay away from crocodiles, you just don't go near the river/lake they live in. (Unless, of course, it is your only source of drinking water.)

Inhumi, on the other hand, can fly so you can't avoid them. I would think, although I can't recall a specific example of this, that they could also squeeze through very small openings, since they have no bones.

Certainly, dumb inhumi would be orders of magnitude easier to defend against that smart ones, but I think it would be more difficult than you suggest.

William Ansley

2001-04-26 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that sentient inhumi in the Short Sun world will not prey on humans, and that voluntary blood-giving could make them harmless, a special case that the poster frames as an ideal society. Supports this by citing the reader's recollection of Fava and Salica, and by comparing the arrangement to Octavia Butler's story "Bloodchild".
disputes · close-textual-reading · genre-and-craft · theories: Sentient inhumi will not target humans after humans love one another; Voluntary blood-giving makes inhumi non-threatening on Short Sun

At the risk of being contentious, let me observe that only the sentient inhumi do so. But in the universe-of-discourse where humans have come to "love one another," the sentient inhumi will very specifically _not_ target humans; and the non-sentient inhumi, well, won't.

a) Special case: Given that the sentient inhumi have developed "human souls," it is possible and even
somewhat likely that at least some humans will choose to voluntarily give blood to sentient inhumi so that their children will also have "human souls." This, of course, is not a "danger" situation at all; in fact, this may represent the ideal state of society on the Short Sun whorls: humans _deliberately_ giving blood voluntarily to _all_ inhumi, so the nextgen of inhumi (and thereafter) will be non-threatening,
sentient inhumi, who will continue to take blood given voluntarily by humans, etc. (Consider also in this context Octavia Estelle Butler's horrendously effective story "Bloodchild.")

My recollection also.

Are you sure? I'm not. Again, death-by-inhumi-attack seems to be _exceedingly_ rare.

--Dan'l

2001-04-27 Mark Millman #

Argues that sentient inhumi are the real danger to humans on Blue even though attacks are rare, and that they can kill. Responds to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes by citing Teazel's attack in Long Sun and General Oosik's son being drained into weakness, and notes Blue's other dangers.
disputes · close-textual-reading

Contention welcomed, Dan'l. It's true that only the sentient inhumi specifically target humans; but then it's equally true that these are ex- actly the inhumi that the human population of Blue fears; and I'd be surprised if there were many non-sentient inhumi loose on Blue. The sentient inhumi are as much a source of fear as the (much less common) man-eating shark or tiger or wolf-pack is on Earth, and are the ones that humans would have to bring themselves to accept in an agape-driven world (though the entire humanization-of-the- inhumi-by-voluntary-blood-donation scenario is now discredited).

You are right to say that we hardly hear of any deaths from the inhumi's depredations in Short Sun (and of course this has now been discussed at greater length on the list), but there are examples in which victims have been left in a very bad way indeed. Kevin Maroney mentions Teazel in Long Sun, and I recall that General Oosik's son (is his name Muktuk?--again, I don't have the book for reference) is drained so badly that he loses consciousness, leaving him so weak that he cannot stand. These attacks may not be char- acteristic of the situation on Blue, but do demonstrate that inhumi can probably kill if they're so minded (or if they misjudge, etc.). Blue is also a colony world, and there seem to be enough dangers aside from the inhumi that one probably wouldn't want to go around in a condition of chronic weakness from blood loss.

Mark
