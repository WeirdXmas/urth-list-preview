The thread: Piotr Szczęsny objected that the Polish Long Sun translation renamed Silk as Jedwab, which he felt was unjustified, and that the translator who handled The Book of the New Sun had changed a name. Greg Kurzawa suggested the translator may have consulted Wolfe directly, while Gerry Quinn defended the rename by pointing to the city's naming system of animal, vegetable and mineral names. Entonio argued that ambiguous names are part of the design and resist translation, and Marc Aramini compared how other translations handled the doggerel and Vodalus' coin, while reporting that Wolfe no longer answers his questions. The thread shifted into examples of translation choices rather than reaching a resolution.
  • Piotr Szczęsny: renaming Silk as Jedwab is unjustified and the translator erred, with puzzle-like changes such as dial rendered as clock
  • Greg Kurzawa: the translator may have agreed the name with Wolfe, so the choice may reflect his intent
  • Gerry Quinn: translating Silk's name is justified because the city names people after animal, vegetable and mineral products
  • entonio: ambiguous names are part of the design and resist translation; Wolfe is very hard to translate
  • Marc Aramini: Italian translations differ across books in hiding Vodalus' coin, and Wolfe no longer answers his questions
  • Piotr Szczęsny: a Polish Citadel translation rendered a dial as clock, showing the translator's habits

Themes: Names and their meanings, Books, libraries and writing, Power, rulers and politics

2014-08-27 Marc Aramini replying to Greg Kurzawa #

Reports that Marc Aramini no longer gets answers from Wolfe to his questions about the Long Sun books, unlike earlier games of narrative exchange. Responds to Greg Kurzawa's suggestion that Polish translators may have consulted Wolfe directly, offering his own experience as evidence that Wolfe ignores such questions now.
informs · author-and-biography

I don't know what Wolfe's policy is on answering translator's and editor's questions, but he sure won't answer any of mine about his work anymore, and hasn't since like 2003. He doesn't mind hearing what I have to say but he kind of ignores the questions. He used to play little narrative games with me like write his letters on the back of a page from his original draft. The only thing he will do now is say very indirect things like gee I always loved 'lil abner after I told him my "changeling" ideas, for example.

Proposes that the Polish translator of the Long Sun books may have consulted Wolfe directly and agreed with him to render Patera Silk as Patera Jedwab, so the choice may reflect Wolfe's intent. Grounds this in the poster's own experience of being contacted by a Polish translator about tone and minor details in a short story.
proposes · author-and-biography · theories: Polish translator rendered Silk as Jedwab with Wolfe's approval

It's also possible that the translator communicated directly with Mr. Wolfe, and they decided together to do it that way. I've been contacted once by a translator (also Polish) seeking clarification on intended tone and minor details, and that was just for a short story. With work like Wolfe's, I would imagine (hope) that the translator received input, direction, and clarification from the author himself.

... which might mean that Wolfe's translators possess insight (straight from Wolfe) to which the rest of us don't have access.

2014-08-27 Marc Aramini replying to Piotr Szczęsny #

Reports that the Italian translation renders the 'like glass grow to any eye not of me' doggerel differently in the first and last books, hiding Vodalus' coin in a different way each time. Responds to Piotr Szczęsny's claim that the Polish translator added puzzle hints, such as rendering a dial as the word for clock, by comparing translations from memory.
informs · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion

The Italian version translates the entire "like glass/grass grow to any eye not of me" doggerel to hide Vodalus' coin completely differently in the first and last books. I haven't looked at that in more than 15 years, though. I think the Spanish translation was pretty good, but I only read through parts of New Sun, and I picked up my Spanish conversationally rather than through any kind of education.

2014-08-27 Gerry Quinn replying to Piotr Szczęsny #

Argues that translating Silk's name into Polish is justified because in the Long Sun city men are named after animal products, women after vegetable products, and androids after minerals. Responds to Piotr Szczęsny's complaint that the Polish translator rendered Patera Silk as Patera Jedwab, defending the choice as preserving the naming system.
disputes · source-and-allusion · theories: Translating names preserves the Long Sun naming system

I think it can, because in Silk's city, men are named after animal products, women after vegetable products, and androids after minerals. So to preserve that in the translation, he must translate the names. At least it is a very legitimate reason for doing it this way.

- Gerry Quinn

2014-08-27 António Pedro Marques replying to Gerry Quinn #

Argues that the ambiguity of certain character names, Silk's name especially, is part of the story's design, since Silk's city names men after animal products, women after vegetable products and androids after minerals, so such names resist translation. Responds to Gerry Quinn's claim that translators must render the names, and adds that Wolfe is very hard to translate well.
disputes · genre-and-craft · close-textual-reading

But notice that the ambiguity of some such names (and Silk is one of them) is part of the story (why have such a naming scheme anyway if not to abuse it), and such things can hardly expect always to be translatable. A writer like Wolfe is very difficult to translate well.

2014-08-27 Piotr Szczęsny replying to entonio #

Reports that a Polish translation of The Citadel of the Autarch rendered a dial as "zegar", a word that means clock, so the room Severian finds full of dials reads as a room of clocks. Responds to a thread on the Polish translation of the Long Sun books, drawing on the poster's own memory of reading the Polish and English texts side by side.
informs · close-textual-reading

I've read only Book of the New Sun in Polish, then I started reading in English (In fact Peace was my first novel that I've read in English). But then I re-read some parts in English and boy, the translator added some to the puzzles. Remember the part at the end of the Citadel, when Severian discovers the room with dials? The translator used polish word for dial - "zegar", which is also used in polish for clock, in fact the first thing that pops in your head when you read this word in polish is a clock not a some kind of dial. So for me this place was a room full of clocks...
