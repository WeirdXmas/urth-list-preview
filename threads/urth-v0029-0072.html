Themes: Buildings, cities and labyrinths, Myth and folklore sources

2000-06-23 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Thanks Robert Borski for pointing out that the house's basement reflects Greek myth, which resolves the poster's earlier puzzle about the house's four faces and architectural styles. Responds to Borski's suggestion as the thing that finally made the story's structure open up.
supports · myth-and-structure · theories: the house's basement is Greek myth

There, now see?!

I did all this noodling around with the house, its four faces, the architectural styles from neo-classical to contemporary, and I was able to make some headway, or at least amuse myself. But there were all these strange angles I still couldn't quite get to form a picture.

And Robert Borski comes along to, in essence, reveal to me that the basement of the house is, in fact, Greek myth. Well Shazam! Much, much suddenly becomes clear; and I shake my head in wonderment that I couldn't see the Greek myth. Tunnel vision.

The story starts to open up, and up, and up . . .

Incredible.

Thank you Robert!

=mantis=
