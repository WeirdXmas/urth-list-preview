Themes: Ghosts, dreams and visions

2001-02-18 Dennis G. Berdanis #

Argues that Horn in The Book of the Short Sun is in denial about his death on Green, and only later admits it when Silk makes him face he is alive, mentioning the killing of the boys' father only then. Agrees with Adam and responds to James Jordan's objection to Horn's words, citing Horn's behavior through the three books. Also thanks alga for information about which Scylla description Wolfe used and explains why an Oreb detail drew the poster to one version.
supports · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Horn progresses from denial to admitting his death

I agree with Adam. I believe the 3 BotSS are a beautiful progression of 1. a man in denial finally facing the truth of his death 2. a man who has lost his reason for living finally admitting he is still alive

Horn knew his body had died on Green. He seemed to have no confusion about this, but still did not mention that he had killed Hoof and Hide's father. Horn even knowing he was in not in his body, still believed himself very much alive through most of the three books. It was only when Silk admitted he was alive and Horn either released himself or Silk released him that he mentions killing the boys' father.

alga,
Thanks for the info on which description of Scylla, Wolfe based his character on. The only thing that drew me to the first description was the line
"Other accounts say that she was changed into a lark by the gods and her father into a hawk."
relating to Scylla residing in Oreb.
