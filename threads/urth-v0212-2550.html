2002-12-29 Malcolm McGugan #

Reflects that The Book of the New Sun taught the poster, first read as a fifteen-year-old from a town library, how to read demanding books attentively, citing Severian's advice to reread from chapter one. Offers this personal experience as a reason the novel is valuable, with no textual argument beyond the poster's own learning; the post ends with an unrelated spam signature.

At age 15 I read my first Gene Wolfe book from the
local town library, a book chosen on account of the
cover (a dude dressed in black with a big sword) and
due to being on the sparse sci-fi/fantasy shelf. It’s
been mentioned in the archives before now but I also
feel that one of the (many) things BotNS does is to
teach a very casual modern reader like myself how to
read books of this kind. Needless to say I followed
Severians advice and went straight back to chapter one
to enjoy the book again, this time using my newly
developing powers of attentive reading to get just
that little bit deeper. I was then happy to apply the
same techniques to other books, which would certainly
have been otherwise beyond me. Gene Wolfe taught me
how to read...
