The Green Man and the Circus

1998-05-09 William H. Ansley #

Proposes that the green man in Claw's chapter 3 echoes Finney's The Circus of Dr. Lao, blending the hound of the hedges with Apollonius of Tyana as fortune teller. Grounds this in the chapter title 'The Showman's Tent' and faint resonances, and recommends Finney's novel while noting its passages may offend modern readers.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Green man echoes Finney's circus showman

Is anyone else reminded of _The Circus of Dr. Lao_ by Charles G. Finney when reading the passage describing Severian's encounter with the green man in ch. 3 of _Claw_ (pp. 229-231, Orb ed.)?

I think the echoes are faint and distorted but unmistakable. The green man combines characteristics of the hound of the hedges and Apollonius of Tyana in his role as fortune teller. There are many other resonances, as well. The chapter is even called "The Showman's Tent".

For people that haven't read _Circus_, I highly recommend it, with one caveat: it contains passages that might not be considered politically correct by some today. But taking into account it was first printed in 1935, I think the likelihood of anyone taking offense is minor. It is an unique fantasy and it has had a wide influence in the field.

Finney's writing is lupine at least to the extent that he leaves the reader with some deliberately unanswered questions and ambiguities.

William Ansley
