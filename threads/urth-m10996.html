Seven American Nights procession

Urth list · 6 messages · 2010-08-18 to 2010-08-19

2010urthThe Book of the New SunDave TallmanJames WynnDavid Stockhoff
The thread: Posters debated whether the procession in Seven American Nights falls on Good Friday, Holy Saturday or Easter, given the theater's Easter closure and the timing of the party. Dave Tallman argued for Good Friday, citing the plaintive singing, the lifted crucifix and the three-o'clock hour of Christ's death, and worked through Thursday, Friday and Saturday options; he also floated a speculative secret-police theory. David Stockhoff disputed the Good Friday reading, saying the party's stated timing does not fit a holiday procession, but later supported plausibility by pointing to Catholic city customs such as Philadelphia's Easter shutdown. James Wynn joined in favor of Good Friday and defended the Easter closing as consistent with a Catholic city. The thread ended with the Good Friday reading gaining support, though the calendar question stayed unresolved.
  • Dave Tallman: procession is Good Friday, Thursday-to-Friday passion parallel with Ardis as Judas, floated secret-police theory
  • David Stockhoff: calendar of party and holiday do not fit, later granted Catholic city closure is plausible
  • James Wynn: Good Friday because of three-o'clock hour and priest carrying cross, defends Easter closing

Themes: Catholic faith and sacraments, Christ figures and messiahs, Time loops and causality

2010-08-18 Dave Tallman #

Argues that the procession in Seven American Nights is more likely a Good Friday observance than a Holy Saturday or Easter one, weighing the plaintive singing and the lifted crucifix against the night setting and theater closing. Floats a theory that the final day's events are forged by American secret police to make Nadan disappear. Responds to David Stockhoff's suggestion that a theater closed for Easter on a Saturday implies a Good Friday procession.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theology-and-religion · theories: the last day's events are forged by American secret police; Nadan disappears through a staged forgery

You can't have a Good Friday parade on Holy Saturday, I don't think. The party was on the evening of the day he saw the procession. (It's my theory, however, that nothing described on the last day is real. I think it was all forged by the American secret police, who wanted Nadan to disappear.)

Case for Good Friday:
Singing was plaintive? "ritual pleas for renewal of life." A crucifix rather than an empty cross? "the dead leader lifted up"

Case against it:
Not done at night (but that might be too dangerous).
Odd theater closing day.

Case for Holy Saturday:
Good party day.

Case against it:
I've never heard of Holy Saturday processions.

Case for Easter Sunday:
Good party day (though maybe not for costume parties).
Processions of this sort are done around the world on Easter Sunday.

2010-08-18 Dave Tallman replying to Dave Tallman #

Works through the day-sequence of the Seven American Nights journal, testing whether the procession and party fall on Thursday, Friday or Saturday against the Easter closure of the theater. Argues that the Thursday-to-Friday option gives a Christ-like arrest and Ardis as Judas, then weighs the other two options for plausibility.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · allegory-and-typology · theories: Nadan's arrest on Thursday parallels Christ's passion; Ardis functions as Judas in the story

I thought you said the party was to be "tomorrow" night. I haven't looked it up, but if that's the case, then today is the procession and tomorrow is Saturday. A theater would be closed all that weekend if Sunday is Easter.

I'm sorry I wasn't clear about the context. Let's call them Day N and Day N+1.

On Day N, Nadan sees his last performance of "Mary Rose" (actually he performs in it himself). Afterwards he goes home with Ardis and has sex with her. As he is leaving for his hotel, she tells him, "You will see me tomorrow. You're going to take me boating and we'll picnic by the water. Tomorrow night the theater will be closed for Easter, and you can take me to a party."

Nadan returns to his hotel that night and finds his room disturbed, an egg taken, and his journal moved. He strips certain pages out of the journal and... (I say the police return and arrest him and he is seen no more).

On Day N+1, the journal continues the story. Nadan and Ardis walk up the shore from his hotel near the Arena Theater to the Jefferson Memorial (this is impossible, if you check a Washington map). They do all the picnic and boating things exactly as she described. On the walk back to the hotel, they see the procession. That night they attend a costume party (both in disguises that would easily conceal their identities if impersonators did it in their place). They go back to Ardis' place and Nadan lights some arrack in order to see her naked. He supposedly sees she is a werewolf monster, perhaps the very one he shot at before, so he kills her.

So the options are:

N = Thursday, N+1 = Friday
Symbolically this is great. Nadan gets arrested on Thursday night, like Jesus. The story has parallel to a passion play, with Ardis as Judas. I like this a lot, except for the theater being closed on Friday for Easter.

N = Friday, N+1 = Saturday
Maybe this isn't so bad. Like you say, the theater could be closed the whole weekend for Easter. There may be processions that would normally be held Friday night or very early on Easter morning that were moved to Saturday mid-day for safety. Nobody wants to be out at night with werewolves around.
N = Saturday, N+1 = Sunday
This has the theater open on Saturday but closed on Easter Sunday, and a procession late in the day for Easter itself. Again, a mid-day hour might be selected for safety. The symbolic revelation of a negative Easter (either an Ardis monster or an American plot) is reasonable.

2010-08-19 David Stockhoff replying to James Wynn #

Argues that the Good Friday and Easter closing in Seven American Nights is plausible because the city's customs, like Philadelphia's Easter shutdown, would make a Catholic holiday closure ordinary. Responds to James Wynn's reading that Nadan's arrest on Thursday night parallels Christ's Passion, with Ardis as Judas, and that the three-o'clock hour matches Christ's death.
supports · theology-and-religion · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Nadan's arrest parallels Christ's Passion; Ardis is Judas in the Passion parallel

Ah, closed EARLY for Easter. Of course.

I was going to check the text before I said anything, but James is right--- everything depends on how Catholic are the customs of the city. In Philadelphia, for example, everything shuts down on Easter weekend, starting Friday afternoon if not morning.

This custom may seem strange to those who did not grow up with it.

Thematically, this also makes sense because after the procession, the text says: "It is very late-three, my watch says." Three o'clock is the hour of Christ's death.?
