The Book of the Short Sun

The thread: Nicholas Gevers speculated before publication that the inhumi in The Book of the Short Sun may be more sympathetic than readers expect and that the book will examine whether settlers have a right to occupy another people's land, citing colonial guilt in earlier Wolfe books and ambiguities around Blue and Quetzal. Michael Straight argued that Horn, the colonist narrator of the Long Sun books, may have portrayed Quetzal and the inhumi unfairly, since many scenes of Quetzal are reconstructed or secondhand rather than observed. Peter Westlake then asked whether the Whorl list had gone dead and suggested it could host post-publication discussion with spoilers kept separate. The thread ended without replies to any of these points, so the debate stayed at the level of opening proposals.
  • Nicholas Gevers: inhumi may be more sympathetic than expected and Short Sun will address settler occupation rights
  • Michael Straight: Horn is an unreliable colonist narrator who likely misrepresents Quetzal and the inhumi
  • Peter Westlake: Whorl list could host Short Sun discussion after publication with spoilers separated

Themes: Colonization and the native, Unreliable narration, Beasts and the animal in us

1999-04-10 Nicholas Gevers #

Speculates that the inhumi of The Book of the Short Sun may be more sympathetic than readers expect, and that Short Sun will address whether settlers have a right to occupy another people's territory. Grounds this in the colonial guilt of The Fifth Head of Cerberus and The Book of the New Sun, a possible identity of Blue with Ushas, and Quetzal's ambiguous intentions in Long Sun.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Blue may be Ushas; Short Sun concerns justice of occupying foreign territory

Thanks to Michael for his introduction and to Robert Borski and Alice Turner for their welcomes. Thanks also to all who've responded to the "Nova Express" article. I've written a sequel article which has yet to find a publisher; if anyone's interested, I can e-mail that to you. Regarding my speculation that the inhumi may be a good deal more sympathetic than most would believe (sorry for discussing this here, but Whorl is inactive these days): first, my gratitude to Michael for calling on the pitchforks to stand down; a speculative spoiler is not a spoiler at all. And Michael, you say you know no more about the content of Short Sun than the publishers do: but don't they have the manuscripts of Volumes One and Two to hand? So what do you know? I don't mind spoilers; you can tell me in private correspondence. Second, the colonialism question: there's a rather useful commentary on Fifth Head in a volume entitled "Science, Myth, and the Fictional Creation of Alien Worlds" by Albert Wendland (Ann Arbor, Mich: UMI Research Press, 1982). Something worth adding to secondary bibliographies. I think it's a reasonable guess that, considering how colonial guilt haunts both Fifth Head and TBotNS, a concern of Short Sun will be the justice of anyone occupying someone else's territory unasked (assuming, of course, that Blue isn't Ushas, in which case the cargo are simply returning home). Wolfe does a masterful job of keeping Quetzal's true intentions and moral nature ambiguous in Long Sun; this is a foundation for SOMETHING remarkable.
//--------------------------------------
Nicholas Gevers

1999-04-10 Michael Straight #

Argues that Horn, a colonist narrator in the Long Sun books, may have portrayed Quetzal and the inhumi unfairly, projecting his own reasons for disliking them into the story. Builds this on the point that scenes of Quetzal, such as those with Remora, are speculative reconstruction or second- or third-hand accounts rather than direct observation.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Horn projects colonist bias onto Quetzal and the inhumi

Speaking of ambiguity, we might remember that the Long Sun books were written by a colonist (Horn) who may not have been entirely impartial in his portrayal of Quetzal and the inhumi. Some of the scenes where we get the most characterization of Quetzal must be somewhat speculative reconstruction on Horn's part, or at best second or third hand accounts (such as the scenes with Quetzal and Remora).

As a colonist, Horn may have reasons (real or imagined, just or unjust) to dislike the inhumi that he projects back into the Long Sun narrative.

-Rostrum

1999-04-12 Peter Westlake #

Asks whether the Whorl list has stopped working and suggests it could host discussion of The Book of the Short Sun after publication, keeping spoilers separate until readers have finished it. Responds to Nicholas Gevers's remark that Whorl is inactive and a post about the new book was off-list there.
proposes

Has it stopped working? It seemed okay last time I posted to it. It wouldn't be inactive if you had posted this to it, and there are quite a lot of spoilers in these messages. Of course, it is a while since tBotLS was published, and most people will have read it by now; so maybe "whorl" could become a list for discussion of the Book of the Short Sun when it is published, to keep messages separate until we all get a chance to read the Book?

SBear.
