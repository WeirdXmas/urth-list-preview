2006-11-14 Rex Lycanthrosaurus replying to thalassocrat #

Argues that the god unseen in Latro's books is Hades, since Latro's phrase 'Receiver of Many' translates back to Polydectes, an epithet of Hades, and Pindar may know him by such names. Replies to thalassocrat's question about Pindar, citing TheoWiki's etymology, and rejects East's Akhenaten guess for the memory palace's lost temple.
answers · puzzle-solving · source-and-allusion · theories: Receiver of Many is Hades' epithet Polydectes; Latro's memory palace based on Mesopotamian temple

Latro uses the name 'Hades' only once in the first two books, preferring instead 'Receiver of Many,' which he uses at least three times. 'Receiver of Many,' translated back into Greek is Polydectes--indeed, an epithet of Hades. So perhaps Pindar is more familiar with Polydectes or any of Hades' other names.

TheoWiki (http://www.theowiki.com/index.php/Hades) also asserts that "[Hades] is best known in Hellas under the name Plouton/ Ploutonas" and that "Hades is the most recent form. An older form of his name is Aides or Aidoneus and an even older form is Ais, which means house and refers to the Underworld as the House of Hades."

Again, according to TheoWiki, "the meaning of the names Ais, Aides or Aidoneus or Hades is 'The Invisible' or 'Invisibility giving', in contrast with Helios 'The Visible', 'the Visible Giving'. It also makes a greater contrast between Zeus and Hades whose name once meant 'the brightness of the day'."

Latro's memory palace is almost certainly based on a real structure, and it seems probable that something he's done here (along with his 100 fellow mercenaries) is what's earned him the enmity of Gaea/Demeter. But is this "lost temple" Egyptian in origin or Assyrian/Sumerian? In my opinion the sphinxes, griffins, winged bearded bulls, and man-bull make the latter more likely.

2006-11-15 thalassocrat #

Argues that the "god unseen" in Latro's Sidon is not Hades but a radiant solar deity linked to Latro's memory palace, possibly an image of Akhenaten's destroyed Aten temple at Karnak. Grounds this in Pindar's ignorance of the god, the Christian usage in Colossians 1, the Maiden's description of the god as radiant, and Latro's repeated meetings with Hades; replies to Rex Lycanthrosaurus, who had argued for Hades.
proposes · source-and-allusion · puzzle-solving · theories: the god unseen is a radiant solar deity; Latro's memory palace is Akhenaten's Aten temple at Karnak

On Wed, 15 Nov 2006 04:24:45 +1100 Rex Lycanthrosaurus

Good point! But I'm going to stick with the my interpretation for now. Pindar says he doesn't know who is meant by "the god unseen" - why wouldn't he have picked up on a Hades reference? And it seems to be a well-recognized christian usage - eg Colossians 1 (tks to Google). Plus the Maiden's reference to the god (assuming it's the same one) as a radiant being, which doesn't seem to fit Hades very well. Finally, Latro actually meets Hades a couple of times, which weakens the "unseen" aspect.

Though there could be a double meaning.

Anyway, my half-baked guess at the moment is that there's a connection with Latro's memory palace, which will turn out to be an image of Akhenaten's destroyed temple to Aten in Karnak. East of the Nile; huge, open, airy; a radiant supreme/exclusive solar deity; a dead city; Karnak has an avenue of sphinxes (although all ram-headed, I think, but there were sphinx's in the Aten temple too).
