Fechin

Urth list · 3 messages · 1998-12-07 to 1998-12-14

1998urthThe Book of the New SunKieran ClearyMichael Andre-DriussiDerek Bell
The thread: Participants asked whether the name Fechin, borrowed by Gene Wolfe for the Old Man in Casdoe's house, traces to the 7th-century Irish saint St. Fechin. Kieran Cleary supplied the saint's biography from a county archaeology text, and m.driussi checked the Oxford Dictionary of Saints, noting a death date of 665 and a Scottish cult, and tested the link through the shared red hair of Fechin and journeyman Roche. Derek Bell disputed the red-hair argument, saying the Irish are not notably redder than other Europeans, and suggested Fechin's monks might have founded a Scottish church. The thread stays at the level of source-hunting and does not settle whether the name is meant as an allusion.
  • Kieran Cleary: Fechin is the Irish saint whose church stands near his Sligo home, and his biography supports a link
  • m.driussi: the Oxford saint entry and the shared red hair with Roche make a possible link worth testing; asks for the root meaning
  • Derek Bell: red hair is a weak sign of Irishness, so the link is doubtful; the saint may have had Scottish ties

Themes: Names and their meanings, Myth and folklore sources

1998-12-07 Kieran Cleary #

Reports that St. Fechin, the 7th-century Irish saint whose church stands near the poster's home in Sligo, is the source of the name, and quotes a county archaeology text giving his royal descent, his education, his abbacies at Cong and Fore, and his death in 664. Offers this as supplementary information for a mantis post, noting the poster lacks Lexicon Urthus at hand.
informs · source-and-allusion

mantis,

I don't have Lexicon Urthus to hand, so you probably already have this, but seeing as St. Fechin's 7th century church is in my locale, I should report that:

"Legend tells us that St. Fechin was of the royal blood of Ireland, a descendent of Fionn Fuathart, brother to Conn of the Hundred Battles, and of the same race as St. Bridget. St. Nathy of Achonry supposedly educated St. Fechin. The Annals tell us that St. Fechin was also abbot at Cong in County Mayo and at Fore in Westmeath. He died in 664 AD.", Archaeology in County Sligo, Highwood Community Resource Centre, 1998

FWIW.

1998-12-07 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Reports what the Oxford Dictionary of Saints says about the saint Fechin (died 665, also called Vigean, with a Scottish cult) and tests whether he links to Fechin of Urth, the Old Man in Casdoe's house. Grounds the search in the red hair shared by Fechin and journeyman Roche, and in a failed attempt to link the Old Man to Rudesind via a shared saintly friend, then asks for the root meaning of the name.
informs · source-and-allusion · theories: Fechin of Urth derives from the Irish saint Fechin

Hello Sligo (and hello Kieran Cleary)!

Well we do have some info on Fechin via THE OXFORD DICTIONARY OF SAINTS (which mentions the Co. Sligo), but the stingy Lexicon only says "Saint Fechin died 665" (date from Oxford book, at odds with your date) because I couldn't find any hint leading to "artist," "portraits," or even "painting" or "art" (i.e., threads leading to Fechin of Urth) connected with this saint.

According to the Old Man in Casdoe's house, Fechin of Urth had red hair on his head and arms (III, ch. 15). So that's an easy association with "Irish" right there (it also links back to journeyman Roche's red hair, which prompted the autarch-as-procurer to speak about how "Far to the south, in the narrow lands, the savages paint a fire spirit much like you" [I, ch. 9] . . . ), but it isn't much to go on.

According to the Oxford, Fechin is also known as Vigean, which "corrupted to Virgin" (I like that!). Fechin had a cult in Scotland.

Trying to deduce the name of the Old Man in Casdoe's house by triangulating "Rudesind" with "Fechin," in the hope that these two saints might have a saintly friend in common (ooh, clever!), makes the "crash and burn at the starting gate" as Rudesind is a 10th century fellow!

Maybe somebody can find the root meaning (onomastic) of Fechin (it ain't in any of my books), and maybe this has some connection to Fechin of Urth.

=mantis=

1998-12-14 Derek Bell #

Argues that Irish people are not notably more red-haired than other European peoples, so the red hair of Fechin of Urth gives only a weak link to Irishness. Suggests Fechin or his monks may have founded a church or monastery in Scotland, since Sligo is close to Scotland; the post answers a quoted note on Fechin's red hair and Roche's.
disputes · source-and-allusion · theories: Fechin is linked to Irish origins through red hair; Fechin or his monks founded a church in Scotland

Just to note that Irish people aren't really that more
redhaired than most other European peoples. Unfortunately, I don't have figures to back that up, but I haven't noticed any visible difference.

I wouldn't be surprised if Fechin or some of his monks founded a church or even monastery in Scotland - it's not too far from Sligo to Scotland.

Derek
