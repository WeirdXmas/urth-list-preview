2008urthThe Book of the New SunPedro PereiraMatthew GrovesYves Meynard
The thread: A roll-call introduction by Yves Meynard, a longtime Canadian reader, turned into a brief exchange about other fantasy writers. Meynard recommended Vance and Crowley and praised Mieville's Perdido Street Station and The Scar while calling Iron Council disappointing. Matthew Groves said he had abandoned Crowley's Little, Big and The Solitudes, wanted to like Crowley, and suggested Alice Turner's book on Wolfe as a reason to reread. Pedro Pereira replied only that Wolfe is among the greatest living writers, offering no argument about the texts. The thread ended without real debate about Wolfe's work.
  • Yves Meynard: longtime reader who recommends Vance and Crowley and praises Mieville's early novels
  • Matthew Groves: abandoned Crowley's novels but wants to like him and may reread with Turner's book
  • Pedro Pereira: Wolfe is one of the greatest living writers, asserted without argument

Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Genre: science fiction, fantasy, both

2008-05-12 Yves Meynard #

Introduces the poster as a longtime Canadian reader who first bought The Shadow of the Torturer in 1981 and has met Wolfe, joining a roll call thread. Adds recommendations of Vance and Crowley and gives opinions on Mieville, praising Perdido Street Station and The Scar while calling Iron Council sloppy and disappointing.
chats

I was wondering why there was so much traffic on the list all of a sudden and feared it was bad news followed by a rash of shocked comments. Glad to find out it's a roll call instead -- I'll briefly add my voice to the choir.

I'm a Canadian, first bought a Wolfe novel (_Shadow_) in 1981, the day before my 17th birthday. I've met Gene, he's a very nice guy. His work has influenced my writing -- I used to joke I wanted to be Gene Wolfe when I grew up.

I'll second the recommendations for Vance and Crowley
enthusiastically. I really liked Mieville's _Perdido Street Station_ and _The Scar_. _Iron Council_, on the other hand, feels to me like his writing has gone sloppy. I stopped about halfway through it and expect to finish it some day, but it's been a disappointment.

- Yves

2008-05-13 Matthew Groves replying to Yves Meynard #

Asks why Crowley's Little, Big and The Solitudes failed to engage the poster after abandoning both, noting that Wolfe's Wizard Knight seems to show a feedback loop of influence with Crowley. Responds to Yves Meynard's remarks on Vance, Crowley and Mieville by saying the poster wants to like Crowley and points to Alice Turner's book on Wolfe as a reason to look again.
questions

Judging by Wizard Knight, there seems to be a feedback loup of influence.

I have started and abandoned two Crowley novels, Little, Big and The Solitudes. I want to like Crowley, but The Solitudes just seemed pretentious and boring to me. Everyone who reads Wolfe says he is great, and Alice Turner wrote a whole book on him. What am I missing?

Matt G.

2008-05-13 Pedro Pereira replying to Matthew Groves #

Replies to Matthew Groves, who found John Crowley's novels pretentious and asked what he was missing in Gene Wolfe, by asserting that Wolfe is one of the greatest living writers. Offers no argument about the texts; the post is a brief rebuttal to Groves's question and a defense of Wolfe's reputation.
