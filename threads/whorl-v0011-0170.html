Odin and Mithra

Themes: Myth and folklore sources

2000-08-24 Alice Turner #

Compares Odin and Mithra as sources for the Long Sun books, matching Odin's steed, ravens, wolves and Mithra's raven, dog, snake and scorpion companions to Blue's elephant, Horn, Silk and the snakes. Grounds the comparison in a quoted description of Odin from H. R. Ellis Davidson, and also backs Falcon's view against identifying Oreb with Scylla or Kypris.
proposes · source-and-allusion · myth-and-structure · theories: Long Sun characters echo Odin and Mithra companions; Silk's bull slaughter echoes Mithra

I was looking through H. R. Ellis Davidson's -Gods and Myths of Northern Europe- (for reasons entirely unrelated to this list) and was struck by this pair of sentences relating to Odin.

"He was the ancient one-eyed god, crafty and skilled in magic lore, a great shape-changer, and an expert in the consultation of the dead. He was the rider on the eight-legged steed, the wanderer up and down the earth, the god knowing the secrets of travel between the worlds."

The steed is, on Blue, an elephant. Odin had a pair of ravens and a pair of wolves. Horn has only one of each, but that's fair enough.

The companions of Mithra, OTOH, are a raven, a dog, a snake and a scorpion. We lack only the scorpion, so far (but we have a series of snakes). Moreover, Mithra is usually portrayed in ritual slaughter of a bull. That's what Silk does for a living too. I bring these fellas up to remind our pateras that Wolfe is nothing if not inclusive.

And speaking of Oreb (weren't we?) I agree with Falcon on the Scylla (or Kypris) question: it is not logical and it would make Oreb considerably less novelistically interesting than he is.

-alga
