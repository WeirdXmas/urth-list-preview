Themes: Christ figures and messiahs

2009-02-10 David Stockhoff #

Agrees that Severian is a quasi-Christ figure and adds a contrast: a line in Claw where a character says Severian could become a carpenter or fisherman is read as drawing a difference between Severian and Jesus. Grounds the point in that quoted line, comparing Severian's torturer trade with the carpenter and fisher of men, and quotes the post from another list.

I am fully on-board in the thoughts that Severian is a quasi-Christ (as has been said here a number of times and a number of different ways that he represents some of the aspects of Christ).

I posted this on the Gene Wolfe Book Club yesterday:

The following occurs in Claw:

"To me you're Life, and you're a
young man named Severian, and if you wanted to put on different clothes and become a carpenter or a fisherman, no one could stop you."

To me this indicates that Severian is like Christ in some ways but Wolfe is here drawing a difference between Jesus (carpenter by trade and fisher of men) and Severian (torturer by trade and doesn't so much fish men as wangle his way into position as head of the fish factory).
