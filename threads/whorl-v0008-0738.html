1998-09-07 mary whalen #

Lists dictionary senses of the word pike: a long carnivorous fish (Esox lucius), the Esocidae family, and a spear-type weapon, then notes that Latin lupus can mean both wolf and pike. Ties these to the Whalen-named character Sean (prion), an old augur on Sun Street who is Blood's dead father. Offers no argument, only a gloss of the name and its animal and weapon associations.
informs · source-and-allusion

This is Sean Whalen (prion).

Pike. A long thin carnivorous fish (Esox lucius), also called the northern pike. Also, any species in family Esocidae that resembles the pike. Also the name for a variety of weapons like the spear. Also, the Latin word lupus can mean either wolf or pike. Old augur on Sun Street. Blood's father. Dead.

1998-09-07 Alastair Reynolds #

Admits that the word "Pike" in the quoted glossary entry calls to mind Private Pike from the British sitcom Dad's Army rather than the fish or weapon. Suggests this may be a peculiarly British association and jokingly speculates on Wolfe's taste in comedy.
chats

permission to speak...

I don't know if this was a phenomenon unique to British readers of the book, but I couldn't read "Pike" without thinking of Private Pike from Dad's Army, probably not quite the effect Wolfe intended...(although, as apparently GW is a Benny Hill fan, who knows?)
