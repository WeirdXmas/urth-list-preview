Inhumi secret

Urth list · 7 messages · 2012-11-28 to 2012-11-29

2012urthThe Book of the Short Sunnate jarvisJerry FriedmanLee BermanDavid StockhoffAndrew Mason
The thread: Readers debated whether the Rajan's doubt that the inhumi secret is a weapon too heavy for human hands also applies to the Neighbors, and whether the Neighbors' final coordination changed the inhumi's fears. David Stockhoff argued the Neighbors were morally better at handling dangerous knowledge and that the inhumi see potential for good in humans that humans miss. Andrew Mason questioned why the inhumi would fear a secret no people could wield, noting the Neighbors were overwhelmed. Jerry Friedman playfully speculated the inhumi might prefer working to feeding, while Lee Berman proposed a spiritual progression across the Sun series that makes the Neighbors immune to inhumi evil. The thread shifted from the secret itself toward broader speculation rather than settling the question.
  • David Stockhoff: the Neighbors handled dangerous knowledge better than humans and the inhumi see unrecognized good in humans
  • nate jarvis: asks whether the Rajan's weapon remark also covers the Neighbors and whether their coordination frightens the inhumi
  • Andrew Mason: the Rajan's doubt undercuts the secret's usefulness and the Neighbors could not have been the threat
  • Jerry Friedman: inhumi raised by self-reliant humans might have preferred work to blood, with playful roles like messengers or clerics
  • Lee Berman: Neighbors in Short Sun are immune to inhumi evil because they exist less fully in matter, within a spiritual progression across the Sun series

Themes: Gods, angels and the Increate, Gnosticism, heresy and other theologies, Power, rulers and politics

2012-11-28 David Stockhoff replying to nate jarvis #

Argues that the inhumi see a potential for good in humans that humans do not see in themselves, and that they may exaggerate it because the secret endangers them. Responds to Nate Jarvis's quotation of the Rajan by noting Blue's smaller population and possibly surprising uncorruptible Neighbors make the inhumi's hope more plausible.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Inhumi inflate human potential because the secret endangers them; Neighbors may have surprised the inhumi by being uncorruptible

Actually, I think they see a potential for good in us that we don't. They may inflate it somewhat because it represents a grave danger to them. But they are smart enough to not take it for granted---at least those who converse with the Rajan don't. Of course, the population of humans on Blue is much smaller than that on Earth, so it may seem more realistic on Blue than here.

Perhaps their experience with the Neighbors surprised them in some way---they may have found a few uncorruptible Neighbors; presumably not too farfetched for humans, however "impossible."

2012-11-28 Andrew Mason #

Argues that the Rajan's doubt about the inhumi secret undercuts its usefulness, and questions why the inhumi fear it if no people could wield it. Quotes the Rajan's line calling the secret a weapon too heavy for human hands and notes the Neighbours were overwhelmed by the inhumi, so they could not have been the threat.

We should bear in mind, though, that the Rajan himself is very doubtful about whether the secret is really useful. "If you will, it is a great and terrible weapon. That is how the inhumi themselves see it, and I will not call them wrong. But it is a weapon too heavy for our hands."

This does of course raise the question why the inhumi themselves feel threatened by it. Did they ever meet a people who were able to use it? Certainly not the Neighbours, who were also overwhelmed by them.

2012-11-29 Lee Berman replying to Andrew Mason #

Argues that the Neighbors in Short Sun have become immune to Inhumi evil because they no longer exist fully in the material plane, and proposes that the whole Sun series traces a spiritual progression across its twelve books. Builds the case by ordering Green, the Whorl, Urth, Blue and Ushas as successive stages toward higher beings like the hierotypes and the Green Man, and agrees with David Stockhoff's reply to Andrew Mason.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · theology-and-religion · theories: Sun series traces a spiritual progression across twelve books; Neighbors are immune to Inhumi because they left the material plane

I agree with this. I get the sense that the Neighbors, though previously highly susceptible to the evil of Inhumi are now immune to them. Perhaps this is related to the fact that they no longer exist entirely in our physical, material plane

I think this general direction of thinking might get to the heart not only of Short Sun but perhaps the whole Sun series. I've long gotten the sense that Wolfe is depicting a kind of spiritual progression across the 12 books.

I'll guess Green is the starting point and the Edenic associations to it support that. Urth and The Whorl, war-torn and ruled by superhuman pagan gods, is probably the next stage in the progression. Ushas and Blue would be next; the Flooding somehow keeps the rule of the giant gods at a more manageable level?

As seems to be Wolfe's wont, he doesn't directly address the impact of Christ's arrival in his fiction. But advanced beings like the hierotypes, The Green Man and Neighbors would seem to illustrate what the next stage beyond our own current status might be.
