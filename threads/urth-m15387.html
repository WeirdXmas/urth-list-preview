The thread: The thread began with Lee Berman arguing that Return to the Whorl links Typhon/Pas to wind and that Pas's name derives from Pazuzu, which Gerry Quinn and others dismissed as minor imagery. It sprawled into whether the Conciliator is a composite or Christ-like figure, whether Severian makes repeated trips to Typhon's era, and whether Severian and recurring characters are watched by monkeys, aquastors or time-loop forces. Participants including James Wynn, Jeff Wilson, Andrew Mason and Sonofwitz offered competing time models such as cycles, amphisbaena, and two opposed futures. Exchanges grew increasingly personal, with António Pedro Marques and Berman attacking each other's readings and methods, and the thread drifted into health digressions. It ended without agreement, trailing into Dumézil and Pan comparisons.
  • Lee Berman: Typhon/Pas is a Spring Wind god linked to Pazuzu, Severian made other trips to Typhon's era, and Wolfe's gods parallel monkeys and Dumézil's pairs
  • James Wynn: The why questions matter most, cycles collapse time and make Christian echoes present in the Solar Cycle
  • Gerry Quinn: Whirlwind imagery is minor, and the Long Sun Christian parallels are real but Wolfe's works are not puzzle boxes
  • Jeff Wilson: The Conciliator's wonder-working is sincere, Severian's appearances are stagecraft, and the time model is two opposed futures
  • António Pedro Marques: Theories must rest on evidence, the monkey and GUT schemes trample facts, and Christian echoes need an actual basis
  • Sonofwitz: Time is an amphisbaena with forward and backward perspectives so the story stays a single fixed timeline

Themes: Time loops and causality, Catholic faith and sacraments, Christ figures and messiahs

2010-11-03 Lee Berman #

Argues that Return to the Whorl repeatedly links Typhon/Pas to wind, offering new support for the Typhon-as-Spring-Wind theory and a Greek-Egyptian allegorical reading of Typhon. Proposes that Pas's name derives from the wind demon Pazuzu rather than Pan, citing its explicit wind imagery as evidence and noting the earlier books never addressed the connection.
proposes · source-and-allusion · myth-and-structure · theories: Typhon is Spring Wind; Pas's name derives from Pazuzu; Typhon is Greek and Egyptian allegory

Still pecking through RttW and finding interesting revelations. One thing I find striking are the numerous mentions of Typhon/Pas as a god of winds. Why are we hit over the head so hard with this when the connection wasn't addressed at all in the previous five books of the Long/Short Sun series? (unless we count the hot, drought winds plaguing the Whorl during Silk's ascendence).

I wonder if Wolfe might have treated RttW a bit like UotNS: some extra help on understanding some of the more impenetrable mysteries of the previous books. I can see more support for James' Typhon-as-Spring Wind theory here. Plus more full support for seeing BotNS Typhon as mythological (Greek and Egyptian) allegory not just a guy with multiple heads. I guess it might also support my idea that Pas' name is derived from wind demon Pazuzu rather than just Pan.

Moreover in current research, I'm finding more religious connection between Pan and demons than I was previously aware of. Others more versed in religion might have been very familiar but I'm finding some interesting associations in regard to the full Sun series. More on that later.

It TELLS us nothing in itself. The _why_ questions are the point. If Typhon is Spring Wind and/or the Student, what is the significance of his sons? "Why did Wolfe have the character mention this here?"*** For me, wondering why is always the start. Sometimes you come up with answer.

2010-11-04 Lee Berman replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that the late wind references to Typhon/Pas in Sword of the Lictor are deliberate, supporting James's Spring Wind theory and linking Typhon's Titan status to the gods of Urth. Replies to Gerry Quinn's question about the wind's meaning, citing Mandragora ties, the seventeen gods, and Severian's exaggerated legend as a parallel.
supports · source-and-allusion · myth-and-structure · theories: Wind references signal Pas as Typhon; Urth's gods echo Olympian pantheon; Severian's legend parallels Typhon's myth-making

I'd have to check but I think there are at least 3 remarks on the wind stuff. I agree with James that the very fact of the mention of wind here has meaning. Starting with Sword of the Lictor, Wolfe has had 10 books in which to deal with this Typhon/Pas character. Why, finally, in the tenth is wind mentioned?

Perhaps it is just a late attempt to add a bit more decoration and color to a boring character? If that rings true for you then it is a good hypothesis. For me it seems almost impossible for that to be Wolfe's only intention with this.

It seems like a pointer that shouldn't be ignored. To James it points to Spring Wind To me, mythological Typhon and his cognates. The mythological connection does suggest things to me about the character. First that he is connected to the Mandragora. Next that mythological Typhon's status as a Titan might relate to a current Olympian status of the "gods" on Urth. A shame there seem to be 17 instead of 12 but hey, can't be TOO obvious. Some other stuff too.

I was more skeptical of James' spring Wind theory at first but I can see the slim but still supporting evidence. Moreover it seems to bolster the theme of exaggerated legend- religion that Wolfe seems to address. We are explicitly shown that Severian's modest accomplishments as Concilliator (a few healings, a resurrection, maybe some geological manipulation) are exaggerated into a society altering cult-figure. Not hard to see how the same process could take Urth's Typhon and turn him into a hundred-headed man- dragon. Or a benign demiurge on the Whorl.
