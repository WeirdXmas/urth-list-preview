Themes: Hidden identities and parentage

2001-04-17 Allan Lloyd #

Argues that Horn's adultery before Seawrack is hinted at through a line in On Blue's Waters, where Seawrack mentions Tamarind, whom the glossary calls a fishmonger's widow. Grounds the claim in the page 249 quotation and the Proper Names list, answering Adam's question about clues to Horn's affair.
answers · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Tamarind was Horn's lover, a fishmonger's widow

Adam asks what clues there are to Horn's adultery before Seawrack. Page 249 of OBW Seawrack says "Well, a woman is what I am. Like your Nettle, or the Tamarind you talk about sometimes..."

And in the list of Proper Names in the Text, Tamarind is listed as "a fishmonger's widow".

So that's what Horn got up to on his trips to town to sell paper!

Allan
