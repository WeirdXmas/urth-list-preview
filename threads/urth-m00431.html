2005-08-01 Don Doggett replying to Tony Ellis #

Proposes that the tomb in The Book of the New Sun may hold Ymar as well as Severian, and that Severian, Ymar, Horn and Silk may be twins or clones separated through time. Offers the ox symbolism of the name Ymar and the Able and Arnthor doubling in The Wizard Knight as support, while calling the idea speculation.
proposes · puzzle-solving · allegory-and-typology · theories: Severian's tomb also holds Ymar; Ymar and Severian are twins separated through time; Horn is a clone of Ymar; Ymar is Typhon's bastard

(followed by relevant support)

However, this doesn't preclude it being Ymar's tomb as
well. It's a mausoleum which probably holds many
bodies. The name Ymar can also mean ox (horn
anyone?hoof, hide, sinew?) and the ox is symbolic of
the Aleph. And Sev doesn't have to be a clone of Ymar
to -be- him. Witness how Able and Arnthor are both
"King Arthur" in WK. My fancy (and that's all it is)
is that they are clones of each other in the most
mundane of all ways: they are twins, separated
through time. (though I guess being separated through
eons of time isn't so mundane after all). Come to
think of it, if Horn is a clone of Ymar, (who is
undoubtedly, in my mind, Typhon's bastard- there is no
other way to legitimize his rule. The conciliator's
intercession was far too peripheral to support a
successful rebellion) it makes much more sense why
Silkhorn would meet Severian. But I'm rampantly
speculating now. I'll do that in my own head and save
you the eyestrain.

Don

2005-08-01 Tony Ellis #

Argues that Severian is the man buried in the small stone mausoleum with a rose, fountain and flying ship that he mentions at the end of Citadel, so he has himself tombed across history after bringing the new sun. Grounds this in the closing lines of The Citadel of the Autarch and in Severian's time-travelling and resurrection abilities, answering Roy's point about the funeral bronze resemblance.
proposes · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Severian is buried in his own tomb; Severian travels through time after bringing the new sun; The rose fountain and flying ship mausoleum belongs to Severian

Apologies for the change of thread, but I wanted to distance this from any discussion of clones.

It's Severian's tomb. At the end of Citadel, talking about the 'other Severian', he says that he "became himself a walker of the corridors [of time]. I know now the identity of the man called the Head of Day.... I know too in whose mausoleum I tarried as a child, that little building of stone with its rose, its fountain and its flying ship all graven. I have disturbed my own tomb, and now I go to lie in it." Citadel XXXVIII

That last sentence pretty much says it all.

After Severian brings the new sun he has both the ability to travel in time and the ability to resurrect, so there's no reason why he couldn't leave tombs all over history. And as someone who has already brought the new sun, there's no reason why he shouldn't include a white fountain in his coat of arms when he does so. (It would be a bizarre thing for Ymar to do, on the other hand.)

To answer your point, Roy, about Severian in Apu-Punchau's chamber looking so much like the figure on the funeral bronze... I think that's just to underline the fact that Severian has now reached the age when he's about to start his time-wanderings.
