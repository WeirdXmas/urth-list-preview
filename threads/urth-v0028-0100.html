The thread: Jim Henley asked the list for the meanings of the names Odilo, Thais and Pega in Wolfe's work. Michael Andre-Driussi answered with saints and historical namesakes, giving Odilo as a tenth-century Cluniac abbot and Saint Thais as a penitent courtesan. 1999-09-11 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Answers Jim's request for the meanings of the names Odilo, Thais and Pega by giving each name's religious or historical namesake and its etymology. Gives Odilo as a tenth-century Cluniac abbot with a Teutonic 'rich' meaning, Saint Pega as a reputed healer of blindness, and Saint Thais as a penitent courtesan with a Greek 'beloved' meaning.
answers · source-and-allusion

Odilo--tenth century Abbot of Cluny who established All Souls's Day. A Teutonic name, meaning "rich."

Pega--Saint Pega (died 719) was reputed to have cured a man of blindness.

Thais--Saint Thais is penitent, saint, patron of fallen women or courtesans in 4th-century Christian legend. A Greek name meaning "beloved."

I hope that helps.

=mantis=

1999-09-13 Tony Ellis #

Explains that Thais is the name of the Athenian slave-courtesan traditionally said to have urged Alexander the Great to burn Persepolis during a drunken victory celebration. Offers this as the origin of the name, answering Jim's request for the meanings of several names, and notes critics use the episode to portray Alexander as a barbarian.
answers · source-and-allusion

Well, I can do Thais: traditionally, that's the name of the Athenian slave\courtesan who encouraged my hero Alexander the Great to torch beautiful Persepolis at the height of a boozy three-day victory celebration. One of those incidents that his critics use to portray him as a drunken barbarian, but you can see how it could have seemed like a really good idea at the time...
