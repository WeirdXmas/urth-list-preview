Themes: The dying sun and the cosmos

2001-03-09 Tony Ellis #

Disputes the suggestion that Erebus and her allies deliberately placed the black hole in the sun, arguing that freezing the only element that supports life and annihilating the world they want to rule would be poor strategy. Points to the statement in The Urth of the New Sun that Severian's bringing of the white fountain helped his enemies, and says nothing in the books suggests a culprit.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: Erebus did not cause the black hole

Jeff Wilson wrote

Well, not Erebus and co, for a start. Freezing the only element that can support you into a solid block of ice isn't much of a long-term strategy. Neither is annihilating the world you want to enslave. In any case, we're specifically told in UOTNS that by bringing the white fountain Severian has helped his enemies.

If there's anything in the books to suggest that anyone is responsible for actually putting the black hole in the sun, I'm not aware of it. I have to say it would considerably weaken the whole premise of the books if there were.

2001-03-10 Jeff Wilson #

Proposes that the forces of darkness may be divided into factions, arguing that Abaia's undines could not survive an icy future while Erebus and Master Ash persist into it. Grounds this in the Urth of the New Sun passage Tony Ellis quotes about Severian's bringing of the white fountain helping his enemies, and the Antarctic setting.
proposes · myth-and-structure · theories: Powers of darkness are divided into factions

Could there be factions among the powers of darkness? Since while Abaia and his undines would be unable to function in the Ragnarok future, Erebus is already in the midst of the frozen wastes of Antarctica, and Master Ash gets by. Ash could be a lower level dupe, of course, but he must serve something that persists into the icy future.
