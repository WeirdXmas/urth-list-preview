2006-11-03 James Crossley replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that the second Borski collection of Wolfe criticism is more interesting than the first because it covers many more texts and avoids overselling a unified theory. Responds to Mo Holkar's remark about the essays' coverage by saying he is glad Wolfe criticism now appears in print on his shelf beside Melville and Perec.
supports

In a way, I find Borski's second book more interesting. By the end of SL, it being all about Urth, I felt that the presentation of some interesting ideas was turning into theory-flogging. <The Long & the Short> covers many more texts, so there's less time for the reader to feel that there's a unified field Wolfe theory to be oversold.

I'm just glad, really, that there's some Wolfe-related criticism in print. Not that the online discussions are less valid, but it's tremendously pleasing to see some actual books on Wolfe sitting on my LitCrit shelf near names like Melville and Perec.

James

2006-11-03 Roy C. Lackey #

Reports that only one essay in the Borski collection The Long and the Short of It addresses the first two Soldier books, while the rest resemble Solar Labyrinth but focus less on the Urth cycle. Responds to Mo Holkar, who asked whether the collection is a useful companion to Soldier of Sidon and had mentioned Solar Labyrinth.
informs

Only one of the essays really touches on the first two SOLDIER books, if that's what you wanted to know. The rest is similar to SL, but isn't concentrated on the Urth Cycle.

-Roy
