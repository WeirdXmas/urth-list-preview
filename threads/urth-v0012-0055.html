I Bet Abo French Girls Are Easy, Too

Urth list · 1 messages · 1998-06-02 to 1998-06-02

1998urthRobert Borski

Themes: Colonization and the native, Hidden identities and parentage

1998-06-02 Robert Borski #

Proposes that Mrs. Blount's wistful memory of French girls at the dance reflects the Annese talent for mimicry, and suggests her resentment may stem from being the daughter of penal colonists. Grounds this in her complaint that boys ignored her at the dance, while allowing she may simply have been unattractive.
proposes · close-textual-reading · genre-and-craft · theories: Mrs. Blount is a mimic of Annese ability; Mrs. Blount resents penal-colonist discrimination

<"those little French girls... weren't they the cutest things? ..one of <those Frenchies would come in, just in rags you know, but with a ribbon <and a flower in her hair, and every boy's head would turn."

<Now doesn't that sound like the famed Annese ability for mimicry?

Another notion is that Mrs. Blount--who's reminiscing about this poutingly, whining about how she'd come to the dance much more elegantly dressed and no boys would look her--is that there is a measure of discrimination being leveled against her, because she's the daughter of penal colonists.

Of course she could have just been plain shovelface ugly too.

I went where Liev went, sinisterly.

Robert Borski
