Rudesind / Inire / Lunar Picture

Urth list · 42 messages · 2010-07-26 to 2010-07-31

2010urthThe Book of the New SunJohn WatkinsJane DelawneyJerry FriedmanRyan DunnLee BermanEdward cottonJosh GellerGerry QuinnDavid StockhoffJeff Wilson
The thread: The thread asks whether Rudesind, the picture-cleaner in The Citadel of the Autarch, is a Cacogen or the secret Father Inire, and whether the phrase 'your blue Urth' betrays that. Edward Cotton proposes the Cacogen-Inire identity, and Jeff Wilson, Gerry Quinn and Jerry Friedman argue the 'your' is a translator's or dialect idiom, citing Hamlet and Irish usage. John Watkins pushes Inire as a real person, while Ryan Dunn and Watkins spar over whether two overlapping Severians appear in the Atrium of Time, with Watkins favouring one time-knotted Severian. Much of the list devolves into jokes about one being behind several posters. The thread ends without agreement, with the Rudesind-Inire identity and the Severian timeline still open.
  • Edward Cotton: Rudesind is a Cacogen and possibly Inire, working covertly with the Autarchs
  • Jeff Wilson: 'your blue Urth' is a translator's marketing idiom, not evidence of identity
  • Jerry Friedman: the 'your' usage is long established in English, citing Hamlet
  • John Watkins: Father Inire is a real person and a Trinity parallel; one time-knotted Severian rather than two
  • Ryan Dunn: Inire may be an individual, Rudesind relays a message to a younger Severian, the two Severians overlap
  • Gerry Quinn: the phrase is an Irish-style dialect idiom, not ownership

Themes: Hidden identities and parentage, Time loops and causality, Unreliable narration

2010-07-26 Jeff Wilson replying to Edward cotton #

Argues that the phrase "your blue Urth" is the translator's idiom rather than evidence that Rudesind is Inire, and that a secret Inire would have hidden this from many autarchs. Responds to Edward cotton by citing the idiom's age and suggesting the two earlier cleanings match the two pre-Severian autarchs tried in Yesod; closing remarks are chatty.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Rudesind is secretly Inire; Cleanings match the two Yesod trials

If Rudesind is secretly Inire, Inire has also hidden this from the Old Autarch, and presumably from as many autarchs as overlap a false-Rudesind's plausible lifetime.

Also, "your blue Urth" is ultimately the translator's choice of words, not necessarily a literal translation of what Severian recorded. "Your [item]" is part of an English idiom that is popular among craftsmen who work on other people's property, perhaps like an engineer who has been hired to improve an existing potato chip production line, and this has become a marketing tool where "your" addresses the audience even if they don't own the item yet, and even if the crafter is promoting a recipe or abstract idea rather than a service.

Googling "your basic" yields 2 million + examples of this modest variation, but the idiom is at least decades old; "Your Show of Shows" debuted in 1950.

Inire has been around since at least the reign of Ymar, about a thousand years before Severian, and there have been dozens of Autarchs during this time. However, there were only two before Severian that stood trial in Yesod, they might correspond to the two earlier cleanings.

2010-07-26 John Watkins replying to brunians #

Argues that Rudesind may be Father Inire in disguise, because Severian, Thecla and the Old Autarch all know Inire and Severian discusses Inire with Rudesind in The Citadel of the Autarch. Responds to Wilson's question about who has met Rudesind by shifting attention to who has met Inire, and admits uncertainty about the deception's purpose.
disputes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Rudesind is Father Inire in disguise

It isn't who else has met Rudesind, it's who else has met Inire.

Severian is for all intents and purposes also Thecla and the Old Autarch, both of whom know Inire. He might has missed the connection in Shadow, when he wasn't other people yet, but in Citadel he is and is thinking and talking about Inire with Rudesind. Sort of a big thing to miss.

It's possible that virtually everything Rudesind says is a lie and he is elaborately disguised to hide the fact that he is Father Inire. I'm not certain what purpose that deception would serve by the time of Citadel.
