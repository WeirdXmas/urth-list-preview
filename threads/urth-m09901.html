Cues and Lolita?

2009urthJonathan GoodwinMatthew Groves
The thread: Matthew Groves proposed a link between the Q, cue and clue wordplay in Nabokov's Lolita and Wolfe's short story "Cues", tentatively and admitting he had not reread it recently. Jonathan Goodwin asked whether anyone had made that connection, said he teaches "Cues" in a short fiction class, and called the story opaque, recalling earlier list talk of scatological readings. Groves replied that the opening quotation mark in the title is simply the start of a line of dialogue and rejected the scatological reading. The exchange was brief and ended with Groves saying he would reread the story, with no broader resolution.
  • Matthew Groves: proposes a Lolita link to Wolfe's "Cues" and recommends the collection as a starting point
  • Jonathan Goodwin: asks whether the Lolita connection has been made, and finds the story opaque
  • Matthew Groves: the opening quotation mark is just dialogue, and the scatological reading of the title is wrong

2009-09-11 Matthew Groves #

Proposes a link between Nabokov's Lolita, with its wordplay on Quilty, cue and clue, and Wolfe's short story "Cues" from The Island of Doctor Death and Other Stories. Offers the idea tentatively, admitting the poster has not reread "Cues" recently, and adds a recommendation that this collection is the best place to start with Wolfe.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Wolfe's "Cues" alludes to Nabokov's Lolita wordplay

Glad to see some activity on the urth list. In the interest of possibly stirring up a little more:

I Lolita for the first time a few months ago -- Amazing!!! (But difficult; I recommend an annotated copy or an accompanying class notes booklet.) There's lots of wordplay throughout, and one that I seem to remember reccurring frequently is play with "Q" (as in "Quilty"), "cue," and maybe "clue." Has anyone made any connection between Lolita and Wolfe's short "Cues" from TIoDDaOSaOS? I haven't even reread "Cues" in a year or so, but I thought I'd throw this idea out there anyway.

If any of you doesn't have any of Wolfe's collections, then that one (The Island of Doctor Death and Other Stories and Other Stories) is the one to begin with, both because it's first and because it's best.

2009-09-11 Matthew Groves replying to Jonathan Goodwin #

Explains that the opening quotation mark in the title "'Cues" is simply the start of a line of dialogue ("Cues?" the young man said), and rejects the scatological reading of the title. Responds to Jonathan Goodwin's teaching plans and his question about the punctuation, and says the poster will reread the story.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Title's opening quote mark is dialogue punctuation

The initial open quote on " "Cues " is there because it's, well, the opening of a line of dialog:

"Cues?" the young (not really so young any more) man said.? "I'm afraid I don't understand."

Are you attaching some additional significance to this punctuation?

I don't like the scatological interpretation. I suppose I'll go ahead and reread the story, now that I've got the book out...
