1999-11-02 Kieran Mullen #

Argues that science is largely absent from the Urth books because publication of work is banned, so scientists work alone, unlike the open criticism that marks real science. Offers the Seekers of Truth and Penetance as a possible scientific group, while conceding that their knowledge looks more like lore and that discovery on Urth is mostly rediscovery of the Ancients.
proposes · genre-and-craft · myth-and-structure · theories: Lack of publication separates Urth science from modern science

Hmmm... As a scientist myself, let me throw in a word or two.

The "scientific method" may still exist after a fashion, but one critical aspect is missing: publication. By that I mean the communication with others of your work so that it can be criticized or checked. The writing of books is banned, so each scientist we see is off on their own. While this may seem a stupid point, I think that it is part of the major break between alchemy and real science. The alchemists tended to guard their secrets jealously.

One possible group of scientists in the Seekers of Truth and Penetance. Theirs is an old and applied art, true, but I get the feeling that they have had to learn from experience and pass that knowledge on. (This argument is a bit weak, since most of their knowledge seems like lore.)

I think that science is pretty absent because on Urth most discovery seems to be rediscovery - i.e. uncovering the wisdom of the Ancients. Everyone seems to be oppressed by how much was known in the past.

Kieran Mullen

1999-11-02 Redhaven1 #

Asks whether Wolfe's idea of recovering a person's whole memory from a single cell parallels the ghola cloning in Herbert's Dune series, and which author came first. Also asks whether there is any real scientific evidence that cells can hold a person's memories or whether this is purely science fiction.
questions · source-and-allusion

I'm sure that someone on the list must have remarked somewhere along the line how much Wolfe's concept of absorbing all a person's memories from one cell in their body resemble's Hebert's work in the Dune series with a ghola being cloned from one cell and still having all of the person's original memories. [Whoa, run on sentence there. . .] Anyway, which came first? Did Wolfe get the idea from Herbert's works or did Herbert develope the idea from Wolfe's? Also, is there any real scientific evidence that our cells can hold all of our memories, or is this probably pure science fiction?

-Steve
