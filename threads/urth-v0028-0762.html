Themes: Women, sex and gender

2000-01-31 Nicholas Gevers #

Argues that the stories in the Shadows of the Sun collection share a theme of escape, with only one route, the path to Heaven, being true while others are snares, and reads Emory's route in "The Ziggurat" as self-indulgent domination leading nowhere good. Grounds this in comparing Emory's course with the trends of the other stories in the collection, and defers a detailed reading of "The Ziggurat" until a later review.
proposes · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: Stories in ST share a theme of escape; Emory's route in The Ziggurat leads to damnation

Nutria, I'm afraid I'll have to delay my detailed take
on "The Ziggurat" until I've finished another, rather
vexatious critical chore; but you're quite right that
my ST review will only appear in print some time off
(in FOUNDATION), so here's a general comment. It seems
to me that the stories in ST were selected with a
theme in common, that of escape. The world must be
escaped or transcended; but the escape routes are
many, and only one (the route to Heaven) is a true
path. The rest are snares, roads to damnation and
disaster. Emory's route is one such: self-indulgence,
an impulse to dominate. It may make him feel in charge
of his destiny for a while, but read in comparison
with the trends of the other stories, it won't lead
anywhere good.

2000-01-31 James Jordan #

Accepts Jim Jordan's overall view of the Strange Travelers collection as a book about escape, but reconsiders the link between 'taming' and 'Tamar' in 'The Ziggurat' as male chauvinism rather than a step forward. Says this reading would give a sadder ending without pushing it into the most negative readings discussed on the list, and asks Alga for thoughts.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Taming and Tamar link shows male chauvinism

Mebbe. I'll need more to be convinced. I accept your overall assessment of the book as a collection. Maybe I should take the link between "taming" and "Tamar" more negatively, as a further example of male chauvinism instead of as a step in the right direction. Hmmm. That would not push me into any of the extremely negative interpretations we've discussed here, but would indicate a sadder ending than I'd originally read. Any thots, Alga?

Nutria
