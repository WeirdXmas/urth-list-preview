PEACE: Gift Exchange

Urth list · 3 messages · 2008-04-22 to 2008-04-23

The thread: Dave Tallman argued that Della, not Mr. Elliot, sent the malicious Christmas gift exchange in Peace, pearls for toilet water, as a warning to Mab, and he offered a supernatural alternative in which the midnight visitor is Old Nick. Helen Algmin agreed that his reading was a good explanation and found the toilet-water gift a terrible present. Roy C. The thread ends in a split between the Della and old-man readings, with no resolution.

2008-04-22 Dave Tallman #

Argues that Della, not Mr. Elliot, sent the malicious Christmas gift exchange of pearls for toilet water in Peace's first chapter, meant to warn Mab off her father. Supports this by pointing to Della's condescension toward Mrs. Green, her "Call me Della" line, and Mab's retreat upstairs, and offers a supernatural alternative in which the midnight visitor is Old Nick.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Della sent the Christmas gift exchange, not Mr. Elliot; The midnight visitor was Old Nick, the Devil

I started thinking about the Christmas gift exchange (pearls for toilet water) in the first chapter, and what it reveals about Weer's family.

I don't believe Mr. Elliot did it. Not that he didn't sleep with Mab that night; Roy Lackey proved he that did in this post:
http://www.urth.net/urth/archives/v0210/1901.txt.shtml. But "Old men... do not make such gifts."

I believe that Della did it, but her motive wasn't generosity. She exploited her social inferiors (e.g. Mrs. Green as her "slavey"). The exchange was malicious, like her "Call me Della." It told Mab "I know what you're up to with my father." This seems to have worked as intended -- after her initial joy, Mab rushed upstairs to her room and remained there half a day.

If you prefer a supernatural explanation, note that Mr. Elliot didn't say the midnight visitor was "St. Nick," but "Old Nick" (the Devil). A malicious gift exchange is more in his line of work than Santa's. While he was at it, he could have replaced one or more stories in "The Green Fairy Book" with wicked prophecies.

2008-04-23 Roy C. Lackey replying to Dave Tallman #

Argues that the old man in Peace's Christmas gift exchange was not constrained by propriety, and that his switching of the gifts was a deliberate move to end Mab's pretense of being only an employee. Grounds this in the house layout, his being upstairs both before and after Den saw him, and his anger at Mab during dinner, responding to Dave Tallman's claim that old men do not make such gifts.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Elliot switched the gifts to expose Mab's pretence

Wolfe was in his early forties when he wrote that, so he wasn't speaking from personal experience. My experience is just the opposite. Older people tend to be less constrained by social conventions with each passing year, less likely to give a damn what others think. Unless Wolfe screwed up regarding the layout of the house when he wrote that Christmas episode, then the "old man" (huh!) was already thumbing his nose at propriety on Christmas morning by conspicuously having spent the night upstairs.

Note also that he had already been upstairs the night before when Den came down to see the tree. He told Den that he had "just come down myself". (p. 23, Berkley pb) Apparently he was upstairs both before and after Den saw him that night. At dinner earlier that evening he had been pissed at Mab for her pretence that she was anything but an employee. If a six-year-old child was perceptive enough to pick up on that, so was his mother. The old man's switching of the gifts forced an end to the charade. Mab's reaction was embarrassment that the "Bible Christian" (p. 20) conventions she affected in polite company could no longer be maintained.

Della had nothing to gain by switching the gifts but petty spite on a woman she had never even met before; Elliot maintained a bed warmer.

-Roy
