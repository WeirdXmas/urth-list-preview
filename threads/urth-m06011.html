Breakfast with Apheta

Urth list · 20 messages · 2008-07-22 to 2008-07-29

The thread: Roy C. Lackey asked what the breakfast Apheta serves Severian on Yesod signifies, and the thread ranged widely. b sharp read it as a drugged meal echoing Pia's, suggesting Apheta is a prisoner-slave serving the Hierogrammates, while thalassocrat proposed a sham-trial set-up with a Circe hint. Brunians argued the food is not ambrosia, Sue suggested the milky drink reads as semen, and Gwern Branwen disputed John Watkins's claim that the meal is not a Eucharist. Paul B's separate dispute over castration and succession drew back-and-forth with brunians, and the thread drifted toward jokes and hypotheticals. It ended without consensus, with Stanisław Bocian's late post reviving a hierogrammate-proxy theory.
  • b sharp: Apheta is a prisoner-slave who drugs Severian for the Hierogrammates
  • thalassocrat: the drugged breakfast sets up a sham trial, with a Circe echo
  • brunians: the food is not ambrosia; ambrosia and nektar are honey and mead
  • Sue: the milky fluid suggests semen
  • Paul B: castration cannot stop dynasties, and the stated reason for choosing an heir is not the real one
  • Stanisław Bocian: the Hierogrammates used Severian as a lawful executioner to keep their own guilt absent

Themes: Slavery and service, Power, rulers and politics, Gods, angels and the Increate

2008-07-22 Roy C. Lackey #

Asks what significance lies in the breakfast Apheta serves Severian on the morning after they sleep together, noting it is the only meal he mentions eating on Yesod. Quotes the tray's contents, small cakes, cool water and a milky steaming liquid, and recalls that Apheta tastes a cake before he eats one.
questions · close-textual-reading

On the morning after Severian bedded Apheta, she brought him breakfast on a tray. It consisted of "... small cakes, a carafe of cool water, and cups of some steaming liquid that looked like milk and yet was not milk." (URTH, XX, 146) He ate one of the cakes and drank a cup of the milky fluid, after Apheta took a bite of one of the cakes. I think this was the only meal Severian mentioned having on Yesod, and I believe there is some significance to its components or their combination that eludes me. Any ideas?

-Roy

2008-07-23 b sharp replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that Apheta's breakfast tray in The Citadel of the Autarch echoes Pia's drugged meal, suggesting Apheta may be a prisoner-slave who sedates Severian and serves the Hierogrammates. Grounds this in matching details: the cakes, the cup, the tasting order, the repeated mouse simile, and Hethor's voice inside a water carafe in a dream.
proposes · close-textual-reading · allegory-and-typology · theories: Apheta is a prisoner-slave serving drugged meals; Milky drink helps create Severian's New Sun status; Apheta serves the Hierogrammates deceptively

I wish I had a better answer for Roy's query but this is all I come up with. Maybe it is worth something:

The meal reminds me somewhat of the meal Pia serves Severian on a brassy tray. It also has cakes (made of cattail flower and clams) and Pia tests the wine, the fish and the duck before Severian tries them. She doesn't taste the salad and of course that's where the drug mushrooms are.

Apheta's meal is also served on a tray. She tastes the cakes first and then Severian tries them and sips milky drink from the cup. Apheta has to urge Severian to eat before he finshes both. After that Severian says, among other things, "As though the cup were drugged, I seemed to stand infinitely far from myself, to watch myself as a man watches a mote, to hear my own voice as a hawk hears the squeaking of a meadow mouse".

The seeing of himself from far above definitely reminds me of Severian's feeling of double identity when he is both a star (white fountain) and a man. Perhaps the milky drink (in addition to the sex?) did serve as some sort of drug which assists in creating Severian's New Sun status.

Pia was a prisoner-slave when she rather unwillingly served the drugged meal to Severian. Could Apheta be in a similar role? There are a bunch of guys standing watch in her chambers. And in conversation during the meal, Apheta admits that she lied the previous night and didn't desire Severian at all before they coupled; considers him a monster.

Wolfe used the "squeaking of mice" as a simile twice previously in this story. Once by Master Palaemon to say how the words of a prisoner should be heard in SotT. So again we must wonder about who might be a prisoner in this situation. Plus we have a predator/prey relationship mentioned.

The other use of the mouse simile is from CotA in a dream sequence. Severian hears Hethor's voice as the squeaking of a mouse. And in this dream, Hethor happens to be inside a carafe of water, the third item from Apheta's meal. The dream has Hethor babbling on about his ship with its "demon haunted mirror sails, our hundred-league masts" and "My lady, the mate of my soul", "she is her own ship, she is the figurehead of her own ship and captain". Tzadkiel? The dream also includes the Cumaean and Malrubius, who guide Severian to Typhon's eye where he is able to see the fruits of the Urth of the New Sun.

Perhaps I have been unduly influenced by the What's So Great About Ushas thread but thanks to that and Roy I now may perceive another deceptive and perhaps sinister side to Apheta's role and thus the Hierogrammates. Still a rather jumbled view I concede, but it makes one go, "hmmm...".

-bsharp

2008-07-24 Paul B replying to Matthew Keeley #

Points out an inconsistency in the Book of the New Sun: the castration of Epitome candidates is said to prevent dynasties, yet most autarchs never went to Yesod, so the rule would not stop them. Notes that Severian seems unable to recall any autarchs besides Ymar and his predecessor, and that no dynasties appear anyway.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading

Well, they do ostensibly have some surplus autarch testicles lying around... Which incidentally brings up an interesting inconsistency: if I remember correctly, the reason given for the castration of Epitome candidates is to prevent them from forming dynasties, but this measure would clearly not work for the majority of autarchs who apparently never even went to Yesod. (Severian seems to have trouble recalling others besides Ymar and his predecessor)
It's an odd rule to apply to a minor, and perhaps better, part of the pool. Nevertheless, there don't seem to be dynasties anyway.

Paul

2008-07-29 Stanisław Bocian replying to thalassocrat #

Argues that the hierogrammates could create the White Fountain themselves but avoid acting directly, so they needed Severian as a lawful autarch executioner to destroy Urth while keeping their own guilt absent. Grounds the case in the cock-and-angel fable, Apheta's account of hierogrammatic speech, and the Baldanders discussion of hierodules and modulated acts.
proposes · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: Hierogrammates avoid guilt by modulating others' acts; Severian is an autarch executioner, not a magician

Hierogrammates shouldn't be underestimated. They don't NEED Severian to create the White Fountain, they can do it by their own technology. However, they prefer not to act at all. They must be conscious that they know the will of the Uncreate no better than anyone (the fable of the cock and angel says this openly), and so perhaps are unwilling to bear the guild for the mass murder on Urth.

Accordingly, they take care to do nothing themselves at all: Apheta's explanation of their speech shows that they generally don't act themselves, but only modulate the acts of others. The discussion of Hierodules with Baldanders shows that they consider themselves not to be guilty for so "modulated" actions of others.

And so, they needed Severian as an executioner who would lawfully execute Urth. He had to be an autarch, so he could act as the lawful representant of humanity. Except for that, he had no "magic powers". The white fountain and Severian's connection to it was provided by Hierogrammates - of course, in a way which absolves them of all guilt in the matter.

Stanislaus B.
