Wolfe's Out-of-Print Novels

Urth list · 9 messages · 2008-10-10 to 2008-10-13

2008urthThe Book of the New SunTim WaltersMatthew KeeleyMo Holkar / UKGNigel PriceDon DoggettJames CrossleyDan'l Danehy-Oakes
The thread: Readers ranked Wolfe's out-of-print novels and asked which are worth finding, with Free Live Free, Pandora by Holly Hollander, Operation Ares and The Devil in a Forest the most-discussed titles. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes and Mo Holkar offered rankings, Matthew Keeley guessed one that put The Devil in a Forest first, and Nigel Price defended Operation Ares and Pandora. Don Doggett found The Devil in a Forest disappointing next to Barry Unsworth's Morality Play, which James Crossley and Holkar backed, and the thread drifted into non-sf reading recommendations and a wiki page request. It ended amiably with no real critical disagreement resolved.
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: Free Live Free is best, Pandora is the one to skip, Operation Ares beats at least one other
  • Matthew Keeley: asks which out-of-print novels are worth finding, guesses The Devil in a Forest on top
  • Mo Holkar: Free Live Free first, then Castleview, The Devil in a Forest, Pandora; all worth reading
  • don doggett: The Devil in a Forest disappoints beside Unsworth's Morality Play
  • Nigel Price: Operation Ares, Pandora and The Devil in a Forest worth reading despite uneven Free Live Free and Castleview
  • James Crossley: Morality Play is a superb, tightly written short novel

Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Genre: science fiction, fantasy, both

2008-10-11 Nigel Price replying to Matthew Keeley #

Argues that Wolfe's out-of-print novels are worth reading, praising Operation Ares for early themes like alien animals and rebellion, Pandora by Holly Hollander as a Nancy Drew inversion, and The Devil in a Forest. Grounds these views in personal recollection and comparison with the Book of the New Sun and Long Sun books, while finding Free Live Free and Castleview uneven.
supports · genre-and-craft

I enjoyed all the novels on the out-of-print list - even "Operation Ares", which Wolfe himself doesn't seem to rate. All right, it's not a masterpiece, but there are early appearances of several distinctive Lupine themes (from memory - alien animals naturalised on Earth as in TBotNs and rebellion as in TBotLS) and a deep strangeness that I very much enjoyed.

"Pandora by Holly Hollander" is a fine piece of work, a pastiche and inversion of the Nancy Drew novels with an assured use of a young female narrative voice that is relatively unusual (unique?) in the Lupine canon.

"The Devil in a Forest" is also excellent, a well-written and accomplished account of adolescent discovery that hovers on the boundaries of historical fiction and fantasy.

"Free Live Free" and "Castleview" seem uneven to me, the former having its longueurs while the latter seems overly rushed and compressed. They're both very definitely worth reading, though "Free" might have been better shorter and "Castleview" longer.

The completist in me says you really ought to try and read them all!

Nigel
