Father Inire guises

Urth list · 7 messages · 2008-07-04 to 2008-07-05

2008urthThe Book of the New SunJeff WilsonDave TallmanRoy C. Lackeyb sharp
The thread: Posters debated whether Father Inire is a shape-changing immortal like Charon and whether Dorcas' husband, seen on a bier in Severian's account, is really dead or is Inire in disguise. b sharp argued for the fantastic reading, citing the youthful face, Wolfe's habit of veiling marvels in mundane description, and an interview about VRT, while Jeff Wilson countered with prosaic explanations: muscles relax at death, rigor mortis comes only hours later, and the magic has ordinary causes like a tent balloon and an optical illusion. Dave Tallman noted that if Inire plays several roles he must be lying, making his coordinates unreliable. Roy C. Lackey ended the thread by charging that Wilson's method of trusting supporting statements and dismissing contrary ones as lies is unfalsifiable. No agreement was reached; the exchange stalled between the two camps.
  • b sharp: the dead man is Inire in a deliberate fantastic disguise meant to draw Severian into resurrecting Dorcas
  • Jeff Wilson: prosaic explanations suffice, including relaxed muscles, timing of rigor mortis, a tent balloon, and an optical illusion
  • Dave Tallman: if Inire plays several roles he must be lying, so his coordinates are false; a chant-caught Charon might have moved Dorcas
  • Roy C. Lackey: Wilson's method dismisses inconvenient statements as lies, which makes the theory unfalsifiable

Themes: Masks, disguise and shapeshifting, Hidden identities and parentage, Ghosts, dreams and visions

2008-07-05 Jeff Wilson replying to b sharp #

Argues against the idea that Father Inire's apparent youthfulness and straight back on his bier show he is a disguised immortal, saying death relaxes muscles so a tense or emotional person may look younger and straighter. Responds to Jeff Wilson's earlier defense of the theory that Dorcas' husband is not really dead, questioning why Inire would spend years wandering alone if he were immortal.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Father Inire is secretly immortal and not dead

One of the ways of telling a person is dead is that opposing muscle groups are relaxed, so it would not be unusual for a person often in the grip of strong emotion to look more youthful in death, or a wry necked person to lay straighter now that the abnormal muscle tension is gone.

And why would Inire spend years trolling alone, again?

2008-07-05 b sharp replying to b sharp #

Argues that Dorcas's husband could be Father Inire despite the mundane explanations offered, suggesting Inire's youthful look in death is a deliberate, fantastic act meant to draw Severian into resurrecting Dorcas. Responds to Jeff's prosaic readings by noting Wolfe's characters all lie, that Occam's razor suits real-world rather than fictional explanation, and that Wolfe veils fantastic ideas behind mundane description, citing an interview about VRT.
disputes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Dorcas's husband is Father Inire; Inire's youthful appearance in death is deliberate; Inire feigns trolling to lure Severian into resurrecting Dorcas

Jeff if you are trying to make argments for why Dorcas' husband might not be Father Inire you are breaking down an open door. I agree, he might not be. But I think he could be. If you are trying to argue he couldn't possibly be, that's a different story and you'll need more definitive proof, I think.

If he was Father Inire then I doubt he was really spending so much time poling around. More likely it is a designed act to draw Severian into resurrecting Dorcas. Yep, he lies. But so does Severian, Apheta, Tzadkiel, even the old autarch who says he doesn't lie.

On the other hand, who knows.... perhaps Father Inire, who has 1000 years to get his work done has a lot of free time on his hands.

You seem to prefer prosaic explanations, in this case the similarities between Dorcas' husband and Father Inire are explained by you as an attempt at character dialog irony by Wolfe. His young appearance in death is a sort of anti-rigor mortis that relaxes the muscles. There are other examples where you reject a fantastic explanation in favor of a humdrum one. Occam's razor as Roy likes to invoke?

For me, Occam is a prinicple for real world applications. This is a story. Some authors are the sort that would spend pages finding incredible descriptive beauty in a rock or something. Gene Wolfe seems the opposite to me. He seems like a guy with a lot of fantastical ideas in his head who likes to veil them behind mundane and overly casual descriptions sometimes.

Since he is very careful about reavealing anything it is hard to know which portions of his stories are veiled fantaticisms and which are really mundane. The one slip up I know of is where he seems to accidentally spill the beans in an interview that yes, VRT was written by a Shadow Child who has killed and replaced Marsch. With that as my model I am willing to guess that other hints at fantastic scenarios can be believed, if only you guess correctly.

2008-07-05 Jeff Wilson replying to b sharp #

Argues that the corpse Dorcas's husband is not Father Inire's disguise, since muscle relaxation follows death immediately while rigor mortis only sets in hours later, and that Wolfe's magical elements have prosaic explanations like a tent balloon and an optical illusion. Responds to b sharp's challenge to Jeff Wilson's earlier argument, pressing that the prosaic reading applies to the book's marvels generally.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Dorcas's husband is Father Inire in disguise; Wolfe's magic has prosaic explanations

Okay, you have no explanation to back up your earlier supposition then.

Relaxation vs rigor mortis is a question of how long has he been dead. The body relaxes immediately as soon as the nervous system stops delivering signals, and RM doesn't begin to take up until hours later.

He goes the other way as well: the flying cathedral is really just a tent serving as a hot air balloon, and the magical painting is really just an optical illusion.

2008-07-05 Roy C. Lackey replying to b sharp #

Disputes Jeff Wilson's method for Father Inire's guises, arguing that accepting statements which support a theory and dismissing contradictory ones as lies makes the theory unfalsifiable. Quotes Wilson's reply and mocks it as neat, unprovable reasoning, where any inconvenient statement by a character who is more than one character can be safely dismissed as a lie.
disputes · theories: Father Inire is one character in several guises

Jeff Wilson wroted and quote:

That doesn't matter. He cannot be proved wrong. In this school of theorizing, whatever a character (particularly a character who is more than one character) says that seems to support a given theory is to be accepted as true. Whatever that character (or characters) says that is inconvenient to or contradicts that theory can be safely dismissed as a lie. Very simple, very neat.

-Roy
