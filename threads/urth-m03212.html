The thread: Batrinque asked whether Ormsby in The Wizard Knight is an alternative form of Pendragon and whether the helm of true sight has a Norse or Arthurian source, and also proposed that a Wolfe element inverts Wagner's Tarnhelm. Jeff Wilson questioned whether the Tarnhelm's dragon-form power is authentic myth or a Wagnerian invention. Thalassocrat and Martinus Scriblerus disputed the Pendragon link, the latter citing place-name dictionaries that derive Ormsby from Scandinavian words for snake or dragon plus 'by'. Bob Miller offered Advanced Dungeons and Dragons as the easy source of the helm. The thread ended with a drift into an off-topic chat about book buying.
  • Batrinque: asks open questions about Ormsby and the helm, floats a Tarnhelm inversion
  • Jeff Wilson: the Tarnhelm's dragon power may be Wagner's invention, not Teutonic myth
  • thalassocrat: Ormsby means 'village of the dragon', so no Pendragon link, meaning unclear
  • Martinus Scriblerus: Ormsby derives from Old Scandinavian snake/dragon names, supporting thalassocrat
  • Bob Miller: the helm of true sight comes straight from Advanced Dungeons and Dragons

2007-04-08 thalassocrat replying to Batrinque #

Disputes the claim that Ormsby is an alternative form of Pendragon, arguing that the -by suffix denotes a village or town so the name means 'from the village of the dragon'. Responds to Batrinque's question about The Wizard, offering an etymological reading while admitting the story meaning of that name is still unclear.
disputes · source-and-allusion · theories: Ormsby is not an alternative form of Pendragon

I'm in a minority, I think, in believing it isn't. The -by suffix usually means something like "village" or "town", and I take the name to mean "from the village of the dragon". But what *that* means, in story terms, I'm still trying to work out ...

2007-04-09 Martinus Scriblerus replying to thalassocrat #

Reports that the poster checked the Oxford Dictionary of English Place Names and found Ormsby and Ormesby derive from Old Scandinavian personal names meaning snake or dragon plus 'by'. Supports thalassocrat's doubt that Ormsby in The Wizard Knight is an alternative form of Pendragon by citing the Great Ormes Head and Ormside place names as parallels.
informs · source-and-allusion

While visiting a 2nd hand book shop & cafe in St Ives today I bought a copy of the Oxford Dictionary of English Place Names. According to this Ormesby or Ormsby are considered to be a combination of the Old Scandinavian personal names 'Ormr' or 'Ormarr' and 'by' meaning farm of village. Of course the personal name probably derives from 'snake' or 'dragon'. I make the distinction because the Great Ormes Head in Conwy and Great & Little Ormside in Cumbria are linked directly to Old Scandinavian 'Ormr' meaning 'snake'.

Fascinating book - I could browse it for hours.

While I was there I also picked up a copy of "Endangered Species". A good day out.
