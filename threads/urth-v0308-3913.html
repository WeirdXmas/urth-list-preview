One Ship: reply

Urth list · 9 messages · 2003-08-16 to 2003-08-19

2003urthThe Book of the New SunMatthew MalthouseLisa Schaffer-DoggettMichael BuiceAshley CrillChris PRoy C. LackeyJames Jordan
The thread: Ashley Crill's 'One Ship' theory, that the Whorl and Tzadkiel's ship are one ship with Tzadkiel's as archetype, drew both support and dispute. Lisa Schaffer-Doggett doubted the Whorl is a faster-than-light ship, and Roy C. Lackey argued that a talus and a chem are distinct, citing the stockpiling passage in Lake of the Long Sun. On the Latin-looking inscriptions, Crill argued they are an ancient language rendered as Latin, matthew.malthouse argued that languages drift apart over time, and Chris proposed that Typhon revived them on purpose. James Jordan backed the archetype reading and treated the Latin, French and Spanish as translation conventions. The thread ended without agreement, with Michael Buice adding a light aside on translation.
  • Lisa Schaffer-Doggett: disputes the One Ship theory and doubts the Whorl is a faster-than-light ship
  • James Jordan: supports the One Ship archetype reading and says the foreign languages are translation conventions
  • Roy C. Lackey: talus and chem are distinct, with chems made on Urth and stockpiled before reaching the Whorl
  • matthew.malthouse: the Latin-like language only resembles Latin because languages drift apart over time
  • Chris: Typhon deliberately revived archaic languages, either for social control or to keep cargo from subverting the mission
  • Ashley Crill: the inscriptions are an ancient language rendered as Latin, since dead languages persisted in written form in the library

Themes: The dying sun and the cosmos, Robots, androids and artificial life, Power, rulers and politics

2003-08-16 James Jordan #

Relays Don's argument that Tzadkiel's ship is the archetype of all ships, the Whorl a wicked ectype, and Typhon the Pancreator's ape. Replies to Ashley Crill's corrupt-symbolism remark by citing Luther, and argues that the Latin, French and Spanish in the books are only translation conventions.
supports · myth-and-structure · source-and-allusion · theories: Tzadkiel's ship is the archetype of all ships; The Whorl is a wicked ectype slave ship; Typhon is the Pancreator's ape; Foreign languages in the books are translation conventions

I think this might even be "two points in favor of parallelism." Sure Tzadkiel's ship is the only one of its kind, though archetypally related to all other ships. And what can the Cargo be but "souls being carried to heaven" in some SF sense, within the construct of the epic. Satan is God's ape, said Luther, and doubtless many before and after him. Typhon is the Pancreator's ape. His ship also carries souls to his future hell, where he will somehow rule (as Pas, or as a revived Typhon, depending on where you think the Whorl is going). Tzadkiel's Ship is the archetype. Whorl is one of countless ectypes, and in this case a wicked ectype, a slave ship.

Are these languages extant? Don't forget Wolfe's ur-note at the very beginning of the whole cycle, that he has translated Severian's manuscript in ways that 20th century readers can identify with. The appearance of Latin, French, Spanish, Arabic, etc. need mean no more than that Wolfe has assigned these languages to correspond to various languages of the far "future" (in whatever sense Urth is "future" to us). I'm not sure we can make anything out of the appearance of these languages in these books. All three of these epics are books that Wolfe has graciously translated for us.

Argues that the Latin-looking inscriptions in The Book of the New Sun are not really Latin but a distant ancient language that only resembles it, since languages drift apart over time regardless of writing. disputes · source-and-allusion · theories: Latin in the Whorl is a translation convention

That I can go with easily enough if I had to.

Nope. An ancient language (epitomized by the inscriptions in the Atrium of Time) presented to us as Latin not because it is Latin but because it bears a similar relationship to Severian's tongue as Latin does to ours.

There is a general assumption that writing slows the change of language and the more widespread literacy, the larger the published corpus and the more durable and disparate the means of recording the slower changes will be.

Yet it is actually rather hard to demonstrate that this is in fact the case.

Take as an example Hindustani. A language formed over half a millennium from the Sanskrit rooted languages of the mid western Deccan and the Persian of the Muhguls.

Hindustani is grammatically indistinguishable from Urdu and Hindi. Technically all three are the same language. Or were.

But starting in the late c19 and formalized at the beginning of the c20 Hindustani was divided. Urdu re-acquired words of Persian origin while de-emphasizing Sanskrit based vocabulary. Hindi delved into Sanskrit adopting words to supplant Persian vocabulary.

One single century since this was formalized a grammatically identical, lexically identical language has been split to the point where spoken Urdu (eg from TV and radio broadcasts) has only 60 percent comprehension for Hindi speakers and Hindi - made more obscure by the flowery circumlocutions of classical Sanskrit - is only 40 percent comprehensible to Urdu speakers.

Should this rate of change persist the two languages are likely to be mutually incomprehensible within another 100 years. More divergent than classical and modern Greek. English is not immune, 400 year old Shakespeare needs occasional footnotes; 600 year old Chaucer needs entire explicative texts; American English and British English differ in lexicography and usage sufficiently to require a choice between them when installing a computer! French has hallowed institutions dedicated to preserving the immutability of the language yet Rabelais can be as obscure as Shakespeare and Chretien do Troyes needs translating. Castillian and American Spanish. Arabic in variations from Morroco to Syria.

Consider then the span of time between ourselves and Severian. Consider also the upheavals that are alluded to in the history. The likelihood is that if Greek, Latin, French, Spanish English or any other current language "existed" it would be as archeological artefacts as obscure and incomprehensible as Minoan / Archaean Linear A and Linear B.

Matthew
