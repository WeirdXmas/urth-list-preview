TSH: Martha and Thelma

Themes: Doubles, twins and clones, Ghosts, dreams and visions, Hidden identities and parentage, Unreliable narration

2010-04-20 Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that the Murray sisters, Martha and Thelma, being identical twins forty years apart in age proves the Faerie events in The Sorcerer's House really happened. Grounds this in the text's stated ages: Martha in her late forties, Thelma close to ninety, and Martha's roughly forty years in Faerie where no one ages.
proposes · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Martha spent forty years in Faerie; The supernatural events in The Sorcerer's House really happened

Another point against the idea that none of the Faerie and/or supernatural stuff really happened are the Murray sisters, identical twins. Bax's mother, Martha, looked to be in her "late forties" (16). His Aunt Thelma was "close to ninety" (244). The math is simple enough, and Martha "spent about forty years" (280) in Faerie as "a sorcerer's wife", where no one ages. I can think no other reasonable explanation for identical twins to be forty years apart in age (without dragging in time travel or really fast spacships).

-Roy

2010-04-20 James Wynn replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that if the Faerie events in The Sorcerer's House are false, Bax is lying about finding his mother, and asks how Mrs. Murray could enter Bax's con except as he described. Responds to Roy C. Lackey's argument that the identical-twin ages show the supernatural events happened, and raises why Martha willed the house to Bax or George.
questions · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Bax is lying about Faerie and his mother

Well, I *do* think that the Faeri stuff occurred, but (let's face it) if Bax is lying about Faeri then he's lying about having found their mom.

But again, how does Mrs Murray come into Bax's con if not in the way he described? Why did she will him the house (or to George if that's who she thought she was)?

M.
