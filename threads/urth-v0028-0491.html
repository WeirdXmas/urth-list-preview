Daniel Fusch proposed Vodalus might hide on Lune, citing Rudesind in The Shadow of the Torturer, and argued that travel is possible but hard enough to hamper large search parties, while admitting he had no view on the New Sun's effects. The thread stayed a short series of speculative answers with no settled conclusion.

1999-11-20 Jim Henley #

Asks what happens on Skuld, Lune and Verdanthi (Venus, Moon, Mars) at the end of the Age of the Autarchs and how the New Sun affects them. Suggests the New Sun may be killing Lune's vegetation and asks whether exultants travel freely between Urth and these worlds, and whether they are poor or playgrounds for the rich.
questions · puzzle-solving · theories: New Sun may kill Lune's vegetation

Okay, we got three terraformed planets (in the old sense) we know about in the solar system: Skuld, Lune and Verdanthi, aka Venus, The Moon and Mars. Two questions:

1) What's going on on those three worlds during the tail end of the Age of the Autarchs?

2) What are the effects of the New Sun on those worlds?

Frex, might the NS kill all the vegetation on Lune, returning it within a couple of centuries or a millenium to the appearance we all know and love? Was the terraforming of Skuld possible only with the cooling of the Old Sun? Did life on Verdanthi really start to _suck_ as the OS grew weaker over the millenia? (It's interesting that while Lune is green, Verdanthi is still?/again? red...)

I'm especially interested in hearing relatively rigorous planetological speculation on point 2, but theories on point 1 are more than welcome. Do the exultants travel freely between Urth and the other worlds of the solar system? Are the other worlds also terminally poor, bitter-end societies like we know on Urth? Are they playgrounds for the remnant of "rich" humans? Or are they they Checkpoint Charlies of cacogen defense policy?

Best,

Jim

1999-11-20 Daniel Fusch #

Argues that Vodalus could be hiding on Lune, a terraformed moon, citing Rudesind's remark in chapter V of The Shadow of the Torturer about the Autarch's search. Reasons that the suggestion implies travel from Urth to Lune is possible but difficult, enough to hamper large search parties, while admitting no view on the New Sun's effects.
proposes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Vodalus may be hiding on Lune

Regarding Question # 1:

When Severian is talking with Rudesind in chapter V of TSOTT, they mention the theory that Vodalus might hiding "in the forests of Lune." That this suggestion is even made suggests that it is, in fact, possible for an Urth exultant to hide in the forests of Lune; Rudesind's comment that if Vodalus in on Lune, it may be a while before the Autarch catches him--seems to suggest that travel from Urth to Lune is difficult...at least to the point at which you have trouble landing large search parties there.

That's one possible reading, anyway.

Question #2--I have no idea about this one.

--Daniel

1999-11-22 JMCAzurin #

Argues that Verthandi and Skuld, once cooled, would lack geothermal power and rely on the darkened sun, and floats a playful idea that the Hieros could have ignited Jupiter instead of moving a white fountain. Responds to Jim Henley's question about the three worlds at the end of the Age of the Autarchs, using reasoning about planetary cooling and energy sources, with a joking aside about Mikah.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Verthandi and Skuld rely on the darkened sun for energy; Hieros ignite Jupiter instead of moving a white fountain

Given that Urth's core has solidified by Sev's time, the same would have happened to Verthandi and Skuld. They wouldn't have geothermal (urr...venothermal? marothermal?) plants. Barring their possession of technogical wizardry that's not present on Urth, their primary energy source would likewise be the darkened sun. They'd probably be pretty dismal places to live as well.

Their mineral-richness or -poorness is arguable though. They may not have been as thoroughly mined.

Another loony idea: what if Wolfe had tipped his hat to Arthur C Clarke and had the Hieros ignite Jupiter instead? Certainly seems easier than transporting a white fountain.

Mikah
