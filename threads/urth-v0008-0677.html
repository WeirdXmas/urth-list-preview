Themes: Names and their meanings

1998-03-28 Charles Dye #

Answers C R Culver's list of unknown names in The Book of the New Sun by identifying Dux Caesidius as a commander who resembles Severian and tracing the Latin meaning of "Dux" and Valeria's remarriage to Claudius and Messalina. Also questions whether the Group of Seventeen have individual names and notes that the undine Juturna's name echoes Diuturna, the lake of Baldanders's castle.
answers · source-and-allusion · puzzle-solving · theories: Valeria's remarriage echoes Claudius and Messalina; Juturna is named after Diuturna

This one, at least, we know. Dux Caesidius was one of Severian's military commanders during the Ascian War. He never appears in the texts, but we do know that he looks something like Severian himself.

"Dux" is, I believe, a Latin word meaning "leader," a cognate to "duke." The whole bit about Valeria's remarriage seems to be meant to suggest Claudius's betrayal by Valeria Messalina, but Severian's Valeria comes off as simply lonely, rather than nympho-bitch.

Wasn't "Abbadon" Mercury? I believe this tidbit appears in Severian's Night on the Bald Mountain.

What makes you think that the G17 *have* names? The captured Ascian soldier in the lazaret has presumably had far less contact with Erebus, Abaia and Company. He's still human, more or less. But he has to make do with "Loyal to the Group of Seventeen" -- not much of a name, IMHO. I suspect that the actual components of that group will have even less individuality.

Oddly enough, one of the undines has essentially the same name as the lake where Baldanders's castle stands: Juturna = Diuturna, as "diurnal" became our "journal."
