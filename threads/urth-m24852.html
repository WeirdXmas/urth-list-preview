Blue, Green, Urth, and the Ring of Glass

Urth list · 4 messages · 2013-10-10 to 2013-10-10

2013urthThe Book of the New SunJeff WilsonDan'l Danehy-OakesMarc Aramini
The thread: Readers asked whether Urth, Blue, and Green are the same planet and what the Ring of Glass and Atropaic Gardens imply about the solar system's changes. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes proposed that the Ring of Glass is the ruins of Father Inire's Atropaic Gardens and tentatively suggested Blue and Green might both be Urth. Marc Aramini argued in two posts that the ring is the same one Auk brought to Blue, the same planet having changed color, and then that Blue is a distinct world from Green, citing its cold, nutrient-poor soil. Jeff Wilson proposed that the Yesodis restored Urth to an Edenic state, leaving glassy Botanic Gardens remains among Citadel towers. The thread ended without consensus, with competing identifications left open.
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: Ring of Glass is ruins of Atropaic Gardens; Blue and Green might both be Urth
  • Marc Aramini: ring is the same one Auk took to Blue, so it is one planet changed in color
  • Marc Aramini: Blue is a different world from Green, with unsuitable soil and biota
  • Jeff Wilson: Yesodis restored Urth to Edenic state; glassy Botanic Gardens remain among towers

Themes: The dying sun and the cosmos, Deep time and ruins, Gods, angels and the Increate

Proposes that if Urth is Blue, the Ring of Glass is the ruins of Father Inire's Atropaic Gardens, and suggests Blue and Green might both be Urth. Offers no evidence beyond conditional reasoning and tentatively asks whether both identifications could hold, hedging on the remembered name.

The thing, the thing is, that if Urth is Green then there's no easy explanation, but if Urth is Blue, then the obvious explanation for the Ring of Glass is that it's the ruins of Father Inire's Atropaic Gardens. (I hope I'm remembering that name right.)

Perhaps *both *Blue and Green are Urth?

2013-10-10 Marc Aramini replying to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that the planets in the Long Sun books are symbolized by the fava and her friend and by the ring, which changes size and color because it is the same ring Auk brought to Blue, the same planet having changed colors. Responds to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's claim that Blue is a different planet by pointing to the ending of The Urth of the New Sun and saying the solar system's change must be understood before Blue can be proven.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · puzzle-solving · theories: Blue, Green and Urth are symbolized by the fava, her friend and the ring; the ring is the same planet changed in color; Blue is not yet provable until the solar system's change is understood

Breed There, sorry. I think the planets are symbolized by the fava and her friend (switcheroo on who is inhuma) and the ring: seems like two rings because it changes size and color, but it's the same ring that auk brought to Blue after repairing the lander - the same planet had changed colors. What have the hiero's done at the end of urth of the new sun? I've always been "right" about these books save the fumbling attempt to ascribe a mechanism to the transport to urth when considering it as Blue, and I was corrected and the mechanism sprung out. Until we comprehend what has changed in the solar system there is no means to "prove" what Blue is.

2013-10-10 Marc Aramini replying to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that Urth as the Blue planet is a different world from Green, not the same planet after a catastrophe, citing its cold, nutrient-poor soil and unsuitable biota. Suggests the Atropaic Gardens lead elsewhere or elsewhen, supported by a New Sun passage about reflected light creating things; responds to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's suggestion that Blue and Green may both be Urth.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Blue Urth is a different planet from Green; Atropaic Gardens lead elsewhere or elsewhen

I think the yesodi's did something catastrophic, but the lack of nutrients on blue and it's coldness lead me to believe it is a completely different planet. It's biotic system doesn't have the salinity and the micro-organisms of green- the inhumi can't effectively breed their but they rather like old Urth. I think it's pretty clear the botanic gardens lead elsewhere or elsewhen- remember that reflected light can "create" according to one passage in new sun.

2013-10-10 Jeff Wilson replying to Marc Aramini #

Proposes that the Yesodis restored Urth to an Edenic state, leaving glassy Botanic Gardens remains among surviving Citadel towers. Answers Marc Aramini's question about the hiero's at the end of The Urth of the New Sun, reasoning that many-faceted domes would be stable enough to be left open unattended.
proposes · close-textual-reading · myth-and-structure · theories: Yesodis restored Urth to Edenic state; Botanic Gardens survive as glassy remains; Many-faceted domes explain unattended operation

The Yesodis substantially restore urth to its Edenic state via quakes and tidal floods and renewed solar output, but there are still some Citadel towers for Juturna to sit among after the floods, so I guess it could be possible for there to be glassy remains of the Botanic Gardens.

The reflective geodesic dome does have interesting implications about time and space. Considering we have 2-fold books and 8-fold chambers, the many-facets domes could have superior stability, explaining how they were able to be left open and unattended and still do their intended function.
