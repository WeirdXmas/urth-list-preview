Themes: Justice, torture and mercy

1998-10-22 Dan Parmenter #

Asks where the image of the bare-chested hooded executioner originated and whether such figures carried swords or axes, noting Wolfe's sword choice in the series. Also proposes the name Shellac for the Whorl, drawing a parallel to the name Silk, and asks Mike for a reference work Wolfe cited on medieval executioners.

Where did this image originate?

1998-10-22 Mark Millman #

Argues that the sword suits Wolfe's torturer better than the axe because of its symbolic weight and the Commonwealth's Spanish heritage, and suggests the bare-chested hooded image may be idealized. Responds to Dan Parmenter's question about whether the canonical executioner carries a sword or an axe, and where the image originated.
answers · genre-and-craft

On 22 October 1998 at 4:55 pm GMT,

In England (and in the Germanic countries generally,
to a lesser extent) the axe was favored; on the Con-
tinent (and especially in the Romance countries) the
sword was preferred.

The sword, of course, carries better symbolic freight
for Wolfe's purposes, even leaving aside the South
American (thus Spanish) cultural heritage of the
Commonwealth.

I leave this for others to answer. (I think it may be an idealization of the actual appearance of executioners,
but I'm far from sure).

Mark Millman
(who would be using "Nacre" as a nom-de-Verticille
if he were posting to the Whorl, and so supports
Dan's "Shellac" without reservation)
