How much of a fake IS the green man?

Urth list · 3 messages · 2011-01-17 to 2011-01-17

2011urthJerry FriedmanDan'l Danehy-OakesJeff Wilson
The thread: Readers asked whether the Green Man in The Fifth Head of Cerberus is evidence that a race of green people exists, since the only one seen is reported by a self-confessed liar. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes doubted such a people exists at all, while Jerry Friedman offered two alternatives: that the green man's creators exploited a superstition, possibly tied to a New Sun prophecy, or that the old woman repeats a showman's rumor. Jeff Wilson cited a tea seller's remark at Saltus Fair that a green man was caught, noting it may be hearsay the exhibitor spread. The thread ended open, with the evidence left unresolved rather than settled.
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: doubts green people exist, since the only evidence is a self-confessed liar
  • Jerry Friedman: the green man's race may be fabricated, either exploited superstition or a showman's rumor repeated by the old woman
  • Jeff Wilson: a tea seller's Saltus Fair remark that a green man was caught is evidence, though possibly hearsay

Themes: Unreliable narration, Myth and folklore sources, Stories within stories

2011-01-17 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Questions whether the Green Man in The Fifth Head of Cerberus is evidence that a race of green people exists, since the only one is seen and his claim comes from a self-confessed liar. Responds to Jerry Friedman's question about whether the green people share the Author's respect for the Creator by doubting that such a people exists at all.
questions · close-textual-reading · theories: The Green Man is a fake and no green race exists

Do we even know that there are "green people?" We only see one, and have to take the word of a self-confessed liar that he is one of a race of green people.

2011-01-17 Jerry Friedman replying to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that the green man's claimed race may be a fabrication, offering two alternatives: that the green man's creators exploited a superstition, possibly tied to a prophecy of the New Sun, or that the old woman repeats a showman's advertising rumor. Grounds this in the Claw of the Conciliator chapter where the old woman says green men know everything, responding to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's doubt about whether green people exist.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: The green man is a fabricated figure, not a member of a green race

"'They caught a green man, do you know that? Got him right over there where you see the tent. Green men know everything, people say, if you can but make them talk.'"

TCotC Ch. III. To argue that there's no race of green people, I suppose you could say that the creators of the green man took advantage of a superstition (which could even be based on a prophecy of the New Sun), or that the old woman is repeating a rumor that started as the showman's advertising. Or maybe other possibilities that I'm missing.

Jerry Friedman
