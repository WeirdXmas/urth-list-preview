2005-04-04 Maru Dubshinki #

Asks whether Severian's tossed book in The Urth of the New Sun explains the translator's notes in The Book of the New Sun, and whether the ship's time-travel and multiple possible futures explain why it never lands in a future universe. Raises the significance of the name Urth/Ushas and draws on Apheta's account of two possible futures reaching back into the fixed past.
questions · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Severian's tossed book explains the translator's notes

I finally bought Urth o' the New Sun, and have been
carefully re-reading it.

Aside from the murky issues of just what Severian is at this point or what's up with the time travelling or the Increate, or how the whole Kabbalistic scheme works, I want to clear something up:

Sev, at the begining, tosses his Book of the New Sun (our BOTNS as well) toward a 'new universe', when the ship is apparently floating on the stream betwixt Briah and Yesod. Is that the explanation for the 'translator's notes'
in our BOTNS? Cause they always confused me otherwise.

Another thing: It seems clear to me, from what Apheta says about how there were two possibilites for Urth/Ushas (another thing: Is there a special significance
to that name? I do not recognize it at all.), with each having a certain possibility/
chance-of-coming-into-being,
but each nevertheless reachs back into the fixed past whether through Sev's premature
access to New Sun powers, or Master Ash. So, in 4-d space, each future exists. We know
further that the ship can navigate not just in 3-d, nor in universe-d, but it also moves about
in time, which suggests that it should 'land' in multiple future universes. But, as far as I can
tell, it never does. Why not? or what did I miss?

~Maru

2005-04-05 Andrew Kertesz replying to maru #

Proposes that the ship in The Book of the New Sun moves backward through time because faster-than-light travel incurs a relativistic time debt, so it arrives before departing. Suggests Wolfe may have framed the book as found, like Eco's opening in The Name of the Rose, and asks whether Yesod exists apart from Briah's time continuum.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · source-and-allusion · theories: Ship moves backward through time via relativity; Wolfe framed the book as found manuscript

That was my understanding of it. I don't know whether Wolfe was operating under the conceit that he was the one who found the book (like Eco did in the opening of _The Name of the Rose_) but it'd be neat if he was.

My understanding was that the ship can only move backwards through time. I understood it as this - when the ship accelerates beyond light speed, the effect of relativity means that it incurs a time "debt", and thus it can arrive at its destination before it has left its point of origin.

So, does Yesod exist separate to Briah's time-continum, or did Sev. arrive there before he left Urth?

Supports maru's point that Ushas, the Sanskrit word for dawn, suits The Urth of the New Sun's new Urth and new Sun. Adds a personal anecdote about California people from the 1960s generation who took the name Ushas in the 1970s, which gave the poster a chill after finishing a first reading of the book.
supports · source-and-allusion · theories: Ushas means dawn, fitting a new Urth

Absolutely, and rather neatly (although that is no surprise, given that it's Wolfe <g>).

As a personal aside, I have met more than one 60s survivor out here in California whose near-obligatory 70s name-change was to "Ushas." Gave me a chill the first time I was introduced to an Ushas, particularly since I'd only recently completed my first reading of UOTNS.
