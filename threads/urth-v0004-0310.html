Themes: Books, libraries and writing

1997-09-16 Alice Turner #

Argues that the language Latro writes in cannot be determined, so an anachronistic name like Lucius may simply be a translation from a period dialect. Responds to Rostrum's classicist friend's objection, and also hopes the Latro series stays within the historical Mediterranean world rather than drifting into invented or New World settings.
disputes · source-and-allusion

An answer to Rostrum's classicist friend (for sake of argument) is that we don't know what language Latro is writing in. If you accept the fact that he can read and write at all, he could be doing so in any local dialect. All we know is that his name is Lucius---perhaps that name is not acceptable for the period, but perhaps that it could be an acceptable "translation" from period dialect.

You know, if he goes on with it, I hope Wolfe does stay within a modicum of discipline. I don't want Latro sailing off to the New World to smoke the Pipe of Peace with Cutthroat's pals. I'd much rather he stayed within the incredibly rich Mediterranean world and had some more interaction with historical figures.

-alga-

1997-09-17 Michael Straight #

Points out that the introduction to Soldier of the Mist says the text was translated from scrolls written in Latin with Greek letters, responding to Alice Turner's point about Latro's language. Wonders whether Wolfe wrote the ending of Soldier of Arete after deciding no more Latro books were wanted, or planned further discovered journals.
questions · source-and-allusion

Doesn't the introduction to Soldier of the Mist say that the books are translated from scrolls found in an urn that were written in Latin except using Greek letters?

I wonder if Wolfe wrote the ending of Soldier of Arete after deciding no more Soldier books were wanted, or if he had an idea in mind for "discovering" more of Latro's journals.

-Rostrum
