Pas's gammadion and the voided cross

Urth list · 13 messages · 2007-01-21 to 2007-01-22

2007urthThe Book of the Long SunMatthew KingCliff JudgeJosh GellerMarc AraminiChris MulderMatthew GrovesJon Capps
The thread: Matthew Groves asked whether Pas's voided cross and the gammadion are the same symbol or two distinct ones, and whether two gammas can form a true voided cross. Chris Mulder and Matthew Groves, citing Litany of the Long Sun and geometry, argued they are the same figure since voiding a Greek cross through its ends yields four gammas. Others joined with playful or visual descriptions and archive links, while Aramini1 and Jon Capps argued over whether the symbol is a slashed cross or simply a cross outline, with Capps citing a heraldry definition. The thread ended with the identification largely accepted, though the cross-outline versus slashed-cross dispute was left unresolved.
  • Matthew Groves: the voided cross and gammadion are the same symbol, since four gammas form a voided cross
  • Chris Mulder: voiding a Greek cross through its ends yields four gammas, so the figure may be called a gammadion
  • Transentient: the voided cross is built from four gammas and the pateras are ship's staff
  • Matthew King: Pas's symbol is a gammadion placed in the top row, per Wolfe's Q&A description
  • aramini1: the voided cross is Typhon's family totem and the gammadions may bisect it like a slash
  • Jon Capps: a voided cross is simply a cross outline, not a slashed cross, per heraldry

Themes: Myth and folklore sources, Names and their meanings, Christ figures and messiahs

2007-01-21 Matthew Groves #

Asks whether the pateras' voided cross and gammadion are the same symbol or two distinct symbols, noting that the gammadion's detachable gammas and the voided cross's shape seem different. Grounds the question in the Long Sun books' descriptions and in a geometric argument that two voided gammas cannot form a true voided cross.
questions · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion

This is my first post to the list. Having discovered The Book of the New Sun and developed an almost fanatical devotion to Wolfe, I quickly gobbled up The Urth of the New Sun and then The book of the Long Sun. I read too quickly, paying too little attention to detail, and so I am left with many questions about all these books, with which I won't inundate the list all at once. But until Powell's ships me my Short Sun trilogy, I hope to be able to pick the list's brain on a couple points. First:

The pateras carry the "voided cross" and make the "sign of addition." They also carry a "gammadion" (whose gammas detach to become tools for the sacred windows). Each of these symbols is highly suggestive for the Whorl and its culture in ways that I'm sure have occurred to you. Are "cross" and the "gammadion" supposed to be the same thing, or are they separate symbols?

In my too-fast reading of the series, the impression I got is that the "voided cross" and the "gammadion" were one and the same. But this simply can't be right -- a cross is not a gammadion. (I can make a rather clumsy voided cross out of *two* voided gammas by rotating one 180 degrees and then aligning their stems vertically, but this would not be truly "voided," and it is certainly not what anyone would really call a "gammadion.") If someone can reassure me that there are two separate symbols carried by the pateras, then I'll be most grateful.

2007-01-21 Matthew Groves replying to Matthew Groves #

Argues that the voided cross and the gammadion are the same symbol, citing the passage in Litany of the Long Sun where Silk scratches a sign with one of four gammadions forming the cross. Explains that voiding a Greek cross through its ends yields four gammas, which makes the shape clear to the poster.
informs · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion

Well, it turns out the answer was right in front of me if I'd had a little more imagination or more knowledge of heraldic crosses. I just started leafing through the Long Sun novels in search of the first mention of the gammadion. The "voided cross" and the "gammadion" are indeed the same thing, as proved in this passage (on p. 172 in the omnibus edition "Litany of the Long Sun") when Silk is warding Teasel's window from the return of the creature that had preyed upon her in the night. Silk leans out the window to reach outside wall above it, and...

"With the pointed corner of the one of the four gammadions that made up the cross, he scratched the sign of addition on the bricks."

When I first read this, I struggled to imagine a voided cross each of whose four arms was itself a swastika, and I was more confused than ever. But it's much simpler than this, as I learned after a little poking around on the internet:

If you take a Greek cross (whose arms are of equal length) and you void it *all the way through the ends*, then you end up with four gammas in four different orientations. (I wish I could draw this for you, but no doubt you all know this already and are wondering why it ever puzzled me in the first place; but I was imagining an outline drawing of a cross (i.e., with *closed* ends), and so I couldn't pick out any satisfactory gammas.) Apparently, any such symbol, being made up of gammas, may be called a "gammadion," not just those that are pinwheel-like (although I'm guessing it has to be symmetrical).

2007-01-21 Cliff Judge replying to Matthew Groves #

Proposes that a voided cross can be built from four gammas, and that unfolding them gives a gammadion, imagining the symbol as a folding multi-tool used by clergy on the Whorl. Responds to Matthew Groves's question about whether the cross and gammadion are the same symbol, offering a playful geometric construction and a sci-fi reading of the pateras as ship's staff.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · close-textual-reading · theories: Pateras are ship's IT staff with folding gammadion tools

Seems to me that you can make something of a voided cross out of four gammas, if you don't mind the arms of the cross being open at the ends.

Now unfold each gamma, one northward, pivoting around its northernmost point, one eastward, one southward, one westward. You've got something that looks like a manji or a swastika. That's a gammadion, right? Now suppose it's a little four-in-one pocket tool where each "gamma" is one of the tools, and it folds out if you want to use it.

Give your IT staff these tools wear around their neck, then have them set up as clergy for interacting with the simulacrums of you and your family. Erase their memory of the lives they had before the ship embarked on its voyage and voila.

2007-01-22 Jon Capps replying to aramini1 #

Disputes the idea that Wolfe meant the gammadions to form a slashed cross, arguing instead that a voided cross is simply a cross outline. Supports the claim by quoting a heraldry reference defining the voided cross as one where only the edge is traced, approximated by four gammas.
disputes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion

I seriously doubt Wolfe intended the voided cross to be interpreted as one with a slash through it. See:

http://www.seiyaku.com/customs/crosses/voided.html

which says: [The Voided Cross] is simply a heraldic term for when only the edge of the cross is traced. It can be any shape or form of cross.

So, a voided cross is just the outline of a cross, which can be approximated quite closely with four gammas.

jon
