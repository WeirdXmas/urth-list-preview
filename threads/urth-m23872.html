5hoc: carapace, the cemetery columns, Sandwalker's feet, predendritic

Urth list · 6 messages · 2013-04-07 to 2013-04-07

2013urthAllan AndersonDavid StockhoffMarc Aramini
The thread: Marc Aramini opened by linking the cemetery-like columns and trees in The Fifth Head of Cerberus to the Aborigines' life cycle, asking whether 'predendritic' means a woody carapace forms at death or maturity, and tying this to the river tree and Sandwalker's fall. Allan Anderson offered a light Kate Bush digression about a man mistaken for a tree, which prompted David Stockhoff to dispute that Aboriginal people were ever mistaken for trees, calling it an old racist canard. Aramini argued the carapaces are more than removable shells because the trees seem to expect greetings and claim children, and Stockhoff answered that 'dendritic' may mean living in trees, while also using the Gondwanaland reference to argue that the future people think on deep time scales. The thread ends with the question left open, with no settled reading of the trees or the term.
  • Marc Aramini: columns and trees tie to the Aborigines' life cycle and carapaces are more than removable shells
  • David Stockhoff: 'dendritic' may mean living in trees, the Gondwanaland reference shows deep-time thinking, and the mistaken-for-trees idea is a racist canard
  • Allan Anderson: offers the Kate Bush association as a light digression, not a textual argument

Themes: Stories within stories, Deep time and ruins, Beasts and the animal in us

2013-04-07 Marc Aramini #

Speculates that the columns in 5HC dreams, bearing the dreamer's name and dates, and the cemetery-like circle of trees link to the aborigines' life cycle, asking whether 'predendritic' means a woody carapace forms at death or maturity. Ties this to the river tree reaching for VRT in his death scene, the foot imagery, and Sandwalker's fall, asking how complicated the aborigines' life cycles are.
proposes · close-textual-reading · myth-and-structure · theories: Predendritic aborigines form a woody carapace at death or maturity; Columns in 5HC dreams are graves bearing the dreamer's name; River tree in VRT reaches for the dead Sandwalker's carapaced corpse

One more quick thought.? Everyone dreams of being surrounded by columns or trees.? In Number 5's dreams, eventually these columns are revealed to have words that are rubbed off (the same time he realizes it is like a mausoleum or cemetery that all carry his name and different dates).? the only word he can make out is "carapace". ?
Later, the aborigines are confused with old wood, posts, etc.? Notice the post like/pillar nature of many of the demi-mondaine's legs. ?
I am still trying to put together the aboriginal?life cycle.? The term pre-dendritic is very interesting to me, as Marsch describes the aboriginal culture.? Since the columns in the first novella surround a graveyard, are the big circle of trees like a graveyard, too?? In VRT, that "temple" is cut down (402 trees in a big circle).? ?
Specifically, should we take pre-dendritic literally?? For a certain non-imitative aborigine who has not become "human", does death or maturity prompt a hard woody carapace that then makes them dendritic and rooted? ?
The reason I ask this is that in the death scene of VRT, the river tree that reaches out for?VRT to save him?is mentioned with "feet" imagery.?? Only one character always has foot imagery associated with him - he who drops from a higher place to a lower - Sandwalker. (and there, VRT drops down - and I do liken "Sandwalker's" fate to VRT - replaced by somebody who dreams that he is in fact the dead one (thus i?juxtapose castrated ancestor Eastwind?with the airborne Shadowchild))? Is this?big tree in the river reaching out for VRT Sandwalker's carapace encased corpse?? How complicated are their life cycles?? I am assuming that of course one who imitates another species would leave this portion of the life cycle behind.? ?
The classification as predendritic got me thinking about that.

2013-04-07 Marc Aramini replying to David Stockhoff #

Argues that the creatures' carapaces in The Fifth Head of Cerberus are more than removable shells, since the trees seem to expect greetings and claim the children. Replies to David Stockhoff's idea that 'dendritic' means living in trees by pointing to the subtext of the trees' relationship with the creatures.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Trees in 5HC claim the children as their own

And making them move around and reach out and mate, just using them as removable carapaces? I think the subtextual presence of keeping the trees satisfied and expecting greetings from them and them saying that children are theirs is a bit more than that.

2013-04-07 David Stockhoff replying to Marc Aramini #

Argues that the Gondwanaland reference in The Fifth Head of Cerberus shows that the novel's future people think on larger time scales, so they coined the term. Responds to Marc Aramini's remark about trees and carapaces by pointing to Gerry's suggestion and noting that primate ancestors were arboreal when the continent broke up.
supports · close-textual-reading · theories: Future people think on larger time scales

Sure. I suppose I was thinking as much of the reference to Gondwanaland, i.e., Gerry's suggestion that people of the novel's future simply know more than we do, or think on larger time scales, thus they coined the term. Our primate ancestors might have been living in trees around the time it broke up.
