Madregot & Yesod Redux

Urth list · 8 messages · 2010-08-15 to 2010-08-17

2010urthThe Book of the New SunAntónio Pedro MarquesDavid StockhoffLee BermanDave Lebling
The thread: Lee Berman argued that Yesod lies in Urth's past, so Earth cannot sit inside Yesod, and that the Juturna and ship-time passages support a time-flowing Madregot. David Stockhoff disputed this, insisting universes advance in sequence and that a Christ element might still be possible in the Urth books. António Pedro Marques rejected any temporal ordering across universes and denied Earth is in Yesod since nothing in the text links them. Dave Lebling backed the idea that Wolfe may have originally intended a Jesus figure in the Conciliator, and Berman ended by arguing Jesus never appeared on Urth and might enter at Yesod. The thread ended without agreement, with the Jesus question left open.
  • Lee Berman: Yesod lies in Urth's past, Earth cannot be in Yesod, and Jesus never appeared on Urth but may enter at Yesod
  • David Stockhoff: universes advance in sequence, and a Christ element can still be worked into the story
  • António Pedro Marques: cross-universe temporal order is meaningless and nothing in the books links Earth to Yesod
  • Dave Lebling: the Conciliator seemed Jesus-like on first reading and Wolfe may have intended it, though he is unsure

Themes: Time loops and causality, The dying sun and the cosmos, Christ figures and messiahs

2010-08-15 Lee Berman #

Argues that Brook Madregot runs from Yesod to Briah as an energy-entropy gradient, so Yesod lies in the past relative to Briah and Severian's time-travel moves follow that stream. Grounds this in the text: Tzadkiel's descriptions, Juturna's line, the seven strides downstream on Typhon's-to-Valeria's journey, and Tony Ellis's observation about the ship going backward in time.
proposes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Yesod is the past relative to Briah; Brook Madregot is an energy-entropy gradient; Urth is a past iteration of Earth

With all the question flying around about Yesod I decided to really re-check the text on Brook Madregot. I felt the answers would be there and I do think I have a clearer view of what is going on now.

Tinkerbell Tzadkiel say Brook Madregot runs from Yesod to Briah. The "glory of Yesod "upstream to the "destruction of Briah downstream. Juturna confirms that it "rises in Yesod and rushes to Briah".

Severian uses Madregot (there is a meadow also) to travel four times. The first is from Tzadkiel's "skipper's quarter" to Severian's stateroom. Later he uses the Brook for time travel. We are not told which direction he moves except once. In his trip from Typhon's rule to Valeria's rule he takes 7 strides downstream. so we learn that downstream carries him to the future.

Brook Madregot doesn't represent time as much as energy and entropy. Entropy increases as you move through time, so Yesod, with higher energy, lower entropy would seem to be in the past from Briah

Dowstream is to the future, and Yesod is upstream, I think we must conclude that Yesod is in the past from Briah. I think this is confirmed by Tony Ellis' observation that the Ship travels backward in time to get to Yesod.

With regard to branching, I will speculate that Brook Madregot is a straight, single path as Severian moves (upstream) toward the past. The past, his orgin, is fixed. Severian only encounters branchings as he makes the long, future-ward journey all the way from Apu Punchau to the blossoming of Ushas.

One other feature about Brook Madregot is that tinkerbell Tzadkiel warns Severian "Don't jump across it though, or you'll end up in another place". I'm not sure what that means..perhaps that there is spatial travel involved in Madregot also and Severian doesn't want to step out onto a place in Briah which isn't Urth/Ushas.

As the Jordan-Wolfe interview clearly suggests that Urth is a past iteration of Earth I think this rules out the guess that Yesod is our universe. Too bad. It would have been nice to get Jesus worked into the story somehow, however tangentially.

Responds to David Stockhoff's question about why the interview rules out Yesod being our universe, presenting the reasoning as a logical chain while admitting its limits.
questions · chronology-and-plot-logic · author-and-biography · theories: Yesod is not our universe

But that's just being logical. I don't really claim that the puny mental tools of a primate species from a small blue planet on the edge of the galaxy are sufficient to grasp the universe and beyond.

But perhaps my logic is faulty. Do you see an alternate interpretation?

2010-08-17 Lee Berman replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that Jesus never appeared on Urth, which explains the book's shape, and speculates that Christ might enter the story at Yesod after the Hierogrammates build the planets. Grounds this in the absence of Jesus on Saint Croix and Sainte Anne and in the Conciliator's echo of Jesus on first reading, responding to Dave Lebling and David Stockhoff's debate about whether Jesus was ruled out.
proposes · theology-and-religion · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Jesus never existed on Urth; Christ may appear at Yesod

I also saw some echo of Jesus in the Conciliator on first reading. I remain convinced that Urth never had a Jesus nor a Flood and that's why it is the way it is and why it needed Severian.

(FWIW, Jesus doesn't seem a strong presence on Saint Croix or Sainte Anne either..I think Wolfe was going through a dark period in his life in the 70's)

Anyway, the absence of Jesus makes His presence felt through the story anyway. But it would be nice if we knew where he was going to eventually turn up and if it is anywhere in the story, I'd say the most likely place would be Yesod, after the Hierogrammates finish building all the planets and stars including Earth and Sol. If we have to bend what we think is logical time to have him, then so be it.
