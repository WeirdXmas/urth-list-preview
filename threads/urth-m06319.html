Sev's common lineage

Urth list · 45 messages · 2008-06-29 to 2008-07-02

2008urthThe Book of the New SunMark MillmanthalassocratDavid WellsJosh GellerJeff WilsonDave TallmanRoy C. Lackeyb sharp
The thread: Readers debated whether Severian is related to Casdoe and Little Sev, with thalassocrat proposing a family tree (Dorcas's husband as a Severian, Fechin as Dorcas's father) and Dave Tallman and others disputing it on resemblance, age arithmetic and the 'Are you my uncle?' line. The thread spiralled into rival time-travel readings of the Countess/loggia woman as Catherine, with Dave Tallman, b sharp, and Roy C. Lackey pressing competing claims and Jeff Wilson questioning the logic of the time-travel account. Exultant ancestry, armiger height and Inire-as-shapeshifter ideas were also argued, but the thread ended in impasse, with Roy C. Lackey mocking the idea that every lineage theory is equally valid.
  • thalassocrat: Severian is related to Casdoe and Little Sev through an incestuous family lineage explaining his silence
  • Dave Tallman: the loggia woman is Catherine, a time-travelling figure from the past, and Severian's silence is self-preservation
  • Roy C. Lackey: time-travel and lineage theories are overly speculative, and armigers are not taller than optimates
  • Jeff Wilson: the time-travel account is logically flawed, and the manuscript and grave-robbing details undercut the theories
  • Mark Millman: Severian has exultant blood stated outright in the text
  • b sharp: the time-travel and portal readings are worth floating, and Rudesind, Fechin and Dorcas's husband may be Inire

Themes: Unreliable narration, Time loops and causality, Fathers, mothers and children

2008-06-29 Dave Tallman replying to thalassocrat #

Argues against the theory that Fechin is Dorcas's father and that Casdoe and Li'l Sev are Severian's relatives, citing the lack of family resemblance such as hairiness and hair colour. Also disputes that Li'l Sev's question "Are you my uncle?" implies a known uncle named Severian, reading it instead as a child seeking a relative after losing his family.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Fechin is Dorcas's father; Li'l Sev is Severian's relative; Fechin is Father Inire or Rudesind

Usually Wolfe tries to provide a family resemblance between related characters. Fechin was "a tall, wild boy with red hair on his hands, his arms." Dorcas is gold-blonde, with no mention of hairiness. Sev and his potential sister Merryn aren't hairy either. Dorcas has artistic talent, but I don't think that's enough of a connection.

You don't deal with b sharp's identification of Fechin with Father Inire and Rudesind. One or both of those could be true, given their monkey-like characteristics. But if so, chances are that the alien could not have children with human women.

I'm not sold that "Are you my uncle?" means that little Sev knows of an uncle named Severian. He's just hoping for a relative to take care of him since he lost his family, in my view.

2008-06-29 thalassocrat #

Proposes that Severian is related to Casdoe and Little Sev, arguing that Dorcas' husband, the old boatman, is named Severian and is Little Sev's uncle. Builds the case with a family-age chronology: Fechin as Dorcas' father, Casdoe's father as Fechin's contemporary, and Casdoe as a late child, supported by the Casdoe/Dorcas name resemblance and Little Sev's question.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · puzzle-solving · theories: Severian is Dorcas' husband's name; Dorcas is Fechin's daughter; Casdoe and Little Sev are Severian's second cousins; Casdoe's name echoes Dorcas

Sev's lineage is a problem which I'm sure Wolfe intended people to drive themselves crazy over, and I don't think there's enough info to get really definitive. But on the flip side, I think he's inviting us to have fun making up stories, so what the hell ...

I want Sev to be related to Casdoe and Li'l Sev. "Casdoe" is so similar to "Dorcas", with the syllables reversed. Li'l Sev asks Sev if he's his uncle, which I don't think makes a lot of sense unless Li'l Sev does in fact have an Uncle Sev. If I'm right and Fechin is Dorcas' father, then at the very least the families have known each other for 70+ years.

Most characters, even if relatively minor, get named. Two exceptions are Casdoe's father and Dorcas' husband. I don't know what the first was called, but I think I know the name of the second: Severian.

Here's how it works. Say Fechin is Dorcas' father. The ages work out OK. Base-lining against the time when Sev left the Citadel, we have Dorcas born 55-60 years previously (she died 40+ years ago, according to her husband, and I think we can assume that Sev is 17- 18). Rudesind was painted by Fechin as a boy; Rudesind is 70 years + a boy's age old, say early 80's. Estimate Fechin as having been born 85-90 years ago, so he had Dorcas when he was 30-40.

Casdoe's father is a contemporary of Fechin, so he's 85-90 also. Quite old enough for Dorcas' husband, the old boatman, to in fact be his son. Eg: say he has the old boatman when he's about 20, which makes the guy 65-70.

It's a very plausible story: Casdoe's father is close to Fechin, and obviously neighbors, at least when young. Their kids grow up together and marry each other.

Casdoe must be a late offspring for her father: she has twins who seem to be about 6 years old. Say she was born 26 years ago; or when her father was around 60. Not impossible, of course, but it does perhaps suggest other things which I won't go into here.

In any case, this ties everybody together, and the guess of "Severian" as Dorcas' husband's name explains why Li'l Sev asked Our Sev if he was his uncle. Dorcas' husband is his uncle, on this theory, and Li'l Sev presumably knows he has an uncle with this name. Our Sev and Li'l Sev are in fact second cousins.

Proposes a fan story in which Catherine Ouen is Dorcas's daughter, raised by Dorcas's family, so that she and Ouen unknowingly become Severian's parents. Builds the case from Dorcas's death after a few years of marriage, the religious sensibility of Fechin's optimate family, the shop and craft ties to Agia and her brother, and the Pelerine connection described by Ava.
proposes · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Catherine Ouen is Dorcas's sister; Catherine Ouen is Dorcas's daughter; Severian is Catherine and Ouen's child; Agia descends from Dorcas's brother

Pushing this story along, let's make Catherine Ouen's sister. Dorcas died in childbirth, after 4 years or so of marriage, during part of which Ouen was a toddler. If the child lived, she would be about 40 when Sev leaves the Citadel. If it was a girl, she would have been the right age to be Sev's mother: a couple of years younger than Ouen.

The story: Old Boatman falls to pieces when Dorcas dies. Baby Catherine is taken by Dorcas' family - (ie, Fechin's family, in my story, but Fechin as Dorcas' father isn't ssential for this part). Old Boatman slides down to skid row & is lost to them.

Fechin etc are the same kind of optimate family as Ava's. They are religious; witness eg the notorious enamelled religious work which Thecla requests, no doubt a piece produced by Fechin & son. Dorcas reborn seems to retain a religious sensibility.

Agia seemes to know more about religious matters than you'd expect from somebody like her, and it's at least plausible that Agia & brother are Dorcas' brother's grandchildren (their shop, inherited from their mother like the shop given by Fechin to Dorcas; an eye for quality craft work; a hint of more education than you'd expect from guttersnipes etc.)

So Fechin's family may well have had the kind of long-standing relationship wih the Pelerines which Ava describes as necessary for an optimate daughter to be accepted into the order.

As Ava, so Catherine. She joins the Pelerines, and however it happens, she and Ouen meet, not realising they're siblings. Sev results.

This doesn't seem to deliver us any exultant genes for Sev, but you can't have everything :) Anyway, there's nothing which suggests any particular exultant characteristics for Catherine, and it's hard for me to imagine an exultant being sent to the torturers for any crime less serious than treason or lese majesty.

2008-06-29 b sharp replying to thalassocrat #

Argues that the woman Severian sees from the loggia in Urth of the New Sun is being taken back into the past to face torture, not arriving from the future, and suggests she may be linked to Catherine, Valeria and Countess Carina. Defends this against Roy's objections, citing Thais, the self-portrait-in-painting idea, and the Path of Air portal as a parallel to Ultan's Library.
disputes · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: loggia woman is taken back to the past; Valeria is daughter or clone of Catherine; Path of Air connects to a Citadel tower; Thais is Thea's kaibit

Regarding Dave Tallman's Catherine scenario, I just have a gut feeling that if there is an exultant cell donor for the Catherine maids, Wolfe wouldn't be so unfair as to not toss her name out somewhere. Haven't found it and haven't had as much time to read and ponder this as I'd like. I hope to soon. Having Sev do the healing of the beheading seems more plausible than Catherine (or is it Carina? or Lybe?).

My impression is that the woman Sev sees from the loggia is not from the future but rather being taken back to the past where she belongs. I think this because the woman in the pale gown in the coffin appears in the first chapter of the first book of the series and the woman Sev sees from the loggia appears in UotNS, an afterthought book by Wolfe to help explain the first four. I don't get the sense from the first 4 books that Catherine (or the coffin woman) is from the future, quite the opposite. I think Wolfe put the woman in UotNS on the Path of Air mostly to connect her to the Play and Countess Carina, (whom Dave, through her maids, has neatly connected to Catherine, even without the physical resemblance).

So anyway, if she is from the past, and weeping now, I think she is being brought back to face her necessary destiny, to be tortured to death in Allowin's Necklace. If forced to speculate on her crime I think it might be the same as her sister, Cyriaca's.

In my view, we are sort of told that Ultan's Library, which is at basement level, extends from the Citadel to the House Absolute (in hyperspace or something?). Rudesind's art gallery at ground level, has a similar connection. I see the Path of Air as a similar portal but with a terminus at the top of a Citadel tower (probably Valeria's). I'm sure Roy would argue that the flood would have covered even the tallest Citadel tower but I'm happy enough with this interpretation that I'm willing to consider a Wolfean goof there, or some sort of waterproofing by Father Inire.

Dave Tallman, I too have thought the morning glory comment was a clue. Didn't know moonflowers were a kind of morning glory! And I still think maybe Valeria is daughter (perhaps clone) to Catherine.

Responses to Roy:
Obviously Thais isn't in her 70's so if she is Thea's kaibit, she, like the loggia woman, had access to the Path of Air or some other time portal. I'm not sure why else a girl in her 20's on flood day would have any reason to give Severian knowing glances.

The "self-portrait gambit" (hah!) is not so loony...if we see an artist in a painting and there are brushes present it could be a self-portrait. A quick Google produces numerous examples of artists who some very famous like Chagall and Munch and one not quite so famous self-portrait with a brush by Russian artist Nicolai Fechin. Coincidence? (triple hah!) You be the judge.

Marc Aramini- Roy's nuts and bolts interpretations and insistance that real-world logic should be obeyed might leave gaps in understanding. Surely Eschatology And Genesis is best interpreted as symbolic art within art rather than as a mechanical reality. Still without Roy, theories can (and do despite him) spiral out of control. I think his input is especially apt for a story written by an engineer.

2008-06-30 Dave Tallman replying to b sharp #

Argues that the woman Severian sees from the loggia is a time-jumping adulteress from the past rather than a future figure, reconstructing a full timeline for the Contessa from the play alone. Builds the case from the maids' saint names, the morning-glory reference and Meschia's remark about wandering Urth, and replies to b sharp's reading by invoking Occam's razor against multiple time trips.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: Contessa is an adulteress from the past; Contessa time-jumps to the last days of the autarchy; Contessa is buried in the necropolis as the corpse

Since UotNS was an afterthought, Wolfe must have expected us to be able to construct the loggia scene from the play alone. What is the Contessa doing in the play? She seems like a throw-away character until we examine clues like her maid's names, the morning-glory reference, and Meschia's comment about her prolonging her life by wandering Urth and being unable to return home. Note that he said Urth, not Ushas; this implies time-travel to the past.
