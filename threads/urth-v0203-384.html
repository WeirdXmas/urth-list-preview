Sev's Dream QUOTATION

Urth list · 4 messages · 2002-03-19 to 2002-03-20

The thread: Gage Kerrigan quoted Severian's dream passage from The Shadow of the Torturer to address whether it hints at a Green or Blue outcome for Urth. The thread turned on whether the disputed word is 'bush' or 'brush'. Michael Andre-Driussi checked the first edition hardcover, first edition paperback and Easton Press hardcover and reported 'brush'. Matthew Malthouse first argued for 'bush', asking whether the dead, brown bush was revived by the life-engendering force, then conceded 'brush' while keeping his reading of the passage's logic. The thread ended by settling the textual question rather than the interpretation.
  • Gage Kerrigan: supplied the quoted dream passage as the requested text
  • Michael Andre-Driussi: the word is 'brush', confirmed in several printings
  • matthew.malthouse: first argued for 'bush' and a revived plant, then conceded 'brush' and kept the logic of his reading

Themes: Deep time and ruins, Names and their meanings, Light, darkness and colour

2002-03-19 Gage Kerrigan #

Quotes Severian's dream passage from The Shadow of the Torturer, in which he imagines time stopping and the sun winking out and a miraculous light animating living things. Supplies the requested text, which an earlier poster had said hints at a Green or Blue outcome for Urth, citing page 15, chapter II.
informs · close-textual-reading

Here's Severian's quote... I think somebody asked for
it yesterday... it adresses his premonition of the
future, both outcomes for Urth, and somebody said that
it indicated Green or Blue.

"Two thoughts (that were nearly dreams) obsessed me
and made them [various events related to nature and
animals] infinitely precious. The first was that at
some not-distant time, time itself would stop the
colored days that had so long been drawn forth like a
chain of conjuror's scarves come to an end, the sullen
sun wink out at last. The second was that there
existed somewhere a miraculous light - which I
sometimes conceived of as a candle, sometimes as a
flambeau - that engendered life in whatever objects it
fell upon, so that a leaf plucked from a bush grew
slender legs and waving feelers, and a rough brown
bush opened black eyes and scurried up a tree."
Severian (to himself), page 15, chapter II, The Shadow
of the Torturer.

Ps. Your welcome.

«LORD ESN»
