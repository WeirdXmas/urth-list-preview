Cutthroat "identity" from "TRACKING SONG"

2010urthThe Book of the New SunTony EllisDavid StockhoffJeff Wilsonsean ako
The thread: Sean ako proposed that Cutthroat, the amnesiac narrator of "Tracking Song," is an anthropologist doing fieldwork among beast-human tribes, pointing to his daily logs, questioning of other groups, and the tape recorder, shaver, knife and firestarter he carries, and to Wolfe's recurring scientist-observers. David Stockhoff supported the idea and reframed the amnesia as a deliberate method, possibly induced, voluntary and temporary, linking it to Wolfe's play with memory. Tony Ellis agreed, citing the same suggestion and adding that "The Lost Pilgrim" shares the amnesiac-scholar premise. The thread stayed agreeable, with no visible dissent, and ended as reinforcement rather than resolution.
  • sean ako: Cutthroat is an anthropologist doing fieldwork, and his amnesia is a fieldwork protocol
  • David Stockhoff: amnesia is deliberate, possibly induced, voluntary, temporary, perhaps even temporary decapitation
  • Tony Ellis: agrees Cutthroat is a fieldwork anthropologist; cites The Lost Pilgrim as a parallel

Themes: Memory and forgetting, Unreliable narration, Beasts and the animal in us

2010-04-20 sean ako #

Proposes that Cutthroat in "Tracking Song" is an anthropologist doing fieldwork among the beast-human tribes, citing his daily logs, his questioning of other groups, and the tape recorder, shaver, knife and firestarter he falls with. Grounds the idea in his amnesia being a fieldwork protocol and in Wolfe's recurring scientist-observers, naming the anthropologists in The Shadow of the Torturer and the astronauts in "Alien Stones".
proposes · close-textual-reading · genre-and-craft · theories: Cutthroat is an anthropologist studying beast-humans; Cutthroat's amnesia is a fieldwork protocol

Urthers;

I believe the identity of Cutthroat is tied to his actions among the various "tribes" of beast/humans. For example, he compulsively records his daily adventures along with his ethnological observations. He maintains a log organized by day/number; and he consistently joins and talks with other groups/tribes to find out information, much of it of little practical use (but interesting from an anthropological view.)? I believe Cutthroat is an anthropologist; a scientist studying the biologically engineered beast/humans of the planet.? Is it more than coincidental that he happens to fall off the Great Sleigh with a tape recorder, shaver, pocketknife and firestarter? Sounds more like he's ready to do fieldwork. As to his amnesia, who can say that it isn't their protocol for any fieldwork that they do (to encourage objective and honest observations/actions.) Of course, the Great Sleigh didn't "lose" him as many readers (and even characters in the story) assert. It is just their way; consider the way they listen in on his recordings (a handy way of keeping a handle on his situation.) Another thing to consider is that GW has an affiinity with these "scientist" types; consider the two anthopologists in "THE SHADOW OF THE TORTURER" in the Jungle Garden (with Isangoma) or the astronaut/scientists in "ALIEN STONES."? They "immerse" then "observe," "absorb," and "report."? All three of these examples above do this in one way or another (or literally.)

Sean Ako

2010-04-20 David Stockhoff replying to sean ako #

Argues that Cutthroat's amnesia is a deliberate anthropological method rather than a loss, suggesting induced, voluntary, temporary amnesia or even temporary decapitation. Responds to sean ako's claim that Cutthroat is a scientist fieldworker by building on his idea and linking it to Wolfe's recurring play with memory and amnesia.
supports · myth-and-structure · theories: Cutthroat's amnesia is an induced anthropological technique

Sean

You raise an interesting possibility: that "amnesia" is merely an advanced anthropological method, easily "learned," that relieves the observer of (at least the more conscious) prejudices.

That would provide one explanation for Cutthroat, if nothing else. And we all know how Wolfe likes to play with memory/amnesia---he probably has a list of all possible variations on the theme, and induced, voluntary, temporary amnesia is bound to be on it.

Perhaps the technique includes something like temporary decapitation---why not?---and that would explain about the only other thing we know about Cutthroat. That, or some surgically implanted translating/speaking device.

2010-04-20 Tony Ellis replying to sean ako #

Points out that Cutthroat, the amnesiac character in the story Tracking Song, could be an anthropologist doing fieldwork, and links back to an earlier post making the same suggestion. Adds that the story The Lost Pilgrim from Innocents Aboard shares this premise of an amnesiac scholar in an alien, barbarous world, and notes Wolfe reusing old ideas.
supports · source-and-allusion · theories: Cutthroat is an anthropologist studying the beast-humans

Yes, I suggested much the same thing a while back:
http://lists.urth.net/pipermail/urth-urth.net/2007-June/007862.html

It's occurred to me since then that The Lost Pilgrim, collected in Innocents Aboard, has a similar premise: an amnesiac scholar, thrust into an alien and barbarous world. It certainly wouldn't be the first time Wolfe revisited an old idea.
