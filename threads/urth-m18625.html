The Trailer of Ink

Urth list · 6 messages · 2011-01-31 to 2011-02-01

2011urthThe Book of the New SunAdam ThorntonGwern BranwenMarc AraminiJeff WilsonDavid Duffy
The thread: Jeff Wilson proposed that Severian's 'wandering trail of ink' in Urth of the New Sun sets up a second time loop tied to Ultan's quotation in Shadow, and asked whether one of Severian's memoir volumes reached Ultan's library. Gwern Branwen disputed this, arguing the phrase lacks the idea of endless writing and derives from Ecclesiastes rather than a time loop. Wilson countered with a double meaning in 'terminus est' and the word 'unslakeable', while David Duffy suggested Ultan's quotation glosses the Ecclesiastes verse on the endlessness of writing many books, citing the sundial inscriptions as precedent. Marc Aramini separately raised a link between the sacrificial virgin in The Circus of Dr. Lao and Cassie in An Evil Guest. The thread ended without resolution, with the Ecclesiastes reading gaining ground over the time loop.
  • Jeff Wilson: the trail of ink sets up a second time loop linked to Ultan's quotation
  • Gwern Branwen: the phrase comes from Ecclesiastes and lacks the idea of endless writing
  • Jeff Wilson: 'terminus est' double meaning fits endless writing
  • David Duffy: Ultan's quotation glosses the Ecclesiastes verse, like the sundial inscriptions
  • Marc Aramini: the sacrificial virgin in Dr. Lao may link to Cassie in An Evil Guest

Themes: Time loops and causality, Books, libraries and writing, Myth and folklore sources

2011-01-31 Jeff Wilson #

Proposes that Severian's remark about a "wandering trail of ink" in Urth of the New Sun sets up a second time loop, linking it to Ultan's quotation in Shadow. Asks whether one of Severian's memoir volumes, or a work quoting one, ended up in Ultan's library.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian's memoirs reach Ultan's library through a time loop

In URTH, Chapter XXXIX, "The Claw Of The Conciliator Again", Sev gives the new thorn to Typhon's guardsmen, establishing a one-off timeloop for it to come into his younger self's possession a thousand years later.

However, he also says, "Whence comes this unslakable thirst to leave behind me a wandering trail of ink, I cannot say" when contemplating a further volume of memoirs. This may be another timeloop setup, as in SHADOW VI,

"'Of the trail of ink there is no end,'" Master Ultan told me. "Or so a wise man said. He lived long ago?what would he say if he could see us now? '"

Could this be actual textev that at least one of the volumes, or a work that quotes a found volume of Sev's manuscript, has come to rest in Ultan's library?

2011-01-31 Marc Aramini replying to David Duffy #

Asks whether the sacrifice of a beautiful virgin in The Circus of Dr. Lao relates to Cassie's final trip to Woldercan in An Evil Guest, and whether her glamorization makes her worthy of sacrifice. Responds to David Duffy, citing Dave Tallman's earlier comparisons of An Evil Guest with other literature and the sacrificial beauty pageant as the link.
questions · source-and-allusion · myth-and-structure · theories: Woldercan sacrifice parallels Cassie's trip in An Evil Guest; Cassie's glamorization makes her worthy of sacrifice

Just had a chance to read Circus of Dr. Lao this week, and was shocked by how much of the stuff has at least some resonance with Wolfe. The Hound of the hedges is a chlorophyl driven green hound that just kind of sprung into existence, but I was wondering if anything had been made of how the entire Woldercan scenes are just geared around the sacrifice of the most beautiful virgin.

As far as Evil Guest goes, I believe it was Dave Tallman who made a lot of cogent comparisons between it and other literature, (my apologies if it was another Dave, about all of my friends somehow are named David) and I was wondering if that sacrificial beauty pageant has anything to do with Cassie's final trip to Woldercan. I know there is the sacrifice on the Island, but is there something about her glamorization that has made her worthy of human sacrifice, too?

Also, the most fantastic part of the book is Dr. Lao just walking into a department before closing and getting the circus permits one night before the show. That was my job for three years, pretty much impossible anywhere nowadays.

Argues that the Latin phrase "terminus est" carries a double meaning of end and final word, which ironically fits the endlessness of writing that Severian's phrase only hints at. Responds to Gwern Branwen's point that Severian's wording misses the idea of endless writings, building on his remark about the word "unslakeable".

2011-01-31 Gwern Branwen replying to Jeff Wilson #

Argues that Severian's phrase about a wandering trail of ink lacks the key idea of endless writing, and that the source is Ecclesiastes rather than a timeloop. Supports the point by citing Ecclesiastes, one of the wisdom books, and its line that of making many books there is no end, which the poster says is closer to Ultan's version.
disputes · source-and-allusion · theories: Trail of ink phrase derives from Ecclesiastes

Severian's phrase doesn't include the key idea - the endlessness of writings. (Or at least, in a rather indirect way, with 'unslakeable'.)

I would look instead to Ecclesiastes. It is one of the wisdom books of the Bible, without doubt read several times by Wolfe, and internally attributed to a wise man. We read in one translation:

(I seem to recall seeing it as "Of many words there is no end", which is even closer to Ultan's version.)

2011-02-01 David Duffy replying to Jeff Wilson #

Proposes that Master Ultan's quotation about the trail of ink may be a gloss on the Ecclesiastes verse about writing many books having no end. Supports this by pointing to the earlier sundial inscriptions as a pattern of glossed scriptural phrases, answering Jeff Wilson's query about the quotation's source.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Ultan's quotation glosses Ecclesiastes 12:12

There is the question of who he is quoting. It might be a gloss, as per the sundials, of

...Of writing many books there is no end, [and much study is a weariness of the flesh]. Ecclesiastes 12:12
