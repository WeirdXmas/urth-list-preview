The Severian Behind Curtain #3

Urth list · 20 messages · 2008-06-15 to 2008-06-19

2008urthThe Book of the New SunDavid WellsthalassocratDave LeblingAdam ThorntonJosh GellerFred KiescheJeff WilsonDave TallmanRoy C. Lackeyb sharp
The thread: The thread opened with David Wells asking how Juturna's intervention and the Sev1/2/N divergence work, and whether the New Sun's ending was meant to leave political gains unclear. Responses split on how Severian relates to Christ and Urth's religion: Dave Lebling said he is only a Christ-like figure, Dave Tallman argued Urth is post-Catholic, and b sharp rejected the SevN repetition idea in favor of finite lives tied to five coffins. A long side debate over the stained-glass Temptation in the whorehouse door, with thalassocrat favoring Meschia over Eve or Jesus, ran alongside jokes about brothel decor. The thread then drifted into Fred Kiesche's drowning-hallucination theory, Ambrose Bierce, and Jacob's Ladder, and ended without agreement.
  • David Wells: asks whether the ending's political outcomes were meant to be unclear and jokingly proposes everything is Severian's drowning hallucination
  • Dave Lebling: Severian is a Christ-like figure, not the Conciliator, reviving a dim memory of Christianity
  • Dave Tallman: Urth is post-Catholic with Christian customs surviving, and leans against the SevN repetition idea
  • b sharp: Severian's lives are finite and tied to five coffins, not repeated iterations
  • thalassocrat: the whorehouse Temptation depicts Meschia, not the Christian scene, as the Talos play suggests
  • Fred Kiesche: backs the theory that Severian drowns in the Gyoll and the rest is hallucination

Themes: Christ figures and messiahs, Catholic faith and sacraments, Time loops and causality

2008-06-15 David Wells #

Admits confusion about how Juturna's intervention and the Sev1/2/N divergence work, recalls an earlier self-resurrection theory, and draws a Feynman single-electron analogy in response to Adam Thornton's proposal that every character is Severian. Asks whether Wolfe intends the political factions' gains and losses under the New Sun to be unclear, or whether the ending's happiness was meant to be explicit.
questions · puzzle-solving · myth-and-structure · theories: Every character is an iteration of Severian

What a fascinating discussion. I have never really understood exactly how Juturna's intervention and the Sev1/2/N divergence worked (about a decade ago my wild and unsubstantiated theory that Severian had drowned and unknowingly self-resurrected via the "Claw" was robustly, and rightly, criticised by this forum).

This, though not meant seriously, is interesting and inventive. It reminds me (warning: off topic) of Richard Feynman's comment in his lecture accepting his Nobel Prize for Physics (see http://nobelprize.org/nobel_prizes/physics/laureates/1965/feynman-lecture.html) that "I received a telephone call one day at the graduate college at Princeton from Professor Wheeler, in which he said, "Feynman, I know why all electrons have the same charge and the same mass" "Why?" "Because, they are all the same electron!". The idea being that a single electron bounces back and forth from the Big Bang to the Big Crunch, becoming a positron going backwards in time when it "bounces".

Or maybe not.

...but the thing that most puzzles me about Sev1/2/N is that it appears so unclear in what way(s) the interested factions actually benefit or lose out when SevN brings the New Sun. Is this because Wolfe is commenting on the uncertain effects of political machinations (and thus, by implication, that their most important effect is on the egos of the machinators)? Or was it supposed to be clear whether the NS was a happy or unhappy ending (for whomever) and Wolfe didn't really succeed in explaining this? (As Dave Tallman seems to be suggesting)?

newt

2008-06-15 Dave Lebling replying to Dave Tallman #

Argues that Severian is not literally the Conciliator, since the Conciliator is Christ and Severian is only a Christ-like figure whose role revives a dim memory of Christianity. Grounds this in the timeline of the first Severian, noting that his mother could have fled a convent because nuns already existed before his time, and that the hierodules planned this renewal.
proposes · theology-and-religion · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Severian is a Christ-like figure, not the Conciliator; Christianity is a dim memory in the far future; Hierodules planned Severian's renewal of Christian memory

Severian is not actually /the/ Conciliator. The Conciliator, as should be obvious to anyone who knows Wolfe's religious background, is Christ. In the timeline of the first Severian, the Conciliator was a dim memory of Christ and his religion a dim memory of Christianity. His mother could still run away from an order of nuns, because nuns existed before Sev's turn as a "Conciliator-like" figure.

Severian, as a "Christ-like" figure, renewed these dim memories and caused that aspect of society and his own personality to be slightly different.

It was all part of the hierodules' plan.

-- Dave Lebling, aka vizcacha

2008-06-16 b sharp replying to David Wells #

Argues against the SevN idea that Severian's lives are repeated iterations, proposing instead that they are finite and tied to the five coffins in his mausoleum. Speculates Sev1 may be Ymar or a Severian from another universe, and reads the five coffins as a nod to The Fifth Head of Cerberus, answering David Wells.
disputes · puzzle-solving · myth-and-structure · theories: Severian iterations are finite, tied to five coffins; Sev1 may be Ymar; Severian is an avatar of Wolfe; Mausoleum refers to The Fifth Head of Cerberus

Dave Lebling says:

I'm pretty convinced that there has never been a Jesus on Urth (and that is a major underlying theme of the series). Hence the Norse name "Urth" and the many allusions to pre-Christian gods and monsters ruling Urth long after the equivalent time period of Jesus on Earth. The occasional reference to roods or Bible verses can be attributed to souvenirs brought from a more spiritually advanced univiverse (Yesod?) to Briah by people (mostly Father Inire) able to make such travels.

I lean against accepting the SevN concept because I think iterations of Severian are finite- tied to the 5 coffins in Sev's mausoleum. Two of them open means two have been released, with three more to go, if needed. Was Sev1 Ymar? or Severian in a different time-line or universe (Abaddon?).

I admit this is a literary interpretation, not scientific. I think Wolfe puts avatars of himself in his work and much of the most difficult stuff to decipher comes from his own personal experiences and thoughts, distorted and veiled. I see the mausoleum as a 5HoC reference....Number 5 and Severian seem very similar to me.....maybe Wolfe at the time of writing BotNS figured 5 literary avatars of himself would be enough.
