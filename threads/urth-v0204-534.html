2002-04-03 Nicholas Gevers #

Recommends John C. Wright's The Golden Age, a far-future romance newly published by Tor, calling it a masterpiece in the vein of Vance and Wolfe. Repeats an earlier recommendation and offers no close reading, evidence, or interpretation beyond praise for its subtle touches and eloquence.
supports

Just to repeat an earlier recommendation, which I can
now doubly back up: John C. Wright's THE GOLDEN AGE: A
ROMANCE OF THE FAR FUTURE, just out from Tor, is a
dazzling masterpiece in the Vance/Wolfe vein, with a
lot of subtle touches AND a huge eloquence. First in a
series, but that just guarantees further wonders...

--Nick Gevers.

2002-04-28 Adam Stephanides #

Argues that John C. Wright's The Golden Age is an enjoyable read but not especially profound, and does not feel particularly Wolfean. Compares it instead to the work of Jack Vance, Iain M. Banks and perhaps A. E. van Vogt, while noting limited familiarity with van Vogt. Responds to Nicholas Gevers's earlier recommendation that praised the novel as a Vance/Wolfe kind of book.
disputes · genre-and-craft

I got around to reading this. It's a good read, though hardly profound, but didn't strike me as particularly Wolfean. If I had to describe it in that manner, I'd call it a cross between Vance, Iain M. Banks, and perhaps van Vogt (perhaps, because I've read very little van Vogt, and that long ago).

--Adam
