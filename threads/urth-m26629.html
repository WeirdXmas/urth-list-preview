2018-01-14 Angus Maclean #

Proposes that English speakers naturally prefer saying "six six six" over "six hundred and sixty-six", and suggests Wolfe may have meant the number's digits to be read this way in the Apocalypse-related thread. Offers no textual evidence beyond the speaker's own linguistic intuition and a comparison with how French speakers might phrase it.
proposes · close-textual-reading

It just came to me that as a speaker of English I would rather say "six six six" than "six-hundred-and-sixty-six". Perhaps a French speaker would, instead of "six-cents-et-soixante-six", prefer to say "six six six", which, when I think about it, is perhaps what Wolfe meant.
