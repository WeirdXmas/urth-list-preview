2014-07-07 Marc Aramini replying to Andrew Mason #

Argues that wolves appear in Gene Wolfe's work only in a very loose sense, joking that the story Silhouette has no real wolves but a wolf hidden in its protagonist's name. Responds to Andrew Mason's list of wolves across the books by pushing wordplay to absurd lengths before concluding there are no wolves for practical purposes.
chats · source-and-allusion · theories: Johann in Silhouette hides Goethe as a wolf

I mean this really depends how tangential and desparate you want to get. Like Silhouette, which I am almost finished writing up, clearly has no wolves, as there are no animals on the space ship, but johann, our protagonist, is called "betrothed of fortune" by another character (and the name Faust means fortunate or lucky) ... So the wolf in that story is johann Wolfgang goethe ... But really ... ... No wolves for all reasonable intents and purposes

2014-07-07 Andrew Mason #

Lists the books where the poster finds wolves, including An Evil Guest, Peace and The Land Across, and suggests they may be hidden throughout Wolfe's work. Asks whether Wolfe is including them to fulfil a prediction attributed to Gaiman, or making them more obvious to spare readers the search for hidden wolves.
questions · close-textual-reading · theories: Wolves appear hidden throughout Wolfe's works

Continuing this discussion from a while ago: I have just been reading _An Evil Guest_, and that contains one explicit werewolf, perhaps with suggestions that there are also hidden ones.

Also - I thought I had posted this before, but I can't find it in the archive, so will repeat it: Marc's recent post on _Peace_ showed that there are wolves in that, in 'The Stag at Bay'.

So, we have wolves in:
Cerberus
Peace
New Sun
Castleview
Soldier
Long Sun (perhaps)
The Wizard Knight (perhaps)
An Evil Guest
The Sorcerer's House
The Land Across.

I think it's likely that they are indeed everywhere, though I'm still worried about where they can be in _Short Sun_. They do seem to be becoming more obvious in recent works, though. Is Wolfe putting them in to fulfil Gaiman's prediction? Or is he making them more blatant to save us the time and trouble spent in searching for the hidden wolf?
