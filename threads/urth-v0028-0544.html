1999-11-29 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Quotes a passage from Wolfe's new story 'Has Anybody Seen Junie Moon?' in MOON SHOTS, in which antimatter would fall up, and says it validates earlier list speculation about the fliers of Urth. Asks where Wolfe got the idea, noting that he has toyed with antimatter repulsion before and that the earlier speculations now look confirmed.
informs · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Urth fliers exploit antimatter repulsion from gravity

We have talked in the past about the curious details surrounding the fliers of Urth, which seem to have features of anti-gravity and anti-matter. This, in turn, seems to fly in the face of facts and theory as we understand them.

A new story, "Has Anybody Seen Junie Moon?" (in the MOON SHOTS anthology), has a passage that relates directly to our speculations:

"It's only theoretical so far. But current theory says that although antimatter would possess mass just as ordinary matter does, it would be repelled by the gravitational field of ordinary matter. It would fall up, in other words" (MOON SHOTS, p. 32).

Interesting to see, cut and dry, black and white: the speculations we rolled over and over become starkly validated. Whether the Science be Good or Bad, this is a concept that Wolfe has toyed with and continues to toy with; and I am curious as to where he got the idea.

1999-11-29 Jim Henley #

Argues that Wolfe's apparently fanciful science in the New Sun series, such as the antimatter-repelling fliers of Urth, has real scientific speculation behind it rather than being gobbledygook. Builds on Michael Andre-Driussi's quoted antimatter remark and ties it to the recent alzabo and memory RNA discussion and the white fountain as a possible wormhole.
proposes · genre-and-craft · theories: Wolfe's science is plausible speculation rather than gobbledygook

Nice reference, Michael. The larger lesson is surely to be drawn in conjunction with the recent alzabo/memory RNA discussion: every time someone thinks Wolfe is spouting gobbledygook in the series (which would push it in the direction of fantasy) it turns out that there's plausible scientific speculation behind Wolfe's approach. (One might adduce the "white fountain"/wormhole too.)

Best,

Jim
