The thread: Dan'l Danehy-Oakes proposed explaining the climate difference between Green and Blue through atmospheric chemistry and albedo instead of different orbits, citing insolation physics and noting both are life-zone planets. Charles Reed largely agreed that greenhouse gases or albedo could account for the contrast without radically different orbits, while still insisting Green does not orbit Blue the way Lune orbits Urth. Danehy-Oakes then apologized for a sharp reply and acknowledged that list members have debated the Bleen system's orbital mechanics in On Blue's Waters, without making a new claim. The exchange ended in partial agreement, with the orbital question left unsettled and the discussion drifting toward apology and retraction.

Proposes explaining the climate difference between the planets Green and Blue through atmospheric chemistry rather than different orbits, suggesting Green has more greenhouse gases and Blue a higher albedo. Offers the physics of insolation and average surface temperature as the grounds, noting both are life-zone planets so the difference is small.

Apologizes to Charles Reed for a sharp reply and concedes that many list members have debated the orbital mechanics of the Bleen system in On Blue's Waters, including odd orbit shapes. Charles,

I didn't mean to direct my Ahem at you; you are hardly the first to get involved in the orbit trap in discussing the mechanics of the Bleen system. I've seen some of our smartest list members go on at great length about possible orbits (including some extreme cases like a sort of W-shaped orbit that actually works but that I really doubt would be stable for a planetary pair for any long period of time).

Nor had I thought of this possibility prior to today, so I'm not claiming any special credit for brilliance here ...
