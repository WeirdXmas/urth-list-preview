2000-11-22 Jacob Corbin #

Bets list members fifty cents each that the giant figure on the cover of Return to the Whorl is the Outsider, Silk, a Silk/Pas composite, Gene Wolfe himself, or a mixture of these. Offers the wager playfully, doubting Wolfe would insert himself into the book but admitting one never knows.
proposes · theories: Cover figure is Silk, Pas, Outsider, or Wolfe

I hereby bet everyone on this list fifty cents apiece that the giant figure on RETURN TO THE WHORL's cover is either a) the Outsider, b) Silk, c) Silk/Pas, d) Gene Wolfe, or e) some mixture of any of the above.

I doubt that Wolfe would resort to that cheesy trope of sticking himself in the book, but you never know....

Jacob

2000-11-23 Timothy Reilly #

Argues that In Green's Jungles is weaker than On Blue's Waters, citing confusing astral dream travel, a familiar clever-ruse battle plot, and an obscure protagonist whose motives are hard to identify with. Also disputes a reading of Silk's blood-covered body as a suicide attempt, since a dead body would not suit his planned new body, and mentions the cover of Return to the Whorl.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Silk's bloody body is not a suicide attempt

Sadly I don't think IGJ is even half as good. I thought OBW was wonderful, but IGJ is getting too confusing with its astral dream travel all over the place. Also do we really need yet another story about how a clever man helps defeat a superior enemy on the battlefield by a ruse of some sort? This occurred not only in OBW as well, but constantly in Western literature since at least the Iliad. I missed the point of this part of the plot. The obscurity of who the protagonist is and why he does what he does left me a bit irritated after a while. Too much magic and uncertainty seems to be creeping into the plot, and it makes it much harder to identify with the protagonist's problems or choices. As has been pointed out before, why doesn't he just try, with Fava's help perhaps, to astral travel back to Nettle, where he says he wants to be?

On a point of detail, I didn't read the bit where the narrator sees Silk covered in blood as being a suicide attempt - since he's meant to be getting a new body, a dead or dying post-suicide one wouldn't be very effective! The speculation about a head removal is fascinating, though I confess I just read it as a sacrificial orgy to try to revitalise Hyacinth.

The cover for RTTW looks pretty bad, but they've been going downhill since OBW. Could be a giant super-Baldanders type in RTTW, though the one pictured is far too big for earth strength gravity (which is why Baldanders went underwater ofcourse). Or could be Wolfe in a silly hat, but I hope not!

Tim
