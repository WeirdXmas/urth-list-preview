2014-09-21 Antonin Scriabin #

Reports finishing Acceptance, the final book of The Southern Reach trilogy, and saying it gave enough answers while leaving lingering open questions. Notes Lovecraftian themes near the end and says some parts recall The Book of the New Sun and The Urth of the New Sun, then asks others for their opinions.
questions

Just finished Acceptance. Anyone else finish the trilogy? What do you think?

Overall, I was satisfied. There were enough answers to make it worthwhile, and enough unanswered / new questions to keep it intriguing well after having finished.

There were some serious Lovecraftian themes towards the end as well, and some stuff that reminded me of BOTNS and UOTNS.

2014-09-21 Matthew Keeley replying to Antonin Scriabin #

Praises Vandermeer's Southern Reach trilogy, ranking the first and third volumes highest and crediting him with fine writing on nature, both everyday and sublime. Compares him to Peter Matthiessen and Annie Dillard, and reads the unresolved ending as everyday acceptance rather than giving up, replying to Antonin Scriabin's post.
supports · genre-and-craft

I liked the series very much. I think the first and third books are the best, but I admired all three volumes, and I suspect I would enjoy the second volume more if I were to reread it.

Vandermeer has a real talent for the unnatural, but I've rarely encountered a sf writer who is so good on nature in its quotidian and sublime aspects. I think in a parallel universe, he's one of the most acclaimed nature writers, a Peter Mathiessen or Annie Dillard.

And perhaps that talent for the quotidian carries over into the book's ending: We don't see the puzzle put together, we don't even see all the pieces, but don't we have that experience everyday? "Acceptance" isn't the same as giving up.

-Matt
