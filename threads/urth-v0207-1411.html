arcologies like mountains

Urth list · 13 messages · 2002-07-16 to 2002-07-18

2002urthThe Book of the New SunMatthew MalthouseAndy RobertsonJames WynnDavid A BairdDan'l Danehy-OakesMichael Andre-Driussi
The thread: Michael Andre-Driussi proposed that Urth's mountains, especially the single cliff, might be artificial arcologies built by non-human civilizations, and that the 97 Kyear dating is debatable. Several posters, led by Dan'l Danehy-Oakes and matthew.malthouse, disputed this, arguing the text describes natural, uncarved peaks that Severian reaches on his northward journey, and that the Saltus mines dig up debris of the past. James Wynn supported a short timeline and the debris view, while the thread drifted into the Hieros and Hierogrammates, where Crush, David Baird and Wynn argued over whether those beings arise from an earlier cycle or from a far future, with no clear resolution.
  • Michael Andre-Driussi: mountains may be artificial arcologies built by non-human civilizations
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: mountains likely built structures, but the text does not prove it; uncarved peaks are the ones Typhon carves
  • matthew.malthouse: new uncarved peaks would have been a huge upheaval never mentioned; mines hold past detritus
  • James Wynn: short timeline, beyond a million years characters are not human; Saltus mine is debris
  • Crush: Hieros beings go backward in time and drop into it, created in a far future
  • David A Baird: Hierogrammates and Hierodules are creations from Urth's past who want the new sun

Themes: Time loops and causality, Deep time and ruins, The dying sun and the cosmos

2002-07-16 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Argues that the mountains on Urth, especially the single cliff, may be artificial arcologies built by non-human civilizations rather than natural formations, and that the 97 Kyear dating is itself debatable. Supports this with analogies to Stapledon's supercities and Asimov's Trantor, and with the brown book's descriptions of vast cities like skeletons of dragons, plus Wolfe's 'perhaps one million years' remark.
proposes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Urth's mountains are artificial arcologies built by non-humans

Oh dear, back to this chestnut.

Yes, the 97 Kyear figure is _very_ debateable. (For all sorts of reasons. Let a thousand timelines bloom!)

But, as far as I am concerned, so is the notion that the "mountains" are natural (especially the single cliff that is being referred to here, however obliquely). I'm sure anyone who has read Olaf Stapledon can instantly recall the supercities like continental coral reefs that sprout up in superscience civilizations -- indeed, they are rather puny compared with Asimov's "full metal planet" of Trantor. The concept is rather a staple of sf, and iirc, the brown book says that non-humans built vast cities like "banks of cloud" (note scale) and like "skeletons of dragons" (note fossil imagery) on Urth after the zenith of the Empire.

As for "multiple millions," Wolfe has written, more than once, iirc, that it is "perhaps" one million years distant (which I have always taken as an outer limit rather than an inner limit, because of the qualifier). FWIW.

=mantis=

Denies that the text describes artificially raised mountain ranges, suggesting the reader may be thinking of Urth and Apu Punchau instead. Argues that many Wolfe passages seem big then shrink, and that mountains could be decayed supercities turned into tells, citing the Saltus mine as a comparison with Roman excavations.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Mountains are decayed supercities turned into tells

Nope, not in the text I read. If you can find it again, please tell. (Perhaps you are mistakenly thinking of URTH and the era of Apu Punchau.)

You don't have to believe it. There are plenty of readers who believe, for example, that the Old Sun is dying of natural causes; or that Lune's orbit is a natural function of time rather than engineering; or that all those weird animals evolved on Urth (implying evolutionary time scales); etc. (Everyone has their own mix and match.)

It seems to me that there are many such cases of misdirection in the text where things once seem very big and then shrink down.

2002-07-17 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that the mountains and cliff in The Claw of the Sun are not natural but more likely built structures, a common science fiction trope, while doubting this reading is textually proven. Responds to an earlier post by citing the Saltus mines, the cooled core of Urth, and the Planet of the Apes apartment mine, and dismisses the million-year figure as exaggeration in Wolfe's stories.
disputes · close-textual-reading · genre-and-craft · theories: Urth's mountains are artificial arcologies

More of a walnut, I think. A hard nut to crack.

Bravo!

I was not referring to the single cliff; others have mentioned the mines of Saltus, (which are pretty darn deep down), the newer mountains ...

... and perhaps the most important piece of evidence, which I didn't cite because I'm not prepared to go and reread the whole darn thing right now looking for one sentence: but ... somewhere in there it's mentioned that Urth's core has cooled solid. And that is not going to happen in the next 97K years, or the next million.

Well, yeah (though it's not a full metal planet; it's a planet with a full metal jacket ... later, after some of the metal has been stripmined, people can farm on Trantor). And my own favorite examples of this, the plastic-coated planet of Bunch's "Moderan" and the continental cities of Wingrove's "Chung Kuo." Posdef a known and negotiable trope of the stfnal ecology: I grant that readily. (Indeed, the Autarch's palace may be an example.)

Still: while I have no textual proof of this, in my first reading of tSotL, and since, I didn't read the cliff that way at all -- a standard SF trope _did_ enter my mind, but the emblematic example of the trope was the "apartment mine" in the original film of PLANET OF THE APES. (Where, granted, it is unlikely that "millions" of years have passed...)

...and? I don't imagine you'll disagree if I say that the stories in "Wonders" are _not_, in general, to be taken as historically factual (in the universe of the discourse). Aside from the blatantly poetic language, I also expect these descriptions to be, well, exaggerated.

And I have always taken it as typical Lupine vupinity ... "perhaps one million years" doesn't actually commit Wolfe to anything.

--Dan'l

Grounds this on the absence of any mention of such an upheaval against the long continuity of decline implied across the Commonwealth.

2002-07-17 James Wynn #

Argues for a short timeline for Wolfe's future, holding that beyond about a million years the characters could not credibly be considered human, and defends the idea that the Saltus mine works debris of the past rather than raw ore. Supports the point with an analogy to Roman excavations and with the scale of archaeological artifacts accumulated from the Bronze Age to the present, responding to the earlier mine discussion without quoting it.
supports · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: Saltus mine extracts debris of the past, not ore

I'm inclined toward short-timeline theories myself - simply because I don't think beyond a maximum of a million years in the future, the characters could credibly be considered human. Although a review of the archives reveals a wealth of debate on this subject, I wasn't on the list then. ;-) So I'll offer my pennies:
As for the mine at Saltus: well, urk -- do you believe that excavations of Roman cities prove that they were in place a million years ago? Probably not.
But the point I think was made about the mines is that they are not mining the crust for raw materials (ores) as we would understand it but mining the detrius of the past - that's unlikely to be less than many many times what we'd find today in the whole archological deposit between the Iron Age and now.
From the beginning of the Bronze Age til now - 5000 years --- the currently available technologically-advanced archeological artifacts available for us to "mine", is still impressive.
Imagine that extended to 100,000 years - mostly encompassing the output of artifacts since the first industrial revolution a mere 250 years ago --- well, one could probably mine for generations in the New Jersey garbage dumps even if we shut them down today.

-- Crush

2002-07-18 James Wynn #

Argues that the Hierodules and Hierogrammates cannot be explained by a simple loop of self-replication, since Crush says their original creation comes after the New Sun's advent. Responds to James Wynn and David Baird's timeline reasoning by citing Dan'l's proposal that they arise in a previous universe-cycle and by re-reading The Urth of the New Sun.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: Hieros created by humans after New Sun; Hieros created in previous universe-cycle; Hieros ensure their own original creation

... the Hieros/Hierogrammates/Hierodules are creations from Urths past and now they want the new sun so that Urth can regain it's glory and then in turn recreate them again. 100K years seems too few for such a cycle.

Crush asked:
Wait a minute. I don't think I'm understanding this point. What possible limitations of time could the Hieros offer? They go backwards in Time, go outside of Time, drop in on Time. I presumed that Hieros were created in a future far beyond the Age of the BothNS.

David responded:
If there's no time limitation to the Hieros activity then why do they rely on Urth regaining its power?

Now Crush says:
Because...oh wait... now I understand your timeline. You are saying that the Hieros are created between our time and Severian's and that they are backing the new Sun in order to have themselves created a second time around? Right?

That's not the way I read tUotNS. My recollection of my reading was that their ORIGINAL creation had been brought about by beings created by humankind AFTER the New Sun's advent, AND that those beings would *never* be created in the Future where the Dying Sun continues to decline. Dan'l has offered another fold, saying that the Hieros are created during a previous universe-cycle - I'll have to re-read the book I guess. But even so, all this takes place (relatively speaking) AFTER the advent of the New Sun.

Hence, the Hieros are not goosing the Urth toward the New Sun just to replicate themselves in the future. They are ensuring their own original creation. And with Dan'l,s twist the possibility arises that THEIR eventual creation is necessary for humanity's development (and the Hiero's creation, etc.).

Twisted enough for ya?
--Crush
