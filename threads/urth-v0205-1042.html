Themes: Colonization and the native, Doubles, twins and clones

2002-05-22 David Duffy #

Proposes that the mention of cut dogs in the thread mirrors the castrated twin in "A Story" and argues that colonized land loses its physical traces. Grounds this in Marsch's remark that the Aborigines were dendritic rather than paleolithic, and in the Brisbane example of bora rings built over by settlement.
proposes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion · theories: the cut dog mirrors the castrated twin in "A Story"

The only reason for its mention I can see is the symmetry with _"A Story"_, where one of the twins is cut (probably actually castrated).

About level of destruction of physical evidence, there is firstly Marsch's comment that the abos are dendritic rather than paleolithic, so that few durable objects are made. The other is that in densely settled regions, the physical evidence does tend to disappear (where I live in Brisbane, there is argument about the number of Aboriginal groups who lived in the area and what languages they spoke, while all their places such as bora rings have been largely built over). The other twinning is the "back of beyond" (where evidence might persist) versus settled farmlands. It is pretty obvious that few colonists visit the wilds.

David Duffy
