The thread: Lee Berman asked why the narrator of On Blue's Waters writes [sic] after both Hoof and Horn and Horn and Hide, and whether Hide's identity is meant to be questioned. Gerry Quinn read the mislabeling as a typo that proofreaders missed. Marc Aramini argued the [sic] markers show Silk's personality intruding, while Sergei Soloviev held that the uncertain identity belongs to the narrator, who knows the names and substitutes Horn almost indifferently. Berman ended by proposing that Silk's paternal feelings for Horn blur with Horn's feelings for Hoof and Hide, which he said accounts for both [sic] marks, and he agreed partly with Gerry and Marc. The thread ends with a reading that combines several positions rather than a clear resolution.
  • Lee Berman: asked whether the [sic] marks and Hide's identity are deliberately questioned, later argued the names and paternal feelings blur together in the Sil
  • Marc Aramini: the [sic] markers are signs of Silk's personality intruding, not typos
  • Gerry Quinn: the mislabeling is a typo that proofreaders could easily have missed
  • Sergei SOLOVIEV: the identity in question is the narrator's; substituting Horn for Hoof or Hide is stylistically deliberate and well done

Themes: Unreliable narration, Names and their meanings, Fathers, mothers and children

2012-09-18 Sergei SOLOVIEV replying to Gerry Quinn #

Argues that the [sic] in On Blue's Waters, Ch. 5 corresponds to both Hoof and Hide and to Horn, so the identity in question is the narrator's. Answers Lee Berman's confusion, quoted in Gerry Quinn's post, by noting the narrator knows the names and may substitute Horn for either almost indifferently, and calls this stylistically well done.
answers · close-textual-reading · theories: Narrator's names for Hoof and Hide are interchangeable

To my mind, [sic] corresponds to both names in both cases, and attracts the attention
to the fact that the narrator knows the names Hoof and Hide, but may substitute Horn
for each almost indifferently - so the identity that is questioned is rather the
identity of the narrator. My impression was that it is stylistically nicely done.

Sergei Soloviev
