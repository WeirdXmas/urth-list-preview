The thread: The thread began with Lee Berman and James Wynn disputing whether the inhumi and Shadow Children are imitators, colonizing natives, or simply human, and whether Urth is the Blue or Green sun. Gerry Quinn pressed textual objections and conceded little, while Marc Aramini reported Wolfe's private remark that Green is Urth, which sparked a long debate over planetary identity. Later posts shifted to a speculative cosmic spiral of universes, then to the City on Green as Nessus, the closed roads, and the centaur myth of Nessus, which Adam Thornton and Jeff Wilson corrected. The thread ended in drift rather than agreement, with the Nessus and closed-roads threads trailing off into classical trivia and an unrelated link.
  • Lee Berman: inhumi cannot perfectly imitate humans and mirror the Shadow Children as predators
  • James Wynn: natives are colonizing shapeshifters or altered abos, and Green may equal Urth
  • Gerry Quinn: natives are non-human in spirit, and Green-as-Urth is metaphorical
  • Son of Witz: closed roads symbolize stagnation and the City on Green may be Urth
  • Jeff Wilson: closed roads paralyze social order; Nexus name fits poorly with walls
  • Adam Thornton: Nessus name is ironic inversion of Buenos Aires and centaur myth

Themes: Colonization and the native, Beasts and the animal in us, The dying sun and the cosmos

2010-12-29 James Wynn replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that Wolfe's inhumi in The Fifth Head of Cerberus are not imitators but are simply who they are without bodily clothing, and that the book is about colonization, where arrivals take on the natives' form. Grounds this in Marsch's outback encounter with aboriginal beings who shift into cats, elks and white worms, and in Cilinia's night chough form, responding to Lee Berman's quoted passage on inhumi being unable to imitate humans.
disputes · close-textual-reading · allegory-and-typology · theories: Inhumi are not mimicking humans in dream-travel; Fifth Head of Cerberus is about colonization

I don't think this applies to the inhumi and I think you have misinterpreted the lesson. I think Wolfe is making a statement about colonization here. It has always gone on. One group arriving to push out the previous one. But in order for the colonization to stick, the arrivals with have to take on the customs and eventually even the appearance of the natives--those attributes were not invented out of nothing. So the arrivals become the natives--in this case, by the natives becoming the arrivals. And the native environment is huge and inexhaustible. The arrivals will never overwhelm the environment's ability to absorb them. When Marsch goes into the outback, he finds abos existing quite happily occassionally taking the form of cats or four-eyed elks. He even find the abos in their original native state: lurid, white worms.

As for the inhumi, when they are in dream-travel, they aren't mimicking. That is who they really are without the clothing of their bodies. They are no more mimicking than Cilinia was mimicking a night chough (as Pas did before her). Returning to their reptilian form is only a matter putting their old clothes back on.

The black sword that Horn was given came from a selection of swords inside a "bronze tablet" (23) that was located in a ruined building. The Neighbor told him, "This was our goddess of purity". That is, it was a Neighbor shrine for a Neighbor goddess. Horn prayed to it and was rewarded with the little light that he used in the sewer.

There is no doubt about it; the ruined City on Green was built by the Neighbors, for the Neighbors and used by the Neighbors before they abandoned it. If this is another "asinine" reading by me of the simple textual facts, so be it.
