Themes: Colonization and the native

1999-04-08 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Welcomes Nick Gevers and jokingly notes that his Nova Express essay seems to reveal the Short Sun's human boatpeople and native bat people, a colonization plot. Supports that reading by comparing it to Le Guin's Hainish novels The Word for World Is Forest, where humans behave badly, and Planet of Exile, where a human enclave faces native attack.
supports · theories: Short Sun's human colonists and native bat people

Hey everybody, look--it's Dr. Nick Gevers.

<??>

Author of that fine essay in "Nova Express."

<applause, cheers>

And he has just given away the major surprise of THE BOOK OF THE SHORT SUN, seven months before the first book will be published <g>!

<!!>

No, no, put down those pitchforks and torches--I'm only joking.

<?!>

Joking about the next series--I mean, I don't know anything about the next series, I certainly don't know as much as the publishers know.

But what Nick Gevers writes seems quite likely to be true. The human "boatpeople" and the native-alien "bat people." Thoughts of interstellar colonization, with sinister effects, turns my mind toward the Hainish books of Le Guin: THE WORD FOR WORLD IS FOREST being a case where the humans are being very, very naughty; PLANET OF EXILE being an example where the human enclave is in danger of being wiped out by alien-native Mongol-types, and thus deserving of sympathy (sympathy for humans, that is).

=mantis=
