Aquastor

Urth list · 3 messages · 1998-08-02 to 1998-08-04

1998urthThe Book of the New Sunmary whalen
The thread: Mary Whalen opened the thread by passing along a golem-legend text and pointing readers to Meyrink's The Golem as background on the figure behind the aquastor idea. Over the next two posts she, as the only poster, proposed etymologies for the word aquastor, first from Latin quasi ('almost as if') because aquastors mimic living things, then from Arabic or Latin roots such as acu-, aequus, aqua or quaerere, offered explicitly as speculation. The thread ended without other participants responding, so none of the etymologies was confirmed or challenged.
  • mary whalen: aquastor may derive from Latin quasi, since aquastors are artificial beings that mimic life
  • mary whalen: aquastor may come from Arabic al quastr, an alchemical term, or from various Latin roots, as speculation

1998-08-03 mary whalen #

Proposes that the word aquastor may derive from the Latin quasi, meaning almost as if, because aquastors are artificial beings that mimic living things. Admits no other information about aquastors has been found, so the etymology rests only on the word's apparent sense and the creatures' artificial nature.

This is Sean Whalen (prion).

I still haven't found any other information about aquastors, but I think it's possible that the name comes from quasi, meaning almost as if. This would be because of an aquastor's artificial nature in mimicking something.

1998-08-04 mary whalen #

Proposes that the word aquastor may come from Arabic, perhaps an original form like al quastr, since it is an alchemical term. Also suggests possible Latin roots such as acu-, aequus, quasi, aqua or quaerere, offering the etymology as speculation about Wolfe's word choice.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Aquastor name derives from Arabic al quastr

This is Sean Whalen (prion).

I've been thinking about from where the name aquastor could come, and since it's an alchemical term, it might be Arabic. The original form might be something like al quastr. Also, if it is Latin, it might be from acu- (sharp) or aequus (equal), as well as from quasi, aqua, or quaerere.
