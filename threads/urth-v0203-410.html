Right. Well you've just caught me at a bad time for such a thing: recent discussion about a story that has bugged me for 20 years ("Hour of Trust"), and then the rediscovery of a (for me literally forgettable) story ("The Blue Mouse") that imho makes a very nice pairing with the first story (slightly raising the value of the second story).

But to what end? Both are collected; neither is one of my favorites. Discussion bogs down, ceases . . . which is just as well, since where is the enthusiasm anyway?

It is too early to say for more recent stories, but stories published before 1996 have been passed over by Wolfe and his editors many times, so this would be caveat number one. Unlikely to find a "Tracking Song" or "Forlessen" there.

Caveat number two: we argue about everything around here, and something as subjective as ranking of stories seems likely to draw argument (if anybody else has read the stories; if, among this subgroup, anybody can raise the ergs to respond).

Still.

2002-03-21 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

States that the poster is fond of the stories "Loco Parentis" and "Robot's Story", calling the first chilling and the second slight and dated, and compares "Loco Parentis" to The Fifth Head of Cerberus. Gives personal reading history as the reason, noting these were first Wolfe encounters, and dismisses "Tarzan of the Grapes" as slight and unmemorable.
supports

Mantis proclaims:

Very well.

Perhaps it's simply because these (along with "With the Lafayette Escadrille") were my first Wolfe encounter of the Gene kind, but I'm rather fond of both of them.

"Robot's Story" is, to be fair, slight and somewhat dated - it is very much a product of the '60s, in the way that much of DV and A,DV are.

"Loco Parentis" is neither slight nor dated, and is (to my mind) rather chilling in the way Wolfe does so well. Not _as_ chilling as, say, FIFTH HEAD, but in the same kind of way.

Tarzan of the Grapes F&SF 6-72, MWALB (Farmer)

The only other story on the "uncollected Wolfe" list I believe I have read, this is slight indeed and quite unmemorable.

--Blattid
