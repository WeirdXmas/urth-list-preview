Another (Possibly Weak) Theory

Urth list · 8 messages · 2011-01-06 to 2011-01-09

2011urthThe Book of the New SunAntónio Pedro MarquesDan'l Danehy-OakesLee BermanDavid Stockhoff
The thread: Dan'l Danehy-Oakes opened by proposing that an ancient non-human star-faring race lived on Ste. Anne, was destroyed by a tectonic event, and that survivors became or were imitated by the shape-changing natives, then asked for ways to strengthen the idea. David Stockhoff answered with lost-colony and elf-like origin ideas, linking the Shee and Dreamtime and Gondwanaland notions to racial memory, while Danehy-Oakes responded by identifying the natives with the Sidhe and attributing their avoidance of tools to cold iron. Lee Berman backed the prehuman-source idea and linked the Shadow Children to child-stealing myths, and Danehy-Oakes extended that to changelings and racial memory while voicing doubt. The thread stayed a freewheeling speculative exchange with no resolution, and its last post only quoted Stockhoff.
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: ancient non-human star-faring race destroyed on Ste. Anne, survivors imitated by natives; later identifies natives with Sidhe
  • DAVID STOCKHOFF: green eyes and Shee link to lost colonies; Shadow Children and Sandwalker as racial memory of African origins
  • Lee Berman: prehuman race as source of myths; Shadow Children linked to Inhumi and child-stealing faerie lore
  • António Pedro Marques: quotes Stockhoff that non-literate peoples forget anything older than a grandfather

Themes: Myth and folklore sources, Ghosts, dreams and visions, Evolution and the future of humanity

2011-01-06 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Proposes that a very ancient, non-human star-faring race lived on Ste. Anne and was wiped out by a tectonic event, with survivors becoming or being imitated by the shape-changing aborigines. Grounds this on Wolfe's claim that many mythological beings are real, a McCaffrey-Wolfe quote, and an earlier crash suggested in the text, and asks for suggestions to strengthen it.
proposes · myth-and-structure · author-and-biography · theories: Ancient elf-like race on Ste. Anne wiped out by tectonic event; Shape-changing abos became or imitated the ancient race

That is my current working hypothesis, for which I do not have a real timeline: my mind doesn't work in such an
organized manner. But roughly I am thinking of Wolfe's
claim to believe that many "mythological" beings are real.

So let's look at a Very Very Long Time Ago, in a place
that has no name in English but which we might call
Atlantis, Mu, or Gondwanaland. There was a race,
which was not precisely what we would call _human_,
that developed, among other things, the ability to
travel to the stars, not necessarily with the aid of
"starcrosser" technology. (See, for example, Peter
Hamilton's "Silfen.") We might have called them
elves, or faerie.

A Very Very Long Time Ago, their culture was
completely(?) wiped out, possibly by a massive
tectonic event. But some of them were left on
Ste. Anne.

I haven't quite decided to my satisfaction what
happened next: either they _became_ the shape-
changing abos, or they were imitated _by_ them.

Any suggestions to "plump out" this theory would
be appreciated.

2011-01-06 David Stockhoff replying to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Asks whether the green eyes of the Ste. Anne people can be explained by an ancient lost-colony origin, and whether that can be reconciled with a recent lost-colony origin for humans on the planet. Responds to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's theory that an ancient elf-like race was wiped out, with survivors on Ste. Anne becoming or being imitated by the shape-changing aborigines, by linking it to the Shee and the idea of hidden, magic-using descendants.
proposes · myth-and-structure · source-and-allusion · theories: Ste. Anne people descend from an ancient lost colony; Ancient and recent lost-colony origins can both be true; Shee are the ancient race hiding on Earth

Would this explain the green eyes? That is, to the extent that these people also evolved from primates on Earth as we did, they are human, but?they are physically marked?as another race? I personally accept the recent-lost-colony origin hypothesis, but I can see how the ancient-lost-colony hypothesis fits some of Wolfe's favorite themes, including the near-repetition of events over cosmic stretches of time. And the Shee (if I may call them that), if they survived on Earth (as they must have done to be real at all, because otherwise humans would not know them), were very interested in hiding themselves from men, using magic to do so. Their descendants might well have psychic star-shields. Is there a way in which the recent-origin hypothesis can be reconciled with the ancient-origin hypothesis? Can both be true? The Shee were not exactly tool-users to begin with, even on Earth. Of course, if we are expected to know that, then the links to them may simply be by analogy.?

2011-01-06 David Stockhoff replying to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Speculates that the Shadow Children, the Shee and the Sandwalker in Wolfe's books may reflect a racial memory of humanity's African origins, tying them to Aboriginal Dreamtime and the Gondwanaland hypothesis. Responds to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's theory of faerie child-replacement by admitting uncertainty, citing Dogon lore and the pale-to-brown fairy idea, and calling the Shee interstellar travellers.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Shadow Children reflect racial memory; Sandwalker resembles an Australian Aboriginal; Shee are interstellar travellers; Fairies were originally brown-skinned

Oh, no problem. I am very much on the fence and can't keep abos and Shadow Children straight any more than I can keep the various Hiero-dudes straight. I'd note that "racial memory" memes once were very popular and seem to be indirectly played on here---someone mentioned the Dogons. Also, Sandwalker always reminded me of an Australian aborigine, so the Dreamtime (very long time ago) is not a stretch ?for me. And Australian aborigines came from Africa as much as India.?Fairies are often pale and blond, but before that, they were brown.? There really are strong hints of the Shee as interstellar travellers. But then, in our real world, there are also "memories" of their existence. Yet, they are not actually real outside those stories! So what does this say about the Gondwanaland hypothesis? It's maddening.

2011-01-06 Lee Berman replying to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Supports Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's theory that a prehuman race in a lost age was the source of Wolfe's mythological beings, and adds a link between the Shadow Children and the Inhumi. Grounds the link in lamia and faerie myths of child-stealing and suggests the trait may recall early human interactions with Homo habilis and Homo erectus.
supports · source-and-allusion · myth-and-structure · theories: Mythological beings were a prehuman starfaring race; Shadow Children and Inhumi share a child-stealing lineage; Faerie child-stealing recalls Homo habilis and erectus interactions

A very good theory, in my opinion. I think David S. must agree since he suggested something similar recently. I like it especially because it relates both to WOlfe's professed beliefs and to his other works.

My contribution to plump it out would be this: Many have noted the similarities between Shadow Children and Inhumi. In the earlier days of vampire myths, the lamia (deriveed from Lillith/Jahi myths). were associated as much with child-stealing as with blood sucking. Faeries (and kelpies and other Wolfe references) are also known for child-stealing. For me this takes a thread of connection between 5HoC and Short Sun and plumps it out to a nice fat piece of string. :- ).

(the faerie-child stealing stuff may well relate to anthropology..ancient memories of H. habilus and H. erectus interactions, perhaps. Differences in tool using ability surely come into play also, a fact Wolfe had to be aware of).
