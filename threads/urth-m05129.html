Aside on Christ figures in TBotNS

Urth list · 20 messages · 2008-12-11 to 2008-12-12

2008urthThe Book of the New SunThe Book of the Long SunJohn WatkinsJordon FlatoDavid StockhoffSon of WitzDave Tallman
The thread: Participants debated whether Tzadkiel, the Conciliator, and the other beings in The Book of the New Sun are Christ figures or Demiurge-like false gods, with David Stockhoff proposing a parody reading in which Tzadkiel's son parallels Christ. Jordon Flato, John Watkins, and Son of Witz argued Tzadkiel is an angel or higher hierarchy member who never claims to be the Increate, while Stockhoff insisted the Christ pattern holds despite imperfections. Dave Tallman challenged the idea of two different gods via Marcion. Views on whether the aliens are gods or angels remained split, and the thread ended without resolution, with Son of Witz defending circular reasoning as fitting the book's time loops.
  • David Stockhoff: Tzadkiel is a counterfeit Demiurge whose son parallels Christ; the aliens are gods in a sci-fi sense with a deeper layer
  • Jordon Flato: Tzadkiel never claims godhood and sits in a higher angelic hierarchy, not as a Christ figure
  • Son of Witz: Tzadkiel is the closest candidate but an archangel or djinn; the aliens are human frameworks for higher beings
  • John Watkins: Wolfe was an orthodox Catholic; Tzadkiel is likely a parody of angels, and aliens are Sufficiently Advanced Aliens
  • Dave Tallman: Yahweh and the New Testament God are one being, since Marcion's split was rejected as heresy

Themes: Christ figures and messiahs, Gods, angels and the Increate, Gnosticism, heresy and other theologies

2008-12-11 David Stockhoff #

Proposes that Tzadkiel's son, who fights and dies in the judicial combat confirming Severian as Epitome, parallels Christ as Tzadkiel's Son, exposed to death rather than sacrificed. Grounds this in identifying Tzadkiel as the Demiurge and cites the theory that the book parodies true religion, linking it to Borges's 'atropious or banal reality' from Tlon, Uqbar, Orbis Tertius.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · theology-and-religion · theories: Tzadkiel's son is a Christ figure; Tzadkiel is the Demiurge; TBotNS parodies the true religion

I had forgotten that Tzadkiel's son fought and died in the judicial combat that confirmed Severian's success as Epitome.

If Tzadkiel is the Demiurge, and the Demiurge is God, then that makes the son, well, his Son. Not sacrificed, but exposed to the risk of death, because Tzadkiel thought it wrong to judge Urth without putting something of his own on the block.

Again, I am reminded of the theory that TBotNS presents parodies of the true religion, and the phrase in Tlon, Uqbar, Orbis Tertius that Wolfe has pointed at as an inspiration for TBotNS: "an atropious or banal reality."

2008-12-11 Jordon Flato replying to David Stockhoff #

Argues that Tzadkiel differs from the Demiurge because he never demands worship, denies the Increate, or sets himself in its place, instead acknowledging his nullity before it. Responds to David Stockhoff's parody theory by contrasting the two beings' stances toward the divine, drawing on their conduct in the books.
disputes · theology-and-religion · theories: Tzadkiel is not a parody of God

Well, a big big difference here is that I don't see Tzadkiel asking for worship, or denying the Increate, or putting himself in place of the Increate. He acknowledges his nullity in the face of the Increate, and also never demands worship. The Demiurge demands worship, and tries to set himself up as God with a capital G.
