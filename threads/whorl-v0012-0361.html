Themes: Gods, angels and the Increate

2001-02-26 Robert Borski #

Supports a reader's idea that a godling wounded Silk, adding that a godling may also have attempted to kill Caldé Mint, and asks when the godlings stopped preaching that landers must leave the Whorl. Notes that the change to a message of staying coincides with Horn's arrival via the Silk transfusion and questions whether that timing is coincidental.
proposes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: A godling wounded Silk; A godling attempted to assassinate Caldé Mint; The godlings' leave-the-Whorl message changed after Horn arrived

vizcacha having written:

<In fact, spinning this into more of an idea, what if Horn/Silk's wounds were
made by a godling? I brought this idea up before but didn't have any idea for a motive. It occurs to me that if a godling commanded Silk to preach the end of the exodus, he might well have refused, even to the point of violence. A thin reed to build a theory on, I know...>

This was pretty much the argument I made in my first Piggie post, only I had Pig being the godling involved, and postulated slightly different motives than you for the dastardly deed.

But as far as your core thesis goes--that a godling may have killed Silk--I'm right with you.

I've also speculated with David DiGiacomo in private correspondence that the person who attempted to assassinate Caldé Mint might have been a godling. Mint, as she tells us, put a halt to the leaving of all landers shortly after the first darkday--thus putting her at odds with the godlings' instructions at the time.

Now for the tie-in question. Up until what point did the godlings preach their thou-must-leave-the-whorl dictate? Is it just a coincidence that the first time we hear the new message about staying is shortly after Horn has come to the Whorl via the Silk transfusion? Why now, and not earlier/later?

Robert Borski

2001-02-27 Dennis G. Berdanis #

Argues that the godlings' message changed to 'stay in the Whorl' only after Horn interfaced with a godling, and that Silk then took control of the Passilk mainframe, wrote off Blue and Green, and began preparing to leave. Responds to Robert Borski's question about why the godlings' message shifted right after Horn arrived via the Silk transfusion, offering this as an answer based on the sequence of events.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · puzzle-solving · theories: Silk takes control of the Passilk mainframe after Horn's report; Godling message changed after Horn interfaced with godling

My opinion I suggested in an earlier post. I think the godlings message did not change until after Horn had interfaced with the godling. I think Horn gave a report of the (sorry) state of Blue/Green. I think at this time Silk took control of the Passilk mainframe entity and wrote off Blue/Green and began the mission of preparing to leave the whorl and look for a better location.

Dennis/Endy
