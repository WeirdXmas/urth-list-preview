Severian's sister/ancestry - query.

Urth list · 16 messages · 2010-08-05 to 2010-08-09

2010urthThe Book of the New SunJane DelawneyRyan DunnLee BermanPaul BDavid StockhoffJeff Wilson
The thread: Participants debated whether Severian, once Autarch, would have consulted the Guild's records to learn about his family, and whether he has a sister. Jane Delawney argued he opened the records at once and that the Guild would have recorded twins, one given to the Seekers and one to the Witches, while Jeff Wilson argued the records would reveal little and the sister must still be unfound. Paul B. suggested Severian respects guild secrecy, while Jeff Wilson countered that Severian breaks rules throughout. Lee Berman argued Severian knows Dorcas is his grandmother and hides it. The thread drifted into alien-beings readings and jokes about Gurloes, and ended without agreement.
  • Jane Delawney: Severian opened the Guild records at once and has a twin sister
  • Jeff Wilson: Records would reveal little, and the sister is still unfound and alive
  • Paul B.: Severian does not seek records out of respect for guild secrecy
  • Lee Berman: Severian knows Dorcas is his grandmother and deliberately hides it
  • Ryan Dunn: Severian's acts are not rule-breaking evil, though he deceives authority at times
  • Jeff Wilson (alien reading): Alpheta, Tzadkiel and others are alien beings who caused the flood

Themes: Fathers, mothers and children, Hidden identities and parentage, Unreliable narration

2010-08-05 Paul B replying to Jane Delawney #

Proposes that Severian does not seek his parentage records because the torturers' guild keeps such secrets, like an adoption agency, and he would not want to force Palaemon or Gurloes into torture for the information. Grounds this in Severian's character as someone who respects rules he was raised under even when he could override them, answering Jane Delawney's question about why he never asks.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian avoids asking because of guild secrecy tradition

One possible explanation is that it's guild tradition not to tell (sorta like an adoption agency, see), and Severian didn't want to resort to something like having Palaemon or Gurloes tortured for the information. I find that all the more plausible since Severian seems to be the sort of guy who doesn't contradict rules, especially ones he was raised in, even if he technically can now.

Paul

2010-08-06 Jane Delawney #

Asks why Severian, once Autarch with full authority over the Commonwealth's bureaucracies, never orders access to the Seekers' records to learn his parents, grandparents and sister. Grounds the question in Severian's early speculation in Shadow that the Guild keeps ancestry records, the Guild's recruiting from victims' children, and Severian's reform rather than abolition of the Guild.
questions · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic

Just wondered if any more knowledgeable person on the list had any suggestions about this query, which has bugged me for years.

Severian speculates early in *Shadow* that the elders and masters of his Guild must have information on the ancestry / antecedents of newly recruited apprentices. Indeed this is highly likely given the recruitment methods of the Guild which takes its apprentices solely from amongst the children of their 'clients' (victims). We get only brief insights throughout the four volumes of BOTNS into the bureaucracy of the Commonwealth, and less than that into the records kept by the Seekers of Truth and Penitence; but there *are* records, and we are not given any reason to doubt that early speculation on Sev's part.

So - well, when Severian becomes Autarch, he has, effectively, total command of the Commonweath and its various bureaucracies. He has the Words and he has the authority. After ten very, very successful and popular years in power (he is by that time 'Severian the Great' after all) he has likely won over many early hold-outs who may have disapproved of his succession or of the Autarchy in general (though I doubt he's won over the 'Agiatii' or whatever they are called after Vodalus's unlamented demise ... what wouldn't I give to hear more about this no doubt epic struggle!). So one may assume that most if not all of the Commonwealth's bureaucracies are his to command.

He has 'reformed' but not abolished his old Guild; also of note, since there would have been no need for a wholesale destruction of records during a mere 'reformation'. One would hope indeed that the Autarch's representatives would have been able to prevent such a thing had it been thought of.

So ... why on Urth, with the power and authority invested in him as Autarch, does Severian not require access to the records of the Seekers of Truth and Penitence, and thus learn beyond any doubt just who are his parents, his grandparents, and his sister? The flip answer is of course 'because GW does not want us to know', but I hope for more than that. Sev could have asked, and he would have been obeyed. He doesn't ask.

Why? Any ideas?

jd

2010-08-06 Lee Berman replying to Jane Delawney #

Argues that Severian knows and deliberately hides that Dorcas is his grandmother, and that his major loves (Dorcas, Thecla, Valeria, Jolenta, Cyriaca) are family members. Supports Jane Delawney's reply to Jeff Wilson by reasoning that an Autarch with Severian's curiosity would have searched the records, and that the omission itself signals the secret. It also doubts Wolfe would disguise such a scenario so thoroughly, while admitting later revelations keep convincing him.
supports · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Dorcas is Severian's grandmother; Severian's loves are family members

I generally agree with Jeff Wilson. Severian is least forthcoming with us in regard to his problems with incest. We are given enough information to fairly easily deduce that Dorcas is his grandmother though he never comes out and says so.

Given his initial intense curiousity, it seems almost impossible that, as Autarch, he would not use his power to investigate his own family tree (just as it seems impossible that there was no buggery going on among torturer's apprentices). Such things draw attention to themselves by their conspicuous absence.

How can Severian admit to us that he discovered all the major loves and conquests of his life were family members, Dorcas, Thecla, Valeria, Jolenta and Cyriaca all being prime candidates, I think. Sometimes I doubt whether Wolfe would set up a scenario and then disguise it so impenetrably. Then some other new subtle revelation makes an appearance 30 years after publishing and I once again believe it.

Argues that Severian, as Autarch, should have learned his sister's identity from the Guild records, since he could already guess Dorcas was his grandmother and Ouen his father. Replies to Jeff Wilson, restating her earlier view that Severian opened the records at once, and cites a later book's scene where Merryn calls Severian her brother and the twin setup.
questions · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Dorcas is Severian's grandmother and Ouen his father; Severian has a twin sister

Sorry for monkeying with the subject line again, I want to get my own posts sent back to me and this is the only way I can get Sky's mail server to do this. And apologies *again* if you have heard this a few too many times already.

Dunno. Seems to me that Sev has had sufficient information prior to becoming Autarch to at least be able to make an informed guess that Dorcas was probably his grandmother and Ouen his father. Opening the records would have provided confirmation for sure, but did he really need that to take the actions he did ie. compel Ouen to care for Dorcas (since he knows that he (Severian) would be the last person D. would ever want to see again after her return to Nessus).

Whether this is the case or not though - one is still left to wonder why, given that he simply must have been able to demand the relevant information from the Guild, Severian still does not know (even at the end of UOTNS he does not know - one might think that by that time, both Sev and GW have passed beyond worrying about it, but if Juturna is in the mix that is not the case at all, is it?) who his *sister* is. GW spends altogether too much time and effort setting Severian up as one of twins (or at the very least of a brother-sister pair with appropriate names) for this to be an irrelevant or insignificant question, surely?

I gather that in one of GW's later works (which I have not read) there is a scene set within the Matachin Tower which involves Severian, a 'cacogen', and Merryn (at the time either apprentice or 'journeywoman' Witch) and that during this scene Merryn claims that Severian is her brother. This may be a generic term (Severian refers to 'the Witches, our sisters' in 'Shadow'; here the usage appears to be generic, though for any individual Seeker or apprentice there is the possibility that this term is literally true, given what we are told about how these two guilds obtain recruits) but it's possibly not. Merryn I know has been fingered as Severa by more than one commentator, but so have quite a few other characters (Juturna, the mandragora, Jolenta, and so on and so forth). If Sev as Autarch can be assumed to have confirmed his earlier suspicions and deductions about Dorcas, Ouen et al., then why, apparently, has he not succeeded in discovering further information about his sister?

I still think this is a major lacuna and pretty strange.

2010-08-07 Jeff Wilson replying to Jane Delawney #

Argues that the Guild's records would hold little about Severian's family, so a sister is unlikely to be traceable through official documents. Builds this on the kinds of records the Guild would keep, including the imprisonment order, the disposition of children, and masters' internal logs, responding to Jane Delawney's question why Severian still does not know of a sister.
disputes · close-textual-reading · myth-and-structure · theories: Severian's sister is not in Guild records

Let us think what information that the guild would have retained. We could expect them to have:

* the official documentation commanding Catharine's imprisonment and her torture and execution if any. These would record her name, residence and position, a brief or lengthy physical description doe identification purposes, and the charges against her.
* the official guild records kept for inspection by the authorities, accounting for her disposition and that of any children who arrived with her or she bore.
* the internal accounts or logs kept by the masters for later consultation of their own, their fellow masters, and masters yet to come.

I don't think this would necessarily include any details of any family members who had not fallen into their hands, except by chance inclusion: "...found guilty of the murder of her husband the Autarch's servant Pancras for which the sentence of death is to be carried out...". Of course, an exultant's family would be identifiable from their name, *if* that name were faithfully recorded and there was no complicating factors like disinheritance or abdication.

Of any siblings, the guild records would say that a girl with such-a-color hair and eyes and marks was given to the Witches if the birth came after incarceration, or such-a-color hair and eyes and marks and the name of X if the girl came with her mother to the tower.

Severian's lack of mention of a sister suggests to me that either he came without one, or that any on record are no longer locatable.

2010-08-08 Jeff Wilson replying to Jane Delawney #

Argues that Severian's missing sister must still be alive and unfound, since the clues to her identity are aimed at the reader rather than at Severian. Grounds this in the narrator's brevity about fetuses and the Witches' guild, and in the places Severian would have looked, such as Dorcas, the boatman, Ouen, and the mausoleum.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Severian's sister is still missing

There's a sister somewhere, but that doesn't mean the clues to her identity have to be where Severian can find them, just where the *reader* can find them.

He was probably being brief for the benefit of his listeners at the time. The guild would not perform c-sections on *every* pregnant woman who comes into their hands regardless, that could be extra punishment without orders. But after the woman has passed, the restriction no longer prevents them from opening her body to take the babes.

Nah, there's likely no shortage of aborted fetuses in Nessus, and there seem to be viable fetuses kept at the Witches' Keep, some must go bad now and then. Besides, the mandragora's been locked in the alchemy lab for centuries.

I'm sure there's a sister *somewhere*, but even if we figure it out, that doesn't mean that Severian ever would.

I'm speaking in particular of the final sections regarding Dorcas, the boatman, and Ouen, the natural times for him to mention his sister with regard to his new information.

The Witches are also a service guild of the Autarchy, and he would simply go to their tower and have their records opened. In fact, I suspect he would do so regardless. If the sister were dead, given his fascination with the mausoleum he almost certainly would have had the body moved there from its humble grave, or at least have had some kind of closure worthy of recording. So, the sister must surely still be missing as far as he and the Witched know.

Argues that Severian's twin sister exists and that the Guild's records would have noted the birth of twins, one given to the Seekers and one to the Witches. Responds to Jeff Wilson's point about the Guild's records, suggesting the text's repeated hints about a sister and the mandragora as twin would otherwise be pointless.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian has a twin sister given to the Witches; The mandragora is Severian's twin; Severian's sister became Autarch's untraceable relative

Of course, this kind of record would have been routine for any 'client'.

Re: the second point - Well yes, one might *indeed* expect these details to be recorded, especially if she bore twins and one of them was retained by the Seekers (because a boy) and the other handed over to the Witches (because a girl). As I've already mentioned I think GW provides the reader with so many hints and suggestions re: Sev's name and the possible existence of a sister that I can't think why he would have done this, unless we are supposed to assume that he did in fact have a sister. Otherwise, why bother? Just to be able to turn around and say, 'Ha ha, fooled you?'

Can't see it somehow.
For sure. But if Sev was raised by the Guild from a baby (as his recollection of being nursed by a woman within the Tower strongly suggests he was - though his assumption that this woman was his mother is yet another example of his 'unreliable narrator' status since he has informed us within the first chapter or two of his narrative that when the Seekers have a suitable newborn in their hands, they will engage a wet-nurse; the baby doesn't necessarily get nursed by his own mother) and he did have a twin sister, the delivery - by natural means, or by the Guild's rough form of C-section as the text suggests - of twin babies, one of each sex and one taken or given to be raised by each of the sibling Guilds would surely be a notable event, and I can't see it not being recorded.

It's always possible of course that Catherine was subjected to this rough surgery and that consequently, only one of her twins survived. If so, why keep harping on the 'twins' theme? Unless of course the (IMO) wild suggestion that the mandragora is Sev's twin happens to be correct.

Though people who came into contact with him keep suggesting the possibility, over and over again.

This on the other hand is entirely plausible. Sev as Autarch could have requisitioned the records and discovered that he had a sister, given to the Witches, who was not named at birth (because the Witches would give her a name) and was therefore untraceable. This bit of information would close out all kinds of hints and suggestions within the four volumes; but it's not given.

I still find this odd. Further comments more than welcome, this stuff (alongside plenty more in the books) has puzzled me for years!
