on the setting of short sun

Urth list · 6 messages · 2007-12-17 to 2007-12-20

2007urthStone OxJoe RileyMarc AraminiChris PJoel Sieh
The thread: The thread opened with Marc Aramini restating his theory that the trees of Blue and Green consume vanished people and spit out copies, and that Horn's farewell under the big tree marks the switch to Silk as narrator in Short Sun. Chris agreed that a narration shift there is well supported, but gave no textual evidence. The discussion then turned to Seven American Nights, where Joe Riley asked what shows the journal is authentic, and Joel Sieh argued it is partly true with later sections written by someone else. Joe Riley came to agree that the journal is partly fabricated, and stoneox17 pointed readers to Robert Borski's post on the missing night. The thread ended without resolution, and it drifted from its Short Sun subject to the other book.
  • Marc Aramini: trees consume and copy people, causing time travel; Horn-to-Silk narrator switch at the big tree, and he no longer wants to debate it
  • Joe Riley: asks what evidence shows the Seven American Nights journal is authentic, then agrees it is partly fabricated and the finale is Wolfe's trick
  • Chris: finds a strong case for a narration shift in Short Sun at Aramini's point, with no textual evidence
  • Joel Sieh: the journal is probably partly true, with later sections completed by another hand; asks which day was the hallucination day
  • stoneox17: recommends Robert Borski's post as solving the missing night problem, though disagreeing with much of his reading

Themes: Unreliable narration, Stories within stories, Time loops and causality

2007-12-17 Marc Aramini #

Restates the poster's theory that the trees of Blue and Green consume the vanished people and spit out copies, causing time travel and the strange events of the text, and that Horn's farewell under the big tree marks the switch from Horn to Silk as narrator. Points readers to his linked essay for the full argument, including polyploidy and transubstantiation, while saying he no longer wants to debate it.
proposes · puzzle-solving · myth-and-structure · theories: Trees of Blue and Green create copies of vanished people; Horn's farewell under the tree makes Silk narrator; Urth-Lune is Blue Green

Well, the face of time travel has reared its head
again on the Urth discussion list.

My arguments that Urth-Lune was Blue Green were never
as important to me as the idea that the trees of Blue
and Green were creating the vanished people by
consuming them, spitting out copies, and causing all
the weird stuff in the text to happen (at the end of
On Blue's Waters, when Horn says good-bye under the
big tree, I fervently believe that it is the end of
Horn as narrator and the beginning of Silk as narrator
- facilitated by the tree, whereas Horn goes to ride
the beast with three horns, babbie.)

I believe this link is the entire argument, including
polyploidy, time travel, and transubstantiation, for
those who have not been exposed to it before.

http://www.urth.org/whorlmap/chras-writers/marc-aramini1.htm

I hope those who have not yet read it have a chance
to, but I have little desire to argue about it anymore
with the list.

Marc

Asks what evidence shows that the journal in Seven American Nights is authentic, noting that nothing in the self-writing machine episode or the finale's handwriting exchange confirms the story as factual. Refers to the poster having searched the archives first, so the question is raised as an unanswered one rather than a claim.
questions · close-textual-reading

What evidence do we have that the journal is authentic? Between the self-writing machine and the finale, where in response to the question about handwriting, the old woman says, "Perhaps, perhaps," we see nothing to confirm this story as factual.

2007-12-18 Chris P replying to Marc Aramini #

Agrees that there is a strong case for a significant shift in the narration at the point Marc Aramini suggests in Short Sun, offering several plausible reasons for it. Ends with a quotation from Lin Yutang about small men casting big shadows as the sun sets, without giving textual evidence.
supports

I actually believe there is a good argument to be made for there being an important shift in the narration at exactly the point you suggest. I think there are a number of plausible reasons for it, though.

--
"When small men begin to cast big shadows, it means that the sun is about to set." -- Lin Yutang

2007-12-18 Joel Sieh replying to Joe Riley #

Argues that the journal in Seven American Nights is probably partly true, with later sections completed by someone else, and proposes looking for fabrication through voice changes and plot inconsistencies. Responds to Joe Riley's question about the journal's authenticity, admitting he has no answers yet, and asks which day was hallucination day.
proposes · puzzle-solving · theories: Journal is true at first then completed by another

That's a good question, and I have to admit that I was too lazy after reading it to go back and try to figure it out. Is it safe to assume that at least some of the journal is real? If not, then I think there would be little point to the story, as it would just become an elaborate lie to the main character's family. Maybe we could discover some kind of motivation for the lie, but I don't think it would go much farther than that.

So let's assume that at least part of the story is true. Now, from that, I'm tempted to say that the story is true starting at the beginning, but has been completed by someone else. Of course, it would be easy enough for some fabricator to get a whole new journal and replace any parts he wished, but it would be much easier to just "finish" the story.

this to happen, like when the main character visits the police station. Voice changes or motivation/plot inconsistencies would be useful clues to help determine fabrication.... However, I don't remember seeing these off the top of the head. Maybe the whole drug-infused candy egg portion of the story is an allusion to a fabrication, but I'm not sure.

Speaking of which, any ideas which day was hallucination day?

Anyway, determining if and where there was fabrication should be an interesting exercise in puzzle solving. Sorry that I'm not much help with answers right now. :) I may take this as an opportunity to give the story another look, though.

--Joel
