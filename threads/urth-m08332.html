The thread: The thread asked whether Cassie in An Evil Guest (Margaret) is an elevated or transformed figure and whether she gained shape-changing at Woldercan. Roy C. Lackey argued she was only enhanced, not transformed upward, and that Margaret's werewolf change is downward; Dave Tallman argued Cassie's change is spiritual and Margaret is transformed downward, then proposed that Reis became Wally and appeared as the Volcano God, and later that Reis lied throughout. Tom Foster offered a werewolf-seeing reading and questioned the out-of-character notes. Debate ranged widely into Kingsport, Ian Mersey, and the play-within-the-novel, but the thread mostly drifted into competing speculation without consensus, ending in a garbled late post about Margaret as Cassie's soul.
  • Roy C. Lackey: Cassie was enhanced, not elevated; she is not a goddess and wields no power; Reis and Wally may be different people
  • Dave Tallman: Cassie transformed upward spiritually and Margaret transformed downward; Reis became Wally, who appeared as the Volcano God; Reis lied throughout
  • Tom Foster: the leashed man is the human side of a werewolf that Cassie sees through; notes from Gid and Wally seem out of character
  • David Duffy: Bill becomes a Shadow-like vigilante figure named Wally
  • stoneox17: Margaret may be the post-Woldercon Cassie and a symbolic soul of Cassie

Themes: Stories within stories, Unreliable narration, Doubles, twins and clones

2009-01-16 Roy C. Lackey #

Asks whether Margaret in An Evil Guest is an older Cassie who was elevated after Gideon's help, and what she accomplished at Woldercan and whether she gained shape-changing ability there. Reasons from the text that Cassie was lifted to a heightened, angel-like state and that Gideon says he cannot transform himself, so Cassie probably did not learn shape-changing on Woldercan.
questions · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Margaret is an older Cassie seeking a second chance; Margaret is a shape-changer who gained the ability at Woldercan

Speaking of Cassie as Margaret . . .

After Gideon aided Cassie to bring out her "star" power, it is implied that she was then elevated, transformed "up", to a state higher than normal for humans. The text even suggests that her heightened state was somewhat angelic, though not, I believe, angelic in the usual religious sense. Cassie was certainly no angel.

So if Margaret is really an over-the-hill Cassie who wants to give her younger self a second chance at a happier life, who went to Woldercan seeking something she apparently didn't find, what did she accomplish there? If, as Dave Tallman has suggested, Margaret is now a shape-changer, whether or not an actual werewolf, when and where did she acquire that ability, and was it her choice?

I mean, only humans can transform themselves; Wolders can't do it, and Gid said he (personally) couldn't do it. (99-100) So it doesn't seem likely that Cassie learned how to transform on Woldercan.

-Roy

2009-01-16 Dave Tallman replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Proposes that Cassie began acquiring Margaret's shape-changing ability when the Volcano God danced with her, since he too took Vince's form by choice. Answers Roy C. Lackey's question about when and where Margaret acquired the ability, citing Chase's remark that werewolves want the wild and freedom from human morality and the dance passage at page 286.
answers · close-textual-reading · theories: Volcano God's dance began Cassie's shape-changing ability; Volcano God chose to appear as Vince

There's another place where Cassie could have begun to learn this. Chase said that werewolves "want the wild and a liberation from human morality" (207). When the Volcano God danced with her, "It freed something that had been bound before; after it she danced as he did..." (286). The Volcano God was something of a shape-changer himself; he chose to appear to her as Vince. This may have been the start of her own ability.

2009-01-17 Roy C. Lackey replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that Cassie in An Evil Guest was not elevated toward angelhood but only enhanced, and asks who in the story has actually been transformed upward. Grounds this in Gideon's warning that it is easy for shape-shifters to go down, the single wolf encounter with Scott, and India's theatrical use of the word angel for Wally Rosenquist.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Cassie was enhanced, not transformed into a higher being

That's not right. What Gid did for Cassie to bring out her star quality was to enhance what was already there. That is not the same thing as shape-shifting as defined by Gid. (99-100) Cassie still looked like Cassie, only better and more charming. To shape-shift is to morph into another physical form that is different from the original.

It is true that Cassie is no angel;there are none in the story. The only person in the story to be called an angel is Wally Rosenquist, and that usage by India is a theater term for a financial backer with deep pockets. Still, one has to account for Gid's warning to Cassie about how hard it is for humans to transform up and how easy it is for shape-shifters to transform down. There should be someone in the story who has been transformed up (probably with "expert assistance").

Gid may be talking to Cassie in oblique terms about Margaret-as-werewolf, if he believes that she is one, but even then, he cannot mean Margaret as an example of a human who has transformed up. As Gid explains it, "It is so easy to go down that werewolves have trouble maintaining their human forms at times." (100) That statement suggests that the animal state is the normal state for a werewolf, the state that they will revert to when they lose control. In the only example we see in the book, the large wolf that accosted Scott was all animal, not some hairy-man form like we see in movies that looks neither quite like a man nor a wolf.

Cassie asked about humans who have transformed up, "Do they become angels? Or--or . . ." Whatever she meant by "or", who in the story has been transformed up?

-Roy

2009-01-17 Dave Tallman replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that Cassie in An Evil Guest was transformed upward rather than shape-shifted, so that her change is internal and spiritual rather than physical, and that Margaret is a person transformed downward. Responds to Roy Lackey by citing the warning about slipping down, Sharon's phone test, the voice change, and Chase's use of the word form in the text.
disputes · close-textual-reading · allegory-and-typology · theories: Cassie transformed up and Margaret transformed down; Transformation in An Evil Guest is internal not physical

I have to disagree. Cassie was told "It is easy, terribly easy, for someone who has transformed up to slip back down; and it wouldn't be right for me to die without having warned you" (p. 100). Why would Cassie need a warning about someone else slipping down? She was transformed up, and needs to know that she could lose it. You say that it cannot be transformation if she doesn't look different. I say at least her voice was different. Sharon asked her a test question about her mother's name in order to be sure it wasn't someone else tricking her over the phone (45).

What Chase calls a "higher form" for Cassie even appeared spontaneously at times in her performances before he did anything for her. She clearly didn't grow angel wings or something -- she manifested "an indescribable something that electrified me" (p. 207). He must use the words "form" and "transform" rather loosely and metaphorically.

Could a plainer woman with a more subdued voice be a lower form? I think so. Chase admits the possibility of a "man-man" (p. 100), at least in jest. The main differences are probably internal, manifested as a loss of human qualities. After years as Margaret she might have difficulty getting herself to look as good and be as good as Cassie (even gray Cassie).

In short, I see no reason not to accept Cassie as a person who transformed up, and Margaret as a person who transformed down. There is a parallel with morals and aspirations, which is also rendered in physical terms in the Wizard Knight. Don't they say the same thing about it being easier to go down than up when they talk about the universe levels?

He thought she might be flattered and mollified if he told her he made it especially for her.

Or attack them at all without telling you what I plan" (254). He put a concentration of gold around the city, leading to Navy attack; he chose to consider that a gypsy trick rather than an attack in itself.

I think you're right that Vince was just an actor. But symbolically he was a representative of Reis in the play. Here are some things I deduce about the play:

1) The Volcano God was a dangerous villain in the play -- "I only hope I never meet Vince" (249).

2) "Remember the show? The banquet you made for me? The way you danced with Gil and me?" (285). It seems he was wooing Mariah in a love triangle with Mr. Sharpy. He offered her a banquet. This is a very close match to the Cassie/Gideon/Wally triangle, including dining out. The dance might have been a dream sequence, like the Laurey/Curly/Judd dance in "Oklahoma."

3) Vince had a speech about coconuts (107). This may relate to the banquet, but I suspect it was a double-entendre about Mariah's breasts.

4) Aunt Jane loved the Volcano God: "And how I love his boiling lava..." (138), suggesting she will betray Mariah in the play, and foreshadowing Norma's possible betrayal of Cassie. She has an unhappy role. since she is only supposed to smile once (131).

5) Like the gold bracelet, the play was designed for Cassie's destruction. I suggest there was a scene where she was thrown down a stage volcano; that would be a fine way to arrange an "accidental" death.

6) Cassie on Zelda trying to get her to sign up for the play: "She's sold me down the volcano..." (83).

7) As a musical, the play would normally have a happy ending of some sort. But there could be a twist. One would expect Mariah to end up with the sailor, but Reis named him "Mr. Sharpy" (a sharper, a confidence trickster). He was the parallel to Chase. Since the play was intended to send a message to Chase, Mariah probably ended up with the Volcano God.

The island of Takanga Ha'i seems to have two mountains, one for the palace and one an active volcano. Probably the King's mountain was also a volcano, now inactive. That gaves Reis a strong association with the Volcano God. Perhaps he joined with the Volcano God's spirit after death. His appearance as Vince Palma rather than Wally suggests the being Cassie saw was not simply the ghost of Reis.
