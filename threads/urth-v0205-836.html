2002-04-30 Marc Aramini #

Proposes that the unnamed officer reading the report in The Fifth Head of Cerberus is an Aborigine, based on his reluctance to use tools and his having a woman open a package for him. Rests on a vague memory of the text and a promised re-read, and uses a derogatory term for Aboriginal people.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: The officer reading the report is an Aborigine

Say, I haven't been following the discussion of 5th Head too closely, but doesn't anyone think that the officer reading the repot is an abo? He makes that women who services him open the package for him, and he seems reluctant to use tools (like a knife to open a package, if I remember correctly). That is just a vague impression from reading it a few years ago. I will have the text in my hands again in about two weeks to re-read it. Those abos are everywhere.
Marc Aramini

2002-05-02 Jerry Friedman #

Responds to maa32's suggestion that the officer reading the report in The Fifth Head of Cerberus is an aborigine, by citing the scenes where the officer has a male slave draw his dagger to cut the package ropes and later cuts a page of the notebook himself. Notes that the cut-out pages and the slave's dagger-drawing puzzle the poster.
supports · close-textual-reading

Apparently! The officer does have the male slave (a Gene Wolfe clone, right?) draw the officer's dagger and cut the ropes on the package. On the other hand, later the officer draws his own dagger and cuts a page of V. R. T.'s notebook to compare the cut with the way the first three pages have been removed. The cut-out pages and the business with the slave's drawing the dagger both mystify me.

Jerry Friedman

2002-05-02 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Proposes that the officer in the V.R.T. notebook scene is an Annese, clumsy with tools in a particular way, so cutting a page himself would let him tell whether the earlier pages were cut by a human or an abo. Responds to Jerry Friedman's puzzlement over the cut-out pages and the slave drawing the dagger, offering this explanation.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: The officer is an Annese, clumsy with tools

Assume that the officer is Annese.

Assume that the Annese not only are clumsy with tools (like knives), but are clumsy in a particular way.

Then cutting a page himself, and comparing it to how the pages already cut appear, may be a way for him to determine (to, at least, his own satisfaction)
whether they were cut by a human or by an abo...

--Blattid
