2000-08-17 Dennis G. Berdanis #

Asks whether readers agree with Clute's estimate that On Blue's Waters is set at the same time as The Book of the New Sun, rather than at least a thousand years later as the list had assumed. Reports surprise at Clute's OBW review, which mentions a possible link between Horn, Silk and the inhumi and dates the two books together.
questions · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: On Blue's Waters is set concurrently with The Book of the New Sun; Horn and Silk may be inhumi

I was searching for the IGJ review that other's have relayed some of the details here, and instead found the OBW review that I had not read yet.

The first shock I had was what was relayed in Clute's latest response to the whorl list. That he mentioned the Horn/Silk/inhumi? possibility in that review.

The second shock was reading his opinion that OBW is happening at the same time as TBOTNS? I had inferred from reading the whorl list that most were of the opinion that OBW was at least 1000 years later than TBOTNS? Do any agree with Clute's timeframe estimates?

Thanks,
Dennis/Endy

2000-08-17 Kevin J. Maroney #

Restates the common view that the Whorl left Urth about a thousand years before the New Sun books and spent roughly a thousand years in transit, with only 358 years passing aboard. Responds to Endymion's question about Clute's timeframe by citing Robert Borski's proposal that the end of In Green's Jungles matches the swimming scene in The Shadow of the Torturer.
answers · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Whorl left Urth about 1000 years before Book of the New Sun; End of In Green's Jungles parallels Shadow's swimming scene

I thought that the common wisdom was that the Whorl left Urth about 1000 years before _Book of the New Sun_ (during the time of Typhon) and had spent 1000 years in transit (though only 358 years passed on-board because of time dilation). Someone here--Robert Borski, I think--pegged the scene towards the end of _Green_ as taking place during the swimming scene in chapter 1 of _Shadow of the Torturer_, and I suspect he's right.

*Must we assume that, with a name like that, Endymion is a monster?
