Fuligin in illustration

Urth list · 31 messages · 2008-10-23 to 2008-10-31

2008urthThe Book of the New SunMatthew KeeleyNessun SapràAdam ThorntonPedro PereiraNigel PriceRanjit BhatnagarSon of WitzJeff WilsonDan'l Danehy-OakesKerry BentonDaniel SlatkinJohn Smith
The thread: Son of Witz opened by asking whether the fuligin passage, which says the substance reflects no light, rules out highlights on the torturer's cloak, and asked illustrators for views. Kerry Benton and Son of Witz argued the text never mentions a coloured lining and that the Orb cover's varicoloured lining is wrong, while Adam Thornton joked lightly about cover art. Pedro Pereira and Son of Witz then debated the accuracy of the GURPS sourcebook illustration, and of Hungarian and Pennington covers, mostly over shirtlessness and armour details. Later the talk drifted to whether Severian and future Urth people would be hairy, with Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, John Smith and Jeff Wilson offering evolutionary and textual speculation. The thread ended in drift and scattered image-sharing rather than agreement.
  • Son of Witz: fuligin rules out highlights, but dirt would dull the cloak and future folk may be hairy
  • Kerry Benton: the text never mentions a coloured lining, so the Orb cover is inaccurate
  • Pedro Pereira: the GURPS illustration is the most faithful depiction of Severian despite being less stylish
  • Jeff Wilson: Severian's chest hair is never described, and bred-in smoothness from Exultants explains it
  • John Smith: natural selection would not make ice-age people hairier, though sexual selection could
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: Severian's people should resemble native South Americans

Themes: Robots, androids and artificial life, Beasts and the animal in us, Evolution and the future of humanity

2008-10-23 Son of Witz #

Asks whether the fuligin passage, which erases folds and gatherings, also rules out highlights, arguing that light from the claw would still catch a dimmer sheen on the torturer's cloak. Quotes the passage and Dan Parmenter's 2001 complaint about cover art, and invites readers' views as an illustrator drawing the cloak.
questions · close-textual-reading · theories: Fuligin cloak would still catch dim highlights

Old topic, I know. hopefully it's not unwelcome to respond to some very old posts.

I'm an illustrator, and I've been sketching some scenes from this book over the last month or so. Fuligin is a challenge that I didn't think it would be.

First of all, while the text says:
"the hue fuligin, which is darker than black, admirably erases all folds, bunchings, and gatherings so far as the eye is concerned, showing only a featureless dark."

Does anyone read this as saying that highlights wouldn't show up? It seems to me that it says "folds, bunchings, and gatherings" ? all things that create shadows, become a "featureless dark". But what of highlights? Surely when a light (like the claw's) hits it strongly, the cloak would catch some of the highlights. I picture this as a lot dimmer of a highlight than an ordinary black, but still a highlight.

Anyway, I'm aiming that my illustrations would be considered "legitimate" if they were seen by someone familiar with the story. Does anyone have thoughts on this, especially ones that would argue with what I've said above about highlights?

I was looking up the word 'fuligin' in the dictionary the other day.  I was
interested to see that sitting right in front of the 'fuligin'-like words
(namely, 'fuliginated', meaning 'of a sooty color or appearance'; 'fuligo',
meaning 'soot'; and 'fuliginous', meaning 'blackened with soot'), there were
were several similar sounding words derived from a root with a quite
different meaning.  These were 'fulgurate' ('emit flashes like lightening');
'fulguration' ('the action of lightening'; 'in assaying, a brightening in
the appearance of a molten metal'); 'fulgurous' ('resembling, full of or
charged with lightening').

Like the little girl in the jacal Severian brought back to life, we all know
that the fuligin that is blacker than black, when worn by Severian, the
torturer who is not a torturer, is in reality whiter than white.  Severian
is the shadow of the torturer, which means, in Chesterton's terms, that he
is not the torturer, not death, but something in fact like the opposite of
death; and it is he who brings the white sun as the new sun.  As I recall,
toward the end of Citadel of the Autarch Severian even laughs at himself as
he describes how he exchanged the cloth that is blacker than black for the
white that is whiter than white.

So I wonder if Wolfe intentionally selected the term fuligin in part because
its root word meaning 'soot' is so similar to the root word meaning
'lightening'.   Sort of a play on words.  Something like that would
certainly be within his bags of tricks, uh, I mean, repertoire.

Steve

2008-10-24 Kerry Benton replying to Adam Thornton #

Argues that the text never mentions a red or other coloured lining for Severian's cloak, and that the Orb Shadow & Claw cover's varicolored lining is inaccurate. Responds to Adam Thornton by admitting uncertain memory, recalling that a rereading exposed the cover's error, and noting a Japanese cover apparently based on the same painting.
disputes · close-textual-reading

I don't recall any reference to a red (or any other color) lining in the book, but I admit it's been a while.

The most common book cover illustration seems to be the one that's still on the current Orb "Shadow & Claw" edition, which shows not merely a red, but a beautiful varicolored lining that in fact spills over the top in a frilly sort of collar. It also shows a great deal of shadow and lighting effects on the fuligin and has severian wearing a black (or fuligin) shirt. I've always thought this illustration was gorgeous and would love to have a large sized print of it, but I also have a distinct memory of the moment I realized it wasn't accurate (upon my 2nd or 3rd reading probably).

There's a Japanese cover that I think is fascinating... it appears to be inspired by the painting referenced above, but with a really distinctive style : http://flickr.com/photos/78615114 at N00/2481269054

Anyway, my 1 or 2 cents.

-k

Asks whether Severian's cloak or only his mask bore red embroidery, and notes that a GURPS supplement draws him shirtless, which the poster calls the most faithful illustration. Also disputes the Lexicon Urthus entry calling the matachin a Spanish sword dance, arguing it is a Mexican dance with only indirect Spanish conquest influence.
disputes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion

I may be mistaken, but I seem to remember that there was some red embroidery on the cloack? Or was that on only on the mask?

As an aside, in the GURPS rolepleying supplement Severian is actually depicted without a shirt. It's the most faitful illustration I've seen to date Severian.

As ANOTHER aside, I was reading the entry on MATACHIN on LEXICON URTHUS and the book states that it's based on a Spanish sword dance. This is incorrect. It's a Mexican dance with no conection per si to Spain, although it certainly incorporates influences from the history of the spanish conquerors in Mexico, but still saying that it's a spanish dance leads one to wrong conclusions.

Pedro

And let's not forget sexual selection, another of Darwin's great insights. If women choose men who are relatively hairless, then hairlessness becomes more common. On the other hand, if women prefer hairy guys, we'll all end up looking like gorillas.
