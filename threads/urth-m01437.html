Increate on trial

Urth list · 18 messages · 2005-03-25 to 2005-04-01

2005urthThe Book of the New SunthalassocratAdam StephanidesStone OxAndy RobertsonDavid KirbyChris PUwe SchuerkampDan'l Danehy-OakesRoy C. LackeyDavid DuffyMaru Dubshinki
The thread: Posters debated whether the Increate actively directs events in The Book of the New Sun, mostly through the Hierogrammates. Roy C. Lackey argued they take direct orders and that Urth's destruction was symbolic punishment, while Andy Robertson, Chris and Adam Stephanides replied that the divine stays unseen and the Hierogrammates are servants who can die. thalassocrat and Uwe Schuerkamp pressed the sacrifice reading and the risk of overreading Christian symbols. Later David Kirby disputed that the Sun cycle lies in a past universe, citing the text over Wolfe's remarks. The thread ended without agreement, with several posters keeping their readings.
  • Roy C. Lackey: the Increate directly orders the Hierogrammates and Christian imagery shows divine intervention
  • Andy Robertson: the Increate never appears in the text, only its servants, so divine contact is absent
  • Adam Stephanides: the Hierogrammates are not archangels and the New Sun brings damnation, not salvation
  • thalassocrat: Urth's destruction was a real, purposeful sacrifice requiring Sev's consent
  • Uwe Schuerkamp: warns against reading too much Catholic meaning into Wolfe, including his own past overreading
  • David Kirby: the text outweighs Wolfe's later statements that the Sun cycle is set in a previous universe

Themes: Gods, angels and the Increate, Catholic faith and sacraments, Christ figures and messiahs

2005-03-25 Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that Typhon's Second Empire was established before he reached Urth, and that the Hierogrammates and Hierodules, acting for the Increate, stopped him and shaped the New Sun, so Severian's account would be a farce otherwise. Supports the claim by citing Duko Rigoglio's account in The Fifth Head of Cerberus-era text, the sleepers, and passages in Urth of the New Sun about the Hierogrammates, rebutting Crush's reading that Typhon's empire was thriving.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theology-and-religion · theories: Typhon's empire predates his arrival on Urth; Hierogrammates are archangels serving the Increate; Increate permitted or willed Urth's destruction

You're really reaching now. You know perfectly well that Typhon established his Second Empire before he even came to Urth; that the _Whorl_ was built and stocked _after_ he came to Urth (Marble and Hammerstone were manufactured on Urth, and where do you think those "sleepers" came from? Remember Duko Rigoglio, aka Roger? He recognized his former neighborhood in the ruins of Nessus and said "They forced me to board the _Whorl_ and put me to sleep." IGJ, p.-315), and that Typhon ruled neither Blue nor Green, which were colonized three centuries (ship's time) after his death.

What part of "you are sown over tens of thousands of worlds" do you find ambiguous? As for the Hierodules, see p.-137-38 in URTH. To summarize: In a previous iteration of universes, uppity Man created the Hierogrammates. That universe died and the Hierogrammates were the only work of Man to survive its death. The Hierodules were created by the Hierogrammates to be their servants. My point here is that the Hierogrammates exist _outside_ of time and the boom/bust cycle of universes. If the Hierogrammates can survive the death of a whole universe, then they will certainly survive anything that happens to a single planet.

In the current universe, where Urth's sun is dying, Typhon set out to build a Second Empire to rival the First. The H's weren't going to let him. They stopped him, partly out of spite, as revenge for the wrongs done to their ancestors during the course of their creation by Man, and partly at the behest of the Increate, who had been down that road before and didn't like where it ended. The H's are archangels who have the ear of the Increate, and who do his bidding. If that last statement isn't true, then Severian was a colossal dupe in an epic crime and Wolfe's Urth Cycle is a farce.

Do you think Tzadkiel lied? I don't. Do archangels lie? If he lied about that he may have lied about everything. Do we get to choose which things to believe? Urth wasn't drowned by a metaphor. Two things are certain; the New Sun was created by the efforts of the Hierogrammates, and old Urth was wiped clean by it. If the Increate, who "so loved the world ...", allowed that world to be destroyed, then it must have been by his will; that or he was powerless to stop it and the H's were running the show. Pick one. Either one. If the former then the text means just what it says; if the latter then the Christian veneer of the New Sun/Conciliator falls to pieces.

-Roy

2005-03-26 Roy C. Lackey replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that the Hierogrammates receive direct orders from the Increate, so the Increate actively directs events in the Book of the New Sun. Supports this with the Urth quote about death agonies offered to the Increate, a dictionary definition of "rescript" and Wolfe's Christian imagery across the Sun Cycle.
supports · close-textual-reading · theology-and-religion · theories: The Increate directly commands the Hierogrammates

As with much else in Wolfe, it is largely inferred. The quote about "The death agonies of the world you know will be offered to the Increate" implies that the Hierogrammates have, at the least, the Increate's attention. And Urth, as Man's planet of origin, has a unique place in the eyes of the Increate, from a Christian perspective. We know Wolfe is a Christian and that the Urth Cycle was written with that perspective in mind. Unmistakable Christian imagery pops up throughout the Sun Cycle -- from Ctesiphon's Cross, water into wine, the Temptation, death and resurrection, in NS, to Silk's vision of the Cross during his epiphany in LS. There are probably more, but that's just off the top of my head.

Probably the best quote I can give is the one from pages 137-138 of URTH: "Severian, do you know the meaning of that word you used? Of Hierogrammate?"
I told her that someone had once told me it designated those who recorded the rescripts of the Increate.
"So much is correct."

My old _Webster's New World Dictionary Of The American Language_, College Edition, 1966, defines rescript this way: "1. an order or decree issued by a Roman emperor or by the Pope in answer to some difficulty or point of law presented to him, and having the force of law; hence, 2. any official decree or order. 3. a) a rewriting. b) something rewritten; copy. 4. in _law_, an order, as from a court to its clerk, or from an appellate court to a trial court, giving the disposition of a case."

I gave all of the definitions because I didn't want to be accused of picking the ones that suited my case, or twisting them to fit my fancy. (I could have used my compact edition of the OED, but the type is a hell of a lot smaller than it was twenty years ago, and it says much the same thing, anyway.[g]) Definitions 1, 2 and 4 each involve some sort of communication between a higher and lower power, any of which suffice to support my assertion that the H's receive their orders from a higher authority, and what authority is higher than an archangel but the Increate? But even #3 requires an author, and the above quote specifies that the Increate was the author of the rescripts, the H's merely the recorder. All in all, I don't see any way around it; the Increate was calling the shots.

-Roy

2005-03-26 Chris P replying to Andy Robertson #

Argues that the divine contact in boTSS is at best gnostic, since humanity once served the Increate and will return the favor. Responds to Andy Robertson's claim that boTNS shows no divine contact, citing Silkhorn's turning away from the presence behind him, and casting Wolfe as mediator.
disputes · theology-and-religion · author-and-biography · theories: Divine contact in boTSS is gnostic; Wolfe as mediating creator between God and characters

There is an implication that this role is only perceived to be with the Hierogrammates/Hierodules because of the particular stage of the cycle that humanity was on at the time. That is, at some other time humanity acted as the servants as the Increate to uplift the H's; they are returning the favor, and in turn presumably humanity will (in its improved state) act to improve the H's even further.

I think that this is at best a gnostic sort of divine contact. The scene in which Silkhorn feels the presence behind them... if he had turned around they would have seen Wolfe's face.

Of course he did not, because that is not what we do. It is not for the created to look on the face of the creator. But Wolfe in a way I think has to acknowledge his role as a mediating creator between God and the characters in Wolfe's novels.

2005-03-28 Adam Stephanides replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that the Sun cycle's universe cannot easily hold a Christian Incarnation on Urth, since it is either our universe (already past the Incarnation) or a previous one, and questions whether the Hierogrammates receive orders from the Increate. Grounds this in the quoted URTH passage, which may only mean they keep sacred writings, and notes the Hierogrammates were made by earlier men and can die, so they are not archangels.
disputes · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: Hierogrammates are keepers of sacred writings; Hierogrammates are not archangels; Sun cycle universe is a previous universe

True; but, if the universe of the Sun cycle is our universe, then the Incarnation has already taken place; whereas if it's a previous universe, as Wolfe once said in an interview, then it won't take place on Sev's Urth at all. Either way, there seems no particular reason for the Increate to concern himself with the Urth of Sev's day.

This is an important quote, but it doesn't seem to me to be as straightforward as you imply. It could just as easily mean that the Hierogrammates are keepers of sacred writings, rather than that they receive a continuous stream of orders from the Increate. If the latter were the case, one would expect to see the Hierogrammates described as carrying out the orders, rather than just recording them.

I'm also dubious about your identification of the Hierogrammates with archangels. For one thing, they were created by the men of the previous universe. For another, they can die, or at least their children can (Tzadkiel's son).

--Adam

2005-03-28 Roy C. Lackey replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that the destruction of Urth was a symbolic punishment, and that if the Sun cycle occurs in our universe the Incarnation makes Urth sacred ground for the Increate's concern. Grounds this in Christian doctrine on the unique Incarnation, Sev's Yesod conversation with Apheta, Genesis, and Lexicon Urthus on the Hierogrammates as archangels.
disputes · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: Urth is Earth, the site of the Incarnation; Destruction of Urth was symbolic punishment; Hierogrammates are angelic beings

As I've said earlier, the destruction of Urth was largely a symbolic punishment; symbolic because Urth was man's ancestral birthplace, and mankind was dispersed too far and wide in the galaxy (galaxies?) for the Hierogrammates to overcome entropy (135) and hit mankind all at once. If the Incarnation took place in the Sun cycle universe, and that universe was also ours, then the site of that Incarnation seems to me quite sufficient reason for the Increate to concern himself with Urth. All the Christian echoes I noted before indicate that Urth was that site, otherwise how did they get there from a previously collapsed universe?

Also, if Urth is Earth, as I think it _must_ me, then the Increate walked the planet with various Old Testament luminaries -- to say nothing of the life and death of Jesus. There can be no more hallowed ground in any universe.

If the Sun cycle _did not_ take place in _our_ universe, then the Incarnation presents a religious problem. It is central to the Christian faith that the Incarnation was a _unique_ historical event. If that is so, and Jesus didn't suffer on Urth, then the people of Urth were doubly damned. Not only were they paying a price for the 'sins' of the Hieros of a prior universe -- something they had no control over, but they had no chance for 'salvation', for, as Christians maintain, only by way of Jesus might they be redeemed.
