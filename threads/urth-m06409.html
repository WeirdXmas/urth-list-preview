Themes: Catholic faith and sacraments, Unreliable narration

2008-05-06 Joshua A. Solomon #

Questions whether Chris's memoir in the Wolfe novel is a sincere confession or a rationalisation, arguing his actions (pursuing Burt's treasure instead of seeking Novia or revenge) contradict a need for forgiveness. Grounds this in the narrator's own claims about confession and his ancestral mobster background, and asks whether Wolfe means him to escape a light penance as he did at Coruña.
questions · close-textual-reading · theology-and-religion · theories: Chris's confession is a rationalisation

Neither Fr. Houdek nor Fr. Phil believed in confession, but Chris says it's good for the soul. Ostensibly, that is why he compiles these memoirs, but the actions they describe are not consistent with a character driven by the need of forgiveness. Chris says he needs Novia and revenge, but he went to find Burt's treasure instead. Are Chris's actions any less depraved than his ancestral mobsters? Is Chris's confession just one big rationalisation, or does Wolfe think Chris should get away with a light penance, as he did in Coru?a? Clearly Wolfe watched the Sopranos, right?
