Orwellian geography

Urth list · 3 messages · 2002-07-29 to 2002-07-31

2002urthAdam StephanidesMichael Andre-Driussi
The thread: Michael Andre-Driussi proposed that Orwell's Nineteen Eighty-Four places its command centre in Australasia, with North America a battered province, grounding this in the atomic devastation, the name Oceania, and Big Brother being Australian. Adam Stephanides disputed this, citing Part Two, Chapter IX where the United States absorbs the British Empire and noting the text puts Oceania across the Atlantic. Andre-Driussi replied by citing the chronology around that passage and population figures for the Americas and England, and the exchange ended there with his reading unrevised and the textual objection unanswered.
  • Michael Andre-Driussi: Oceania is Australasia, with the command centre in Australasia and North America a battered province
  • Adam Stephanides: the text places Oceania across the Atlantic, with the US absorbing Britain, so the Australasia reading fails

Themes: Power, rulers and politics, Colonization and the native

2002-07-29 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Argues that Orwell's Nineteen Eighty-Four has its command centre in Australasia rather than America, with North America left a battered province. Grounds this on the atomic devastation of North America, the name Oceania as a geographical term, and a suggestion that Big Brother is Australian; it responds to Josh Geller's comparison of Oceania with Wolfe's Urth superstates.
proposes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion · theories: Oceania is Australasia in Nineteen Eighty-Four; Big Brother is an Australian

In general, yes.

But in details, hmmm.

I have this theory that most published pieces on NINETEEN EIGHTY-FOUR (and many readers) see its geopolitical set up as being "World War Two carried on for decades" and basically the USA is the location of the command/control structure, or "America is on top, just like during the Cold War era." (Not that Josh Geller has said this, mind you -- just that this is one of those things that I've been mulling over for years now.)

While there are threads of textual truth to this viewpoint, still it is clear to me that: North America is scarred by the atomic war to a degree greater than Europe was scarred by WWII; the name "Oceania" is not a poetic one (for a sea power superstate) is a geographical one (like the names of the other two superstates) that in this case describes the area of Australia, New Zeland, and islands around there -- Australasia -- and this is the location of the command/control structure.

The former USA is a battered, poisoned province, less well off than Airstrip One.

And Big Brother is an Aussie.

=mantis=

2002-07-31 Adam Stephanides #

Disputes Michael Andre-Driussi's claim that Orwell's Oceania is a geographical name for Australasia, citing the passage in Part Two, Chapter IX of Nineteen Eighty-Four where the United States absorbs the British Empire. Asks him for his reasons while pointing out that the text itself puts Oceania across the Atlantic.
disputes · close-textual-reading

I don't think so; "the book" says that "With the absorption of Europe by Russia and _the British Empire by the United States_ [my emphasis], two of the three existing powers, Eurasia and Oceania, were already effectively in being." (Part Two, Chapter IX; p. 153 of the Signet Classic paperback). But I'd be interested to hear your reasons.

--Adam

2002-07-31 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Argues that Orwell's 1984 places the atomic war of the 1950s between the superstates and that Airstrip One's position as third most populous province of Oceania makes the geography hard to reconcile. Replies to Adam Stephanides's quotation by citing the chronology of the sentences before and after the passage and population figures for North America, South America and England.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading

But Adam, the passage you quote refers quite specifically to 1949! The sentence before says before mid-century; the sentence after says that Eastasia only emerged after another decade of fighting (i.e., 1959); the next paragraph says that these three superstates have been fighting for 25 years (1984-1959 = 25 years).

The atomic war was in the 1950s and left scars that had still not been cleaned up in 1984. Presumably this war was between the two superstates (i.e., exactly like the Cold War situation of US/USSR).

Again, my main argument is one of the geographical terms: Eurasia, Eastasia, and Oceania.

Another point, tangential and interpretive: Airstrip One, bedraggled, rationed, WWII-era Airstrip One, is nevertheless the third most populous of the provinces of Oceania (p. 7). (If we use 1950 population numbers for England/Wales, that is 44 million.) How would you interpret that, given that the other provinces include North America, South America, Western Africa and Southern Africa (but nothing in geographical Oceania, since that, I argue, is the core area)? Personally I think it tricky to envision entire continents as "provinces," but that might be an easy solution: North America as most populous (1950 around 200 million), South America as second most populous (1950 around 135 million), and then Airstrip One. But if the provinces are smaller, and realistically closer to national boundaries (or fractions of former nations), I think it gets more difficult unless, say, full scale atomic war has run its truly awful course and caused serious population decreases in the bombed areas.
