The thread: The thread began with Roy C. Lackey disputing whether Merryn and Severian's failure to recognize each other in the Stone Town at the end of The Claw of the Conciliator is a real discrepancy, and whether a week passes between the Narrator's visits. Adam Thornton and thalassocrat argued that Silk and Horn's Whorl journey could be relativistic, making it a journey into the future, while Jeff Wilson disputed the physics and suggested Wolfe does not treat relativity as meaningful. Roy Lackey and Dan'l Danehy-Oakes kept arguing that astral travel does not necessarily involve time travel, while Dan'l and aramini1 countered with textual support from the storytelling context and the tyrant's recognition of Urth. The thread ended in impasse with the relativity debate and the astral-time question unresolved, and the Triskele and Merryn points only briefly settled.
  • Roy C. Lackey: Merryn and Severian's mismatch is a real discrepancy, and astral travel does not involve time travel
  • Adam Thornton: Silk and Horn's Whorl journey could be relativistic and so a trip into the future, linked to free will
  • Jeff Wilson: Relativity works both ways and Wolfe does not treat it as physically meaningful
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: Astral travel into past stories is time travel, supported by the storytelling context
  • thalassocrat: A tiny constant acceleration could compress the Whorl voyage, and Triskele's stay may be altered by Silk's visits
  • aramini1: Silk can become unmoored in time when focusing on a location, citing the snake tooth story

Themes: Time loops and causality, The dying sun and the cosmos, Deep time and ruins

2006-04-05 Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that Merryn and Severian's failure to recognize each other in the Stone Town at the end of The Claw of the Conciliator is a real discrepancy with Long Sun, and points out that Severian held Triskele only briefly. Responds to Andrew's observation by reconstructing the time frame from the text, noting that Merryn would surely remember her experience in the torturers' tower and the apprentice who put her there.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Merryn would remember Severian despite the time gap

I think what Andrew is getting at is the rather glaring lack of apparent recognition between Merryn and Severian at the end of CLAW. From Sev's personal perspective, the meeting in the stone town took place less than two years after the time he first found Triskele. (I can elaborate on the time frame with citations from the text, if necessary.) Presumably the interval was the same for Merryn. During the time he had Triskele was when Silkhorn & Co. made their astral visit to him. Seeing someone walk through a steel wall should have impressed Merryn, and it did. I don't think she would be likely to ever forget her experience in the torturers' tower, or the young apprentice who caused her to be there.

For the record, Sev had Triskele for only a week. (SHADOW, chapter IV, p-31, Timescape hb.)

-Roy

2006-04-05 Adam Thornton replying to thalassocrat #

Argues that Silk and Horn's journey in the Whorl could be relativistic, so that a thousand years pass outside while only about three hundred pass for the travellers, making their trip a journey into the future. Responds to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's doubt that the books suggest time travel, grounding the claim in how fast the Whorl can accelerate and in relativistic compression.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · genre-and-craft · theories: Silk and Horn travel into the future via relativity

On Apr 5, 2006, at 10:54 PM, <[email]>

I'm deeply suspicious that the accelerations allowable in the Whorl (without the lakes sloshing all over everywhere, etc) would give you sufficient relativistic compression to make a thousand-year journey only last 300. Michael Andre-Driussi and I have batted this one around a little, and the question comes down to, "how fast can the Whorl accelerate."

So, uh, SilkHorn would seem to be going into the *future*. Not merely outside the light cone, but into the future even if there IS a Universal Time (me, I don't see what's wrong with regarding the comoving frame as Really At Rest and thereby declaring time, relative to the comoving frame, to be Real Time, and applying relativistic shrinkage to everything else).

Adam

2006-04-05 Jeff Wilson replying to Adam Thornton #

Argues that relativistic time dilation works in both directions, so the frame that accelerated more has aged less when brought back to rest, and suggests Wolfe does not treat relativity as physically meaningful in the New Sun, Long Sun and Short Sun books. Responds to Adam Thornton's quoted claim that Silk and Horn would travel into the future, by disputing the physics of that claim rather than the plot.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Wolfe does not believe in relativity

Shrinkage works both ways, but when frames are brought back to relative rest, the one that has experienced greater acceleration has experienced less duration. However, I don't think GW really believes in relativity, or at least it doesn't have physical meaning in the New Sun books, and by extension, the Long and Short Suns.

2006-04-06 Adam Thornton replying to thalassocrat #

Argues that the Whorl's gravity would need only a thousandth of a g rather than the one-percent grade suggested, since nothing in the books shows buildings built on slopes to offset acceleration. Responds to thalassocrat's point about constant boost by noting the Whorl's propulsion is unknown, then links Wolfe's view of relativity and static spacetime to free will.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theology-and-religion · theories: Whorl gravity is about a thousandth of a g; Wolfe's spacetime is static, not dynamic

A 1% grade is certainly enough to ensure you don't, say, put a ball down on a shelf. And there's no indication in the Whorl that you have to build things on slopes to counteract the effects of acceleration. I was figuring a thousandth of a G would be more reasonable. But on the other hand....we really have no idea how the Whorl is powered, do we?

Of course one could say that of course Wolfe doesn't believe in relativity inasmuch as spacetime is a static rather than a dynamic concept, which does not appear to allow for free will. Put another way, free will is a refutation of the notion that time is essentially spacelike.

Adam

2006-04-06 Marc Aramini replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that Silk can become unmoored in time when he focuses on a location, citing the Inclito's mother's story of the man who dies from the snake tooth, in which Silk and Oreb appear. Responds to Roy C. Lackey's doubt about whether time passes for Severian during Silk's visits, offering the tyrant's recognition of Urth as counter-evidence.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: Silk can travel in time by focusing on location; Silk appears in the snake tooth story from the past

<[email]>

You must know I'm a huge proponent of the idea that Silk can become unmoored in time, if he focuses on location. The biggest argument against time travel is that that tyrant guy who was a sleeper on the whorl obviously says that a lot of time has passed on Urth and he no longer recognizes his city.

The best argument for time travel is more easily dismissed: when Inclito's mother tells of the man who dies from the snake tooth in her youth, somehow Silkhorn and Oreb (or a darned good facsimile of them) show up in the story, something that definitely happened in the past. Maybe Silk heard the story, and that night when he fell asleep he went off to live it. Or maybe, as those against time travel would say, she just told him into the story to screw with him. Either way, it's weird.

Aramini

2006-04-06 Jeff Wilson replying to Adam Thornton #

Explains that Coriolis effects on the Whorl would not disrupt daily life much but would be visible in flying objects, since towns can be seen overhead. Gives the example of a ball thrown against the rotation traveling farther than one thrown with it, answering Adam Thornton's question about how big the Whorl is.
answers · close-textual-reading

It should into present a problem with day-to-day living, but given the Whorl is of a size that towns are visible overhead, any activity involving flying objects will be visibly affected. For instance, a ball thrown or hit against the rotation of the Whorl will be seen to go farther than one hit traveling with the rotation.

2006-04-06 Adam Thornton replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that whether Severian lies within Blue's light cone matters, since astral travel's instantaneity would either exclude relativity from Wolfe's universe or force relativistic effects to apply. Responds to Andrew and Roy Lackey, who had called the relativity talk irrelevant to the astral travel Dan'l mentioned, and reasons from Silkhorn's long trip to Urth.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Astral travel is instantaneous in the Long Sun universe

But it isn't: knowing whether Severian is in Blue's past light cone, its future light cone, or outside its light cone entirely *is* a relevant question. It only becomes irrelevant if we take as a given that relativity doesn't exist in the New/Long/Short Sun universe, and even then we're left with the question of, if there is a Universal Now and astral travel is instantaneous, then is Severian in the same Now that Silkhorn is?

Well, if it is instantaneous--and it must be, or Silkhorn would be gone a very long time on his trip to Urth--then either there's no relativity in Wolfe's universe, or it *does*.

Adam
