2011-11-18 Lee Berman #

Argues that the Father-Son-Father pattern runs through the Sun series, citing Severian's staff, Typhon-Zeus sinew myths, and a dark echo of Terminus Est in the Short Sun sewer scene. Responds to Dan'l Dannehy-Oakes, David Stockhoff and Nick Lee by linking the mercury naming of the sword, Jovinian's Roman name, and Wolfe's family life to the Urth cycle.
proposes · myth-and-structure · source-and-allusion · theories: Severian's grandfather connects to the Father-Son-Father pattern; Terminus Est created by a non-human like Inire

Yes, I meant to mention that but forgot. I don't think it is so simple though. Who is Severian's grandfather? We don't have to consider his connections to Father Inire but we can't ignore that he also carries a staff.

Heh, damn straight! But it isn't some obscure, backwater myth. It is an essential part of the Typhon myth. Any Typhon+Zeus search will bring up multiple sinew-mentioning sites. The Father (Inire)-Son-Father theme stretches across the Sun Series in my opinion (and for more than mythological/religious reasons, as noted below).

I think that continues through the whole Sun Series, but especially in Short Sun. If you are looking, it isn't so hard to spot Gene, his wife, at least some of their children and some of the family problems they have experienced.

Argh! it is the alchemical name! I didn't know that, thanks. And let us not forget that the mercury-bearing Terminus Est was created by "Jovinian", named for Mercury's father. (Such a Roman name implies the sword was fashioned by a non-human like Inire, Cumaean, Barbatus, etc.) How long will Wolfe keep surprising me?

So, Terminus Est makes an appearance in Long Sun. Cool. I already think a dark version of it appears in Short Sun, in the sewer scene. Horn even carries a light along with his sword as does Severian (and Frodo).
