For Jerry- a response

Urth list · 9 messages · 2002-02-26 to 2002-03-01

2002urthThe Book of the New SunThe Book of the Long SunJerry FriedmanAndy RobertsonBen Hutchison [Intamission]Marc AraminiJeff WilsonDan'l Danehy-OakesMichael Straight
The thread: Participants debated whether the red star in the Long Sun and Short Sun books is a distance in time or space, and how the Whorl's voyage could show the old sun. maa32 argued the distance is temporal, tied to Silk's time travel and dreams; Michael Straight rejected that, holding that Urth lies in a distant solar system and that Silk was not wrong. Jeff Wilson proposed curved space or gravity manipulation to explain the visible sun and Lune's atmosphere, and Jerry Friedman countered with a pre-existing warp and a joke that light-bending could justify any reading. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes disputed Marc's idea that Blue and Green are older Ushas and Lune, finding the timelines and details too neat to discard. The thread ended without agreement, with several readings left standing.
  • maa32: distance to the red star is temporal, tied to Silk's time travel and dreams
  • Michael Straight: Urth lies in a distant solar system; Silk was not mistaken about time travel
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: Blue and Green as older Ushas and Lune makes the reading contrived
  • Jeff Wilson: curved space or gravity manipulation could explain the visible sun and Lune's atmosphere
  • Jerry Friedman: a pre-existing warp, not curved space; light-bending could justify any reading
  • Jeff Wilson: Hiero-people and Father Inire show powers beyond Terran technology

Themes: Time loops and causality, The dying sun and the cosmos, Deep time and ruins

2002-02-26 Marc Aramini #

Argues that the distance to the red star in the Long Sun books is a distance in time rather than space, linking this to Silk's temporal travel and to Severian's gift of perceiving ages at once. Grounds the claim in the repeated stress on ancient, old imagery, the present-tense description of Silk's gift, and Silk's own remark that travel back in time happens through dreams.
proposes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: The distance to the red star is temporal, not spatial; Silk travels through time via dreams

Jerry, The quote you bring up keeps emphasizing how old and ancient the world around the red star is. The distance is one of time. The present tense refers to the transtemporal nature of Silk's gift (and Severian's - a thousand ages in his sight are like an evening gone). God is outside of time. The distance for him is no more real than one of space. The ancient and old images imply that it is from a long, long time ago. The distance is real because the universe has moved so much since then. It was a long way away in space and time, but Silk can travel there because he moves through time, like the song of the mother is a song of time, as he claims that the only way to go back in time is through dreams, as he claims that his travels are dreams, etc. That's the best I can do.
Marc Aramini

2002-02-27 Michael Straight #

Argues that the red sun seen in Blue's sky cannot be Urth's sun, because the universe has not moved faster than light, and that Silk/Horn is right that Urth lies in a distant solar system. Replies to maa32's claim that the universe's movement makes distance real, and rejects the alternative that Silk/Horn was mistaken and travelled in time.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Blue is Urth in the distant past; Urth lies in a distant solar system

The universe has moved, but it's not moving faster than light, which it would have to in order to be able to see the old red sun in the sky of Blue.

I think the only way you can reconcile this detail with the idea that Blue is Urth is to say that Silk/Horn was mistaken when he tried to point out the Red Sun of Urth, that he simply didn't know he was going back in time rather than to a different solar system. (And note that at the time Silk/Horn points out the Red Sun in the sky, he's saying all sorts of odd things to try to get Juganu to take him to Urth -- he goes on for several paragraphs about all the women who are waiting, of how grateful the women prisoners of the Matchin Tower would be to someone coming to rescue them, etc.)

However, I think the better explanation is that Silk/Horn is right, and that the Urth solar system is far away and not the same system as Blue/Green.

-Rostrum

2002-02-27 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Disputes Marc's theory that Blue and Green in The Book of the Short Sun are an older Ushas and Lune, arguing the timelines of the Whorl's voyage, the gravity on Green, the visible red sun on Blue and doubled body parts on Blue are too neat or inconsistent to drop. Responds to Marc by granting the appeal of his idea, questioning whether the inhumi could pass as Neighbors, and concluding that forcing Blue onto Urth and Green onto Lune makes the reading feel contrived.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: Blue is an older Ushas and Green an older Lune

Chiming in -

Marc, while I find a lot of attraction in your theory that Blue/Green is a much older Ushas/Lune (particularly the implicit identity of the Mother and Scylla-on-Urth), it just isn't plausible for a largish variety of reasons.

Probably the most significant of these, for me, is that if we take the more-or-less canonical set of assumptions about the voyage of the _Whorl_ (discussed in some detail in the archives by Mantis and others), the timelines are just too _neat_ to drop: but there are also the questions of Greenish gravity (despite the tower-climbing scene, I believe that lunar-type gravity would warrant some pretty direct observation by a character coming from urth-type gravity), of the apparent visibility of Urth's red sun in the Bluvian sky, of the doubled body parts of Bluvian life forms*, etc., etc.

* but not Greenish life forms? I don't recall enough description of other Greenish beasties, but it seems pretty clear that the inhumi, at least, are four-limbed. Though ... h'mmm. Is it at any point stated clearly that the inhumi were equally successful at masquerading as Neighbors?

Anyway, Marc, I think most of your other hypotheses about tBotSS work just as well (or as poorly; I'm still sceptical-but- interested, with the observation that there aren't very many overall models of this Book on display as yet) without trying to force identity of Blue with Urth, Green with Lune. And, in the end, it _does_ feel forced: which is the problem. In general, when you have a "good" model of one of Wolfe's texts, it doesn't feel forced.

--Blattid

2002-02-27 Jeff Wilson #

Proposes that the Whorl may have crossed curved space so the old sun stays visible behind it, and that gravity manipulation could explain why Lune holds an atmosphere. Responds to Michael Straight's objection, quoted from maa32's remark, that the old red sun cannot be seen without faster-than-light travel in The Book of the New Sun.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theology-and-religion · theories: the Whorl travelled through curved space; Lune's gravity was increased in the age of wonders; the Whorl's gravity was set lower than Urth's

If the Whorl traveled through a region of space that curved back upon itself, then it's reasonable that the old Sun would be visible back along the way they came. There's no particular reason for this to be impossible based on the speed of light, as the BOTNS glosses over many such concerns to start with.

It's been previously discussed that Lune hasn't sufficient gravity to retain an atmosphere; given that gravity manipulation is featured discretely in Severian's time, it's possible that Lune's gravity was increased during the age of wonders to keep the air held down. There is also the possibility that the Whorl's gravity was intentionally set lower than Urth's.

2002-02-28 Jerry Friedman #

Argues against Jeff Wilson's idea that the Whorl passed a curved space region where the old Sun would be visible, suggesting instead a pre-existing warp the ship crossed unknowingly. Grounds this in the lack of such physics elsewhere in the books and jokes that light-bending warps could justify any reading, including Green as the other side of Blue.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Whorl passed a pre-existing space warp

I suspect Jeff is thinking of a space warp that was already there, either naturally or created by someone like the Hierodules, which the _Whorl_ passed through without anyone's knowledge (at least in the Cargo). This strikes me as a lot to conclude on very slim evidence, since the physics is very hard to swallow and I don't remember anything like it anywhere else in the books.

With light-bending space warps and gravity control, you could make a case for anything you want: that Green is the other side of Blue, that the Bluvian fauna's doubled members (stop me now!) are an effect of double refraction by space warps in the atmosphere, that the whole thing was really written by Charles Kinbote.
...

Jerry Friedman

2002-02-28 Jeff Wilson #

Argues that the Sun books' moon-terraforming and gravity questions need not be limited to Terran technology, since the Hiero-people and Father Inire's gardens already show far greater powers. Grounds this in the books' internal wonders and in Wolfe's SF reading, contrasting the Sun books with Heinlein's morally indifferent universe in Orphans.
disputes · genre-and-craft · close-textual-reading · theories: Hiero-people's powers exceed Terran technology

No. During the Apollo program, the measured amount of gas surrounding the moon remained fairly constant, despite each landing adding an amount of gaseous exhaust equal to the original natural contents.

That would require "adding" the mass of Mars, at least. Even Mars won't keep an atmosphere over large geological eras, but the time from Apu Punchau to Ushas is maybe 100 millenia.

I don't see why it is necessary to limit things to Terran technology. The Hiero-people already meddle heavily in Briatic affairs, and stepping up the moon's gravity seems like small potatoes next to Father Inire's time and dimension defying gardens.

Father Inire's gardens
the various magic mirrors
The Ship
The Atrium of Time
Master Ash's house
Hildegrin's wrestling match
Severian discovering his own ancient tomb
the pool in Yesod
Severian whispering in his own ear

please stop! :)

I'm trying to keep to extrapolations from what's in the books, and what science would have been available to Wolfe as reader of SF at the time he turned in _Shadow_.

Heinlein's "Orphans" is set in a morally indifferent universe and its wonders appeal to the authority of science to justify suspension of disbelief. The Sun books are set in a universe the under the continuous supervision of a moral God, and science is merely a justification for bad guys to have evil wonders to compete with the good guys' righteous resources.

Now I won't argue that what Wolfe chooses to call SF is myth making, and in fact I am judging it on its internal standards, since it incorporates astronaut mythology of which _Planet Of The Apes_ is a part.

--
Jeff Wilson

protects the atmosphere from the erosion caused by solar wind by deflecting
incoming solar particles. This is a greater determinant of the presence of
atmosphere than a planet's mass. I suspect the moon could retain an
atmosphere too if a magnetic envelope could somehow be induced around it.

Gradual losses from the moon's future atmosphere might be replaced by gas
extraction from moonrock; rich in oxides, though Im unsure if it contains
enough carbon or nitrogen.
