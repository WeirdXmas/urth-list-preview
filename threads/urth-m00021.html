2004-12-02 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Shares quotations from a Guardian interview with China Miéville about his love of monsters, including beastmen, underwater creatures and pig monsters, and his view that SF, fantasy and horror readers never outgrow enjoying the strange. Offered as possibly relevant to the ongoing discussion of Wolfe's work, with no argument drawn from the quotes beyond that suggestion.
informs

China M. has an article in the Guardian (http://tinyurl.com/6s587) from which I pull the following quotes as possibly interesting and/or relevant to the recent-ongoing discussion of his work:

*****BEGIN QUOTED TEXT*****
I'm in this business for the monsters. My single favourite monsters are the beastmen in The Island of Doctor Moreau. I love the octopoid creatures and the giant swine spirit in William Hope Hodgson. I have a lot of time for pig monsters. I've always liked being terrified of monsters from underwater coming up, like the Creature from the Black Lagoon. There's a picture of Beatrix Potter's Jeremy Fisher with the trout about to bite his foot and he hasn't seen it yet. Completely terrifying.

...

Loads of children read books about dinosaurs, underwater monsters, dragons, witches, aliens, and robots. Essentially, the people who read SF, fantasy and horror haven't grown out of enjoying the strange and weird.

...

The weirdest thing I ever saw was in Hyde Park. There was a crowd, I joined them, and for the next 10 minutes we stood aghast and fascinated watching a pelican eating a pigeon. *****END QUOTED TEXT*****

--Dan'l

2004-12-02 stilskin replying to Lisa Schaffer-Doggett #

Argues that China Miéville is worth reading and not a hack, recommending The Scar as his purest work and Perdido Street Station as a fair introduction while advising against Iron Council. Responds to Lisa Schaffer-Doggett's remark about wanting to try Miéville after Dan'l Danehy-Oakes posted quotes from a Guardian article; it is a social recommendation rather than a close reading of Wolfe.
supports

Mieville is worth reading. He is no hack, in my
opinion, although he has clearly been influenced by
hacks -- as has Gene Wolfe. I recommend The Scar as
the "purest" Mieville -- that's the book I feel has
come closest to showcasing his strengths. Perdido
Street Station will also give a fair appreciation of
his talents. I would avoid Iron Council, his latest.
He's not the tidiest of writers under the best of
circumstances, but IC is just plain sloppy.

Paul
