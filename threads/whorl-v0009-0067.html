Horn the Narrator

Whorl list · 3 messages · 1998-12-14 to 1998-12-15

1998whorlThe Book of the Long SunTerence NickoletteMichael Straight
The thread: Terence Nickolette asked whether readers missed clues that Horn, not Silk, narrates The Book of the Long Sun, and doubted Wolfe would switch narrators just to launch a new series, a point raised after Kevin Maroney said he was surprised by the reveal. The post was repeated nearly verbatim, so the same question appears twice. Michael Straight answered that the opening of Nightside the Long Sun already explains the handoff: Silk 'talked about it afterward', so Horn knows Silk's enlightenment only through Silk's own retelling. The thread is short and ends with that single answer; no rebuttal appears.
  • Terence Nickolette: doubts Wolfe switched narrators merely to launch a new series; asks whether clues were missed
  • Michael Straight: the first paragraph of Nightside the Long Sun shows Horn learned of Silk's epiphany from Silk's later account

Themes: Unreliable narration, Stories within stories, Memory and forgetting

1998-12-14 Terence Nickolette #

Asks whether readers missed clues that the narrator of the Long Sun series would be Horn rather than Silk, questioning how the story moves from Silk's epiphany to Horn narrating. Responds to Kevin Maroney's remark about being surprised to find Horn was the narrator, and says the poster doubts Wolfe would have switched narrators merely to launch a new series.
questions · close-textual-reading

Kevin Maroney said of the surprise of finding Horn the narrator:

I had the same reaction. I also felt I had missed some clues along the way. Did I? I must have, for surely Wolfe would not have switched to first person Horn at the end in order to provide a vehicle for a new series. How does one go from Silk's epiphany to Horn narrating the tale?

I'll have read more carefully when I again reread the Long Sun series.

Terry Nickolette

1998-12-15 Michael Straight #

Argues that the opening of Nightside the Long Sun shows where Horn gets the account of Silk's enlightenment: Silk 'talked about it afterward', so Silk's inner life may be known only through how he related it to others. Responds to Terence Nickolette's question of how the move from Silk's epiphany to Horn narrating the tale happens, citing the first paragraph as the answer.
answers · close-textual-reading · theories: Silk's inner life known only through his retelling

(Ah, this bit of my last post belonged here.)

The first paragraph of Nightside identifies the enlightenment narrative as the way Silk "talked about it afterward" -- so that tells us where Horn gets it, and is a very subtle clue that everything we read about Silk's inner life may be from how he related it to others.

-Rostrum
