Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Unreliable narration

1997-06-25 Michael Straight #

Argues that Horn's narration in the Long Sun books parallels the shifting narrator of Luke and Acts, drawing a comparison with the sudden first-person 'we' passage in Acts 16:11. Supports Kevin McGuire's earlier observation about early church storytelling by citing the Gospel and Acts structure as evidence of a deliberate parallel.
supports · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Long Sun parallels the Luke-Acts 'we' passage

A really good parallel is with Luke/Acts (the books of Luke and Acts make up one long "letter" by Luke chronicling the life of Jesus and the history of the early church). Luke starts out talking about how he interviewed various people to get the whole story straight and, like the other Gospels, all of Luke and most of Acts is in the third-person, but then all the sudden in Acts 16:11, where Luke has been telling about Paul's travels, out of nowhere it says "We set sail from Troas" (It would be somewhere in the fourth book if you broke Luke/Acts up into four books). Acts continues to use 'we' for a few chapters without explanation, and then goes back to third person to finish Paul's story.

-Rostrum
