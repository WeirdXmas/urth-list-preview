1999-10-17 Kieran Mullen #

Argues that whale size and lung capacity offer data for testing the square-cube law objection to giants, suggesting the lung-surface problem may not bind marine giants. Cites whales' long submersions as evidence that their lungs are not near a breaking point and asks what fraction of body volume whale and elephant lungs occupy, offering to fit a power law.
proposes

One data point would be the size of whales. They don't seem to be working on the evolutionary edge of lung capacity, since they can stay submerged for long periods of time. (That is, they seem to have enough O2 marginal capacity to be able to hold their breath for long periods.) If they were too close to the break point of the square cube law, I don't think that would be easy for them.

Our lungs are a fairly small fraction of our volume. Anyone know what fraction of a whale's volume their lungs are? Or an elephants? If we have these few data points, I can fit the power law for you. (I am pretty sure that the scale isn't linear. Our legs, for example, are proportionally thicker than an insects, as dictated by the SC law.)

Kieran Mullen

Recalls that the square-cube law column was by Willy Ley rather than Ben Bova, and jokes that the giant-insect article has been written many times. Ahh... I was only ever a Cub. I never graduated to the big time.
