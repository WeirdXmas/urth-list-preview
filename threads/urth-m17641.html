2011-01-07 Lee Berman replying to Andrew Mason #

Argues that the word macaque is too specific to serve as a usable term for Wolfe's simians, and that macaques remain worthwhile because the Barbary ape, Inuus sylvanus, links to Inire in The Book of the New Sun and to Silk in the Long Sun and Short Sun books. Responds to Andrew Mason's earlier suggestion of the term, pointing to the species' connections across Wolfe's series and noting a US politician's remark that shows the word's sensitivity.
supports · source-and-allusion · theories: Inire and Silver Silk connect to the Barbary ape

Antonio Pedro Marques: I suppose 'macaque' would be too far off to be usable.

Heh! Too specific actually. There is broad ranging monkey group called macaques which must be distinguished from other monkey groups like langurs and vervets and of course the New World monkeys which have prehensile tails.

A US politician recently lost his election for publically calling a person of Indian ethnicity a "maccaca".

Anyway, mention of macaques are good in my book, since the one called the barbary ape is Inuus sylvanus which has connections to both BotNS (Inire) and Long/Short Sun (Silver Silk).

2011-01-07 Andrew Mason #

Argues that apes and monkeys are different kinds of animal, comparing the distinction to wolves and dogs, and notes that the distinction holds differently across languages. Supports this with a Terry Pratchett character who objects to being called a monkey, which proved hard to translate into French where one word covers both.
supports

. ?My feeling is that the distinction has taken hold better than most

This may differ a lot between languages. Terry Pratchett has a character, to whom someone referred obliquely a little while ago, who is an ape and gets annoyed when people call him a monkey. This proved very hard to translate into French, because in French the same word 'singe' covers both.

I'm with Jerry - to me they are different kinds of animal. to call an ape a monkey would be like calling a wolf a dog. (Of course, that might be appropriate in some contexts; but if I said there was a dog in the back garden, and it turned out to be a wolf, that would be deceptive.)

2011-01-07 António Pedro Marques replying to Andrew Mason #

Continues the thread on translating ape versus monkey, suggesting macaque would be too inaccurate to use in translation. Adds a joke that dogs are now classed as a subspecies of wolf, so no word remains for wild wolf, and remarks that genetics and taxonomy get confused; it is off-topic from Wolfe.
chats

I suppose 'macaque' would be too far off to be usable.

Well, good luck: dogs have been reclasssified as a subspecies of wolf (i.e. not all wolves are dogs, but all dogs are wolves, so your garden is safe), which leaves us without a word for proper wolf. If only people realised genetics and taxonomy are independent.
