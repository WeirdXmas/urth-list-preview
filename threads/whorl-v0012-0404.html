Loganstone

2001-03-04 Dan Rabin #

Reports that the lighter Loganstone's name derives from an old English term for a rocking stone with Druidic associations, citing web sources and a famous Cornish example. Argues the name suits the lander's rocking motions and the Whorl's origin as an asteroid, offering it as a fit for the ship's naming.
informs · source-and-allusion

"Loganstone" is the name of the lighter that took Magnesia (Marble) to the Whorl. I got a little bothered about the name yesterday, so I did a Google search and came up with

<http://www.quinion.com/words/weirdwords/ww-log1.htm>
<http://www.pitt.edu/~dash/monuments.html>.

It's an old English name for a rocking stone, with Druidic/magical associations. There's a famous one in Cornwall.

The name fits in with the motions attributed to landers, and also fits in with the provenance of the Whorl as an asteriod.

-- Dan
