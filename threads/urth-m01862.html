2006-08-18 Adam Thornton #

Argues that the Latro books resemble Petronius's Satyricon: a damaged Latin-speaking hero travels the Roman world to appease his god, and Latro's gaps in memory mirror the lacunae of a fragmentary ancient text. Notes the poster is rereading the Satyricon for an outside project and found it strikingly familiar. Offers no evidence beyond these broad parallels and asks whether others have noticed the connection.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Latro's holes mirror the Satyricon's textual lacunae

I assume this has been mentioned before, but, uh, everyone's aware of how much the Latro books read like _The Satyricon_--the damaged Latin- speaking hero on a quest throughout the Roman world to appease his god--right?

It's just that the holes in Latro's story come from the hole in his head, rather than the holes in a text passed down less-than- faithfully for two millennia.

I'm rereading _The Satyricon_ as research for a project I'm working on, and I was struck by how familiar a lot of it felt.

Adam

2006-08-18 Russell Wodell replying to Adam Thornton #

Suggests that the Latro books may owe a structural debt to Fellini's 1969 film of the Satyricon, whose abrupt narrative leaps resemble Latro's gaps, and jokingly floats a similar link to The Book of the New Sun. Offers Harryhausen's Jason and the Argonauts as a film that more directly comes to mind about Latro.
proposes · genre-and-craft · theories: Latro's narrative gaps echo Fellini's Satyricon

Hmmm...that begs that question whether Wolfe ever saw Federico Fellini's remarkable 1969 film version of Sayricon, in which the bold leaps across the narrative gaps are memorably disorienting, if you will excuse the pun. There might just possibly be a structural influence. ... even though there are many centuries between pre-classic Greece and Petronius.

Or could that have inspired the future-influencing-the-past structure of Book of the New Sun? (Just kidding.)

Actually, the film that springs to mind re: Latro would be the classic Ray Harryhausen version of Jason and the Argonauts.
