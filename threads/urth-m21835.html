Seal of Pas

Urth list · 10 messages · 2011-10-28 to 2011-10-28

2011urthThe Book of the Long SunThe Book of the New SunLee BermanGwern BranwenGerry QuinnDavid StockhoffJames Wynn
The thread: Lee Berman and Gerry Quinn, with Gwern Branwen and James Wynn, debated whether the numbers on the Seal of Pas in Wolfe's New Sun carry real significance or are arbitrary. Berman argued the numbers encode Earthly or Urthly meaning, linking them to Revelation's Seventh Seal and the Twelve Tribes, and read them as relevant to the VRT and Dr. Marsch question. Quinn repeatedly argued they are meaningless, credit-card-like or barcode-like strings, and that any hidden meaning would be a poorly set-up game or an irony about Wolfe as author. Gwern objected that a fixed sequence cannot be a serial number and that earlier ASCII and EBCDIC decodings failed. The thread ended without agreement, with Quinn's reading of the numbers as a contrast between eras and a Typhon-sealing reading left alongside Berman's.
  • Lee Berman: the numbers carry real Earthly significance tied to Revelation and the Twelve Tribes
  • Gerry Quinn: the numbers are arbitrary or barcode-like, with no encoded meaning
  • Gwern Branwen: the seal is one fixed sequence, so storage or serial-number readings cannot work
  • James Wynn: the characters could report the exact numbers only if Silk recalled them precisely, raising the question of how they know them
  • Gerry Quinn (later posts): the numbers mark the computer-ID age of Typhon's era, or are an authorial irony

Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Myth and folklore sources, Names and their meanings

2011-10-28 Gwern Branwen replying to Gerry Quinn #

Argues that the Seal of Pas is a single fixed number sequence, so the suggested readings of it as serial numbers, storage dates or chamber contents cannot work. Responds to Gerry Quinn's naturalistic claim that the numbers encode storage details, pointing out that identical seals on every chamber would not vary, and notes that earlier ASCII and EBCDIC decodings found nothing.
disputes · puzzle-solving

It has been a while, but wasn't there just one sequence of numbers which made up a Seal of Pas? If the sequence doesn't change, then none of those 3 suggestions would work (serial numbers which don't change are not useful; it's unlikely that every single chamber would have been sealed at the same time (especially since a datestamp that big ought to be accurate down to really small units like nanoseconds) or would have the same contents).

The Seal is a little frustrating; I had tried looking at it as ASCII and, I think, an EBCDIC, but nothing popped out.

2011-10-28 Lee Berman replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that the numbers on the seal of Pas in The Urth of the New Sun carry a real Earthly or Urthly significance readers should decode, not arbitrary serial numbers. Grounds this in Wolfe's unusual attention to the seal, the fact that Urth was unplanned when The Book of the New Sun was written, and the hotly debated VRT and Dr. Marsch question, while suggesting the Bible and the Seventh Seal as sources.
proposes · puzzle-solving · source-and-allusion · theories: Seal numbers encode Earthly or Urthly significance; Seal refers to the Bible and the Seventh Seal

Yes, you have answered your own question. And Wolfe most often takes that route relying on a description of "gnostic" or "teratoid" symbols non-specific adjectives to convey to his readers that the protagonist is seeing symbols he (and we) are not expected to understand the specifics of, only the nature of.

Well, this is literature. Nothing *has* to be anything. But Wolfe's departure from his usual way of describing alien symbols seems pretty significant to me.

This wouldn't be the first example of that. UotNS was not planned when Wolfe wrote BotNS. Therefore Wolfe expected all the mystery solutions revealed in UotNS to be soluble without that book. But they aren't. At least not by the readers who have published their interpretations in books or online. Plus we have the example of whether VRT replaced Dr. Marsch. Hotly debated until Gene Wolfe set the record straight in two separate interviews.

Yes. But there was mention by Hammerstone of many other seals which had already been broken. A random serial number doesn't make sense for Wolfe to devote this much attention to. I think it has some sort of Earthly or perhaps Urthly significance that we are supposed to figure out. I still think the Bible and the Seventh Seal and the 12 x 12,0000 Tribes of Israel is a likely suspect. Perhaps not to be ignored is that there are words also.
