The thread: Participants debated when the neighbors first appear in Wolfe's Short Sun books and whether Horn is a plant-human hybrid created through tree DNA in the pit. maa32 argued the neighbors appear first in the pit, tied to Horn's genes and new powers, and rejected Steve's claim of earlier appearances. William Ansley disputed Marc's hybrid theory as genetically questionable and unhelpful for understanding the books, while admitting Wolfe's intent is unknown and that he may revisit the ideas on a reread. Steve conceded he had the sequence wrong in a hasty reply, praised Marc's insights, and said he would later test Marc's theories against the text. The thread ended without consensus, with the hybrid theory left open.

2002-02-21 Marc Aramini #

Argues that the neighbors Horn meets first appear in the pit, not earlier, because they are created from his hybridized genes and Horn's new powers come from sharing tree DNA. Responds to Steve's remark about earlier neighborly appearances, citing the pit scene, the neighbor who calls himself Horn, and He Pen Ship's usage, while declining to restate earlier posts.
disputes · myth-and-structure · close-textual-reading · theories: Neighbors are created from Horn's hybridized genes; Horn's powers come from sharing tree DNA; Horn is eaten and reborn in the pit

Steve commented that the appearance of the neighbors was before the pit. This is incorrect. There are neighborly artifacts and holy places before that, but in the pit is the first time he meets the neighbors. They are created from his hybridized genes. All of these powers Horn gets after this are a result of sharing tree DNA. He was eaten there. And born again with new powers. And the neighbor he meets identifies himself as "Horn" and He Pen Ship calls Horn a neighbor. I've already commented on all of those features and his powers in previous posts in the last archive if you want to review them. The Pit is where it all happens, (and under a tree at the end of OBW). Notice that the island is completely composed of huge trees, as Horn realizes later. I won't repeat myself again; you are all familiar with my theories by now. If you want to find a more detailed explanation, just follow my threads through the last archive.
Marc Aramini

2002-02-21 William H. Ansley #

Disputes Marc's theory that Horn in the Short Sun books is a plant-human hybrid created by sharing tree DNA in the pit where he is eaten and reborn. Says the genetic claims seem questionable and that the theory does not clarify the books, though he admits Wolfe's intent is unknown to him and he may keep the ideas in mind on a reread.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Horn is a plant-human hybrid from tree DNA

Marc,

Yes, you have expressed your theories before and at considerable length. You are certainly positive enough about them, but I am not buying any of it at the moment. I definitely find the genetic aspects of your "plant-human hybrids" to be highly questionable. Of course, that doesn't mean anything in science fiction. In fact, the only thing that matters is whether what you express in your theories is what Wolfe had in mind.

And, despite having read through the Short Sun series twice, I have no idea what Wolfe had in mind, so I can't criticize you on that account. But your theories don't help me to make any more sense out of the books, so they don't appeal to me either.

If I can work up enough enthusiasm to re-read the SS series (which is doubtful) I will be keeping your ideas in mind, however. --

William Ansley
