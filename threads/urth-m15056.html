2010-03-22 Allen Short #

Points out that Gideon's order of "Renaissance French Toast" in chapter 6 of An Evil Guest echoes a joke usually credited to Steven Wright, and asks whether it hints at time travel. Also asks why food references recur in the novel, noting no clear treatment of them.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Renaissance French Toast hints at time-travel themes

In case anyone missed the significance of Gideon ordering the "Renaissance French Toast" at the beginning of chapter 6, it's from a joke usually attributed to Steven Wright:

??"I went to a restaurant that serves 'breakfast at any time.' So I ordered French Toast during the Renaissance."

Other than a clever allusion to restaurants like International House of Pancakes that serve breakfast all day, could this be another hint at the time-travel themes in the story? (Also, what's with the constant food references? This got brought up early on but I don't recall any significant exploration of it...)

2010-03-22 Fernando Q. Gouvea replying to Allen Short #

Reports a personal anecdote in which Wolfe said he was inserting references to salad dressings into An Evil Guest, including asking a waiter for Green Goddess dressing. Responds to Allen Short's theory that Gideon's Renaissance French Toast joke hints at time travel by indicating Wolfe attached no special significance to the menu reference.
informs · author-and-biography

I met Gene for dinner when he was writing the book, and he told me that he was having lots of fun inserting references to as many kinds of salad dressing as he could. This came up because he asked the waiter whether they had Green Goddess salad dressing. I told him one of the local restaurants did have it, and he mentioned the scene in the book. It didn't sound as if there was any special significance to it.

Fernando

Suggests that the Renaissance French Toast joke in An Evil Guest may hint at its time-travel themes, and notes that Wolfe seems to like naming salad dressings. Builds on Allen Short's quoted question about the Steven Wright joke, and relies on Fernando Gouvea's report that Wolfe mentioned Green Goddess dressing at dinner and had no special meaning in mind.

And the time-travel toast joke seems in line with the use of salad dressing names.
