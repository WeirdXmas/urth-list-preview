The end of Soldier of Arete

Urth list · 10 messages · 2006-11-29 to 2006-12-01

2006urththalassocratMo Holkar / UKGJeff WilsonDan'l Danehy-OakesMichael Straight
The thread: Readers of the Urth list worked through the ending of Soldier of Arete: who the Sons of Sco are, what Pindaros's penultimate speech means for Pausanias, Hegesistratus, and the Rope Makers, and whether the scroll could be read by others. Michael Straight pressed a series of questions about the chain of cargo and power, while thalassocrat proposed that Latro secretly struck a deal with Pausanias trading the Phoenician cargo for holding back, a theory Straight disputed. Jeff Wilson argued that the scroll may have been censored, scraped, or read by people beyond the known readers, against Straight's claim that only Hegistratus could read it. The thread ended with thalassocrat supplying the Theseus and Amazon legend behind Pindaros's line about peace, but the deal theory and the readability question were left unresolved.
  • Michael Straight: questions the Pausanias deal theory and insists the scroll is readable only by a limited circle
  • thalassocrat: Latro struck a deal with Pausanias trading the cargo for holding back, tied to Gaea and Artemis
  • Jeff Wilson: the scroll may have been censored or erased by others, so an omission need not be Wolfe cheating
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: asks who the Sons of Sco are and seeks an answer about the archers

Themes: Unreliable narration, Memory and forgetting, Books, libraries and writing

2006-11-29 Michael Straight #

Asks for help parsing the penultimate passage of Soldier of Arete, where Pindaros speaks of the Spartan regent gaining from Latro's escape and Hegesistratus says Themistocles must be discredited and the Rope Makers destroyed. Lays out a chain of who holds the Crimson Men and the cargo, working through Argos and Sparta, and questions whether Artemis or Gaea wants Latro to win.
questions · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Argives hold the Crimson Men and the Phoenician ship; Hegesistratus's line may contain a typo

I just finished re-reading the first two Soldier books in preparation for Soldier of Sidon. I'm convinced that they are Wolfe's very best.

But, wow, to completely follow the plot you really must keep careful track of every single character, which city they are from, which god they serve, and all the alternate names for them, their regions, and their gods. (And also whether or not they are a werewolf, dryad, centaur, ghost, or transgendered via possession by lamia.)

I think I mostly got it this time but I'm still unclear about the penultimate paragraph.

Pindaros says that the ship Latro stole carried the cargo of the Spartan regent (Pausanius). But I thought the Crimson Men (Phonecians) had been robbed of the cargo by the pirates from Hundred-Eyed (Argos?).

I'd originally thought the pirates had taken the treasure and that the Spartans were holding the Crimson Men and their empty ship. But if Hundred Eyed=Argos, and Pindaros says the Argives were asking for Spartan help in chasing Latro, that means the Argives were holding the Crimson Men captive and had the Phonecian ship with Pausanius's cargo sitting right there in the port under Pausanius's nose! Talk about hutzpah and trust in the truce of the games!

So it makes sense that Pausanius would order the Spartans to "shun the Phonecian steel" and prevent others from pursuing Latro--if he can't have the cargo, neither can the Argives--but what does Pindaros mean when he says "the shrewd prince gained ten times over what he lost"? I don't see how he gains anything by Latro's escape except in the sense of "my enemy's loss is my gain."

I'm also not clear about Hegesistratus's comment "He [Themistocles] has become a friend of the Rope Makers...For the good of the world, he must be discredited and they destroyed." But he believes he has been bribed by Artemis to make sure Latro wins. I can see how losing the race might be a blow to Themistocles, but I don't see how it would lead to the Rope Makers being destroyed.

And does he think that's why Artemis wants Latro to win the race? Because it would lead to the destruction of her people, the Rope Makers? Surely that's more like what Gaea wants. (Is it possible there is a typo and Hegestratus is saying Themistocles must be discredited and *then* destroyed?)

It's funny how Pindaros writes of the sighting of Artemis on Latro's ship as if it's a sign that her hand was in Latro's escape in the victory of the silver chariot. But that's the opposite of what Artemis wanted and if that was her on Latro's ship, it must be because he's in big trouble with her.

Rostrum

2006-11-30 Michael Straight replying to thalassocrat #

Doubts thalassocrat's theory that Latro struck a secret deal letting Pausanias keep the ship cargo in exchange for holding back, and asks how Pausanias can be "twice a hero". Weighs Pausanias's pride and greed against the risk of breaking the games' peace, citing Pindaros and the quoted gold line, and hopes the deal would ease Pasicrates's fate.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Latro made a secret deal with Pausanias over the ship cargo

Hmm. That's kind of a stretch. Hard to believe, given Pausanias's pride. And would he really let Latro go, given that he believes that with Latro at his side he has Nike there as well? On the other hand, he really wants that gold, and this is a way to break the truce without being blamed. And given the high regard he has for Latro's opinions about the gods, he might feel that helping Latro was the way to get the gods on his side.

The other problem is that this has to be a secret deal or Pausanias is guilty of breaking the peace of the games. But Pindaros implies that everyone can see that Pausanias ended up gaining more than he lost.

I'd like to believe it if only because it means Pasicrates's fate isn't quite as bleak as it appears. The gods punishment on him was pretty relentless from the moment he tried to have Latro whipped.

I suppose this makes sense. We see how Themistocles is being damned by the Spartans' praise (this is their intention). I guess we're supposed to assume something similar will happen to Pausanias?

Especially since Latro promised Elata he would write down in his journal what happened there. This is one of the few places I think Wolfe cheated, leaving out something he wanted left unsaid but that Latro ought to have written.

Maybe, unlike Artemis, she thinks Pausanias's downfall will actually be bad for Sparta in the long run.

Yeah, that makes sense. I'd forgotten about the meeting where some kind of deal was struck between Artemis and Gaea. But again, how can Pausanias be "twice a hero" if his profit from Latro's escape is that Latro secretly gives him the treasure (under the cover of Pasicrates going to Tower Hill to get some gold)?

2006-11-30 thalassocrat replying to Michael Straight #

Argues that Latro struck a deal with Pausanias, trading the Phoenician cargo for Pausanias holding back, and that Pausanias is a favorite of Gaea whom Artemis wants destroyed through the hubris of a chariot-race victory. Grounds this in Latro's remark about gold, Pasicrates sent to Corinth, Gaea's scene with the helots, and a presumed deal between Gaea's and Artemis's representatives in chapter 38.
proposes · myth-and-structure · close-textual-reading · theories: Latro bargained with Pausanias for the cargo; Pausanias is Gaea's favorite; Artemis wants Pausanias destroyed through hubris; Gaea and Artemis's representatives struck a deal in chapter 38

On Thu, 30 Nov 2006 17:04:51 +1100 Michael Straight

The end of Arete is Wolfe at his toughest, IMO. For this part of it, my conclusion is that Latro did a deal with Pausanias; the ship cargo in return for Pausanias holding back when Latro makes his play. Towards the end, he writes: "I still do not know what can be done, but I know that for gold these Hellenes will connive at anything." Then a little later, Pausanias sends pasicrates off to Corinth, telling him not to come back without gold.

My reading on this: Pausanias is actually Gaea's favorite in this. Recall the scene where a decrepit Gaea phrophesizes for the helots. Pasuanias will, perhaps, raise them up or whatever.

Artemis, presumably for similar reasons, wants Pausanias destroyed. In typical Greek fashion, the weapon of destruction is to be hubris, specifically a victory in the chariot race. As she says to Latro, H and Elata: "My queen must win in order that the prince may be destroyed."

In the end, things work out a little differently: Pausanias in fact loses the chariot race, but nevertheless "wins" in a sufficiently hubris-inducing way. (And Themistocles will suffer the same fate.)

I think there was a deal worked out between the representatives of Gaea and Artemis, in Ch38. Infuriatingly, Latro doesn't say what happened at this meeting, finishing with Elata murmuring, "Now we can begin."

So I don't know what Gaea gets in exchange for Pausanias' downfall - maybe a victory for her grand-daughters, the Amazons?

But Artemis did get what she wanted - a "victory" for Pausanias, "accounted twice a hero among the strategem loving Greeks." Gaea presumably gets the consolation prize of a victory for her grand- daughters, the Amazons.

2006-11-30 Jeff Wilson replying to Michael Straight #

Argues that someone other than the known readers could have sneaked in and erased or scraped off a recent entry in Latro's record that they feared would incriminate them. Responds to Michael Straight's point that only Hegistratus could read Latro's writing, noting that the text only says no one admitted to reading it, so the claim that no one else could read it is not established.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: someone secretly erased an entry in Latro's record

We only know it was written that no one admitted being able to read what Latro writes. Even if this is true, someone could still sneak in and erase or scrape off a recent entry they fear records something damning.
