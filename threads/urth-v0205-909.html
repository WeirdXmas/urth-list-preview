2002-05-08 Mark Millman #

Adds a further example to the discussion of the title "Under Hill" by noting that the dragon in Ursula K. Le Guin's Earthsea story "The Rule of Names" calls himself Mr. Underhill before his disguise is breached. Builds on Josh Geller's reply to Matthew Davis, which cited Crowley's Mrs. Underhill, by pointing to another fantasy use of the name as a disguise.
supports · source-and-allusion

Also, the dragon in Ursula LeGuin's Earthsea short
story "The Rule of Names" calls himself Mr. Under-
hill before his disguise is breached. Before that,
he poses as a somewhat ineffectual wizard.

Mark Millman
(Nacre)
