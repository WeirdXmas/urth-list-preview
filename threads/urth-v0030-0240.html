"Triskele" is unrelated to "Triste"

Urth list · 2 messages · 2001-01-11 to 2001-01-11

2001urthThe Book of the New SunPeter WestlakeDan Rabin

Themes: Myth and folklore sources, Names and their meanings

2001-01-11 Dan Rabin #

Argues that the dog name Triskele refers to the three-legged sun symbol, linking it to the Isle of Man and Apollonian sun imagery rather than to Triste. Cites a web page on the Isle of Man flag, the Norse sun-movement claim, and Lexicon Urthus citing John Clute, then suggests Severian's healing of Triskele symbolically brings the New Sun.
proposes · source-and-allusion · myth-and-structure · theories: Triskele named for the triskelion sun symbol; Triskele's healing symbolically brings the New Sun

It is more likely that "Triskele" is used in the sense of a design in the form of three running legs (or stylizings thereof) arranged at 120-degree angles with the "hip" being the center of a circle. Merriam-Webster online gives the term as a variant of "Triskelion". Triskele has three legs (and a stump, but perhaps

Such a design serves as the symbol of the Isle of Man see <http://www.crwflags.com/fotw/flags/im.html>. It would not be un-Wolfean for "Isle of Man" to have some bearing on the choice of the name for the dog. (Hmmm--isolation of Man???) BUT (!!!) the web page mentioned says, "According to Norse mythology, the triskelion was a symbol of the movement of the sun through the heavens".

I was all set to crow at this find just now, but I calmed down enough to check _Lexicon Urthus_, in which mantis cites John Clute giving an Apollonian connection for the symbol. Darn.

So Severian's healing of Triskele symbolically brings the New Sun, or the New Sun symbolically arises at his touch, or (if we admit what we learn in _Urth of the New Sun_) the New Sun gives a symbolic token of his coming to the young Severian, which token also sort-of leads him to the Atrium of Time, and also makes the structure sort of cyclic (just like the sun, and like Divine Years), and...

Someone else please pick this up and run with it--my head hurts, and I have to save it for _Return to the Whorl_ :-).

It would also be non-un-Wolfean for the dog character to have been *derived* from the symbolic connection.

-- Dan Rabin

P.S. The "-skele" element also occurs in "isosceles".

2001-01-11 Peter Westlake #

Argues that the dog's name Triskele has no real link to Triste, rejecting a suggested Isle of Man connection as too clever to be true. Responds to Dan Rabin's remark that the triskele symbol of the Isle of Man might bear on the dog's name, playfully noting that saying the phrase aloud sounds like a dog's sentiment.
disputes · source-and-allusion

Spectacled Bear.
