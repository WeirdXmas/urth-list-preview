Ackroyd's Milton as Wolfean Hero

Urth list · 3 messages · 2013-04-11 to 2013-04-11

2013urthBrian LovelyMarc Aramini
The thread: Brian asked whether readers had seen Peter Ackroyd's Milton in America and whether its blind, injured hero, pulled into a tree, recalls anything in Wolfe. Marc Aramini argued the novel reads more like Paradise Lost and the Biblical fall than anything Wolfe would write, and that Ackroyd's riffing on famous literary figures differs from Wolfe's allusive method. Brian replied that he had first seen no link to the Short Sun but later noticed shared details: a tree, identity confusion, a deer, a leg injury and a restoration. The thread ends with the two holding their positions, with Brian's parallels resting on surface details that Marc rejects as grounds for comparison.
  • Marc Aramini: Milton in America is closer to Paradise Lost and the fall than to Wolfe, and Ackroyd's literary riffing is unlike Wolfe's allusiveness
  • Brian: the tree, identity confusion, deer, leg injury and restoration in Ackroyd's novel make a Wolfean comparison worth considering

2013-04-11 Marc Aramini replying to Brian #

Argues that Ackroyd's novel Milton in America, with its injured protagonist pulled into a tree, reads more like Paradise Lost and the Biblical fall than anything Wolfe would write. Responds to Brian's description of the forest scene and the broken leg, comparing Ackroyd's riffing on famous literary figures with Wolfe's more allusive method.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · source-and-allusion · theories: Ackroyd's Milton episode echoes Paradise Lost temptation and fall

Fairly interesting - Ackroyd seems to have made his career by riffing on famous literary figures - Wolfe does the same thing but in a less obvious and more?allusive way, though of course he is not?above writing short sequels to David Copperfield and such.? It is fascinating how some authors have such a consistent mode of operation. ?
I haven't read the book in question, but this might be more Biblical/Miltonian than even Wolfe.? Satan falls from a great height, injures his leg.? Tree of the knowledge of good and evil and serpent imagery to tempt man to the same fall.? So, while Wolfe might have made our leg injured protagonist pulled into a tree a hero, this seems like a definite Paradise Lost and subsequent temptation of mankind theme playing out.

Has anyone read Peter Ackroyd's Milton in America?? At one point, the blind Milton goes into the forest and sits under a tree.? Then follows a passage of pronoun/identity confusion, with "I" and "he" switching back and forth.? Milton walks on, and is pulled up into a tree by a deer snare, which breaks his leg.? This experience has a healing or rejunenating effect, because his vision seems to be restored at least for a time.

Any of this remind you of anything?

--B-Love
