Proposes that the President in An Evil Guest is modeled on Lyndon Johnson rather than George W. Bush, with the aide an analog of Jack Valenti. Grounds this in the Texan speech but LBJ-style one-on-one button-holing, Valenti's 2007 death, and the theatre allusion to Bacon's essay On Truth.

2009-09-30 Dave Tallman replying to Stephen Hoy #

Questions the link Stephen Hoy draws between a quoted line about Pilate's question of truth and the presidential assistant John in An Evil Guest, asking whether the connection rests on a Bacon/Shakespeare idea. Calls the connection a big stretch, offering no textual evidence of its own.
questions · source-and-allusion

I don't see the theatrical connection between the quote "What is truth? asked jesting Pilate..." and the presidential assistant John. Is it because of the Bacon/Shakespeare connection? This seems a big stretch.

2009-09-30 James Wynn replying to Stephen Hoy #

Disputes John Watkins' claim that the President's aide in An Evil Guest recalls Jack Valenti, arguing instead that the aide's description brings Karl Rove to mind. Grounds this on the quoted physical description of the aide (forty-plus, getting fat, thick round glasses) and notes that one-on-one conversation is typical of any president, not specifically LBJ.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: President's aide modeled on Karl Rove

Hmmm...."forty plus, and starting to get fat. The thick, round lenses of his glasses"...
This does not make me think of Jack Valenti. But it did call to mind Karl Rove. Not a one-to-one comparison any more than the president was. But it was difficult not to imagine Rove while reading this scene.

I really didn't see the one-on-one conversation as excluding GWB or inclining more to LBJ. It's just the sort of thing any President would do.

J.
