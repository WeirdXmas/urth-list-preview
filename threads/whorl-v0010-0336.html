2000-05-04 Alice Turner #

Shares a web essay on The Book of the Long Sun, about Ultan and the Briah, to help readers regain momentum in the series, and asks the list what they think of it. The poster says parts dazzled and parts put off, but gives no specific argument or evidence from the essay.
informs

Those of us who feel we've lost LS momentum might want to check out this web-essay to pick it up again (full disclosure, mantis gave it to me): http://members.tripod.co.uk/laidlow/ultan/briah.htm

I was quite dazzled by parts of it, and a bit put off by others. What do y'all think?

-alga

2000-05-05 Nicholas Gevers #

Announces that Nicholas Gevers's two essays on The Book of the Long Sun, "Five Steps Towards Briah" and "The Reader as Augur," are now online at Jonathan Laidlow's Ultan's Library website. Notes that the second essay appears in a fuller form than the version sent to some list members the previous year.
informs

The redoubtable alga set out to provide the URL for my
essays on THE BOOK OF THE LONG SUN, but the text she
was using doesn't seem to have been html-friendly (if
that's the right term). So, just for the record, the
twin essays "Five Steps Towards Briah" and "The Reader
as Augur" are available online at Jonathan Laidlow's
still-developing ULTAN'S LIBRARY:

http://members.tripod.co.uk/laidlow/ultan

The second essay is being published for the first
time, and is more complete than the version I sent
some people on the list last year.

--Mr. Gethers.

2000-05-08 Matthew Malthouse #

Complains that a fan website link for Ultan's Library is dead and that the replacement page says the library is closed until June 2000, grumbling about post-holiday mood. Responds to a quoted link posted on 05/05/2000 and offers no reading of the books.
chats

Grrr...

Just fired off a misserable message 'casue the other URL was duff then this pops up making me feel curmudgeonly. However the new one just says "Ultan's Library is currently closed. Opening soon - June 2000!"

Now maybe I'm miserable because I've just got back from holiday and I'm trying to get back in the work spirit (by not doing any, reading Urth & Whorl is _usually_ more fun) but c'mon such tricks are not lightening the post-vacation depression any.

Matthew
