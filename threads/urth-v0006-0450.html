Legion of unmanned Autarchs?

Urth list · 1 messages · 1998-01-26 to 1998-01-26

1998urthThe Book of the New SunDan Rabin

Themes: Hidden identities and parentage

1998-01-26 Dan Rabin #

Answers Alice Turner and Christopher Culver by supplying a citation showing that Ymar and Severian's predecessor were unmanned, and asks where the term Legion for the Autarch comes from. Quotes a passage in Citadel where Malrubius says the predecessor failed, and notes that Appian was proposed as a name for an Autarch in a story.
answers · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion

Alice Turner states, in urth.v0006.n020.1:

Christopher R. Culver concurs:

Could we have citations, please?

I'll accept the statement as concerns Ymar and Severian's predecessor, on the strength of the passage in _Citadel_, XXXI:

"If you fail, your manhood will be taken from you, so that you cannot bequeath the Phoenix Throne to your descendants. Your predecessor also accepted the challenge." [aquastor Malrubius _dixit_]

"And failed. That is clear from what you said." [quoth Severian]

"Yes. Still, he was braver than many who are called heroes, the first to go in many reigns. Ymar, of whom you may have heard, was the last before him."

Since we learn in _Urth_ that Ymar was the first Autarch (as opposed to Monarch), this passage would seem to deny that castration was usual for Autarchs.

I am unable to find another citation I seek: a passage in which Severian notes how many of his predecessors met their violent ends by foolish exposure to danger.

Christopher also asks,

It was one of the woman-cats in _Citadel_, XXIV.

There's another passage (again, I've misplaced it) in which the application of the term is explained. It's certainly not his real name. Michael Andre-Driussi assigns him the name Appian based on a mention of a recent Autarch by that name in the short story "The Cat", but I don't recall the evidence being conclusive that Severian's predecessor reigned at the time of the events there recounted.

-- Dan Rabin
