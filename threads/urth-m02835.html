Is "The Death Of Doctor Island" Offensive? Why not?

Urth list · 1 messages · 2006-10-16 to 2006-10-16

2006urthJack Redelfs

Themes: Justice, torture and mercy

2006-10-16 Jack Redelfs #

Argues that "The Death of Doctor Island" is brutal and disturbing, unlike Severian's story, because a violent young man murders a girl and the doctor declares the act a therapeutic step and cures him. Responds to Jack Redelfs's point that Wolfe's books already feature an executioner by contrasting Severian, who renounces the torturers' guild in the end, with the unpunished killer; concedes the story is not offensive to him.
disputes · close-textual-reading

I don't think that David is going out on a limb, at all. Severian may have been a torturer, but in the end he took off his fuligin and disbanded the torturer's guild.

While "The Death Of Dr. Island," a violent young man murders a young girl and, far from being punished, the good Dr. deems it was a vital therapeutic step and declares the offender cured. It's one of Wolfe's better early stories, but certainly one of his most brutal and disturbing. I don't find it offensive, but I can easily see how some might.
