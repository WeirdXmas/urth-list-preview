The Wonderful Free Live

Urth list · 3 messages · 1999-01-30 to 1999-02-01

1999urthWilliam H. AnsleyMichael Andre-Driussi
The thread: Driussi asked William H. Ansley to annotate The Wonderful Free Live for Oz allusions and whether other Wolfe books draw on Oz, and lamented the lack of a hardcover edition of The Island of Doctor Death and Other Stories. Ansley disputed the common view that the book loosely retells The Wizard of Oz, citing only a clown's parody of the brain, heart and nerve song. He then retracted part of that claim after recalling Little Ozzie singing "We're Off to See the Wizard," admitting he may have forgotten other Oz material and planning a reread. The thread ended with Ansley conceding the point, with no clear resolution on how much Oz is in the book.

1999-01-30 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Asks William H. Ansley to annotate Free Live Free for allusions to Oz after his work on Eyeflash, and whether other Wolfe books draw directly on Oz. Also laments that The Island of Doctor Death and Other Stories never received a hardcover edition, and floats rebinding reprint paperbacks as an alternative to costly small-press publishing.
questions · source-and-allusion

William H. Ansley,

No pressure, but once you've "completed" your work on "Eyeflash," you can mine another Wolfe-does-Oz by annotating FREE LIVE FREE. (Are there any other direct Oz expressways in Wolfe?)

While you've got the Oz loaded up in your brain.

But first things first.

As for THE ISLAND OF DOCTOR DEATH AND OTHER STORIES AND OTHER STORIES, I still weep that there has never been a hardcover edition of this book. And at this rate and/or point, there will probably never be one.

For a time I toyed with the madcap idea of buying a half-dozen copies of the reprint (trade paperback) and having them re-bound as hardcovers--an expensive proposition, but not when compared to the cost/labor of small press publishing, and/or the time wasted waiting for someone else to do something that will probably never be done, and/or paying the premium price if/when some specialty press actually does it.

=mantis=

1999-02-01 William H. Ansley #

Disputes the common view that Free Live Free is a loose retelling of The Wizard of Oz, arguing that the only clear Oz reference is a clown singing a parody of the song about wanting a brain, heart or nerve. Grounds this in the poster's own repeated readings, saying any other Oz resemblances are too vague to notice, and replies to mantis's suggestion to annotate the book.
disputes · source-and-allusion · theories: Free Live Free is a loose retelling of The Wizard of Oz

mantis,

I may try this; I am rather fond of FLF. But despite the many times I've read that FLF is a loose retelling of the _Wizard of Oz_, I don't buy it. The only real Oz reference is the clown character singing a parody of the song "If I only had a brain/heart/nerve" from the musical (movie) version. Any other Oz resemblances are so vague as to have been lost on me so far. And I have read FLF more than once.

Well, maybe next time... but not any time real soon.

William Ansley

1999-02-01 William H. Ansley #

Retracts part of the poster's earlier claim that Free Live Free has little Wizard of Oz content, after recalling that Little Ozzie sings "We're Off to See the Wizard" in the book. Admits that he may have forgotten other Oz material and plans to reread the novel to check.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Free Live Free is a loose retelling of The Wizard of Oz

I take the above back, partly. Shortly after I sent it, I remembered that Little Ozzie sings "We're Off to See the Wizard" at some point in FLF. Maybe I'll have to re-read FLF sooner than I thought and see if I forgot any more Oz stuff.

William Ansley
