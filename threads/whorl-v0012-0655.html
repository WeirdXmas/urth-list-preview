2001-04-19 James Jordan #

Proposes that the inhumi secret in Return to the Whorl gives two reasons humans could not simply wipe them out: killing would be too cruel or too laborious, or humans are too wicked to treat them rightly. Responds to Wombat's complaint about the detail and cites Adam's post on Horn and Silk as a shift in view.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Inhumi secret forms a dilemma explaining the Neighbors' departure; Humans too wicked to save inhumi by offering blood

At 02:52 PM 4/19/2001 -0400,

There've been a couple of suggestions.
1. Wiping out the inhumi would be so cruel that people would not be able to bring themselves to do it. Or, it would require so much work that humans would not be able to do it.
2. Human beings are too wicked to treat the inhumi rightly, saving and transforming them by offering our blood. Along these lines, I think Adam's fine post "Dangling Thought Threads" makes some good points, and I'm coming around to his point of view on Horn and Silk.
3. One can view this as both true, as horns of a dilemma, and as an explanation of why the Neighbors departed.

Nutria

2001-04-24 Peter Westlake #

Points readers to an August 2000 archive discussion in which Adam Stephanides linked the Neighbour's sewer remark in The Book of the Long Sun to the Secret about the inhumi. Notes the Neighbour says humans must wake for their safety, which Horn grasps only after Krait reveals the Secret, though the remark seems to point elsewhere.
informs · close-textual-reading

That detail wasn't mentioned directly in the text, I'm sure, but we did discuss it here. Have a look around August 2000 in the archives. Adam, you drew attention there to the Neighbour who takes Horn to the sewer saying that the Neighbours wanted to wake humans up, not to their danger but to their safety. Horn only understands this after Krait tells him the Secret, though it doesn't appear that the Neighbour is talking about that directly, but about some other implication of the sewer. No idea what, though.

Peter.
