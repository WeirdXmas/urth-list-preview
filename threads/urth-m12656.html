Peace: the fate of Lois

Urth list · 1 messages · 2010-02-24 to 2010-02-24

2010urthGwern Branwen

2010-02-24 Gwern Branwen #

Questions the allegorical reading that equates Den and Lois with figures in the St. Brandon tale told in Peace, asking who the Angel and the fleeing pieces stand for. Points out that the same story appears nearly unchanged as a standalone piece in Book of Days, which weakens the claim that it is tied to a key event in the novel.
disputes · allegory-and-typology · source-and-allusion · theories: St. Brandon tale allegorizes Den and Lois's marriage

I wonder about that. Allegories and analogies are treacherous territory. Who is the Angel, then, and what are the pieces running off into the woods?

If this story is an allegory of a key event in _Peace_, why does it show up nigh identical (as far as I noticed) shorn of its _Peace_ wrappings as a standalone story in _Book of Days_?

(Also, it's a good thing to trim your emails to include only the context necessary.)
