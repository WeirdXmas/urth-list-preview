Themes: Names and their meanings

2003-03-12 Andrew Bollen #

Argues that the King Jesus/Typhon connection is a red herring, since Graves' Set/Typhon story is too tendentious for Wolfe to use, but that an Egyptian Typhon fits The Book of the New Sun. Grounds this in Typhon's identification with Set, the slayer of Osiris, and a quoted passage making Set the adversary who keeps Ra from rising in the East.
disputes · source-and-allusion · theories: Typhon in The Book of the New Sun is Egyptian Set

FWIW, I think that the King Jesus/Typhon business is a red herring. I = think that Graves' construction of a Set/Typhon story from fragments of = Roman/Greek slander against Jews & Christians and other bits & pieces is = too fragile and tendentious to be the kind of thing Wolfe melds into his = stories. =20

But it is interesting to think of an Egyptian rather than a Greek Typhon = in the context of New Sun. Typhon was identified with Set, the slayer of = Osiris, and thus a Satanish kind of figure. Even more explicitly (from = the link below):

"In its second form the two Combatant gods are Ra and Set, and the chief = object of the latter is to prevent Ra from appearing in the East daily."

So "Typhon" is quite an appropriate name for an enemy of the New Sun.

--
