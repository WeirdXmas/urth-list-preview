The thread: Participants debated whether the Short Sun is set on Blue, the Whorl's third world, or on Urth or Ushas, with Jordon Flato proposing Blue mirrors Urth and Ushas and Son of Witz arguing the three-whorl imagery and Seawrack and Mother clues support it. James Wynn and Matthew Groves backed the Blue or Green-Urth reading and the idea of the Whorl returning, while Matthew Groves recalled an earlier list post making the thematic case. Roy C. Lackey declined to re-argue dream-travel and time-travel questions and urged readers not to treat thread claims as settled, prompting James Wynn to ask for an actual refutation. The thread ended in impasse, with no agreed reading and several requests for sources left open.
  • Jordon Flato: Short Sun is set on Blue, mirroring Urth and Ushas; Horn is not a Neighbor
  • James Wynn: Blue or Green-Urth theory holds, time travel is obvious, Short Sun probably not Sol
  • Matthew Groves: Whorl has returned to Urth, thematic return is the key point
  • Son of Witz: Short Sun is on Blue, possibly only three whorls, Neighbors descend from Green Man
  • Roy C. Lackey: Declines to settle time-travel or Sol questions, warns against treating claims as settled
  • James Wynn: Asks Lackey for a real refutation rather than dismissal

Themes: Time loops and causality, The dying sun and the cosmos, Myth and folklore sources

2009-03-18 Jordon Flato replying to Son of Witz #

Proposes that the Short Sun's Blue world mirrors the events of Urth and Ushas, suggesting Abia's people may have come from Blue and that a Severian-like figure existed among the Neighbors there. Grounds this in resonances between the worlds and in the multi-limbed beings, while rejecting the idea that Horn is a Neighbor.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · close-textual-reading · theories: Blue is where Abia's race came from; Blue mirrors Urth and Ushas events; A Severian equivalent exists on Blue; Horn is not a Neighbor

There are some possibilities. Maybe Blue is where Abia's 'race' is from? I've always assumed Abia came to Urth at some point. Must have come from somewhere, no?

And I am against the theory of Ushas as Blue, you know from our discussions. However, there are so many resonances between the two worlds (some of which you point out), that I think we are being directed to the idea that what happened on Urth/Ushas happened on Blue as well. Perhaps there was a "severian" equivelant on Blue. One of the Neighbors. And we have the Abia analogue, just like Urth. There is more to this, which until I finish on this read through of SS I don't want to lay out, but I think the multi-limbed beings and what not are a clue to this.

And I can't get with the idea that Horn is a Neighbor. Doesn't make a lick of sense to me, thematically or contextually.

2009-03-18 James Wynn replying to Son of Witz #

Explains why the poster abandoned the Blue Earth theory once Horn was identified as Typhon and the Raja as a Neighbor, and rejects Marc's later Green Urth switch. Responds to Son of Witz's question about the Mother and the brides of Abia, adding that dream travel obviously involves time travel and doubting the Short Sun is Sol.
disputes · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Horn is Typhon; The Raja is a Neighbor; Urth is Blue Earth; Urth is Green instead of Blue

I was an enthusiastic proponent of Marc's Blue Earth theory, because it was an attempt to resolve some open puzzles originally rather than must shrug your shoulders, and because it made several comments by Horn make a lot of sense. But, guess what? When I realized that Horn was Typhon and the Raja was a Neighbor, those comments made comprehensive sense and Blue Earth ceased to have any value I'm afraid. Also, a puckish comment by Wolfe to Marc caused him to flip and declare that Urth was Green instead. Actually, there is thematic value to having Blue or Green be Verthandi. But I simply don't believe it is. Just as I don't believe Blue and Green are St. Anne and St. Croix.

Dream-travel self-evidently involves or enables Time-travel. This is so obvious from the Long Sun and the Short Sun stories that I'm a little impatient with those that don't casually accept it. So I'd best not get into it again.

But I don't believe that the Short Sun is Sol. It is a pointless flourish on a narrative that is already troubling enough.

J

2009-03-18 Matthew Groves replying to James Wynn #

Argues that The Book of the Short Sun is thematically about returning, so the Whorl has come back to Urth, supported by the corn genetics and hyacinth symbols at the book's end. Responds to James Wynn's Blue/Green-Urth theory, noting it is more convincing than the idea that resurrected Horn is a Neighbor, which he finds lacks textual support.
supports · myth-and-structure · close-textual-reading · theories: The Whorl has returned to Urth; Urth is Blue or Green; Resurrected Horn is a Neighbor

The best argument that Urth is Blue (or Green), to my mind, is the thematic argument, which Marc made long ago, that The Book of the Short Sun is all about returning. I can't seem to dig up Marc's old post... Together with the corn genetics symbol and the hyacinth symbol at the end, I'm afraid Marc has me convinced that the Whorl has returned whence it came. (But Blue/Green still isn't St. Anne/St. Croix.) I'm much more convinced of this than I am that resurrected Horn is a Neighbor--a proposition I've never been able to find much textual support for. But I'll be reading SS later this year with both ideas in mind.

2009-03-18 Son of Witz replying to Jordon Flato #

Argues that Short Sun is set on Blue, the Whorl's third world, and that Return to the Whorl's three-whorl imagery makes that reading thematically strong despite hard SF objections. Also proposes that the Neighbors descend from the Green Man and that the Epitome's androgyny reflects a better, androgynous future humanity, citing Plato's Androgyns.
proposes · myth-and-structure · source-and-allusion · theories: Short Sun is set on Blue, not Sol; Neighbors descend from the Green Man; Epitome's androgyny signals a better humanity

Dang. I didn't think of that.

I'm cool with the dream/time travel trip. I threw it out there to see if there were strong objections to that idea.

I don't think SS=Sol would be a pointless flourish.
"I've drawn the three whorls again"
"Return to the Whorl"
Hoof sees Green in the sky. He's looked at Green his whole life. If it wasn't Green, wouldn't he make some sort of comment about a change. I think that if I just "traveled to another whorl, and looked up and saw a moon that was different than my home planet's Lune, I wouldn't call it Lune.

I'm not convinced, I just think the Three Whorls, and the Return make A LOT of thematic sense. So much, in fact, that I'd consider two more Whorls in a book constantly talking about Three Whorls a pointless flourish.

The real problem, aside from hard SF moon distance/size tidal issues stuff people bring up against the idea, is that Blue has a lot of ruins of an ancient race, and very strange flora and fauna. Is it at all possible that The Whorl jumped sequential creations? don't know. I don't have a problem with the idea that The Whorl might have been out there thousands of Urth years before returning. You can't count on any of the cargo's timeframe ideas, can you?

but if Neighbors can be trees (I haven't really grokked Aramini's argument) couldn't that be descendant from the Green Man?

And every time I read about the Neighbors' anatomy, I was reminded of Plato's Androgyns. What were they called? they're like Two people in one. Some are are hetero/some homo pair ups. Anyway, a huge theme in New Sun seems to be this Androgyne quality in the epitome. Much was made of the Old Autarch's androgyny, and of course the true Epitome was Severian/Thecla/+++++. I think there is really something to this idea that a better humanity is truly androgynous.
Perhaps somewhere in Usha's future, these physical androgyns evolved, immigrated, or were engineered? Who knows.
And, hell, if that were a "fact" in the future, it might resonate all the way back to create Plato's Myth about them. :-D

~Witz

2009-03-18 Son of Witz #

Asks what hidden scheme Seawrack and the Mother might have in The Book of the Short Sun, and argues that the Mother, the brides of Abia and buried walls under the sea suggest Urth or Ushas is Blue. Raises the idea that there may be only three whorls rather than five, citing Horn and Silk's repeated drawing of the three whorls and a timeline problem with the Red Sun Whorl projection noted by Jordon.
questions · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Urth or Ushas is Blue; there are only three whorls

Somewhere I read a suggestion about Seawrack and the Mother having a hidden scheme in Short Sun. What is it? Where is it?

I REALLY don't get final bit about The Mother and the brides of Abia unless Urth is Blue. I appreciate all the arguments against that idea, and i'm not convinced, but this aquatic mother, and buried walls under the sea just really seem to scream out that Urth or Ushas is Blue. Or are we to suppose that a Sea Mother Goddess is inherent on watery worlds? It just seems so much more tidy if there are only 3 whorls and not 5. And Horn/Silk keeps saying "I've drawn the three whorls again" Why would there be two additional whorls. Jordon pointed out to me that if Ushas is Blue, then the projection of Horn and Co to the Red Sun Whorl is not in simultaneous timeline to Horn's, which is maybe problematic, though maybe not. we've scene time travel, and astral projection, why not both at once?

~witz
