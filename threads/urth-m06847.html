Themes: Catholic faith and sacraments, Christ figures and messiahs

2008-11-20 David Stockhoff #

Proposes that Wolfe deliberately built Severian as a Christ figure who embodies everything Christ's followers cannot be, modeled on Tertullian's severe Christian prohibitions. Argues the choice reflects a question of temporal versus spiritual authority, clarifying his earlier remark that Severian's sin and repugnance miss the point.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theology-and-religion · theories: Severian constructed as everything Christ's followers cannot be; Severian built against Tertullian's prohibitions

Let me clarify my thought.

I meant that it's almost as though Wolfe read Tertullian (or the debate he was part of) and and responded to him by deliberately constructing a character according to those severe Christian prohibitions /as though they were precise specifications/: a Christ figure who is everything Christ's followers cannot be. Why these specifications and not others?

I think this goes beyond "sin" and repugnance, neither of which is mentioned here. It's a question of temporal authority vs spiritual authority, at least. But we knew that.
