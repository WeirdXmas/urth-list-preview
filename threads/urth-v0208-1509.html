The Ascian's Shadow

The thread: Participants debated whether the title's 'shadow of the torturer' and the shadowless Ascians mean the Ascians are excluded from Severian's salvation. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued the shadow falls only on the Commonwealth-era torturers' targets and excludes the Ascians, while Matthew Malthouse and Adam Stephanides countered that the New Sun's cataclysm cannot be selective and that Ascians are touched by it. James Wynn read the shadow motif as tied to Severian as New Sun and held that the Ascians lose their claim once the sun overcomes darkness. Adam Stephanides also linked the Ascian's sayings to criticism of the Autarch's closed roads. The thread ended in impasse, with Dan'l conceding only that some Ascians are touched and the others still divided on who is saved.

2002-08-01 James Wynn #

Argues that the shadow motif in The Book of the New Sun is tied to Severian as the New Sun, so that the Ascians, who cast no shadow, reject the new order and serve Erebus. Grounds this in the torturers' ritual of casting one's shadow on the condemned in The Sword of the Lictor and in the title The Shadow of the Torturer, and responds to a earlier post's point about the title's article.
proposes · myth-and-structure · close-textual-reading · theories: Ascians are shadowless because they reject the New Sun; Shadows symbolize the New Sun and his new order

That is an -excellent-- point! Except the title is not "The Shadow of **A** Torturer", it is "The Shadow of THE Torturer".

Is it "The Sword of the Lictor" where Severian describes the proper procedure in a public execution as requiring that he cast his shadow on the condemned (IIRC)? All Commonwealth torturers follow that ritual. All beings on Urth cast shadows - even the Ascians and their torturers. Only for Severian does his interposition before the Sun constitute a symbol.

"Shadows" in tBotNS represent the NEW SUN and the new order and future it brings. Only Severian is affirmed by the sun symbol and only he --the NEW Sun-- in coming ordinally and chronologically after the old Sun, brings death (in his shadow) as well as life (ie. All the people killed in the Deluge). That is Erebus' and Abia's hold on the New Sun's enemies - that the New Sun will destroy everything.

So.... The Ascians are shadowless. They repudiate the "shadow" (i.e. the destruction and change) of the New Sun, thus they serve (i.e. are "under") Erebus (the TOTAL Darkness of Death).

-- Crush

2002-08-01 Matthew Malthouse #

Questions whether Ash's remark about the Ascians in The Citadel of the Autarch, chapter 16, fits the book's shadow imagery, since the Ascians seem to have torturers too despite their supposed perfection. Quotes Ash's line about the men without shadows and their home north of the waist of Urth, and notes the apparent irony against the rule of the Group of Seventeen.
questions · close-textual-reading

I can't find the thread this subject came up in a day or two ago but I chanced upon the matter last evening.

TCotA Ch 16. Ash is speaking to Severian:

"The men without shadows..."

"You call your enemies Ascians, which of course is not what they call themselves, because your fathers believed that they came from the waist of Urth, where the sun is precisely overhead at noon. The truth is that their home is much farther north."

As an allusion to the shadow of the torturer it seems inapt or perhaps ironic: the Ascians seem to have no lack of tortuers despite the "perfection" of the rule of the Group of Seventeen.

Matthew

If a few people survived the destruction of the Commonwealth, isn't it probable that a few Ascians would also have survived? OTOH, if "the shadow of the torturer" is the death and destruction Severian brings, the Ascians certainly are touched by it.

2002-08-01 Adam Stephanides #

Argues that the Ascian's sayings in The Book of the New Sun echo shadow imagery and criticize the Autarch's closing of roads. Responds to James Wynn's shadow reading by citing the Ascian's 'sun behind Urth' remark and women's indifference to husbands, and suggests the storytelling paragraph comments on Wolfe's reception.
supports · close-textual-reading · author-and-biography · theories: Ascian's hands-blood-veins saying criticizes road closures; Storytelling paragraph is Wolfe's comment on reception

One of the Ascian's sayings also alludes to shadow imagery: "Behind everything some further thing is found forever; thus the tree behind the bird, stone beneath soil, the sun behind Urth." (ch. XI; p. 80, Timescape, ed.) This can't be an accident on Wolfe's part, though I don't know what its significance is.

Some of the Ascian's other remarks also seem to have a wider significance:

"How are the hands nourished? By the blood. How does the blood reach the hands? By the veins. If the veins are closed, the hands will rot away," (Ibid.) which Foila interprets as a reference to roads; whether intentional on the Ascian's part or not, this can be taken as a criticism of the Autarch's policy of closing the roads.

"[A] brave woman desires children, and not husbands." (ch. V, p. 33) Sure enough, very few of the women we meet in TBotS desire husbands; even Foila doesn't seem that eager to marry, despite organizing the storytelling contest herself.

The longish paragraph which Foila interprets as meaning that the stories by the skill of their telling, not their content, may be Wolfe's ironic comment on his own reception.

--Adam

2002-08-02 James Wynn #

Argues that the Ascians, though they are allied with Erebus, the darkness of death, lose their shadowlessness and their claim once the New Sun overcomes darkness after the Deluge. Responds to Matthew Malthouse's point that the Ascians' repudiation avails nothing, and draws a theological aside about whether afterlife beliefs shape the afterlife.
disputes · theology-and-religion · theories: Ascians lose shadowlessness after the Deluge

True. But they ARE in league with Erebus ("the darkness of Death" - that is the literal definition of the word). The fact that the New Sun eventually overcomes the darkness does change with whom they through their lot.

After the Deluge, there is no more Ascia and the Ascians are no longer "shadowless". And if Erebus had won, there would be no New Sun and the Concilliator's title and *declared* destiny would have been academic.

2002-08-02 Matthew Malthouse #

Argues that the Autarch's policy of deliberately fostering stagnation in the Commonwealth does not serve the goal of humankind returning to the stars with some "wholeness", since the Ascian's progress will not achieve that aim. Responds to Adam Stephanides and James Wynn's discussion of the Ascian's sayings by questioning how the Autarch's stagnation supports the wholeness he describes.
disputes · close-textual-reading

It may be so. The Autarch in explaining to Severian says that the policy is bad, deliberately fostering stagnation. Yet the implication is that the Ascian's progress is a chimera (it may be explicit, I haven't the book with me today).

The Autarch speaks of human kind going again to the stars when it can carry with it some "wholeness". The Ascian's progress will not achieve this aim but I don't see how the stagnation of the Commonweatlth fosters it.

Matthew
