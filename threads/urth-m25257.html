The thread: The thread opened as a link-sharing post about R.A. Lafferty's comeback, unrelated to Wolfe, and then Auspex Rex asked whether Flann O'Brien's The Third Policeman could illuminate the third border-guard figure in The Land Across. Marc Aramini disputed the resemblance, arguing the novel links instead to Beckett, Lafferty and At Swim-Two-Birds through phantasmagoric mistaken identity, and contrasting Wolfe's rigorous logic with Lafferty's dream logic. David Stockhoff proposed that Wolfe's ghost narrator might echo O'Brien's dead narrator, admitting he had not read The Land Across. The replies from Antonin Scriabin and entonio offered general praise for O'Brien without engaging the textual parallels, so the discussion ended without resolution.
  • Auspex Rex: The Third Policeman may help read the third figures in The Land Across
  • Marc Aramini: The Third Policeman resembles Beckett and Lafferty more than Wolfe, and Wolfe's logic is rigorous where Lafferty's is dreamlike
  • David Stockhoff: The Land Across may draw on The Third Policeman's ghost narrator and unaware dead, though he has not read the Wolfe novel
  • Antonin Scriabin: Flann O'Brien is a genius for metafictional play, judged from At Swim-Two-Birds
  • entonio: Readers should try O'Brien's novels, which are hilarious and range widely under his pen names

Themes: Ghosts, dreams and visions, Doubles, twins and clones, Unreliable narration

2014-09-14 Marc Aramini replying to Auspex Rex #

Argues that The Third Policeman does not much resemble Wolfe's The Land Across, linking it instead to Beckett, Lafferty, At Swim-Two-Birds and Lanark through shared phantasmagoric mistaken identity and a blurred living-dead state. Grounds this in recollection of the novel and in the contrast between Wolfe's rigorous logical structure and Lafferty's dream logic.
disputes · genre-and-craft · source-and-allusion

I read it years and years ago ... didn't really remind me of Wolfe at all. Definitely reminded me of a bit of Beckett's Molloy trilogy/Waiting for Godot and RA Lafferty (though much closer is At Swim Two Bird's, with its very similar parallels to the Lafferty canon in The Devil is Dead, but it could just be the "Irishness" of it all.) Maybe I will read it again some day. I am thinking there are parts of Lanark that resonate with The Third Policeman as well. There is this sense of phantasmagoric mistaken identity and a blurring between the living and the dead held forever in a holding pattern that all those works seem to have in common.

I think Wolfe and Lafferty are actually very different, with Wolfe being more rigorously logical in structure and Lafferty's stuff often not adhering to strict ordering - more like dream logic.
