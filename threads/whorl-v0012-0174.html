2001whorlThe Book of the Long SunAlice Turner

2001-02-10 Alice Turner #

Argues that the godling in The Book of the Long Sun seems senseless, too large for a resource-starved environment, technically awkward and novelistically useless. Responds to Henry Kaiser's complaint about the godling's size and lack of explanation, and recounts Mantis's reply about the super-taluses.
supports · chronology-and-plot-logic

This bothered me more than anything else in the book (and there were other things that did too--I'm less happy with this book than some of the rest of you seem to be). The godling seemed absolutely *senseless* to me--way too big for an environment rapidly running out of resources, technically totally awkward and novelistically sort of useless too. I mentioned my dismay to mantis and also, as an aside, that after the elaborate preparation for super-taluses at the end of BotLS, I was disappointed that we never got to see them. And he replied along the lines of, What makes you think we didn't? This may have been a playful answer--but the godlings seem so wrong as "meat animals" that I throw it out as something to think about.

-alga
