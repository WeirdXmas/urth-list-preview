Dead Languages; Literally, "Apu-Punchau"?

Themes: Myth and folklore sources, Time loops and causality

1999-04-01 Mitchell A. Bailey #

Asks whether the Quechua name Apu-Punchau in The Book of the New Sun is a literal borrowing or a substitute for a future language, as Latin substitutes for a dead tongue. Grounds the question in the Quechua meaning 'Lord Sun-God' and a glossary the poster owns, and notes Thea's remark that English is amusingly ambiguous as a dead language.
questions · source-and-allusion · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Quechua use is a substitute for a future language; Severian travels back to pre-Inca times

I was hip from the first with the fact that Latin was used in BNS as a stand-in for some language which to Severian was "dead", but from our perspective probably has not even come into existence yet.

Until some points made in the recent "Thorn" discussions, however, it never occured to me to question the literal authenticity of the name "Apu-Punchau". The name is Quechua, the tongue of the Incas of Peru, and means roughly "Lord Sun-God". I'm not a Quechua scholar, but I am interested enough to own a book which includes a Quechua glossary.

I'm wondering if the use of Quechua is a substitution of the same sort for some future autochthonous language, or whether Sev actually traveled back in time to live among the pre-imperial Inca, c. 1200 AD?

For what it's worth, Chatelaine Thea thought English, a dead language remembered by a few scholars in her time, was amusingly ambiguous.

1999-04-09 C R Culver #

Proposes that Severian traveled back in time to 1200 AD South America, since the ending of Urth and the Quechua usage point to a pre-20th-century setting. Responds to a remark about the ambiguity of 'present' in Thea's speech and suggests a 13th-century total eclipse may explain the eclipse in the ending.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Severian traveled back to 1200 AD South America; Quechua use substitutes for a future autochthonous language

<< For what it's worth, Chatelaine Thea thought English, a dead language remembered by a few scholars in her time, was amusingly ambiguous. >>

Yes, she remarked that she didn't know how a language where "present" had two meanings ("now" and "gift") could endure such confusion.

<<I'm wondering if the use of Quechua is a substitution of the same sort for some future autochthonous language, or whether Sev actually traveled back in time to live among the pre-imperial Inca, c. 1200 AD?>>

The events at the end of _Urth_ mystify me even after I have finished my third reading of the BotNS. However, I thought it was pretty clear that Sev did go to 1200 AD South America, he has spoken on the time being that of the dawn of man, so it was certainly pre-20th century. Regarding his eclipse, wasn't there an eclipse during the 13th century that was total over much of the world? It rings a bell.

- Christopher R. Culver <[email]>
