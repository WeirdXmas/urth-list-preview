The thread: Marc Aramini proposed that the oaf in Short Story 13 is the human child exchanged for the elf's changeling, citing an etymology of 'oaf' from changeling lore and drawing a parallel with Mary and Christ's immaculate conception. In a later post he argued that the child in 'The Changeling' is not Joseph's son but a being God placed in Maria's womb. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes disputed the Mary idea, arguing that Catholic and mainstream Christian doctrine holds that Mary herself bore the divine child, not a substitute. The thread ends with that objection unanswered in the cards, so it stands as an impasse.
  • Marc Aramini: the oaf is the swapped human child, and Mary received a changeling-like substitute as in the immaculate conception
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: doctrine holds Mary bore Christ directly, so a changeling substitution contradicts it

2012-04-05 Marc Aramini #

Proposes that the oaf in 'Short Story 13' is the human child rather than the elf's changeling, reading the Lil Abner character Peter Palmer as a good exchange. Grounds this in an etymology of 'oaf' from elf-changeling lore, drawn from a World Wide Words quotation, and links the swap to the Mary and Christ immaculate-conception image.
proposes · source-and-allusion · allegory-and-typology · theories: The oaf in Short Story 13 is the human child; Mary received a good changeling in Christ

Lil Abner, played by Peter Palmer, born 1931, the real age of our narrator, is a notorious oaf, and I thought this was interesting, from World Wide Words.

"An oaf is a stupid, boorish, or clumsy man.
There?s an intimate connection between oafs and elves. In ancient legend, elves weren?t the noble creatures portrayed in Tolkien?s stories but powerful and dangerous supernatural beings who were more likely to harm humans than to help them. Their name says so: it comes from an ancient Germanic term for a nightmare, a close relative of the first element of the modern German Albdruck with the same sense. Among other nasty habits, elves were thought to bring humans bad dreams and to steal their children, leaving CHANGELINGS in their place.

It?s from that belief that oaf first appeared in English, in the seventeenth century. Originally an oaf was an elf?s child, one that had been LEFT IN A POOR EXCHANGE for a stolen human one. In popular superstition, such children were assumed to be ugly or stupid."

Very interesting, huh? except the oaf here is probably the human one. This was a good exchange, and the religious imagery of a positive spritual immaculate conception conjoins with this - Mary got a good changeling in Christ.

2012-04-05 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes replying to Marc Aramini #

Disputes Marc Aramini's suggestion that Mary received a changeling in Christ, arguing that it contradicts Catholic and mainstream Christian belief that Mary herself bore the Theoanthropos. The post grounds this in the standard doctrine that the divine child was born directly to her rather than substituted for a normal infant.
disputes · theology-and-religion · theories: Mary bore a changeling Christ

I have to say that this would be utterly alien to anything a Catholic (or any mainstream Christian) would believe, as it implies that Mary gave birth to a "normal" child and the Theoanthropos was somehow substituted, whereas the standard belief is that Mary directly gave birth to the Theoanthropos.

2012-04-05 Marc Aramini replying to Marc Aramini #

Proposes that the child in Wolfe's short story "The Changeling" is not Joseph's biological son but a being God placed in the womb, linking this to the Immaculate Conception of Mary. Grounds the idea in the church named in the story and in the changeling's appearance very early in Maria's life.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · theology-and-religion · theories: the changeling is a child God placed in the womb

I mean this in the loosest possible way - he's not Joseph's biological child but something more mysterious God swooped in the womb. Let's wipe it out then - but we should consider the idea of the immaculate conception of Mary since it is the name of the church and the changeling appears very early in Maria's life.
