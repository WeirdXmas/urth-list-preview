2002-09-08 Bratt Ian #

Corrects Roy Lackey's claim that a fusion generator exploding would spread radioactivity, explaining that fusion produces far less radioactive emission than fission. Supports the point by describing fission versus fusion and noting that commercial fusion generators remain science fiction, while joking about the engineers.
disputes · genre-and-craft

A quick technical point: Roy, you seem to be confusing fission and fusion. Fission, what powers modern power plants, is the process of massive nuclei breaking apart and releasing energy. Radioactivity is a natural byproduct of this process. Fusion, however, is the process of lighter nuclei fusing together to make a more massive nuclei. Large amounts of energy are released during fusion, but with significantly less radioactive emissions (on order of 10^9 less than fission).

http://wwwofe.er.doe.gov/More_HTML/Comparison_w_fission.GIF

Unfortunately for society, commercially available fusion generators are still science fiction. I don't think those "fool engineers" would appreciate you being so trigger happy :)

Cheers,
Ian

2002-09-09 Andy Robertson #

Argues that chems' fusion generators would create a neutron flux making surrounding materials radioactive, so Hyacinth is doubtful as a chem or chem with a bio shell. Responds to Roy C. Lackey's quoted complaint about the azoth wrecking a fusion and the soldier dying, reasoning from the physics of the generator and its radiation's short range.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Hyacinth is not a chem

But:: Fusion would create a neutron flux which would turn many of the ordinary elements the generator was composed of into radioactive isotopes. Particularly if the generator had been running for hundreds of years.

Another reason why I doubt Hyacinth was a chem or a chem with a bio shell - the radiation would be bad for living flesh acually in contact with the chem, though it is short range and would not hurt people in the same room (much).

hartshorn

2002-09-09 Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that a breached chem fusion chamber in Wolfe's Long Sun books could still release dangerous isotopes, so the danger is not solely a matter of fusion being clean. Grounds this in Dr. Crane's remark after he and Silk are pulled from the lake, and in the fact that Crane and Silk survived a chem blow-up that would have killed them if it were nuclear.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Chem explosions release isotopes like fission does

Yes, and that's all very well when the fusion chamber remains intact; but when it is breached? When the plasma hits the fan, so to speak, what then? Why was Dr. Crane, when he and Silk were pulled from the lake, concerned to say:

"But we've gotten a thorough washing, and that's good. Lots of isotopes released when a chem blows." (LS2, 13)

I hadn't supposed that when chems exploded it was comparable, in any way, to a present-day nuclear device exploding; had it been, Crane and Silk would have died in the room where and when Lemur did, and isotopes would never have been an issue.

-Roy
