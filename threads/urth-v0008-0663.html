Mellow yellow pharmicon (remember?!)

Urth list · 3 messages · 1998-03-25 to 1998-03-26

1998urthThe Book of the New SunC R CulverAlice TurnerMichael Straight
The thread: Readers asked why the yellow of the pharmacon-related characters in The Book of the New Sun seems so strong, and whether it points to Hare Krishna or Buddhism. Alice Turner argued from Bangkok monks' saffron robes that Buddhism is the source and suggested the Claw's influence explains Thecla's presence, while C R Culver built a fuller case for Mahayana Buddhist robe colours and raised questions about Ossipago, Sidero, and whether the Hierodules can heal Jonas. Michael Straight proposed that Severian's perfect memory makes Thecla feel like continuity rather than stored files, contrasting Vodalus's weaker drug. The thread ends with three posts and no recorded agreement, so the question of the yellow stays open.
  • Alice Turner: yellow signals Buddhism, not Hare Krishna; Claw's influence may explain Thecla
  • C R Culver: robe colours derive from Mahayana Buddhism; Jonas's Korean hints a near-modern time
  • Michael Straight: Severian's perfect memory makes Thecla feel like continuity, not stored files

Themes: Memory and forgetting, Gods, angels and the Increate, Gnosticism, heresy and other theologies

Argues that the saffron-yellow robes of Buddhist monks in Bangkok show that the yellow of the pharmacon-related characters in The Book of the New Sun refers to Buddhism rather than to the Hare Krishna cult. Dan,

I lived in Bangkok for a time, and saw Buddhist monks in their saffron yellow robes daily. In daily life, at least among English-speakers (and TBOTNS is written in English), the terms were used interchangeably. Certainly no one referred to the robes as orange, and as they became well worn (monks are poor) they faded. It is far more likely that the robes refer back to Buddhism than to the odd American Hare Krishna cult.

1998-03-25 C R Culver #

Argues that the robes of the Hare Krishna-like figures in The Book of the New Sun derive from Mahayana Buddhism, noting saffron and black robe colours and the Buddha's rag robes. Also reasons that Jonas's knowledge of Korean suggests he comes from a time not far from modern Korea, and asks whether Ossipago and Sidero are surviving relics of a past science and whether the Hierodules can heal Jonas.
proposes · source-and-allusion · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Robes derive from Mahayana Buddhism; Jonas comes from near modern Korea; Ossipago and Sidero are relics of past science

Probably they go back to Buddhism. According to Mahayana Buddhism, the Buddha made his robes from the cloth strewn around the Ganges, funeral wrappings, menstural napkins, and all that stuff. They were mostly saffron, I imagine. Apply whatever symbolism you want from that to the BotNS, if you'd like. Interestingly enough, Mahayana is the vehicle of Buddhism in which the robes of the modern-day followers are *not* saffron, in my experience. Pure Land and Zen robes are black.

Careful there. Jonas knew of Korea and the Korean language (he could tell KLS's nationality from his name), so he comes from a time not too remote from modern Korea because a significant amount of linguistic drift has not occurred.

Ossipago and Sidero worry me. I wonder if they are relics from a past science, kept in repair but no longer made (like the fliers), or if they are still produced. Do the Hierodules still have the ability to heal Jonas?

1998-03-26 Michael Straight #

Argues that Severian's perfect memory could make Thecla's presence feel like continuity with her self rather than stored files, comparing it to a host and visitor struggle in a telepathy story. Responds to Alice Turner's question about Thecla's strong presence by contrasting Vodalus's weaker alzabo-like drug with Severian's always-loaded memory, citing Larry Niven's World of Ptavvs.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian's perfect memory makes Thecla's personality continuous with his; Alzabo drug strength differs between Vodalus and the autarchs

It depends on how the drug works. And what it's like to have a memory like Severian's. And whether you can draw a sharp line between memory and self.

If Severian's memory is more analogous to a perfect hard-drive, then I agree that simply having Thecla's "files" on his hard drive would not be sufficient to account for the way Severian seems to almost become part-Thecla.

But imagine instead that it's more like what Larry Niven describes in _World of Ptavvs_, where when a telepath reads a person's mind, the two personalities struggle within the brain to deterine which is the "host" and which is the "visitor," and if the visitor's personality is strong enough, the brain may end up convinced that the visitor is actually it's real, host, personality, even after the telepathic link is broken. A poor and totally unjust description of Niven, but it's the best I can do. Read the book instead, it's one of Niven's better ones, IMHO.

Anyway, imagine that having a perfect memory is not just having information at hand, but having a greater degree of continuity with your past self--since you can remember exactly how you felt when you were six, and not just project back, you might even be able to get a sense of how your six-year-old self would have thought/felt about current events.

For Vodalus, et. al., with their weaker version of the alzebo drug, they spend a few minutes where their memories and personality become intermixed with Thecla, but then the experience becomes a memory, subject to degredation, only imperfectly recalled.

For Severian, however, remembering the experience perfectly may be indistinguishable from having the experience. He doesn't just have Thecla's files, he's got her "operating system," and he doesn't just have it on a hard drive, accessible when he chooses, but always loaded in RAM.

The autarch alzebo drug is stronger, so the autarchs never quite forget, (or never quite stop having--is there a difference?) the experience of communion with earlier autarchs.

-Rostrum
