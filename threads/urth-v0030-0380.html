2001-05-06 Michael Straight #

Argues that Pig's phonetic dialect is a cheap device that makes banal lines seem profound, and that in plain English Pig's character amounts to fawning over Silk|Horn and weeping over his eyes. Suggests the last quoted line probably alludes to Cain and Abel, and answers a poster who found Jane Doe's speech easier to follow than Pig's, comparing the effect to Douglas Adams's Sirius Cybernetics Corporation.
disputes · source-and-allusion · theories: Pig's final dialect line alludes to Cain and Abel; Pig's dialect is a cheap device for banal lines

Need I point out this last bit is probably a reference to Cain and Abel?

I hate to say it, loving Wolfe as I do, but this whole dialect thing strikes me as a cheap way of making banal stuff seem more profound. It's as if the satisfaction you get from working out what the heck people are saying is supposed to make up for the fact that what they're saying isn't all that interesting (a strategy swiped from Douglas Adams's Sirius Cybernetics Corporation).

Put all of Pig's lines in plain English, and I suspect it would be painfully obvious that, as someone else pointed out, there's not much more to his character than fawning over Silk|Horn and crying about his eyes.

-Rostrum
