How many acts?

Urth list · 1 messages · 1997-11-22 to 1997-11-22

1997urthCharles Dye

Themes: Stories within stories

1997-11-22 Charles Dye #

Argues that the play-within-the-novel in The Book of the New Sun has a Shakespearean quality, with recycled actors, mistaken identity, and a clever fool, and that its act breaks are marked by rhyming couplets. Counts act breaks from stage directions, suggesting the play is incomplete and that a seventh act is missing and the sixth may be truncated, citing Severian's final line as too short and rhymed.
proposes · close-textual-reading · genre-and-craft · theories: The inset play is cut off and missing a seventh act

What always strikes me about this play (I guess I was reading Tom Swift when the teacher covered Persian creation myths) is a sort of pervasive Shakespearean quality. Characters are constantly entering and departing as the actors are recycled. Comedy by mistaken identity, plus we get at least one fool who demonstrates his foolishness through a torrent of clever wordplay. And the ends of acts are marked by rhyming couplets.

Not only are we missing the seventh act, but we can't be sure we've seen all of the sixth. Look at the last line (Severian's, of course.) It rhymes and it scans, but it's too short. Lupine ambiguity ....
