2003-03-04 James Jordan #

Proposes that the spelling "Werwolf" drops both e's from "Were-Wolfe" as a deliberate hint that the story's hero is a version of Wolfe himself. Builds on Sepia's remark that the spelling signals a missing e, extending it to Wolfe's other lupine stories that are 'about' him.
proposes · author-and-biography · source-and-allusion · theories: Werwolf hints the hero is Wolfe himself

Ah, that may be on the mark. We might have read "Were-Wolfe," which would be in character for all of Wolfe's lupine stories, which are "about" him one way or another. So dropping both "e"s is a hint, of some sort. Maybe to call attention to it, as you say, so that we get the idea the the Hero is a version of Wolfe himself.
