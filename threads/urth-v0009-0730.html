Corrects a earlier post's mythology: the sun-chariot son is Phaethon, not Phaedrus, with Helios as father rather than Apollo, and the name meaning "shining." Cites the poster's references (Graves) and suggests Phaedrus in Zen might have a philological usage meaning "bright one."

No, that is Phaethon (umlaut over the e). His name means "shining" (Graves), and his father is Helios (the sun) rather than Apollo (brightness of day). Phaedra means the same: "bright one." There do not seem to be, in my references, a genuine mythological Phadria or a Phaedrus. But I think that "Zen" (a book I liked a lot at a certain time in my life) may have found a legitimate phililogical usage, also meaning "bright one," or something like it in Phaedrus.
