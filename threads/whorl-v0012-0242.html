2001whorlThe Book of the Long SunDave Lebling

2001-02-15 Dave Lebling #

Argues that Silk's wounds may have been caused by an inhumi attack rather than a suicide attempt, and suggests the inhumi might have killed Hyacinth after an earlier illness. Responds to Adam Stephanides and Mr. Borski, who had doubted the suicide explanation, by offering the inhumi as another possible attacker while still favoring suicide.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Inhumi attacked Silk and killed Hyacinth

<<From: Adam Stephanides <[email]>
vizcacha may concede on the details, but I'm not about to. >>

Which is not to say I agree with Mr. Borski, just that he's right that Silk's wounds don't fit well as suicide attempt wounds. The picture is sufficiently murky that I'm no longer 100% convinced it was a suicide attempt.

Another possible idea for an attacker, if there was an attacker, is the inhumi. Silk's wounds are consistent with an inhumi attack. Perhaps Hyacinth's illness was due to inhumi attack, and they finally killed her.

I want to get all the possibilities out there, in spite of still believing that a suicide attempt is the most likely explanation.

Dave Lebling
aka vizcacha
