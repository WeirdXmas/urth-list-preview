hyle and Wolfe

Urth list · 3 messages · 2013-11-10 to 2013-11-10

2013urthMarc Aramini
The thread: Marc Aramini posted three times on Wolfe's use of hyle and trees. He argued that the recurring trees have a philosophical basis in the Greek word hyle, meaning wood and then matter, tying them to transformation, and cited several works as evidence. He then argued that Wolfe's interest in names, and his marriage to a woman named Rosemary, would lead him to play with names, citing an essay in Castle of Days. Finally he read paper and the trees that become it in the Dark of the June stories as a durable landscape housing the author's words, linked to Nailer's return. No other participants replied in the cards, so the thread ends with Aramini's unanswered claims.

  • Marc Aramini: Wolfe likely plays with names in his fiction, given his interest in names and his wife Rosemary
  • Marc Aramini: paper and its trees in the Dark of the June stories are a durable landscape housing the author's words

2013-11-10 Marc Aramini #

Argues that Wolfe's recurring interest in trees has a philosophical basis in the Greek word hyle, which Aristotle adapted from 'wood' to mean matter, linking it to transformation. Supports this by citing trees in Short Sun, the sessile aborigines in Remembrance to Come, Nailer's tree in The Death of Hyle, and the 'twelve kinds of dead' passage in Forlesen.
proposes · source-and-allusion · allegory-and-typology · theories: Wolfe's trees reflect hyle meaning wood and matter; Trees in Wolfe transform men through reversed eucharist

Examining Wolfe's work as a whole?has often made me wonder exactly why he seems so in love with the transformative presence of trees - you are aware of my cannibal reversed eucharist scheme for Short Sun where trees eat men and become flesh, you are aware of the transformed sessile mature life cycle in the aborigines in the form of those parent trees (with their "predendritic" society), and perhaps we have seen it in the opening paragraph of Remembrance to Come where leaves surround our displaced narrator's head, but I think I finally have some philosophical justification. ?
When I was looking over the Death of Hyle, when Nailer becomes displaced from nature, "His tree spoke to me of the winds, and the different kinds of rains they blow" ... and then there is this from Forlesen, in which there are "12 kinds of dead" : "those who become new gods, for whom new universes are born.??... those who find in their graves their mother?s wombs and in one life circle forever.? Ninth ghosts.? Tenth those born again as me in their grandson?s time.? Eleventh those who return as beasts or trees.? And last those who sleep."
?
Besides the obvious importance of a tree in Christianity, becoming the basis for the fall of man in its fruit and also its redemption when Jesus is sacrificed on wood to undo that, there is this interesting definition of "Hyle":??? ?refers to matter or stuff. It can also be the material
cause underlying a change in Aristotelian philosophy. The Greeks originally had no word for matter in general, as opposed to raw material suitable for some specific purpose or other, so Aristotle adapted the word for 'wood' to this purpose.?
?
The source of transformation and the very word for matter itself is based on the Greek word for wood - I think this appealed to Wolfe's symbolic sensibilities in light of the transformative power of the tree in Christianity: from preternatural grace to fallen to redeemed through the actions taken concerning living and dead wood.

Argues that Wolfe, being interested in names and married to a woman named Rosemary, would likely play with names in his own fiction. Points to an essay in Castle of Days where Wolfe discusses the meanings of many authors' names as one example of that preoccupation.

2013-11-10 Marc Aramini replying to Marc Aramini #

Argues that paper, the trees that become it, and the newspaper picture in the opening paragraph of the Dark of the June stories matter thematically, as a durable landscape housing the author's words. Grounds this in the room described at the story's start and in the idea that dying trees let ideas approach immortality, and links it to Harry Nailer's return in the text.
proposes · close-textual-reading · allegory-and-typology · theories: Paper and trees stand for the author's durable words

?When I do the write up for The Dark of the June stories, it has not escaped my attention that paper is particularly important to a novelist and in those stories from the very opening paragraph, where?the main character's?room could have sprung from the picture in a newspaper (and to the text Harry Nailer shall return), and it seems perhaps thematically important not only to those stories but to Wolfe in general that these trees create a?durable landscape to house the author's words, trees dying so the ideas might flourish and approach immortality.?
