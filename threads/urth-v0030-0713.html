The thread: Jerry Friedman opened a thread proposing a list of fictitious books that exist only inside Wolfe's fiction, to suggest to the Invisible Library catalog. Adam Stephanides noted an earlier archived discussion and an abandoned prior offer, while Michael Andre-Driussi and Hubert posted bibliographies and entries drawn from Bibliomen and other works, and Roy C. Lackey proposed more invented titles from the stories. Discussion remained mostly cataloguing, with Adam Stephanides flagging missing titles from an earlier list and Michael Andre-Driussi explaining it had been retyped from memory. The thread ended with Jerry Friedman compiling an annotated list and asking for corrections, so it closed by collection rather than argument.
  • Jerry Friedman: proposes and compiles a list of fictitious books existing only in Wolfe's works
  • Adam Stephanides: points to the prior archived discussion and supplies titles missing from the newer Bibliomen list
  • Michael Andre-Driussi: posts a bibliography of invented titles and admits the list was retyped from memory
  • Hubert: proposes catalog entries and asks whether Wolfe's use of the number seventeen is significant
  • Roy C. Lackey: argues several titles in the stories are invented rather than real

Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Stories within stories, Genre: science fiction, fantasy, both

2001-10-15 Jerry Friedman #

Introduces the poster and proposes compiling a list of fictitious books that exist only inside Wolfe's fiction, to suggest to the Invisible Library catalog's librarian. Offers two examples: The Wonders of Urth and Sky from The Book of the New Sun, and The Chrasmological Writings from the Long Sun and Short Sun books.
proposes

Hi, everyone! I've been on the Whorl List for a while, but I subscribed to this one to see what I've been missing and to mention the Invisible Library site <http://www.invisiblelibrary.com/ILCatalogf.htm>, a catalog of fictitious books. 2001-10-18 Hubert Rogers #

Proposes fictitious book entries for the Invisible Library catalog drawn from Wolfe's fiction, including The Book of the New Sun attributed to Blaithmaic with a green cloth folio. Notes that the book leans against a folio in green cloth and asks whether Wolfe's frequent use of the number seventeen is significant, also suggesting a subtitle for the Wonders of Urth and Sky.
proposes · close-textual-reading

And remember it 'leans against a folio in green cloth'

Title: The book of the New Sun
Pseudo-Author: Blaithmaic (or is that just part of the title?) Pseudo-Title: Lives of the Seventeen Megatherians
Comments: it's a folio in green cloth

n.b. isn't it strange that Wolfe likes using the number Seventeen so often?

And for the Wonders of Urth and Sky's comments you could put what it says on in the subtitle, "a Collection from Printed Sources of Universal Secrets of Such Age That Their Meaning Has Become Obscured of Time".

-rusty

Proposes that several book titles appearing in Wolfe's stories are invented rather than real, citing a title in "The Doctor of Death Island" and one in "Seven American Nights" from the collection. Offers these as likely further fictional books within Wolfe's fiction, with the poster hedging on their status.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Wolfe's inset book titles are fictional

I hope I don't get bit again, but I don't *think* these are "real" books. And I suspect that there are quite a few more candidates in Wolfe's fiction.

From TIODDAOSAOS:

In "The Doctor of Death Island" (p. 253) is THE DEATH OF LOVE by Kinglake, apparently a book about psychology.

In "Seven American Nights" (p. 383) is MYSTERY BEYOND THE SUN'S SETTING by Osman Aga, some sort of popular account of travels within the ruined interior of America.

-Roy
