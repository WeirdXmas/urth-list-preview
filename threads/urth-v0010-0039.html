Themes: Deep time and ruins

1998-05-01 Tony Ellis #

Disputes FunkMonkey's claim that Urth followed a cataclysm, arguing instead that Urth is so remotely far in the future that civilization simply faded out. Cites Wolfe's 'do-nothing future' remark and Jonas's phrase 'original prisoners from whom they are descended' to argue the antechamber inhabitants are not one homogeneous family descended from KLS.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Urth is a do-nothing future with no cataclysm; Antechamber inhabitants are not all descended from KLS

01/05/98
09:51 RE>Hello, and a rehash of kimleesoong

Cataclysm? What cataclysm? :-)
One of the many many things I've always liked about TBOTNS is that it _doesn't_ follow that well-worn path of "Earth: 500 years after the Great Meteor Storm", or "Earth: 800 years after the Nuclear Deluge", or whatever. We are at a time so remotely far into the future that civilization has just fizzled out, "not with a bang, but with a whimper".

Wolfe confirms this in the The Castle of the Otter, where he talks about Urth being the "do-nothing future", where humanity "stays at home... and waits for the money to run out."

Well, we're told that KLS is the prisoner from whom the family Jonas questions count their descent. That's not quite the same thing. The very fact that the discrete group of prisoners who question Jonas can be called a "family" tells us that the inhabitants of the antechamber aren't one homogenous body. If they were all descended from KLS, or even thought they were, wouldn't they all be one family? And notice that Jonas talks about "the original prisoners from whom they are descended", rather than "original prisoner".
