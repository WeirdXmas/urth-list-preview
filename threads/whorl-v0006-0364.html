Wolfe's Answer re Torturers' Guild -Reply

Whorl list · 3 messages · 1997-06-17 to 1997-06-18

1997whorlThe Book of the New SunWesley Parsons (7065)James Jordan
The thread: Posters debated whether Wolfe's answer about the Torturers' Guild makes Silk or Severian a member of the Order of Seekers for Truth and Penitence. Wesley Parsons first argued that church membership and its antichrist counterpart are mutually exclusive, so Silk, not Severian, is the true member, and questioned Nutria's loose reading of Wolfe's answer. He later backed the view that all men are torturers by pointing to Wolfe singling out Silk as a guild member. Jim Jordan held that both Silk and Severian belong to the Church in the broad sense, since the shadow antichurch lies within it, and cited Catholic teaching on a bad pope as an antichrist. The thread ends without resolution, with the disagreement over the Church's scope left open.
  • Wesley Parsons: Silk, not Severian, is the true member of the Order, since church and antichurch membership exclude each other
  • Wesley Parsons: Severian's 'all men are torturers' line, read literally, makes half the Whorl torturers, and Wolfe picked out Silk
  • Jim Jordan: Silk and Severian both belong to the Church in the broad sense, since the antichurch lies within it
  • Nutria: a broad reading of Wolfe's answer (cited, not posting in this thread)

Themes: Catholic faith and sacraments, Christ figures and messiahs, Gnosticism, heresy and other theologies

1997-06-17 Wesley Parsons (7065) #

Argues that Silk, not Severian, is the true member of the Order of Seekers for Truth and Penitence, since membership in a church and its antichrist counterpart are mutually exclusive. Responds to Nutria's broad reading of Wolfe's answer by calling it too loose, then asks whether Silk's pagan worship is equally anti-Christian and why Wolfe says 'Knowing Typhon as I do'.
disputes · close-textual-reading · allegory-and-typology · theories: Silk is a true member of the Order; Severian belongs to an antichrist copy of the Order; Typhon returns in Short Sun

<<14. "Is there a member (current, former, or future) of the Order of Seekers for Truth and Penitence on the Whorl?" I'm going to interpret this question in the broadest possible sense, since that seems to be what Parsons wants. Yes, there is. It is Silk.>>

Church, or the Catholic Church. Silk is a member of it in the proper sense. Severian starts as a member of a perverse antichrist copy of it. Thus, "in the broadest sense," both are members of it.>>

Nutria,

Thanks for you comment, but I don't follow your logic. Membership in a group usually disqualifies one from membership the group's "opposite." If you're NRA, you're unlikely to have signed up for Citizens Against Handguns. Same for Republicans/Democrats. Or citizenship in different countries (you lose your citizenship when you swear oath to another.) Granted that "opposite" groups share the same subject matter, for example, handguns, or politics, or religion. When you interpret "in the broadest sense" so _broadly_ <g>, it tends to lose any meaning. Wolfe typically does not speak this way.

<<Hmmm. The towers. Consider. The towers at the center of a city, with a graveyard nearby, are usually the Cathedral. Here we have Witches, Animal Torturers (Bear Tower), and Human Torturers. Boy! An anti-cathedral or what?! But as Wolfe has said, Severian is trying to grow out and away from his horrible beginnings. >>

Very interesting analysis. Isn't Silk's worship of pagan gods just as anti-christian as anything Severian did in the Matachin Tower? Neither of them starts out worshiping the Judaeo-Christian God, although both are arguably enlightened by Him later.

Different question: Why would Wolfe say, "Knowing Typhon as I do . . ." (Question 3). Typhon is not that sufficiently characterized, as Typhon in NS, or as Pas in LS, to merit such familiarity. Could it be that Typhon is a prominent character in Short Sun, and Wolfe has been inside his head these many months as he has written SS? And if Typhon is back, in the flesh, whose flesh has he got?

Wes

1997-06-18 James Jordan #

Argues that Silk and Severian both belong to the Church in the broad sense, since the shadow antichurch lies within it. Responds to Nutria by citing Catholic teaching that a bad pope is an antichrist, and by comparing Silk's parish and Severian's monastic order, then proposes tracing sacramental imagery linking the Towers to a cathedral.
supports · theology-and-religion · allegory-and-typology · theories: Silk and Severian both belong to the Church; The Church contains a shadow antichurch; The Towers connect to a cathedral

Well, I sure won't burn at the stake for my thinking here! But in Christian theology, both Church and Antichurch are the same place. Roman Catholic theologians will say that just as a good Pope is the Vicar of Christ, so a bad Pope is an antichrist. Another way of putting it is that the shadow anti-church exists alongside the true church. This is definitely taught in the Bible.
Now, how much of this is in Wolfe's mind? I don't know. But "Seekers of Truth and Penitence" is a perfectly good descriptive name for the Church. And, Severian's order is just like a monastic order in certain ways. Considering that both parish churches (Silk) and orders (Severian) exist within the Church, and that the Church can be more or less faithful, mixed with idolatry (Silk), even reversing her role (Severian) -- then "in the broadest sense" both are in the same institution.
What interests me now is to see if there are any more hints as to a connection between the Towers and a Cathedral. As you know, the early chapters of Shadow are loaded with sacramental imagery. When I have the time, I'd like to explore this a bit more -- unless someone else does so and posts it.

Nutricious Nutria
