Pinocchio vs. the Tin Man

Urth list · 5 messages · 2002-09-25 to 2002-09-25

2002urthThe Book of the New SunThe Book of the Long SunJerry FriedmanJames WynnRoy C. LackeyMichael Andre-DriussiMichael Straight
The thread: Michael Andre-Driussi opened by arguing that Jonas's wish to be repaired reverses the usual Pinocchio pattern, proposing that the Claw restored him as a reanimated corpse, and compared him with the Tin Man and the Steadfast Tin Soldier. Jerry Friedman disputed this, holding that Jonas wants to become fully robotic, perhaps taken over by the Jonas software, and cited Galatea, the Bicentennial Man and Severian's squirrel dream. Roy C. Lackey sided with Friedman, reading Jonas's loathing of the biological replacement parts and quoting chapter XVI, while James Wynn argued that chems naturally long to become human. Michael Straight supported Friedman's question and drew on Greg Egan's Diaspora. The posts stayed on the question of what Jonas wants and did not reach a single conclusion.
  • Michael Andre-Driussi: Jonas follows a Pinocchio path toward biological restoration, with the Claw restoring him in the reanimated corpse
  • Jerry Friedman: Jonas wants to become fully robotic, perhaps taken over by the Jonas software
  • Roy C. Lackey: Jonas loathed the biological parts, so his return for Jolenta means only after mechanical repair
  • James Wynn: chems, unlike gods, naturally want to become human, as Pinocchio, the Tin Man and Data show
  • Michael Straight: some would trade a frail body for a strong robot body, as Egan's Diaspora shows

Themes: Robots, androids and artificial life, Beasts and the animal in us, Power, rulers and politics

2002-09-25 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Argues that Jonas's wish to become repaired reverses the usual pattern, so he follows a Pinocchio path from non-biological to biological, and proposes that the Claw restored him in the reanimated corpse. Compares this with the Tin Man and the Steadfast Tin Soldier, responding to Matthew Weber's jibe that Jonas fits a Pinocchio-type change.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · source-and-allusion · theories: Claw drew Jonas software into reanimated corpse; Jonas follows Pinocchio scenario toward biological life

I'm not sure what is going on with Jonas, what he means by his intent to become repaired (presumably it means he wants to become fully biological so as to be better suited for Jolenta). I guess it all resolves this way: since Severian heard the utterance and saw Jonas go into the mirror, the Claw heard it too, so that later on at that point in Severian's life when he really needed a friend, yet all there was at hand was a corpse, then the Claw drew down the Jonas software from the borderland between Briah and Yesod, installed it in the reanimated corpse, and thereby solved two wishes with one snappy miracle.

In any event, the transition from non-biological to biological is the Pinocchio scenario. Which is interesting because we tend to talk about Jonas as being the posterboy for the opposite, the Tin Man of Oz (a biological made non-biological). Or Jonas as the Steadfast Tin Soldier (crippled; in love with a woman not of his type; swallowed by fish, delivered back to his first place, then cast into fiery dissolution).

=mantis=

2002-09-25 Jerry Friedman #

Disputes Michael Andre-Driussi's reading of Jonas's wish to be repaired, arguing Jonas wants to become fully robotic, perhaps taken over by the Jonas software, and compares Miles's identity recovery to Silk's. Cites Galatea, the Bicentennial Man, Severian's squirrel dream and the chemical nature of humans as parallels.
disputes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion · theories: Jonas wants to become fully robotic; Jonas software took over Jonas

Funny, that wasn't my impression at all--I thought he wanted to become fully robotic--though he is in love or something with Jolenta. Can we imagine a prosthetic-bearing bio in love with a chem wanting to become fully a chem?

Or in possibly more sinister style, the Jonas software was hanging around and took its opportunity.

Either way, do we see Miles going through a Silk-like recapture of his identity?

Not to mention Galatea, the Bicentennial Man, and Severian's brush- into-squirrel dream. Maybe above all, the amazing fact that we Homo sapiens work by the laws of physics and consequently chemistry; we're literally chems.

Where's that from?

Thanks for the comment on _Michaelmas_, by the way. I own that book, but I never made any of those connections!

Jerry Friedman

2002-09-25 Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that Jonas loathed the biological replacement parts he received after the crash, so his promise to return for Jolenta meant only after proper mechanical repair. Supports Jerry Friedman's reading by quoting the end of chapter XVI, where his shipmates can only repair him with biological material, and the image of him discarding the flesh hand.
supports · close-textual-reading · theories: Jonas loathed his biological replacement parts

Jerry Friedman quoting mantis:

I agree completely with Jerry and have said so before. Jonas loathed his bio replacement parts. When he said he would come back for Jolenta when he had been repaired, when he was sane and whole, he meant when proper repairs had been made and he was quit of his bio parts. The proof is on the last page of chapter XVI of CLAW:

"We crashed. It had been so long, on Urth, that there was no port when we returned, no dock. Afterward my hand was gone, and my face. My shipmates repaired me as well as they could, but there were no parts anymore, only biological material." With the steel hand I had always thought scarcely more than a hook, he picked up the hand of muscle and bone as a man might lift a bit of filth to cast it away."

All the more reason to wonder what he wanted with Jolenta.

-Roy

2002-09-25 James Wynn #

Argues that chems, unlike gods, naturally want to become human, citing Pinocchio, the Tin Man and Data as parallel longings for authenticity. Grounds this in Lemur and Kypris calling the Mainframe gods mere semblances, Jonas' and Potto's attitudes toward human parts, and Mr. Million's choice to become a robot.
proposes · close-textual-reading · myth-and-structure · theories: Chems naturally desire to become human, gods do not

Can we imagine a prosthetic-bearing bio in love with a chem wanting to become fully a chem?

Response:
It is not a two-way street. It is the most natural of inclinations to prefer things that are more authentic; to want to BE more authentic. Religious apologist have declared in once sense or another that the *proper* urge to worship the Divine comes from the knowledge that we are made in his image.

By the same token, Pinocchio, the Tin Man, and Data (of Star Trek) all want to become "real" not just the semblance of reality. Lemur disparaged the Mainframe gods as "ghosts" not because he believed in life after death but because he didn't - he would assert that they (and also himself, to his own dismay) were only a semblance of the "real" persons - actually Kypris suggests this understanding of herself as well.

So it IS logical that a chem would want to become human. It is NOT logical that a god would. The reasons for Jonas' disparagement of human parts and Potto's talus' revulsion toward human "filth" are another subject but it's not hard to see why they might feel that way even if they would become human if given the opportunity - and that one of the parallels is people who scorn "holy" relics.

Still, there IS Mr. Million (5HoC) who chose (with regret) to become a robot.

-- Crush

2002-09-25 Michael Straight #

Argues that some people would willingly trade a frail biological body for a strong, long-lived robot body, using Pinocchio-like comparisons with Severian's situation. Supports Jerry Friedman's question about whether a prosthetic-bearing character could want to become fully a chem, citing Greg Egan's Diaspora, where people are bio-modified, uploaded into Mainframes, or placed in robot bodies.
supports · genre-and-craft

Forget about the in love part, I think there are plenty of people who would choose a super-strong, nigh-immortal robot body over a frail biological one, even if one had to give up various human pleasures and expereiences.

Greg Egan's _Diaspora_ imagines a future in which some people are bio-modified, some are downloaded into and live in various "Mainframes," and some live with their minds downloaded into robot bodies.

So yes, some of us can imagine it.

-- M.
