2007-07-15 Nigel Price #

Proposes an explanation for the yellow water at the start of Seven American Nights: sand and mud carried to sea by rivers from a war-blasted American interior. Responds to Aaron Singleton's question about the yellow colouring and the offended American on board, grounding the idea in the novel's post-war devastation and loss of vegetation.
answers · close-textual-reading · theories: Yellow water is topsoil washed out by rivers

Aaron Singleton asked:

yellow
at the beginning of Seven American Nights? The American on board becomes very offended when Nadan points this out to him. Any help is greatly appreciated.<<

Doesn't sound like a stupid question to me.

I imagine that the yellow colouration is caused by sand and mud carried out into the ocean by rivers flowing from the American interior. America has been blasted by war and bombs which have destroyed nearly all the vegetation. No longer secured by the roots of trees and plants, the top soil has come loose and blown and washed away. Now the rivers are washing all this loose topsoil out into the sea, where it discolours the water.

Nigel
