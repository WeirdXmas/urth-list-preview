pig's dialect

Whorl list · 4 messages · 2001-02-12 to 2001-02-13

2001whorlThe Book of the Short Sunjames.kellarDave LeblingPeter Westlake
The thread: Readers debated the dialect of Pig in The Book of the Long Sun, with James Kellar arguing it is Scottish rather than Irish or Welsh and that Pig is a godling. Spectacled Bear agreed on Scottish speech but questioned the extra H sounds that make it hard to read aloud, and rejected the Tartaros-as-Pig theory because Pig regains his sight. Kellar later suggested the Hoof/Hide passages may have misread Horn's description of the accent and argued Pig was blind before attacking Silk. Dave Lebling called the dialect mild compared with Riddley Walker and Feersum Enjin, advising readers to mutter it aloud. The thread ended without resolution, with the Scottish reading broadly accepted and the blindness question left open.
  • james.kellar: Pig's dialect is Scottish, and Pig may be a godling
  • Spectacled Bear: Scottish speech, but the extra H sounds hurt readability; Pig regains his sight, so Tartaros-as-Pig fails
  • james.kellar: Hoof/Hide author may have misread Horn's account; Pig was blind before attacking Silk
  • Dave Lebling: the dialect is mild compared with harder works; read it aloud

Themes: Unreliable narration, Names and their meanings, Myth and folklore sources

2001-02-12 james.kellar #

Argues that Pig's dialect in The Book of the Long Sun is meant to be Scottish rather than Irish or Welsh, citing 'dinna fash' and a passage where Pig says 'wind' so it sounds like 'fiend'. Asks which part of Scotland Pig comes from and floats the idea that Pig is a godling, reading the chapter title as bearing on more than the last paragraph.
proposes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Pig's dialect is Scottish; Pig is a godling

hello all

there's no doubt whatsoever that Pig's dialect is intended to be Scottish, as opposed to Irish or Welsh.
'dinna fash' and other similar expressions are giveaways (to British ears, at least). I don't have the book in front of me, but the author even goes as far as to explain that when Pig says 'wind' it sounds like 'fiend'. Has anyone got an idea as to which part of Scotland he's from? It sounds reasonably old-fashioned to me, I think.

Pig as godling sounds like a plausible hypothesis - after all, the chapter's title could easily be read as referring to more than just the last paragraph, couldn't it?

gerbil

2001-02-12 Peter Westlake #

Questions why the character Pig's dialect adds extra H sounds, noting it makes his speech hard to read aloud, and agrees that Pig's speech is Scottish. Argues against the Tartaros-as-Pig theory because Pig regains his sight, as shown by his reaction to seeing where his new eye came from.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Pig is Tartaros as godling

Hello!

It is - *but*, why does he add all those H-s like a Cockney? It made Pig's speech tongue-twistingly difficult for me to read, even though I wasn't reading it aloud.

Very plausible. The trouble with the Tartaros idea, on the other hand, is that Pig gets his sight back, as indicated by his reaction when he sees where his new eye came from.

Spectacled Bear.

2001-02-12 james.kellar #

Proposes that the Hoof/Hide passages' author may have misread Horn/Silk's description of Pig's Scottish-sounding accent, and argues Pig had been blind for a while before attacking Silk. Responds to an earlier post questioning Pig's extra H sounds, and grounds the blindness claim in Pig's skill at navigating in the dark.
proposes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Hoof/Hide author misread Horn/Silk's account of Pig's accent; Pig was blind before attacking Silk

I was trying to work this out, and it did seem a little incongruous - I wonder if it could be that the author of these passages (Hoof/Hide) was confused by Horn/Silk's description of this accent - it must have sounded peculiar to all of them, I suppose.

I agree that it's clear that Pig has been blind for some time (i.e. he wasn't blinded immediately after his attack on Silk) - otherwise how can we explain his skill at navigation in the dark? He would only be able to do this if he'd been blind for some time and had had a chance to get used to the idea.

gerbil

2001-02-13 Dave Lebling #

Says the dialect of Pig in The Book of the Long Sun is mild compared with harder-to-decipher works, and that readers should mutter along as they read. Responds to Kieran Cleary's remark that Pig disliked deciphering the dialect, comparing it to Riddley Walker and Feersum Enjin and calling Remora and Incus more annoying devices.
supports · genre-and-craft

<<From: Kieran Cleary <[email]>
Couldn't agree more re Pig. Did he like deciphering that dialect? He did nae! >>

Thank you, Kieran! LOL, as they say on the web.

I have, however, read _Riddley Walker_, _Feersum Enjin_, and several even harder-to-decipher works, so I found this pretty mild. As with other dialect-overload books, it helps to mutter along as you read. I find Remora and Incus more annoying auctorial devices.

Dinna fash yourself.
