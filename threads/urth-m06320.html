Themes: Stories within stories, Time loops and causality

2008-06-28 Marc Aramini #

Argues that the play in The Book of the New Sun about the Maids of the Contessa presages Severian's life and the Urth's ultimate fate, including an eschatological flood drawn from Genesis. Responds to Roy by insisting that a stylized, unrealistic fictional play can legitimately map onto the life events of the story's characters and family.
disputes · allegory-and-typology · myth-and-structure · theories: Maids play presages Severian's life and Urth's fate

Roy, certainly you have a good grasp on the literal text, but there are no problems at all in mapping fictive life events from a play in a fictive universe that is both highly stylized and divorced from realism. Sev&#39;s life and the ultimate fate of the Urth, that the eschaton shall come in that most diluvian of stories from genesis, is clearly presaged in the play. It may just as well reflect on the family of its subject, for it is more than a mere play, but a structure in the fictional edifice that can be used to scale ever higher, revealing the whole in mere parts, as Ultan reflected in his dim reveries.

2008-06-29 Roy C. Lackey replying to Marc Aramini #

Disputes Marc Aramini's claim that a stylized play in the fictive world can be mapped onto real events, arguing that Talos' play mixes Canog's book, Genesis, extra-biblical material and Talos' imagination. Grounds the objection in the point that the play reflects the later Urth events only in broad outline, so its particulars are unreliable for fleshing out a character.
disputes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Talos' play is unreliable as a guide to events

problems at all in mapping fictive life events from a play in a fictive universe that is both highly stylized and divorced from realism.<<

That's absurd. Many of the things in Talos' play did not play out in the Urth Cycle quite as depicted in the play. The play mixed elements of Canog's book, "Genesis" and extra-biblical sources with Talos' imagination. Only in gross aggregate does the play reflect the events recounted in the latter part of URTH. The particulars of the play are more than a little suspect when it comes to trying to use them to flesh-out the bare bones of a character scattered about the text.

-Roy
