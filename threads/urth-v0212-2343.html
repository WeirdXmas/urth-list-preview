finally - a mechanism

Urth list · 5 messages · 2002-12-03 to 2002-12-03

2002urthThe Book of the New SunDan'l Danehy-OakesMarc AraminiJames JordanRoy C. Lackey
The thread: On the first day of the thread, Marc proposed that Silk's astral travel in The Book of the Long Sun is a time-travel mechanism powered by entering inset tales, linking Silk's spirit to Severian's baptism in the Gyoll. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes offered a narrower version limited to places and times already visited by the travelling party, and argued Severian's drowning fell on the day he first appears in Nessus. James Jordan disputed the time-travel reading, calling the power more like telepathy, and asked whether history itself changes. Roy Lackey countered that the drowning occurred in summer while the frozen drain was in winter, so Silk cannot appear exactly at the drowning. Dan'l accepted that the timing is only approximate, and the thread ended with the seasonal objection standing against an exact-simultaneity reading.
  • maa32: Silk's astral travel is time travel, powered by entering inset stories and reuniting with Pig's body
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: time travel limited to places and times already visited by the party, with Severian's drowning on his first day in Nessus
  • James Jordan: altering a story's telling looks like telepathy, and only the telling changes, not the history
  • Roy C. Lackey: drowning in summer versus winter drain rules out an exact-simultaneity reading
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: timing is approximate and the barbican word is questionable

Themes: Time loops and causality, Stories within stories, Catholic faith and sacraments

2002-12-03 Marc Aramini #

Proposes a time-travel mechanism: Silk's astral travel is powered by his ability to enter and alter other people's stories, which he picked up from Fava's and Inclito's mother's inset tales. Argues Silk's spirit reunited with his body from Pig carries Pas's and Typhon's fixation on the Conciliator, sending him to Severian's baptism in the Gyoll, and offers alternative routes via the sewer cleansing in Horn's chapters.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · myth-and-structure · theories: Silk's astral travel is driven by entering other people's stories; Silk's spirit carries Pas's preoccupation with the Conciliator; Silk appears at Severian's baptism in the Gyoll; Horn's sewer cleansing reveals a cultural Severian story

Here is a mechanism that we may want to look at for time travel:

Remember that when Fava and Inclito's mother tell their stories, the narrator has the ability to enter their story. In other words, he can alter events that he hears about and use his "power" to enter the story. He actually appears in Inclito's mother's story as a figure from her past, while he changes Fava's story. I argue that his astral travel can be based off of something that he hears about - and that one of Typhon's big concerns when he scanned himself into mainframe was the conciliator, so that when Silk's spirit is reunited with his body from Pig at the very end of the flashback scenes of return to the whorl, it brings with it Pas' preoccupation with the figure of the Conciliator - and it also programs him with Typhon's obsession to meet and possibly influence the conciliator, sending Silk's spirit across time to find that man at his true birth - his baptism in the river. If you want to argue that Severian was not dead at the exact first time that Silk appears, you have to factor in the very significant drowning in Gyoll that must be taking place EXACTLY as Silk appears in that time - the true birth (or death) of the Severian we know - and therefore the logical focal point for his vivimancer-hood.

There are several other options: that when the vanished people made Horn undergo the cleansing of the sewer, they were telling the cultural story of Severian : a man cleansed the detritus from this sewer of Urth and allowed a cleansing flush - which Silk could later use to travel to Urth and influence the story of that cultural, mythological icon - there are whole sections in the book about the formation of myth, etc. SILK HAS THE POWER TO INFLUENCE OTHER PEOPLE'S STORIES - AND THIS IS WHY LONG SUN IS ACCURATE - AND THIS IS WHY HE CAN SHOW UP AT AN IMPORTANT TIME FOR SEVERIAN IN THE PAST. He knows the important features of the tale of the conciliator - either through mainframe, through the ceremony of cleansing the sewers, or perhaps Rigolio has been imprinted with the task of killing the conciliator if he finds him. I'll look again.

Marc Aramini

Argues that the Gyoll drowning in which Severian supposedly died happened in summer, while Severian found Triskele cleaning a frozen drain in winter, so Silk cannot appear exactly at the drowning. Accepts Dan'l's point that the timing is only approximate and adds this seasonal evidence to his objection to an exact-simultaneity reading of Silk's appearance.

takes place at _approximately_ the time of the drowning; "EXACTLY" would take some serious establishing. I accept it as likely, though; it seems to suit the situation.<<

I would add that Sev's "drowning" in the Gyoll obviously took place during the summer, during warm weather. Sev was cleaning out a "frozen drain" when he found Triskele, in the winter.
