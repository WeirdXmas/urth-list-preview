Argues that Wolfe's symbols and metaphors are often meant literally, using the Marshmen in the Long Sun books as an example: Shadow Children riding Marshmen equals riding a man named Marsch. Grounds this in the title misspelling of John V. Marsh in the poster's copy, suggesting the misspelling may be deliberate rather than a typo.
proposes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Marshmen are literally a man named Marsch; Misspelling of Marsh's name is deliberate

"By now everyone know I actually think Wolfe creates symbols and "metaphors" that are actually extremely literal.? Thus, Shadow Children riding Marshmen is the same as Shadow Children riding a man named Marsch (his name is misspelled in my copy as Marsh in the title "A Story by John V Marsh", which I feel might be intentional even if changed in later editions by copy editors)." ?
on the other hand, as everyone KNOWS, sometimes typos are just?typos.
