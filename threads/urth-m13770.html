On Pedophilia and Homosexuality

Urth list · 15 messages · 2010-07-21 to 2010-07-22

2010urthThe Book of the New SunJohn WatkinsNicholas JostJerry FriedmanRyan DunnLee BermanJosh GellerGerry Quinn
The thread: The thread asked whether The Book of the New Sun depicts or merely omits homosexual and pedophilic activity, especially in the torturers' guild and in Baldanders's interest in men and boys. Nicholas read Greco-Roman views of nature and pederasty into the book, while Gerry Quinn disputed that Baldanders's interest makes him akin to pedophilia. Lee Berman argued the novel deflects such content and that Severian is an unreliable narrator on it, and brunians and John Watkins countered that the guild would tolerate abuse and that the behavior is in fact mentioned. Jerry Friedman questioned the textual evidence and conceded a weak argument about Roche's warning, and brunians ended by stressing the guild's professional aims. The thread stayed split with no real agreement.
  • Nicholas: Greco-Roman views of nature make the Badlanders' interest in men and pederasty continuous with homosexuality
  • Gerry Quinn: Baldanders shows no sexual interest in women and the child in his bed is not a man, so the pedophilia comparison fails
  • Lee Berman: The novel hides homosexual and pedophilic content, and Severian is an unreliable narrator on it
  • brunians: The torturers' guild would tolerate abuse of boys, though its members are professionals rather than sadists
  • John Watkins: Guild homosexuality is mentioned while grandmotherly incest is not, and Thecla's memories may explain desire toward young Severian
  • Jerry Friedman: Asks whether the book depicts or merely omits guild homosexuality, and concedes his Roche argument is weak

Themes: Unreliable narration, Power, rulers and politics, Justice, torture and mercy

2010-07-21 Nicholas Jost #

Argues that Wolfe draws on classical Christian and Greco-Roman views of nature, so that in The Book of the New Sun the Badlanders' interest in men and pederasty is not sharply separated from homosexuality. Grounds this in Severian's rejection of his own attraction to men, his acceptance of fatherhood with Little Severian, and a comparison with Marcus Aurelius.
proposes · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian's love for Little Severian marks his move from self-love to sacrificial love; Wolfe's Badlanders reflect classical rather than modern views of sexuality

Wolfe tends to use classical Christian and Greco-Roman perceptions of nature. As such there wouldn't be a great (and very post '70's) chasm between homosexuality and pedophillia. Badlanders in such an understanding is very interested in the attention of men (not so much "gay" as that is a modern construct). He's interested in men because they are like him and he is his own idol.

Seen in this light _all_ men have a tendancy to be sexually attracted to other men in some degree. Severian notices this, groks it, rejects it, and then accepts his first taste of Father hood. He loves as a man rather than an adolecent (in a moment that reminds me of Marcus Aurelius' reflections on the matter). That is he learns to love another rather than the image of himself, even through loss. Little Severian therfore prepares him to sacrifice for the good and further seperates him from Badlanders.

2010-07-21 Lee Berman replying to Nicholas #

Argues that The Book of the New Sun hides or deflects homosexual and pedophilic content, citing the absence of same-sex activity among torturers, the Old Leech's interest in his assistant, and Severian's unsolicited denials. Grounds this in comparisons to prisons and boarding schools and in a Pelerine conversation about soldiers, suggesting Severian is an especially unreliable narrator on the subject.
proposes · close-textual-reading · author-and-biography · theories: Severian is unreliable about his sexuality; Old Leech has pedophilic interest in Little Severian

Here are the suspicious circumstances to me on this issue in BotNS-

1. Conspicuously absent is mention of homosexual activities among the members of the guild.From prisons to Greek armies to British boarding schools, such behavior is rather well-known in such isolated all-male settings but it is not mentioned here.

If this was Lord Of The Flies (1954) leaving such a thing in subtext but unspoken would be understandable. But this story describes some pretty bizarre sexual situations, from grandmotherly incest to sex with alien beings. Why avoid all mention of this thing?

Severian would have us believe he had no sexual thoughts or activities before age 20 and his meeting of Thecla, the House Azure, etc. I wonder about a few mentioned items, such as the fear the apprentices have of the Witches Tower and how Master Gurloes fears he will not be able to perform when ordered to sexually abuse a woman. Severian suggests the beast handler guild is similar to his own, and they each take an animal in marriage. What do the torturers do? I don't get the impression Master Gurloes frequents the House Azure.

2. Severian becomes sure the Old Leech has a pedophilic interest in his 13 year old assistant because of an unsolicited denial of it.

3. Severian gives us an unsolicited denial of sexual play between himself and Little Severian. I always think to myself at that part, "actually, Severian, I never considered the possibility....until you brought it up".

4. Also odd is Severian's conversation with a Pelerine about the lonliness of soldiers and their need for a sense of authority.

Pelerine- "Some, of course, form bonds with others in the ranks. Sometimes several share a single woman or a man who is like a woman. Some make pets of animals, and some befriend children left homeless by the struggle"

Severian- "Remembering Casdoe's son, I said, 'I can see why you object to that'.

Pelerine- "We do not object- most certainly not to that, and not to things vastly less natural".

Severian is known as an unreliable narrator, but on this topic I think he could be especially unreliable.

2010-07-22 Josh Geller replying to Jerry Friedman #

Argues that members of the torturers' guild in The Book of the New Sun are professionals rather than sadists, comparing them to corrections officers who do not hold children prisoner. Responds to Jerry Friedman's point that Roche calls non-Guild sadists "amateurs" by stressing that the guild training aims to produce people not inherently corrupt, citing Palaemon's explicit statement.
disputes · close-textual-reading

The members of the guild are not generally sadists.

What gives you the impression that they are?

They are professionals.

Professionals don't by and large do these things for entertainment.

Do corrections officers hold their children prisoner? I imagine some do.

Parents that are not corrections officers do too sometimes.

I am certain that all do not.

The guild training is designed to turn out human beings who are not inherently corrupt in this way.

Palaemon states this explicitly.

.
