Explains the Chicago geography behind an earlier exchange: Barrington Hills' reputation as a wealthy town, the Loop elevated train line that gives the El its name, and the commuter rail route from Downtown through Naperville to Aurora. Responds to a poster's remarks on distance and travel time, offering personal memory and transit details rather than any reading of Wolfe's text.
informs

I grew up in Naperville, the next town closer to Chicago than Aurora.

And I'll note that Barrington Hills was indeed famous as a town where rich people lived -- the Chicago Sun-Times used long ago to do a yearly list of the communities in the Chicago area ranked by some metric which defined affluence (a combination of average home price and average salary and stuff like that), and Barrington Hills was first on that list some of the time.

Right. 65 miles is getting pretty far out.

It's a loop of elevated (hence, The El) train tracks which surrounds a section of downtown Chicago. The same train tracks are also a subway for a while.

Actually, the Burlington Northern runs from Downtown through Naperville to Aurora, so I don't really think you'd want to take a bus -- just switch trains.

The train route might take 2 or 3 or 4, depending when, Express vs. Local, stuff like that. (My memories of how long the trips into the city, then up to Wrigley Field, used to take have faded.)
