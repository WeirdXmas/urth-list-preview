2011-06-02 Jeff Wilson replying to Sergei SOLOVIEV #

Argues that the sun in The Urth of the New Sun was still nearly at its original brightness in Typhon's time, citing Typhon's account of a 7 in 1000 luminosity drop. Responds to Sergei Soloviev's question about why the sun was already red, noting the old man's remark about a bright day and the possibility that the moon's apparent size shifts with it.
proposes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: the sun dimmed only slightly during Typhon's reign

Typhon says the sun began to dim during his reign and that the luminosity dropped by 7 parts in 1000 while he was on Urth. That and a lack of other changes discussed suggests that in his time it was still 99.3% of its original brightness. It seems to have continued to dim and may have grown a bit larger and redder over the centuries, but there are still many pictures to compare it with and only its redness is remarked upon, so any change in angular size is probably not large.

The old man in the cabin IIIRC tells of a truly bright day when the sun was as bright as people say it was in the old days, which suggests that the sun has good days and bad either from Urthly weather or solar activity or both.

If course, if the moon is truly closer in, it would also appear bigger, so it is possible that the sun is larger by a like amount, and the rule of thum that the sun and moon are about the same visual width would still hold, and any solar eclipses would retain a similar character. If I were more awake I could speculate about lunar eclipses.
