Themes: Christ figures and messiahs

1997-07-09 Ron Crown #

Argues that the word antichrist should be taken strictly as anti-messiah, so the shadowy being in the book has only one messianic trait, borne by Marilyn as a Mary figure. Responds to Alga by accepting a looser demonic sense of antichrist, and questions the Rosemary's Baby comparison, noting that the child there was simply a demon.
disputes · allegory-and-typology · theology-and-religion · theories: Marilyn is Mary figure

Alga, I was disinclined to the "antichrist" motif because I take the word in a strict sense, i.e., anti-messiah. If Marilyn=Mary (which I was also disinclined toward--Now if Marilyn's husband's name was Joe instead of Skip, that would really nail it down! though that would really be too obvious), then the only "messianic" trait the shadow being has (and perhaps that is enough) is being borne by "Mary."

Most people, I suppose, use the term "antichrist" to mean something more general like "satanic," "demonic," etc. and if that's what you mean by antichrist, I have no problem as that fits a general "apocalyptic" scenario. I liked your Rosemary's Baby comparison although you'll have to help me out with the fine points since I've never read the book/seen the movie in its entirety; my understanding was that her child was simply a demon, not an "antichrist" or even Old Nick himself.

I think we agree on the general picture.

Better?

Ron Crown
