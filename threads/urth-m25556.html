2014-06-08 Marc Aramini replying to Marc Aramini #

Argues that the strange trees in "Tracking Song" may reflect the low-gravity growth effect Wolfe describes in "Silhouette", published the same year. Supports the link by quoting Silhouette's Pleasureworld passage, where trees grow like tangled yarn without gravity, and by pointing to the trees Cutthroat explains.
proposes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Tracking Song's strange trees reflect low gravity

Interestingly enough "Silhouette", published the same year, has this quote"...Pleasureworld. That was the first leisure satellite, and because they didn't know what to do with that kind of thing then, they made soil of the guests' excretions and garbage, and planted trees. Without gravity they grew like a tangle of yarn, of course, like ours in the hydroponic module"

So the strange trees Cutthroat explains might be showing some of this low gravity effect which was obviously on Wolfe's mind at about that time.
