First sight of *An Evil Guest*

Urth list · 10 messages · 2008-03-17 to 2008-03-18

2008urthNicholas GeversMatthew KeeleyMike MyersGreg BatesaaronEric Ortlund
The thread: Nicholas Gevers announced that Wolfe's An Evil Guest is due from Tor in September and that he had read an advance manuscript of about 100,000 words, giving no content. Matthew Keeley and Greg Bates asked about its setting, its link to Memorare, and whether the online 'Gene Wolfe does H.P. Lovecraft' description was accurate. Aaron and Eric Ortlund turned to Wolfe's age and remaining output, the Latro series' possible end, and Jack Vance, while Keeley proposed that Wolfe's recent prose is plainer, asking whether this is a 'late style'. The thread ended without settled answers, drifting from the novel toward Wolfe's productivity and Vance.
  • Nicholas Gevers: announces the September Tor release and advance manuscript length, no content given
  • Matthew Keeley: asks whether An Evil Guest shares a world with Memorare and proposes Wolfe's recent prose is plainer, a possible late style
  • Greg Bates: asks how accurate the Lovecraft comparison is, since it made the novel his most anticipated
  • aaron: wishes Wolfe wrote faster, worries about his age, and recommends Vance's Tschai series
  • Eric Ortlund: asks whether the Egypt Latro book ends the series and whether to wait for more

Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Genre: science fiction, fantasy, both, Myth and folklore sources

2008-03-18 aaron replying to Mike Myers #

Expresses wish that Wolfe wrote as fast as Stephen King, worries about Wolfe's age and remaining output, and compares him with Jack Vance's retirement. Asks whether An Evil Guest will resemble Castleview and Free Live Free more closely or Peace and The Fifth Head of Cerberus in style.
questions

I wish Wolfe would write as fast as King. The sad truth is that Wolfe is getting up there in age. Already, my other favorite SF/F writer, Jack Vance, has retired (although he is dictating an auto-biography)largely due to blindness and old age. I think Wolfe may have four or five more novels left, perhaps another collection. 'Tis a sad thought, but one that cannot be ignored.

Anyway, I am curious to know if An Evil Guest if closer in style to Castleview and Free Live Free, or more like Peace and 5HoC. Either way, I will be content. Just curious.

2008-03-18 aaron replying to Matthew Keeley #

Recommends Jack Vance's Tschai series (City of the Chasch, Servants of the Wankh, The Dirdir, The Pnume) as his best work apart from The Dying Earth, and reports Vance's autobiography dictation. Responds to Matthew Keeley's remarks on Vance and Wolfe's recent prose, and offers a passing hope that Wolfe keeps writing and a note on whether Latro is the final book.
informs

Indeed Vance is dictating his auto-biography into tape recorder and his new live-in caregiver is transcribing these tapes to paper. I for one cannot wait for the end result. Vance has led an interesting life, from working as a Naval electrician in Pearl Harbor just two weeks before the disaster there to writing scripts for 20th Century Fox's Captain Video TV show in the 50's.

I have read everything Vance has published with the exception of one of the mysteries. To me, other than The Dying Earth series, his best work has to be the four book series called Tschai (aka Planet of Adventure-not Vance's title) consisting of City of the Chasch, Servants of the Wankh, The Dirdir, and The Pnume. Kind of like Wolfe's 'four book trilogy'. But really, you won't go wrong with any Vance written from the 60's onward.

As far as Latro goes, I am unsure about whether or not this is the final book, but I THINK not. I have yet to read it myself.

I hope Wolfe will continue to write into his late eighties as Vance did, but who can say? I guess we should all treasure each and every piece of work we get from him from now on.

Aaron Singleton
