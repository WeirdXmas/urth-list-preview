PEACE: Quantrill's gold

Urth list · 3 messages · 2002-10-30 to 2002-11-01

2002urthRoy C. LackeyMichael Andre-DriussiStone Ox
The thread: Readers debated whether the Kate and Quantrill meeting actually happens in Peace or appears only inside the diary Weer reads. Roy C. Lackey argued the passage is a forgery, citing page 195 and Weer's confrontation with Gold over Hannah's Boston trip. Michael Andre-Driussi countered that the sectioned text is a ghostly story behind the veil, matching Ghost Kate's italicized intrusions, and that the Boston detail and moonlight tryst suggest Quantrill fathered Kate's child. StoneOx17 rejected Mantis's Boston speculation by quoting page 10, where Hannah says she was never a hundred miles from the little house. The thread ends without resolution, split across these readings.
  • Roy C. Lackey: the Kate-Quantrill meeting exists only inside Weer's diary, so the passage is a forgery
  • Michael Andre-Driussi: the diary section is a ghost story behind the veiled text, and Quantrill may have fathered Kate's child
  • StoneOx17: the book rules out Hannah's Boston trip, as page 10 shows she never went a hundred miles from the house

Themes: Unreliable narration, Ghosts, dreams and visions, Stories within stories

2002-10-30 Roy C. Lackey #

Disputes a fellow reader's claim that the Kate and Quantrill meeting is valid, arguing it appears only inside the fake diary Weer reads, not in the wider narrative. Cites page 195, Lois reading the diary aloud to Weer, and Weer's confrontation with Gold over Hannah's trip to Boston to show the passage is a forgery.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Kate and Quantrill meeting exists only in forged diary; Lou Gold invented Quantrill's buried treasure

Kate really met Quantrill? The "bearded man" in the sectioned-off text on page 195 is presumably Quantrill. I don't know why it isn't written from the first-person point of view, as would be expected in a diary, but that sectioned-off text is from the diary. Lois had been reading to Den from it, as is clear from the italicized line at the top of the next page, where Lois says: "_So you see it has to be near the river. She mentions it several times_." That she was reading to Weer from the diary is explicitly affirmed when Weer confronted Gold with the blunder about Hannah having gone to Boston (200), which piece of information was included in that sectioned-off text on page 195.

Why Weer didn't give the excerpt exactly as written (if he didn't), I don't know. He evidently gave his Aunt Bella's article verbatim. Perhaps he just wrote down his memory of the mental image he created when Lois read the passage to him. Still, Kate's meeting with Quantrill never happened outside of that fake diary, afaik. Or have I forgotten something?

Kate and Hannah I can believe; Kate and Quantrill is hard to swallow. Lou Gold made up the whole thing about their meeting just to spice up the forgery. As Weer told Gold "You shouldn't have included Quantrill's buried treasure---", because that was the bridge too far, the lie that led to his exposure as a forger, to the end of Weer's romance with Lois, to his daughter's sexual blackmail and, eventually, to Weer's stroke when Sherry died.

What I meant was; if Weer had murdered Lois and buried her (and intended to get away with the crime), he wouldn't want to call attention to the fact by marking the grave.

-Roy

2002-10-31 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Argues that the sectioned-off text in Peace is not a verbatim diary but a ghostly story behind the veiled text, pointing to its style matching Ghost Kate's italicized intrusions. Responds to Roy's claim that Kate really met Quantrill, citing the Boston detail and the moonlight tryst as evidence that Quantrill may have fathered Kate's child.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Sectioned-off text is a ghostly story behind the veiled text; Quantrill fathered Kate's child, explaining the treasure

That is not the verbatim of the diary text. If you prefer, it is the image of the "story behind the veiled text" that Weer imagines as Kate reads it, but I think it is too close in style to the intrusions of Ghost Kate interacting with Hannah at the end of the banshee tale (where Hannah speaks in italics) to be anything else but a ghostly "story behind the veiled text."

The Boston detail is a difference, especially when Weer is so certain. OTOH, when teenager Hannah Mills presumably returned with mother and sister to the farm and discovered that bandits had been there, had locked up her dad and the hired man and maybe done something unspeakable to beloved Kate, well that would understandably put a dampener on the whole trip, perhaps even to the point where the trip would not be mentioned by Hannah.

(Plus, the moonlight tryst with Kate provides another solution to "Quantrill's buried treasure," since afaik Q did not have any known offspring, but Kate certainly did.)

=mantis=

Disputes Mantis's speculation that Hannah Mills went to Boston in Peace, arguing the book rules it out. Quotes Hannah's statement on page 10 of the Orb edition that she never was a hundred miles from the little house, as evidence against the theory.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Hannah Mills went to Boston

Mantis writes, speculating Hannah may actually have gone to Boston:

But Hannah herself says (p. 10, Orb) "I never in my life been a hundred miles off from that little house, but it's gone now, and I never saw it go."

-- Stone Ox
