1999-04-27 Dan Parmenter #

Argues that the Ascians' minds and imaginations are not subsumed by their constructed language, so Severian's rejection of the extreme Sapir-Whorf hypothesis in Loyal to the Group of Seventeen is not an error. Suggests Severian's conclusions may echo Wolfe's own thoughts, and replies to an earlier post's claim that Ascian emotion is suppressed by their language.

From: Matthew Malthouse <[email]>

I think that the whole point of Loyal to the Group of Seventeen's story is that the Ascians minds and imaginations are NOT subsumed by the limitations of their constructed languages. I've said this before. Severian rejects the extreme Sapir-Whorf hypothesis. I do not believe that this is Severian getting it wrong. In fact, I'd be inclined to think that this is one of those cases where Severian's conclusions may echo Wolfe's own thoughts.

Lex Shellac
