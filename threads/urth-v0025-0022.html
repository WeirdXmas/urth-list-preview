Dorcas: 'Buried' dead or alive?

Urth list · 2 messages · 1999-04-07 to 1999-04-07

1999urthThe Book of the New SunClifford DraneAlex David Groce

Themes: Myth and folklore sources, Death and resurrection

1999-04-07 Clifford Drane #

Proposes that Dorcas was not truly dead but in stasis, her eyes opening as she sank, preserved by the water for forty years until Severian's touch woke her. Also asks whether Dorcas has been read as a parallel to the Lady of the Lake, citing Severian grasping her hand and the sword together.
proposes · close-textual-reading · myth-and-structure · theories: Dorcas was buried alive, not dead; Water preserved Dorcas in stasis for forty years; Dorcas parallels the Lady of the Lake

Ok. The seemingly accepted point of view is that Dorcas was dead, and Severian brought her back to life while submerged looking for his sword. However, the old man looking for his wife (my shiny new copy of Lexicon Urthus (love it Michael!) is at home, so I don't know his name!) said that Cas' eyes popped open just as she was submerged - which led me to believe that she was laid to rest by mistake. Following that logic, the water, which is said to act as a preservetant, kept her in stasis for 40 years until Severian woke her up by touching her hand (which could be supernatural in origin or not (could have been the shock of the touch alone)).

Also, has Dorcas as a parallel to the Lady of the Lake been mentioned before? Severian grasps both her hand and the sword at the same time, almost like she gave it to him. Any thoughts about that?

Oh, if any of you catch me at Yahoo chat, my name is 'Agilus' (all the name of good guys (or interesting creatures) were taken!)

Cliff Drane

1999-04-07 Alex David Groce #

Argues that the name Dorcas corresponds to the woman Peter raises from the dead in Acts 9, so Severian's revival of Dorcas in the Book of the New Sun is a real resurrection rather than a buried-alive rescue. Responds to Clifford Drane's view that Dorcas was laid in the water alive, and supports the reading with the Acts parallel and a Triskele comparison.
disputes · source-and-allusion · allegory-and-typology · theories: Dorcas was dead and truly resurrected by Severian; Dorcas's name echoes Acts 9 Dorcas raised by Peter

The idea is interesting, although the correspondence of names with the woman Peter raises from the dead in Acts 9:36-42 is suggestive of a real resurrection, the first human raised by Severian as Triskele is the first animal.
