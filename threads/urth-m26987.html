The thread: Fernando Gouvea asked for a good map of the region where Soldier of Arete is set, roughly European Turkey, and said he could find the Hebrus but not the Melas. Eric Bourland replied that he once saw a map with an exegesis of the setting and would try to locate it again, calling the work out of where things lie a pleasure of the book. Robert Pirkola then argued that the Melas is the Thracian river named by Herodotus rather than the Boeotian one, tying it to Pleistorus, the Thracian Mars, and citing Herodotus and Rawlinson's notes, which Wolfe owned. The thread ended there with no recorded reply to Pirkola's argument.
  • Fernando Gouvea: wants a usable map and cannot find the Melas
  • Eric Bourland: recalls a mapped exegesis of the setting and offers to find it
  • Robert Pirkola: the Melas is the Thracian river from Herodotus, not the Boeotian one, citing Arete Chapter 9

2023-03-28 Robert Pirkola replying to Fernando Gouvea #

Argues that the River Melas in Soldier of Arete is the Thracian river named by Herodotus, not the Boeotian one, and that Pleistorus, the Thracian Mars, is central to the book. Supports this with Herodotus Books 7 and 9 and Rawlinson's notes, which Wolfe owned, citing Arete Chapter 9 where the river is called the boundary of the Apsinthians.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: River Melas in Arete is the Thracian river

The River Melas that is referred to in Arete is the Thracian one mentioned by Herodotus. See Histories, Bk. 7, Ch. 198. Wolfe owned Rawlinson's translation of Herodotus which Rawlinson fills with copious notes. Check out pp. 167-168 of the fourth volume and the associated notes, accessible here: https://www.google.com/books/edition/The_History_of_Herodotus/k4kOAAAAQAAJ?hl=en&gbpv=0&kptab=overview. Other translations might directly refer to the Melas River as the "Black" River given the meaning of the word. It is definitely the Thracian river and not the one in Boeotia. Arete, Ch. 9 -- "She says this river is the Melas, the boundary of the country of the Apsinthians." The Apsinthians are Thracians and Herodotus's discussion of them gives us the only ancient reference to Pleistorus, who Rawlinson calls in his notes "the Thracian Mars". See Histories, Bk. 9, Ch. 119 and associated notes also accessible at the above link to Rawlinson's translation and found at pg. 479. Pleistorus is obviously a big part of Arete.
