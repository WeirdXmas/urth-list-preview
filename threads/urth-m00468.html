Travelin' Jahlee

Urth list · 29 messages · 2005-08-10 to 2005-08-18

The thread: Readers debated how Jahlee, a child-sized inhuma, could have reached Blue to attack infant Sinew, given the text's timeline and the 'years' reference. Roy C. Lackey argued she arrived by lander, first as a child and later as a grown inhuma, and that Neighbors used astral travel rather than spacecraft. Thalassocrat countered that landers are unsupported, that the Neighbors carried inhumi physically, and later that the attacker was a starving adult, possibly Jahlee herself. Don Doggett and James Wynn offered alternate origins for Quetzal and timelines involving rapid maturation. The thread ran long without resolution, with the posters repeatedly restating their timelines and sources.
  • Roy C. Lackey: Jahlee reached Blue by lander as a child, and Neighbors work astrally
  • thalassocrat: Neighbors physically carried inhumi by spacecraft; the attacker was an adult, possibly Jahlee
  • James Wynn: Inhumi mature quickly and were likely on Green before the lander arrived; Rajan time-travels
  • don doggett: Quetzal was on the Whorl from birth or is offspring of inhumi from Urth's system
  • Roy C. Lackey: Silkhorn's dream of the attack is not literal; Jahlee's violence stems from inherited human emotions

Themes: Time loops and causality, Robots, androids and artificial life, Doubles, twins and clones

2005-08-10 Roy C. Lackey #

Asks how Jahlee, a child-sized Green creature who had not yet learned to make the Crossing, could have bitten infant Sinew on Blue, and proposes she arrived by lander. Grounds the question in Jahlee's own account in In Green's Jungles of years spent learning to fly and in Sinew's age of two or three.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: Jahlee reached Blue by lander before she could fly

Jahlee came to Blue at least twice. The fact that she fed on infant Sinew and was mother to Krait is sufficient proof of that. She had to return to Green to spawn, then came back to Blue, was caught and buried under stones, then dug up by Silkhorn. That much is certain.

What is less clear is how she got to Blue the first time, when she bit Sinew.

Sinew was conceived on the _Whorl_, so he could have been no more than 2 or 3 years old when bitten; he was still small enough to be sleeping in a basket made by Nettle. In the scene where Silkhorn and Jahlee made that impromptu astral visit to Green, the visit where she got left behind in that room on the high cliff, she told him about learning to fly. She said that it had taken her "years" just to be able to fly up to the room (IGJ, 61), and even after reaching it that she still had to learn more before attempting the Crossing.

It's also clear from that scene that she already had a human spirit when she was very young; that is what motivated her to want to make the crossing. Presumably she was born with it. So, the question is: If Sinew was attacked by Jahlee during the first 2 or 3 years of colonization, before she was able to make the Crossing under her own power, how did she get to Blue?

I can think of only one solution -- she came on a lander. Either she boarded one of those landers that returned to the _Whorl_ automatically (like Abanja's), then went from the _Whorl_ to Blue on the same or another lander, or, she went directly from Green to Blue on a lander, like the slave ship from Pajarocu. Either way, what was Wolfe's point in having her go to Blue before she could fly properly?

Has anyone a better answer?

-Roy

2005-08-12 Roy C. Lackey replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that the Neighbors seeded the inhumi on the Whorl only briefly, during its early orbit around the Short Sun, and that they used the first landers to move inhumi between Green and the Whorl, which explains how Quetzal got there. Responds to Roy's earlier point that Silkhorn's remark about landers reflects his ignorance of Neighbor responsibility, citing Silkhorn's words to Vadsig and the Neighbors' ruined, extinct state on Green.
proposes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Neighbors used early landers to move inhumi and themselves; Quetzal reached the Whorl via Neighbor transport

Yes, the Neighbors took inhumi to the _Whorl_. But I don't think they studied humans for a "long time". Just for the time (beginning thirty-something years before the exodus?) the _Whorl_ was in orbit around the Short Sun. Vadsig asked, "To greet us it was?" Silkhorn answered, "You might put it so, though they were sensible enough to find out a good deal about us -- and infect us with inhumi -- Before they ventured to greet even a few of us." He goes on to say of the Neighbor's, "the civilization they had built had failed them".

That's what I meant about the "point of contamination". I think that point was reached when the _Whorl_ first reached the Short Sun system and sent down exploratory landers. The Neighbors were effectively extinct on Green (as on Blue), therefore there were no sentient inhumi there then. The few Neighbors remaining on Green (as on Blue) were little more than ghostly caretakers. Their technology, like their cities, was in ruins. Maybe they had some magical (to humans) means of transporting themselves and other cargo from one place to another, but I don't recall any evidence of it. I'm suggesting that they made use of the earliest landers to move inhumi (and perhaps themselves) between Green and the _Whorl_. That's how Quetzal got there.

Offhand, I don't know.

-Roy

Explains that Jahlee's attack on Nettle and her final revelation of the Secret were driven by inherited human emotions, namely grief for her lost children and jealousy, rather than hunger. Supports this with her dying words, her daydreams about the tower room, and Silkhorn's last entry in his book.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theology-and-religion · theories: Jahlee's attack on Nettle was jealousy

Oh, I was referring to your speculations about "Why would going to Blue to prey on humans meet a need to become more human-like?" And Jahlee's "day-dreams of returning to the tower room after making the Return Crossing, to spend time there thinking about herself and 'what she had done for her children.'"

When dying she said, "You think we don't care . . . ." [. . .] "You do, so we must."

Silkhorn thought she was speaking about her children, and went on to explain to Nettle, "To pass among us, they imitate us -- even our emotions. Most of their spawn are eaten by fish while they are still very young." Jahlee then gave her version of the Secret, and almost the last thing she said was, "Krait was my son, the only one who lived with the mind it took from yours."

Presumably she was expressing, and feeling, the inherited human emotion of grief for her lost children, particularly the loss of her only son. Nettle's firstborn was also effectively lost to her, to say nothing of her husband. Jahlee had attacked Nettle not because of a need to feed, but because of the human emotion of female jealousy. At the same time, she felt empathy with Nettle, which is why she did what she could as she died to balance the score by revealing to Nettle the Secret, something she knew Silkhorn wouldn't do.

It was the last entry Silkhorn himself made in his book, which should give his opinion here more weight than elsewhere.
