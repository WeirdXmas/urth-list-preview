1998-07-14 Dan Parmenter #

Corrects the earlier poster's source, identifying the fairy tale as Andersen's The Steadfast Tin Soldier, and argues the Tin Soldier's one-legged love for a ballerina parallels Jonas and Jolenta. Grounds this in the witches' hints that Jolenta had metal bands added, suggesting she is bionic and, like the soldier, is loved because she resembles the lover.
supports · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Jolenta is partly bionic like the tin soldier; Jonas loves Jolenta because she resembles him

From: [email]

It's "The Steadfast Tin Soldier" by Hans Christian Andersen. Good observation! But wait, there's more! In the story, the Tin Soldier only has one leg, and he falls in love with the ballerina because he thinks that she's like him due to the fact that he sees her first with her leg up in one of those ballet moves that I don't know the name of. So this parallels Jonas/Jolenta perfectly: the witches tell us that more was done to Jolenta than just hair extensions and drugs, that there appear to be bands of metal. "Bionic" parts? So Jonas falls in love with her because he thinks she's like him!

I'll go with that.

D
