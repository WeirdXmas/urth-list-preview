Amazon has RTW cover up

Whorl list · 6 messages · 2000-11-22 to 2000-11-27

2000whorlThe Book of the Long SunKevin J. MaroneyMichael PurdyWilliam H. AnsleyDan'l Danehy-OakesJames Jordan
The thread: Readers examined the Amazon cover of Return to the Whorl, asking who the giant is and whether a small figure in his hand is humanoid. Several posters, including Dan'l Danehy-Oakes and Michael Purdy, noticed the giant resembling Gene Wolfe and worried a metafictional intrusion would be unwelcome, though Danehy-Oakes trusted Wolfe's taste based on "The Last Thrilling Wonder Story." William Ansley argued the figure could be tiny and the bird distant, suggesting Darby O'Gill as an influence. James Jordan offered a Trinity-based reading in which the giant represents the Father, Oreb the Holy Spirit, and Silk and Horn Christlike figures. Kevin J. Maroney disputed the Wolfe resemblance and the metafiction worry, noting the cover figure is new and Wolfe has a walrus mustache. The thread ended without resolution, with competing readings left standing.
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: giant looks like Wolfe but metafiction should be fine since Wolfe's taste is trusted
  • Michael Purdy: giant resembles Wolfe and a metafictional intrusion would be unwelcome; hopes it is a tip of the hat
  • William Ansley: the figure may be tiny and the bird far away, possibly influenced by Darby O'Gill
  • James Jordan: Trinity pattern on covers; giant may be the Father, possibly Severian grown large
  • Kevin J. Maroney: metafiction matches earlier volumes, Wolfe does not appear, cover figure is new

Themes: Christ figures and messiahs, Books, libraries and writing, Gods, angels and the Increate

2000-11-22 Michael Purdy #

Asks who the giant on the Return to the Whorl cover is and whether the small figure in his hand is humanoid, describing a finger and arm he sees and a light-obscured head. Notes that the figure is very hard to make out and that Oreb is the one certain identification on the cover.
questions · close-textual-reading · theories: Oreb appears on the Return to the Whorl cover

FYI all, the cover for Return to the Whorl is now up on Amazon.

This one's got me stumped. Who's the giant? And is that a humanoid figure in his hand? It's easy to lose the little finger on the giant's hand, but if you can separate that finger out (it points in a different direction than the other fingers), there appears to be an arm from the smaller humanoid figure propped up on it. You can see a waist and legs, but the head of the figure and one of its hands are radiating so much light it's very tough to be certain. About the only thing that can be said with any certitude is that Oreb's on the cover. Very tiny, but it's gotta be Oreb.

Notes that the giant on the Amazon cover of Return to the Whorl resembles Gene Wolfe in a photo online, and worries that a metafictional intrusion into the Whorl storyline would be unwelcome. Expresses hope the artist meant it as a tip of the hat to Wolfe and asks what the giant's odd headgear signifies.
questions

Dan'l,
I just popped over to the one photo of Wolfe online that sprung to mind (it's on Paul Duggan's Wolfe page), and darned if you ain't right. That giant DOES look a lot like Wolfe.
Although I love just about everything Wolfe writes, the prospect of a metafictional intrusion into/resolution of the Whorl storyline provokes pangs of dread. I'm hoping this was more of a tip of the hat to Wolfe on the artist's part.
Can anyone make anything out of that funky headgear on the giant? Michael

2000-11-24 William H. Ansley #

Argues that the large figure on the Return to the Whorl cover need not be a giant, since the glowing figure could be tiny and the bird much farther away, and floats a possible influence from Darby O'Gill and the Little People. Responds to Michael Purdy's cover description, which asks who the giant is and whether a small humanoid figure is held in the hand.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Cover's large figure is not a giant; Glowing figure on cover could be tiny

I don't know why everyone thinks that the larger guy on the cover is a giant. The glowing guy could be teensy. And the bird could be much farther away than everyone is assuming.

Maybe one of the major influences on _RttW_ is _Darby O'Gill and the Little People_.

Or maybe not.

2000-11-24 James Jordan #

Proposes a theory about the cover of Return to the Whorl: Silk and Horn are Christlike without being Christ, Oreb occupies a Holy Spirit role, and the giant with a man and flying Oreb may represent the Father. Grounds this in the Trinity-like pattern of the three covers and in providential history, and floats that the giant could be Severian grown large like Baldanders.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · theology-and-religion · theories: Silk and Horn are Christlike but not Christ; Oreb occupies the Holy Spirit's role; Giant on cover is linked to the Father; Giant on cover may be Severian grown large

Since we're all just guessing in the dark, here's mine:
1. Neither Silk nor Horn is Christ, but both are trying to be Christlike, so to speak. They occupy that position in the narrative, to some degree.
2. Oreb is not a dove or the Holy Spirit, but again, to some degree he occupies that position in the narrative.
3. Now we have a giant, holding what looks like a man, with Oreb flying over the man. I'd say this person is linked to the Father. After all, among the dozens of things Wolfe is doing in these novels, one is to show the mysterious work of the Christian God (the Trinity) in the providential direction of history, through His agents.
One thing I think we can assume, and that is that, like the other two covers, this is an illustration of an event in the course of the novel. A truly wild guess would be that the giant is Severian, grown large somehow (like Baldanders).

Pat. Nut.

2000-11-27 Kevin J. Maroney #

Argues that the metafictional content in Return to the Whorl matches the self-aware, author-centred commentary of the first two volumes, and that Wolfe does not appear in the book. Responds to Michael's worry about a metafictional intrusion by noting the cover figure is new and does not resemble Wolfe, who now has a walrus mustache.
disputes · author-and-biography · genre-and-craft

There's a lot of metafictive content in _Return to the Whorl_, but it's very much in keeping with the metafictive content of the first two volumes--that is, an awareness by the author of the work that he is writing a work about his life, and also commentary by other hands upon that work. Gene Wolfe doesn't appear in it (at least, not that I noticed; Robert Borski is much better than I at spotting hidden Wolves in these works).

The character depicted on the cover of _Return_ is new to that volume, and has not been seen (or mentioned, I believe) previously.

I don't think the illustration looks particularly like Wolfe, but in my mental image of Wolfe, his glasses are an inseparable part of his head. And these days, he's got a walrus mustache, so he looks even less like that picture.
