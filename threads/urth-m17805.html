Spiritual and Ecological Sucession

Urth list · 25 messages · 2011-01-11 to 2011-01-13

2011urthThe Book of the New SunJane DelawneyJordon FlatoJerry FriedmanLee BermanAdam ThorntonAntónio Pedro MarquesGerry QuinnSon of WitzJeff Wilson
The thread: The thread began as a dispute over Wolfe's phrase 'Green is Urth' versus 'Urth is Green,' with Son of Witz and Jordon Flato arguing whether Green is a literal future Urth, Ushas, or a symbolic counterpart. Lee Berman then proposed a combined spiritual and ecological succession cycle of flood, ruin and renewal, which Gerry Quinn and Jane Delawney challenged on astronomy and the thin textual evidence about Lune. Adam Thornton floated a black-hole explanation for Fomalhaut's aging, which Quinn also pressed. The discussion drifted into whether Severian in the Short Sun is a different iteration, with Jerry Friedman, Adam Thornton and Lee Berman disputing his age and Merryn's identity. It ended without agreement, with several posters stuck on contested readings.
  • Son of Witz: Wolfe's wording 'Green is Urth' matters, and Ushas and Urth may be two names for one world
  • Jordon Flato: Green is a symbolic counterpart to Urth, and a literal reading makes Green Ushas, not Urth
  • Lee Berman: Urth/Ushas flood and Blue/Green planets enact a combined spiritual and ecological succession
  • Gerry Quinn: Cosmic cycles do not fit the aging of nearby stars, and a black hole is the better explanation for Fomalhaut
  • Jerry Friedman: The Short Sun Severian may be a different iteration, so Ushas need not exist in that world
  • Adam Thornton: Severian is about seventeen and callow, and the Fomalhaut black-hole idea is only a playful suggestion

Themes: Time loops and causality, The dying sun and the cosmos, Deep time and ruins

2011-01-11 Jordon Flato replying to Son of Witz #

Argues that Green in the Long Sun books is a symbolic counterpart to Urth without a flood, a lush but hellish world ruled by monsters, rather than a literal past Urth. Responds to Son of Witz's quoted Wolfe letter ('Green is Urth') by reading Blue and Green as paired metaphors around Ushas and the flood.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · myth-and-structure · theories: Green is a symbolic Urth without flood; Blue and Green are paired metaphors

harder to discount it as anything other than a nod toward a literal solution. He said Green is Urth. This sets up in my brain in a purely symbolic/metaphoric resonance.

If Green is Urth (and hey, we're being literal here, so let's not get cheeky with this Urth/Ushas are the same thing crap) then Green would have to been in Severian's past, pre Ushas conversion. And that, to me, seems completely impossible, unsupported, and just doesn't make sense.

Urth becomes Ushas. It does not remain Urth.

So if Green is Urth, to me this sets up a clear metaphor that Green is a symbol of what Urth would have become without a flood. A world ruled by monsters, taken over by survival of the fittest, and left to rot and decay in it's own filth. Sure it's lush, but it's a lushness of hell. So, yes, clearing the sewers is a highly resonant act to Severian bringing the flood.

And Blue has a number of thematic resonances with Ushas. So Wolfe has set up a great system for us where he can play with the symbolic consequences of a Urth without Flood (Green) and an Urth with flood (Blue).

At least that's what strikes me in the moment.

2011-01-11 Lee Berman #

Proposes that Wolfe's Urth/Ushas flood and the Blue/Green planets enact a combined spiritual and ecological succession, with the Megatherians and other giant beasts seen as geological as well as biological entities. Grounds this in an ecological succession cycle, a Biblical flood analogy, and the Megatherian sightings in the Short Sun books, and it answers Marc's skepticism about the Urth/Ushas comparison.
proposes · myth-and-structure · theology-and-religion · theories: Urth and Ushas flood enacts spiritual and ecological succession; Megatherians are geological as well as biological entities; Wolfe compares planets at similar succession stages

Feeling at a loss of plain textual (plowman's) explanation of why Urth was flooded to Ushas I've long thought the Bible was the place to find the answer- that being that here on Earth, we had a time period when our planet was over-run with demonic beings/pagan gods too powerful for humanity to control/resist. The Flood did not eliminate all evil/false gods but reduced them to levels at least manageable by human judgement and willpower. Same for Urth/Ushas.

an ecosystem. And so somehow the mystery/big picture had to be ecologically related. There is a principle in ecology called "succession". The basic idea is that when a virgin patch of land appears, the first living things to colonize it will be the mobile-seed fast growing plant types (weeds). Later, slower growing but more competitive shrubbery growth will crowd out the weeds. Later still, small trees, then mature "climax" or "old growth" larger trees will complete the succession. And so it will remain.

That is until some natural disaster occurs which is so great as to completely decimate the old growth forest, leaving a virgin patch of land and allowing the process to start anew. Such disasters might be earthquakes, extended drought and, of course, a major flood.

I am wondering if perhaps Wolfe intended to intertwine the principles of spiritual and ecological succession with Urth/Ushas and Blue/Green.

Many have discussed the Megatherians on Urth as simply, "in the ocean". I find this to be a bit simplistic, along the lines of "God is in heaven". There is evidence that these great beasts can be found elsewhere. Beneath the man-ape cave, in Lake Diuturna, in a volcano in the antarctic, in the Amazon River ("Ouroboros" sometimes refers to the great river in the north and sometimes is described as a snake-type giant than can swallow people alive).

What I'm thinking is that these great creatures have gotten so big and so intertwined with the planet that they may be considered almost as much geological as biological entities. Perhaps with The Mother on Blue and maybe the Giant Trees on Green there is something similar going on there. It is for this reason that a planetary upheaval (with "waves of gravity") is necessary to enact both a spiritual and ecological succession on all these planets.

I know this isn't what Marc is looking for but perhaps when Wolfe says "Urth is Green" (implying Ushas is Blue) it is like comparing Africa and Asia and saying "Sahara is Gobi" and "Congo is Sumatra". Perhaps Wolfe is comparing these as places which are in similar stages of ecological and spiritual succession.

great beasts can be found elsewhere. Beneath the man-ape cave, in Lake Diuturna, in a volcano
in the antarctic, in the Amazon River ("Ouroboros" sometimes refers to the great river in the north 
and sometimes is described as a snake-type giant than can swallow people alive).
 
What I'm thinking is that these great creatures have gotten so big and so intertwined with the 
planet that they may be considered almost as much geological as biological entities. Perhaps
with The Mother on Blue and maybe the Giant Trees on Green there is something similar going on there.
It is for this reason that a planetary upheaval (with "waves of gravity") is necessary to enact both
a spiritual and ecological succession on all these planets. 
 
I know this isn't what Marc is looking for but perhaps when Wolfe says "Urth is Green" (implying Ushas 
is Blue) it is like comparing Africa and Asia and saying "Sahara is Gobi" and "Congo is Sumatra". Perhaps
Wolfe is comparing these as places which are in similar stages of ecological and spiritual succession.
