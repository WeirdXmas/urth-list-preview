1997-06-26 Kieran Mullen #

Argues that Silk in The Book of the Long Sun is a Christian figure in a broad sense without following Christ, and that Wolfe writes theological science fiction. Answers a post denying Severian's Christianity by comparing Silk to Abraham and Moses, citing Lewis's Perelandra and Out of the Silent Planet.
disputes · theology-and-religion · allegory-and-typology · theories: Silk is a Christian figure in the Mosaic sense; Silk resembles Moses more than Christ; Wolfe's theology extends beyond Earth

More fuel for the holy wars...

This seems to be a limited definition, from an sf point of view. How would you classify the creatures in Lewis's _Out_of_the_Silent_ Planet_ or _Perelandra_? They don't follow Jesus specifically, but they are certainly patched into the God of the Judeo-Christian tradition. I think that one facet of Wolfe's writing is theological sf, trying to stretch the boundary of normal sf. (In traditional sf "gods" are just very powerful contingent beings.) He's implying that if the Earthly Christian tradition is true, it must be true *everywhere*.

If that's all too religious for you, remember the ambiguity. Silk could be completely wrong about the Outsider; it could all come from Crane's medical explanation.

It might be helpful to think how a Christian would interpret Abraham from the Pentateuch. He walked and talked with God, and it changed his life. However, Abraham certainly wouldn't be a "Christian" in the sense being "a follower of Jesus Christ". However you might say that in the Christian tradition, he is a Christian figure.

To me Silk has more of a Mosaic flavor to his character than Christic. He leads his people out of bondage to a promised planet. However, he does not get to walk in the Land himself. I mean, "Exodus"? The burning bush?

Kieran Mullen

1997-06-27 Derek Bell #

Proposes that Silk may have a brother high in the Chapter, analogous to Aaron, the High Priest and brother of Moses, while admitting the comparison may be stretched. Responds to a post reading Silk as Mosaic rather than Christic, citing the exodus, the burning bush, and Silk's not entering the Promised Land.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · theories: Silk has a brother high in the Chapter

Maybe I'm stretching the comparison too far, but could Silk have a brother high up in the Chapter - analogous to Aaron, the High Priest, Moses' brother?

Tercel
