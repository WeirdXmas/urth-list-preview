The thread: Posters debated who killed Gilling and whether the figure on the flying horse is really Gilling, with Seth Lombardi doubting that Idnn is the murderer and citing the breakdown in The Knight and the witch's illusion show in The Wizard as signs the identities and deaths are unclear. David Lebling then questioned a Wizard Knight scene where Gerda describes births to a giant, implying a vaginal first birth that may contradict other accounts. Harlekin and Andrew Bollen turned to the witch-ghost's giant-human sexual display in The Knight, suggesting it is false and meant to incite or disgust Toug, and asking whether pregnancy must follow. Andrew also noted Beel's claim that Toug tried to kill Gilling. The thread drifted from the murder mystery into the sexual and reproductive material, ending without resolution.

2005-01-04 Dave Lebling replying to Seth Lombardi #

Asks whether a scene in The Wizard Knight has Gerda describing several births to the giant who owned her farm, noting she implies a vaginal first birth rather than a cesarean. Suggests this contradicts other accounts, since the giant was not one of the Mice, drawing on the earlier poster's point about difficult births.
questions · close-textual-reading

Isn't there also a segment in _The Knight_ where Gerda (not sure if it was her) describes having borne multiple children to the giant on whose farm she was a slave? She comments that after the first birth they could have normal sex together, implying a vaginal birth rather than a c-section. I don't believe her owner was one of the Mice, but an actual giant.

This story seems to contradict the others.

-- Dave, aka Vizcacha

2005-01-04 harlekin replying to Seth Lombardi #

Suggests that the witch-ghost's vision of giant-human unions in The Book of the Long Sun may be false, meant to incite and disgust Toug. Argues that if giants and humans do mate that way, pregnancy need not follow when the woman does not want it, responding to Seth Lombardi's remark about Gilling's size.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: The witch-ghost's vision of giant-human unions is false

Gilling is a particularly large giant, so this may have something to do with it as well. Don

But back to the witch-ghost's nasty little vision of giant-human conubials: it could well be completely false, and designed just to incite and/or disgust Toug. As somebody else mentioned, if that's the way giants and humans really do it, there doesn't seem to be any reason why pregnancy should have to ensue, if the woman doesn't want it.

2005-01-05 Andrew Bollen replying to David Lebling #

Asks why the witch-ghost staged her giant-human sexual display for Toug in The Knight, and suggests it may be false and meant to incite or disgust him. Responds to David Lebling's post by noting Beel's claim that Toug tried to kill Gilling, hinting at a hidden story in the Giants section, and wondering whether pregnancy must follow.
questions · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: The witch-ghost's giant-human vision is false, designed to incite Toug

Mr Lebling says:

I'm really not sure why ummm the witch-ghost put on her repulsive little giant-human sex show for Toug. What gives with that? The only reason I can see for her doing it is to (unsuccessfully) attempt to induce Toug to kill Gilling, but why does she want to do that? She has some kind of agenda amongst the giants, perhaps just in aid of Idnn (who is nice to her cat) and/or Able (who was nice to her bones) - but it's totally unclear to me what's going on. And why does Beel think that Toug did try to kill Gilling?

There may be some kind of Wolfean hidden story in the long Toug-amongst-the-Giants part of the book.

But back to the witch-ghost's nasty little vision of giant-human conubials: it could well be completely false, and designed just to incite and/or disgust Toug. As somebody else mentioned, if that's the way giants and humans really do it, there doesn't seem to be any reason why pregnancy should have to ensue, if the woman doesn't want it.
