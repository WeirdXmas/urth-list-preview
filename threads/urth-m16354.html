2011-08-09 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Recommends Cherie Priest's Clockwork Century novels Boneshaker and Dreadnought as enjoyable steampunk, and names Gibson and Sterling's The Difference Engine as a good example of the genre. Replies to Craig Brewer's request for good steampunk that is not written to follow a fad, and it does not discuss Wolfe's work.

I've really enjoyed Cherie Priest's "Clockwork Century" novels, BONESHAKER and DREADNOUGHT. Whether that makes them "good" is another question.

One that I'm quite sure is "good" is Sterling/Gibson's THE DIFFERENCE ENGINE.

2011-08-09 Lane Haygood replying to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

States that the poster disliked Cherie Priest's Clockwork Century novels and finds steampunk generally pandering, though open to it in principle. Names Moorcock's Oswald Bastable novels and Felix Gilman's The Half-Made World as enjoyed works, and mentions Vandermeer. Responds to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's praise of Priest and Sterling/Gibson's The Difference Engine, but the post is off-topic from Wolfe.
chats

I didn't like those. In theory, I like steampunk, but I am yet to find something that really does well within the genre rather than just try to pander to fans of it. I know Vandermeer has it in him, so maybe I just need to find more of his stuff.

Moorcock's Oswald Bastable novels are genius, though, but I'm generally like, "Oh, another Moorcock novel? Yes, please!" But maybe that's because I liked the political subtext and Moorcock's subversiveness more than others. A "steampunkish" (I guess?) novel that I did enjoy was Felix Gilman's "The Half-Made World," though it was more like a weird Western than a steampunk novel.

LH
