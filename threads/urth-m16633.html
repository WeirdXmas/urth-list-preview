The thread: Antonin Scriabin asked whether HBO's Carnivale borrowed from Gene Wolfe's Peace, citing a carnival visit, a burlesque mother-daughter pair, a character named Scudder, and the shared Depression-era Midwest setting. Larry Miller went further, suggesting Carnivale's creator Daniel Knauf may have known Wolfe's work and pointing to the fetus in a jar as a mandragora echo. David Duffy offered a more modest reading, arguing the carnival elements may come from research and listing earlier precedents such as Bradbury and Dr Lao, and noted Nehemiah Scudder echoes a Heinlein character. The thread ends with the borrowing question left open and no clear agreement.
  • Antonin Scriabin: parallels with Carnivale are notable and worth asking whether the writers knew Wolfe
  • Larry Miller: Carnivale likely borrowed from Peace, and its creator may have known Wolfe's work
  • David Duffy: the carnival material may come from research and earlier literary sources, so borrowing is not needed to explain it

2011-12-16 Antonin Scriabin #

Points out parallels between the HBO series Carnivale and Peace, including a carnival visit, a mother-daughter burlesque dancer pair, and a character named Scudder. Asks whether Carnivale's writers were fans of Wolfe or influenced by his work, citing the shared Depression-era Midwest setting and the book's earlier publication.
questions · source-and-allusion

I don't know if many of you watched the short-lived but well-executed HBO miniseries, *Carnivale*. Basically, it was a pseudo-end times supernatural mystery / thriller set during the Great Depression (dust-bowl Midwest), alternating between the stories of a young "Avatar" of good (a man traveling with a circus / carnival) and an "Avatar" of evil, a preacher in (I think) California who gains a large following. Anyways, I noticed some parallels between it and *Peace*, during the portion in which a character in the book (I can't remember if it is the narrator himself or a story-within-a-story) apprentices with the man turning to stone. The young man goes, for some reason, to visit a neighboring carnival, where he finds a mother-daughter "burlesque dancer" team (just like in the TV show) and a character named Scudder (the last name of the Avatar of good's father in the TV show). "Scudder" is far from a common name; I can't think the similarity is coincidental! The carnival scenes in *Peace* also take place in a Depression area Midwest town. I seem to remember a few other interesting similarities between *Peace* (which was written 28 years earlier) and *Carnivale* that I will try and remember. Anyone notice this / know if the HBO writers were fans of or influenced by Wolfe's work?

2011-12-16 Larry Miller replying to Antonin Scriabin #

Suggests that the HBO series Carnivale, with its good and evil avatars, may have borrowed from Peace and that its creator Daniel Knauf may have known Wolfe's work. Points to the baggage-trailer fetus in a jar that opens its eyes as resembling the mandragora, and cites Gaiman's name-dropping and Ronald Moore's SF reading as context.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Carnivale draws on Wolfe's Peace

The shows creator was Daniel Knauf who also writes comic books (or did) and I wouldnt be surprised if he knew about Wolfes work. Neil Gaiman is always dropping Wolfes name in interviews. BTW Ronald Moore who developed the Battlestar Galactica remake also wrote for Carnivale and has said he was an avid SF reader as a teen. The only literary influences I know that Knauf mentioned myself is Something Wicked This Way Comes and the Grapes of Wrath. If your interested in further elaboration of Carnivales mythology search around online for the "Pitch Document" which is a series bible of sorts. Do you recall the episode where Ben Hawkins is sent to clean out the baggage trailer? Briefly shown on camera is a fetus in a liquid filled jar that opens its eyes. That always reminded me of the mandragora. That may also be a nod to Wolfes work.

2011-12-17 David Duffy replying to Antonin Scriabin #

Reports that the Carnivale-style carnival characters in Peace may derive from research and suggests other literary precedents such as Bradbury's Something Wicked This Way Comes, Dr Lao and The Dreaming Jewels. Notes that the name Nehemiah Scudder echoes the preacher-turned-president in Heinlein's If This Goes On, a link prompted by the name appearing in the thread.
informs · source-and-allusion

I didn't watch it (it looked like too much work ;)), but the Wikipedia article is fairly extensive. It suggests, for example, that the cootch family came into the story following research. I would have thought Bradbury's _Something Wicked This Way Comes_, _Dr Lao_, even _The Dreaming Jewels_ might be other literary precedents. And Nehemiah Scudder appears in Heinlein's _If This Goes On_

"The First Prophet was Nehemiah Scudder, a backwoods preacher turned President (elected in 2012)"

..that was my immediate thought when you mentioned the name Scudder.

Cheers, David Duffy.
