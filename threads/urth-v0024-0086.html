Symbolism of Dorcas

Urth list · 3 messages · 1999-03-23 to 1999-03-25

1999urthThe Book of the New SunC R CulverRoy C. Lackey
The thread: CRCulver proposed that the pomegranate scene with Dorcas in The Book of the New Sun symbolizes death and return, linking Severian's remark that she resembles a vampire to the undead and comparing the fruit to the Persephone myth. Roy C. Lackey disputed the details, noting that Severian ate the pomegranate while Dorcas held an apple in The Shadow of the Torturer, and citing a Sword of the Lictor passage where Dorcas calls him a black bat, not a vampire. CRCulver accepted the apple correction and asked how the Garden of Evil motif should be read given Dorcas as First Woman, tying the fruit to Genesis. The thread ends with that Genesis question open, and the cards show no further reply.

  • Roy C. Lackey: corrected the fruit and vampire details and read Severian's memory as a lapse, citing the black bat remark
  • CRCulver (later): reads the fruit and Garden of Evil imagery through Genesis, with Dorcas as First Woman

1999-03-23 C R Culver #

Proposes that the pomegranate-eating scene with Dorcas in The Book of the New Sun carries symbolism of death and return. Links Severian's remark that she resembles a vampire, with red-stained lips, to the idea of the undead, and compares the pomegranate to the Persephone myth in which eating it binds one to a half-year in the underworld.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · source-and-allusion · theories: Dorcas has returned from death; Pomegranate echoes the Persephone myth

I've long thought that the scene in the BotNS in which Dorcas is eating a pomegranate (cannot remember the volume, but I believe it is the second), has some interesting symbolism. Severian remarks that she looks like a vampire because her lips are stained red, and as vampires are called the "undead," Dorcas herself has returned from death.
Also, I'm wondering if the pomegranate itself has any meaning in relation to the myth of Persephone, who after eating a pomegranate was doomed to spend half of the year in Hades before returning to the land of the living. Dorcas herself has spent some time in the realm of the dead before her return to the Earth.

- Christopher Culver <[email]>

1999-03-24 Roy C. Lackey #

Corrects Christopher Culver's account of the pomegranate and vampire imagery linked to Dorcas, noting that Severian ate the pomegranate and Dorcas had an apple in The Shadow of the Torturer. Cites the scene in The Sword of the Lictor where Severian recalls Dorcas calling him a black bat rather than a vampire, suggesting a lapse in his memory or imagination.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian's memory falters in recalling Dorcas's words

pomegranate (cannot remember the volume, but I believe it is the second), has
some interesting symbolism. Severian remarks that she looks like a vampire because her lips are stained red, and as vampires are called the "undead," Dorcas herself has returned from death.<<

Actually, it was the other way around. Sev had the pomegranate, Dorcas an apple in _Shadow_, chapter XXXIV. Sev recalls the scene to her in _Sword_, chapter X, where she remarks: "It always seems, though, that when I wake up you're bending over me."
Sev then says: "You said I reminded you of a vampire once, because I had been eating a pomegranate and my lips were stained with red." Interestingly, in the scene referred to she didn't actually say "vampire" but "like a black bat bending over me." Sev's perfect memory falters (again?) or his imagination is at work.

Roy

1999-03-25 C R Culver #

Accepts a correction that Dorcas, not Severian, held the apple in The Shadow of the Torturer, and asks how the Garden of Evil motif should be read given that Dorcas is the First Woman. Responds to Roy C. Lackey's correction and builds on Wolfe's Eschatology and Genesis material, linking the fruit imagery to the Genesis garden story.
questions · allegory-and-typology · myth-and-structure · theories: Dorcas as the First Woman of Eden

<<Actually, it was the other way around. Sev had the pomegranate, Dorcas an apple in _Shadow_, chapter XXXIV.>>

Whoa, looks like I was way off. Anyway, if Dorcas had the apple, what can we work in concerning the Garden of Evil motif? After all, Dorcas was the First Woman in "Eschatology and Genesis."
