1997-10-31 Michael Straight #

Disputes Tony Ellis's view of Hell, arguing that in Biblical thought as in Dante it is a place of suffering rather than the headquarters of evil, with Satan its prisoner. Contrasts Cordwainer Smith's "A Planet Named Shayol" with recent SF gory Hells meant to make readers doubt rather than fear, and notes enjoying Peace's ending.
disputes · theology-and-religion · theories: Hell in Biblical thought is a place of suffering, not evil

Just to nitpick your nitpick, in Biblical thought (contrary to Milton and others), as in Dante, Hell is not the headquarters of "evil" but a place of suffering. Satan is not pictured as the ruler of Hell but its most famous prisoner.
And since this message has no Wolfe content anyway, I'll continue with the observation, after reading Cordwainer Smith's "A Planet Named Shayol," that where at one time people wrote gory descriptions of Hell with the purpose of putting a genuine fear of God into people, several modern science fiction writers have written gory stories about Hell with the (seemingly intended) effect of making the reader say, "surely this cannot be."

ObWolfe: I put off reading "Peace" because it sounded less interesting than Wolfe's other stuff, but I really enjoyed it, found the ending quite satisfying, and am finding that it's staying with me as much or more than any of his other books.

-Rostrum

1997-10-31 Tony Ellis #

Disputes Nutria's claim that The Fifth Head of Cerberus has Biblical and Christian religious imagery, arguing Dante's Cerberus borrowed from pagan myth and Dante's Hell is about suffering, not evil. Notes no Christian imagery appears in the novellas, doubts a false-church reading of the whorehouse, and finds stronger parallels with aboriginal Australian destruction by white settlers.
