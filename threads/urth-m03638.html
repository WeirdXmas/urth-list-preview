Typhon & the Whorl

Urth list · 4 messages · 2007-12-21 to 2007-12-21

2007urthThe Book of the New SunPedro PereiraRoy C. LackeyDan
The thread: Dan proposed that Typhon took over the Whorl as a living museum of Earth's cultural diversity rather than as a monument or lifeboat, and that Abaia and the creatures were a family plot transcribed into the ship, citing the chem stockpile timing and a Citadel passage on technology from other worlds. Roy C. Lackey corrected his term to Hierogrammates and placed them in Yesod, outside Briah where Urth lies. Pedro Pereira then quoted a 1997 Wolfe interview saying Typhon's purpose was mostly self-aggrandizement and that the Blue-Green system was the intended destination from the start, which he argued settles the hijacking theories. The thread ended with Wolfe's quoted answer as the apparent resolution.
  • Dan: Typhon hijacked the Whorl as a living museum; Abaia and creatures were a family plot
  • Roy C. Lackey: Hierogrammates made by the Hieros, placed in Yesod outside Briah
  • Pedro Pereira: Wolfe said Typhon sought self-aggrandizement and Blue-Green was the intended destination

Themes: The dying sun and the cosmos, Power, rulers and politics, Fathers, mothers and children

2007-12-21 Dan #

Argues that Typhon hijacked the Whorl as a living museum preserving Earth's cultural diversity, not as a personal monument or lifeboat, and proposes that Abaia and the creatures were part of a family plot against Typhon transcribed into the Whorl. Grounds this on the chem stockpile timing and the rapid decline, plus a recalled Citadel passage about procuring technology from other worlds and the Hieros' reluctance to share it.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · source-and-allusion · theories: Typhon hijacked the Whorl as a living museum; Abaia was part of Typhon's family plot; Whorl holds digitally scanned family personalities

I think that is good evidence for how long it took to construct the Whorl, which reinforces my point that Typhon hijacked it. It wasn't built specifically as a personal monument to his grandeur or chariot to the stars. If anything, it seems like a living cultural museum to preserve Earth's social diversity. Given the rapidity of the decline and the length of time for construction it almost seems that there was extraterrestrial influence to motivate it's construction and then bring about the disaster which would necessitate it's existence. Like Noah's Ark and the great flood.

I have a vague memory ihat in Citadel, there was a discussion about technology (which may have tied in with why the Hieros didn't use their ships to save the doomed population) with the point being that Earth couldn't make everything and some things had to be procured from other worlds. I think FTL technology would fall under this category and no doubt the Hieros would be reluctant to part with it. Especially to a tyrant like Typhon. The ones he had under his control may have been loaners or inherited from previous, more benevolent rulers. Besides, I don't see the Whorl as a lifeboat, more of a living museum. Far more people could be accommodated by better utilizing it's volume than wasting it to isolate culturally different city states.

I always thought Abaia et al was part of the plot against Typhon by his family. That plot got transcribed to the Whorl when they were digitally scanned. They knew about the creatures because it was in the works but the NS Typhon didn't because it never unfolded before he was, uh, dehydrated. I don't recall any indication that Pas knew about them. In Typhon's time they were probably hiding, while in Sev's time they were more actively involved in the war and easier to discover. If Typhon knew of them before he wouldn't have hesitated a second to destroy them if he saw them as a threat. And I certainly doubt he would have given his digital family free run of mainframe if he suspected they were up to something.

It's a neat tie in to the previous series and provides one of those "hey wait a second" moments of revelation/realization. I do wish he would write a short story or novel exploring it further.

I'd certainly like to read that interview as it directly refutes one of my views on the issue.

Dan

2007-12-21 Roy C. Lackey replying to Dan #

Corrects Dan's term 'Hieros' to Hierogrammates and explains that the Hieros fashioned the Hierogrammates in a collapsed universe, with Tzadkiel among them. Places the Hierogrammates in Yesod, outside Briah where Urth lies, responding to Dan's vague recollection of Citadel's technology discussion.
informs · close-textual-reading

[snip]

I believe you meant Hierogrammates, not Hieros. The Hieros were the humans who cruelly fashioned the Hierogrammates in another universe that collapsed. The Hierogrammates were the only things that mankind had made that survived that universe. Tzadkiel was a Hierogrammate. Yesod is where they reside. It is outside the universe of Briah, which is where Urth is located.

-Roy

2007-12-21 Pedro Pereira replying to Dan #

Points to a 1997 Wolfe interview answer, quoted from the Whorl list, to settle why Typhon launched the Whorl and where it was headed. Quotes Wolfe saying Typhon's purpose was mostly self-aggrandizement, and that the Blue-Green system was the intended destination, in reply to Dan's claim about immortality and remembrance.
informs · author-and-biography · source-and-allusion

The answer is right here in this list. Its one of the few interviews in which memebers asked questions that Wolfe later answered:

<<<<From: [email]: (whorl) Train from WolfelandDate: Sat, 14 Jun 97 21:15:00 GMT[Posted from WHORL, the mailing list for Gene Wolfe's Book of the Long Sun]

3. "What was Typhon's purpose in lanunching the Whorl and was theBlue/Green system the intended destination?" Knowing Typhon as I do,he probably had at least half a dozen purposes; but certainly one ofthe chief must have been self-aggrandizement -- to return human kindto the stars would be a very great thing indeed. Yes, the Blue-Greensystem was the intended destination.>>>>

There it is, clear as water.

Pedro

2007-12-21 Pedro Pereira replying to Pedro Pereira #

Reports that Wolfe stated the Blue/Green system was the intended destination of the Whorl from the start, which Pedro Pereira argues settles theories that the ship changed course or was hijacked. Grounds this in a quoted 1997 Urth/Whorl list answer from Wolfe to a question about Typhon's purpose and the intended destination.
informs · author-and-biography · theories: Whorl was always headed for Blue/Green system

And by the way, let me say that the Blue/Green system was the intended destination right from the start according to Wolfe, which puts to rest some theories that the Whorl changed its destination or that someone (or something) hijacked it and changed its original destination.

Pedro
