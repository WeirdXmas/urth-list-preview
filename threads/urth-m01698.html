2006-04-08 thalassocrat #

Argues that SilkHorn's goodness may alter Sev's future, so Sev may no longer be a torturer as the New Sun course predicts. Grounds this on Sev's remark in The Shadow of the Torturer that the Triskele episode changed nothing, and asks what Gurloes will do to Sev and to the prisoners held with SilkHorn.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: SilkHorn's goodness creates a new timeline for Sev; Sev will not continue as torturer after Triskele

On the same theme as before ...

In Ch IV of Shadow, Sev says of his finding Triskele: "I felt that it could have & should have changed everything, but it was only the episode of a few months [ie, the week he had him & the remainder of winter, during which he saw him once or twice a month] and when it was over ... nothing had changed."

In Ch 19 of RTTW, Sev makes his comment about not putting SilkHorn in his book, because nobody would believe it. Remembering this prompts Hide/Hoof (whichever) to write his little passage about SilkHorn's terrifying goodness, concluding: "... I think when somebody is really, really good, as good as he was, the rules change."

So maybe a hint that the change Sev had expected in Shadow will now actually occur in a new time-line, because of SilkHorn.

Thinking about what happens to Sev during the RTTW episodes, I do find it hard to believe that subsequent events will follow the New Sun course. Can Sev really carry on as torturer after basking in the glow of SilkHorn's preternatrual goodness?

Interrupted by Oreb while dragging Sev away, Gurloes flees from what he thinks is a sudden apparition of the dead Malrubius. Instead of following him, Sev calmly takes his visitors down to meet Triskele. What would the next meeting between Gurloes and Sev be like??? I don't see Sev getting away from any such meeting free & unmaimed. I think Sev's off through the underground corridoors with Triskele, a torturer no more ...

2006-04-16 Thomas Bitterman replying to thalassocrat #

Argues that Severian cannot have been transformed by meeting Silk and Horn in the Return to the Whorl episodes, since he later writes the New Sun account and must have stayed the same person. Grounds this in the fact that the narrative exists and that many people keep their bad ways after such meetings, and suggests Severian found Silk tedious, with Wolfe warning against reading earlier books through later ones.
disputes · author-and-biography · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Severian remains unchanged after meeting Silk and Horn; Severian found Silk tedious and was humoring him

If they don't, how will Sev write the book that Wolfe found and we read? Many people were able to continue being bad after meeting SilkHorn. I'd like to think
that Severian found SilkHorn just as tedious as I did and was just humoring a well-meaning
but dotty old man.

Perhaps Wolfe is using the meeting to point out the dangers of using a book written many
years after a previous book to interpret the former.
