Themes: Gods, angels and the Increate

2011-02-09 Lee Berman #

Argues that the Sun books have a moral beyond good versus evil, comparing the Book of the New Sun's religious frame and the Short Sun's ecology, with humans manipulated by superior beings such as the Inhumi and Neighbors. Grounds this in the Short Sun's uakari, margay and Brazilian farmer, and in the reply to Gerry Quinn's claim that the Whorl's humans might fight the Inhumi better.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · myth-and-structure · theories: Sun series humans are manipulated by superior beings

I think it is more complex than this.

We could consider BotNS a religious/mythological based story for which the moral is that the world/universe is more than a battle between good and evil. The battle between good and evil, angels and demons itself constitutes a necessary part of the Creation process.

Likewise we could consider Short Sun an ecologically based story for which the moral is that the world/universe is more than a conflict between predator/prey/parasite. The total interaction of all participating creatures is necessary to drive the ecosystem.

The uakari and the margay do not understand why the Brazilian farmer feels the need to parasitically cut down the rain forest and destroy their natural habitat and way of life. The Brazilian farmer does not understand why Green groups seek to stop them from cutting down the rain forest, taking away their livlihood and way of life.

The Neighbors are a species of being far beyond that of human beings. Inhumi, in some ways, might also be considered superior. I get the sense that humans are the low species on the totem pole in this whole Sun series. Inhumi seem to exploit them at will. Inhumi may be controlled by the Neighbors, or perhaps there is a see-saw battle like the one between humans and insects.

Either way, the humans of Urth, the Whorl and Blue-Green seem constantly manipulated by beings who are superior in ability and vision. As the future of the oblivous uakari and margay of the Amazon are jerked around by farmers and Green types, so humanity is jerked around on all their worlds by superior beings in the Sun Series.

2011-02-09 Gerry Quinn replying to Lee Berman #

Disputes Lee Berman's claim that the Neighbours were defeated only by a failed ecosystem, arguing instead that the Neighbours utterly failed to control the inhumi, who destroyed them. Supports this by citing Horn's line about the Neighbours seeming like minor gods, and notes that the Hierogrammates were originally created by humans and that Urth was once an interstellar hub.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: The inhumi destroyed the Neighbours; The Neighbours failed to control the inhumi; Hierogrammates were originally created by humans

Fair enough - we could also note that the creation process is more *important* than good and evil: "Which does humanity need more? Justice and peace? Or a New Sun?"

That seems a bit of a stretch. It is easier to take the opposite tack: the parasite has destroyed its prey, and will now itself die. The story is driven by the failure of an ecosystem, not its success. [In some sense, all three stories are driven by such a failure, but in BotNS the cause and cure are in some degree external, and in BotSS the system has outlived is design life. I don't think ecosystem failure is the central theme ofd the Solar Cycle.]

I'm not sure I agree. Remember that line of Horn's I quoted earlier: "We are ready to believe that they are practically minor gods - that they know everything and possess all manner of mysterious powers; but they must seem perfectly ordinary to themselves."

They may be more morally, aesthetically, and technologically advanced in some ways - but they were still defeated by the inhumi. Not to go all Campbellian, but perhaps there's a reason why the humans end up in possession of Blue.

That seems quite wrong. The Neighbours failed utterly to control them. The inhumi destroyed the Neighbours.

To some degree, certainly, at ;least at the time that the stories are set.

On the other hand, he Hierogrammates were created by humans in the first instance. Ane Urth has in the past been the hub of an interstellar empire. (This may be the cause of some of its current difficulties.)
