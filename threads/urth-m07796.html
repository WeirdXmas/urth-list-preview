2009-04-29 David Stockhoff #

Asks whether the underwater greenhouses in the text exist, since the poster may have missed them while discussing diploidy and doubled genes in Rajan and food. Responds to Brian Lovely's post, which links the Inhumi, Neighbors and vines to crossbreeding, corn and eight-legged creatures on Blue.
questions · close-textual-reading

In diploidy, the number of genes is doubled. Sometimes the result is all-around greater viability, as with corn, and sometimes it is the opposite.

Either way, it doesn't mean doubling the limbs of animals---but then, if all life on Green and Blue is somehow plant-derived ... why not?

And of course diploidy is a metaphor for something here, and that possibility has been discussed.

***By the way, can you point me to the underwater greenhouses in the text? I may have missed them.

Well, whatever the Neighbors are to trees, the Inhumi are to vines. Maybe their base form is the slug-like thing that drops out of a tree on green, kind of an amphibian that lays its eggs in water. But if there base form is just a vine, then feeding on something higher pulls them up from vine to amphibian. It's all up. The uncrossed Inhumi are still vines, those who have managed to move up a level are slugs, those who have fed on humans are bat-winged devils.

Crossbreeding. The corn that Horn seeks on the Whorl. The underwater greenhouses, the strange doubling of limbs that many creatures on Blue exhibit. I read somewhere in the list a theory about polyploidy something or other--some biology that I don't know anything about--but the upshot was that you can cross corn in such a way that the genetic pattern is doubled...something like that. More to the point, like beasts with eight legs. Who was crossbreeding who, and why? Did the Neighbors have to change themselves in order to survive? Were the 8-legged animals experiments, to perfect the technique?

2009-04-30 Jerry Friedman replying to David Stockhoff #

Corrects David Stockhoff's biology wording by stating that diploidy is the normal state for most organisms, including humans apart from sperm and egg cells, and that doubling the chromosome count is tetraploidy. Responds to Stockhoff's quoted claim about diploidy doubling genes and affecting viability, offering a terminology correction rather than a reading of Wolfe's text.
disputes

Not to nitpick... okay, to nitpick. Diploidy is what we have (except sperm and egg cells) and I think most organisms have. Double the usual number of chromosomes is
tetraploidy.

Jerry
