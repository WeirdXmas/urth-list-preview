2008-11-28 David Stockhoff #

Compares the 2000 miniseries of Dune favorably with the text and with Lynch's film, arguing Lynch captured the mysticism of the Kwisatz Haderach's visions but missed the book's theme of human mental and physical improvement. Asks whether the sound weapons were in the original text, and quotes an earlier post and a reply from Son of Witz, which agrees with the criticism.
questions · close-textual-reading

I'm just now watching the "other" Dune movies---the miniseries---and they are much closer to the text. Really very well done.

The Lynch movie did often seem to miss the point, but I thought it did capture the mysticism of the Kwisatz Haderach/Abomination's visions in a visually poetic manner. I think that's what interested Lynch, who otherwise was drawn to grotesquery and decay, not superhuman evolution.

Weren't the sound weapons in the text?

2008-11-28 Kerry Benton replying to David Stockhoff #

Replies to David Stockhoff's comparison of Dune adaptations, agreeing the miniseries is closer to the text and that Lynch captured the mysticism visually. Corrects the sound-weapons claim, noting only the Voice is in the text, and worries that adaptations of The Book of the New Sun would over-explain magical or supernatural elements.
disputes

They're a whole lot better, I concur...

Lynch did do a good job with the mysticism and the visuals I think. I love the Guild navigators, love the handling of the Harkonnens for the most part, and the worms.

There were no sound weapons in the text, unless you mean Voice, which is also mishandled (IMHO) in both Lynch and the scifi series.

I worry most in adaptations of BOTNS about the handling of anything that appears magical or supernatural... That any film producer/ director/writer would try to explain too much.

My two cents...
-k
