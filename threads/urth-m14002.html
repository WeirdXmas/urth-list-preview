2010urthThe Book of the New SunJeff WilsonLee Berman
The thread: Lee Berman and Jeff Wilson debated whether Severian's eating of the Autarch's brain in The Book of the New Sun is a sacrament or a corrupt act. Berman argued it is a tainted, artificially prolonged life, pointing to the corrupt learning passed from Ultan to Vodalus to the old Leech and to Wolfe's 'satanic eucharist' remark. Wilson defended it as a false but still valid sacrament the Increate may accept, grounded in redemption and the earlier case of the Casdoe's husband. The thread consisted of three posts with no visible reply from Berman after Wilson's defence, so it ended in impasse.
  • Lee Berman: the brain-eating is a corrupt, tainted way to knowledge and prolonged life, not a divine sacrament
  • Jeff Wilson: the brain-eating is a false but still valid sacrament the Increate may accept, grounded in redemption

Themes: Christ figures and messiahs, Memory and forgetting, Gnosticism, heresy and other theologies

2010-07-30 Lee Berman #

Argues that Severian's feast on the old Autarch's brain, through alzabo memory-eating, is a tainted and artificially prolonged life rather than a divine act, and that the corrupt learning will not continue on Ushas. Disputes Jeff Wilson's defence of the feast by citing Severian's relief that the Autarch's memories survive in him, the tainted learning of Ultan, Vodalus and the old Leech, and Wolfe's 'satanic eucharist' remark.
disputes · theology-and-religion · author-and-biography · theories: Severian's feast on the Autarch is a corrupt way to gain grace; Wolfe sees gnosticism and paganism as rivals to Christianity

Yeah, alzabo, avern, abaia, arioch..so many alien "a's" on Urth.

Wolfe himself, in an interview, calls the Vodalus feast a "satanic eucharist". But we are to take Severian's feast on the old Autarch's brain as being divine? I dunno. Perhaps as a non-Catholic and non- Christian I don't have the right background to form an educated opinion. But that seems like an awfully icky way to gain grace.

When Severian receives his new consciousness I seem to remember him feeling a flood of relief as all the inhabitants of the old Autarch find that they have not perished in his old body but have found new existence in Severian. From this I get the sense that this process is a form of artificially prolonged life and perhaps not one to be envied.

Regarding the learning which is involved in the eating of the dead, from Ultan, to Vodalus to the old Leech, I get the impression that the learning is tainted in some way. Severian doesn't seem to put much of his newly acquired wisdom to good use either, aside from learning the secret words needed to be autarch.

I'm not an expert on such things but my impression is that Wolfe finds not only pagan mythology to be the competitor of early Christianity but gnosticism also, with its reliance on arcane knowledge and secrets. I get the impression that brain eating and the resultant learning and life/consciousness extending is a corrupt part of the world of Urth which will not be continued on Ushas.

2010-07-30 Jeff Wilson replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that Severian's eating of the Autarch's brain is a false but still valid sacrament that the Increate may accept, despite Vodalus's satanic mockery of communion. Grounds this in the duty and hope of redemption, Severian's relief at escaping his predecessors' imprints, and the earlier case of the Casdoe's husband eaten by the alzabo, responding to Lee Berman's question about grace and icky eating.
proposes · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian's brain-eating is a valid if false sacrament

Of course it's satanic, Vodalus is a satanic figure and he's twisting any virtue the analept might have into part of his mockery of communion. But the Increate may validate even a false, incomplete sacrament.

This could be from duty and from a hope of redemption, that they can continue to serve the cause of Urth, instead of ending in failure. And it could be some projection on Sev's part, relieved that he didn't become a puppet of the dozens of imperious minds of his predecessors. It's his glands that are still real, after all.

Something in the process may keep the duplicated personalities amiable to the situation, the way the Casdoe's husband's imprint wasn't upset or particularly concerned about having been eaten or helping the alzabo kill and eat the rest of his family.

Hopefully, it won't have to be, since it will no longer be necessary. But remember the big theme: even evil men and abhorrent deeds serve the good of the Increate.
