Starbirth

Urth list · 3 messages · 1998-12-04 to 1998-12-14

1998urthThe Book of the New SunAlice TurnerMichael Andre-DriussiDerek Bell
The thread: Alice Turner argued that the union of Severian and Apheta literally conceives the New Sun, rather than only metaphorically, answering Derek Bell's claim that Severian is full of chaos but creates nothing, and citing a Nietzschean line about chaos giving birth to a dancing star. m.driussi supported the reading of Apheta's name as a star's nativity, calling it circular yet sensible, while also raising a Latin side debate and a Stephanides letter without developing either. Derek Bell conceded that the Starbirth imagery had slipped his mind and wondered what other non-solar stellar imagery the readers had missed. The thread faded without a real clash of views, ending in agreement that the reading is a good one.
  • Alice Turner: the union of Severian and Apheta literally conceives the New Sun
  • m.driussi: Apheta's name reads as a star's nativity; idea circular but sensible
  • Derek Bell: concedes the Starbirth point is good after a long gap since rereading

Themes: Names and their meanings, Mirrors, pictures and art, The dying sun and the cosmos

1998-12-04 Alice Turner #

Argues that the union of Severian and Apheta literally conceives the New Sun, not merely metaphorically, which is close to giving birth. Responds to Derek Bell's point that Severian is full of chaos but does not create the New Sun, citing the Nietzschean line about chaos giving birth to a dancing star.
disputes · close-textual-reading · allegory-and-typology · theories: Severian and Apheta's union literally conceives the New Sun

Er, ahem, let me note parentheticially that not only metaphorically and mystically, but in some sense literally, the sexual union of Sev and Apheta does indeed create or conceive the New Sun. Which is not exactly the same as gving birth, but pretty close.

-alga

1998-12-05 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Agrees with alga's point about Apheta and reads the name as a star's nativity, calling the idea circular yet sensible. Also takes a side in a Latin debate over the phrase "Terminus Est" and mentions a letter from Adam Stephanides, without developing either point.
supports · source-and-allusion · theories: Apheta is the nativity of a star

As alga says.

Apheta. The star of a nativity: the nativity of a star.

Scarey, circular, yet somehow . . . it makes sense.

Back to the "Terminus Est" Latin tangle, obviously I'm on the side that says, "Write the essay anyway, even if your initial impulse wasn't immediately validated by the Vulgate."

In other news: got a snail letter the other day from our old Wolfe list friend Adam Stephanides. He has some things to say. I'll tell all later.

=mantis=
