Themes: Humour in Wolfe

1997-10-24 adam louis stephanides #

Opens a discussion of Wolfe's humor, arguing that most of his work is solemn and that his overtly comic stories succeed less often than his serious fiction. Names the short stories "Car Sinister," "An Article about Hunting" and "For- lesen" as favorites, comparing the dark humor of the last to Kafka, and asks others for their favorites.
proposes · genre-and-craft

This reminds me of a topic that I've long been intending to bring up: Wolfe's humor. Most of Wolfe's work strikes me as utterly solemn (though there are undoubtedly things I'm missing); and his overtly humorous stories have, in my opinion, a lower proportion of successes than most of the other stuff he tries his hand at. But I do like the humorous short stories "Car Sinister" (though I suspect I may be a minority in this) and "An Article about Hunting." And "For- lesen," one of my favorites too, is full of dark humor of the sort that Kafka was so good at. Anybody have any other favorites?

--Adam

1997-10-24 Michael Straight #

Calls Free Live Free very humorous and says There Are Doors has funny bits, especially its G. Gordon Liddy passages, in reply to adam louis stephanides's claim that Wolfe's comic stories succeed less often. Offers both books as examples of Wolfe's humor and quotes no passage, since the reading was too long ago.
supports

If found FREE LIVE FREE very humorous, but it's been too long to single out a particular passage. I thought THERE ARE DOORS has some funny bits too (particularly G. Gordon Liddy).

-Rostrum
