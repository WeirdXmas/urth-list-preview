Mattachine Tower

Urth list · 6 messages · 2003-03-01 to 2003-03-02

2003urthThe Book of the New SunBratt IanJosh GellerAlice TurnerTami WhiteheadJeff Wilson
The thread: Jeff Wilson asked whether a History Channel claim that the Mattachines were masked Italian court jesters explains the torturers' tower in Wolfe's work. Alice K. Turner proposed that the name is an ironic nod to the 1950s-60s Mattachine Society, a coded gay men's group, and doubted Wolfe knew of that reference. Bratt Ian countered with an etymology from Arabic mutawajjihin via Moorish Spain, meaning masking oneself. Tami Whitehead ended the exchange by quoting Wolfe's own glossary entry, which describes matachins as masked sword dancers from a Spanish tradition said to derive from the Moors, and noting Wolfe's noncommittal "Well, maybe" to that derivation. The thread stops there, with the linguistic origin favored by Wolfe's own wording over the homosexual reading.

  • Alice K. Turner: the name is an ironic nod to the 1960s Mattachine Society and Wolfe likely did not intend it
  • Bratt Ian: the word derives from Arabic mutawajjihin via Moorish Spain, meaning masking oneself
  • Tami Whitehead: Wolfe's glossary describes matachins as Spanish masked dancers with a tentative Moorish origin

2003-03-01 Alice Turner #

Argues that the Mattachine name in the Book of the New Sun's Mattachine Tower is a nod to the 1950s-60s Mattachine Society, a coded gay men's group, as an ironic reference to being closeted. Responds to a quoted post that cited a History Channel account of Italian masked court jesters and asked whether it folds into the torturers' tower, and doubts Wolfe knew the homosexual reference.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Mattachine Tower name alludes to the Mattachine Society

Back in the 60s, before the Stonewall Rebellion, before gay liberation of any note, the Mattachine Society on the East Coast (and for all I know on the West) was a coded name for a quite sizeable male homosexual club or group here; I expect the historical note is an ironic nod to being "closeted." I doubt that Wolfe knew that!

-alga

2003-03-02 Bratt Ian #

Supplies an etymology for the word Mattachine, citing a web page that derives it from the Arabic mutawajjihin in Moorish Spain, meaning masking oneself. Responds to Alice K. Turner's note on the 1960s Mattachine Society and Wolfe's possible knowledge of it, offering a linguistic origin instead.
informs · source-and-allusion

According to http://www.sbu.ac.uk/stafflag/mattachine.html, the word matachin or matachine has been derived from the Arabic of Moorish Spain, in which mutawajjihin, relates to masking oneself.

-Insula

2003-03-02 Tami Whitehead #

Quotes Wolfe's glossary entry for Matachin Tower in The Castle of the Otter, which describes matachins as masked sword dancers in a Spanish tradition said to derive from the Moors. Responds to Bratt Ian's post on the word's Arabic origin by supplying Wolfe's own wording, where Wolfe adds a noncommittal "Well, maybe" to the Moorish derivation.
informs · source-and-allusion

I, Razorkittee, will tell you what The Man said. Take
it for what it is worth.

In the book, The Castle of the Otter, by Gene Wolfe,
on page 28, for Chapter II, he says: "Matachin
Tower:In a Spanish tradition, matachins are masked
sword dancers. Some authorities say that the tradition
is derived from the Moors. Well, maybe. To the other
inhabitants of the Citadel, the Seekers of Truth and
Penitence are most visisble on the feast of Holy
Katharine, when the journeymen, weating their masks,
dance with their beheading swords."

kittee
