Original Sin and pagan gods.

Urth list · 8 messages · 2006-08-09 to 2006-08-10

2006urthBob MillerChris PDan'l Danehy-Oakesb sharpJames Jordan
The thread: The thread began as a debate over Genesis 6, with b sharp questioning the Sethite reading and proposing that the sons of God were fallen angels, while James B. Jordan cautioned that Wolfe may use readings he considers mistaken. It then shifted to whether the fallen-angel and giant-offspring myth predates the written text. Chris argued it is an old Jewish myth older than the Talmud, while Dan'l Danehy-Oakes countered that the earliest documentation is later. Bob Miller pointed to the Book of Enoch as older evidence, which Dan'l said they would look up. The exchange ended without agreement on the dating, and little discussion touched Wolfe's books directly.
  • b sharp: sons of God are fallen angels, not Sethites, and questions Wolfe's connection to the Genesis debate
  • James B. Jordan: Wolfe may deliberately use a reading he considers mistaken, so the intended reading is hard to know
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: earliest documentation of the angel-human mating myth is the Talmud, centuries after Genesis
  • Chris: fallen angels mating with women is an ancient Jewish myth predating the written text, not a matter of etymology
  • Bob Miller: Book of Enoch is older and describes giants from such unions, so the Talmud dating is wrong

Themes: Myth and folklore sources, Gods, angels and the Increate, Gnosticism, heresy and other theologies

2006-08-09 b sharp #

Questions the Sethite reading of Genesis 6 offered by an earlier poster, asking why the sons of God would be men and why a flood would cleanse believers, and proposes that the sons of God are fallen angels instead. Responds to a quoted post and asks whether James Jordan, who interviewed Wolfe about pagan gods, and Lilith's role in Wolfe's work fit the discussion, also joking about over-reading the Bible.
questions · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: Sons of God are fallen angels

James B. Jordan writes regarding the fallen angel origin of the Nephilim:

but isn't the word also derived from the Hebrew for "fallen"?

The beginning of Genesis 6 says:
6:1 And it came to pass, when men began to multiply on the face of the earth, and daughters were born unto them,
6:2 That the sons of God saw the daughters of men that they were fair; and they took them wives of all which they chose.

This indicates to me that the "sons of God" were not men and their intercourse with human women was something different from the human reproduction which had already covered the face of the earth. Why would God need to cleanse the earth of "believers" with a flood, which is described immediately after?

and James says:

So, the forbidden fruit Eve plucked was good? And "The serpent's job was to train them in wisdom". And what were these Sethite line guys doing when the rest of humanity was mulitplying. Why did they only discover the daughters of men later? I have trouble understanding all this, especially with a more clear, alternate explanation available.

Yes, but aren't you the James Jordan who did interview Wolfe and to whom he explicitly stated his belief in the reality of pagan gods? Regarding Lilith, yes, James Jordan and Roy Lackey had a discussion of Wolfe's use of this myth in regard to Copperhead about five years ago. Why would his use of Lilith in BotNS be surprising? And at the risk of sounding disrespectful and irreverent I must ask if the admonition to be careful and not over-read also applies to Bible reading ;-)?

-bsharp

2006-08-09 James Jordan replying to b sharp #

Argues that it is hard to know which readings of Genesis 6 (sons of God as angels or as Sethites) Wolfe favours, and that Wolfe might deliberately use a view he considers mistaken for literary effect. Offers this as a cautionary note, noting the poster's own published work on early Genesis without arguing his views. Responds to b sharp's quoted remarks on the Nephilim and the meaning of 'son of God'.
disputes · theology-and-religion · source-and-allusion · theories: Wolfe uses the Sethite reading of Genesis 6 deliberately

Or, those who fall upon others.
But, for the rest, my point was that all of these things have multiple understandings. I don't want to be taken as "pulling rank" or anything, but as it happens I am the author of two books on the early parts of Genesis, so I'm aware of lots of views, very common views. I shared mine, but this is not the place to argue for my views. My point was that it's hard to know which of the various understandings of these texts are the ones Wolfe tends toward, and even if we did know, that would not tell us whether he might be employing a reading that he regards as erroneous for literary purposes. To wit, he may share the (most common) view that the sons of God are Sethites, but choose to employ the (minority) view that they are angels.
I intended only a cautionary note. @>----

2006-08-09 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes replying to Chris #

Disputes Chris's claim that fallen angels mating with human women is part of Jewish myth older than the written text, arguing the earliest documentation is in the Talmud, centuries later. Asks how the claim could be known and whether a reference older than the Mishnah exists, given the redaction dating of Genesis.
disputes · source-and-allusion

"At least?" Now, how would we know that? The earliest place such a myth is likely to be documented is in one Talmud, which dates to ... well, depends on whether you're talking about Mishnah or Gemarah, but in any case, well after the time of Christ. Figure the redaction of Genesis at the fourth or fifth
century BC, and you've got between six and eight hundred years.

Mind, the stuff in the Mishnah was generally considered "ancient" at the time it was compiled, but unless you know of a reference rather older than that, I have trouble with the claim that these myths go "at least as far back as the written text."
