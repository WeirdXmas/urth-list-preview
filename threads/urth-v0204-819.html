2002-04-27 Robert Borski #

Argues that Victor in The Fifth Head of Cerberus must circumcise himself after killing Dr. Marsch, so that he can pass as Marsch, and that the brass instrument he buys is a surgical tool for this purpose. Responds to hartshorn's question about Marsch's remark that the boy is uncut, pointing to an essay the poster wrote and drawing on the instrument described in the text.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Victor circumcises himself to pass as Marsch

hartshorn having rather unprudishly pondered:

"When Marsh remarks that the boy is "uncut" and *therefore* when he was with the girl, would "bind", or something like that (sorry I can't lay my hand on my copy of 5HC rigth now) , what the hell is he talking about?"

Actually, I've written an entire essay on this and it can be found at:

http://webpages.charter.net/rborski/marsch.html

The same essay also contains several reasons why Victor might have killed his abo girlfriend. (Check it out, Tony.)

What I fail to point out and would like to do now is this:

Victor, it's noted by Dr. Marsch, is uncircumcised. If we assume that Marsch, however, has been circumcised, this means that Victor--after he's killed Marsch--must circumcise himself if he wishes to pass as Dr. Marsch. And that is the purpose of the brass instrument he buys, the "thing of clamps and toothed jaws and cruel little hooks."

It's what an Victorian-era mohel would use to perform the necessary surgery.

Robert Borski

2002-04-29 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Relays a claim about tomcats having barbed penises that stick on withdrawal, supposedly making a queen unwilling to mate again so the first male's sperm win. Offers it as a possible source for a quoted line about a cat in Wolfe's fiction, admitting he cannot verify the claim.
informs · source-and-allusion

For what it's worth...

I've read somewhere that the primary sexual characteristic of the tom housecat is, uh, sort of barbed and tends to get stuck coming out. The evolutionary excuse for this is that it makes the queen unwilling to mate again for a while, so the first male's sperm have more time to find & fertilize the ova before any competitors come along.

I can't verify that this is true, but if Wolfe read the same thing I did [and he seems to read just about everything 8*)], then this could explain the quote in question ... after all, she's a cat ...

2002-05-20 Adam Stephanides #

Points out that a 2001 post misquoted a passage in The Fifth Head of Cerberus, where the words about binding do not appear, and quotes the actual Ace paperback text about the uncircumcised boy. Concludes either the editions differ or the binding idea is baseless, which makes Marsh's remark about circumcision pointless.
informs · close-textual-reading

Paging through my old messages, I came across the following year-old post by Andy Robertson:

I've just consulted my copy of 5HC, and there's nothing in that passage about "binding." The relevant parts of that entry are "I could climb down and catch them together (I _know_ she is with him, I can almost hear them), except that I can smell the ghoul-bear somewhere near. They would die, the two of them -- when the boy was washing I noticed he wasn't circumcised. If they were like that when I came, I think I would shoot them both." (p. 251, Ace pb) So either there's a major discrepancy here between the Ace pb and other editions, or the whole "binding" theme is a non-issue (which, of course, makes Marsch's remark about Victor's non-circumcision even more irrelevant).

--Adam

2002-05-22 Adam Stephanides #

Questions why the word "tie" and the link to being uncircumcised matter in the passage about Marsch's rage at seeing dogs tied, and proposes that Victor's uncircumcision marks him as a child under initiation customs. Supports this by noting the OED sense of "tie" for linked copulating dogs and by pointing to Australian Aboriginal puberty rites that usually involve circumcision.
proposes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Victor's uncircumcision marks him as a child

The OED gives as one meaning of "tie" for a dog and a bitch to be linked during copulation. But it's still strange. Marsch is an anthropologist; you'd expect him to have read about uncircumcised peoples, and to be aware that "tying" doesn't happen. And why does he anticipate being especially enraged by seeing Victor and his girl "tied," as opposed to just having sex?

As for the significance of Victor's non-circumcision in itself, here's one theory. In anthropology, the Australian aborigines are known for rigorous male initiation rites at puberty, which generally involve circumcision. If the same is true of the Free People, then Victor's non-circumcision might be one reason he thinks of himself as a child.

--Adam
