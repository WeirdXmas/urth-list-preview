St. Anne and the Inhumi

Urth list · 1 messages · 1999-04-08 to 1999-04-08

1999urthNicholas Gevers

Themes: Colonization and the native

1999-04-08 Nicholas Gevers #

Proposes that the inhumi in The Book of the Long Sun should be seen as victims of colonial invasion rather than as vampires or soulless beings like the Ascians, and that the humans are the transgressors. Grounds this in The Fifth Head of Cerberus's concern with colonialism's effects on colonizers and colonized, and in the inhumi living in a solar system about to be settled as St. Anne was.
proposes · myth-and-structure · theories: Inhumi are victims of human colonization

Seeing as there's recently been discussion on this site about the nature of the Annese and their possible connection with Urth, Green, etc., I'd like to pose to everyone a question that seems to me to strike to the thematic heart of the matter: Given that 5HC is centrally concerned with the sinister effects of colonialism on the colonizers and the colonized, and that the inhumi in TBotLS inhabit a solar system about to be colonized, like St. Anne, by humans, shouldn't we regard Quetzal and his compatriots as victims of invasion, rather than as vampires, or soulless beings like the Ascians? Isn't it possible that the major surprise of The Book of the Short Sun will be exactly this, that the aliens have the just claim to our sympathy, and the humans are the transgressors?
