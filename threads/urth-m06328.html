Themes: Stories within stories

2008-06-29 Marc Aramini #

Argues that whether the play in Wolfe's book reveals anything about particular characters depends on how much effort Wolfe put into it, and that extra-textual references may or may not require outside knowledge. Grants that the aggregate of relevance is a valid point while defending the idea that the play could say something specific, and closes with a joking aside about narrator death; responds to an earlier thread argument without citing specific passages.
disputes · author-and-biography

well, it all depends on how much effort Wolfe put into the play. I don&#39;t find the idea that it could tell us something particular about particular characters absurd at all, but I suppose I will grant that the aggregate of relevance is a valid point. How minute does Wolfe want to make his extra-textual references? Some works require external context, such as a precursory understanding of the basic features of the saints or religious referents, and other works do not. This is all tied up with authorial construction and intent. If a tree falls in a Wolfe book and nobody notices, is the narrator really dead?
