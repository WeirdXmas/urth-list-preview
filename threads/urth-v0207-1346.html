Silk-23 skidoo

Urth list · 6 messages · 2002-07-12 to 2002-07-18

2002urthThe Book of the Long SunAdam StephanidesMichael Andre-DriussiMichael Straight
The thread: Michael Andre-Driussi opened by arguing that Silk's uploaded brain-data in The Long Sun is a neutral copy that could be reloaded into the body of Silk-23, citing a cyberpunk reading and a sequence of data transfers from Horn and Pig. Michael Straight disputed this, finding Pas's influence sinister and arguing that Silk's refusal to face Hyacinth's death shows he is not a full reset. Adam Stephanides questioned Andre-Driussi's view of Horn's motive, then argued at length against the theory that Silk is present inside Horn, listing five textual problems. Andre-Driussi replied that Horn himself, not Silk-in-Horn, quixotically seeks Silk and sacrifices an eye to free Silk-in-Pig. Straight later offered a rival reading in which Horn wants to restore Pig's sight and let Silk-in-Pig leave, possibly preferring his original body. The thread ended with the competing readings unresolved.
  • Michael Andre-Driussi: Silk's brain-data is a neutral copy that can be reloaded into Silk-23; Horn quixotically seeks Silk and sacrifices an eye to free Silk-in
  • Michael Straight: Silk is not a full reset since he avoids Hyacinth's death; Pas may still be pulling strings; Horn wants to restore Pig's sight and let Silk-in
  • Adam Stephanides: Five text-based objections to the theory that Silk-in-Horn drives Horn's plan to move Silk into Silk's body

Themes: Robots, androids and artificial life, Memory and forgetting, Doubles, twins and clones

2002-07-12 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Argues that Silk's uploaded mind in The Book of the Long Sun is a neutral data copy with no vital essence, so that the Neighbors can reload Silk at age 23 into the older body. Grounds this in a new wave/cyberpunk reading, a comparison with a Varley brain datastream, and a sequence of data transfers from Horn and Pig; it answers William Ansley's request to explain the umpires and the 'Silk-23' title.
proposes · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Silk-23 data was transferred into Horn-in-Silk; Silk-43 brain-data was erased from Silk's body; Pas plans a comeback for Silk via uploaded scan; Neighbors are the umpires who killed dying Horn

The umpires are the Neighbors, the ones who told dying Horn what they were going to do and then did it.

Why "Silk-23": because, rather than get into the slippery religous "soul in the machine" line of thinking, I have always taken Wolfe's use of uploading human consciousness in the Long and Short Sun series in the very straight new wave/cyberpunk mode. That is, imho, it is neither "soulful" heavenly, nor is it a mechanical life-caricature nightmare (this either/or state, techno-heaven or techno-hell, has been strongly present in the last few years of YEAR'S BEST SF, fwiw: very current burning issue, at least as far as YBSF goes). It is just there, like a Varley brain datastream that could be beamed into a clone to start-up right at the moment after the scan was first taken: there is no vital essence to the data, but there may be a copy limit (only one check out at a time, please).

(OTOH, the visit to Mainframe heaven at the end of Long Sun was a tad creepy, a shadowy show compared to the near-death experiences Silk had of ghosts, which seemed a bit more rich and "real" in comparison.)

So, in a sense, Pas is planning a "comeback" tour for his favorite son, Silk. The last few decades have not gone well for this ubermensch -- after a meteoric rise from obscurity ("Silk for Calde'") and a heroic adventure, well, the whole thing went downhill. Why not jumpstart the whole thing by downloading that scan made of Silk at the moment of his glory, when he was around 23 years old, into the 43 year old body? Pretend like that depressing stuff never happened -- reload the hero and get on with the heroics, without being paralyzed by mid-life depression and shattered dreams.

Anyway. My position has been that Silk-43 brain-data was erased from the 43-year-old Silk body; Horn brain-data was downloaded via the Neighbors (by-passing the whole monitor mechanism); later on, Silk-23 brain-data was transferred from Pig to Horn-in-Silk via the eye and the monitor.

That is why "Silk-23" and "if it happened, that is."

=mantis=

2002-07-15 Michael Straight #

Responds to Andre-Driussi's theory that Silk's brain-data was swapped and restored in Silk-23, finding it sinister that Pas might still be pulling strings. Argues the narrator cannot be a full reset to backup data because he still avoids facing Hyacinth's death, and asks whether the quoted line echoes a famous poem with another name in place of Hyacinth.
disputes · source-and-allusion · puzzle-solving · theories: Pas secretly still pulling strings in Mainframe; Silk-23 is not a complete reboot to backup

Interesting theory - and kind of sinister - instead of Silk conquering Pas in Mainframe, Pas is secretly still around pulling all the strings.

But the Narrator can't be a complete reboot back to the Silk-23 back-up data, because he still knows and is trying to avoid facing the fact that Hyacinth is dead.

"I could not look upon Hyacinth, dead." - isn't that a line from a famous poem (with someone else's name in for Hyacinth)?

2002-07-16 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Argues that Horn, not Silk-in-Horn, is the one quixotically seeking Silk, so Horn sacrifices an eye to free Silk-in-Pig and complete his mission, which he still feels failed. Responds to Adam's question about Horn's motive, and also discusses whether the scan called Silk-23 contains Pas elements and when the scan was taken.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Horn-in-Silk seeks Silk, a character Horn wrote; Horn sacrifices an eye to free Silk-in-Pig; Silk-23 has few Pas elements; Silk-23 scan taken at hotel pool

I'm confused by the term "Silk-in-Horn" in this context. I would say, in this case and this reading, that it is "Horn-in-Silk" who is still quixotically looking for "Silk" (quixotic because the Silk he is looking for is a character in a book that Horn wrote).

So it is Horn who makes the eye-sacrifice in order to get Silk-in-Pig out of Pig and into the Silk body. I don't see much rhyme or reason in getting Silk-in-Pig back into Mainframe, fwiw, but maybe I'm forgetting something -- did Silk-in-Pig have some important news to report in VR person?

Horn would make the sacrifice in order to complete his mission. But in spite of his sacrifice, the narrator feels he has failed -- because he doesn't have the Silk he came to get (whether it is because Silk-in-Pig went into Mainframe instead of into Silk, or because the real Silk Horn has [Silk-43, Silk-23, or both or neither] does not measure up to the literary Silk that Horn and everybody else on Blue is expecting). (I agree I can't follow the motivation of what you have described.)

About the scan I'm calling Silk-23. Yes, it might have some Pas elements added to it, but since I don't recall any non-Silk details that would be positively Pas-like (for example, it seems to me that Pas would love to be a ruler and would never walk away from such a role; polygamy would be a fine thing; etc.), well. Calling it Passilk seems a bit heavy on the Pas side, but if that helps people grasp the concept, it could be useful.

As for when the scan was taken: I thought it was taken from the pool in the hotel, a very happy time (perhaps the happiest moment in Silk's life), not onboard the airship. But again, I could be wrong.

=mantis=

2002-07-18 Adam Stephanides #

Argues against the view that Silk is present inside Horn throughout Silk-23, listing five problems with the theory that Horn plans to move Silk-in-Pig into Silk's body. Grounds each objection in the text: Horn's ignorance of his situation, his continued search for the physical Silk, the absence of any plan in the sons' briefings, and the logic of the eye-sacrifice and monitor scene.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Silk is present in Horn throughout Silk-23; Horn plans to transfer Silk-in-Pig into Silk's body

This reflects my view that Silk is present in "Horn" (or Silkhorn, the Narrator, or whatever you want to call him) throughout.

Hmm, maybe I'd misremembered the earlier debate. But I still see a few problems with this position.

1) Horn doesn't know, or refuses to admit, that he's in Silk's body, so he wouldn't know that he was transferring Silk-in-Pig into Silk's body.

2) Iirc, even after Horn recognizes that Silk is possessing Pig, he continues to look for the physical Silk. Of course, maybe he just wants a second string to his bow.

3) There is no mention in the text of this plan of Horn's nor afaik any indication that Horn thinks of his mission in any other way than bringing the physical Silk to Viron. Perhaps Horn suppressed this plan when talking to his sons, or his sons when they were briefing Daisy; but why would they do that?

4) When the "is Silk in the Narrator on the Whorl" question was last discussed, it was asked (as Rostrum has just asked) how Horn's failure to recognize that he's in Silk's body was to be explained if Silk wasn't present in Horn. Iirc, the answer was given that Horn can't accept that his unworthy self is in the body of his idol Silk. I find this implausible in any case; but if it's true, then it would surely be all the more inconceivable for Horn to contemplate transferring Silk's personality into the body he was inhabiting.

5) In any case, why would Horn think that just because he gives Pig an eye and puts him in front of a monitor, Silk-in-Pig will jump into *him*? For that matter, if Horn wants to be possessed by Silk, why not just stand in front of a monitor and get possessed by Silk-in-Mainframe directly, rather than at second hand?

--Adam

2002-07-18 Michael Straight #

Argues that Horn's reason for putting Pig in front of a monitor is to restore Pig's sight and let Silk-in-Pig leave for wherever he wants to go, not to be possessed by Silk. Offers the possibility that Silk-in-Pig prefers his original body, and compares it to Scylla leaving Oreb for her own body, which may support mantis's theory.
proposes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Horn wants Silk-in-Pig out, not possessed; Silk-in-Pig prefers his original body; Scylla's escape supports mantis's theory

This objection is so obvious, I'm surprised we haven't articulated it before -- I feel like it's been in the back of my head all this time.

The only answer I can think of would be if Silk-in-Pig wants to go to his Silk body more than any of the mainframe versions of Silk.

But in any case, I think Horn's motives are to restore Pig's sight and allow Silk-in-Pig to get out to wherever he wants to go (like Scylla wanting out of Oreb -- although note here that Scylla does not go from Oreb back to Mainframe, but from Oreb to her original body -- so maybe that analogy supports mantis's theory after all!)

-Rostrum
