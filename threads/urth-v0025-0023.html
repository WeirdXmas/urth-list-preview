The Contessa

Urth list · 5 messages · 1999-04-07 to 1999-04-15

1999urthThe Book of the New SunRobert BorskiTony EllisRoy C. LackeyCharles Dye
The thread: Readers debated whether the Contessa Sev meets in the Secret House and Path of Air is the Contessa Carina of Dr Talos's play, and whether she links to Severian's mother Catherine through name derivations. Robert Borski first proposed a Carina-Karina-Katherine link via a Danish name form, and Roy C. Lackey extended it, arguing the play parodies Urth's last day and citing matching imagery. Tony Ellis disputed both, holding that Severian's statement that her face was unknown to him rules out the identification and that the Danish name sources do not support the chain. Charles Dye's side remark about Severian recognizing his own image on the chrisos drew a separate dispute, but the thread ends with the Contessa identification unresolved.
  • Robert Borski: Contessa Carina is linked to Karina, Katherine, and Severian's mother Catherine via a C-to-K name change
  • Tony Ellis: the woman led away is the Contessa herself, but Severian's unknown face rules out the Katherine link and the name chain is unsupported
  • Roy C. Lackey: the Path of Air Contessa is Dr Talos's Contessa, the play parodies Urth's last day, and Catherine and Katharine are the same person
  • Charles Dye: Severian obviously knew his chrisos image since he posed for it, and he gave three reasons for denying Hadelin's point

Themes: Stories within stories, Names and their meanings, Mirrors, pictures and art

1999-04-07 Robert Borski #

Argues that the Contessa from Dr Talos's play, named Carina in Eschatology and Genesis, is linked to the woman Severian sees led away in the Secret House, via the name Karina. Grounds this in the name change from C to K, a Danish form of Katherine, tying her to Saint Katharine or to Severian's mother Catherine.
proposes · source-and-allusion · puzzle-solving · theories: The Contessa is Katharine or Catherine; Carina's name changed to Karina

Tony Ellis is quite right in stating this, but neglects to add that the Contessa's name in Eschatology and Genesis is Carina.

Change the C to K and you have Karina, a Danish variant for Katherine, so clearly (as I've argued before) the woman is linked to either Saint Katharine, patron saint of the Torturer's guild, or Catherine, Severian's mother.

Whether you think the arrested woman is literally Katharine or Catherine, I lead to the individual to decide.

Robert Borski

1999-04-13 Tony Ellis #

Argues that the woman Severian sees led away is not merely playing the Contessa but actually is the Contessa, and that her mutual recognition with Severian comes from the moment having already been played out in Dr Talos's play. Grounds this in Severian's statement that her face was unknown to him and in his perception of a drama already played out before, rejecting Roy's court-acquaintance reading.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: The woman Severian sees is the Contessa; Severian and the Contessa recognize each other through Talos's play

Either you two are missing the point or I am <g>. She isn't "in the role of" the Contessa, she -is- the Contessa. She's being arrested for whatever reason the Contessa was being arrested in the play (although I seem to recall she wasn't being arrested at all, but escorted). There's no suggestion that she and Severian have met before at court or anything: Severian specifically says that her face wasn't known to him.

He "recognises" her and she him because this moment has happened before - in the sub-reality of Dr Talos's play. That's why Severian sees in the Contessa's eyes the recognition that they are both in "a drama that had been played out before", or words to that effect.

I can still remember getting to this bit the first time I read Urth of the New Sun, and the penny suddenly dropping. My jaw literally fell open.

1999-04-13 Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that the Contessa Sev meets in the Path of Air is the Contessa of Dr Talos's play, and that the play parodies Urth's last day, reprising a further drama. Builds on Robert Borski's earlier arguments and on mantis's claim that Catherine and Katharine are the same person, citing matching descriptions of dark hair, olive complexion, and mirror-like water imagery.
proposes · source-and-allusion · puzzle-solving · theories: The Contessa is the Contessa of Talos's play; The play parodies Urth's last day; Catherine and Katharine are the same person; Severian sees his own image in the Contessa

of" the Contessa, she -is- the Contessa. She's being arrested for whatever reason the Contessa was being arrested in the play (although I seem to recall she wasn't being arrested at all, but escorted). There's no suggestion that she and Severian have met before at court or anything: Severian specifically says that her face wasn't known to him. He "recognizes" her and she him because this moment has happened before - in the sub-reality of Dr Talos's play. That's why Severian sees in the Contessa's eyes the recognition that they are both in "a drama that had been played out before", or words to that effect.<<

You're right; escorted, not arrested, is better. Remember: Nessus is already underwater, the Urth is coming to an end very quickly, and people know it. So why would anyone be wasting time bothering with this woman for any Urthly reason? How about an Ushasly (sic?) reason?

The events of the play are a parody of Urth's last day, parts of which Sev witnesses and relates. I say parody because the play only approximates what actually happens and some of the player's roles are conflated. That the woman Sev sees in the Path of Air is the play's Contessa is undoubted. But is that the only drama being reprised? BTW, some of the arguments made here were first advanced by Robert Borski. See his 4-8-99 post. mantis has argued that Catherine and Katharine are the same person.

Compare the "Contessa's" description:

"At first I could not see her eyes, which were bent toward the floor and lost in her raven-dark hair.
Then (I could not tell by what chance) she glanced up at me. Hers was a lovely face of that complexion called olive and as smoothly oval as an olive, too, with something in it that tore my heart; and though it was strange to me, I had the sensation of return once again."

With that of "Katharine" at the Feast:

"She was tall and slender, though not so tall nor so slender as Thecla, dark of complexion, dark of eye, raven of hair. Hers was such a face as I have never seen elsewhere, like a pool of pure water found in the midst of a wood."

Note the mirror imagery of the water (I believe this has been pointed out before). Could Sev be seeing his own image in her face and not realize it? He couldn't see his similarity to Ouen, though others could. He didn't recognize his own image on coins. Also, many mirrors are oval-shaped. Dark complexion, dark eyes, raven hair; from Wolfe what more can you expect? See also where Sev meets Ouen in _Citadel_.

And the Contessa's line from the play (spoken to Meschia):

"If my body held a part of yours--liquescent tissue locked in my loins..."

Echoes of the conception of the White Fountain? Aka New Sun, aka Severian?

Roy

1999-04-14 Charles Dye #

Disputes Roy C. Lackey's suggestion that Severian failed to recognize his own image on the chrisos, arguing that he obviously knew it since he posed for the coin. Gives three reasons for his denial to Hadelin, dry humour, reluctance to start a discussion that would sound insane, and an echo of Graves's Claudius the God.
disputes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion · theories: Severian knowingly recognized his coin portrait, denial was deliberate; Severian's denial echoes Graves's Claudius the God

Minor quibble here: of *course* Sev recognizes his portrait on "his" chrisos. He posed for it! I think his denial to Hadelin is a mixture of (a) dry humor, (b) an unwillingness to begin a discussion which would only make him sound insane, and (c) a deliberate echo of Graves's _Claudius_the_God_.

1999-04-15 Tony Ellis #

Disputes the claim that the Contessa Carina is Katherine, arguing Severian's statement that her face was unknown to him contradicts it. Also challenges the supporting chain of name derivations (Carina, Karina, Karen, Katherine) as unsupported by Danish name sites.
disputes · puzzle-solving · source-and-allusion · theories: Carina the Contessa is Katherine

<snip>

The similarities are certainly suggestive. But if Severian can give us that vivid description of Katherine, how could he say of the Contessa "her face was not known to me" if she was the same woman?

Robert was kind enough to quote me his reference for this Carina = Catherine identification, and, ingrate that I am, I have to say I find it pretty tenuous. It goes:
Carina = Karina = variation on Karen = Danish form of Katherine None of the Danish Name sites I found on the Web (of which there are a surprising number) made even this second-hand association.

Hmmm. Sorry guys, but I remain skeptical. :-)
