2003-04-12 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Reports that the author is selling leftover imperfect copies of the Lexicon Urthus reference book on eBay, in damaged-shipping and cover-length flaw lots, with the last batch ending soon. Responds to Matt DeLuca's question about whether the book can be found online and whether the author is on the list, answering with sales details instead of an opinion on the book.
answers

I do not know about the availabilty of standard copies, but I've been auctioning-off "less-than-perfect" copies on eBay. (Each one has all the pages, is legible, and all that. It is just that some were "hurt" in shipping, others have manufacturing oddities.)

The "Z" series (hurt books) had 12 copies and each sold for around $20 (plus shipping). The "Y" series (manufacturing oddities -- mainly a front cover that is visibly longer than the back cover) has 17 copies and the first auctions will start in about a week. The Y series is the last of it, and in a month or two they should be all gone.

=mantis=

2003-04-13 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Reports that a Z and Y booklet taxonomy has been discovered and that a book can show both symptoms, with the Y symptom dominant in classifying it. Responds to Jeff Wilson's remark about the anonymous ancestor copy, and mentions Sirius Fiction booklets and eBay sales of the booklets.
informs

Writing about the Z and Y series, Jeff Wilson asked:

More like yours is the annonymous ancestor to all the others, the first to escape the "to burn box"! (I really was going to destroy them, as you know -- it is just my soft heart and the terrible sense of waste that kept me receptive to another way, and eBay auctions seem to be perfect.)

That was before the taxonomies of Z and Y were discovered. OTOH, it is possible for a given book to have both symptoms, it is just that the Y is dominant for determining whether a book is Z or Y.
