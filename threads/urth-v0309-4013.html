reckless speculation on Typhon and Quetzal

Urth list · 7 messages · 2003-09-04 to 2003-09-04

2003urthThe Book of the New SunThe Book of the Long SunJames WynnChris PJames JordanLisa Schaffer-Doggett
The thread: Posters on the Urth list speculated freely about whether Quetzal, Pas, Silk and Severian are linked to Typhon, serpents and Apollo through archetypes and Graves's myths. James Wynn and Lisa Schaffer-Doggett argued for literal or deep links, with Schaffer-Doggett insisting that echoes in a puzzle-like work may be deliberate clues. James Jordan pushed back, warning that readers mistake shared archetypes for shared identity and noting Wolfe said Typhon knew nothing of the inhumi, so he granted only a symbolic link for Quetzal. Chris added that Wolfe drew on many contradictory Attis and Dionysus variants, supporting a reading of Wolfe as a mythic synthesist. The thread ended without agreement, with the identity-versus-archetype dispute left open.
  • James Wynn: Pas is the serpent Ladon and Quetzal is literally related to Typhon, with many Whorl figures being serpents
  • Lisa Schaffer-Doggett: Quetzal and Silk link to Typhon and Dionysus, and Apollo resemblances may be meaningful clues in a puzzle-like work
  • James Jordan: readers too quickly equate similar characters, so Quetzal is only a symbolic serpent, not literally Typhon's kin
  • Chris: Wolfe drew on many contradictory Attis and Dionysus variants, so archetype assignments are hard but his myth command is clear

Themes: Myth and folklore sources, Gnosticism, heresy and other theologies, Doubles, twins and clones

Replies to Don by citing the skin-shedding analogy Wolfe used before and calling his archetypes exasperatingly clever.

Crush responds:
The importance of archetypes in understanding what is going on in the Long and Short Suns can hardly be overemphasized. And I agree that the serpent Ladon the is to be found on the Whorl. The thing is that Quetzal is not the only serpent on Whorl (he's not even the only inhumi). There are lots and lots of them. The Trivigaunte are serpents and so is Newt and Kingcup. The Lemur family are all serpents because they have shed their skin in a bid for immortality as serpents were classically believed to do. Wolfe has used this shedding skin analogy before. And so consequently, I have asserted that *Pas* is Ladon. Pas is Typhon's "son" in the same complex (and Biblical) sense that Number Five was Maitre's "son". He is a serpent in that he Typhon having shed his skin to acheive immortality.

The exasperating part of assigning archetypes in Wolfe's works is that he is so clever about it.

-- Crush

2003-09-04 Lisa Schaffer-Doggett #

Speculates that Quetzal is linked to Typhon and Silk through Dionysus as the only Sun-archetype inhumu, using Graves's myths and cloning to make Silk both father and brother of Severian. Builds on James Wynn's remark about serpent archetypes, adding Apollo/Apollyon as a further link to Severian and the Destroying Sun.
proposes · myth-and-structure · source-and-allusion · theories: Quetzal identified with Typhon through Dionysus; Silk is Typhon's clone and Severian's father; Severian archetypally linked to Apollo and Apollyon

Yes, but Quetzal is the only inhumu who is a Sun archetype, Dionysus. This singles him out as being somehow related to Typhon and Silk (and Severian) imo. According to Graves there are only two, one for each half of the year. Mr. Wolfe is an amazing exploiter of the disconnects between various versions of a myth, using science fiction as the glue. For example, one of the fatal (and annoying) problems with Graves is that he supports an argument with the myth of Cronos and Zeus (father and son) one minute and butresses the rest of the argument with the myth of Set/Typhon and Osiris (brothers) ignoring the obvious problems this poses. Cloning solves this problem in the Sun cycle. If Typhon is Severian's genetic father then Silk (as Typhon's clone) is also Severian's father (Cronos and Zeus). However Silk, as Typhon's clone, is also in a sense his son and thus he and Severian could also be considered brothers (Set/Typhon and Osiris). I'll belabor my point with something I thought of yesterday. I'm not sure if this has been discussed but one of Severian's obvious archetypes is Apollo, or Apollon as I keep seeing it lately. Well Apollo is connected to Apollyon the Destroyer who is not really a fallen angel but the righteous angel of Hell's pit. Severian the Destroying Sun. So to my way of thinking (which I know many discount) it makes perfect sense that Mr. Wolfe would identify Quetzal with Typhon by identifying him with Dionysis (though there are a myriad of other reasons for that in addition. What a genius!) and also identify him as the serpent of Colchis through the biology (two or three books later, good lord!) of the inhumi's reproductive cycle.

Indeed! It's maddening. Speaking of which, I'm being told I have to go to my room and take my medication now.
Don

2003-09-04 Lisa Schaffer-Doggett #

Argues that in a work built as a puzzle, archetypal echoes such as a character resembling Apollo are not placed carelessly and so may be meaningful clues. Responds to James Jordan's caution that readers wrongly see identity where only archetype operates, pushing back on the idea that resemblance to a figure like Apollo cannot signal a deeper link to Severian.
disputes · puzzle-solving · myth-and-structure · theories: Typhon and Quetzal are versions of Severian

Yes, but in a work that is set up as a puzzle archetypal cue and clues should not be (and I think are not) carelessly placed.

Don

Argues that readers too quickly treat characters who resemble one another as the same person, when they may merely share an archetype, using Apollo-like traits as an example. Responds to a quoted remark by Crush that archetypes are central to understanding the Long and Short Sun books, and cautions against identity-based readings.

2003-09-04 James Jordan #

Disputes James Wynn's suggestion that Quetzal is literally related to Typhon, citing Wolfe's remark that Typhon knew nothing of the inhumi. Grants a symbolic link, reading Quetzal as a serpent in Typhon's garden whose Biblical allusions set him against Pas's plans.
disputes · allegory-and-typology · author-and-biography · theories: Quetzal is literally related to Typhon; Quetzal is a serpent in Typhon's garden

IIRC, Wolfe has said that Typhon had no knowledge of the inhumi, so any kind of literal or clone/paternity relationship between him and Quetzal seems to be out. Symbolically you may be on to something, but it has appeared to me that Quetzal is a serpent in Typhon's garden, and the allusions are Biblical in some kind of way. Like the ur-serpent, Quetzal has entered the garden and has his own plans for its people, plans quite different from Pas's.

2003-09-04 Chris P #

Reports that while reading There Are Doors the poster tracked references to Attis and Dionysus, finding many variant myths of Attis that contradict one another. Argues Wolfe drew on all of these versions, sometimes at the same time, and praises his command of myth; this replies to Crush's remark about the difficulty of assigning archetypes in Wolfe's work.
supports · source-and-allusion · myth-and-structure · theories: Wolfe drew on contradictory versions of the Attis myth simultaneously

You said a mouthful. I just read "There Are Doors" and was playing around tracking references to Attis/Dionysus/etc. looking for more keys that I hadn't picked up. In the process I found lots of alternate stories of Attis I hadn't known about, many of which contradict each other, and Wolfe appears to have been using ALL OF THEM, sometimes at the same time. His grasp of myth continues to impress me.
