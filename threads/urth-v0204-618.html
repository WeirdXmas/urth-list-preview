2002-04-15 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that The Fifth Head of Cerberus is full of Derridean play, visible to readers whether or not the author intended it. Defends this against the authorial-intent view by holding that once published, a work's meaning is generated when it meets a reader's mind, though he has reservations about postmodernism.
supports · theories: The Fifth Head of Cerberus is full of Derridean play; Meaning is generated in the reader's encounter with the text

This is why I said it was full of Derridean play. It doesn't have to be intended by the author, only visible to the reader. While I have a lot of reservations about a lot of pomo-ism, and do have some respect for "what the author meant," I am fundamentally in agreement with the pomo concept that, once the work is published, it is out of the artist's hands and its "meaning" is what is generated in the space where it encounters a reader's mind, whether this is what the author intended or no.

--Dan'l
