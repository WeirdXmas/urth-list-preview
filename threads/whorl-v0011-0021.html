Does Gene Wolfe read this?

Whorl list · 6 messages · 2000-07-31 to 2000-08-02

2000whorlThe Book of the Long SunWilliam H. AnsleyPatrick O'LearyAlice TurnerAlex David Grocejismulkstis
The thread: A newcomer asked whether Gene Wolfe reads the mailing list and whether fan speculation has ever led him to revise his plots or twists. William Ansley answered that Wolfe's puzzles are his own responsibility, that fans rarely guess beyond obvious twists, and that the list was too young to shape the later books. Akt relayed a secondhand report that Wolfe does not use email, and Patri10629 added that Wolfe knows the list, has no email, and once had discussions printed for him. Alex David Groce joked that Wolfe should stay offline, and the thread ended in social banter rather than any firm answer.
  • William Ansley: Wolfe's puzzles are his own responsibility; fans rarely guess more than obvious twists; the list is too young to have shaped later books
  • akt: Wolfe does not use email, per what other list members reported; older writers can be technophobic
  • Patri10629: Wolfe knows the list but has no email; Joe Mayhew once printed discussions for him
  • Alex David Groce: jokingly suggests Wolfe should stay offline because his lack of internet seems to help his fiction

2000-07-31 jismulkstis #

Introduces the poster and asks whether Gene Wolfe reads the mailing list archives and whether fan speculation has ever led him to revise a plot or twist. Wonders what secret the inhumi could hold that hasn't been speculated upon, and thanks Wolfe for the books.
questions

Hello. I've not posted before, but I've read all of the New/Long/Short Sun books written to date. I've also read all of the Urth/Whorl archives. Mr. Wolfe creates
wonderful worlds.

I have to wonder whether you all are making life more
difficult for him. Does he read these archives? Does
anyone know whether any of his writing has ever been
influenced by fan speculation? Specifically, has he ever revised a story due to someone having guessed a plot or
plot twist? I wonder what kind of secret the inhumi
could have that hasn't already been speculated upon here.

I'd hate to write a 12 book series with all of you folks second guessing my every keystroke.

Thank you all for the stimulation and speculation. And
thank you, Gene Wolfe. More than once, you've taken me
from the E train (Queens to Manhattan) into strange and
true worlds.

Joseph I. Smulkstis (Muskrat)

PS: Nutria, I'm not ripping you off. I was a muskrat fan before I knew you existed. I'm pleased to share your
habitat.

2000-07-31 William H. Ansley #

Responds to a newcomer's question about whether Gene Wolfe reads the mailing list and has revised his fiction in response to fan speculation. Argues that Wolfe's puzzles are his own responsibility, that fans rarely guess more than obvious twists, and that the list is too young to have shaped the later books.
answers

At 3:22 AM +0000 7/31/00, Joseph I. Smulkstis (Muskrat)

I think you may have hit upon one of the few statements that everyone who reads this list can agree on. <g>

I seem to recall that someone who seemed to know said that Wolfe did read this mailing list, at least occasionally, but I can't find the message. If we are making life tough for Wolfe, it's his own fault; we're not holding a gun to his head forcing him to create all these tricky little puzzles and put them in his books, after all. <g> I very much doubt Wolfe has ever been influenced by fan speculation in the way you suggest. I doubt any of his readers (whether in this list or not) have ever guessed any but the most obvious plot twists. And even if one of us has and posts it to this mailing list, how many of Wolfe's potential readers are we affecting? Not a very high percentage, I would guess.

Actually, this list has only been around long enough to second guess the last Long Sun book and the Short Sun books, I think.

For the minuscule part I may have played in the "stimulation and speculation," you're welcome.

2000-08-01 Alice Turner #

Relays a claim that Gene Wolfe does not use email, passing along what other list members reported, and suggests older writers can be technophobic regardless of background. Responds to Alice Turner's remark about being corrected on the matter, adding a secondhand report rather than a reading of the books.
informs

From William:

Well, my knowledge is pretty limited, but from what mantis and Patrick (both of whom seem to be in hibernation) have told us, Wolfe doesn't even use email. Many people his age do sometimes use email but get flustered by the Web (i.e. checking archives, etc.). It's true that his background would seem to make him more comfortable with technology, but in my own experience with older writers
they can get crankily technophobic no matter what their background. There is also the strange m/f thing that someday I will write a little piece on.

-alga

Reports that the poster is on vacation and comments on mantis torpor, then notes that Wolfe knows the list but has no email and that Joe Mayhew once printed discussions for him. Adds praise for rereading On Blue's Waters and In Green's Jungles, calling them better each time.

Just reread OBW and IGJ--they just get better and better.
