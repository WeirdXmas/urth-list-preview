Themes: Myth and folklore sources

2000-04-11 Timothy Reilly #

Argues that the name Echidna in Wolfe's work refers to the Greek monster, half woman and half serpent, mother of Typhon's brood, not the hedgehog-like animal most readers think of. Supports this by citing Hesiod and standard Greek mythology compendiums, and notes that Lexicon Urthus lists alternative definitions while the poster concedes this is probably what Wolfe meant.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Echidna refers to the Greek monster mother of Typhon's brood

name was applied to a frightful monster, half woman, half serpent, who ate men raw and bore a brood of monsters to Typhon. The name means "she-viper." Check it out in any Greek mythology compendium, or go to the source, Hesiod.

As I said in my original post on the subject, I possess Lexicon Urthus so I know there are alternative definitions, and doubtless this is what Wolfe had in mind. But you see when someone says Echidna here, what springs to mind is the cute hedgehog, not the above. So you see my difficulty. Or perhaps not. Anyway, I'll shut up about it now!
