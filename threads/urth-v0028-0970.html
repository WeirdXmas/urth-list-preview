Little, Big: Tarots and cycles

Urth list · 1 messages · 2000-05-04 to 2000-05-04

2000urthAdam Stephanides

2000-05-04 Adam Stephanides #

Disputes mantis's cycle-and-numerology defence of the fifty-two-card tarot idea in Little, Big by raising two objections: the 'so few' fairies remaining suggests a much larger original number, and nothing shows the Drinkwaters replacing fairies is cyclical. Grounds the objections in Sophie and Lilac's conversation in 'A Parliament' (VI, 2), Sophie's recalled shock at fifty-two, and the single fairy race in the concentric worlds.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Fairy numbers were far larger originally, not fifty-two; Drinkwater replacement of fairies is not cyclical

You're welcome. That's one good habit, at any rate, I derived from my years in grad school.

An ingenious scheme to save your theory, and one which neatly solves the problem of the number of cards, which had occurred to me, if only vaguely (I never saw alga's piece). I see two objections, though.

1) The repeated statements, in the conversation between Sophie and the returned Lilac, that the number of fairies left is "so few" ("A Parliament," VI, 2) suggest that the number remaining is only a small proportion, and that there were originally hundreds or even thousands, too many for any tarot deck. I seem to recall Sophie's shock when she first guesses that there are only fifty-two left, which would support the same conclusion, but I can't find the passage.

2) If the current number of Drinkwaters isn't equal to the original number of fairies, then what reason is there to think that the Drinkwaters replacing the fairies is a cyclical event at all? As far as I can remember, the only "race" of fairies we ever hear about are the ones the Drinkwaters interact with. (Even if we accept the Bramble-Drinkwater theory of concentric worlds as accurate, this doesn't imply successive types of fairies, since it's said explicitly that one race of fairies occupies all the inner "worlds.")

--Adam
