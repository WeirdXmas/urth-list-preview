Urth list · 13 messages · 2009-01-07 to 2009-01-11

2009urthThe Book of the New SunThe Book of the Long SunAllan AndersonTim WaltersMatthew KeeleyGwern BranwenDavid StockhoffSon of WitzStuart Hamm
The thread: The thread opened as a question about the spelling 'Chrasmologic' and proofreading errors in Lake of the Long Sun, which David Stockhoff answered by calling 'Charismatic' a typo. It then wandered into the ravens of Wolfe's Long Sun and Odin's Hugin and Munin, the raven Oreb, and the chough. David Stockhoff argued the Bible and a dictionary decode Oreb, tracing the name to a Midianite prince slain by Gideon. Most other posts were off-topic San Francisco chat about parks, ravens and a dog attack, with no real argument over Wolfe's text. The thread ended in drift rather than resolution.
  • David Stockhoff: Chrasmologic is the usual spelling in the Long Sun books and Charismatic is a typo
  • David Stockhoff: the Bible and a dictionary are enough to decode the raven Oreb, a Midianite prince slain by Gideon
  • Allan Anderson: the ravens of The Hobbit and Wolfe's Dalemen echo Odin's Hugin and Munin, linked to thought and memory
  • Son of Witz: Odin could bear thoughtlessness but not forgetfulness, tied to the raven comparison

Themes: Names and their meanings, Memory and forgetting, Myth and folklore sources

2009-01-07 David Stockhoff replying to David Stockhoff #

Argues that the Bible and a dictionary are enough to decode Wolfe's raven Oreb, tracing the name to a Midianite prince slain by Gideon in Judges 7. Supports this with Shakespeare's references to ravens as omens and notes that the chough mentioned in the books is unlike a raven.
informs · source-and-allusion · theories: Oreb is named after the Midianite prince from Judges

The Bible (along with The Call of Cthulhu and Other Weird Stories and a good dictionary) is all you'll ever really need to understand Wolfe:

Oreb

Meaning: raven [from Hebrew "black"]
a prince of Midian
<http://www.christiananswers.net/dictionary/midian.html>, who, being defeated by Gideon
<http://www.christiananswers.net/dictionary/gideon.html> and put to straits, was slain along with Zeeb
<http://www.christiananswers.net/dictionary/zeeb.html> (Judg. 7:20-25 <http://www.christiananswers.net/bible/judg7.html#20>)
Many of the Midianites
<http://www.christiananswers.net/dictionary/midianite.html> perished along with him (Ps. 83:9
<http://www.christiananswers.net/bible/psa83.html#9>; Isa. 10:26 <http://www.christiananswers.net/bible/isa10.html#26>).

But Silk's Oreb is also clearly the Odin-like familiar of a witch, god, or prophet. Shakespeare referenced ravens in this sense:

"But the most frequent reference made by Shakespeare to this bird has regard to its supposed boding power. It is called the ?fatal raven? [/Titus Andronicus ? II, 3/]. A messenger of ill news is said to ?sing a raven?s note? [/2nd Henry VI ? III, 2/]."

Choughs are native to the Old World and very different from ravens, however. See Wiki for a photo.

2009-01-07 Son of Witz replying to David Stockhoff #

Chats about the proofreading thread, the punctuation problems of OCR scans, and affection for the raven Oreb from The Book of the Long Sun, asking how to pronounce "chough". Mostly personal digression about the poster's work and local ravens, with a passing question whether talking ravens allude to Shakespeare.
chats

Proofreader.
Not a job for me. As a designer, I'm thrilled one of my colleagues is a UCSB Valedictorian. I give her everything, even my weirdo album projects. I considered for a brief second doing a proofreading of those pdfs. no fuckin way. Since it's OCR, the punctuation is the most problematic, and one of the harder things to proofread since we tend to read punctuation subliminally.

While I was writing about laughs in Long Sun, I forgot to mention Oreb. loving that damn bird. Do you pronounce Chough as "Chuff" "Choff" "Coff" "Cuff"???? I live right near a huge park and we've got fat ravens and crows all over the neighborhood. My son and I have seen them catching rats and scarfing down on our neighbors' roofs. I love their obnoxious cackles. If I could have a talking raven, I would. Fuck a parrot. This is a Shakespeare allusion, no? talking ravens? I remember that from somewhere.

~wtiz
