Drunken Jahlee

Urth list · 3 messages · 2005-08-07 to 2005-08-07

2005urthThe Book of the Short SunThe Book of the Long SunthalassocratRoy C. LackeyDon Doggett
The thread: Readers asked whether Jahlee's parentage traces to Chenille's drinking, a question tied to whether the inhumi she came from were sentient. Roy C. Lackey argued Chenille drank only once, at Lake Limna, yet still passed something of herself to Jahlee, citing passages from Return to the Whorl and On Blue's Waters. Thalassocrat proposed that Jahlee's parent drank human blood, that the victim was Chenille, and tested this against the timing of colonist departure and Sinew's infancy. Don Doggett then argued that nothing in the Long Sun and Short Sun books can reliably be attributed to Silk, since Horn and Nettle wrote them and Silk's reality rests only on characters who claim to have known him. The thread ends with three competing positions and no resolution.
  • Roy C. Lackey: Chenille drank only once at Lake Limna, yet Jahlee carries something of her essence, supported by textual passages
  • thalassocrat: Jahlee's parent drank human blood, likely Chenille, since only blood-drinking parentage makes inhumi sentient; checked against the timeline
  • don doggett: nothing in the Long Sun and Short Sun books can be reliably attributed to Silk, given the narrators and authorship

Themes: Robots, androids and artificial life, Beasts and the animal in us, Unreliable narration

2005-08-07 Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that Chenille was drunk only once, at Lake Limna, yet Jahlee nonetheless carries something of Chenille's essence in her soul. Supports Crush's reading by citing a Return to the Whorl passage on voice and spirit and an On Blue's Waters passage where the newly risen inhuma Jahlee looks to Silkhorn like a woman.
supports · close-textual-reading · theories: Jahlee carries something of Chenille's essence

Iirc, Chenille was drunk only once in the seven volumes. That was when she and Silk went to Lake Limna. They had separated, with Silk going out to the shrine. It was Auk who later found her drunk. Afaik, Silk never saw or heard her drunk.

Nevertheless, you are right about something of the essence of Chenille being in Jahlee's "soul". I don't know how it got there, but it's there. The brief conversation Silkhorn had with Silk-in-Pig (RTTW, 174) contains an otherwise pointless discussion about voice and spirit, and even mentions Chenille.

Also, after Silkhorn dug her up and her feet first touched stone after feeding on that cow, the inhuma that was Jahlee looked to him like a woman, "an emaciated woman with burning eyes whose hairless skull somehow conveyed the impression of lank reddish hair." (OBW, 314) This was before Silkhorn knew anything at all about Jahlee, who had been buried for an unknown number of years.

This complicates Silk's theoretical relationship to his sister-in-a-sense Chenille and his "daughter"-in-a-sense Jahlee.

-Roy

2005-08-07 thalassocrat replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that Jahlee's parent must have drunk human blood, since only blood-drinking parentage makes inhumi sentient, and proposes that the victim was Chenille, one of the first colonists. Works from the timing: the parent, Jahlee's birth and her growth must fit between the colonists' departure from the Whorl and baby Sinew's infancy.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · puzzle-solving · theories: Jahlee's parent drank Chenille's blood; Inhumi become sentient only through human blood-drinking parents

On Sat, 06 Aug 2005 23:58:26 -0700 "Roy C. Lackey"
<Interesting things about Jahlee and Chenille, snipped>

What were Jahlee's origins? She bites baby Sinew pretty soon after the first colonists arrive on Blue. She tells of her childhood before then, striving to become strong enough to leave Green.

I don't think that "wild" inhumi would have that striving to go to Blue; Jahlee was surely sentient at the time. In my opinion, inhumi only get to be sentient if they are born to a human-blood-drinking parent. So my conclusion is that at least one of Jahlee's parents drank someone's blood.

If this happened on Green, the timing is very tight. The parent needs to drink the blood of some human, give birth to Jahlee, and Jahlee needs to build up her strength, fly to Blue and find Sinew, all in the time between the first humans leaving the Whorl and Sinew reaching some age which leaves him still an infant. It wouldn't be very surprising to find that the victim was Chenille, one of the first batch of colonists.

(If the blood-drinking didn't happen on Green, it must have happened on the Whorl, and the parent must have been one of the inhumi the Neighbors placed there. Further, the Neighbors must have brought the parent back to Green. Why they would do that, I don't know.)

2005-08-07 Don Doggett replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that nothing in the Long Sun and Short Sun books can be reliably attributed to Silk, since Horn and Nettle wrote the Long Sun books and Silk's spirit may not be in the Short Sun narrator. Grounds this in the Short Sun narrator's identity and the final part written by Chenille's successor, noting Silk's reality rests only on characters who claim to have known him.
proposes · author-and-biography · theories: Silk's spirit is not in the Short Sun narrator; Horn and Nettle wrote the Long Sun books

This brings up an important point that bears
repeating: Nothing in any of these books can be 100%
reliably attributed to Silk. Horn and Nettle wrote
the BotLS, there's no guarantee that Silk's spirit is
in the narrator's body in SS and the last part of SS
is written by what's her name that's married to Hoof
or Hide. The only reason we can even surmise Silk was
a "real" person is that there are people alive in the
narrative who claim to have known him. If it weren't
so brilliant a device it'd be damned annoying.

Don
