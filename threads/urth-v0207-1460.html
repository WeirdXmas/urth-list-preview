2002-07-29 James Jordan #

Proposes reading Peace as a purgatory in which Weer, a ghost, reviews the memory-house of his life on the way to peace, and asks whether Margaret's prayers for Weer could lead to a more positive destiny. Responds to James Jordan's essay-announcement post, suggesting Julius may allude to Julian the Apostate and comparing Weer's situation to Marley's chains in A Christmas Carol.
proposes · theology-and-religion · allegory-and-typology · theories: Peace is about purgatory and Weer's ghostly review of his life; Julius alludes to Julian the Apostate; Margaret's prayers may bring Weer a positive destiny

Cool! If you want to stack something else on top of it all, maybe Julius also alludes Julian the Apostate?
I've always taken *Peace* to be about purgatory, a man (ghost in purgatory) reviewing the memory-house of his life and coming to grips with it, on his way to peace. I take it that you are saying Weer is trapped in the house of his life, doomed to review it forever, cycling through it but never completing it. I think of Marley's "These are the chains I forged in life." Compelling evidence.
But what about the title of the novel? That was what has made me think of purgatory as the overarching concept. Is there any reason to believe that something like the prayers of Margaret for Weer might factor into a more positive destiny for Weer?

2002-07-30 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Disputes Robert Borski's claim that Doris in PEACE is almost certainly the daughter of Den Weer and Sherry Gold, arguing the D'Or connection is too weak for near-certainty. Notes that Doris means "gift" in Greek and was an Oceanid in myth, which might suggest a different, mercantile parentage.
disputes · puzzle-solving · source-and-allusion · theories: Doris is daughter of Den Weer and Sherry Gold; Doris has a mercantile parentage

Cool ... One thing, though ... Robert, you refer to Doris as "almost certainly" the daughter of Den Weer and Sherry Gold. Perhaps I've missed (or simply forgotten) something in the past coinscriptions on PEACE, but ... how does this, which seems to-me-here-now highly conjectural, reach the point of almost certainty?

The "D'Or" connection is not in and of itself sufficient for such a strong claim, and may be weak: "Doris" comes from Greek and means "gift," a point which would not escape Gene "Onomastics B Me" Wolfe. (A gift from or to whom is another question entirely.)

It is also, if I understand rightly, the name of a
daughter of Oceanus in Greek myth, which might actually
suggest another, mercantile, parentage...

--Blattid
