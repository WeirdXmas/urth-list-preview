2008-06-11 Dave Tallman #

Proposes that the sun's output varied in Severian's time, citing the old man in Casdoe's house, and that Ymar the Almost Just parallels Severian. Answers an earlier objection that bright sunshine also occurs in Sev's time, and cites self-fulfilling time loops, including Juturna saving Severian from drowning because he spoke.
proposes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Sun's output varied within Severian's lifetime; Ymar's career parallels Severian's; Ymar's experience began guild child-taking; Juturna's rescue forms a time loop

All right, how about this explanation? The old man in Casdoe's house (Sword XV) says "That was the brightest day I've ever seen. The sun had new life to him, the way a man will when he was sick yesterday and will be sick tomorrow, but today he walks around and laughs so that if a stranger was to come he'd think there was nothing wrong... They always say in prayers that the New Sun will be too bright to look at... But that day I learned it was all true..."

If the sun's output was variable within the old man's lifetime, it might also have been in Severian's. Then Sev might not find it remarkable to see a bright day at times. Most of the days he spent as Conciliator were cloudy, anyway.

By the way, have you noticed how the life of Ymar the Almost Just parallels the life of Severian? He's an apprentice torturer who becomes autarch, and his career is jump-started by miraculous intervention. He underwent the trial, too. Could early interventions by the Hiero-dudes in Reechy's life have formed the basis for a Conciliator-like legend before Sev became Conciliator?

It's vital for Sev's history that the guild start taking the children of women who fall into their hands instead of having their own children (Shadow II). And Ymar apparently did this because of his own personal experience in seeing how Madame Prefect Prisca behaved. If someone else became autarch it might not have happened. This is just one of many self-fulfilling time loops in UotNS. Another fun one is Juturna's saving Sev from drowning "because you spoke" (Urth XLIII).

2008-06-11 Adam Thornton replying to Dave Tallman #

Argues that Wolfe's New Sun is essentially Jack Vance's Dying Earth rewritten by a better writer, citing the wildly variable sun as a shared feature. Responds to Dave Tallman's suggestion that Severian's sun may have been variable within his lifetime, offering the Vance parallel as support for the comparison.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: New Sun is Dying Earth rewritten by a better writer

Note that a wildly variable sun is a feature of Jack Vance's _Dying Earth_, and that there's a lot to be said for the theory that _New Sun_ is _Dying Earth_ rewritten by a better writer.

Adam
