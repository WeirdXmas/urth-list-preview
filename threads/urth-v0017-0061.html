Themes: Masks, disguise and shapeshifting, Hidden identities and parentage

1998-07-21 Dan Parmenter #

Argues that the Jeannine-as-aborigine hypothesis for The Fifth Head of Cerberus is gaining credibility from supporting points, while suggesting the novel may be deliberately ambiguous rather than having one right answer. Responds to Prion's post quoting page 52 where No. 5 compares Jeannine's withered legs to childhood parlor-magic sticks, and finds the image of two boys performing magic for a robot wonderful.
disputes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Aunt Jeannine is an aborigine impostor; The Fifth Head of Cerberus has no single right answer

It's also a wonderful image: two little boys putting on a magic show for a robot.

I must say that some of R. Borski's wilder speculations sometimes leave me scratching my head, this one, with the supporting points of others, such as this post, are starting to convince me about the Jeannine-as-abo hypothesis.

But I still have this niggling feeling that 5HC, more than the other major Wolfe novels that I've read is actually ambiguous: that there might not be a right answer, just greater or lesser probabilities.

D
