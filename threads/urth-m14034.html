more thoughts on Jolenta

Urth list · 9 messages · 2010-06-01 to 2010-06-03

2010urthThe Book of the New SunJane DelawneyJerry FriedmanLee BermanCliff JudgeJosh GellerJeff WilsonJack Smith
The thread: Readers debated whether Jolenta, the troupe member in The Claw of the Conciliator, was fully human, and why the Claw failed to save her. Cliff Judge first proposed she was an android, linking her to Lady Katherine, and Jack Smith argued she was a human waitress remade by Dr. Talos. Jerry Friedman and Jane Delawney agreed she was not fully human, with Delawney citing the hidden wires and metal found after her death. Lee Berman offered a rival reading that she is Severian's twin sister Severa, while Jeff Wilson argued the Claw's failure came from Severian's motives and the mechanics of healing rather than her nature. The thread ended with competing readings rather than agreement.
  • Cliff Judge: Jolenta may have been an android all along
  • Jack Smith: Jolenta is a poor human waitress remade by Dr. Talos's procedure
  • Jerry Friedman: the Claw failed because Jolenta was not fully human
  • Lee Berman: Jolenta is Severian's twin sister Severa
  • Jane Delawney: her hidden wires and metal show she was part android
  • Jeff Wilson: Severian's motives and the Claw's healing explain the failure, not her nature

Themes: Beasts and the animal in us, Robots, androids and artificial life, Hidden identities and parentage

2010-06-01 Cliff Judge #

Proposes that Jolenta may have been an android all along, pointing to her apparent robotic manner, the circuitry in her body, and Jonas's odd name for her. Grounds the idea in her remark that the troupe would "destroy" her, her eating, and Jonas's sleeping and eating, and floats a link to Lady Katherine.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Jolenta was an android the whole time; Lady Katherine and Jolenta are the same person; Dr. Talos is an android built by Baldanders

So we've got these plain, poor girl waitign tables at a cafe who is "discovered" by Dr. Talos, who "casts a glamour" on her to make her superhot.

But something about what she said when the troupe split after the thiasus struck me. She said they were going to "destroy" her.

And then there is the fact that her lifeless body, later, has circuitry and stuff going on.

Now the clearest story here is that she was just a poor waitress, and Dr. Talos' thaumaturgy involved prostheses to make her thighs and breasts plump, her face and hair beautiful, and probably to give her confidence as well.

But Jonas was certainly an android, and he fell for her immediately and completely. He once refers to her as "the woman you call Jolenta;" he had another name for her, but perhaps he KNEW another name for her?

Could Jolenta have been an android as well? Perhaps she was an android the whole time, and without bringing attention to this fact, Dr. Talos simply recognized her as such when they first visited the cafe and thanked his lucky stars that he found a model he could work with.

Jolenta ate food on a couple of occasions, didn't she? I believe Jonas is shown to both eat and sleep. I have read it theorized that Dr. Talos is an android built by Baldanders, and he does not eat, sleep, or require the company of women, and we know that Jonas was in the process of fleshifying himself. But still...the idea nags me with how robotic Jolenta seemed.

I also read a theory that the Lady Katherine woman was an android.....ooh. Hey, wonder if they were the same person.

2010-06-01 Jack Smith replying to Cliff Judge #

Argues that Jolenta is a poor human waitress whose wires come from Dr. Talos's transformation procedure, and asks who she was before becoming Jolenta. Supports this by citing the phrase "the woman you call Jolenta", the meaning of the name given in Lexicon Urthus, and the comparison to Hollywood stage names like Marilyn Monroe and Judy Garland.
proposes · puzzle-solving · source-and-allusion · theories: Jolenta is a poor human waitress transformed by Talos; Jolenta's name is a glamour like a Hollywood stage name

I think Jolenta is simply a poor human waitress and the wires are part of Talos' transformation procedure.

"The woman you call Jolenta" is an interesting statement. It calls attention to the fact that she had another name. Does anyone know of any clues about her real name? *Lexicon Urthus *indicates that the meaning of "Jolenta" is "the pretty one." The name is part of the glamour that Dr Talos, like a Hollywood producer, creates. Norma Jeane Baker becomes Marilyn Monroe; Frances Ethel Gumm becomes Judy Garland. Who was the waitress who became Jolenta?

2010-06-02 Lee Berman replying to Cliff Judge #

Argues that Jolenta is Severian's twin sister Severa, citing veiled mystery about her name and hair, her age and height, and incest as a key to Severian's female relatives. Supports the idea by noting the witches' meeting with Severian near the dying Jolenta and their possible interest in his resurrection powers, and it responds to Cliff Judge's earlier post.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Jolenta is Severian's twin sister Severa; Witches want Severian to resurrect Jolenta

I agree with Jane D. (and perhaps Jack Smith?) that Jolenta is a prime candidate for Severian's twin sister, Severa. There is just too much veiled mystery about her to dismiss her as a random floozy in Severian's life. I
find incestual sex to be a key in identifying all Severian's female family members. Dorcas of course, but also Aunt Cyriaca and cousin Agia (desired if not consummated). Thecla, Valeria, and others remain outside possibilities as relatives.

We also have the mystery of Jolenta's real name and the veiled revelation of her true hair color. Jolenta has comparable height to Severian's. We don't know what Jolenta was doing before being a waitress but we do know she is the same age as Severian. Perhaps she left her guild around the same time he did. We really don't know all that witches do, but it is hinted that they have beautification techniques and perform some sort of sexual activity with their clients, both compatitble with what we know Jolenta is open to.

Witches are also associated with resurrection of the dead. Severian meets a couple witches while carrying a dying Jolenta. They don't appear to recognized her or care about her, but I wonder about that coincidence. Was it a random event that the Cumaean and Merryn, denizens of Nessus, appear in the middle of nowhere in Severian's path on some unrelated necromancing errand? I suspect the witches' interest might really be with Severian and his nascent abilities in resurrection and the possibility of utilizing them in reviving a dying errant member of their guild.

(I put Jonas' attraction to Jolenta on a par with Don Quixote and his quest for the love of Dulcinea, or Borat's quest for the love of Pamela Anderson. Highly eccentric guys who pick an impossible, unattainable dream for themselves.)

2010-06-02 Jane Delawney replying to Jerry Friedman #

Agrees with Jerry Friedman that Jolenta was not fully human, arguing that her death in The Claw of the Conciliator shows she is part android, with wires and steel concealed in her flesh. Grounds this in Severian's discovery after her death of her glamour, hair extensions, cosmetic enhancements and hidden metal, which explains why the Claw had no effect on her.
supports · close-textual-reading · theories: Jolenta is half-android, half-woman

I'd agree with Jerry here, when Jolenta has died Severian realises that not only has she been (quite literally!) put under a 'glamor' to make her believe that she is beautiful, not only has she had hair extensions and the local equivalent of breast and 'booty' enhancements, but that there are wires and bands of steel concealed within her flesh. She's kind of half-woman, half- android. No wonder the Claw had no effect.

2010-06-03 Jeff Wilson replying to Jerry Friedman #

Argues that Severian was not motivated to save Jolenta after their sexual encounter, and suggests that the Claw's healing, which tunes back the clock on injured tissue, could dismember Jonas because he is built from parts of a human and a robot. Responds to Jerry Friedman's suggestion that the Claw failed on Jolenta because she was no longer fully human, offering an alternative explanation grounded in Severian's motives and the mechanics of the Claw's healing.
proposes · close-textual-reading · allegory-and-typology · theories: Severian was unmotivated to save Jolenta; Claw healing could dismember Jonas

I think it's possible Sev just wasn't very motivated to save Jolenta; he had already had the grudge sex with her and was done.

As for the chimera Jonas, remember the Claw heals by selectively tuning back the clock
on the injured tissue - this might end up in Jonas becoming dismembered since he is made from leftover parts of at least two bodies, one human and one robot.
