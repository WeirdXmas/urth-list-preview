More lies

Urth list · 6 messages · 1999-11-08 to 1999-11-10

1999urthThe Book of the New SunKevin J. MaroneyMatthew MalthouseWilliam H. AnsleyRoy C. LackeyJMCAzurinMichael Straight
The thread: Roy C. Lackey listed passages in The Book of the New Sun where Severian's narration seems to contradict itself, concluding either that Severian lies or that his memory is imperfect. Kevin J. Maroney argued the slips come from a laboriously reconstructed, imperfectly organized memory, which even a perfect memory would not prevent. William H. Ansley warned against reading Wolfe's errors as deliberate clues, citing Wolfe's own admission of mistakes. Matthew Malthouse proposed that the narrated memories come from several Sevs, including alzebo-made versions, so the inconsistencies need not be lies. Michael Straight and JMCAzurin each offered innocent readings of the 'for the last time' phrase and the swimming outing, and the thread ended with the lie-versus-memory question unresolved.
  • Roy C. Lackey: Severian lies or his memory is imperfect, based on listed contradictions
  • Kevin J. Maroney: slips come from imperfectly organized reconstructed memory
  • William H. Ansley: critics over-read Wolfe's errors as hidden clues since Wolfe makes mistakes
  • Matthew Malthouse: narrated memories come from several Sevs, including alzebo copies
  • Michael Straight: 'for the last time' means he did not know he would leave, not a last visit
  • JMCAzurin: the apparent lapses can be read without assuming lies or memory failure

Themes: Unreliable narration, Memory and forgetting, Doubles, twins and clones

1999-11-08 Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that Severian's narration in The Book of the New Sun contradicts itself in several places, citing his tomb visits, the glass/grass charm, his swimming statements, and his infancy memories. Lists these passages with chapter references and concludes either that Severian lies or that his memory is imperfect.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian's memory is less than perfect; Severian lies to the reader without reason

Here are some occasions where Sev lied to the reader; that, or his perfect memory failed him. The day after his elevation he saw the order for Thecla's excruciation to begin on the following day. (I, XII)

"I could not sleep after that. Instead I went (for the last time, though I did not know it) to the tomb in which I had played as a boy."

Then (IV, XXXVI), as autarch, he returned to the tomb, "The mausoleum where I had played as a boy stood as I had left it, its jammed door three-quarters shut.", to retrieve the false coin given to him by Vodalus.

Also, compare the little "charm" he recounted both when he hid the coin and when he recovered it; they're not quite the same. One uses "glass", the other "grass".

Here's another: Just after describing where he and his friends swam and before recounting his near-drowning (I, II), Sev says: "I have chosen to describe all this now because I never went again after the day on which I saved Vodalus." Then, when he is captain of apprentices (I, X), "I took parties of apprentices to swim, thinking that to be my duty, though I could never dive in deep water without fear." Both statements can't be true.

During his "conversation" with the mandragora (IV, XXXV), another memory of his infancy is awakened: "I remember a time very long ago when I was cold and hungry. I lay upon my back, encircled by brown walls, and heard the sound of my own screams. Yes, I must have been an infant. Not old enough to crawl, I think." Yet (I, II) elsewhere he says: "From my earliest memory I remember all. That first recollection is of piling pebbles in the Old Yard." Any child old enough to pile pebbles is more than old enough to crawl. Note the "brown walls"; the walls of the cells in the Matachin tower are gray, as noted in the breast-feeding memory.

Now, these are just a few examples I stumbled across without really trying, where Sev is either lying for no good reason that I can see, or, more likely (unless Wolfe is sloppier than I believe), where Wolfe is showing the reader that Sev's memory is less than perfect--or both.

Roy

1999-11-08 Kevin J. Maroney #

Argues that Severian's apparent lies in The Book of the New Sun are better explained as slips caused by his imperfectly organized memory, which he reconstructs laboriously in the telling. Responds to Roy's list of Severian's misstatements, noting that even a perfect memory of the kind Severian describes would not protect him from such errors.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian's misstatements are slips of imperfect memory

Or that Severian slips up. Remember that his memory is not perfectly organized; he describes in some detail the difficult process by which he reconstructs his memories. Even if his memory is perfect in the way he describes, it would not function in such a way as to protect him from misstatements like those you listed.

Still, that's an interesting list.

1999-11-08 William H. Ansley #

Argues that critics who read errors in Wolfe's text as deliberate clues are too quick to find hidden theories, since Wolfe does make mistakes. Grounds this in Wolfe's own admission of errors when list members asked him, and notes his subtle, indirect style invites such readings.
disputes · author-and-biography · genre-and-craft

Poor Gene Wolfe! He can't make any mistakes at all. If he does he is either accused of being sloppy or strange theories start to blossom in the minds of members of this group. (Of course this happens all the time, anyway, and I certainly don't exclude my own mind.)

But we know he does make mistakes. He himself said so, in answer to a question asked him by members of this list.

Then again, Wolfe brings this kind of comment on himself by being incredibly subtle, indirect and downright tricky.

William Ansley

1999-11-09 Matthew Malthouse #

Argues that Severian's narrated memories may come from several different Sevs, including versions cloned or recreated from the alzebo, so contradictions like rooms of different colours or visits to the river and tomb need not be lies. Grounds this in the idea that the Autarch who visits the tomb is so changed he is effectively another person, offering a literary defence of the inconsistencies Roy raised.
disputes · close-textual-reading · author-and-biography · theories: Severian has multiple versions across different selves; Severian's memories conflated from the alzebo

How many Sevs? How many different histories might Sev remember of himself at different times?

And how many memories may not be his own but conflated from the alzebo: regardles of when the events take place Sev is recounting them to us from the position of Autarch, or even re-writing them abord Tzad's ship. if (and I'm not surre that I do) we accept multiply dead Sev to produce the right one might not one Sev have rememberted brown walls and another grey ones? Once never again gone to the river and another done so?

As for the last time but I knew it not, that we certainly the last time that Sev as he then was went to the tomb. I'd say that in litterary terms it was fair comment for even without the died/recreated stuff Sev the Autarch gojng to the tomb was so different as to be another person.

Matthew

1999-11-09 Michael Straight #

Argues that the phrase "for the last time" in Severian's visit to the tomb where he played as a boy does not mean his last visit ever, only that he did not know he would soon leave home. Responds to Roy C. Lackey's claim that Severian lied to the reader by reading the phrase in context, not as a lie.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian's "for the last time" means only leaving home

I don't think "for the last time" here necessarily means "the last time ever in my whole life." In context it just means that he didn't know at the time that he was about to leave his home and would no longer be able to visit the tomb.

-Rostrum

1999-11-10 JMCAzurin #

Argues that Severian's apparent lies or memory lapses in The Book of the New Sun can be read without either, offering alternative readings of the tomb visit and the swimming outing. Responds to a poster's list of quoted passages by suggesting Severian's 'last time' remark and his later swim may mean something different than a lie.
disputes · close-textual-reading

Roy C. Lackey enumerated:

This doesn't have to be either an outright lie or a memory failure. You might simply interpret the quote as, "The day after my elevation, I went to the tomb. Little did I know it would be my last such visit as a member in good standing of the order of seekers of truth and penitence, for I was soon to be exiled for facilitating Thecla's suicide."

He might just mean he never went for a fun afternoon swim with his friends again. He says he had to take the prentices, but the character of that sort of outing would be different. As Sev says he thinks it to be his _duty._

Mikah
