Marc Aramini, James Wynn, and Craig Brewer pressed the case on her name, artificial breasts, fighting ability, and Silk's sanctity; Marc Aramini later disputed the molestation reading. Wynn also floated Hyacinth as Fava and linked her to Pig's insult. The thread ended in side quarrels over hedging and civility rather than agreement.

2011-11-02 Lee Berman #

Asks whether the myth of Hyacinth, a teenage consort of Apollo, bears on Hyacinth's sex and role in The Book of the Long Sun, citing Mantis's remark about implants, Silk's fear of passion, and Hyacinth's fighting. Raises the possibility of Hyacinth being male and links it to Blood's clients, pagan soldiers, and James Wynn's suggestion about Patera Incus.
questions · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Hyacinth is male, not female; Hyacinth's identity follows the Hyacinth-Apollo myth

I was just checking into the Hyacinth myth and I was reminded that this character was a teenage boy, the sexual consort of Apollo. I was trying to think if there is any evidence (for or against) that this might impact the Long Sun story.

Somewhere in his writing, Mantis remarks that Hyacinth's breasts, as described, seem likely to be implants. Silk does seem almost unnaturally afraid of his own passion when he first meets Hyacinth. And she is a shockingly good fighter, we find out toward the end. Is there any evidence that Hyacinth can't be male, like her being pregnant?

It wouldn't be a complete shock to find Blood's clients might have a thing for ladyboys, especially Oosik. In history, pagan soldiers have a reputation for that sort of thing.

James Wynn has suggested Patera Incus (I think it was him, yes?) might be part of a gender bender thing going on. I wonder if James might find the inclusion of Zephyrus (West Wind) as part of the Hyacinth-Apollo story to be of interest.

2011-11-02 James Wynn replying to Lee Berman #

Recounts the poster's earlier theory that Hyacinth in The Book of the Long Sun was a male chem retrofitted to pass as a woman, and now rejects it while still finding her secretive. Notes the parallel with Dracaina in The Soldier of the Mist and that Marc Aramini had proposed a male Hyacinth first.
informs · puzzle-solving · theories: Hyacinth is a male chem retrofitted as a woman; Hyacinth is not male but is secretive

So inquiries like this one make me realize that although my comments on this list don't go back as far as Borkski's or Mantis' or Roy's, it has been a long while now. So long, in fact, that very infamous theories that I helped initiate are not even lore anymore.

Immediately after I finished "The Book of the Long Sun" so many years ago, I posited that Hyacinth might be a male chem who had himself retrofitted to pass as a woman. I did not know at the time that Marc Aramini had posited only a few months earlier that Hyacinth might be male.

I don't believe my "male chem" theory anymore (I only half believed it even then), nor even Marc's "male Hyacinth" theory. I've moved on. But I can still see the appeal of theories along that line. There's something "off" about Hy. She has a secret that Chenille knows to some extent. At the time I offered the "male chem" theory, I noted that Hyacinth followed the same transformative pattern as Dracaina of "The Soldier of the Mist". I asked at that time "what was Hyacinth before"? I have another working theory that I'm currently collating. I don't think Hy is or has ever been male, but I am still convinced she ain't no ordinary girl.

J.

Argues that Hyacinth in The Book of the Long Sun may not be female, citing her name, artificial breasts, unexpected fighting ability, and a statue of Catamitus near her room. Qualifies the case by noting Blood's villa and Musk, and that the Hyacinthus flower link at the end of The Book of the Short Sun does not settle gender.
proposes · puzzle-solving · source-and-allusion · theories: Hyacinth is not female

quite simply it is the name, the fake breasts, here surprising ability to fight, and the statue of Catamitus that is near her room when Silk breaks in. On the other hand, Blood has Musk, and its his villa, so maybe the Hyacinthus/hyacinth connection, while valid in light of the hyacinth flower blooming at the end of Short Sun, is not necessarily applicable in terms of gender, but the statue of Catamitus is a bit thematically suggestive.

I don't want this to be a dismissive question. I suppose I'm just looking for what it means if that's the case. Are we suggesting that it's Wolfe (as a cliche Catholic) undermining Silk's sanctity? Or is it the opposite, opening him to a kind of grace and love beyond traditional sexual relations? Or is it something else altogether?

Proposes that Hyacinth is Fava, a dream-world figure the Rajan retrieves and sends back in time to become Hyacinth, and that Hyacinth's human soul came from her mother. Defends this by answering objections: Chenille's 'sprats' remark, whether Hyacinth eats, Fava's supposed blondeness, and Hyacinth's contempt for her father.
proposes · puzzle-solving · source-and-allusion · theories: Hyacinth is Fava; Rajan retrieves Fava and sends her back in time; Hyacinth's human soul came from her mother; Hyacinth's father is the Rajan who later meets Silk

I believe Hyacinth is Fava (both beans...and beans were believed by the Romans to contain the souls of the dead). I think that after the end of SS, the Rajan reclaims Fava from the World of Dreams where she has been stuck. Then he goes back in time, says hello to Silk, then goes further back with Fava and releases her into the Whorl as Hyacinth. He then takes a job at the Juzgado (Silk's mother's intention for him) and is identified as her "father". Thus, Silk meets him but WE never do.

Between that time and when she meets Silk, she "forgets" her origin (perhaps the same way the Rajan forgot that he was Silk), remembering Green only as a green umbrella and her former form as a dress, both of which she got from her mother (who bequeathed to her a human soul). She suddenly remembers everything at the end of Exodus of the LS when she is faced with the prospect of returning to Green.

This explains why Hy didn't know her mother was dead. The mother she met in Mainframe was the mother of her human soul--her inhumi portion has been left behind on Blue.

Problems (some small some bigger)

1) Chenille says that Hy's father rented her out to the family of Colonel when she was a child. She says "they had a lot of sprats". Many have opined from that that Hy had lots of siblings. Actually, no. It was the Colonel who had a lot of children. Hy was being hired as a nanny.

2) Hy seems to eat. Auk brings her a fruit which she demands from him. Actually, though, we don't see her eat it. She claims to have eaten the fruit whole, pit and all (improbable).
I'm convinced she ditched the fruit. But what was she doing while she was alone when Auk was getting that food?
Also, Marble asserts that Hy doesn't actually eat at all. Remember that the Rajan himself claimed to only pretend to eat until Spring came and he was ravenous (Hello, trees! --because the Rajan is a Neighbor who has possessed the bodies of Horn and Silk consecutively).

3) Dave Tallman has pointed out that it *appears* Fava is blonde in dream world. This is the part I'm still collating. But I don't think so.

4) Hy has some pretty contemptuous things to say about her "father". This is a real toughy -- -- if one takes it at face value and assumes that this is Hy's constant and forever opinion of her father, then it is difficult to overcome. I suspect she is in intermittent communication with each other and this statement represents a recent falling out with him.

So there it is, for what it is worth.

2011-11-04 David Stockhoff replying to Sergei SOLOVIEV #

Argues that Wolfe presents homosexual and transsexual characters as imperfect people striving to do better, and proposes a theory that Hyacinth could be a female clone of Typhon, making her a masculine personality in a female body. Responds to Sergei Soloviev's claim that the transsexual element in Spider's story serves only atmosphere, citing the spy Titi as an explicit case and suggesting Hyacinth as a transsexual figure without genitals-based maleness.
proposes · author-and-biography · puzzle-solving · theories: Hyacinth is a female clone of Typhon; Hyacinth is a transsexual without the concept of transsexuality; Wolfe depicts homosexuals as imperfect humans striving to be better

The spy Titi, yes. It is made quite clear.

I don;t think it's atmosphere, though---I think Wolfe wants to include homosexuals etc. as imperfect human beings trying to be better with poor models to work from. They are imperfect in a certain way that fits with his concerns (obsessions?) with the body and procreation.

I think the "male Hy" theory needs some way to make Hy male in a way that does not involve genitals. For example---just an example---if we knew she were a female Typhon, i.e., a clone of him with a bit of a twist to make her female.

She'd then be a potentially aggressive masculine personality (if you accept Typhon's engineered general genetic superiority) in a very female body. If we assume that sex change surgery is unheard of in the Whorl, then that makes her almost, not quite, a transsexual who has no concept of transsexuality. As a prostitute, she is unspecific about her partners and may even register as bisexual. So she might be another type of the imperfect human striving to work it all out.

Again, just an example, with none of the implications worked out.

2011-11-04 Sergei SOLOVIEV replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that before debating whether Hyacinth was masculine or transsexual, readers should consider the likely medical cause of her death and her infertility, which he links to her past as a sex worker at Orchid. He backs this by noting the Orchid girls' abortions, Silk and Hyacinth's attempts to have children, and that the Apollo myth and the Whorl's plant names do not make Hyacinth male.
disputes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion · theories: Hyacinth died of a gynaecological cancer linked to sex work; Hyacinth's masculinity theory is weak; Plant names in the Whorl ignore grammatical gender

Frankly, I think that before discussing the theories about "masculinity" of Hyacinth it would be useful to think about the causes of her death.

To me it looks like some nasty gynaecological disease, like cervical cancer (she is about 40). It is very probably related to her early situation as a "sex worker". Transsexuals usually die of other causes. If it is breast cancer, again, nothing to do with implants. As far as I remember, it was discussed somewhere (with Chenille?) that the girls at Orchid had to get rid often of the foetuses. It can explain easily the infertility of Hyacinth. It is told also that she and Silk tried to have children.
The desperation of Silk is better explained in this context, if you add to the brew some "masculinity" or "transsexuality" it will only confuse the matter.

More arguments against this theory.

There was some real "transsexual" (or something very close) in the story told by
Spider to Mint and Remora. When it really plays the role in the story it is told explicitly - even if it is needed only to create an "atmosphere".

I think the argument that Hyacinth in the story about Apollo and Hyacinth was male is weak - the Hyacinth (flower) grew from his blood, it doesn't mean
the flower is male. The girls names in the Whorl are given after plants regardless to the gender of the word in Roman languages. (By the way, Lily in French is "Lys" and
it is "male".) Amor (love) also was male, and it didn't bother even the medieval Catholics.

Sergei
