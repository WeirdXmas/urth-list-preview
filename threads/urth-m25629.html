Tracking Song

Urth list · 3 messages · 2014-03-18 to 2014-03-18

2014urthDavid StockhoffAntonin Scriabin
The thread: Participants debated whether the humans of Tracking Song are descendants of colonists on a distant world or on Earth's moon, and how the planet's strange evolution and the Great Sleigh fit together. David Stockhoff argued the humans are both visitors and natives, evolved over tens of thousands of years after seeding by terraforming or star-seeding, and asked whether the world could be our moon. Antonin Scriabin proposed accelerated evolution, with stranded colonists becoming the machine-linked underground folk and surface colonists evolving into tribes, and read the winged man as a rescue crewman. Scriabin then disputed Stockhoff's time-scale and Earth-origin assumptions, suggesting a more distant planet or moon. The thread ended without resolution, with the disagreement left open.
  • David Stockhoff: humans are both visitors and natives, evolved after seeding; the world may be our moon
  • Antonin Scriabin: accelerated evolution of stranded colonists; winged man is a rescue crewman; the world is a distant planet or moon, not Earth's moon

Themes: Deep time and ruins, Evolution and the future of humanity, Colonization and the native

2014-03-18 David Stockhoff replying to Antonin Scriabin #

Argues that the humans of Tracking Song are both visitors and natives, having evolved over tens of thousands of years after colonists were stranded or seeded there. Builds on Antonin Scriabin's colonization account by citing terraforming and Blishian star-seeding as explanations, and asks whether the world could be our own moon.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · source-and-allusion · theories: Humans are both visitors and natives of the moon; Terraforming or Blishian star-seeding explains the human presence; The moon is Earth's moon, heavily terraformed; The moon is undergoing accelerated evolution

That pretty much lines up with what I have always thought. Humans are visitors to the planet/moon (who nevertheless seem somewhat familiar with it), and yet they also are natives, having evolved there. This presents an initially baffling conundrum, but it's actually simple if you assume the passage of huge amounts of time---tens of thousands of years. Terraforming and interstellar travel both offer well-accepted explanations for these extremes.

If not terraforming, then at least some kind of Blishian star-seeding. This explanation would both allow and require the extraplanetary colonizers to leave the planet alone for a far longer time than terraforming would, and the surface could have been settled much sooner if it were always habitable. The underground dwellers are those least adapted to living on the surface and have adapted slightly by becoming dwarfish and using technology, while the surface dwellers have evolved dramatically as depicted.

The dwarf might be taken as a sign that this evolution was not desired, however. And would "improving" circumstances slow evolution (because selection pressures become weaker) or speed it (because humans could interact more with the environment)?

On the other hand, why bother with a moon/tiny planet at all? Could it be our moon? Does the story track with travel around a moon the size of ours? If so, it's been heavily terraformed already.

2014-03-18 Antonin Scriabin #

Proposes that the planet in Tracking Song is undergoing accelerated evolution, with colonizers stranded underground becoming the half-machine denizens and surface colonizers rapidly evolving into humanoid tribes. Offers the Great Sleigh as a land-capable spaceship and reads the winged man at the end as a rescue crew member in a flying suit, citing the low gravity and the Sleigh crew's instructions to the tribes.
proposes · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Tracking Song colonists evolved into humanoid tribes; winged man is a rescue crew member; Great Sleigh is a spaceship that operates on land

I've found little snippets online talking about this story, but I wanted to see what the list thought of this idea: the planet / moon is undergoing some kind of accelerated evolution. Here is my idea:

At some point in the past, human colonizers landed on the planet, built the underground city / research center, and explored the surface.

Something went wrong, that left the colonizers stranded on the planet. Some remained underground, eventually becoming the half-machine, half-man denizens. They seem to have forgotten how to manage the giant mechs.

Others tried their luck on the surface, and they evolved rapidly for some reason, which explains why the various species the narrator runs into are almost all humanoids of varying sizes / social structures, speak the same language (or one very close), etc. They have filled most of the ecological niches, which explains the rampant pseudo-cannibalism.

The narrator is a member of a rescue team, and through some accident is left behind by his crew on the Great Sleigh, which could be some kind of spaceship that can also operate on land, or a vehicle left over from the colonizers. We know that individuals on the Sleigh have interacted with some of the humanoid tribes, giving them instructions (not to harm certain birds, etc) that imply some sort of terraforming sequence is in the works, some way to make the planet more livable. Perhaps they settled on this plan after realizing that the first colonizers had rapidly changed form, and were no longer "recoverable" humans as such.

I think the man with wings that the narrator sees at the end of the story is simply a member of the rescue team (his crew) wearing some kind of biohazard suit / flying device (we know that gravity is very low on this planet, which would make jetpack travel much easier).

2014-03-18 Antonin Scriabin replying to DAVID STOCKHOFF #

Argues that the time gap between the first colonists and the later rescue team suggests a distant planet or moon rather than Earth's moon, and that the Great Sleigh may travel a smaller northerly track. Responds to David Stockhoff's claim that huge elapsed time and terraforming or star-seeding explain humans being both visitors and natives, questioning the assumption that the humans come from Earth.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Tracking Song is set on a distant planet or moon

The only reason I think it might be a somewhat distant planet / moon is that a lot of time seems to have elapsed between the initial colonizers and what I am calling the rescue team. If it were *our* moon, one could presume they would come before the evolutionary changes had taken place. It is also possible that the Great Sleigh doesn't travel around the circumference of the body, but makes some smaller (more northerly?) track. Hard to tell based on the descriptions of the narrators movements if it is a moon-like level of gravity or some larger body that is still smaller than Earth. Assuming the humans in question come from Earth. Gah.
