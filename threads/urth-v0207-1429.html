Lists the different estimates posters have proposed for how far in the future the Book of the New Sun takes place, from billions of years down to thousands, with each estimate tied to a particular argument. Sets out the Stella, Gabriel, Wolfe, MAD, Jeff Wilson, Agia and Jordan/Duggan timelines, citing Talos's play, a Plan[e]t Engineering passage about Severian's time, and Agia's remark in I, XIX about the Conciliator's return.
informs · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: Wolfe timeline is one million years ahead; Stella timeline is five billion years; Jordan/Duggan place the setting in a previous universe

Stella timeline: 5 billion years in the future (Sol's lifespan).

Gabriel timeline (from Talos's play): 50 million years
(geological/evolutionary scale).

Wolfe timeline: 1 million years in Earth's future (plays hell with constellations). _Plan[e]t Engineering_ clip: "Imagine then what the situation will be in Severian's time for the scholar-heirs of a sequence of civilizations that may be over a million years old" (6). (Difficulty in dating our own civilization's geneology: 6000 years? Beyond that, it becomes arbitrary -- in some pre-literary sense, we are already heirs of a sequence over a million years old. Starting with Homo erectus.)

MAD timeline: around 100,000 years in Earth's future (factoring in precession of equinoxes while largely ignoring constellations themselves).

Jeff Wilson timeline: much less than 100,000 years in future (factoring stellar movement/constellations).

Agia timeline: 30,000 years in our future (I, XIX; technically she is talking about the span of time between the first appearance of the Conciliator and the next).

Jordan/Duggan timeline: deep in the past, a previous iteration of universe (so toss out Armstrong, Apu Punchau, South America, stars, planets, etc.; it is all analogy, not direct).
