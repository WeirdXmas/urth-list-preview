2011-05-13 Sergei SOLOVIEV #

Argues that a sundial could not show time on a different world, since the obstacle is that each world has its own sun rather than the 24-hour cycle. Supports Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's objection to Gwern Branwen's suggestion, adding that a sundial works only in daylight and would need planets sharing one sun to rotate in perfect synchronization.
supports · close-textual-reading

I agree with Dan - and even more so. The impossibility of this follows not from the 24 hours cycle, but from the fact that different worlds have different Suns. Sundials to some extent can be used to show time in different time belts of the same planet. To some extent - because they are limited to day time, and not to 24 hours cycle, they cannot display shadow (and time) when the Sun is behind the planet itself.

For different planets of the same Sun you need a perfect synchronization in planet rotation.

Sergei
