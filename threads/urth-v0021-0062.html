1998-11-18 Kevin J. Maroney #

Adds that the chess elephant comes from Xiang Qi, the Chinese game where the piece is the ancestor of the bishop, and that Xiang Qi means 'The Elephant Game', extending Spectacled Bear's remark that Wolfe's Mamillian is a rook. Offers the chess history as a playful follow-up to the Elephant and Castle pun and adds a note on italicizing game names.
chats · source-and-allusion

The Elephant is a piece in the Chinese member of the _Chess_ family, _Xiang Qi_. It is the direct ancestor of the Bishop; it moves two spaces diagonally. This piece was probably present in the ur-game from which all modern _Chess_ games derive. In fact, if I understand correctly, _Xiang Qi_ means "The Elephant Game".

(Note on orthography: I italicize the names of all games, including sports and traditional or folk games. This is an idiosyncracy.)

1998-11-18 Peter Westlake #

Playfully argues that the Mamillian chess-rook clue in the Saltus mine passage points to the London Underground station Elephant and Castle, suggesting it hints at subterranean engines. Responds to the Lexicon Urthus errata and to mantis's reading that the footsteps mark the Deep One as biological, with a joke about Spectacled Bear nearly believing Wolfe intended it.
proposes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Mamillian's chess rook hints at Elephant and Castle

I've been reading my Lexicon Urthus Additions and Errata III, which turned up in the post a couple of days ago - thank you very much, mantis! - and noticed something about the footsteps in the mine at Saltus. Severian is reminded of them by the footsteps of the mastodon, Mamillian, and this biological association makes mantis think the Deep One must be biological too. He goes on to say that mention chess imagery: "that Mamillian is a cross between an elephant and a tower means that it is a Rook, which in some chess sets is represented as an elephant with a small wooden tower on its back." So near, O mantis, and yet so far! A Rook is also called a Castle. So, not elephant and tower, but ... ... Elephant and Castle. Is the London Underground sufficiently suggestive of subterranean engines for you?

Spectacled Bear, who has accidentally almost convinced himself that Wolfe intended this :-)
