Bibliomen

Urth list · 9 messages · 2014-03-17 to 2014-03-27

2014urthDarth EdMarc AraminiFernando Q. GouveaDan'l Danehy-OakesAntonin Scriabin
The thread: Collectors on the Urth list traded prices and availability for Wolfe's Bibliomen bibliography and related chapbooks, with no close reading of the text. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes reported bookseller prices, Marc Aramini said he owns nearly everything and found the bibliography fun but modest, and Fernando Gouvea explained that Bibliomen is a set of character portraits available in an expensive fine press edition and a cheaper reprint. Antonin Scriabin bought the reprint and kept asking about the remaining chapbooks, which he found pricey, while Gouvea and Scriabin shared the urge to collect everything. Darth Ed lamented the lack of an ebook, and Gouvea proposed an imaginary deluxe-format collection; the thread faded into shared collecting talk rather than reaching any conclusion.
  • Fernando Gouvea: Bibliomen is character portraits; the cheaper reprint is the practical buy, and Cheap Street editions are costly
  • Marc Aramini: owns nearly all of Wolfe's work; the bibliography is fun but modest; recommends the later cheaper edition
  • Antonin Scriabin: is compelled to collect the rest of Wolfe's books despite high chapbook prices
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: supplies bookseller price data and notes which titles are scarce
  • Darth Ed: wants an ebook of an out-of-print title and says nothing about the text

Themes: Books, libraries and writing

2014-03-17 Fernando Q. Gouvea replying to Antonin Scriabin #

Informs a newcomer that Bibliomen is a collection of character portraits rather than stories, existing in an expensive fine press edition and a later cheaper paperback reprint. Responds to Antonin Scriabin's question about whether it is hard to find and worth reading, offering availability details and a personal recommendation.
answers

It's not really a collection of stories, but rather of characters. Of course, in many cases describing a character makes for a mini-story.

It exists in two versions. The original edition was an impossibly expensive fine press book from Cheap Street press. The copy I have is a paperback reprint thar came out many years later. Copies of that one shouldn't be too expensive.

Worth having? I think so, but certainly not the main event.

Fernando

PS: at the very first Readercon I had a chance to buy the Cheap Street version for less than $200 at an auction and chickened out. Advice to young self: you'll regret not spending that money!

2014-03-27 Fernando Q. Gouvea replying to Darth Ed #

Proposes a hypothetical collection titled The Wolfe of Cheap Street gathering Wolfe's pieces originally issued in special deluxe formats, noting copyright limits stopped it. I once suggested someone should put together a book called /The Wolfe of Cheap Street/ and collect all of the pieces that they put out in their special deluxe formats. But apparently there were too many copyright restrictions. Most of them have now appeared in one or another of the collections, of course. But I'd still by the book if someone made it.

Fernando
