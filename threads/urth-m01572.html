Themes: Ghosts, dreams and visions

2005-10-16 Kip Tanner #

Expresses disappointment that the new story The Gunner's Mate is good but not Wolfe-good, lacking evolving characters and hidden depths found in Wolfe's stronger stories. Says the ending was guessed early, though the story works as a fine ghost story.
disputes

I was a little disappointed with the story. I thought it was good, but not Wolfe-good, with evolving characters and discovered worlds, like 'Golden City Far' or 'The Tree is my Hat' or a few dozen others. If there was more to the story, hidden references or whatever, I missed it. I guessed the ending early and kept reading hoping I was wrong. I wasn't.

It is a fine ghost story, but a merely good story from the master is somewhat dissapointing.

2005-10-17 Matt Sab replying to Kip T #

Reports a link to Ben Marcus's Harper's essay attacking Jonathan Franzen and quotes a Valve commenter who says he loves and hates Gene Wolfe because Wolfe's puzzling writing feels like a puzzle to reassemble. Responds to Kip T's thread, mainly noting an unexpected Wolfe mention in a non-Wolfe discussion.
informs

In this month's Harper's magazine, Ben Marcus rips
into Jonathan Franzen in "Why Experimental Fiction
Threatens to Destroy Publishing, Jonathan Franzen, and
Life as We Know It."

The Valve's discussion of the essay has an unexpected
Gene Wolfe appearance:

I read very little poetry by design and constitution.
I lack the patience required to work through poetry if
it lacks the puzzling qualities I value in writers
like Joyce. (I mean "puzzling" literally there, as in
"like a puzzle" which I?m enjoined to put back
together. Hence my love/hate relationship with Gene
Wolfe.)
