5HC : Thunder Always knocks twice

Urth list · 5 messages · 2005-02-07 to 2005-02-08

2005urthJim RaylorDan'l Danehy-OakesMaru Dubshinki
The thread: Maru recommended the story Solar Labyrinth from Storeys from the Old Hotel as Wolfe's best short story and claimed it lacks the theology they find annoying elsewhere in Wolfe. Jim Raylor informed the list that the phrase Thunder Always appears in Wolfe's first published story Trip Trap, and then disputed the claim of no theology, citing classical and Toltec allusions, Arthurian references, the tilted crucifix, and the line about God's faded ink. Maru conceded that heavy mood and allusion may serve atmosphere in the manner of Borges rather than theology, but did not fully yield. The thread ended without full agreement, with the debate over whether the allusions amount to theology left open.
  • maru: Solar Labyrinth is Wolfe's best short story and lacks the theology found elsewhere in his work
  • Jim Raylor: the Solar Labyrinth maze is full of religious content, shown by its allusions, crucifix, and God's ink
  • maru: allusions may serve atmosphere and erudition as in Borges rather than theology

Themes: Mirrors, pictures and art, Catholic faith and sacraments, Names and their meanings
