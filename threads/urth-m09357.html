AEG - False Loose Ends

2009urthThe Book of the New SunDavid Stockhoff

Themes: Genre: science fiction, fantasy, both

2009-03-27 David Stockhoff #

Asks whether Wolfe in An Evil Guest is deliberately imitating a dead or dying storytelling mode, with its well-known flaws, and whether he has always worked this way. Responds to a quoted observation that the novel's apparent sloppiness, such as the heroine's shifting address and characters forgotten or killed, is a pulp-novel surface masking deeper craft.
questions · genre-and-craft · theories: An Evil Guest imitates pulp storytelling flaws deliberately

Now that's an observation that makes me really want to read the book. From the discussion here, I first thought it might be silly intertextual fun, then got a growing impression of deeper currents. But this is great. Is Wolfe imitating a dead or dying storytelling mode right down to its well-known flaws?

And has he always done this?

It has been growing on me, how clever this novel is. On the surface, it is not only a pulp novel, it is a *bad* pulp novel, with a poor editing job: why the author and editor can't even keep the address of the heroine straight. People are mentioned, killed, forgotten. It is easy to see someone who doesn't know Wolfe saying 'Eh! The author can't even be bothered to keep a bit of consistency? Onto the trash heap with it!'.
