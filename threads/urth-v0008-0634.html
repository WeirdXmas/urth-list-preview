Hethor's Talk

Urth list · 3 messages · 1998-03-17 to 1998-03-17

1998urthThe Book of the New SunC R CulverMichael Andre-DriussiMark Millman
The thread: Readers debated whether Hethor's speech in Wolfe's Urth of the New Sun is Chinese or Korean, and whether it links to our time. Culver argued for Korean, reasoning that Severian never mentions tones, which Chinese has and Korean lacks. Millman countered that tones are easy for non-speakers to miss as ordinary rhythm, and that Mandarin's heavier nasality and suitability for a spacefaring culture favor Chinese. Driussi clarified that the most information-rich speech appears in Severian's dream, not a waking scene, and restated the Chinese view. The thread ends with the language question unresolved and the positions still divided.
  • C R Culver: Hethor's language is Korean, since Severian never mentions tones
  • Mark Millman: Chinese remains plausible, since tones may pass unnoticed by outsiders
  • m.driussi: Hethor's speech is Chinese and appears in Severian's dream in volume one

Themes: Names and their meanings, Ghosts, dreams and visions

1998-03-17 Mark Millman #

Argues that Severian's language could still be Chinese even though he never mentions tones, since tones are easy for non-speakers to miss as ordinary speech rhythm. Cites Mandarin's heavier nasality, which lets outsiders tell it from Cantonese, and notes that Mandarin suits a spacefaring culture better, in reply to mantis's case for Korean.
supports · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian's language is Chinese

to which Christopher R. Culver replied:

But Severian, since he doesn't speak Chinese, might well not notice the tones--they're not that obvious to non-speakers even in Cantonese, which is the more heavily tonal of the two major dialects. The tones could be interpreted, especially by someone who doesn't speak a tonal language, as simply the natural rhythms and inflections of speech in that tongue. It's interesting to note that Mandarin, which would, given the lack of state resources available to most Cantonese-speakers, seem to be more likely as the language of a spacefaring culture, is more heavily nasal than Cantonese--so much so that even those who don't speak Chinese can sometimes tell them apart by the sound.

Mark Millman
