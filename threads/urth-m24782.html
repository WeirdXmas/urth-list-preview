The thread: Darrell Burgan opened by asking how readers understand Seawrack's origin and role in the Short Sun books, admitting he was stumped. Lee argued she was budded off from the Mother, a sea monster, and later that she was assigned by the Mother Goddess to love Silkhorn, linking her to Juturna, Typhon's brood and sirens. David Stockhoff mostly raised speculative questions, proposing her as a projection of Hyacinth/Kypris and suggesting Silk finds her through Horn's memories, which need not be supernatural. Stockhoff also agreed with the Juturna comparison while calling it imperfect. The thread drifted into interpretive speculation without a settled answer.

2013-11-13 Darrell Burgan #

Asks how readers understand Seawrack's role in the Short Sun novels, noting she is important in On Blue's Waters yet her origin and place in the larger arcs are unclear. Says that grasping her seems key to the Short Sun epic and invites others' views, admitting the poster is stumped.
questions

The more I think about it, the more confused I am about the role of Seawrack in the Short Sun novels. She is clearly an important character in OBW, but there is little information about her origin, or even how she is involved in the many great arcs of the story.

I can't escape the feeling that understanding her is somehow key to understanding the Short Sun epic. I'd love to hear people's thoughts about how they see her role. Myself, I'm just stumped.

Darrell

2013-11-22 Lee Berman replying to Darrell Burgan #

Argues that Seawrack was budded off from the Mother, a sea monster, rather than being a human girl taken in. Responds to Darrell Burgan by linking Horn's pirate-ship encounter and Echidna's sirens to the Mother, then reads Seawrack as autobiographical and cites a Wolfe interview.
proposes · puzzle-solving · source-and-allusion · theories: Seawrack is budded off from the Mother; Seawrack's human origin story is a deception; The Mother is the pirate ship Horn fires on; Seawrack reflects Wolfe's feelings for his own family

Regarding her origin, I think there are two conflicting stories presented. One is that she is a human girl who happened to fall into the water and was taken under the wing (so to speak) of the monstrous Mother who nurtured her and mutated her into a sea being.

I think that story is a lie and a deliberate deception to make her more acceptable and appealing to Horn. I think her true origin can be found between the lines.

Seawrack is a female figure who was budded off from the larger mass of the giant undersea monster/god called The Mother. This is in parallel to the female buds we see on the back of Great Scylla in RttW. And probably also in parallel to the production of undines by Abaia on Urth.

Abaia is conflated with a boat armed with cannon in BotNS and I think the same thing happens when Horn encounters the all-black pirate ship with its female crew. Seawrack loses her arm when Horn shoots and hits the most prominent female on that boat. I think that "pirate ship" was The Mother.

In mythology, Echidna is the "mother of all monsters" (Typhon is the "father" but that's a different thread). By some accounts, Echidna is the mother of the sirens (beautiful but monsters nonetheless). Two of the Whorl goddesses have the names of sirens, Molpe and Thelxiepeia. On Blue, the Mother's genesis of the siren Seawrack (not her real name) establishes her connection to Echidna for me.

Seawrack is a big part of OBW. In the next two books, she is there, but mostly just as a presence, a memory, a longing. A song that continues to sing in Horn's mind.

I find the explanation of her importance to be autobiographical in nature. Just as I get the sense that Horn's relationship with Nettle and his children (natural and inhumi) is meant to reflect something of Wolfe's feelings for his own family, I think Seawrack serves a similar purpose. What more can be said? Isn't there someone in your life who was a "Seawrack"? Someone who is still provoking a sense of wistful longing?

2013-11-25 David Stockhoff replying to Lee #

Proposes that Seawrack is a projection of Hyacinth/Kypris rather than a separate personality, and asks whether Silk's bond with Hy parallels theories of Jesus married to Mary Magdalene. Also raises the recurring Mary names and St. Catherine's maids, noting the etymology of Mary, mostly through speculative questions.
proposes · theology-and-religion · source-and-allusion · theories: Seawrack is a projection of Hyacinth/Kypris; Silk's relationship with Hy parallels Jesus and Mary Magdalene

Any chance she is a projection of Hyacinth/Kypris? She couldn't possibly have her own personality. Something suppresses the memories of eating dead sailors and allows her to communicate with Horn.

On another note, Silk's relationship with Hy parallels the nontraditional theories of Jesus' being married to Mary Magdalene? Does that lead us anywhere, theologically or otherwise?

And speaking of Mary, it has been noted by mythicists that if you added up all the Marys in the life of Jesus you'd have about a dozen---all the women are named Mary. This reminds me of St. Catherine and her maids, of course. Same question as above.

Speaking of Mary part 2: "The meaning [of the name Mary] is not known for certain, but there are several theories including "sea of bitterness", "rebelliousness", and "wished for child". However it was most likely originally an Egyptian name, perhaps derived in part from /mry/ "beloved" or /mr/ "love"."

(Will Dan Brown's next novel assert that Mary was cloned? And why not---she was perfect enough to be chosen as Jesus' mother!)

2013-11-25 Lee Berman replying to Darrell Burgan #

Proposes that Seawrack is most closely analogous to Juturna the undine and that Blue is an origin world for Typhon and his brood, whose kin became sea megatherians like Scylla and Abaia. Supports the idea with the Cumaean's remark about Fomalhaut, Kypris as sea-foam Aphrodite, and a speculative reading of Silk as Moses in reply to Darrell Burgan's quoted exchange.
proposes · myth-and-structure · source-and-allusion · theories: Seawrack is an undine analogous to Juturna; Blue is origin world for Typhon's brood; Fomalhaut planet is Blue, the Cumaean's home; Hyacinth may be a transgender prostitute

I think other Sun Series beings budded off from a larger whole are meant to have their own personalities, including the various incarnations of Tzadkiel, and Idas.

But surely the closest analog to Seawrack the siren is Juturna, the undine. There is a clear mythological connection between sirens and mermaids. Also, there is that moment of overwhelming mutual passion depicted between Severian and Jurturna, though Seawrack's human size makes her a much better candidate for sexual fulfillment.

Anyway, I see The Mother as being related to the gods of the Whorl but it is difficult to see the direct connection. My best guess is that Blue is an origin world for Typhon and his brood and they took human form for their trip to Urth. Once there, they could keep their human form, as we see with Typhon and Cilinia. And/or part of them could hit the ocean and start growing into such megatherian beings as Great Scylla and Abaia, like the Mother creature we see on Blue.

Thus, when The Cumaean (who is almost exactly what we'd expect of a human-form of Echidna), speaks to Severian of a planet around the star Fomalhaut (fish's mouth), I think it might be her home planet, Blue.

By choosing the epithet "Kyrpis" for Aphrodite, Wolfe may be invoking the sea-foam version of that Greek goddess, in contrast to the traditional hellenic Aphrodite, who is the daughter of Zeus. So maybe that connects Kypris with Seawrack. But otherwise it is difficult to see how Kyrpis in Mainframe on the Whorl is related to Seawrack on Blue.

In regard to Hyacynth, I dunno. I think it was Andre-Driussi who commented that WOlfe's description of Hyacinth's breasts make them sound like silicone implants. Surely Wolfe knew that mythological Hyacinth was male, making me wonder if perhaps Vironese Hyacinth was a transgender kind of prostitute. This might explain both Silk's joy and despair in his love and passion for her.

Heh. And Borski says Father Inire is Moses.

I guess Silk fits the Moses bill in a number of ways. Silk was an insider of the establishment when he heard the call from God. He was leading his people to the Promised Land, but was not permitted to enter himself.
