1999-05-03 Michael Straight #

Recommends Vernor Vinge's new novel A Deepness in the Sky, saying it addresses whether an interstellar empire can hold together when ships travel slower than light with sleeper crews. Responds to Michael Andre-Driussi's remark about generational and sleeper ships and colonies drifting from central control, offering no argument about Wolfe's text.
chats

Vernor Vinge's new book "A Deepness in the Sky" is partly about the issue of whether you can have a cohesive interstellar empire with stl sleeper ramships.

And it's a great book.

1999-05-03 Alastair Reynolds #

Recommends Linda Nagata's novel Vast and Robert Reed's stories about a huge slow ship as examples of fiction without FTL communication. Replies to an earlier question asking for novels using only slower-than-light travel, noting that Vast's immortal crew fits neither sleeper nor generation-ship categories.
answers

mantis:

Linda Nagata's recent novel Vast, (which I thought was terrific) has ships limited to 0.4 c (so only marginal relativistic effects) and no ftl communications. As the immortal crew can be copied in and out of computer storage at whim (and can grow new bodies for themselves equally easily) it doesn't fall into either "sleeper" or "generation starship" category.

Robert Reed has been doing some interesting stories recently, set aboard a gigantic, slow-moving ship larger than Jupiter (eg "Marrow").

Al
