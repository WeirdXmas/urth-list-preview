Themes: Hidden identities and parentage

1997-07-11 Patrick O'Leary #

Argues that the mystery of Mint's five sacrifices is obvious, since her cloistered vows explain them, and then praises how densely Wolfe packs the opening of The Book of the Long Sun with delayed detonations. Points to the first lines naming Maytera Marble who was also Maytera Rose as an example of the book's dualities of identity and naming, which pay off at the end.
supports · close-textual-reading

Mantis--

Thanks much, Gene, for the enlightening and charming answers to our pestering questions. And thanks Mantis for being the engineer.

Somehow, it is extremely amusing to me that the great mystery of Mint's 5 sacrifices is obvious. She's a cloistered nun, right? She's taken certain vows. We all knew that. Why do we bend ourselves into such knots?

<<20. "Mint states that she had to give up many things, `five of them very great,' to become a sibyl. Can you tell us what those five things were?" I can try, knowing I will be accused of banality. In no particular order: freedom, marriage, the right to own property, the companionship and support of her family, and motherhood.>>

On a more general note, I've begun rereading the Long Sun books for the forth time and stopped after oh 3 pages. Look at them again. I can hardly believe how much Wolfe has packed into these several dozen paragraphs. Knowing the rest of the story I am treated to a catalog of subtle delayed detonations, in what at first read is deceptively clear or merely puzzeling and estranging narrative. Just one example... (p.1, second line) "Maytera Marble, who was also Maytera Rose." And thus we are introduced to his major theme of dualities, dual identities, dual names, odd naming conventions, Robots who are stone, Women who are plants--dualities that are sustained throughout until the two aligned planets on the last page. The head reels, the jaw drops.

We were there when this masterpiece was published. How incredibly lucky for us.

best

Patrick
