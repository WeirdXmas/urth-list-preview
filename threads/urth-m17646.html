Recent human crash-landing on Sainte-Anne

Urth list · 5 messages · 2011-01-07 to 2011-01-08

2011urthGerry QuinnDan'l Danehy-OakesLee BermanAndrew Mason
The thread: Lee Berman proposed that the name John in the Sainte-Anne story is the narrator's own, treating the tale as autobiography of a divided being reunited, since Shadow Children might be immortal. Andrew Mason argued that names like Atlantis, Gondwanaland and John point to a recent human crash-landing, with the Shadow Children as incomers who might adopt the names of the first humans they met. Gerry Quinn disputed this, saying John and Mary could be honorific or saint-like names and that people commonly have several given names. Quinn also argued that Shadow Children die much as humans do, citing the last Shadow Child's wish to live and noting that V.R.T., Sandwalker and Eastwind are not Shadow Children. The thread ends without resolution, with Quinn's rebuttals standing against the crash-landing and immortality readings.
  • Lee Berman: John is the narrator's own name; the story is true autobiography of one divided being
  • Andrew Mason: names like John and Atlantis point to a recent human crash-landing; Shadow Children are the incomers
  • Gerry Quinn: John and Mary may be honorific names; Sainte-Anne people are natives, and people have many given names
  • Gerry Quinn: Shadow Children are mortal, not essentially immortal; V.R.T., Sandwalker and Eastwind are not Shadow Children

Themes: Unreliable narration, Names and their meanings, Doubles, twins and clones

Why is the name "John" used? Indeed a good question, in my opinion. I take the most fantastic view and that is that Shadow Children, or their source material, are essentially immortal. How can you kill a shadow? When Shadow Children die, what do they mean? Not the same thing as we mean for death, I suspect. Perhaps "taking another form" is meant.

2011-01-07 Andrew Mason #

Argues that the names Atlantis, Gondwanaland and especially John point to a recent human landing on Sainte-Anne, since modern people rather than prehistoric ones would invent or use such names. Supports the view in the thread that the Shadow Children are the incomers and the Free People the natives, reasoning that shapeshifters might adopt the names of the first humans they met.
supports · source-and-allusion · puzzle-solving · theories: Sainte-Anne was recently crash-landed by humans; Shadow Children are the incomers and Free People the natives

Two things that might support the 'recent landing' theory at least if 'A Story' is taken as basically factual. One is the names Atlantis, Gondwanaland,etc., which certainly were not used in Atlantis or Gondwanaland; these are names that modern people might come up with if they want to think of themselves as ancient. The other, which I find really striking, is the name 'John'. I can't see why prehistoric people, or those influenced by them, would call themselves John. (I think this tends,as well, to support the view that the Shadow Children are the incomers and the Free :People the natives.It's hard to see why a race of humans, who presumably had different names when they arrived, would decide they would all be called John or Mary; a race of shapeshifters imitating humans might do so, if those were the names of the first humans they met. But I'm less sure of this.)

2011-01-08 Gerry Quinn replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that Shadow Children die much as humans do rather than being essentially immortal, since death means dinner for the Marshmen. Answers the immortality view by quoting the last Shadow Child's wish to live and noting that V.R.T., Sandwalker and Eastwind are not Shadow Children.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Shadow Children are mortal like humans; Shadow Children are essentially immortal

From: "Lee Berman" <[email]>

If you always lean towards the most fantastic view, and also get bent out of shape whenever people disagree with you, you are apt to have a hard time.

I would have thought that there is good evidence that for the Shadow Children, death is much the same as it is for us. Death for them means dinner for the Marshmen, and the last Shadow Child is not inclined towards this fate.

"I hear your song," the Shadow Child said, "and I wish to live too. I am not, perhaps of your blood, but I wish to live."
[--]
"Nothing is worse than that I should die," he says, and breaks the psychic barrier around the twin planets.

But VRT isn't a Shadow Child anyway, and neither were Sandwalker and Eastwind.

- Gerry Quinn
