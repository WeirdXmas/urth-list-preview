shapeshifter hearsay

Urth list · 3 messages · 2006-07-31 to 2006-08-01

2006urthThe Book of the New SunTony EllisDan'l Danehy-Oakesb sharp
The thread: The thread asked whether deliberately placed hearsay in Wolfe's fiction, notably reports of shapeshifters and mimics in The Fifth Head of Cerberus and The Book of the New Sun, signals real significance. Tony Ellis argued that such reports cannot be dismissed and their volume matters, offering textual reasons to think the Annese are mimics. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes agreed with the principle but thought Ellis overstated the case, suggesting identity is Wolfe's major theme. b sharp questioned whether the principle extends to Urth's shapeshifter evidence, and Ellis clarified his claim without fully settling the disagreement.
  • Tony Ellis: hearsay and legends about shapeshifters and mimics are deliberately placed and significant; the Annese are mimics for textual reasons
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: shape-changing is overstated as definite in Cerberus, though central, and identity is Wolfe's major theme
  • b sharp: asks whether the Chekhov's gun principle applies to New Sun shapeshifter evidence and whether shapeshifters exist on Urth

Themes: Doubles, twins and clones, Masks, disguise and shapeshifting, Hidden identities and parentage

2006-07-31 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes replying to b sharp #

Argues that Tony Ellis overstates the case that shape-changing and mimicry are definitely happening in The Fifth Head of Cerberus, though something important is going on that makes them central there. Responds to Tony Ellis's claim about deliberately placed hearsay and legends by agreeing with the principle while questioning its strength, and proposes that identity is Wolfe's major theme across his major works.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Identity is the supertheme of Wolfe's major works; Shapechangers exist in The Fifth Head of Cerberus

and b responds:

Yes, the same principle applies. However, I think Tony may be overstating the power of the principle; or perhaps I'm misreading him. As he says, "...something is definitely going on." However, it is too much to say, simply from this principle, that
"shape-changing and mimicry" "is definitely going on." (Though I do believe that there's enogh evidence in 5HC that, indeed, there are shapechangers.) Rather, _something_ is going on that makes shape-changing and mimicry deeply important in that book.

Do shapechangers exist on Urth, then?

Howinole should I know?

But mimicry -- especially memory-based mimicry -- is going on all over the place, and disguises of various sorts are rampant throughout the Book of the New Sun and its various sequelae.

In my never sufficiently humble opinion, it's clear to me that identity is one of Wolfe's major themes, not only here but in all his major work since 5HC, and that memory, mimicry,
shape-changing, disguises, cloning, and many other things that go on in those major works are all aspects, or subthemes if you will, of this supertheme.

2006-08-01 Tony Ellis replying to b sharp #

Clarifies that the poster never claimed hearsay alone proves the Annese shapeshift or mimic, arguing instead that such reports cannot be dismissed and their volume signals something important. Responds to b sharp's objection that the principle overstates the case, offering a concrete reason to think the Annese are mimics in the text.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Annese are mimics; widespread hearsay signals a real basis

You are slightly misreading me, I think, or more likely I wasn't being as clear as I thought I was, as I thought I was being very careful *not* to say "because there's so much hearsay, we know that shape-changing and mimicry are definitely going on." There are far more concrete reasons to believe the Annese are mimics.

My only points were that (a) Annese shape-shifting/mimicry can't be *dismissed* just because it's hearsay, and that (b) the sheer volume of this hearsay should warn us that something important is being communicated.

Exactly what that 'something' is we can argue about indefinitely, and knowing this list we no doubt shall. My own take on it is that we are simply being told "the myth that the Annese could change their shape is too widespread not to have some basis in reality."
