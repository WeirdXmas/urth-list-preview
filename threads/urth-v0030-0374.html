Themes: Ghosts, dreams and visions

2001-05-04 Alice Turner #

Argues that the London Review of Books article on Edward Kelley will likely speculate whether he was a fraud or genuinely hallucinating, and links this to Crowley's use of Kelley and Madimi in Aegypt. Grounds the claim in Crowley's thesis that reality frays during a passage time and in the book's hints that Kelley lies at least twice; also mentions the poster's Locus interview with Crowley.
proposes · author-and-biography · source-and-allusion · theories: Kelley's visions may be fraud or hallucination

It will be relevant. It will give what is known of the facts of Kelley's life and then it will speculate on whether he was a scam artist or someone genuinely given to hallucinations. I predict that with confidence--there's no other reason to do a piece on Kelley. And that, of course, is what Crowley is also playing with. He leans toward presenting Madimi as real, since his thesis is that during a "passage time" reality is stretched so thin that it is frayed, but he never quite asserts it; he also lets you know that Kelley is lying at least twice.

I would very much like to read this piece when it goes up; if anyone notices, please post. BTW, my interview with Crowley (somewhat different from the chapbook, though they overlap) is in the new Locus. Charlie Brown claims to have added to it, but in a quick skim through it I can't see where. It looks just what I turned in. Hmm, I guess that's what we call a seamless edit.

-alga
