2000-01-29 Patrick O'Leary #

Disputes Swanwick's reading of the Ziggurat story as a tale of madness, cloning and incest, arguing instead that it is a reconciliation between the sexes. Points to the text: the violence falls on the narrator himself, there is no sign of self-delusion or threats toward family, and four readings show nothing suggesting cloning.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Ziggurat narrator is not untrustworthy; Ziggurat is a story of sexual reconciliation

Must agree with Alga and Nutria.

Swanwick's reading seems cockamamie to me. I mean where do we point at the text and say: There! He's a totally untrustworthy narrator? See? Where do we see some evidence of self-delusion, true psychosis? Where do we see him as murderous? All the violence in the story is directed at himself. And in self-defense at the end. Where do we see him threatening, wishing harm or ill on the coyote, his son, his children, his wife? Cloning? Where do we get cloning? Sure there's an analogy between the 3 woman and the women in his life. But cloning?

The text does not suggest this to me after 4 readings.

This reading, as far as I can tell, demands that we deny 90% of what we're told happened in the story.

Wish I could expand, but must go. It's a dreadful interpretation of what I take to be a story of reconciliation between the sexes not madness or incest.

But that's my take.

Rave on lupines!

Patrick O'leary
