Themes: Robots, androids and artificial life

1997-07-15 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Argues that the monitors in the Whorl's telecom units and the lander's control intelligence differ enough in context to act differently, though they behave alike as nannies. Responds to Kevin McGuire by suggesting a special directive against non-humans or aliens, not specifically the inhumi, could explain why the lander monitor ignores chems, citing Wolfe's remark about Typhon.
proposes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Lander monitor has a special directive against non-humans

Reply: Item #7414904 from [email]@INET#

Kevin McGuire,

Well the contexts (being a polite intercity telecom service unit versus being the controling intelligence for a planetary landing vehicle) are different enough that, even if the monitors are virtually identical (i.e., more similar than a tv and a computer display), they would have room to act differently. But they always act like nannies, so maybe the perception that there is a difference is flawed?

Since Wolfe said that if Typhon had known anything about the inhumi he would have been quite against them, maybe this also shows up in a special directive, as you suggest, but not specifically "inhumi" just "non-human" (which would cause lander monitor to ignore chems) or "alien."

=mantis=
