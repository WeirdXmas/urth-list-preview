The thread: The thread began with John Watkins suggesting that claimed memories from before age three may be later recollections of writing them down, and brunians countering with anecdotal evidence of their own early memories and a memory-recovery exercise. Joshua Solomon disputed the claim of remembering one's circumcision at eight days old, citing general research on early childhood memory rather than Wolfe. Stuart Hamm, Son of Witz and others drifted into unrelated anecdotes, and brunians shifted to lifelong alien-abduction dreams linked to The Book of the New Sun and Wolfe's Catholicism. Matthew King ended on a note of agreement, pointing brunians to psychologist Endel Tulving's idea of mental time travel that echoes the backward-acting reading idea. The discussion largely drifted from Wolfe to general memory science and personal anecdote without resolution.
  • John Watkins: pre-three memories may be later recollections of writing them down
  • brunians: personal and acquaintances' memories reach before age three; reading the book affects the reader backward along the timeline
  • Joshua Solomon: adults rarely recall anything before three or three and a half, per general research
  • Stuart Hamm: memory is unreliable, citing false recall of an uncaught Amsterdam crash film
  • Matthew King: Tulving's mental time travel matches brunians' backward-acting idea

Themes: Memory and forgetting, Ghosts, dreams and visions, Gnosticism, heresy and other theologies

2008-12-05 Josh Geller replying to John Watkins #

Recounts the poster's lifelong dreams of being taken by aliens and castrated, which stopped being mere visions after reading The Book of the New Sun. Describes an early conversation with Wolfe, where Wolfe said he was an orthodox Catholic rather than a Gnostic and the two agreed the visions might echo back up the timeline.
informs · author-and-biography · theories: Reading the book echoes back up the timeline

Interestingly enough, long before I read TBoTNS I would have these occasional dreams or visions or hallucinations etc wherein I was to be taken up by space aliens and judged in some way and if I was found wanting I would be castrated. These were in no way pleasant, and sometimes could be quite distracting. Of course, remember that this is someone whose earliest memory is getting the end of his penis cut off talking to you.

Anyway.

You can imagine that when I read TBoTNS I was completely blown away. At that point I knew I had to talk with Wolfe about it. I met him for the first time in the early 80's in the company of John Cramer, a theoretical physicist and childhood friend of his, and we had a long discussion, in the course of which and among many other things I discovered that Wolfe was not a gnostic but an orthodox Catholic, I first heard the name 'Jorge Luis Borges' as someone whose writing he thought I might enjoy and we agreed that my hallucinations or what have you were probably caused by the experience of reading the book (not necessarily just *my* experience of reading the book) echoing back up the timeline.

Anyway, the whole thing was pretty seminal for me and is deep in towards the core of my ideas about time and Reality and so on.

.
