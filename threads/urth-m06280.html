The thread: Dave Tallman opened by tracing Thecla's epitaph "Here Rose the Graced, not Rose the Chaste" to the Rosamond legend, and asked whether the maze and library motifs echo Earth history and the Long Sun Rose. Mo Holkar questioned whether A Solar Labyrinth misplaces Rosamund's maze at Hampton Court, which dates from the late seventeenth century, while Woodstock was the legendary site. Dave Lebling joked about solving the Hampton Court maze quickly and added an anecdote. The thread then shifted to whether the library echoes Eco and Borges, with Jeff Wilson giving an architectural explanation and Matthew Keeley arguing the parallels were coincidental because the English Name of the Rose came out after The Shadow of the Torturer. Holkar ended with a whimsical suggestion about shared Borges reading rather than evidence, so the thread drifted without resolution.

2008-06-22 Dave Tallman #

Traces Thecla's quoted epitaph "Here Rose the Graced, not Rose the Chaste" to the historical Rosamond legend, noting Higden's account of a labyrinthine house and a clue of thread. Observes the maze and library references, suggests the cycle may repeat Earth history, and asks whether the Long Sun Rose connects.
informs · source-and-allusion · myth-and-structure · theories: Thecla's epitaph derives from the Rosamond legend

In Shadow X, Thecla quotes:
"Here Rose the Graced, not Rose the Chaste, reposes.
The scent that rises is no scent of roses."

In http://www.bibliomania.com/2/3/255/1183/23925/1.html I found the following:

Rosamond (Fair). Higden, monk of Chester, says: ?She was the fayre daughter of Walter, Lord Clifford, concubine of Henry II., and poisoned by Queen Elianor, A.D. 1177. Henry made for her a house of wonderful working, so that no man or woman might come to her. This house was named Labyrinthus, and was wrought like unto a knot in a garden called a maze. But the queen came to her by a clue of thredde, and so dealt with her that she lived not long after. She was buried at Godstow, in an house of nunnes, with these verses upon her tombe:-

Here Rose the graced, not Rose the chaste, reposes;
The smell that rises is no smell of roses.

This story is referenced in "A Solar Labyrinth": "The Fayre Rosamund dropped her embroidery with her needle thrust through it, but forgot the yarn in her pocket, thus furnishing Queen Eleanor's knights with the clue they required to solve Hampton Court Maze."

I have a couple of observations from this. First, this is yet another example of a reference to Earth's history. Urth is either in Earth's future or the Big Bang/Grand Gnab cycle repeats in so many details that free will is suspect.

Second, this is one of many maze/labyrinth references in the New Sun books. Another interesting one is the labyrinthine library of Master Ultan, which recalls the library ruled by another Borges-like figure in Eco's "The Name of the Rose." (This connects roses and mazes once again).

Can anyone see a connection with the character named Rose (or Rose-in-Marble) in the Long Sun books?

2008-06-23 Jeff Wilson replying to Matthew Keeley #

Argues that the labyrinthine library in Wolfe's New Sun books is less a literary allusion to Borges than a product of how early twentieth-century university buildings were constructed. Responds to Dave Tallman's suggestion, quoted by Matthew Keeley, that Ultan's library echoes Eco's The Name of the Rose and Borges, by offering a mundane architectural explanation linked to Wolfe's own university days.
disputes · author-and-biography · source-and-allusion · theories: Ultan's library reflects university building construction rather than Borges

In the same vein, the realities of early 20th century university construction dictate a juxtaposition of diverse styles and periods as well as enclosed and exposed spaces connected via a subterranean maze of utility tunnels to inspire writers in residence, whether as students (Wolfe) or professors (Borges).

2008-06-23 Matthew Keeley replying to Dave Tallman #

Argues that the parallels between The Book of the New Sun and Eco's The Name of the Rose are coincidental, not deliberate allusions. Supports this by noting that The Shadow of the Torturer appeared in 1980, the year the Italian Name of the Rose came out, while the English edition followed only in 1983, so Wolfe could not have drawn on it.
disputes · source-and-allusion · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Wolfe's Ultan library echoes Eco's Name of the Rose library

I once started theorizing about references to The Name of the Rose in The Book of the New Sun, but I'm afraid the parallels between the two books are just (beautiful) coincidences. The Shadow of the Torturer appeared in 1980, the same year that The Name of the Rose appeared in Italian. The English edition didn't appear until 1983.

I guess great minds really do think alike.

-Matt

Playfully suggests that Gene Wolfe and Umberto Eco, born eight months apart, may both have been reading translated Borges at the same time, offering this as a possible explanation for parallels between The Book of the New Sun and The Name of the Rose.
