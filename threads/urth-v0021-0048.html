1998-11-06 Kevin J. Maroney #

Notes that Neil Gaiman's fondness for G.K. Chesterton is well documented because a character in Sandman is clearly modeled on Chesterton, and asks whether the other poster was remarking on the oddity of finding this on a Borges site. The post is social and off-Wolfe, offering a comment on a Gaiman link rather than on any Wolfe text.
questions

The Gaiman fact is pretty well-established--there's a character in _Sandman_ who is clearly Chesterton. (Gilbert, who features prominently in the second story arc, "A Doll's House", with occasional other appearances throughout the series, is physically and temperamentally very much modeled on Chesterton.)

Or were you commenting on the delightful oddity of learning about Gaiman's fondness for Chesterton on a Borges site?

1998-11-06 Alice Turner #

Informs readers that Harold Bloom's Shakespeare: The Invention of the Human mentions G.K. Chesterton, quoting a passage where Chesterton claims Shakespeare was a Catholic in spirit and compares him unfavourably with Dante. The post shares this quotation without commenting on Wolfe, offering it as a passing sighting of Chesterton in another book.
informs

He's turning up everywhere. I've just started to read Harold Bloom's -Shakespeare: The Invention of the Human-. On p 10:

"Though G.K. Chesterton liked to think that Shakespeare was a Catholic, at least in spirit, Chesterton was too good a critic to locate Shakespeare's universalism in Christianity. We might learn from that not to shape Shakespeare by our own cultural politics. Comparing Shakespeare with Dante, Chesterton emphasized Date's spaciousness in dealing with Christian love and Christian liberty, whereas Shakespeare "was a pagan, in so far as he is at his greatest is describing great spirits in chaos."....

-alga
