Gormenghast

Urth list · 9 messages · 2001-06-24 to 2001-07-09

2001urthJoshua A. SolomonPeter WestlakeEndy9Alice TurnerJohn-Paul WellcoatE Dorian
The thread: Alice Turner asked American readers whether they would watch the PBS two-part Gormenghast adaptation, and the thread drifted into chat about the BBC production. John-Paul Wellcoat advised recording it and praised its vivid staging, and Spectacled Bear credited Mervyn Peake's paintings and China years for the look. Endy9 admitted unfamiliarity and later reported giving up on a film after an hour, while Joshua A. Solomon called Peake's novels classics. E Dorian recommended the adaptation he saw at Readercon. The thread never touched Wolfe's fiction and ended in scattered social remarks.
  • John-Paul Wellcoat: record the PBS adaptation and set aside expectations, its staging is surprisingly vivid
  • Spectacled Bear: the vivid look comes from Peake's paintings and China years, approved by his daughter
  • Joshua A. Solomon: Peake's Titus Groan and Gormenghast are classics everyone on the list would enjoy
  • Endy9: unfamiliar with the book, finds it strange but appealing; later could only watch an hour of a film
  • E Dorian: the PBS adaptation beats most book-to-film adaptations, worry it will disappoint after reading the novels

Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Genre: science fiction, fantasy, both, Mirrors, pictures and art

2001-06-25 John-Paul Wellcoat #

Advises Alice Turner to set aside expectations and record the PBS Gormenghast adaptation rather than stay in to watch it, remarking that the director's vivid, colourful staging is surprising. The post responds to Alice's message about planning to watch the two-part PBS broadcast, and it is social chat about a television adaptation of a non-Wolfe novel.
chats

You will have to do more than lower your expectations to enjoy this, you will have to ensure you have no expectations. Then just maybe you may see some light. I can't imagine how the director was inspired to make Gormenghast so vibrant and colouful.
Don't stay in, I suggest setting the video.
JP

2001-07-09 E Dorian #

Recommends the PBS Gormenghast adaptation he saw at Readercon, praising it over most book-to-film adaptations and asking whether it will disappoint once he reads the novels. Responds to Alice Turner's thread-opening note about the PBS two-parter, noting Part One aired and he taped it.
chats

This video was shown at Readercon a year ago. I knew nothing about Gormenghast, but decided to check it out, and thought it was excellent.

I find that most movies based on books are horrible, and that the screen writers need to show some creativity and write their OWN stories, instead of shredding the written stories we love. When I finally read the Gormenghast books, will this film fall into the same disappointing group of films as, say, Starship Troopers?

BTW, The Part One of Gormenghast was on last night, and I taped it. Part Two is on tonight!

Darryl
