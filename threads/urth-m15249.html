2010urthThe Book of the New SunJohn WatkinsJerry FriedmanCliff JudgePaul BPedro PereiraJeff WilsonDan'l Danehy-OakesRoy C. Lackey
The thread: Readers debated whether the Wall around Nessus is a buried colony ship like the Long Sun ships or a relic of spaceport infrastructure, and whether the Whorl was made by hollowing an asteroid. Jerry Friedman disputed the colony-ship idea because building on Urth makes no sense, while Roy Lackey cited Return to the Whorl's proper-names list to support asteroid origin. Cliff Judge questioned the Baldanders dream exchange and the Whorl's size, and Paul B and Jeff Wilson offered spaceport-based readings of the Wall, with Jeff arguing the Wall cannot be as large as the Whorl. Pedro Pereira offered a cyclotron claim without grounds. The thread ended without consensus, drifting from the dream question into construction and scale.
  • Jerry Friedman: Wall is not a Long Sun-style colony ship, since building on Urth makes no sense; dream exchange may be memory confusion or a lie
  • Roy C. Lackey: Whorl was hollowed from an asteroid, confirmed by Return to the Whorl's proper-names list
  • Cliff Judge: proposes buried colony ship; questions Whorl size and Baldanders' dream denial; contragrav would make atmospheric construction possible
  • Paul B: Wall is a scaled-up Citadel blast shield for spaceship launches from an earlier age
  • Jeff Wilson: Wall cannot match the Whorl's size; likely a former spaceport perimeter with Citadel towers as grounded ships
  • Pedro Pereira: Wall is a cyclotron, offered without supporting grounds

Themes: Stories within stories, Memory and forgetting, Unreliable narration

2010-05-18 Jeff Wilson replying to Cliff Judge #

Argues that the Wall around Nessus cannot be as large as the Whorl, since it is overtly artificial and lacks room for miles of height or depth, and proposes it was once a spaceport perimeter with the citadel's towers as grounded ships. Responds to Cliff Judge's question about the Whorl's diameter and the rural land inside the Wall, citing the Whorl's asteroid origin and the Wall's artificial construction as grounds.
proposes · close-textual-reading · genre-and-craft · theories: The Wall was a spaceport perimeter berm; Citadel towers are grounded ships; The Whorl is an asteroid

They might have similar diameters, but the Whorl is similarly long or longer lengthwise, and is visibly (from the outside) made from an asteroid. The Wall is overtly artificial and there's just not enough room for miles of height extending into the sky or depth below the soil.

The best-sounding guess I've heard is that the great Wall surrounded the world's primary spaceport in some earlier age, partly for controlling ingress and egress and partly as a protective berm in case of an accident. The towers of the citadel are the grounded ships of course, and the citadel's curtain wall was added in a later but still antique age to form a redoubt within the Wall. The curtain wall is generally masonry, but the Great Wall may also be an atom smasher or otherwise serve a third purpose.

2010-05-18 Cliff Judge replying to Jeff Wilson #

Questions how large the Whorl must be, arguing that Nessus is huge and that Severian's walk from the Citadel to the Wall within a day implies a considerable distance. Grounds this in the sequence of events: walking all day and night, the Inn with Dr Talos and Baldanders, the fiacre ride, the Sanguinary Field, and the short walk to the Wall the next day.
questions · chronology-and-plot-logic

I assumed Nessus was really really big. It's very old, still expanding, and yet there is a bunch of real estate inside the wall that is rural.

How big in diameter does the Whorl need to be? Fifty miles?

Apparently the Citadel is in the center of Nessus...and Sev leaves it, spends all day and all night walking, stays at the Inn and meets Dr Talos and Baldanders, encounters Agia, has a crazy ride in a fiacre, winds up at the Sanguniary Field, then after executing Agilus and rejoining the troupe, it seems like the next day they don't have to walk too far to get to the Wall.

It seems to me he could have crossed a pretty considerable distance in that time.
