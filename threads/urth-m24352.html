The future of cloning, continued‏

2013urthThe Book of the New SunDavid StockhoffLee Berman
The thread: Lee and David Stockhoff discussed whether cloning obstacles are technical and how clones might be made from embryonic or fetal cells, with David asking how khaibits would replace dead originals in The Book of the New Sun. Lee argued that names like Thea, Pega and Thais, plus shared heights and sunken cheeks, reveal clone status in the text, while conceding the evidence is contradictory. Lee suggested Wolfe deliberately withheld clone details, extending the identity mystery of The Fifth Head of Cerberus. The thread ended without resolution, with the exchange moving from science to textual evidence.

2013-12-24 Lee Berman #

Speculates that cloning obstacles are mainly due to immature technology, and that cloning from a gastrula, embryo or fetus cell would avoid epigenetic problems. Responds to David Stockhoff's question about whether Doctor Talos is in the house, linking the idea to the cloned khaibit that supplies blood in the books.
proposes · theology-and-religion

Or even Doctor Crane?

I'm not an expert but it seems to me the current obstacles to cloning animals (plants are easier) reside mostly in the technology being in its infancy. I think epigenetic problems derive mostly from the accumulated centromere shortening and acquired mutations found in adult animal cells.

If you started your clone from a cell culled from a gastrula or even an embryo or fetus, I think most of the problems are avoided and you have your khaibit, ready to supply you blood (and body parts?) as needed.

2013-12-24 David Stockhoff replying to Lee #

Speculates that cloning a person at birth, using stem cells as the source, would yield a khaibit of the same age, and asks whether khaibits of dead originals take their place in secret or are discarded. Builds on Lee's quoted point about starting clones from embryonic or fetal cells to avoid centromere shortening and mutation problems.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · source-and-allusion · theories: Khaibits could secretly replace dead originals

Makes sense. Stem cells, in other words. And you'd want to clone a person at birth anyway to get a khaibit of the same age. Not to mention that the decision to clone you is not based on your adult accomplishments but your status at birth.

I wonder what happens to khaibits whose originals die. Do they take their place in secret? They would then be able to marry and carry on the family. Or are they discarded?

2013-12-30 Lee Berman replying to Lee #

Argues that names such as Thea, Pega and Thais reveal clone status in The Book of the New Sun, citing shared heights and sunken cheeks. Concedes the evidence is contradictory, and suggests Wolfe deliberately withheld clone details, continuing the identity mystery of The Fifth Head of Cerberus. Replies to David Stockhoff's question about khaibits whose originals die.
disputes · puzzle-solving · source-and-allusion · theories: Names like Thea and Pega denote clone status

The implication from House Azure is that the khaibits are notably shorter than their exultant donor/mistresses.

Still, I remain enamoured of my theory that nomenclature can denote clone status, as Thea is a derived diminutive of Thecla and Pega is the same for her mistress named Pelagia.

But Thecla and Thea seem both to be equally of exultant height. And Thea has her own khaibit named Thais (wordplay for "Thea's"?)

Then there are the "sunken cheeks". Thais has them. So does Severian. And is a "heart- shaped" face a sunken-cheek version of a "triangular face"? Mark of a clone? A product of having blood siphoned off?

I dunno. Too much contradictory and inconclusive evidence. I have to concede that Wolfe did not want us to fully know so much about everyone who is a clone in BotNS, perhaps to maintain the dark sense of identity mystery first established in Fifth Head of Cerberus.
