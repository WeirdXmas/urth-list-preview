Summary of the case for Silk as Typhon's clone

Urth list · 14 messages · 2011-01-19 to 2011-01-20

2011urthThe Book of the New SunThe Book of the Long SunJane DelawneyAntónio Pedro MarquesGerry QuinnMarc AraminiSon of WitzDan'l Danehy-OakesAndrew Mason
The thread: The thread debated whether Silk is Typhon's clone, son, or unrelated, with Dan'l Danehy-Oakes proposing the clone reading and Marc Aramini arguing for a son born of Kypris. Critics including António Pedro Marques, Gerry Quinn, and Andrew Mason rejected the clone thesis for lacking positive evidence and conflicting with the text, such as Pas directing the gods' appearances and Typhon's mother. A side debate arose over whether Mucor's offspring in the tunnel are dogs or lynxes, which Jane Delawney settled from the text. Agia's and Hethor's pursuit of Severian was also argued as revenge versus a deeper motive, with no consensus reached and the thread ending in unresolved disagreement.
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: Silk is Typhon's clone made to lead people with Typhon's psychic power
  • Marc Aramini: Silk is Typhon's son, possibly a love child with Kypris, and Agia's pursuit is explained by Severian's killings
  • António Pedro Marques: Clone theory fails since Pas would have made Typhon's family look like Typhon; Agia and Hethor's stalking hints at another motive
  • Gerry Quinn: Clone evidence is weak and invoking ancient super-science risks obscuring the author's meaning
  • Andrew Mason: Silk is probably not a clone; lack of positive evidence and problems with the download idea
  • Jane Delawney: Mucor's offspring are lynxes, not dogs, according to the Long Sun text

Themes: Doubles, twins and clones, Hidden identities and parentage, Robots, androids and artificial life

2011-01-19 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that Silk is Typhon's clone rather than Pas's son, reasoning that Typhon wanted a new body with his psychic power to lead people. Grounds the case in Typhon's refusal to download his consciousness, the Mainframe god-forms, and Mamelta's possible unreliability in recognizing Pas, while conceding these are not positive evidence.
proposes · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Silk is Typhon's clone; Typhon preserved his brain and needed a new body; Mamelta failed to recognize Pas as an adult

Suppose that Silk were Pas's son of the body, or even his clone. Would people point at him and say, "There goes Pas"?

Not necessarily.

If this were the case then Silk would look -- modulo
environmental/developmental differences -- like young Typhon, to be sure.

But does Pas look like young Typhon? I question this. We know that at least _some_ of the other gods of Mainframe present as god-forms, either idealized versions of themselves or otherwise different. Why would Pas be different? I suspect that Pas-in-Mainframe does _not_ look enough like Typhon for a much-younger version of himself to attract that much notice.

This leaves two special cases: Mamelta and Rigoglio.

Rigoglio we can dismiss; the Silk (body) he sees is missing an eye and otherwise scarred by time and sorrow.

Which leaves Mamelta -- whose memories may have been tampered with, and who has been possessed by Mucor. Is it that unreasonable to suppose that she wouldn't recognize a much-younger version of the Monarch?

I believe then that there is no serious bar to Silk being Pas's son, or even his clone. But that's not the same as positive evidence.

Why then would I believe that Silk is Typhon's clone?

Typhon is psychically adept in some way. And he isn't willing to have his consciousness downloaded into another (such as Piaton), though the technology is clearly available to him.

I suggest that it isn't just (though Typhon seems to say so) that people are "accustomed" to obeying that particular face; it is that Typhon has psychic ability to make people follow him. He was unwilling to give this up, so he had to preserve his brain -- and a brain transplant was too risky for a number of reasons. So he sought to immortalize himself by having his head put onto Piaton's body.

But also he sought to be the Father-God of the _Whorl_; and I suggest that he was not going to give that authority up when the _Whorl_ reached its destination. He intended to lead the Cargo to its new home, and to do so required a body. What body would Typhon want?

Why, one with the psychic ability to make people follow him. He may be a god, but he isn't giving up any advantage. That body would be a clone of the one he left behind.

Now, who has an uncanny ability to make people follow him? Why, Silk...

2011-01-19 Marc Aramini replying to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that Silk is Typhon's son, weighing whether he is a clone of Typhon or a genetically distinct love child, and whether the embryo was ever grown. Grounds this on Kypris being named as mother, Silk's face appearing on Pas, the repeated wish for a successor son, and the embryo's predesigned powers. Replies to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's point about Silk's uncanny ability to draw followers.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Silk is Typhon's love child rather than clone; Silk is Typhon's genetic son

Yes, and this is listed as why he was included as an embryo on the whorl. I think all the evidence, SAYING Kypris is mother OUTRIGHT, not even interpretationally, is ridiculously hard to ignore that Silk is her son, especially in light of his face on Pas and the thrice repeated monarch wanted a son to succeed him idea. However, I don't know if that embryo was ever fully grown before being placed on the whorl - was it "new" or just a copy? That is why I lean toward him being Typhon's love child more than a clone, genetically distinct, different in countenance, and then if he was engineered and then loaded into the whorl but never grown, no one but Kypris and Pas would know he was anyway, since Pas is not happy with his current brood, according to Lemur.

Against this is that they seem to know already what the embryo will do when it grows up (that it will exert that special attraction for others, but honestly Silk's gift seems to be intuitive leaps with that cheek circling bit.) And the appearance of Silk's face on Typhon's body. So I think the evidence is stronger for genetic son, but I just don't know if Silk was ever an adult or not, but he definitely was an embryo that had pre-designed powers, I just don't know if they knew that through scientific prediction or because they had the adult version already and knew what he did.

2011-01-20 Jane Delawney replying to Marc Aramini #

Corrects Marc Aramini's claim that Mucor's offspring are dogs, arguing they are lynxes, medium-sized cats such as Lion, not the bufes of the tunnels. Grounds this in the Long Sun text: Mucor's 'son' and the cats met in Blood's mansion, Lion's death in the Grand Manteion, and Silk's needler shots that were not confirmed fatal.
disputes · close-textual-reading

wth? Yes, Mucor does tell Silk she's some time before been implanted with some animal embryos and given birth to them. She refers to one of them at least as her 'son', in consequence. But these are not dogs; still less are they the gods/bufes of the tunnels. Her 'children' are the lynxes (medium-big cats) to whom Silk is introduced in the course of his first excursion into Blood's mansion. One of them is called Lion; others are not individually named but it is clear that there at least six of them, male and female.

At the end of BoTLS one of these cats has definitely been killed (Lion himself, in the gallery of the Grand Manteion, defending Silk and Auk) but the fates of the others (at least five others) are not determined. Silk shot a couple of them with Hyacinth's needler, but there's no definite evidence that these were fatal shots. Even at the end of RTTW there could still be 5 or more lynxes wandering the Whorl, looking for mama Mucor.

Not at all sure why anyone has a confusion between these cats and the bufes. Just saying.

jd.

I don't think you can dismiss him, actually. Silk has just replicated Typhon's military strategy of taking the side of the underdog in a military conflict and winning, something which Rigoglio has discussed. 'Almost like a young Typhon' must have been a phrase that crossed his mind.

The Monarch who dominated the lives of his subjects, to the extent that his family danced through their dreams? The Monarch she TALKS ABOUT with Silk? Wouldn't she at least mention a resemblance?

But you also left out Echidna, Kypris and Scylla. Remember, the gods may not appear as they used to in life.
But all three know how they iused to appear.

The five people listed above effectively rule out the possibility that he is a clone of Typhon. Remember, this is literature, not life. It is a variant of Checkov's gun. A clone must be recognisable as such, or the author must give a reason why. Wolfe would never write anything so lame.

Of course, if he is Typhon's son the negative evidence is probably just about surmountable. He seems to have the same colour hair, at least. You'd still expect a stronger gesture from the author to note a familial resemblance.
