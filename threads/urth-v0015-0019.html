1998-06-25 Peter Cash #

Defends a reading that Marsch killed V.R.T., arguing the text leaves who killed whom deliberately ambiguous and that a spy-story conjecture is no more ridiculous than rivals. Replies to Peter Cash's jab about fan fiction, conceding the theory involves construction while inviting a more elegant explanation of Marsch's motives.
disputes · puzzle-solving · theories: Marsch killed V.R.T

Like scolex! I am crushed. But not dissuaded!

I fail to see how my central contention that Marsch killed V.R.T. is "ridiculous" or involves making up text. Indeed, who killed whom seems to have intentionally been left ambiguous by the Author; I fail to see why one conjecture is innately more ridiculous than the other. True, my "spy story" involves some ...er...construction on my part--you may suggest a more elegant explanation of Marsch's motivations, if you like. I would welcome it.

Since you are a civilian, madam, that is hardly relevant. And the Sergeant is very fond of mud.

Sgt. Rock

1998-06-25 Peter Cash #

Proposes that the name Forlesen derives from the German verb Vorlesen, meaning to recite or read aloud, and suggests Wolfe may intend the story as something read to children at night. Responds to Rostrum's question about the word's origin, offering a linguistic guess rather than assuming Wolfe invented it.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Forlesen derives from German Vorlesen

Well, it's very close to the German "Vorlesen", which means to recite or read aloud. I suppose maybe Wolfe thinks this is a good story to read to the kids at night?

Sgt. Rock
