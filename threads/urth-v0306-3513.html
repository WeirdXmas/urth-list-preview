Themes: Memory and forgetting

2003-06-22 Marc Aramini #

Argues that Latro's wrestling skill in Soldier of Arete reflects selective amnesia, with muscle memory from an older life that verbal coaching only awakens. Grounds this in his perfect execution after a reminder, his defeat of the Spartan trainer, and his losing to Pausanias only because Heracles told him to throw the fight.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Latro's wrestling skill comes from a remote past life

Latro has very selective amnesia - note that he is able to perform the wrestling moves with perfection once he is reminded of basic strategy. He needs a reminder - he doesn't know the full extent of his capabilities. Your average soldier, no matter how he is coached, won't be able to beat a master from verbal advice. Latro didn't need to learn the moves through repetition - they were already in his muscle memory. This is one aspect of realism that I think we've ignored - no matter how much you tell the average man on the street how to punch, or what kind of advice you give him, he can't outbox Roy Jones or Lennox Lewis. Unless the advice reawakens old or natural abilities. Note that Latro defeats the trainer of the Spartans with ease. He has problems with wrestling Pausanias because ... Hermes likes him?

So Latro remembers how to use a sword but not to wrestle ... why? Perhaps because the knowledge of wrestling was from a different, more remote lifetime. Remember that he only loses because Heracles tells him to throw the fight.

Marc Aramini

2003-06-23 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that Latro's head injury impairs recall of long-term abstract memories, which explains why he can use a sword but not the wrestling moves he once knew, while he may still be forming such memories. Answers Marc's question about Latro's selective amnesia, then asks whether Latro recounts any dreams in the Soldier books.
answers · close-textual-reading · theories: Latro's head injury blocks long-term abstract recall

In short: The injury to Latro's head seems to have created an inability to recall informaiton from long-term stores at some cognitive levels -- some fairly abstract cognitive levels; this is clearly an injury in the "higher" centers of the brain.

One thing that I find interesting is that it is quite possible that he's still
actually able to _form_ long-term abstract memories. He'd never know (nor, presumably, would we), because until/unless he's cured, he still can't recall them.

It's been a while since I've read the existing-so-far _Soldier_ books. Can any of you tell me, offhand: Does Latro recount any dreams at all?
