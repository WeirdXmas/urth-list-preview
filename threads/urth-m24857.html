2013urthThe Book of the New SunGerry QuinnMarc AraminiAndreas Johansson
The thread: Participants debated whether the Whorl and Blue are literally linked to Urth or only symbolically, and how the Whorl's chronology and Blue's red sun should be explained. Marc Aramini argued the link is intended literally, though impossible to prove, and read the Blue solar system as humanity's destiny, not a doppelgänger. Andreas Johansson proposed a symbolic link, with Typhon sending the Whorl to colonize a pristine-Urth-like planet, and raised time dilation. Gerry Quinn backed Johansson's subtler reading, questioned Blue's science, and offered a hard-SF account with about fifty light years and no significant dilation. The thread ended as an impasse between literal and symbolic readings, with no shared conclusion.
  • Marc Aramini: the Urth-Whorl link is intended literally, though unprovable, and Blue is humanity's destiny rather than a doppelgänger
  • Andreas Johansson: the link is symbolic, and Typhon sent the Whorl to colonize a planet resembling a pristine Urth
  • Gerry Quinn: Wolfe aims at subtler targets than 'Earth all along', and Blue's red sun implies dilation Typhon's motive does not explain
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: the Ring of Glass could be Father Inire's Atropaic Gardens if Urth is Blue

Themes: The dying sun and the cosmos, Time loops and causality, Deep time and ruins

2013-10-11 Marc Aramini replying to Gerry Quinn #

Argues that Wolfe intended the link between Urth and the Whorl to be literal though impossible to prove, and that hybridization could cause instant speciation, changing Urth's few remaining animals. Responds to Gerry Quinn's remark on Johansson's "Earth all along" by citing Maytera's 300-versus-3000-year slip and the Inhumi city's echo of Nessus and Severian's myth.
proposes · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Urth and the Whorl literally linked but unprovable; Hybridization causes instant speciation of Urth animals; Maytera's 300-versus-3000-year slip creates timeline problem; Inhumi city echoes Nessus and Severian's myth

This time line is exacerbated by the decimal slip in maytera's thoughts at one point in long sun. You know, 300 years, 3000 years, whatever.

I think Wolfe intended the relationship to be literal but impossible to prove, a nagging feeling of possibility that requires a sophisticated physical and biological mechanism just hinted at throughout. Enough equation between the city of the inhumi and Nessus and enough synchronicity between the mythic re-enactment of a man with a sword and a light letting free the waters and clearing out a sewer to be an echo of Severian's myth on Green.

Hybridization equals INSTANT Speciation. Eat em all, spit em out. Could be very little time for the few animals left on urth to be changed.

2013-10-11 Marc Aramini replying to Andreas Johansson #

Argues that the Blue solar system is not a symbolic doppelgänger but the story of humanity's destiny, in which Severian's salvation is not for mankind and his new world becomes hellish for those who do not move on. Responds to Andreas Johansson's rationalist idea that Typhon sent the Whorl to colonize a planet resembling a pristine Urth, calling his own reading the one that stays with him despite criticism from around 2002.
disputes · allegory-and-typology · theories: Severian's salvation is not for mankind; Blue's world is Urth's hellish future

This one will always be my hobgoblin no matter how many other of my readings are seen as plausible, probably from the heaps and heaps (and heaps) of abuse it garnered circa 2002.

It is quite literally the story of humanities destiny- sev's salvation turns out to be not at all for mankind, an his new world becomes hellish for those who don't move on, completely unrecognizable. Terrible and unforeseeable, not a cliche the way he does it.

2013-10-11 Andreas Johansson #

Proposes that the Whorl's link to our solar system is symbolic, and that Typhon sent it to colonize a planet resembling a pristine Urth. Responds to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's idea that the Ring of Glass could be Father Inire's Atropaic Gardens if Urth is Blue, and raises time dilation as an answer to the Whorl's chronology.
proposes · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Whorl is a symbolic doppelgänger of our solar system; Typhon sent the Whorl to colonize a planet like pristine Urth; Ring of Glass is ruins of Atropaic Gardens if Urth is Blue; Time dilation explains the Whorl's chronology

Seems more and more as if the relations between "our" solar system an that of Blue should be considered symbolical, another doppelg?nger. Which has a very rational explanation - Typhon sent the Whorl to colonize another planet, seems reasonable that he should send it to a planet that resembles a pristine Urth. A very down-to-earth, common sense explanation that's not very satisfying, but nonetheless it gives all the mind-blowing depth of Wolfe a refreshing rationality. Which is of course already there, the SF tropes hinted at from time to time (it's still just a generation ship run by computer-gods, loaded with deeper meaning).

And come to think of it, Urthlings in our deep future/Severian's deep past travelled all across the galaxy, right? Are Horn & the other whorlings necessary the first humans to reach Blue & Green?

Was going to ask also about the chronological work-out between the Whorl and Urth, since Typhon's reign seems to be much more than three centuries past from Severian's age (if the astral projection to Urth isn't a time travel as well), but then I realized: time dilation & relativity. Ship time doesn't equal planet time - who knows how long the Whorl was really out there, and how far they've travelled? The crew at the East Pole could surely answe that but then again no-one asks them. Fascinating how Wolfe created the Whorl cultures in such a pre-enlightenment setting.

2013-10-11 Gerry Quinn replying to Andreas Johansson #

Agrees with Andreas Johansson that Wolfe aims at subtler targets than 'it was Earth all along' and questions the science of Blue's red Sun in Return to the Whorl. Notes that such a distance implies time dilation, which Typhon's motive for warming Urth does not explain, and offers a hard-SF reading with about fifty light years and no significant dilation.
supports · chronology-and-plot-logic · genre-and-craft · theories: Blue is 1000-2000 light years from Urth; Wolfe did not consider the science closely; Hard SF reading: fifty light years, no dilation

From: Andreas Johansson

Agreed. Indeed, I would argue that "it was Earth all along" (to quote from the Planet of the Apes musical in The Simpsons) is actually too much of an SF cliche. Wolfe aims at subtler targets than such cheeseball revelations.

It's worth noting that in Return to the Whorl, Silk/Horn specifically points out the Sun as a faint red star in the sky of Blue.

That does actually raise some questions if you look at it in depth, because you shouldn't be able to see such a star more than about fifty light years away. Yet three hundred and fifty years seems a bit short for the time between Typhon and Severian on Urth, so one naturally invokes time dilation, which would in turn place them a multiple of 350 light years away, perhaps up to 2000 light years or so, from where the Sun would certainly be invisible.

There is also the question of why Typhon was bothered by the dimming of the Sun, if he could build engines powerful enough to accelerate the Whorl to a substantial fraction of light speed, he could easily have built satellites to warm Urth.

I take the view that Wolfe probably didn't consider these details so closely, and that we are intended to assume that time dilation took place and Blue is in fact 1000-2000 light years away from Urth. [In short, I would apply the same caution to reading the science too closely as I would apply to reading other elements...]

But if you want to go with a hard SF interpretation, they went about fifty light years in total, and there was no significant time dilation. 350 years ago on Earth today was 1663, so it is still time enough for a lot of history.

- Gerry Quinn
