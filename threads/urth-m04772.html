2008-12-03 Josh Geller replying to O'Donnell, Tim (BOSI) #

Explains that a ghost is the remnant of a dead person while a ghoul is a creature that eats the dead, and agrees with Tim O'Donnell that Wolfe uses eidolon and aquastor interchangeably. Responds to O'Donnell's comparison of the two terms to ghoul and ghost, offering a definitional distinction as support for his point.
supports · close-textual-reading · theories: Wolfe uses eidolon and aquastor interchangeably

A ghost is a remnant of a dead entity, normally a person.

A ghoul is a creature that eats the bodies of the dead. It may or may not have been a living person at some point.

That said, I agree with you that Wolfe is using eidolon and aquastor interchangeably.

2008-12-03 O'Donnell, Tim (BOSI) #

Argues that the terms eidolon and aquastor are not strongly distinct in Wolfe's books, and offers a working distinction: aquastors are machine-generated and physically present, while eidolons are non-physical images beamed into the brain. Relies on a previously quoted list rule of thumb by Mantis, and compares the difference to that between a ghoul and a ghost.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Eidolons and aquastors are interchangeable terms; Aquastors are machine-generated and physically present; Eidolons are non-physical images beamed into the brain

Hi Son of Witz,

I don't think there is a strong difference between them at all.

Mantis came up with a useful (to me anyway) rule of thumb a while back.

"Wolfe uses (or seems to use) "aquastor" and "eidolon" interchangeably. However, at times in the past when I was trying to ferret out the different forms of "ghosts" involved (i.e., hair-splitting) I leaned on "aquastor" as being (1) machine generated (as opposed to biological mind generated), (2) having physical presence;
leaving "eidolons" to be the (1) non-physical, beamed directly into the brain, seen by nobody else forms." http://www.urth.net/urth/archives/v0009/0739.shtml

To be honest, I think the difference is akin to explaining the difference between a "ghoul" and a "ghost" to someone. (Nitpickers have at it, I haven't checked my OED so there could be a distinct difference between the two!)
