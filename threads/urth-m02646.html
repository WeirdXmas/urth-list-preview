The thread: Participants debated whether non-human beings in Wolfe's Book of the New Sun and related books carry saint names or mythological ones, and whether such names imply human descent. James Wynn argued that only Commonwealth citizens bear saint names, so aliens are mythological, and that Hierogrammates descend from humans because humans made them. Mornings Glory corrected this, pointing out that the hypernaut sleepers keep saint names such as Mamelta and Roger, and Wynn accepted the refinement. b sharp argued that mechanical men like Jonas and Sidero count as human-descended only if their brains were scanned from human ones, and placed the Sun Cycle in the Fifth Head of Cerberus universe. Roy C. Lackey added that aliens crewed other decks of Tzadkiel's ship and cited a Citadel passage on the Hieros' manipulation of beings to make the Hierogrammates. The thread ended with a partial correction
  • James Wynn: saint names belong only to Commonwealth citizens, so aliens are mythological; Hierogrammates descend from humans because humans made them
  • mournings glory: hypernaut sleepers keep saint names like Mamelta and Roger, refining Wynn's claim
  • b sharp: mechanical men count as human-descended only if brains were scanned from humans; Sun Cycle lies in the Fifth Head of Cerberus universe
  • Roy C. Lackey: aliens crewed other decks of Tzadkiel's ship; Hieros manipulated beings to engineer Hierogrammates, cited from The Citadel of the Autarch

Themes: Robots, androids and artificial life, Beasts and the animal in us, Names and their meanings

2006-05-26 James Wynn #

Disputes the claim that humans in The Book of the New Sun have saint names and aliens mythological ones, arguing that only Commonwealth citizens bear saint names. Cites Typhon's daughter's saint name, the unnamed Whorl crew, and whether Abia, Erebus and Scylla were originally human, then asks whether any sentient beings are non-human.
disputes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Only Commonwealth citizens have saint names

b sharp says:

Coming late to this, but it is verifiably not *humans* that are named after saints. It is citizens of the Commonwealth who are. Ascians are not named after saints. Alternately, Typhon's daughter (and I presume we can extrapolate the others as well) IS named after a saint.

Later, the crew and cargo of the Whorl will not have saint names.

So were Abia, Erebus, Scylla, etc originally human or were they "aliens"? IMO Likely they were originally human. There is certainly nothing especially inhuman about Typhon in appearance -- except that one little thing, and that *is* a human body he's got, right? Until the Neighbors and the Inhumi, are there any sentient beings in the entire Sun Cycle that are positively not human or decended from humans?

Isn't there something *Wolfe himself* wrote or said either in one of the BotNS appendices, Castle of the Otter, or in an interview in which he discusses that one obvious way to extend human life indefinitely is to extend growth? I'd bet a body part that there is, although I can't remember the location right now.

J

2006-05-26 b sharp replying to James Wynn #

Argues that the mechanical men of the ship Tzadkiel, including Jonas and Sidero, count as human-descended only if their brains were made by scanning a human brain as with Mr. Million, not if programmed from scratch. Responds to James Wynn's question about sentient non-human beings in the Sun Cycle, and suggests the Sun Cycle lies in the Fifth Head of Cerberus universe.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Tzadkiel mechanical men have scanned human brains; Book of the New Sun lies in Fifth Head of Cerberus universe

James makes a good correction on the saint names being for those humans of the Commonwealth, not all humans.

James asks:

Ossipago (bonegrower) says it toward the end of Sword and that topic was discussed in more depth in some of the (mine included) Baldanders posts of this month.

James asks:

Would you consider the original Jonas (and his brothers), Sidero etc. to be examples? Someone recently asked how Talus brains were made and the same question could be asked about these mechanical men from ship Tzadkiel. If their brains were programmed from scratch then I think the answer to your question is yes.

But if their brains were created by the process which created Mr. Million, scanning a human brain, then I'd call them human descended. Since I strongly suspect BotNS takes place in the 5th Head of Cerberus universe I think mechanical brains are human in origin.

-bsharp

2006-05-26 Roy C. Lackey replying to James Wynn #

Recalls that aliens served as crewmen on other decks of Tzadkiel's ship, and names the beings the Hieros manipulated to engineer the Hierogrammates as humanity's great sin. Cites a passage in The Citadel of the Autarch titled "The key to the Universe" as support, replying to James Wynn's post.
informs · close-textual-reading

Iirc, there were aliens serving as crewmen on other decks of Tzadkiel's ship. But the most obvious groups are all those beings who were manipulated by the Hieros to engineer the Hierogrammates. Mankind's great sin. ("The key to the Universe", CITADEL)

-Roy
