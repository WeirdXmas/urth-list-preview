Urth list · 8 messages · 2008-10-10 to 2008-10-13

2008urthMatthew KeeleyNigel PriceJames WynnDon DoggettMatthew Grovesaaron
The thread: Readers of the Urth list discussed the contents of the new Best of Gene Wolfe collection, starting with Don Doggett posting its table of contents without argument. Aaron and James Wynn disagreed over whether the book offers longtime fans anything new, with Aaron saying it mainly suits newcomers and Wynn defending it as a loaner for them. Nigel Price and Matthew Groves questioned the selection's logic, Groves naming Parkroads and On the Train as odd choices and suggesting novel excerpts instead. Matthew Keeley noted On the Train was the only Wolfe story The New Yorker accepted, which explained Wolfe's affection for it. Aaron ended by preferring other stories and suggesting the selection aimed to show range as well as quality, with no clear resolution.
  • Aaron: little reason for longtime fans to buy; prefers Eyebem and The Changeling, misses A Solar Labyrinth
  • James Wynn: useful for lending to newcomers; regrets omission of Counting Cats in Zanzibar
  • Matthew Groves: selection is odd; Parkroads and On the Train unrepresentative; novel excerpts would serve better
  • Nigel Price: selection may reflect publishing practicality over quality; anthology revives back catalogue
  • Matthew Keeley: On the Train is the only New Yorker acceptance, explaining Wolfe's fondness for it
  • Don Doggett: posted the table of contents and invited comment

Themes: Books, libraries and writing

2008-10-10 Matthew Keeley replying to don doggett #

Remarks on the contents of a Wolfe story collection, noting that it draws mainly on earlier stories and omits some well-known ones like A Solar Labyrinth. Compares The Recording Hour of Trust to the work of William Maxwell or Bradbury and says the poster will buy the book.
chats

Thanks for the info Don.

I'm not familiar with all the stories in here, but it seems like he's drawing a lot from his earlier stuff - three-fourths of the Wolfe Archipelago, plus "Fifth Head" and the novellas from his first two collections. I don't see anything new in here?

"Hour of Trust" ? Didn't see that coming. And "And When They Appear" - people tell me that's about the bleakest thing Wolfe ever wrote. I think I read an Amazon review that said that one story made the reader stop enjoying Wolfe.

No "A Solar Labyrinth" ? I'm rather surprised, as it's significant (I think) and short. Still, Storeys from the Old Hotel is pretty well-represented. I like "The Recording" quite a bit - it reads a bit like Wolfe channeling fellow Illinois writer William Maxwell. Or perhaps Bradbury in a dark mood. Good story either way.

I'll buy the book.

-Matt

2008-10-11 aaron replying to Matthew Keeley #

Argues that the new Best of Gene Wolfe collection offers little reason for longtime fans who already own the earlier story collections, though it may serve newcomers. Responds to Don Doggett's table of contents by noting that he has read all the listed stories and owns the collections containing them, and wishes some hard-to-find stories had been included.
disputes

I have read all these stories and own all the collections they are in. I'm not sure if Wolfe just didn't have enough uncollected material to do a 'new' collection, or if this was the publisher's idea (most likely). I suppose this will be a good intro for those new to Wolfe, but for those of us who have all the older collections already, I see little incentive for buying this. I wish they had included a couple of stories that are now near impossible to find for us older fans, but... Oh well. Can't have everything you want, huh?

2008-10-11 Nigel Price replying to don doggett #

Wonders whether the selection of mostly early stories in a Wolfe best-of collection reflects quality judgments or publishing practicality, noting that older anthologies are harder to find. Responds to Don Doggett's contents list by saying he would argue for some other stories and that such an anthology helps revive Wolfe's back catalogue.
questions

Thank you, Don, for the list of contents. That looks like a good selection, for all that I'd argue for the inclusion of some other stories too.

The odd thing reading the list is that while some stories leap instantly to mind, I'd almost forgotten others. I wonder whether the decision to go with mostly early stories was dictated by criteria of quality or publishing commonsense? After all, the anthologies with the older stories are harder to get while the more recent anthologies are still relatively fresh on the shelves. Among other things, an anthology like this is a handy way of reviving the back catalogue.

Nigel

2008-10-11 James Wynn replying to aaron #

Argues that a best-of Wolfe collection is useful for lending to newcomers so they meet his strongest work without the more difficult or middling pieces, and expresses frustration that this collection omits "Counting Cats in Zanzibar." Replies directly to Aaron, who said the collection offers little reason to buy for readers who already own the older story collections.
supports

The purpose of this sort of collection to me is to loan out to friends who are unfamiliar with Wolfe. This way, they can experience the best of Wolfe without getting detracted by some of the more difficult or middling works.

Personally, I'm frustrated by a "best of" collection that does not include "Counting Cats in Zanzibar".

J

2008-10-13 aaron replying to Matthew Keeley #

Argues that the selection in Best of Gene Wolfe is odd, preferring Eyebem and The Changeling over On the Train and Parkroads, while missing A Solar Labyrinth and Silhouette. Responds to Matthew Keeley's remark that Wolfe's soft spot for On the Train comes from its New Yorker acceptance, and suggests the stories were chosen to show Wolfe's range as well as his quality.
disputes

I would rather have seen Eyebem and The Changeling than On the Train and Parkroads, but that's just my taste. Don't get me wrong, I can see the appeal of both stories, but just don't feel they are among Wolfe's best. It could be as you say that Wolfe considers On the Train to be good because it appeared in the New Yorker. I also miss A Solar Labyrinth and Silhouette. Oh well. I suppose the stories were not only chosen because they are his best, but because they show Wolfe's range and diversity.

-Aaron
