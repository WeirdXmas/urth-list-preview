Seeing the signs.....

Urth list · 3 messages · 2010-12-16 to 2010-12-16

2010urthThe Book of the New SunJeff WilsonLee Berman
The thread: Lee Berman and Jeff Wilson argued over a list of symbols in Wolfe's work, with Berman asking Wilson for textual support. Berman pressed for including the jurupari hex sign and argued that Gurloes' iron phallus was wrongly listed, since Severian calls it a plain tool. He also read the Prophet's goatskin as a pagan Pan or Faunus reference, against a Judeo-Christian reading. Wilson countered that the pagan-seeming sign could just as well fit Moses, citing the bronze serpent on a pole as a sympathetic-magic-like ward against death. The thread ended without resolution, with the disagreement left standing between the pagan and biblical readings.
  • Lee Berman: list should include the jurupari hex sign and drop Gurloes' iron phallus, since Severian calls it a plain tool
  • Lee Berman: the Prophet's goatskin links to Inire, Faunus, Inuus, Dionysus and Pan
  • Jeff Wilson: the sign treated as pagan is not particularly pagan; Moses and the bronze serpent fit as well
  • Jeff Wilson: asks for textual grounding of his symbol list and defends his contributions

Themes: Myth and folklore sources, Names and their meanings, Christ figures and messiahs

2010-12-16 Lee Berman #

Asks Jeff Wilson for text quotes supporting his list of symbols and argues the jurupari hex sign should be included. Questions the inclusion of Gurloes' iron phallus, and proposes that the Prophet's goatskin links to Inire, Faunus and Inuus and to pagan Dionysus and Pan in the Sun books.
disputes · source-and-allusion · theology-and-religion · theories: Prophet's goatskin alludes to Inire-Faunus-Inuus; Dionysus and Pan figure in the Sun series

Ryan does say: "If you have more to add, by all means share." I'd be interested in more detail or text quotes for your contributions, Jeff.

The jurupari hex sign is wreathed in letters so I think it needs inclusion on the list. I was most confused by the inclusion of Gurloes' iron phallus. Severian thinks it is a symbol but then realizes it is not. It is just a plain old tool (argh! sorry).

Given my current exploration of the role of Dionysus and Great God Pan in the Sun series I was less interested in the PROPHET's crudely carved staff and more interested in the goatskin he was wearing (heh). Another Inire-Faunus-Inuus reference? Awfully pagan at any rate, which might be a bit odd from a straight Judeo-Christian interpretation for a prophet in a play called Eschatology and Genesis .

Still, at least two possible Inire incarnations do carry a staff, one topped with a monkey-head another with a man-head.

2010-12-16 Jeff Wilson replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that a list item about a sign or symbol, which the quoted reply treats as pagan, is not particularly pagan, since Moses leading the tribes through the desert could look much the same. Grounds this in Moses' bronze serpent effigy on a pole, a sympathetic-magic-like act that wards off death by snake venom, and responds to Lee Berman's request for detail on Jeff Wilson's contributions to a list of signs.
disputes · allegory-and-typology · source-and-allusion · theories: Jurupari-style sign is not particularly pagan

This doesn't strike me as particularly pagan - Moses leading the tribes through the desert could have looked much the same, and he did sympathetic magic like nailing an effigy snake to a pole to ward off death by snake venom.
