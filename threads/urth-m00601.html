TWK: Able's future

Urth list · 5 messages · 2005-12-06 to 2005-12-10

2005urthRoy C. LackeyAndy RobertsonDon DoggettJames Wynn
The thread: Readers debated whether Able can rise to Kleos, the perfect realm of God's servants, as Michael's words suggest. Roy C. Lackey argued no, placing Able's fate on another fourth-level world and reading his leaving Skai as an echo of the angels' fall, citing the Ymir passages. James Wynn countered that the novel's upward-progress theme and Michael's wish for a new Able point beyond Kleos. Don Doggett explained Michael's free movement through Christian doctrine on angels and humans as God's favoured children, which Andy Robertson disputed, arguing the Neighbours are special too and that the descended angels were bound to Skai by Ymir's blood. The thread ended with the positions still apart, with no settled reading.
  • Roy C. Lackey: Able cannot reach Kleos; the great lord lives on another fourth-level world, perhaps ours
  • James Wynn: Able's rise points beyond Kleos, given the upward-progress theme and Michael's search for a new Able
  • Don Doggett: Michael moves freely as God's messenger, and humans are special as God's favoured children
  • Andy Robertson: Humans are not uniquely special since the Neighbours are too; angels were bound to Skai by Ymir's blood

Themes: Gods, angels and the Increate, The dying sun and the cosmos, Gnosticism, heresy and other theologies

2005-12-06 Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that Able cannot be promoted to Kleos as Michael's words suggest, and proposes that the 'great lord' needing a champion lives on another fourth-level world, perhaps ours. Replies to an earlier post about Able's future, citing Able's letter from Aelfrice, the other worlds on intervening levels, and the Gawain-Norse mix as evidence.
disputes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Great lord needing a champion is on another fourth-level world; Able will not reach Kleos; Able's Celtic and Norse mixing reflects cross-world contamination

[snip]
will be given a position in a still higher realm than Skai;<< [snip]

That can't be right. There's no place up for him to go but Kleos. Able's association with Michael is not enough to get him to Kleos. Able wrote from Aelfrice, where he saw the events in Mythgarthr play out across the sky. He mentioned a future time when he will return to Mythgarthr. The "great lord" mentioned by Michael as being in need of a champion is not God, who has no need of one, least of all in Kleos. We are told that there are other worlds on all the levels except the first and last. It may be that the "great lord" in need of a champion is on another world on the fourth level, a world other than Dream or Mythgarthr. Maybe our own. It may even be that the "great lord" is on a world on the third level other than the Valfather's. That might be the explanation for the apparent mixing of unrelated Celtic (Gawain) and Norse traditions that Able seems to have experienced, a cross-contamination of worlds in the same way that he went from America to Mythgarthr.

-Roy

Grounds this in Able's quick prominence in Skai and his having met few Kleos people, answering Roy C. Lackey's claim that Able's link to Michael cannot lift him to Kleos.

2005-12-07 Roy C. Lackey replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that Able cannot rise to Kleos or be a hero there, since the novel's cosmology places Kleos as a perfect realm of God's servants and Skai as the fallen, imperfect place where Able belongs. Grounds this in quoted passages about servants who descended to kill Ymir and could never return, and reads Able's leaving Skai as an echo of that fall.
disputes · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: Able cannot rise to Kleos; Able's leaving Skai echoes fallen servants

It would surprise the hell out of me for Able to even get to Kleos, much less be a hero there. As hartshorn said in another context, Able is a knight not a saint. Just who would a crude dude like Able fight in God's backyard? Kleos was a place made by God as a reward for the "servants" he had created. (W, 46) (I think those "servants" are what are usually called angels.)

Because anything apart from God is by definition less than perfect, those servants were unhappy with that imperfection in Kleos. They wanted to cast out as much of that imperfection as they could. God created a place for them to put it, which was Skai. The imperfection was embodied in the form of the giant Ymir. "Some servants of the Highest God surrendered their places in Kleos and went down to kill him. They did, but they could never go back." (ibid.) Some hold that the Valfather was one of those who descended and could never return. (48) (Echoed by Able leaving Skai?) The farther removed from God's perfection, the worse the corruption, descending by degrees to evil.

Able was no angel, and I don't think he ever would/could be. I don't think it's even theoretically possible for a post-creation being to attain the status of a pre-creation being. But this begins to stray into religious territory it's probably better not to traverse.

I don't think Able was all that prominent in Skai; clearly he was nowhere near as powerful as the Valfather and his immediate family, and/or the other original "servants" of God.

Michael didn't meet Able in Kleos, so clearly he had come down from his high place. Presumably he was able to return, unlike his brother servants/angels who went down to Skai. What's the difference?

-Roy

2005-12-09 Don Doggett replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that Michael can move freely between realms because angels are God's messengers, while Able is special as a human, which is the Christian view of humanity as God's favoured children. Responds to Roy C. Lackey's question about why Michael could return from his high place when other angels who went down to Skai could not, offering an explanation grounded in Christian belief and the story's setup.
answers · theology-and-religion

Angels are by definition messengers of God, so it only
makes sense that Michael can travel wherever he needs
to do his duties. Able is special because he is human.
According to Christian belief, we are God's special
children and this whole setup is for our benefit,
though we are unworthy of it. At least that's my
understanding. I'm not sure I buy that in the "real
world" but for the purpose of story that's the
situation. I think.

Don

2005-12-10 Andy Robertson replying to don doggett #

Disputes the Christian idea that humans are God's special children in Wolfe's fictional universes, arguing that the Neighbours are special too and that everyone is. Suggests the descended angels were bound to Skai by the slaying of Ymir and the shedding of Ymir's blood, a Norse-derived idea about The Wizard Knight's setting.
disputes · theology-and-religion · source-and-allusion · theories: Descended angels bound to Skai by Ymir's blood

I don't believe this is the case in any of Wolfe's fictional universes. Humans may be special, but so are the Neighbours. Everyone is special.

I suspect the thing that bound the descended angels to Skai was probably the slaying of Ymir and the shedding of Ymir's blood.
