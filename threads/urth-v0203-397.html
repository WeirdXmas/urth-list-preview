2002-03-20 Marc Aramini #

Defends a reading of the frog-like polyploid creatures in Horn's Long Sun material as regenerative rather than polyploidy-driven, and suggests the liana vines might cause extra limb growth. Also argues that 'brush' in the Long Sun text means brushwood, citing the Merriam-Webster first definition and a leaf-brushwood-tree analogy, and concludes the disagreement with Jerry over resurrection versus new life is largely semantic.
supports · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion · theories: Liana vines stimulate extra limb growth in creatures

Someone asked why I bothered with the trematode/frog thing. Jerry previously commented that he hadn't seen anything that indicated these polyploid froggies X. Leavis might have multiple limbs... but that article indicates that this is a common mutation they experience. Obviously it has nothing (or little) to do with their polyploidy and more to do with their regenerative powers. If I wanted to be a real jackass I could say that the liana's are like Trematodes to the trees, stimulating extra limb growth with their cysty presence (remember that the pit Horn fell into was covered with vines). However, I will do no such thing and just leave it as meaning nothing because that is pushing the realms of credibility and rationality far beyond anything I am willing to do. (Of course, that was a nice little rhetorical occupatio right there, wasn't it?)

Incidentally, the first definition for "Brush" at merriam webster online is "Brushwood". I am here going to imply that we use a test to see which of the following relationships hold: leaf: brushwood: tree or leaf: scrubbing brush: tree
Can't brushwood be rough and brown if it's dead?
I shouldn't beat that to death. I'll leave it alone. I'm just saying that simple words often have more than one definition ... and in this case the context just seems to insinuate foliage of somekind. If you still want to argue about the word, then I will copy all of the dictionary definitions over. We are both saying the same thing about new life to decayed things, I'm just saying the new sun is granting a different kind of life rather than a resurrection. I guess there is no sense discussing it further - we are "kind of" saying the same thing.
Marc

2002-03-21 Jerry Friedman #

Argues that the extra limbs of the Neighbors, hus and felwolves in the Urth books stem from developmental interference rather than polyploidy, and notes plant-animal ambiguities such as the Claw that is a thorn and the names Chenille and Marrow. Responds to maa32's rationale by citing a biological article and speculating that Chenille may have XY chromosomes with testicular feminization syndrome.
disputes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion · theories: Chenille has XY chromosomes and testicular feminization syndrome; Extra limbs come from developmental interference not polyploidy

Or with their process of development when it's interfered with. The article was interesting but I doubt very much that it has anything to do with the Neighbors' or the hus's or felwolves' extra limbs. ...

And the brush is at least partly made of plant material (wood).

A couple plant-animal ambiguities that I haven't seen mentioned yet: the Claw that's a thorn, and the names Chenille and Marrow (the the ambiguity of both names is explicitly pointed out, but not interpreted by the Vironese characters as sexual ambiguity--I'll admit to wondering, though, whether Chenille has XY chromosomes and testicular feminization syndrome, which is consistent with her being tall and never having children). Wolfe seems to like this plant- animal theme--I'm almost tempted to call it a trope.

Jerry Friedman
