Vodalus & the Old Autarch

Urth list · 17 messages · 2010-08-10 to 2010-08-11

2010urthThe Book of the New SunDave GottliebRyan DunnthalassocratLee BermanGerry QuinnDavid StockhoffJeff Wilson
The thread: Readers asked why Vodalus never recognizes his secret agent as the old Autarch, despite the Autarch's face on coinage and Thecla's recognition. Lee Berman and Gerry Quinn argued Vodalus never met the agent in person, while Jeff Wilson proposed a Machiavellian ambush scenario and later argued the coinage was imprecise and Thecla was misled by class attitudes and a mask. David Stockhoff insisted Thecla would have recognized the face, and Mr Thalassocrat read the lapse as a narrative glitch. The thread drifted into speculation about emasculation, succession, time reversal, and whether the Autarch conceals himself with power words, without reaching agreement.
  • Jeff Wilson: Vodalus may not recognize the agent because meeting and flyer identified him; the coinage is imprecise and Thecla's class attitudes and mask blinde
  • Lee Berman: Vodalus never met his accomplice, so the face on the coin goes unrecognized
  • David Stockhoff: Thecla would surely recognize the coin face, and the Autarch may have funded Vodalus with counterfeit coins
  • Mr Thalassocrat: Vodalus should have recognized him; the lapse is a narrative glitch with thin in-text explanations
  • Gerry Quinn: Vodalus never met the agent, the only real possibility; emasculation may act backwards in time
  • Dave Gottlieb: The Autarch may hide his identity by hypnosis or power words, choosing who recognizes him

Themes: Unreliable narration, Hidden identities and parentage, Masks, disguise and shapeshifting

2010-08-10 Jeff Wilson replying to Mr Thalassocrat #

Argues that Vodalus could plausibly fail to recognize his agent as the old Autarch, since spies often never meet their handlers and the Autarch's trust-building would require sacrificing a subordinate. Answers Mr Thalassocrat's question by offering a Machiavellian scenario in which the Autarch sends a compromised heptarch to an ambush, then leaks details through "the agent" to win Vodalus's confidence.
proposes · puzzle-solving · theories: Autarch sacrificed a heptarch to win Vodalus's trust; Vodalus's agent was insulated by cutouts and never met directly

In both real and fictional espionage, it's not unusual for turncoats to remain unmet by the other side's leader, especially if he is not his own spymaster. Even if they meet someone, that person may be a cutout for that purpose only, with the messages originating from another source. Vodalus is doubltess aware of this, so his trust would have to be gained by releasing truly secret but verifiable information, and information leading to the loss of something with a perceived value too high to be considered a willing sacrifice.

The Autarch/Inire at some point had to screw someone over by betraying them to Vodalus. This may have involved the Machiavellian form of sending a problematic subordinate (like a heptarch who has been discovered to have a ruinous secret subjecting him to blackmail but has too many family connections to dismiss) to the front, collecting favors from the family connections to redirect them elsewhere, then using "the agent" to leak the selected details. The subordinate's column is ambushed, the rebels get some plunder, "the agent" is vindicated, the subordinate is either killed or cashiered for being unprepared, and the prince has a stronger position in his own country.

2010-08-10 Lee Berman replying to Mr Thalassocrat #

Proposes that Vodalus never met his secret accomplice in House Absolute, so he fails to recognise the old Autarch's face on the false coin. Answers Mr Thalassocrat's question about the glitch by citing Vodalus' claim that the accomplice has never lied, which rests only on microfiche-style communication, not personal presence.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Vodalus never met his accomplice in person

I've wondered the same thing, especially regarding the coin and most especially because it is a false coin. I don't know if Vodalus himself is the counterfeiter but whichever of his associates made the coin had to be very aware of the image he was engraving.

My text-based explanation is that Vodalus has never actually met his secret accomplice in House Absolute. All their communication has been by those micro-fiche thingies (was it called a stele?) and Vodalus' assertion that his accomplice has never lied is based only on that or other forms of communication which do not require personal presence.

2010-08-10 Jeff Wilson replying to David Stockhoff #

Argues that the coins Severian describes as struck from the same die suggest imprecise minting, so Autarch coinage may not closely resemble the real ruler, and that Thecla failed to recognize Inire because of her class attitudes and the shaman's mask. Responds to David Stockhoff's remark that Thecla would have recognized the Autarch's face on the coin, using the comparison with Caesar's coins and statuary.
disputes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion · theories: Thecla did not recognize Inire as the Autarch

Severian sees that Vodalus' and Dr Talos' counterfeit coins are struck from the same die, implying that he's seen variations among other coins and that dies are not accurately reproducible, implying fairly crude minting technology. So the Autarchs' coinage may be distinguishable from one another, but might resemble the actual person about as much as Caesar's surviving coinage resembles his surviving statuary.

Inire's face was covered by the shaman's mask, and Thecla was probably the sort of aristocrat who took little notice of the lower classes and the filthy natives.

2010-08-10 David Stockhoff replying to Gerry Quinn #

Argues that the Autarch's succession was threatened by a rival with charismatic sons, so a failed Epitome would simply be smothered as an infant rather than emasculated. Responds to Gerry Quinn's point about whether the Autarch's visible emasculation fits the succession mechanism, and to Quinn's idea that emasculation might act backwards in time.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · puzzle-solving · theories: emasculation makes Autarch's failure visible to succession

Keeping in mind that the true succession is a mystery to most people, it would have been easily threatened by a guy with effective, charismatic sons and who would have reason to rebel against it, having been rejected by the Hieros.

However, I can't think of a reason they couldn't go back to do whatever they want. Of course, in that case, you would think that simply smothering a failed Epitome in his crib would be the way to go.

From: "Jeff Wilson" <[email]>

If you take this plot element seriously (the failed Autarch being emasculated lest he father a dynasty) it might be that the changes *had* to be obvious.? Clearly hormone technologies are available such that the Autarch could have maintained at least the appearance of masculinity (*much* more than that was done to Jolenta, and the Autarch surely has technologies available to him comparable to those of Baldanders and Talos, especially considering that Talos modified Jolenta using only the equipment he was travelling with).? And the freezing of sperm is barely more hi-tech than the ancillary turkey baster technology.? Furthermore, the hierodules could presumably have sterilised him without undesired (or should I say undesiring) side effects.

So perhaps the Autarch had to be visibly emasculated in order to make it clear that he was so; that nobody could validly claim the throne on the basis of being his issue.? On the other hand, this fits very poorly with the means by which the throne is inherited!

Could it be that the emasculation acts backwards in time? That is, if Severian failed, he would have been *born* an androgyne in a new iteration? Thinking about it, none of the other options make a whole lot of sense.

- Gerry Quinn

Clearly hormone technologies are available such that the Autarch could have maintained at least the appearance of masculinity (*much* more than that was done to Jolenta, and the Autarch surely has technologies available to him comparable to those of Baldanders and Talos, especially considering that Talos modified Jolenta using only the equipment he was travelling with). And the freezing of sperm is barely more hi-tech than the ancillary turkey baster technology. Furthermore, the hierodules could presumably have sterilised him without undesired (or should I say undesiring) side effects.

So perhaps the Autarch had to be visibly emasculated in order to make it clear that he was so; that nobody could validly claim the throne on the basis of being his issue. On the other hand, this fits very poorly with the means by which the throne is inherited!

Could it be that the emasculation acts backwards in time? That is, if Severian failed, he would have been *born* an androgyne in a new iteration? Thinking about it, none of the other options make a whole lot of sense.

Argues that Vodalus should have recognized the old Autarch when they met, since he was eager to learn the Autarch's appearance from Severian and seems to have had no doubts about his agent. disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic

I guess Vodalus may never before have met the old Autarch, but he certainly doesn't seem to have any trouble accepting him as his agent when they do meet, after Agia et al pluck Sev and the old Autarch from the Ascians. I suppose Vodalus might have regarded a password or something, as sufficient to establish bona fides.

But note how eager Vodalus is to get a description of the Autarch from Sev (which Sev makes up, based on the appearance of Talos in the Autarch role in his play). Don't you have to assume that he must have been similarly anxious to get descriptions from others who had met the old Autarch in the past? Eg, from Thecla, if not from Thea herself (who has certainly spent time at court during the old Autarch's reign).

The message is enough to make Vodalus suspect Sev of being the Autarch, but he appears to have no such thoughts about his agent.
