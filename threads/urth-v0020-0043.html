Agia: Thief?

Urth list · 3 messages · 1998-10-16 to 1998-10-16

1998urthThe Book of the New SunRobert BorskiMark MillmanAlex David Groce
The thread: Readers debated whether Agia stole the Claw in The Book of the New Sun and what Barbatus's question 'Where did you get it?' requires for passing Famulimus's test. Robert Borski argued the Agia-as-thief theory is hard to accept and that the right answer to the question is the key to passing the test. Alex David Groce doubted Agia took the Claw, suggesting it may have taken itself and that Severian misattributes it, based on rereading the theft scene. Mark Millman argued the question is a test rather than a literal query, so no physical answer like Agia planting the Claw could pass, and cited the Pelerines' search of Agia as evidence. The thread ended with three posts and no clear agreement.
  • Robert Borski: Agia-as-thief is hard to accept; passing the test depends on answering Barbatus's question correctly
  • Alex David Groce: Agia probably did not steal the Claw; it may have taken itself and Severian misattributes it
  • Mark Millman: The question is a test, not a literal query; Pelerines searching Agia while ignoring Severian undercuts the thief reading

Themes: Unreliable narration, Memory and forgetting, Mirrors, pictures and art

1998-10-16 Robert Borski #

Argues that the Agia-as-thief theory is hard to accept and proposes that passing Famulimus's test in SWORD depends on correctly answering Barbatus's question 'Where did you get it?' Cites Barbatus's repeated demand, Baldander's unsatisfying answer, and Famulimus's remark to Sev, responding to raster's suggestion that Agia is after the keys rather than the Claw.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Agia is the thief of the Claw; Passing the test requires answering 'Where did you get it?'; Agia is after the door keys

raster having written:

"But it occurs to me that your theory can still be salvaged. If Agia isn't trying to filch the Claw, then she must be after the keys. We know S. has the door keys; where else would he carry them but his sabretache? Agia doubtless assumes that he has the key to Agilus's chains as well."

Actually, I like this notion rather well. And I don't think the coins theory can be totally discounted either, since Agia takes the orichalk Sev tosses her.

Re Agia as the Claw's thief. I've already stated I find this difficult to accept, and wonder what mantis or other proponents of the Agia-as-thief theory make of the following scene in SWORD. Sev wants the Claw back from the cacogens, and Barbatus asks, "If it is yours, where did you get it?" Sev lays his Agia theory on him, but Barbatus seems almost incensed by this. "All this is speculation. You did not see this jewel upon the altar, nor did you feel the woman's hand when she gave it to you, if in fact she did. _Where did you get it?_" (p. 271, and these are Wolfe's italics, not mine.)

Baldander's answer to the same question--'I got it from the drawer in a table'--also does not seem to satisfy Barbatus, and not much later Famulimus says to Sev, as they're walking to their timeship, "Though you did not now pass our test..."

It seems to me that passing "the test" somehow involves correctly answering the question, "Where did you get it?" What think you?

Re: EMPIRES OF FOLIAGE AND FLOWERS. I have a copy of the non-deluxe edition I'll let go for $175.00. It is a nice book, but perhaps too much so--I'm afraid to handle it, fearing fingerprints or drool may soil it.

Robert Borski

1998-10-16 Alex David Groce #

Doubts that Agia stole the Claw, suggesting instead that the Claw may have taken itself and that Severian misattributes his possession of it. Grounds this on rereading the theft scene and Agia's groping in the cell, noting that the reader is initially led to agree with Severian's belief that she took it.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: The Claw stole itself from Severian

Hmm... Rereading that and the section of the theft, I'm now doubtful that Agia did steal the Claw--I'm not sure what she was groping for in the cell, but I think there's a strong suggestion that the Claw may have "stolen itself" or something of the kind. Severian's failure may, in fact, be that he has failed to attribute his possession of the Claw to the proper agency. I think it's clear that Severian thinks Agia took it, and that at first the reader is meant to concur; but I'm not so sure it's actually the case now.

1998-10-16 Mark Millman #

Argues that Famulimus and Barbatus's question 'Where did you get it?' is a test rather than a literal query, so no physical answer such as Agia planting the Claw could pass. Responds to Robert Borski's objection to the Agia-as-thief theory by citing the Pelerines searching Agia while ignoring Severian and the chief Pelerine's ability to see truth.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Agia is the Claw's thief; Correct answer to the Claw question is 'It's a part of me'

On 16 October 1998 at 5:00 PM GMT,

I'm not convinced that the literal question "Where did
you get it?" is really at issue for Famulimus and Bar-
batus here. I think it's the only way that they can ask the real question without giving away the answer; if
Severian knows the answer, then the phrasing won't
throw him off, but if he doesn't have a clue (as he, in
fact, proves he hasn't), then he'll try to answer it as
it stands. Any physical answer--"Agia planted it",
"it was in the drawer", even "well, it just appeared
there one day"--is going to be incorrect. The correct
answer is something like, "It's a part of me." But as
Severian doesn't yet recognize this, he can't pass
the test.

Remember also that the Pelerines search Agia be-
cause she looks guilty to them, and don't bother
with Severian because he appears plainly to be an
innocent. There's more to it than just appearances,
too; if I recall correctly, mention is made of the chief Pelerine's (is she a Mother Superior?) ability to see
the truth regardless of circumstance.

Drool, Robert? If you love the book that much, I'm
surprised that you're willing to let it go at all.

Mark Millman
