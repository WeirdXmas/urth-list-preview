Inhuma continued

Urth list · 7 messages · 2009-01-26 to 2009-01-27

2009urthThe Book of the Short SunO'Donnell, Tim (BOSI)Steve McCannCraig BrewerJames WynnMatthew GrovesJeff Wilson
The thread: Readers debated whether the inhumi can really cross space to reach Blue in Wolfe's Long Sun books. Craig Brewer argued the claim is a lie or myth, citing Fava's death in the snow and Jahlee's remark about thin air, and pointed to the inhumi flying a lander at the end of On Blue's Waters. Jeff Wilson countered with thermal reasoning that a void crossing could be survivable. James Wynn argued the inhumi story about Blue is true because attacks would need landers or Dream-Travel. Tim O'Donnell asked about inhuma breeding and lifespan, and Steve McCann briefly endorsed space-traveling vampires as science fantasy. The thread ended without resolution.
  • Craig Brewer: inhumi space travel is a lie or myth, narrators keep it at arm's length
  • Jeff Wilson: inhumas could survive the void, sunward heat and shaded warmth from blood circulation
  • James Wynn: the inhumi story about Blue is true, attacks need landers or Dream-Travel
  • Tim O'Donnell: asks how long inhuma take to breed and mature and whether they were bred on the Whorl
  • Steve McCann: agrees vampires crossing space is appealing, since it is science fantasy

Themes: Myth and folklore sources, Beasts and the animal in us, The dying sun and the cosmos

2009-01-26 Craig Brewer replying to O'Donnell, Tim (BOSI) #

Argues that the Inhumi's claimed ability to cross space in the Long Sun books is a lie or myth, citing Fava's death in the snow during astral travel and Jahlee's remark about thin air in the void. Supports this by noting that the inhumi flying a lander at the end of On Blue's Waters suggests they could cross space on their own, and that the narrators keep the idea at arm's length.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: Inhumi space travel is a myth; Inhumi acquired other landers and crossed by conjunction

I've always thought it was a lie. Fava dies in the snow when she and Horn do the astral travel bit. And when Jahlee is talking about learning to cross the void, she complains about how thin the air is...when, of course, there won't be any air, thin or thick, in the void. They're always complaining about the cold, but space would be just a touch colder than Blue, I'd imagine. And being reptilian and, I assume, cold-blooded...absolute zero would present some problems, no?

Plus, I'd always gotten the impression that the end of OBW with the inhumi flying the lander pretty much put the lie to that idea. My guess was that the inhumi had gotten hold of other landers as well and "conjunction" just made it much easier for them to cross. Those are assumptions, of course. But the very fact that their ability to cross space always seemed held at arm's length by the narrators made me think that, at the least, we were supposed to suspect that it was a myth.

2009-01-26 O'Donnell, Tim (BOSI) #

Asks how long inhuma take to breed and mature, wanting to know how old Jahlee could be, and whether inhuma were brought to the Whorl by the Neighbours and bred there or returned to Green. Also asks whether inhuma can really survive flight through the void. Opens by asking whether Wolfe planned The Book of the Short Sun when writing The Book of the Long Sun.
questions · chronology-and-plot-logic

I am coming late to Wolfe so I am not sure how the books were written. I was under the impression that BOTLS formed a singular entity and that BOTSS was a later book written in the same universe. Essentially that Wolfe did not have BOTSS planned when he wrote BOTLS. This is why there is no hint of Neighbours in BOTLS (unless...Patera Pike's ghost?). But I stand to be corrected here, as I say I am not sure of the planning behind all this.

Returning to my questions though (and I think I can frame this differently): how long does it take the inhuma to breed/mature? I was trying to figure out how long it would take Jahlee to mature but there does not seem to be enough evidence in BOTSS to put much of a timeline on it. So, inhuma got on board the Whorl through actions of the Neighbours, possibly through landers/probes also. Did they breed on the Whorl or did they return to Green before being transported to the Whorl again. What of the inhumas' ability to fly through the void? Is this a lie or can they survive in space?

2009-01-26 Jeff Wilson replying to Craig Brewer #

Argues that inhumas could survive crossing the void in space, reasoning from thermal conditions: the sunward side of a body would be hot, while the shaded side could be kept warm by blood circulation and the lack of air would insulate. Responds to Craig Brewer's claim that the inhumas' void travel is a lie, which he based on Fava dying in the snow and Jahlee complaining of thin air.
disputes · genre-and-craft · theories: Inhumas can survive the vacuum of space

In near-planetary space without the protection of an atmosphere, the side of a body toward the sun will be hotter than the daytime side of the planet. It might be possible for a thick-skinned reptile to withstand the vacuum, and to keep the shaded side of the body warm by blood circulation. The shadow side wouldn't loose heat that much faster than on a planetside night, and the lack of air would actually help insulate.

2009-01-26 James Wynn replying to Craig Brewer #

Argues that the inhumi story about Blue is true despite characters lying, because inhumi attacks would need landers or Dream-Travel to reach Blue. Supports this with the void-journey risk Jahlee describes, the Neighbors' earlier help, and a speculation that inhumi cannot see the stars.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: Inhumi attacks on Blue are true, not a lie; Inhumi arrive by lander and Dream-Travel via Neighbors

Although I think Wolfe *does* have characters lie and never get called on it, I think the story is true. There's simply no other way IMO that Blue could be inundated with inhumi attacks if they had to depend on landers for all of them to get to Blue. I think the lander is how the inhumus (male inhumi) arrive. Jahlee (IIRC) says the void of space journey is pretty risky and many or most don't make it.

There is *some* atmosphere well out in space and where the atmospheres of the planets intersect I guess there's twice as much.

I think the purpose of the lander was to get new humans to Green. But of course the inhumu would require them as well.

But, anyway, they got to the Whorl originally with the help of the the Neighbors. The most dependable way would be for a Neighbor to have grabbed an inhumi and Dream-Traveled there, and then traveled via lander to Green and back.

Hmmm...on the other hand, the inhumi can't see the light of the stars. Can they see Green and Blue in the sky??

J.

P.S.
I suspect the maturation process of inhumi is pretty short for various reasons, but I haven't used any math to test my opinion.
