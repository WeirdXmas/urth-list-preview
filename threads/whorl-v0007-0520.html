1997-07-13 Dave Lebling #

Thanks Gene Wolfe for answering the reader question trains about Mint and Marble, and hopes Short Sun will get the time instead. Adds a speculative remark that Rose has been cannibalizing Marble's parts for decades, responding to Patrick, and notes Marble reclaims rather than takes Rose's parts.
supports · close-textual-reading · theories: Rose has been cannibalizing Marble's parts for decades

I'd like to thank Gene for his forbearance and kindness in answering our question trains. If this is the last one, so be it -- few authors would be nice enough to do this at all, much less with such grace.

Far better the time should be spent on _Short Sun_!

As for Mint/Marble, he should realize that he makes so few mistakes that when we see one (even if it's nearly obviously a mistake), we have to think about it for a while!

All in all, a fine set of answers.

I wonder if as part of the liturgy (ceremony? whatever) of becoming a nun in today's church, one specifically renounces the things Gene listed?

To Patrick:

I think Rose has been cannibalizing Marble's parts for decades. I sent a suggestion to that effect a week ago which seems to have made the "each message" list subscription but not the "digest" version. When Marble cannibalizes Rose's body for parts, she doesn't "take" them, she "reclaims" them, for example.

-- vizcacha
([email])

1997-07-14 Michael Straight #

Explains that vows differ by religious order, noting that some orders take vows of poverty, chastity and obedience. Responds to a question about whether becoming a nun involves renouncing the things Wolfe listed, offering general information about monastic vows without citing any passage of the books.
answers · theology-and-religion

It depends on what order the nun is taking vows in. I know at least some orders take vows of "poverty, chastity, and obedience."

-Rostrum
