The thread: Marc Aramini's ongoing project of write-ups on Wolfe's short fiction prompted mostly encouragement and publishing advice on the list. Posters debated whether the roughly 2,000-page work should be split into volumes, include the novels, or be pitched to university presses, print-on-demand, or Tor and McSweeney's. Several sought bibliographic help, such as Nessun Saprà correcting the story list and identifying Kid Sister and A visit from his confidant. Aramini discussed Kafka and Borgesian metafiction complicating interpretation, but the thread largely ended in praise and well-wishing rather than a substantive debate about the stories.
  • Marc Aramini: short story project should include the novels in a holistic work and be split into volumes
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: pitch the short fiction to university presses without the novels
  • Mo Holkar: publish print-on-demand as well as ebook
  • entonio: split the volume by type of work and avoid a cheap giveaway
  • Nessun Saprà: corrected and extended the compiled story list with bibliographic detail
  • Antonin Scriabin: suggested Tor or McSweeney's as publishers

2014-07-31 Marc Aramini #

Reports on Marc Aramini's progress through Wolfe's short fiction, naming which stories he finds least and most satisfying, and asks whether his roughly 2,000-page project could be published as an ebook or split in two. Grounds the post in his own account of the stories' analysis write-ups, such as The Changeling and Many Mansions, and his observation that Seven American Nights marks a shift toward fantasy.
questions

Well, I am about 33 percent done with Wolfe's published stories after this next one, "The Doctor of Death Island" ... I plan on skipping two numbers and returning to "The Eyeflash Miracles" and "Seven American Nights" after I consider them a bit more.

I would really like to tear through the 80s more quickly than the 70s, and it should be possible since Wolfe's time on long novels and series vastly reduced the length and allusive depth of his short fiction. ... However, I feel that 7 American Nights and probably Cabin on the Coast in the early 80s marked a shift away from SF tropes to more fantastic ones, which are a bit less easy to mark "objectively" - some stories might actually have unresolvable mysteries, though I don't think anything written before 1980 actually does.

The write-ups I feel are least satisfactory are "The Green Wall Said", "The Blue Mouse", "Paul's Treehouse" - though I think the garment district uprising in "A Criminal Proceeding" and mentioned briefly in "Operation ARES" can explain who is actually rioting in that story, "Hour of Trust" (what is the name of the company running everything, whose symbol is gold stamped instruments? That write up gave me more problems than you would think) "The Island of Doctor Death an Other Stories", and perhaps the Thag stories.

I am most satisfied with "The Changeling", "Many Mansions" - (those houses are luring people in and are merely dressed up as familiar, tempting human houses with mimicry, and might even be planting something, human-alien symbiotic style, inside people through the strong drink), "The HORARS of War", and "Trip, Trap" write ups.

I already know exactly what type of essays I will be finishing up on Long Sun, Latro, and Home Fires, but the rest will take some time and I might not return to New Sun until the majority of the short fiction and shorter novels are done.

All considered the project might be close to 2,000 pages or so when I am done.
Of course it would need hefty revision and citation, but lately I have considered perhaps trying to publish it upon completion - short essays on the long fiction in front which would be my own thesis based developments, not comprehensive looks at all, and the story analysis section after that. A traditional publisher would never touch it, I imagine, and it would be far too big for a small or university press, but perhaps an ebook would work. Anyone think that would be viable? Should I even, say, try to split it in half? Just thought I would see what you guys thought, as this has been a WHOLE lot of work, even though I love it.
