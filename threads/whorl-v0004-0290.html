1997whorlThe Book of the Long SunAlice Turner

Themes: Christ figures and messiahs, Myth and folklore sources

1997-04-03 Alice Turner #

Argues that the lame foot in Wolfe's work belongs to a long mythic tradition of the wounded foot or thigh, linking Severian and Silk to the Fisher King and one-sandaled Jason as symbols of a barren land. Grounds this in Severian's restored leg at the story's end and Silk's lameness standing for the dysfunctional Whorl, and notes Wolfe's childhood polio, then asks whether Silk will be abandoned for a new generation.
proposes · myth-and-structure · allegory-and-typology · theories: Lameness symbolizes impotence and the wounded land; Severian becomes a mythic figure with healed leg; Silk is the designated savior of the Whorl

Re Nutria's excellent post:

Yes! Time to analyze enlightenments and mythic linkages.

I wouldn't pin the lame foot on Claudius alone. There is a long, long Western tradition of the symbolic wounded foot/wounded thigh. Think about the Fisher King. Or one-sandaled Jason. Or Sevarian! The wound seems nearly always to refer to impotence, and the monarch who bears it is symbolic of the wounded land; to heal it/him (as some of the Arthurian knights attempted to do in their travels) is to heal sterility/barrenness. His leg's new wholeness is quite explicitly a symbolic healing with Sevarian, who is clearly, by the end, a mythic figure. And, as Silk becomes more and more the designated Savior, his lameness stands in for the dysfunctional Whorl. Wolfe's having had polio as a child has got to have made him super-aware of the tradition. I wonder how it will figure in the new books---or do you think Wolfe will abandon Silk in favor of a new generation? Oh no!

Nutria, could you track down a root for Chrasmology? I'm sure you're right, though.

-alga-
