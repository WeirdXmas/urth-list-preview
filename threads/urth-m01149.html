powers of Erebus and Abaia (was Re: cthulhu mythos, BotNS)

Urth list · 3 messages · 2005-06-03 to 2005-06-07

2005urthThe Book of the New SunMaru DubshinkiDavid DiGiacomoSarah Dorrance-Minch
The thread: The thread asked what powers Erebus and Abaia confer, and whether Severian's abilities such as resurrection and apparent immortality come from them. David DiGiacomo answered that the pair resurrect Severian after he drowns in the Gyoll and that Severian shares their powers. Maru Dubshinki disputed the framing, arguing the powers arise from the New Sun rather than contagious magic, and that Urth is essentially about that idea. Sarah Dorrance-Minch called the gift dubious rather than good, citing zombies, a masochist fan club, Severian's heartbreaks and accidental resurrections, and closed with poetry. The exchange ended without resolution, drifting into a light, playful tone.
  • David DiGiacomo: Erebus and Abaia's powers, including resurrection, are shared by Severian and are good powers
  • Maru Dubshinki: the powers come from the New Sun, not contagious magic
  • Sarah Dorrance-Minch: the resurrection gift is dubious, not good, attracting zombies and masochists

Themes: Death and resurrection, Myth and folklore sources, Suffering and sacrifice

2005-06-03 Maru Dubshinki replying to David DiGiacomo #

Argues that the powers Severian shares with Erebus and Abaia, such as resurrection and immortality, come from the New Sun rather than from contagious magic. Responds to David DiGiacomo's question about the powers, saying the Urth of the New Sun is essentially about that idea.

pace Frazer, much magic is 'contagious'.

2005-06-07 Sarah Dorrance-Minch replying to David DiGiacomo #

Argues that the powers Erebus and Abaia grant Severian, resurrecting the drowned and the dead, are a dubious gift rather than good powers, since they attract zombies and a fan club of masochists. Responds to David DiGiacomo's "pretty good powers" remark by citing Severian's heartbreaks and accidental resurrections, then closes with verse by Omar Khayyam and D.H. Lawrence.
disputes · theories: Erebus and Abaia's resurrection gift is dubious

And what a dubious gift it is, to attract, well,
zombies. As if the "fan club" of masochists and thrill
seekers weren't awkward enough. Sometimes you just
have to feel sorry for him.

(Not to be facetious; sometimes I really did feel
sorry for Severian, because he got his heart broken
badly a few times too many, but his I guess you could
call it habit of accidentally resurrecting people was
rather amusing, in a way...)
