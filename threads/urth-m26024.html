The thread: Participants in this Urth thread discussed a proposed second best-of list of Gene Wolfe short stories, mostly as a list-making question rather than a textual debate. Marc Aramini objected that his own list already reached 36 entries if the Thag stories count as four, so adding fourteen more seemed like too much, and he asked whether the list must reach fifty. Marc Aramini also jokingly compared the list's control to a tyrannical teacher and argued that Trip, Trap and Suzane Delage must be included, reading Trip, Trap as Wolfe's view of an objective spiritual reality seen through fragmented viewpoints. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes posted a list of fifty favourites with WolfeWiki links and no argument, and Aramini replied that a story he called his favourite had been left out. The thread ended with a list posted and a personal correction, without agreement on the selections.

2014-10-16 Marc Aramini replying to Marc Aramini #

Jokingly reacts to Marc Aramini's call for a fairer list voting system by comparing the list's control to a tyrannical teacher, then argues that Trip, Trap and Suzane Delage must be included on any Best of Gene Wolfe list. Claims Trip, Trap embodies Wolfe's view of an objective spiritual reality seen through fragmented subjective viewpoints, which the poster takes as the viewpoint behind his whole corpus.
disputes · theology-and-religion

On Thu, Oct 16, 2014 at 9:56 AM, Robert Pirkola <[email]>

This is off topic, but your call for suggestions on a more equitable way to run the system evoked a snicker from me. When I was teaching college, my students never realized how innately authoritarian I was because I was always so kind and easy to approach, and naturally gave people the benefit of the doubt. Every once in a while, they would come up with the idea of voting for something and I would laugh and smile, and say, do you imagine you are in a democracy? This is a tyranny. And they were content, never realizing that they had seriously entered the soft determined universe of my will. (When you have cancer, you don't poll your friends on what to do - however, I do admit that in this case the list is closer to the cancer specialist of my metaphor than the general public would be and a similar weighted vote system might be appropriate). But ... you can't leave out Trip, Trap and Suzane Delage, in any case. Trip, Trap is the quintessential Wolfe universe: an objective spiritual reality occluded by sundered and fragmented subjective viewpoints, but still more "real" than even the physical world. In microcosm this is the viewpoint behind his entire corpus.
