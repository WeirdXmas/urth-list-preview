2009-05-19 Jordon Flato replying to David Stockhoff #

Questions whether trees on Blue are carnivorous at all, disputing the idea that the Pit shows a tree that ate Horn, and contrasting this with Green where carnivory is clear. Grounds the challenge in a recent reread of the Short Sun books, noting that no passage supports the claim for Blue's plant life.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Blue's trees are carnivorous like Green's

Is there any evidence that trees on Blue are Carnivorous in any way? The Pit is certainly not evidence. People talk about it being part of a tree that 'ate' Horn, but on this last recent read through I can't see a single thing that supports that, or the idea that the trees on Blue are Carnivorous or maleficent in any way at all, as opposed to Green, where that is abundantly clear.

2009-05-19 David Stockhoff #

Argues that the plant life on Green and Blue, including the islands Horn sees, is carnivorous, which makes consciousness in the Neighbors and inhumi more plausible. Responds to Marc Aramini's quoted passage from On Blue's Waters, where a storm reveals the islands to be giant herbs rather than trees, suggesting the pit Horn falls into may be such a plant.
supports · close-textual-reading · theories: Plant life on Green and Blue is carnivorous; The pit Horn falls into is a giant herb

CARNIVOROUS trees, then. Of course. One big fly trap.

I think one key to understanding Green and Blue is that, for some reason, all the major "plant" life on both planets is carnivorous. This does not imply consciousness, but it makes it more plausible---whether you're talking about the Neighbors or the inhumi. Consciousness is especially fluid in The Short Sun.

a few weeks ago I made the claim the island of the pit on blue was composed of trees. I am always travelling and so did not have access to my books until now. On page 161 of oBW, chapter 6, the storm shakes up the islands and reveals to Horn that they are actually composed of &quot;giant herbs&quot; that are not trees or ferns but have the nature of them, and that Babbie and he walked on the branches and leaves believing them to be ground.

Just a bit more hinting that the pit Horn falls in might be the maw of one such ocean growing herb.
