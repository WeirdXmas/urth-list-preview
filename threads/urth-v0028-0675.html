2000-01-09 James Jordan #

Proposes that Tzadkiel's chess image of pawns, which advance until promoted, means the pawn ultimately becomes the bride of Christ, while the larger pieces, though more powerful, do not reach that end. Connects this reading to the ending of Strange Travelers and responds to Alga's quoted account of Tzadkiel's remark that not all pieces on the board are pawns.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · theology-and-religion · theories: Pawn promotion symbolizes becoming the bride of Christ

Given *Strange Travelers,* we now recall that pawns advance until they become queens. The other pieces may look bigger and be able to do more, but at the end, the pawn becomes the bride of Christ.

Nutria

2000-01-09 Nicholas Gevers #

Asks Nutria whether God's shift of focus to another part of the board near the end of "To the Seventh" will save Chance and company and let the pawn promote, and why the story's comic-book texture is there. Suggests Wolfe may be implying Chance's cosmos is an inferior parody with a demiurgic God, building on the chess analogy Jim Jordan quoted from Alga's exchange with Tzadkiel.
questions · allegory-and-typology · theology-and-religion · theories: Chance's cosmos is an inferior parody of reality; God in To the Seventh is a demiurge

The chess analogy arises in "To the Seventh", a story
I found problematic in various ways. Nutria, do you
think God's shifting of focus to another part of the
board near the end will save Chance and co, diverting
Satan long enough for the pawn to queen? And: why the
comic book texture of the universe portrayed? I
thought Wolfe might be implying Chance's cosmos is an
inferior parody of reality, its "God" a demiurge...
