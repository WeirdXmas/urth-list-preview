2003-08-06 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Shares two double dactyl verses the poster composed after waking with them, one teasing Alice K. Turner for pondering whether Wolfe, Crowley, Wynne-Jones and Jack Vance plotted their novels by chance. Second verse jokes that Andre-Driussi hoped the market would bear more of his erudite lore on Wolfe's vocabulary; it is a playful social post, not an argument.
chats

Though you might not think it to look at my list output, I've been in a terrible writer's block
for over a year, which broke recently and all sorts of stuff has been pouring out (rather like
the fellow in the "Calliope" issue of THE SANDMAN). Which includes these -- don't ask me
why, but I woke up this morning with the beginnings of these, uh, things ringing in my
aural cortex.

Higglety-pigglety, Alice K. Turner
considered Wolfe, Crowley, Wynne-Jones and Jack Vance endlessly pondering, microbotanically,
whether their novels were plotted by chance.

Hoccitus-poccitus, Andre-Driussi,
while publishing volumes of erudite lore,
on Gene Wolfe's vocabular armamentaria
hoped that the market might bear a few more.

Okay, I'll shut up now ...

--Blattid

2003-08-07 William H. Ansley #

Shares an original double dactyl praising Gene Wolfe, written in response to a previous poster's playful verse and recalling the form from an Isaac Asimov essay. The post is social and off-topic in content, consisting of a light comic poem about Wolfe's underappreciated status.
chats

Ah, double dactyls. As I recall I first came across this verse form in an Isaac Asimov essay.

You have inspired me Dan'l. Here is my own modest effort.

Clickety-Clackety
Writer Gene Rodman Wolfe
Really ranks up with a
Scintillant few.

But wearing sheep's clothing
Scientifictional,
He almost never is
Given his due.

William Ansley
