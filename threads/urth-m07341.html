The thread: Readers debated whether the Neighbors and other life in Wolfe's Short Sun and Long Sun books can be compatible across worlds, and how Horn and the vanished gods should be read. Marc Aramini proposed that Horn passes into Babbie and that the vanished gods are engineered parasites, while James Wynn argued Horn is a Christ-figure merging peoples. Dave Tallman, Dan'l Danehy-Oakes and brunians offered origin explanations and a Creator argument that brunians rejected. Pedro Pereira repeatedly argued the books are science-fantasy whose biology need not be real science, and Wynn echoed the Flash Gordon comparison. The thread ended without agreement, drifting from textual interpretation into biology and the Creator question.
  • Marc Aramini: Horn transfers into Babbie and vanished gods are engineered alzabo-like parasites
  • James Wynn: Horn is a Christ-figure whose merging of Neighbors and Earthlings is needed for colonies
  • Pedro Pereira: Wolfe's biology is science-fantasy, not hard science, and can be granted liberties
  • Dave Tallman: Neighbors' compatibility explained by colonists, time travel, or parallel evolution
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: a Creator guiding evolution would make life across worlds compatible
  • brunians: life on Earth is not even self-compatible, so no Creator is implied

Themes: Christ figures and messiahs, Mirrors, pictures and art, Robots, androids and artificial life

2008-09-22 Dave Tallman replying to Marc Aramini #

Disputes Marc Aramini's claim that Urth is Green, calling it a stretch, and proposes alternative explanations for why Neighbors, inhumi and shearbears are compatible with humans. Offers three options: settlement by human colonists using FTL travel, time travel bringing Whorl colonists back, or parallel evolution, noting that doubled limbs suggest different life forms.
disputes · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Urth is Green; Neighbors descend from human colonists using FTL travel; Neighbors derive from time-travelling Whorl colonists; Neighbors result from parallel evolution

Marc,

Your article is fascinating and I agree with nearly all of it except for the "Urth is Green" which seems to me a stretch too far. (I know that Gene Wolfe once said this, but I believe it was an engineering joke about ground wires).

Those who don't accept this part of the theory need an alternate explanation for the extreme compatibility of Neighbors/inhumi/Blue life with humans. They are blood-eating and soul-copying compatible (inhumi), "change-blood" compatible (shearbears), and "hybrid" compatible (Horn as a Neighbor-man, whatever that process entails).

Blue or Green being Urth is only one possible explanation. Here are some others:

1) Settlement long ago by human colonists using FTL travel. Typhon might or might not have known of this, perhaps intending an invasion rather than a fresh colony.
2) Time-travel, such that the Neighbors derive from the present Whorl colonists.
3) Parallel evolution. The doubled-limbed character of the life forms does suggest they are different. Hybridization that doubles genes wouldn't automatically double limbs.

2008-09-22 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes replying to brunians #

Replies to brunians by defending the view that life on Urth is compatible enough for one kind to eat another, rather than being made of interchangeable parts. Grounds this in the blood comparison of mollusks and vertebrates, noting that copper-blooded shellfish are edible to humans while lobster or chimp blood transfusions would not work, and extends it to the Alzabo.
disputes · theology-and-religion · theories: Creator made life compatible enough to eat one another

That would be a very odd thing for me, as an adult convert to Catholicism, to argue!

I will point out, to begin with, that at least some of those copper-blooded mollusks are edible by iron-blooded humans. I don't particularly care for them myself, but there it is. We couldn't accept a blood transfusion from a lobster, but then I suspect it would be touch-and-go (at best) with blood from a chimp.

I am not suggesting that the Briahverse's Creator made all life of interchangeable parts. Rather, I am suggesting that He made it sufficiently "compatible" that life from one world can eat at least some of the life from another.

Thus, for example, the Alzabo...

proposes · close-textual-reading · theology-and-religion · theories: Vanished gods are artificial genetic eaters like alzabo; Liana parasites overthrew a vegetative sentience

2008-09-22 Pedro Pereira replying to Dave Tallman #

Argues that Wolfe's books are not strictly plausible hard science fiction, so readers should allow liberties in biology, citing gene-doubling and alien blood drinkable by the inhumi. Responds to Dave Tallman's critique of Marc's article, classing the books as science-fantasy closer to Flash Gordon but deeper.
disputes · genre-and-craft · theories: Wolfe's books are science-fantasy rather than hard SF

Regarding point 3) we have to take into account that Wolfe's stories are not strictly hard sci-fi plausible in many points. You are correct that doubling-genes doesn't mean doubling limbs, not to mention that we are talking about completely diferent biochemistries and biologies and I pretty much doubt it would be so easy to cross two or more alien species so "simply". The same can be said of the blood-drinking inhumi, which assumes that alien blood can be drank with no problems, when veins and blood as we know it is only common to the animal kingdom on Earth (which makes up such a minor percentage of the total number of species). So some liberties have to be taken into account. I would consider Wolfe's books more like Science-Fantasy ? la Flash Gordon than anything else, albeit quite more deep than those and at a completely diferent level.

Pedro

2008-09-23 James Wynn replying to Dave Tallman #

Relays Dave Tallman's three alternatives to Blue and Green being Urth and Lune: Wolfe simply likes that pairing, Blue is St. Croix, or Blue is Verthandi with Burroughs's Mars as model. Grounds these in parallels with The Fifth Head of Cerberus, Burroughs's Pellucidar and Mars stories, and Don Doggett's point about the Whorl returning to Sol.
proposes · puzzle-solving · source-and-allusion · theories: Blue is Saint Croix from The Fifth Head of Cerberus; Blue is Verthandi, the Whorl returned to Sol; Blue's multi-limbed monsters homage Edgar Rice Burroughs

Dave Tallman says:

Unfortunately for people like me who find the Urth/Lune = Blue/Green concept appealing, there are three more very glaring alternatives.

1) Wolfe just likes the Blue/Green combination for reasons no one has been able to identify (to my knowledge). We see it in The Fifth Head of Cerberus and in Pandora By Holly Hollander.

2) This is not the only parallel I've noted between tBotSS and tFHoC, so it is worth considering that Blue is St. Croix.

3) Thematically, the reason Blue has multi-limbed monsters is because it is another tip of the hat to Edgar Rice Burroughs. The Short Sun is an homage to Rice's Martian stories just as the Long Sun is an omage to the Pellucidar stories. So as Don Doggett once pointed out, if the Whorl has returned to Sol, then Blue is just as (or more) likely to be Verthandi. Remember that Mars might once have been covered by water, and I suppose one could imagine that it could be again.

J
