Asks several questions about Wolfe's story Memorare: March's apparently deep Catholic knowledge and possible future conservative shift in Catholicism, his historical knowledge of the Thugees, the aims of the Founder and whether he sends people back to Earth, and whether the Founder represents Wolfe. Mentions a hardcover edition and the story's online availability. Grounds the questions in details from the story's text, such as March reading the Memorare in Latin and contemplating Jesus and his mother.
questions · close-textual-reading · author-and-biography · theories: Founder represents Wolfe; Wolfe posits future conservative Catholicism

I know there are some very good discussions of eclipses, shadows, and solar sails going on now, but I thought I'd take a moment to bring up a slightly-more-obscure story.

I finally got around to reading Memorare tonight, and really liked it. I looked up the past discussions of it, but there are several questions I still have.

(By the way - though the story was originally published only in the print version of Fantasy and Science Fiction Magazine, it's been more than a year since release and the story now appears on the F&SF website: http://www.sfsite.com/fsf/fiction/gw01.htm )

By the way, did that hardcover edition of Memorare ever appear? I've heard it was delayed until June 2008 at least, but I haven't heard much more. Is it still on track to come out this month? If it's out, is it a quality product?

Questions:
1. What exactly IS March's religious background? He's clearly a lapsed Catholic of some sort, but he knows a lot more Catholic lore than most of today's Catholics. He more or less knows the Memorare, and he's even read it in Latin. There's also a short passage where he contemplates the relationship between Jesus and his mother. Plus, March, though perfectly capable of living in sin with Kit, seems very interested in actually marrying her. Given that March is 43 or so, presumably born a few years from now, are we to take it that Wolfe posits a future move back towards conservatism in Catholicism? I can't think of other plausible reasons for March to know so much about his religion.

2. In a similar vein, March seems to be a pretty competent historian - he's heard of the Thugees and how they operate, and I think his knowledge is just a *bit* more complicated than what you could glean from watching the second Indiana Jones.

3. What exactly are "the Founder's" goals for Earth? Is he really sending people back there? What exactly do these hypothetical people do? (Perhaps there are clues to this in An Evil Guest, which I gather may be set in the same universe)

4. If, as several other Urth readers have posited, the Founder is supposed to represent Wolfe, what does that mean? I wonder if it's a comment on a magazine "idolizing" Wolfe by devoting an entire issue of a magazine to him?

Thanks,

-Matt
