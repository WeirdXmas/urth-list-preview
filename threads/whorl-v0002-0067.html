Typhon & Pas

Whorl list · 4 messages · 1997-01-22 to 1997-01-25

1997whorlThe Book of the Long SunRanjit BhatnagarDerek BellJames Jordan
The thread: Ranjit Bhatnagar opened by arguing that Pas is Typhon, pointing to the two-headed monitor image and Typhon's grafted head, and raised questions about scanning and a 1971 transplant film. Jim Jordan proposed that Pas's division of cities by language mirrors the Tower of Babel, making Pas a counterfeit of God, and offered no further textual citation. Derek Bell extended the linguistic point by reading Wolfe's Latin and Old French as analogues to Severian's tongue, then argued Pas is a demiurge whose Whorl stands for life and Green and Blue for the afterlife, which he said conflicts with Catholic teaching. The thread ends with Derek's counterclaim against Jordan's counterfeit reading, with no visible resolution.
  • Ranjit Bhatnagar: Pas is Typhon, based on the two-headed image and the grafted head in The Sword of the Lictor
  • Jim Jordan: Pas's language division repeats Babel, making him a counterfeit of God
  • Derek Bell: Wolfe's languages mirror Severian's tongue, and Pas is a demiurge rather than a counterfeit of God

Themes: Doubles, twins and clones, Myth and folklore sources, Catholic faith and sacraments

1997-01-22 Ranjit Bhatnagar #

Argues that Pas is Typhon, citing the two-headed image on the monitor and Typhon's account of his head grafted onto the slave Piaton's body in The Sword of the Lictor. Also raises whether Kypris scanned Silk into Mainframe, questions how ancestors were scanned there, and cites a 1971 two-headed transplant film as Typhon's possible inspiration.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Pas is Typhon; Typhon got the idea from a two-headed transplant film; Kypris scanned Silk into Mainframe

Inconsistent or not, I don't think there's any doubt that Pas is Typhon. I don't remember exactly when it occurs, but when Pas appears in a monitor or Window, he's shown with one head speaking and the other gasping, trying to catch its breath. In _The Sword of the Lictor,_ Typhon explains to Severian that he extended his life by having his head
grafted onto the body of a healthy slave, Piaton, but that Piaton's head couldn't be removed because it still
controlled the heart. Severian: "You told me about Piaton. Why can't he speak?"
"He can't get his breath, poor fellow," Typhon said. [...] "As I told you, I control all the voluntary functions--I will control the involuntary ones too, soon."

How many tyrants do YOU know who are in this particular fix? There can't be a lot of them.

I agree that the time scales don't seem to match up, though. Pas's time is close enough to our own time that French is still in use. Heck, I can't even figure out how they could be consistent within the Book of the New Sun alone.

But I really write to alert you to the movie "THE INCREDIBLE TWO-HEADED TRANSPLANT." I just stumbled across it in Video Movie Guide 1997: "A sadistic killer's head is grafted to the body of a dim-witted giant. Rated PG. DIR: Anthony M. Lanza. CAST: Bruce Dern, Pat Priest, Casey Kasem.
1971." Typhon must have rented this movie; that's where he got the idea.

[Exodus spoilers below]

Also, near the end of Exodus, on the airship monitor, Kypris shows Silk a vision of himself as Pas. I wonder if she has already scanned him into Mainframe without his permission? Or was she just using a monitor's demonstrated ability to show people cosmetic improvements?

Speaking of which, what's this business about people
speaking to their dead ancestors at Mainframe? If Mainframe really is a kind of digital heaven, what's the point? It might be useful to have a repository of personalities and knowledge, but not if few people are ever allowed to consult it. Perhaps if the stored personalities could be brought to the new colony planets, they'd be useful in establishing a new civilization, but there's no indication that Mainframe is portable. And how did all these ancestors get scanned, anyway? Most people die far from a monitor or Window.

- r

1997-01-23 Derek Bell #

Argues that Wolfe's use of Latin and Old French in The Book of the New Sun stands in for languages that relate to Severian's tongue as the text's languages relate to English, and suggests French marks a language contemporary with Silk. Grounds this in Silk's remark about Latin surviving as a native tongue and in the Trivigauntis speaking an Arabic-like language, reading it as Pas creating linguistic barriers between cities.
proposes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion · theories: Old French represents a language contemporary with Silk; Pas created linguistic barriers between cities

IIRC, Wolfe used languages such as Latin and Old French in the Book of the New Sun to represent languages that bear the same relationship to Severian's language as the ones used in the text do to English. Perhaps French is used to represent a contemporary (to Silk) language.

Latin is once mentioned by Silk as an example of a
language that might survive in another city as the native tongue; again this is probably used in place of what, to Silk, would be a dead language that is still known of.

I see that Pas didn't just divide cities by physical barriers - it looks like he deliberately created linguistic, if not cultural, barriers too. (Damn clever, these two-headed dictators! :-)) The Trivigauntis are shown as speaking something that sounds like Arabic, which is a totally different family of language to English.)

Derek

1997-01-25 Derek Bell #

Proposes that the Whorl stands for life and Green and Blue for the afterlife, suggesting Pas is a demiurge, which conflicts with Catholic teaching. Grounds this in the colonists entering the tunnels through a crater in the graveyard and in Silk's wrong interpretations, which may be a warning about reading. Responds to the claim that Pas is a counterfeit of God.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · theology-and-religion · theories: Pas is a demiurge; Whorl is life and Green and Blue are afterlife

The whorl could be interpreted as a metaphor for life, with Green & Blue as a metaphor for the afterlife. (Don't the colonists in Exodus enter the tunnels through a crater in the graveyard?) In this interpretation, Pas is a demiurge - this is contrary to Catholic teaching, no? (Then again, Silk comes to interpretations that are wrong, so perhaps Wolfe is making a subtle warning about interpretations.)

Derek
