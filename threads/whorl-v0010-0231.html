2000-01-20 Nicholas Gevers #

Answers Shannon Wilde's question about which Long Sun characters are engineered embryos by stating that only Silk and Mucor are clearly grown special embryos. Notes Mucor's adoption by Blood and her special powers as grounds, and says Chenille was the natural daughter of Calde Tussah.
answers · close-textual-reading · theories: Mucor is a grown special embryo

The only character in LONG SUN other than Silk who is
clearly a grown special embryo is Mucor (thus her
adoption by Blood, and her special powers). Chenille
was the natural daughter of Calde Tussah.

2000-01-21 Alex David Groce #

Argues that the fish-possession episode on the island in The Book of the Long Sun may not be Mucor alone, suggesting that a Neighbor or another figure such as Babbie, Mother or Seawrack could be the possessing presence. Grounds this in Horn's ignoring Mucor's known abilities, the mysterious references to someone else on the island, and hints involving Remora.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Something other than Mucor possesses the fish on the island; A Neighbor possesses the fish in the island episode

I also thought it was just Mucor possessing the fish at first--although Horn, who knows about Mucor, ignores this possibility, and there are the mysterious references to someone else on the island. Babbie, Mother, and Seawrack have all been proposed, and I think a Neighbor is another possibility (the weird hints with Remora, etc.)

MAYBE it was just Mucor (or have we been told she can only possess humans?) but something fishy is definitely going on in this bit. Pun intended.

2000-01-21 Alastair Reynolds #

Asks how Mucor's possession ability works in the Long Sun books and whether it can be rationalised with an SF explanation like Wolfe usually offers. Also voices puzzlement about Seawrack and Horn's casual attitude toward Seawrack's missing arm while reading On Blue's Waters.
questions · close-textual-reading

Alex:

How does Mucor's possession ability work? Maybe I missed something (very likely) but it seems hard to rationalise her talent with an underlying SF-nal explanation, in the usual Wolfe manner. I probably need to re-read TBOTLS...

I'm about a third of the way through OBW and enjoying it immensely. Very puzzled by Seawrack, and (as was noted here by other readers), Horn's nonchalant attitude to her missing arm. Oh well...I'm sure it will all become clear in the end...not!

Al

2000-01-28 Sheila Herndon #

Proposes that the Inhumuni secret may be loving the Inhumuni, so Horn is saved when Krait drinks his blood and Horn loves him, while noting the implementation problem. Recalls Wolfe's WindyCon remark about inserting a ghost to mix genres and answers Alastair Reynolds's question on Mucor's possession by raising the same problem of blood-taking.
proposes · puzzle-solving · genre-and-craft · theories: Loving the Inhumuni saves Horn; Krait's blood-drinking is the secret

<relatively new subscriber delurking for a question>

At WindyCon, GW mentioned at one of his panels
that He purposefully put a ghost into the middle
of one of his novels so that the reader would
not expect only science fiction from the novel.
(The panel was about mixing the genres).

So perhaps he is mixing sf and the supernatural.

But I don't really know, and just wanted to throw
that out there to see what other people think.

When I first read TBLN I thought the Outsider
god was supernatural, and the others were
technological, but that is probably naive.

...About the Inhumuni secret. I agree with
whoever said that the love-thy-neighbor angle
doesn't seem to be a good candidate because of
the impossibility of implementation. If something
were impossible to implement, then I don't think
they would consider it a threat.

But the love-thy-neighbor theory is still
intriguing and something has to be there. All of
the technologically oriented ideas so far seem
hokey.

One offshoot of the love idea is to love the
inhumuni. Maybe this is what saves Horn. Krait
drinks his blood, and then Horn loves him. Most
people wouldn't. Maybe that's the secret. So it's
a bigger version of love-thy-neighbor (what *is*
thy neighbor).

*Except* that, again we have the implementation
problem (even if maybe it isn't so difficult,
people can protect themselves and it doesn't depend
on the actions of other people), and if blood taking
is required, what protection is there against
inhumuni who would kill their first time victom
outright?

<back to lurking, don't even have a name.>

sh
