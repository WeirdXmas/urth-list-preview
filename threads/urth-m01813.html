new history of the Peloponnesian War

Urth list · 6 messages · 2006-08-08 to 2006-08-09

2006urthChris PMo Holkar / UKGMatthew KingRussell Wodell
The thread: Readers discussed whether a modern military history of the Peloponnesian War, by Victor Davis Hanson, helps in reading Gene Wolfe's Soldier books. Matthew King argued that knowing Salamis, Themistocles' strategy and Persian losses enriches Latro's story, and asked where modern authors get their information if not from ancient sources. Chris cautioned against reading Latro through a modern book, since Wolfe views the period mainly through ancient authors. Mo Holkar argued that Wolfe draws on Herodotus's oddities and fantasies, which can inform a reader more than a strictly factual modern history. The thread drifted from the modern book toward Herodotus as the real source, with no clear resolution.
  • Matthew King: modern military history like Hanson's enriches reading Latro's Salamis and the Soldier books
  • Chris: be wary of reading Latro through modern historical books; Wolfe works mainly from ancient authors
  • Mo Holkar: Wolfe draws on Herodotus's fantasies and oddities, which inform readers more than modern factual history

Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Myth and folklore sources, War and soldiers

2006-08-08 Matthew King replying to Russell Wodell #

Points out that the reviewer Victor Davis Hanson wrote a chapter on the naval battle of Salamis in Carnage and Culture, which Latro's Salamis (rendered as Peace) draws on. Responds to Russell Wodell's link to a review of a Peloponnesian War history by noting the poster's own prior reading and recommending audio lectures on Herodotus as background for the Latro books.
informs · source-and-allusion

The author of this review (Victor Davis Hanson) wrote a chapter about the naval battle of Salamis in his book Carnage and Culture. Salamis is the name "translated" by Latro as meaning Peace.

I had read Carnage and Culture shortly before I discovered Wolfe, and I was thrilled when I recognized what Latro was writing about.

Another great resource for historical background is the Teaching Company's audio lectures on Herodotos.

2006-08-08 Matthew King replying to Chris #

Argues that a modern military history book on Western warfare is not directly relevant to Soldier of the Mist, yet knowing the battle of Salamis, Themistocles' strategy and Persian losses enriches reading Latro's story. Responds to Chris's wariness about modern historical books by asking where those modern authors get their information if not from Herodotus and other ancient writers.
disputes · source-and-allusion

The focus of _Carnage and Culture_ is on the nature and development of Western military prowess, so the chapter on Salamis is not directly relevant to _Soldier of the Mist_. As I recall it, Latro doesn't tell us much about the battle of Peace.

Knowing a little about the battle, the strategy of Themistocles, and the appalling human loss on the side of the Persians: I say knowing some of this makes for a richer reading of _Mist_.

Regarding wariness toward modern books: where do you suppose the authors get their historical information about the Persian war if not from Herodotos, et al.?

2006-08-09 Mo Holkar / UKG replying to Russell Wodell #

Argues that Wolfe draws on the oddities and fantasies of Herodotus, which modern scholars may dismiss as unhistorical, so Herodotus can inform a reader of Wolfe more than a strictly factual modern history. Responds to Russell Wodell's point about wariness toward modern books by reframing the original claim about Wolfe's use of Herodotus rather than defending modern historians.
disputes · source-and-allusion

I think the point was though that Wolfe relishes and draws on the oddities and fantasies of Herodotus, which may be dismissed as unhistorical by modern scholars. So Herodotus may be in some ways more "infomative" when reading Wolfe than a 'but this is what actually happened' account.
