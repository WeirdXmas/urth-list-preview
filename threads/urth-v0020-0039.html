Themes: Names and their meanings

1998-10-16 Alex David Groce #

Proposes that Wolfe's Severian may be named after the bishop Severian, rather than the Encratite heretics, citing an entry in the Catholic Encyclopedia. Grounds this on the Encratite article mentioning Severus only in passing and on Severian's record as a preacher opposed to Chrysostom, while noting the name could be unchanged or altered by Wolfe.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Severian named after bishop Severian

Well, there may be some question as to which Severian Wolfe was thinking of. The heretic sect Severians (who are indeed in the online Catholic [email]/advent) are better known as the Encratites, and
it is under that name that you will find them online.
However, there is also an entry for Severian. The Severians are actually named after a man named Severus mentionied in Eusebius' Hist. Eccl. (and a mention is all he gets in the article on the Encratites, and he doesn't appear to have an article of his own).
Severian was a bishop who battled with St. John Chrysostom, but doesn't appear to have been a heretic (he was noted as a good preacher, and apparently good at church politics). Assuming Wolfe didn't play around with the names (not neccessarily a safe bet) it looks like this is our Severian's namesake. I'll type up the article and send it to the online encyclopedia and to the mailing list. I may go ahead and also transcribe and post the article on Severus, the Roman emperor, which given the Claudian connections might also be relevant.

Just an amusing side note--the facing pages in the CE with the entries on Severian and Severus end with an entry relevant to Wolfe's perennial favorite, Proust--an article on Madame de Sevigne!
