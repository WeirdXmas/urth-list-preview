How big is the whorl?

Whorl list · 1 messages · 1996-11-27 to 1996-11-27

1996whorlThe Book of the Long SunRanjit Bhatnagar

1996-11-27 Ranjit Bhatnagar #

Estimates the Whorl's size by multiplying the population of Viron and other cities mentioned in the Long Sun books by city count, comparing the total to Japan and California. Also questions whether the flier Silk sees three-score leagues away, far above him, is plausible given how far a person can be seen, and suggests leagues may be shorter or the narrator exaggerating.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Whorl is roughly the size of California rolled into a tube; Whorlish leagues are shorter than ordinary leagues

Some clues: when Sand and Hammerstone are talking to Silk about soldiers and the Plan of Pas in the tunnels, they mention something like a couple hundred cities, and about half a million people in Viron. If we take that to mean 200 cities with half a million each, that's a hundred million people... roughly comparable to the population of Japan (125 million), whose land area is 375,000 square kilometers/15000 sq mi, about the size of California. It's often implied that there's a lot of unpopulated, wild space between cities, so the cities are probably more evenly distributed in size and location than cities in Japan or California. Could the Whorl be about the size of California rolled up into a tube?

When Silk was lying woozy on Blood's roof, having been whacked by the White-Headed One, he saw a flier flying "three-score leages" (289 km/179 mi) beneath (above) him. That sounds unlikely, though -- who could see a small person 179 miles away? You can't see people from 5 miles up in an airplane. Maybe whorlish leagues are a lot smaller, or the narrator was exaggerating because Silk was so out of it.
