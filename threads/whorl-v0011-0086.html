Suicide

2000whorlThe Book of the Long SunJames Jordan

2000-08-10 James Jordan #

Disputes the reading that Silk killed himself in The Book of the Long Sun, arguing instead that he is dying of wounds from a fight in which Hyacinth is dead. Replies to Dennis/Endy, citing the bleeding and torn house as signs of defence and warning against dire readings, as with Weer in Peace.
disputes · close-textual-reading · author-and-biography · theories: Silk is dying from wounds in a conflict; Silk did not commit suicide

At the moment I'm on your side here. The fact that Silk is bleeding might mean that he had cut himself, but it might also mean he's been defending the house. Not only is Silk bleeding, but the house is completely torn up. My first guess would be the Silk is dying from wounds he has received in some conflict, and that Hyacinth is already dead from the same. Dire interpretations of Wolfe events are tempting, but recall how often they have been wrong. Weer in *Peace* was supposed to be a multiple murderer, according to some, until Wolfe said that was nonsense. I assumed Severian was a much worse character than Wolfe intended him to be. So, as regards Silk's death, well, with GW you never know! But I would not jump to the more dire reading, without good evidence.

Pat. Nut.
