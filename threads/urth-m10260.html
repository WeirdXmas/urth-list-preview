Which character from BotNS do you identify with most?

Urth list · 15 messages · 2010-08-02 to 2010-08-05

2010urthThe Book of the New SunRyan DunnPaul BDavid StockhoffMarc AraminiJeff WilsonJack Smith
The thread: Ryan Dunn opened by asking which Book of the New Sun character readers identify with, naming himself as Drotte. The thread then drifted into speculation about the Autarch's memory transfer: David Stockhoff and Jeff Wilson proposed in-world mechanisms for succession, including preserved brain tissue, Sulpicius's stored brain, and memory-eating by autarchs. Jack Smith and Paul B reframed the question as which character one would choose to be, while Marc Aramini confessed identifying with Hethor. Little was settled; the thread ended in speculative in-world theorizing with a few personal confessions.
  • Ryan Dunn: identifies with Drotte for leadership and honesty, rejects Severian and Vodalus
  • Jack Smith: would choose to be Dr. Talos over Severian
  • Paul B: the Autarch's life is enviable, and assassins striking too early seems implausible
  • David Stockhoff: autarchs' succession is preserved by murderers inheriting access codes; Inire seeks smart children as future candidates
  • Jeff Wilson: autarchs revive from preserved brain tissue and absorb memories of the freshly dead
  • Marc Aramini: identifies most with Hethor for revenge, idolizing an unattainable woman, and slender build

Themes: Memory and forgetting, Robots, androids and artificial life, Power, rulers and politics

2010-08-02 Ryan Dunn #

Asks which Book of the New Sun character readers identify with, then answers personally by identifying with Drotte because of a lifelong leadership role. Explains the choice through shared traits of sarcasm, honesty and quiet respect, while rejecting Severian and Vodalus as too far from his own experience.
informs

Courtesy Josh, but an exercise to consider, and got me to thinking...

"Which character in TBoTNS do you identify with and why?"

I'll go first. I most identify with DROTTE, because I have functioned as a leader most of my life, from being the oldest child in a single-parent family to creative directing teams of people as a professional and everywhere in-between. But of course there are other leaders in this story, and I'm not prepared to identify with Severian that much. And Vodalus is above my pay grade.

So I consider my style of leadership more akin to Drotte's, meaning a good balance of sarcasm and honesty, and a knack of garnering an understated respect from those around and beneath you.

...ryan

2010-08-02 David Stockhoff replying to Paul B #

Proposes that anyone who murders an autarch and learns his access codes would inherit the office, so the succession is preserved and a hired assassin could become ruler. Offers this as an explanation for autarchs who reigned only weeks or months, and responds to Paul B's question about why no autarch was killed before passing on his memory.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Assassins who kill the autarch succeed him

Possibly, the lure of gaining all that information---as well as the access codes that make one the autarch---ensure that anyone who murders the incumbent (if he knows about it) would succeed him even if he had no intent to do so, thus preserving the succession. A hired assassin could become autarch instead of his employer.

This could also explain a huge number of autarchs who ruled for only weeks or months.

2010-08-02 Jeff Wilson replying to Paul B #

Proposes that the Autarch's succession could be arranged by preserving leftover brain tissue, so that Father Inire revives a new Autarch from Sulpicius's stored brain. Grounds this in the preserved bodies of Thecla and the mandragora, and suggests Sulpicius was detained out of sight rather than killed.
proposes · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Father Inire preserves brains to revive Autarchs; Sulpicius was detained rather than killed

I've been pondering that hazard of succession. If Vodalus can keep Thecla's body fresh for days in an ice cave, and the mandragora remains even slightly animated after centuries unattended, Father Inire can probably preserve some leftover brains for just such an occasion.

Maybe that's what became of Sulpicius - he is detained somewhere out of sight for an extended period, and is presumed dead by Inire, who thaws out one of the unconsumed portion of Sulpicius-minus-1's brain and initiates Autarch Sulpicius-plus-1. Meanwhile, just in case his time-bending cousins did pick him up, he advises Sulpicius-plus-1 to let stand his contingencies.

2010-08-02 Jack Smith replying to Ryan Dunn #

Argues that the reader would most want to be Dr. Talos in The Book of the New Sun rather than identify with Severian, citing Talos's creative work writing and staging his play, his independence through his own wits, and his status as a valued expert rather than a lackey to Baldanders. Responds to Ryan Dunn's thread prompt asking which character readers identify with, by reframing the question as which character one would choose to be.
proposes · close-textual-reading

I think I always identify with the main character of a novel, especially if the main character is also the narrator. But identifying with Severian is too obvious and boring. So I'll answer a slightly different question: "Which character would you like to be, if you could choose to live on Urth?"

I would want to be Dr. Talos. He gets to exercise his creativity by writing and staging his play. He lives on his own terms and by his own wits. Baldanders may be his master, but Talos is a valuable expert, not a lackey. On the other hand, he's not really human...

Best wishes,
Jack

Proposes that the Autarch's life would be an enviable role on Urth, and questions whether it is mere chance that no Autarch has been assassinated before transferring his brain's contents. Builds on Jack Smith's question about which character one would choose to be, reasoning from the Autarch's luxury and freedom to the implausibility of assassins striking too early.
questions · chronology-and-plot-logic

Which actually raises a curious point: is it purely by chance that no Autarch has ever been killed before passing on the contents of his brain? It would seem like an assassin would kill him too quickly for the Autarch to explain the rather unexpected task his killer faces.

Paul

2010-08-02 Jeff Wilson replying to David Stockhoff #

Proposes that Autarchs add memories of freshly dead Commonwealth people to their own, which would explain the many children and farmwives and why Severian, with his eidetic memory, can revive fading memories and use them at his trial. Builds this on David Stockhoff's question about what happens to memories when codes pass without the alzabo.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Autarchs add memories of freshly dead Commonwealth people; Severian's eidetic memory revives fading succession memories

Another possibility is that the Autarchs add memories of freshly dead Commonwealthers of some merit or useful perspective. As memories fade with succession, they may feel the need to add another of the now inaccessible kind, explaining the multiple children and farmwives.

This also explains why Severian is the final Autarch in another way: his eidetic memory revives the fading imprints so that he has sufficient awareness of them that all of them serve him in a significant way at the trial, while someone attempting to add all the memories directly to himself would be driven mad or incapacitated by the myriad, vivid memory-sets all second hand fresh.
