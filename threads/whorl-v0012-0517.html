The thread: Dan Parmenter proposed that Horn survives alongside Pas and Silk in the later Silk persona, citing a page 252 exchange in In Green's Jungles and Michael Straight's point that Pas favors the weaker side. Michael Straight disputed this, arguing the Narrator of The Book of the Long Sun may contain no Pas and may instead be an alloy of several Silk versions plus Horn and Typhon. Adam Stephanides questioned whether Horn's remark that Pas is absent could mean Pas is inside him, asking whether Silk's spirit was absent from Horn before the Divine Silk arrived. The thread ended with competing readings of the same passage rather than agreement.
  • Dan Parmenter: Horn survives alongside Pas and Silk in the later Silk persona, supported by the page 252 quote
  • Michael Straight: the Narrator may hold no Pas and may be an alloy of Silk versions, Horn and Typhon

Themes: Memory and forgetting, Doubles, twins and clones, Gods, angels and the Increate

2001-03-30 Dan Parmenter #

Proposes that Horn survives alongside Pas and Silk in the later Silk persona, citing a page 252 exchange in In Green's Jungles where Silk says Pas is present through prayer. Supports this with Michael Straight's point that Pas favored the weaker side, as Incanto did in the Blanko/Soldo conflict, and answers an earlier post on Silk's Heinleinian omnicompetence.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Horn survives within Silk; SilkHornPas as a composite being

From: William Ansley <[email]>

I've often wondered where the Hornsilk "military mind" comes from, particularly in IGJ. SilkHorn himself seems to provide the answer on page 252 with the following exchange.

"This better work, Incanto," Inclito said, studying them as they leaned against the earth-filled bags or crouched in the snow to eat.

"Will Pas drive us from this Whorl if it doesn't?"

He looked around at me, surprised. "I don't think he's even here. We left him up there with the Long Sun.

"In one sense he wasn't there, either. In another he is here with us at this moment, because I am."

Inclito said nothing for a tiume, but I am not at all sure how long a time it was; I was lost in thought. At last - "Because you pray to him, that's what you mean. He hears you praying."

I nodded. "I hope so at least."

I suppose the "how much Pas?" question remains, and is not unlike the "how much Horn?" question by the end of the series. I tend to favor the view that there's something of Horn left, along with Pas and Silk, indeed, as Rostrum noted:

From: Michael Straight <[email]>

Certain Pas traits remain. Aren't we told somewhere or other in LS that Pas tended to favor the weaker side in a conflict? Isn't this what Incanto did in the Blanko/Soldo conflict?

Right. And consider, if the "Horn lives" hypothesis is correct, you've got SilkHornPas running around on the old Whorl: the body and mind of a superhero with the "love vibe", the mind of a tyrant and the mind of an expert propagandist. A terrifying thought.

2001-04-01 Michael Straight #

Argues that the Narrator of The Book of the Long Sun may contain no Pas, since Silk was never possessed by Pas, and instead proposes he may be an alloy of several Silk versions plus Horn and Typhon. Responds to Dan Parmenter's quoted passage, which suggested Horn and Pas remain in the Narrator, by reconsidering mantis's theory of a downloaded god-version of Silk.
disputes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Pig is possessed by Passilk; Narrator has no Pas in him; Narrator is an alloy of Silk versions, Horn and Typhon; Narrator received a download of god-version Silk

If it weren't for the passage you quote, I would have said there is no Pas in the Narrator. Pig probably has some Pas because he's been posessed by the Mainframe version of Silk, which is Passilk. But the the human Silk was never posessed by Pas that we know of, so why would the Narrator have any Pas in him?

The passage you quote makes me want to reconsider mantis's theory that the Narrator got a download of the god version of Silk at some point (At the West Pole? Via Pig? During the sacrifice at the manteion? Talking to the monitor on the lander going back to Blue?). But then we have to start thinking about the Narrator as an alloy of multiple versions of Silk (Free Live Free?), as well as Horn, Typhon, and who knows who else.

-Rostrum

2001-04-01 Adam Stephanides #

Argues that Horn's remark that Pas is not present cannot literally mean Pas is inside him, since Horn would then know Silk was also there, and rejects the passage as proof of Pas in Horn. Asks whether Silk's spirit was absent from Horn before he received the Divine Silk, or whether both Silks were present afterward. It replies to Dan Parmenter's quotation from Inside the Green Jungles, partly agreeing that the passage suggests Pas is present.
questions · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Pas was downloaded into Horn with the Divine Silk; Horn has both human Silk and Divine Silk

Horn can't be literally saying that Pas is in him, because if he knew that Pas was in him, he'd know that Silk was in him, and he doesn't. Inclito's gloss is accurate as far as Horn's conscious meaning goes. Still, I agree that this passage is suggestive of Pas being present in Horn, more so than anything anyone else has put forward, though I remain unconvinced.

If Pas was downloaded into Horn, it was presumably along with the Divine Silk, when Pig got his eye. So do you hold with mantis that Silk's spirit was completely absent from Horn until then (which I'm convinced is wrong)? Or do you think that after receiving the Divine Silk Horn had both the human Silk and the Divine Silk in him?

--Adam
