Themes: Stories within stories

2000-12-08 Michael Straight #

Disputes Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's claim that Wolfe inserts himself into his fiction, arguing that the author threatening the hero in "The Last Thrilling Wonder Story" is a separate character rather than Wolfe. Contrasts this with Vonnegut's self-insertion in Breakfast of Champions and asks which collection holds "The Sister's Tale".
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: The author threatening the hero in The Last Thrilling Wonder Story is not Wolfe

No, no, no. The author who threatens the hero in "Wonder Story" is just another character, he's not Gene Wolfe himself. It's a story about a writer whose story gets away from him. Wolfe is obviously on the side of the hero. Would Gene Wolfe be angry at one of his characters for praying? And retaliate by throwing the devil at him?

It's a very different feel from, say, Breakfast of Champions, where Vonnegut really inserts *himself* into the story, imagines a conversation with his protagonist, considers the idea that he, Kurt Vonnegut, is responsible for what happens to his characters, and tells us how he feels about it.

Alas, I don't remember "The Sister's Tale." Which collection is it in?

-Rostrum
