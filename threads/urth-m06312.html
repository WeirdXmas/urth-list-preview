2008-06-27 Dave Tallman replying to Marc Aramini #

Argues that the Contessa's fate in the play is foreshadowed by Meschia's lines, reading them as meaning that a pregnancy with the seed of the New Sun would prolong her life while stranding her away from her own time. Grounds this in a close quotation of the dialogue between the Contessa and Meschia, reinterpreting the words in light of the Catherine identification.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Contessa is Catherine who becomes pregnant with the New Sun seed

We can also look with fresh eyes at the lines in the play that predict the Contessa's fate:

Contessa: If my body held a part of yours -- drops of liquescent tissue locked in my loins...

Meschia: If it did, you might wander Urth for a time longer, a lost thing that could never find its way home. But I will not bed you. Do you think you are more than a corpse? You are less.

In other words, if the Contessa (Catherine) becomes pregnant with the seed of the New Sun, her life will be prolonged but she will never be able to return to her home (her own time).
