The Apu-Punchau Eclipse

Urth list · 32 messages · 2008-06-10 to 2008-06-13

2008urthThe Book of the New SunLane HaygoodDave TallmanthalassocratStone OxAndy RobertsonRob ThorntonMatthew GrovesJeff WilsonDan'l Danehy-OakesRoy C. LackeyDavid Duffyb sharpEric Ortlund
The thread: The thread asks whether the Apu-Punchau eclipse in Wolfe's Urth books was natural or caused by Tzadkiel's Ship, Severian himself, or a staged miracle. Jeff Wilson and Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argue the Ship must be either too close to Earth or too large to cast the shadow, with solar-sail physics dominating. Roy C. Lackey and Dave Tallman argue the eclipse was staged by the Ship, while b sharp and Eric Ortlund argue Severian caused it through his body or emotions, and Matthew Groves rejects that as implausible. Scale disputes over the Ship, sails, and Wall of Nessus spread the thread far beyond the eclipse, and it ends in impasse with no consensus.
  • Roy C. Lackey: eclipse was staged by the Ship, which is planetary in scale
  • Dave Tallman: Hierogrammates staged the eclipse to rescue a time-traveling Severian
  • b sharp: Severian himself caused the eclipse, possibly subconsciously
  • Jeff Wilson: the Ship cannot be close enough or large enough to cast the shadow
  • Matthew Groves: Tzadkiel's ship is far more plausible than Severian casting a shadow
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: the Ship must be far wider than a mile for its solar sails to shade the sun

Themes: Time loops and causality, Robots, androids and artificial life, The dying sun and the cosmos

2008-06-10 Stone Ox #

Proposes that the Apu-Punchau eclipse could be caused by the Ship rather than the Moon, since a Ship orbiting close to Earth would need only about a mile in diameter. Responds to a quoted question asking whether the Ship could be large enough to shadow the Sun, by scaling its size to its distance using the Moon's proportions.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: The Ship close to Earth caused the eclipse

"I have another possible explanation for the eclipse. The exact dimensions of the Ship aren't given, but could it really be large enough to shadow the Sun and let the stars be seen like that? It would have to be almost as big as the moon. Why couldn't the eclipse be a normal one, caused by the moon?"

If the ship were a lot close than the moon, it could be a lot smaller and shadow the sun. If it were 3000 times closer (low earth orbit), it could be 3000 times smaller, or only around a mile in diameter.

Responds to Jeff Wilson's estimate that the ship sits about 80 miles up, using the physics of solar sails to rebut the size figure implied in the eclipse discussion.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic

Tzadkiel's Ship -- *counting its sails*, is significantly bigger than a mile in diameter, at least for purposes of shadow-casting. Solar sails have to be (a) very very large and (b) quite opaque in order to be effective.

2008-06-10 b sharp replying to StoneOx17 #

Disputes the theory that a hiero-ship or black hole caused the Apu-Punchau eclipse, calling the timing too coincidental since Severian cannot die and needs no rescue. Favors Dave Tallman's reading that the eclipse alludes to the sacrifice scene in Twain's A Connecticut Yankee, and suggests Severian's emotions as Conciliator could cause it, replying to StoneOx17.
disputes · source-and-allusion · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Eclipse caused by a hiero-ship or black hole; Eclipse alludes to Connecticut Yankee sacrifice scene; Severian's emotions as Conciliator cause the eclipse

I've seen the arguments about the eclipse before. The timing of a black hole or hiero-ship caused eclipse seems too coincidental. Quite a double duty for that event....trans-universal stellar events also stopping a primitive ritual sacrifice among savages on Urth? Yes, it is our hero, but we already know Severian can't be killed, as his resurrection from having his head bashed in by the savages later shows. Why would the hieros time the crucial event to rescue Severian when he doesn't need rescuing and would be killed again fairly soon?

I've always been partial to Dave Tallman's interpretation. The scene SO echoes the Connecticut Yankee scene and Wolfe makes numerous other literary allusions through this series. Moreover, as Conciliator, Severian's emotions during crisis situations cause natural, urthly upheavals...storms, earthquakes. An eclipse seems like a reasonable next upheaval. Though magnitudes more energy might be required than the other events I think the pleasure in alluding to Twain overcame any reservations Wolfe might have had.

-bsharp

2008-06-10 Eric Ortlund replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that the Apu-Punchau eclipse was not a natural event but was caused by Severian's own body blocking the sun, since he is the new sun. Responds to Roy Lackey's point that the eclipse was staged by the Ship by offering this simpler explanation, citing the way Wolfe hints at it in his postscript to the book.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Severian's body causes the Apu-Punchau eclipse; Eclipse was staged by the Ship

Even the most ignorant savages can tell the difference between an eclipse and a man's shadow. Dawn had been delayed by an eclipse. The natives knew that the sun should have been up, so they feared that what they had regarded as a boast by Severian -- that he was somehow connected with the sun and its daily comings and goings -- might be true. That is the point at which they bowed to him, *then* the sun came out and cast his shadow over them. (URTH, XLIX, 350)

In a natural eclipse, the sun doesn't come back that quickly. It was a staged event. The Ship can move out of the way much faster than the moon, and it did.

-Roy

Hi Roy -

I think I understand you, but, instead of a difference between an eclipse and Severian's shadow, I think the two are the same - Severian is blocking out the sun behind him with his own body. If that sounds strange . . . well, he's the new sun! I think this is a simpler theory than positing the ship being there right at the same time, and I think better fits with the way Wolfe hints at us in his own postscript to the book. Blessings!

Argues that the Ship in the Apu-Punchau eclipse scene came far enough into the solar system to block the sun, agreeing with Roy C. Lackey that the staged, quickly ending eclipse was deliberate. supports · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: The Ship moved into the inner solar system to cause the eclipse

You make a good point about the sudden ending of the eclipse, which also coincided with exactly when Sev needed it. I was doubtful that the Ship would come that far into the solar system, since it usually stays beyond the orbit of Dis (Pluto), but I found evidence in the text that corroborates this theory, in Urth XXVI.

Getting between eyes and one star in particular (the Sun) is exactly what we need, so I'm convinced.

2008-06-10 Jeff Wilson replying to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that Tzadkiel's ship in the Apu-Punchau eclipse would have to be far wider than a mile to cast the shadow described, using orbital speed and penumbra geometry. Responds to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's claim that solar sails must be very large and opaque, adding that the ship would also need rapid manoeuvring or a large crew, or else the unseen tender could have done the work.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Tzadkiel's ship must be miles wide to cast the eclipse shadow; the unseen tender explains the eclipse

LEO velocity is about 5 miles per second, so each second of eclipse is 5 miles worth of ship, plus the penumbra penalty (altitude times twice the tangent of the sun's arc width (0.5 degrees)). There would also need to be some means of maneuvering the entire ship within a moment or a massive number of crew available to trim the sails in a like period of time.

The mentioned but not seen tender could also have been used, as it seems to have some indefinite capacity and size.

2008-06-11 b sharp replying to StoneOx17 #

Proposes that Severian himself, subconsciously, caused the Apu-Punchau eclipse, as a competing theory to the spaceship explanation. Responding to StoneOx17, it cites Roy C. Lackey's point that the eclipse ended too quickly and argues the Hierodules would not park a ship for a pointless rescue.
proposes · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Severian subconsciously caused the Apu-Punchau eclipse; The Apu-Punchau eclipse was caused by a spaceship

I feel the need to tout my interpretation of the eclipse again. By having Severian himself (subconsciously) cause the eclipse, the multiple problems of a ship-caused eclipse are eliminated. It addresses Roy C. Lackey's observation that the eclipse ended much more quickly than a natural eclipse.

I haven't heard any explanation for why the Hiero-dudes on a cosmic mission would bother parking their vehicle in an exact spot for an exact time period to perform the useless task of saving Severian's life, when he will be killed again soon and can't actually BE killed.

But I was thinking about it and came up with a couple medium-weak explanations for those convinced the eclipse was spaceship-caused:

1. The hiero-dudes needed to create the illusion Severian could control the sun because the legend of Apu Punchau (Head of the Day) was necessary to prepare Urth for the legend of the Concilliator-New Sun. Though Apu Punchau seems to have been completely forgotten by humans in Severian's time. Was this eclipse necessary for sungod worship in the future of Urth?

2. Severian had to have the name/title "Apu Punchau" to make the scene with Hildegrin and the Cumaean work in the future. I'm not sure why that would be necessary.

Are there any other reasons the eclipse is so essential the Hieros had to create it?

If not, I'll have to consider the idea of Severian causing the eclipse at least as a strong competing theory to the spaceship theory. It makes poetic sense to me that Severian's first (in Urth history) use of Conciliator/New Sun power would be to control the sun's light.

Moreover I am not in the camp that sees Severian as a contemptible dupe for the Hiero-dudes. Though they surely manipulate him and deny him knowledge at some points, I perceive they have a true reverence for him. He isn't just a tool. Since this reverence couldn't be based on his intellect or personal morality or his accidental selection as New Sun, it must be partly based on powers he has that exceed their own.

-bsharp
