Horn in Babbie

Urth list · 3 messages · 2002-07-04 to 2002-07-05

2002urthThe Book of the Long SunStephen CaseChristopher CulverMichael Straight
The thread: Stephen Case proposed that Horn fled into Babbie, which would explain why Babbie stays on Blue when Silk and the others leave, and asked why Nettle goes with Silk, suggesting she seeks Horn. Michael Straight answered that Seawrack's song enthralls listeners, drawing Silk and then Nettle and Seawrack along, grounded in Nettle's devotion to Silk and Silk's devotion to Hyacinth. Christopher Culver disputed this, arguing Nettle leaves because she believes Horn is dead and her children grown, reading the ending as a Harrowing of Hell, and questioning why Seawrack would follow a Silk no longer carrying Horn's personality. The thread ends in unresolved disagreement with no shared conclusion.
  • Stephen Case: Horn fled into Babbie, and Nettle leaves to search for him
  • Michael Straight: Seawrack's song enthralls Silk, who draws Nettle and Seawrack along
  • Christopher Culver: Nettle leaves believing Horn dead; the ending is a Harrowing of Hell

Themes: Memory and forgetting, Death and resurrection, Ghosts, dreams and visions

Argues that if Horn fled into Babbie, as Marc suggests, this explains why Babbie stays on Blue when Silk, Seawrack and the others leave at the end of the Long Sun books. Raises a follow-up question about why Nettle leaves with Silk, speculating she is searching for Horn.

At first I didn't understand this omission, = but if Babbie does indeed contain Horn, then conceivably he wanted to stay = and watch over his children remaining on Blue. That still leaves Nettle = though. Why in the whorl did she go along with Silk (who isn't her = husband) and his girlfriend? To find Horn, who she believes might still = be wandering the Whorl or Green? Kind of tragic . . .

2002-07-05 Michael Straight #

Proposes that Seawrack's song enthralls listeners, so Silk, already tuned in through Horn, hears and is drawn to her, explaining why Nettle and Seawrack follow Silk. Grounds this in Nettle's devotion to Silk, Horn's death, and Silk's continued devotion to Hyacinth, answering Stephen Case's question about why Nettle went along.
proposes · myth-and-structure · theories: Seawrack's song enthralls listeners who hear it

Nettle was as much a disciple of Silk as Horn was. If Horn hadn't gone to search for him, Nettle would have. Now that Horn is dead, following Silk is the natural thing to do. Following Silk is the natural thing for anybody to do, actually, which may explain why Seawrack went with him, although I'm not sure why Silk wanted her to come.

Here's a theory. Hearing Seawrack's song makes you fall in love with her. Silk heard her singing because Horn was already tuned in. Just being next to Horn and having him point out the song is enough for that military officer to start hearing her. Maybe her song is like one of those hidden pictures, that once it is pointed out, you can't not see it anymore. So, even though Horn is gone, Silk still hears and is enthralled by Seawrack's song. Kinda creepy, especially given his continued devotion to Hyacinth.

-Rostrum

2002-07-05 Christopher Culver #

Argues that Nettle leaves Babbie for the Whorl because she believes Horn is dead and her children are grown, and reads the final scene of Return to the Whorl as a Harrowing of Hell. Responds to Stephen Case's view that Nettle seeks Horn, and questions why Seawrack would join a journey with Silk who no longer has Horn's personality.
disputes · allegory-and-typology · theology-and-religion · theories: Final scene of Return to the Whorl is a Harrowing of Hell; Nettle knows Horn is dead and returns home

I think Nettle is leaving for the opposite reason, that she knows that Horn is dead, her children are now full grown, and she has no reason to remain on an isolated island near crime-ridden New Viron. Thus she returns to the Whorl, which apparently still seems her true home to her.

And of course the final scene of RttW could be scene as a representation of the Harrowing of Hell, the medieval notion that in his three days of death Christ entered Limbo and freed the Old Testament righteous.

For me, the most annoying issue concerning the group's departure for the Whorl is the inclusion of Seawrack. While Silk had some of Horn's memories and wanted to see her again enough to learn to call her from Scylla, I don't understand while Seawrack wishes to depart for a strange new place with a person she doesn't even know, and who possesses no longer the personality she loved three years before.

Christopher Culver
