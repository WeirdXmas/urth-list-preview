The thread: Readers of the Latro books debated the mysteries left open in the series, with the thread centred on what Sidon will reveal. Matthew DeLuca argued that the prophecy about the God Unseen and the dead city is fulfilled in Sidon, while Rex Lycanthrosaurus countered that Hades, whose name means unseen, is the likelier answer. Thalassocrat listed open questions from earlier volumes, including the Earth Mother's favour and who won at the end of Arete, and later proposed a divine genealogy for Latro that traces the sphinx's claim to descent from Aeneas and Gaea. Michael Straight offered a separate reading that the Earth Mother keeps Latro for a use and that Aphrodite's gift is lasting love. The thread ended without resolution, with competing readings left open.
  • Matthew DeLuca: the God Unseen prophecy is fulfilled in Sidon's undead city
  • Rex Lycanthrosaurus: the Unseen God is Hades, Latro is nearing the underworld
  • thalassocrat: lists unanswered questions about the Unseen God, the Earth Mother and Aphrodite
  • Michael Straight: the Earth Mother keeps Latro for her own use; Aphrodite's gift is lasting love for Pharetra
  • thalassocrat: the sphinx's claim reflects Latro's descent from Aeneas and Gaea

Themes: Myth and folklore sources, Gods, angels and the Increate, Memory and forgetting

Well like right in the middle of Sidon, Latro goes to the Undead city and it's probably one of the best scenes (very American Gods-ish) in the book so far. Suffice it to say, this prophecy does take place in Sidon.

2006-11-14 Rex Lycanthrosaurus replying to thalassocrat #

Disputes the quoted suggestion about the Unseen God, arguing instead that Hades, whose name means unseen and who rules the sunless underworld, is the likely answer. Grounds this in the Latro books: Dis Pater is met only after death, and the final chapter of Soldier of Sidon, with its boatman and Cerberus-like beast, suggests Latro is nearing that realm.
disputes · source-and-allusion · myth-and-structure · theories: The Unseen God is Hades

Not sure I agree, since the name Hades means "unseen" and he's king of the underworld, where there is no sunlight--plus Dis Pater is never encountered until we're dead.

The very last chapter of _Sidon_ with its boatman and Cerberus-analog (besides reinforcing the Latro-as-Heracles subtheme) seems to indicate Latro is close to the particular realm in question.

2006-11-14 thalassocrat #

Lists four open questions the poster hopes Sidon will answer: the identity of the Unseen God, why the Earth Mother favours Latro, what Aphrodite made him say or do in Kalleos' garden, and who won at the end of Arete. Grounds each question in quoted lines from Soldier of Mist and Soldier of Arete, such as the Maiden's remark that the Unseen God wanes and the second sun over Cybele.
questions · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: the Unseen God is the true God above the gods

Sidon should arrive here in a couple of days, and I've been re- reading Mist & Arete in preparation.

Mainly I'm hoping for a good story well told, but I'm also hoping that there'll be some answers to a few questions the first two books left hanging for me.

1. The Unseen God

"I send you to the God Unseen!
Whose temple lies in Death's terrene!
Then you shall learn why He's not seen."

and : "Long after, you will find what you seek in the dead city."

I assume that "the God Unseen" is the really truly God or at least something further up the chain of comamnd than the gods encountered so far.

Google says that "terrene" means "belonging to this world, not heavenly" so I guess "Death's terrene" = "the dead city", a real place, maybe in Egypt? (I hope we're not going to get some kind of identification of Akhenaten's Aten with the God of Moses, but I'm nervous ...)

We get a bit more on the Unseen God. Outside Sestos, the Maiden tells Latro that the gods still meddle in the affairs of men: "Not often now, but the Unseen God wanes, and we are no longer lost in his light. We will never be wholly gone." Be good to get some kind of explanation for that.

Which brings me on to ...

2. Why does Latro end up as a favorite (apparently) of the Earth Mother after starting out (apparently) cursed by her?

The scene with her as Cybele in Thrace is interesting. It appeared to Latro "that a second sun rose behind her ... a splendid light enfolding her in a mantle brighter than the purest gold." He harps on this, so maybe it's more than just a god-ish aura; maybe the sign of the God Unseen in whose light Cybele is illuminated but not lost - referring back to the Maiden's comments.

Presumably all are working to further the designs of the Unseen God, whether or not they know it. In the story so far, Latro has been a pawn in what seems like a rather petty squabble between goddesses, but maybe in Sidon there will be more hints of a Larger Design.

3. What did Aphrodite hypnotize Latro to say or do or believe in Kalleos' garden?

4. Who actually won at the end of Arete - Diana, the Mother, both?

2006-12-01 Michael Straight replying to thalassocrat #

Proposes that the Earth Mother keeps Latro because she finds a use for him, not because she lifts her curse, and that Aphrodite's gift is the lasting love Latro retains for Pharetra. Grounds this in Aphrodite's promise that Latro will not forget her, read as love, and in Latro's emotional response to Pharetra's name despite his amnesia.
proposes · myth-and-structure · close-textual-reading · theories: Aphrodite's gift is Latro's remembered love for Pharetra; Earth Mother keeps Latro for her own use

I think it's just that she's decided she has a use for him. It's not like she revokes her curse and heals him.

Aphrodite=love. She says Latro will not forget her=will not forget love. One of the few things Latro seems to remember is his love for Pharetra (he gets choked up hearing her name even when he hasn't read about her that day). Maybe this is the gift Aphrodite gave him with her visit?

2006-12-02 thalassocrat replying to thalassocrat #

Proposes that the sphinx's claim to be Latro's mother and grandmother reflects his descent from Aeneas, son of Aphrodite, and a chain of divine ancestry running back to Gaea. Supports this with mythological genealogies of Aphrodite, Dione, Mars, Juno and the Amazons, and with an incident in Sidon where Aphrodite seems to intervene again.
proposes · myth-and-structure · source-and-allusion · theories: Sphinx is Latro's mother and grandmother via Aphrodite and Gaea; Latro descends from Aeneas and is a son of Mars

On Fri, 01 Dec 2006 19:25:45 +1100 Michael Straight

Could be. But by the time he encounters her in sphinx-form, she's instructing him in a perhaps motherly way; apparently guarding his memory palace; and actually declaring that she's his mother.

And also his mother's mother.

Here's my guess at what that's supposed to mean. As a Roman, Latro is perhaps a descendant of Aeneas. Aeneas was the son of Aphrodite/Venus. This might make Latro a "son of Aphrodite/Venus" in the same way that the Amazons are "daughters of Ares/Mars".

In Homer & elsewhere, Aphrodite is the daughter of Dione, who seems to be a great mother goddess, another aspect of Gaea. So that could make Gaea Latro's "mother's mother".

If Latro is himself supposed to be some kind of aspect of Mars/Ares, that also makes him a "son of Juno/Hera", arguably another aspect of Gaea.

(Latro is at least a "son of Mars", like the Amazons - Mars was the father of Romulus, the other great Roman ancestor, and Romans often referred to themselves in this way. Maybe this explains why he & Amazons get on so well together, as well as injecting some Wolfean quasi-incest into the story.)

It could be. At any rate, there's a little incident in Sidon which hints at Aphrodite gently interfering once again, maybe in the same kind of way ...
