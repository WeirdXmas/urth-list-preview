RTW: Hoof and Horn made this bit up

Urth list · 1 messages · 2002-02-16 to 2002-02-16

2002urthThe Book of the Short SunMichael Straight

Themes: Ghosts, dreams and visions

2002-02-16 Michael Straight #

Argues that the prophetic dream in RTW's third-person passages, which previews the pen case, Jahlee and Olivine's eye, was invented by Hoof and Hide rather than dreamed by Horn. Grounds this in the implausibility of the Narrator recounting dreams to them, and reads the rushed writing as Horn's spirit hurrying before fading.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Hoof and Hide invented the Silk dream; Dream is literary foreshadowing, not prophecy; Horn's spirit urges writing before fading

Near the beginning of RTW, "He" (the person whose adventures are chronicled in the third-person passages) has a dream that had me quite confused. I'm refering to the one He has the first night He and Pig sleep at Hound's house, when Hound and Pig talk about how they think He is Silk after He has fallen asleep (p. 83).

It features several specific events that have not happened to Him yet: the black pen case, Jahlee as Krait's mother, Olivine's eye, the opening lines of OBW. At first I thought it must be a dream of Incanto's, taking place in the 1st person storyline, but no, it's definitely one of the third-person passages.

How can this be? Did Horn-in-Silk have prophetic dreams in the Whorl? Most of the other dreams in the book seem related to the Narrator's dream travelling, not prophesy, (there's also the dreams the Narrator has about Scylla which are a third thing). When the Rajan/Incanto writes about Jahlee, he never mentions having dreamt about her in the Whorl.

Then I realized that this part was written by Hoof and Hide. I have a hard enough time believing that the Narrator recounted whole conversations to them verbatim, much less that he told them in detail about all the dreams he had in the Whorl. So I'm guessing that they made up this dream, for "literary" reasons, forshadowing Silk-in-Pig, etc. Maybe its a way of putting into the narrative ideas that didn't fit elsewhere. For instance the parts of the dream about the Narrator first starting to write (including the first draft of the beginnin of OBW) could be based on the Narrator telling Hoof and Hide how he got started writing.

One of the most interesting passages in this dream, is at the beginning: "He...began to write furiously, conscious of how short--how terribly short--a time was left to him." Is this Horn's spirit, knowing that he only has a limited time in Silk's body, urging him to write before Horn's spirit fades away?

-Rostrum
