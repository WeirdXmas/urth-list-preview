tincture of Pas

Whorl list · 4 messages · 2001-04-03 to 2001-04-05

2001whorlThe Book of the Long SunDan ParmenterAdam StephanidesMichael Straight
The thread: Readers debated whether Horn, the narrator of The Urth of the New Sun, carries Silk's spirit or identity, and what Incanto's line 'Pas is here' means. Dan Parmenter proposed that Horn has carried something of Silk since Lake of the Long Sun, with a download-and-alloy theory and a timeline of which spirit entered which body that is hard to pin down. Michael Straight, after recanting an earlier view, argued the narrator is merely human Silk with Horn's spirit, with no evidence that Pas or the Silkgod was downloaded, and built on vizcacha's reading that Pas now means Pas plus Silk. Adam Stephanides objected that Pas being linked to divine Silk does not put Pas in human Silk, and that Horn would not say so unless he consciously knew he was Silk. Straight then argued that Incanto's Pas is Silk's presence rather than any Typhon downloaded into Incanto. The thread ended without resolution, with
  • Dan Parmenter: Horn carries something of Silk since Lake of the Long Sun, with a download-and-alloy theory
  • Michael Straight: narrator is human Silk with Horn's spirit; 'Pas is here' means Silk's presence, not a download
  • Adam Stephanides: Pas linked to divine Silk does not place Pas in human Silk; Horn would have to consciously know he is Silk

Themes: Doubles, twins and clones, Hidden identities and parentage, Gods, angels and the Increate

2001-04-03 Dan Parmenter #

Argues that Horn has carried something of Silk since Lake of the Long Sun, where he mimics Silk, and that the timeline of which spirit entered which body is hard to pin down. Grounds this in the precedent of Severian and asks where in the text a Gaonese prophet predicts Horn will lead three cities to victory, which Horn calls a charlatan.
proposes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Horn has something of Silk in him; Horn is the least reliable Wolfe narrator

From: Adam Stephanides <[email]>

I don't know if it's so cut-and-dried as all that. I guess I'm on the side that takes note that in a sense, Horn has had something of Silk in him since LAKE OF THE LONG SUN, where he mimics Silk. Remember, those who imitate the gods may start to become them. It's very difficult for me to say when each spirit entered another body and when it became aware of it. One of these days I, or someone (probably Mantis!) will come up with a Short Sun timeline that make help make some sense of what happened when.

That seems par for the course, with the precedent of Severian, even if as you point out earlier, it's not quite the same.

Shellac

p.s. Can anyone find the place in the text (I'm pretty sure it's OBW) where a prophet (Gaonese I think) predicts that Horn will lead three cities to victory? Horn denounces him as a charalatan (!) I thought that this was very significant the second time I read the series as further proof of Horn's status as possibly the least reliable Wolfe narrator of all, but I can't seem to find the place in the text to triple check on it!

2001-04-04 Michael Straight #

Recants an earlier view and argues that the narrator is merely human Silk with Horn's spirit, with no evidence that Pas, the Silkgod, or Pigsilk were downloaded into Silk's body. Builds on vizcacha's reading of Incanto's line that Pas is present in a sense, since Pas now refers to Pas plus Silk, and rejects Dan Parmenter's download-and-alloy theory.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Narrator is human Silk with Horn's spirit; No download of Pas or Silkgod into Silk

Too late! I recant. I'm convinced that vizcacha's interpretation is correct: Incanto means that since "Pas" now refers to Pas+Silk that "Pas" is present because Silk is present (I guess this is sufficiently eliptical that Incanto can say "Pas is here, in a sense" without admiting to himself that he is Silk). So I go back to my original assertion there is no evidence that Pas or the Silkgod or Pigsilk have been downloaded into Silk's body. The narrator is just human Silk + Horn's spirit.

-Rostrum

2001-04-04 Adam Stephanides #

Disputes Michael Straight's recanted view that Incanto's line 'Pas is here' means Pas is present because Silk is present, arguing that Pas being linked to the divine Silk does not put Pas in the human Silk. Argues that even if Pas were present, Horn would not say it unless he consciously recognized he was Silk, so the reading fails.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Pas is present in Silk because Silk is present

I'd like to be able to agree with this, since I don't believe Silk-in-Pig has been downloaded into Silk's body either. But I can't. Just because the divine Silk is linked with Pas, it doesn't follow that Pas is present in the human Silk. And even if it did, I don't think Horn would say "Pas is here" based upon Pas's presence in the human Silk unless he consciously recognized that he was Silk.

--Adam

2001-04-05 Michael Straight #

Argues that the figure called Pas on the Whorl is now principally Silk, so Incanto's remark that Pas is present in a sense means Silk's presence, not any Typhon downloaded into Incanto. Responds to Michael Straight's claim that Incanto's words show Silk-in-Pig was not downloaded, by restating vizcacha's reading and insisting Incanto is wholly non-Typhon.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Pas is now principally Silk; Incanto is not Typhon

You misunderstand. Vizcacha is not saying that Incanto has any of Typhon downloaded into him. Incanto is 0% Typhon. But "Pas," as he exists now on the Whorl, is at least partially and perhaps principally Silk. So if the human Silk is present, then Pas is present in a sense because Pas is now based on the human Silk.

Thus the first part of the quoted passage, where Incanto says that Pas doesn't exist on the whorl anymore either. The original Pas who was equal to Typhon is gone. The new Pas is Passilk.

-Rostrum
