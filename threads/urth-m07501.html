2009-04-02 John Watkins replying to Son of Witz #

Argues that Wolfe's political passage about monarchy is an allusion to C.S. Lewis, where political and theological hierarchies are inverted like Dante's cosmos, so loyalty to a monarch's person is theology while loyalty to principles is politics. Supports this by citing Lewis's The Discarded Image and The Great Divorce and linking it to Oreb's view of Silk as a god in The Book of the Long Sun, responding to John Watkins's reading.
supports · allegory-and-typology · source-and-allusion · theories: Wolfe's monarchy passage alludes to C.S. Lewis; Loyalty to monarch is good theology bad politics

I think it's the latter. Another Lewis bit that comes to mind is his point (in *The Discarded Image*) that the physical arrangement of Dante's cosmos (with Satan in the center and the Empyrean at the periphery) is the inverse, as though in a mirror, of the metaphysical reality of the cosmos (in which God is centrally important and Satan is the universe's least significant being.) Lewis uses the idea in *The Great Divorce *as well when he describes the Harrowing of Hell in terms of Christ "shrinking" himself into something tiny and seemingly insignificant to enter into the infintesimal portion of Creation that is Hell.

Anyway, if I'm right about the connection, I think what Wolfe is doing here is suggesting that the "highest" level of political governance might be the "lowest" level of theological--that is, loyalty to the abstract principles of good and evil is good politcs and bad theoloy, but loyalty to the person of the monarch is good theology but bad politics. The passages doesn't come out and say that, of course, but it seems to me to be the allusion.

Also, I know you're fond of the bit in Long Sun when NarratorHorn portrays Oreb as viewing Silk as his god--that, it seems to me, is an elaboration on the bit about Triskele here. The loyalty of an animal to its master is more akin to the loyalty of a worshipper to God than it is to the loyalty of a citizen to the state (at least, so we hope!)

2009-04-02 Son of Witz #

Asks whether Wolfe presents monarchy as a practical political solution or as a theological metaphor, questioning why Malrubius asks the future Autarch about governance. Builds on John Watkins's reading that monarchy works only if the monarch is God, and his C.S. Lewis parallel, by weighing Severian's shift toward the Increate against his political role and the Democracy answer.
questions · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: Malrubius's question about governance is a theological metaphor for orienting the ruler to God

I like this Lewis quote.
that's my point. Is Wolfe positing this as a practical solution. I agree that it couldn't work with out the monarch being essentially god. which is why I wonder why Malrubius is asking this of the future Autarch. I understand Severian is in a process of revoking false allegiance and oaths, and orienting himself to the Increate, but that's theology, not politics. Severian will also be the political ruler, and his first answer is the Democracy, but it's as if Malrubius's switch to theological perspective is intended to point out that the governance should be thought of in those terms.

OR, is this Malrubius's way of using this theological metaphor as a way of telling this future ruler to orient himself to God so that he can rule best.
