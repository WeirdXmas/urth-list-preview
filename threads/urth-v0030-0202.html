Proposes that Mantis's Shadow Children are Wolfe's reworking of the Fair Folk and changeling motif, a faerie-tale pattern given a science-fictional rationale, and that they are remnants of a race older than humanity. Corrects the etymology of 'a-maston' to 'without breast', not 'lacking', and jokes that the name suggests its founders were bottle-fed.
proposes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Shadow Children are the Fair Folk; Shadow Children are remnants of an older race; a-maston means without breast

[...]

Actually, Mantis' list of Shadow-child characteristics immediately makes me think of the Fair Folk. Given Wolfe's penchant for reimagining fantasy, and especially faerie-tale, tropes in a plausible-enough-for-SF-"realism" form, I'd almost bank on it.

That's certainly indicative; some folks in Eire _still_ leave a bowl of milk out for the Fair Folk.

Leaving the question of elders aside, this is the one that made my Roach-sense go BING! BING! BING! -- Wolfe's potschke'd with the changeling motif more than once before.

Weird but not alien to this demi-theory: the FF seem to have a different awareness from ours.

These two seem to qualify as stfnal justifications of the one before.

Again, a stfnal version of the idea that the FF are a race much older than humans, and what we see (when we do) are the last remnants.
