The thread: Readers debated whose body Severian occupies after his fall from the sailship and whether that body wrote the early books of The Book of the New Sun. Roy Lackey argued Tzadkiel built a new aquastor body, while David Stockhoff said the text leaves it open, and Son of Witz and Lane Haygood offered rival accounts involving Gyoll, eidolons, and Apu-Punchau. Jordon Flato pushed a resurrected-eidolon loop reading, and Roy later argued Severian is a petty god unlike Christ, which drew objections from brunians and John Watkins. The thread ended in several unresolved lines, with the Christ comparison and the aquastor question still open.
  • Roy C. Lackey: Tzadkiel made a new aquastor body on the Ship, which later became the Conciliator; Severian is a petty god, not a Christ figure
  • David Stockhoff: the text leaves open whose body Severian takes; the autarchs are failed messiah candidates
  • Jordon Flato: Severian is resurrected as an eidolon in a time loop, kept alive by God's mind
  • Lane Haygood: Severian is an intentional aquastor whose consciousness is not tied to the brain; Gyoll death explains his necessity
  • Son of Witz: Severian died and was resurrected in Gyoll; the Hierodules saw his potential to pass the Trial
  • brunians: Severian's resurrections are less unique than Jesus's, comparable to Elijah and Lazarus

Themes: Death and resurrection, Christ figures and messiahs, Robots, androids and artificial life

2008-12-02 Lane Haygood replying to Son of Witz #

Explains the difference between eidolon and aquastor by tracing their origins, Platonic Forms versus Paracelsus's thought-formed beings, and argues Severian is an intentional being whose self-consciousness is not tied to the brain. Responds to Son of Witz's question about where consciousness lies, citing Severian's absorption of Thecla's memory and personality without her consciousness.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theology-and-religion · theories: Severian is an aquastor, an intentional being formed of thought; Severian absorbs Thecla's memory but not her consciousness

The etymology of these words shall help.

Eidolon is a Greek philosophical term, denoting the abstract reality of an idea. For Plato, these are the universal Forms, the perfect instantiations of a given idea that all particular instances of are imperfect emanations. Aquastor comes from Paracelsus, an entity formed from thought alone. So an Eidolon is a projection from a higher world (the Platonic world of ideals/Yesod) to a lower world (the physical world/Briah). An aquastor would be a created being formed of pure thought (intention).

It's also important to note that Severian only absorbs some of Thecla's memory and personality, but not her consciousness itself. While these things may be in some way linked to the brain, the sense of identity, the self-consciousness and transcendental unity of apperception, is not, and thus, Severian-as-intentional-being never experiences a disconnect between his death(s) and rebirth(s).

Lane

Yes, which is why I said what I said. Tzadkiel is the Hierogrammate who created the new body on the Ship. There were two bodies on the Ship. The dead one had written the first four books. The new aquastor body Tzadkiel made went on to become the Conciliator and Apu. After Apu got older and was strangled, B, F & O created a new body in the tomb, which is why there were two bodies in that tomb. The newly created body went on to write the fifth book. The dead Apu also came back to life when the first faint light of the white fountain reached Urth that night Sev fled the tomb, but that's another issue.

Btw, aquastor and eidolon are interchangeable in the Urth Cycle. The former is the more formal term, the latter is the common, just as Increate is the learned term for what the less educated call God.

-Roy

Argues that Severian's resurrections differ from Christ's because they are revivals or new bodies given an Aristotelian form, not a true death and resurrection with a descent into Hades. disputes · theology-and-religion · allegory-and-typology · theories: Severian's resurrections are not true Christlike resurrections

2008-12-04 Matthew King replying to Jeff Wilson #

Argues that the common natural/spiritual translation of Paul's psuchikon and pneumatikon in 1 Corinthians 15 is misleading, proposing psuche as life-force rather than soul or a natural-spiritual contrast. Supports this with Greek word roots and the observation that phusike, the Greek for natural, survives in English 'physical', and links it to Jeff Wilson's question about the imperishable attribute.
disputes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading

http://www.gnpcb.org/esv/search/?q=1+Corinthians+15:42-49

The natural/spiritual dichotomy is misleading, and a result of troubled translation--though there aren't any major translations that get it right. Some even call it physical/spiritual.
http://bible.cc/1_corinthians/15-44.htm

The words involved are psuchikon and pneumatikon, deriving from psuche and pneuma. Pneuma is "spirit", but psuche is usually translated as "soul" or sometimes as "being". The idea of "life-force" isn't far off. There's a perfectly good Greek word meaning "natural": phusike, which has descended to us in "physical".
