5HC Strong Evidence of Telepathy?

Urth list · 3 messages · 2002-04-26 to 2002-05-03

2002urthJerry FriedmanAdam StephanidesMichael Andre-Driussi
The thread: Michael Andre-Driussi proposed that Number Five and V.R.T. in The Fifth Head of Cerberus are telepathically linked, citing the shared Gondwanaland coinage, the mind-singing passage, and parallel dreams of berserk robots and fratricide, suggesting A Story retells V.R.T.'s life. Adam Stephanides floated a more radical reading in which Number Five wrote all three parts, including V.R.T.'s section, as fiction, while admitting he did not believe it. Jerry Friedman disputed the robot parallel, noting that in the book one robot goes berserk and kills many prisoners while V.R.T. dreams of several non-berserk robots killing him, though he thanked the poster for the resemblance. The thread ended after three posts with the telepathy claim unsupported and no agreement reached.
  • Michael Andre-Driussi: Number Five and V.R.T. are telepathically linked; A Story retells V.R.T.'s life
  • Adam Stephanides: Number Five wrote all three parts, including V.R.T.'s section, as disguised fiction (not his real belief)
  • Jerry Friedman: the robot dream parallel does not hold; the scenes differ in key details

Themes: Unreliable narration, Doubles, twins and clones, Memory and forgetting

2002-04-26 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Proposes telepathic linkage between Number 5 and V.R.T. in The Fifth Head of Cerberus, since the name Gondwanaland seems coined by Number 5 at a specific library lesson. Cites the Old Wise One's mind-singing passage and parallel dreams of berserk robots and brothers killing each other to suggest A Story retells V.R.T.'s life, replying to Tony Ellis.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Number 5 and V.R.T. are telepathically linked; Gondwanaland name coined by Number 5 in library; A Story retells V.R.T.'s life prior to prison

Leaving aside the issues of shape-changing (yes/no), and abo existence (yes/no), one thing I think this quote does point to rather strongly is telepathy in general and the weird psychic linkage between Number 5 and V.R.T.

Because while nearly all of the other names could plausibly be plucked out of passing starships over time, Gondwanaland seems to be coined by Number 5 in his lesson at the library, on a very specific day.

Does this mean that the moment when Number 5 is saying it out loud is simultaneous to the moment when OWO is saying it? (Not in the "distant Annese past" sense of "A Story," but in one of the other senses: for example, the "the story is a disguised version of V.R.T.'s real life and crimes" sense, where it might thereby point to V.R.T. being in some kind of sandpit/prison at the time when Number 5 was in the library.) Maybe, maybe not.

Furthermore, this detail adds to the other strange confusions between V.R.T. and Number 5: two others include the fact that Number 5 sees robot guards gone berserk, killing prisoners in the third year of his sentence (Ace pb p. 70), and V.R.T. had a dream of robots going berserk, killing him (210); Number 5 has dream of court fenced with pillars, which echoes the woodhenge observatory on Sainte Anne seen by Marsch, shown by V.R.T.

One brother who dreams the life and trials of the other brother, this is a big element of "A Story." And when they finally meet, one brother kills the other; and Number 5 may have set in motion V.R.T.'s arrest when he denounced him as an abo spy (that is: what if "A Story" is retelling V.R.T.'s life prior to prison).

2002-05-03 Adam Stephanides #

Proposes that Number Five wrote all three parts of the 5HC narrative, including the V.R.T. section, as fiction in disguise, explaining the echoes between V.R.T. and Number Five. Grounds this in his stated aim to disclose himself to himself and in the satirical targets it would serve, while admitting he does not believe it.
proposes · close-textual-reading · author-and-biography · theories: Number Five wrote all three parts of the narrative

There is another possible explanation for the strange echoes between V.R.T. and Number Five: that Number Five has written all three parts, not just the first part. He wrote his autobiography "to disclose myself to myself"; he might very well have retold his story in fictional guise for the same reason. (And if one Gene Wolfe can write fiction, so can another.) And it would no doubt have amused him to portray Marsch as being in reality what he had accused him of being to insult him and drive him away; to show the Port Mimizon beauracracy as slipshod and inefficient; and to show the museum at Roncevaux, and by extension the library at Port Mimizon, as gullible (Trenchard's fake artifacts).

I don't really believe this theory, but I don't think it can be disproven either, at least not through internal evidence.

--Adam

2002-05-03 Jerry Friedman #

Disputes Michael Andre-Driussi's claimed parallel between The Fifth Head of Cerberus's Number Five and V.R.T., arguing the robot scenes differ: one robot goes berserk and kills many prisoners, while V.R.T. dreams of several non-berserk robots killing him. Grounds the objection in the poster's recollection of the book's events and thanks the poster for pointing out a resemblance he had not noticed.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Number Five and V.R.T. share a robot-massacre parallel

They're not quite that similar, if I remember correctly. Number Five sees one robot go berserk and kill a number (15?) of prisoners; V. R. T. dreams of several non-berserk robots killing him. But I don't think I'd have noticed the resemblance at all, so thanks! ...

Jerry Friedman
