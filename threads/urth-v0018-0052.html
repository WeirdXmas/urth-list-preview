1998-08-28 Kieran Mullen #

Agrees with Peter Grimes that Robert Anton Wilson's Illuminatus is poor writing and that comparing it to Wolfe is painful. Argues Foucault's Pendulum by Eco suffers from poor editing yet is a brilliant critique of postmodernism, pointing to the ectoplasm in the church as an overlooked supernatural twist.
supports · theories: Foucault's Pendulum ends with a genuine supernatural reality

Very well put and very true. It is painful to see Wolfe and Wilson compared.

I think FP suffers from poor editing. Eco was too big a writer to have his prose altered, I suspect. You could cut a fair bit of the middle bit out.

That said, it's a brilliant criticism of Postmodernism, and a wonderful book. The fact that there _is_ a supernatural reality (evidenced by the ectoplasm manifested in the church) is a oft overlooked sly twist.

Kieran Mullen

1998-08-28 Jack Lyons #

Jokes about comparing Robert Anton Wilson's Illuminatus with Wolfe, calling a rubber chicken versus a Rodin sculpture, and suggesting a Trinity link among three books. Responds to Kieran Mullen's criticism of Illuminatus's writing and its treatment of women, defending Wilson's portrayal of Portinari and Eco's Foucault's Pendulum as playful.
chats

:>Ugh, I just can't let this go by unremarked. Illuminatus is garbage :>writing... [Stuff
:> Deleted] .... Robert Anton Wilson and :>his co-conspirator are just not terribly good writers. :
: Very well put and very true. It is painful to see Wolfe and Wilson :compared.

Well, it's like comparing a rubber chicken to a Rodin
scuplture. Both do have their uses, and I enjoy them both, though rarely together.

As far as women in Wilson's book go - I seem to recall that Miss Portinari wasn't a sex object, and in fact had the
mantle of Leader of the Illuminatus abdicated to her in the end, no small feat. I should also note that while nearly all of the women in ILLUMINATUS are indeed caricatured as sex dolls, the men get far worse treatment as dolts and morons. Take your pick which offends you more.

I treat Focault's Pendulum as an entertaining prose history book, a mixture of book types in the same way MAUS mixed history and comics. Eco was having *fun*. Lighten up. :)

Of course now that we have mentioned *three* books we can posit a Trinity connection...perhaps the rubber chicken
represents the Host...

1998-08-28 Michael Straight #

Disputes Kieran Mullen's claim that Foucault's Pendulum suffers from poor editing and could lose its middle section. Argues that the long passages on the conspiracies are meant to feel interminable, comparing their repetition to Ravel's Bolero.
disputes · genre-and-craft

Absolutely not. That's like saying you could cut out a bunch of those repeated phrases in Ravel's Bolero. The fact that the long sections in the middle about all the conspiracies is interminable is part of the point.

-Rostrum
