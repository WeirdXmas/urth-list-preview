Typhon's Mission

Whorl list · 3 messages · 1999-07-01 to 1999-07-02

1999whorlThe Book of the Long SunMichael Andre-DriussiJosh SmithMichael Straight
The thread: Readers asked whether Typhon sent the Whorl ship to find a new home or to supply humans as food for the inhumi. Josh Smith argued for the food-supply reading, citing Quetzal's role and an earlier post. Michael Andre-Driussi held that Typhon launched the ship to found a colony for himself, with the ship already orbiting its target star for a century or two. Michael Straight argued that Typhon sent a conquering army rather than livestock, reading the robot soldiers and Pas's children as evidence. The thread ends with three competing readings and no agreement.
  • Josh Smith: Typhon or Pas sold humans to the inhumi as a food supply, based on Quetzal's presence and an earlier post
  • Michael Andre-Driussi: Typhon sent the ship to found a personal colony, orbiting the target star for 100 to 200 years, with Quetzal boarding after swimming thro
  • Michael Straight: Typhon sent a conquering army, since a ruler would not be content with livestock; robot soldiers and Pas's children were meant to possess bodi

Themes: Power, rulers and politics, Colonization and the native, Robots, androids and artificial life

1999-07-01 Josh Smith #

Asks whether Typhon sent the Whorl ship to find a new home or sold the humans to the inhumi as food, and proposes that Typhon or Pas sold the Cargo as a food supply. Grounds this in Quetzal's presence on the Whorl and his role as Prolocutor, and quotes an earlier list post with the same supply-ship idea.
proposes · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Typhon and Pas sold the Cargo to the inhumi as food; Whorl is a supply ship for the inhumi; Typhon was an inhumi scout sent to conquer planets

Hello All,

I'm new to the list, just finished Exodus last night,
my heart is racing, and my mind is reeling about one
issue: did Typhon send the ship to search for a new
home (sort of the "default setting" for any big space
exodus story), or did he sell out the humans to the
inhumi (or literally sell them as food)? - By the way,
I think that the act of springing a dilemma of that
order on us in the last few pages constitutes one of
the most startling scope-shifts in literary history.

I raced through the archives of the list and found
this before my eyes just got too tired: (if you've
spent more time on the "Cargo" issue, and solved
everything by now, I apologize for the waste of time)

"One more explanation: I'm of the opinion that the
Whorl is a supply ship,
that the Cargo, which consists of the dregs of Urth
society, was sold to
the inhumi by Pas/Typhon as a food supply. Q was in
effect a "shepherd,"
but over time he turned into something of a Good
Shepherd, against his own
nature. I don't expect everyone to agree however
(continued butting up
against mantis has made me philosophical)."

(I hope that shows up okay onscreen, I'm on vacation
and writing via yahoo mail).

If Typhon was just sending out humans to the nearest
inhabitable (colonizable) planet(s) to find some new
place to live, and that place happened to be teaming
with vampiric inhumi, then Quetzal was either (a) a
stowaway from Typhon's planet and there had already
been Earth/Green commutes, or (b) a recent addition to
the Whorl who came in from outside, which seems absurd
since he's been the Prolocutor for decades.

The dilemma of (a) Quetzal is on the Whorl + (b) the
Whorl is bound for the inhumi planet + (c) the Whorl
was built by Typhon/Pas seems like it can only resolve
with Typhon having built the Whorl to sell to the
inhumi, who perhaps were running out of food. Again,
you could suppose that Quetzal just redirected the
Whorl toward his homeworld, but his very existance on
the Whorl implies that the inhumi were somehow
involved in its origins.

I won't get into the complicated "knot" of Pas vs. his
Children vs. the Prolocutor unless and until people
show interest in the Typhon/Inhumi complicity
possibilities - i.e. a human Q would have tried to
prevent theophanies because he feared a breakdown in
power or even a revolution once the people of Viron
heard about the inability to find the new calde,
figured out that they were being ruled illegally, plus
hearing of Pas' "death" would be disheartening to say
the least. An inhumi Quetzal however, is primarily
motivated by getting the Cargo to Green. He therefore
requires that the Plan of Pas be followed. So don't
let anyone know Pas is dead lest they get other ideas
in their heads and start listening to the other gods.
Then again, it seemed that Tartaros was equally bent
on seeing it through. Oops, I get I did get into it
after all.

Maybe Tyhon himself was part of a legion of inhumi
scouts sent out millenia ago to conquer distant
planets, form empires and ship sustainable
"agriculture" back to Green. That would be quite an
undertaking.

Sorry if these thoughts are less than cohesive. I'm
still trembling from that incredible finish.

1999-07-01 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Argues that Typhon launched the ship to found a colony for himself, that the ship has orbited the target star for maybe 100 to 200 years, and that Quetzal boarded only after arriving by swimming through space. Grounds this in Gene Wolfe's remarks about inhumi swimming through space, the butterfly woman of the Book of the New Sun, and Pas's planned hundred-year timeframe (II, p. 235).
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · author-and-biography · theories: Typhon launched the ship to found his own colony; Ship orbited target star for 100 to 200 years; Quetzal boarded after swimming through space

Hello there Josh Smith, and welcome aboard!

FWIW (and that ain't much, since the new trilogy is about to begin and it will probably give some new information), I think that:

1) Typhon launched the ship to make a colony by, of, and for Typhon (the bright side of Typhon's egomania is the refusal to be pizza boy to a planet of vampire <g>).

2) The ship has been orbiting the target star for decades--maybe even 100 or 200 years.

3) Quetzal came onboard the ship =after= it arrived in his native star system, not before. (And he arrived by swimming through space--long ago, on the _Queen Mary_, Gene Wolfe said some things to me that made it seem likely that the inhumi [I'm forgetting my conjugation here] actually swim through space, like the butterfly woman of TBOTNS, or like many of Lovecraft's critters.) If the ship has been orbiting for 100 years (the end of Pas's planned time was one hundred years ago [II, p. 235]) then this gives plenty of time for Q to come aboard and have his long career; if it has only been orbiting for decades, then there are problems.

=mantis=

1999-07-02 Michael Straight #

Argues that Typhon sent a conquering army rather than cargo meant as cattle for the inhumani, since a ruler like Typhon would not be content to rule someone else's livestock. Grounds this in the robot soldiers being inedible and in Pas and his children being set up to possess bodies on arrival, and reads Tartaros's help as motivated by the Whorl's decline rather than Pas's plan.
proposes · close-textual-reading · myth-and-structure · theories: Typhon sent a conquering army not cattle

One problem with the cargo=cattle idea:

Even if you imagine that the cargo are being sent to set up a "real" colony which will then be harvested in a "sustainable" manner by the inhumani, you have the problem of why Typhon would go with them. Pas and his children seem to be set up to take on bodies via posession (probably those special embryos) upon arrival and rule. But I can't imagine Typhon being content to rule someone else's cattle.

Maybe the inhumani *think* they're getting a shipment of cattle, but Typhon sent a conquering army instead! All those robot soldiers sure aren't very edible.

Also, I don't think Tartaros is helping with the disembarkment effort because he's following the plan of Pas or because he wants to deliver cargo to the inhumani. I think he just knows that the Whorl is running down and if they stay they're all gonna die.

-Rostrum
