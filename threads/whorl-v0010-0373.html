prophecy in a novel, Q's coming for dinner

Themes: Ghosts, dreams and visions, Unreliable narration

2000-06-21 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Argues that The Book of the Long Sun is itself a novelistic construct, a historical novel by Horn meant to recapture his lost youth, and that the Short Sun books tension between realistic record and a larger religious seed reflects this. Also proposes that Q bit Pike and absorbed some of his essence, explaining why Pike and Q look alike, reasoning from what is now known about inhumu.
proposes · author-and-biography · close-textual-reading · theories: Pike's ghost is Q, who bit Pike; The Book of the Long Sun is a historical novel by Horn

Re: the prophecy thing. I'd like to point out that TBOTLS is a novelistic construct even within its own context. A historical novel written by Horn to capture the lost world of his youth; like Twain or Proust, if you will. More than usual, even with Wolfe: after all, this is part of the deep tension in the Short Sun series (or so it seems to me); the clash between the "realistic historical record" and something that seems (or is wanted to be) larger than that (the seed of a new religion, among other possibilities); the gap between talking the talk and walking the walk.

Re: Pike's ghost as Q. Not to give new life to this notion, but now that we know an awful lot more about inhumu and how they come to be like people, well then, it makes it seem (possible/probable/essential) as if Q bit Pike and got some of his essence (must be embarassing for an old codger to suddenly start skipping like a wee girl, after all); thus no surprise that Pike an Q look alike (more alike than, say, any two old white guys).

=mantis=

2000-06-21 Michael Straight #

Agrees with Michael Andre-Driussi that the prophecies and dreams in The Book of the Long Sun may be Horn's own embellishment, adding this to a list of explanations. Praises the ambiguity about Horn as storyteller and anticipates further development of this in On Blue's Waters.
supports · close-textual-reading · theories: Horn embellishes the dreams and prophecies

Good point. I left off "Horn embellishing" from my list of possible explanations for the dreams/prophesies. That whole ambiguity regarding Horn's role as the storyteller is one of my favorite things about the series. I gather there's more good stuff to come in OBW in that regard.

-Rostrum
