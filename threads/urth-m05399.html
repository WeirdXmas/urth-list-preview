The significance of Apu Punchau

Urth list · 16 messages · 2008-12-24 to 2008-12-31

2008urthThe Book of the New SunCraig BrewerPedro PereiraDavid StockhoffSon of WitzJeff WilsonDan'l Danehy-Oakes
The thread: The thread asked whether Apu Punchau's moon-landing image and the Urth setting mean Urth is a later phase of our Earth, a separate universe in the Long Sun cycle, or an analog. David Stockhoff first argued Apu Punchau is a solar incarnation linked to Severian, then rejected the later-universe and analog readings, citing the lunar module picture. Pedro Pereira argued most persistently that Urth is simply our Earth in the future, backed by Wolfe's interview remarks, while Dan'l Danehy-Oakes and Craig Brewer defended or qualified the Hiero-guided variations-on-a-theme view. Participants split over whether the picture is a photograph or a painting, with Son of Witz and Craig Brewer arguing for painting and Jeff Wilson questioning that. The thread ended in impasse, with the photograph's reality and the universe question left unresolved.
  • David Stockhoff: Apu Punchau is a solar incarnation tied to Severian, and the later-universe analog reading is unnecessary
  • Pedro Pereira: Urth is our Earth at a later time, not another universe, citing Wolfe's interviews
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: Each new universe's Earth is shaped by the Hieros and their servants steering evolution
  • Craig Brewer: The lunar picture may be a painting, and the Urth setting may be variations on one theme across universes
  • Son of Witz: The moon image is a painting, since photo paper would not survive about seventy-five years
  • Jeff Wilson: Painted realism like Holbein's could be mistaken for a photograph, so the painting argument is not proven

Themes: Myth and folklore sources, The dying sun and the cosmos, Deep time and ruins

2008-12-24 David Stockhoff #

Argues that Apu Punchau is a symbolic incarnation of the Old Sun and the Eternal, appearing in The Urth of the New Sun because of geographical proximity, and that Severian may be read as the same solar being. Grounds this in the Inca sun-god name and its meaning, the Stone Town episode, and comparisons with other solar deities such as Ahura Mazda and Christ.
proposes · source-and-allusion · allegory-and-typology · theories: Apu Punchau is the Old Sun incarnate; Severian is Apu Punchau and the Conciliator; Solar deities are one consciousness across incarnations

Witz:

So as far as plot is concerned, Apu Punchau is likely to be way in OUR past. It makes sense he would be the Inca sun god because of proximity, although really he should be ALL sun gods, which would make him very busy. (Like Santa Claus will be tonight.)

And if it's true that AP has no other plot role outside the Stone Town episode, then he is best understood symbolically, as you say: he is the Old Sun and just another incarnation or emanation of the Eternal. And that's why he's in TUotNS.

You'd think the various emanations would not need to "be" one another, so it is geographical proximity that leads to their merging. Had Severian been landed in Persia and bumped into Ahura Mazda, presumably he would have caused a proximity explosion then, and had he then run back to Zarathustra's time, he would be Ahura Mazda. Except the Persians burned or chopped up their dead, so that might not have worked out so well.

Plotwise, Apu Punchau is a sun god from ancient prehistory of Urth. Of Course, the name comes from the Incan "Apu Punchau", (aka "Inti"), "Head of Day" or "Leader of Day". He is a Sun God. He is The Sun.

On one hand, very simply, if this story is a parody or allegory for a Solar Redeemer, like Christ, or like the other Sun Gods, then that Severian is Apu Punchau & the Conciliator & the New Sun speaks to the idea that the various incarnations of Solar Deities are incarnating the same Being. One consciousness is literally every Solar God mentioned in the microcosm of the book. That works fairly well at a basic plot level, I suppose. That it maps to an Earth culture's Ancient Sun God is probably a metanarative device to indicate this. Of course, it speaks interestingly to the notion that Christ is analogue to the other solar gods prior to him.

2008-12-29 David Stockhoff replying to David Stockhoff #

Disputes the idea that Urth is Earth in a later universe or run, calling it unnecessary and unsupported and citing the lunar module photograph as the plainest problem. Responds to b sharp's claim that Urth is an earlier-universe analog of Earth lacking real Incas, Persians or Jesus, and rejects the analog framing.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Urth is Earth in a later universe

Well, that's one theory that has been raised---that Urth is in a "later" universe than Earth. The idea of Urth being a subsequent "run" is intriguing. But it's both unnecessary and unsupported.

Perhaps the plainest problem with this is the existence of the lunar module photograph.

2008-12-30 Jeff Wilson replying to David Stockhoff #

Argues that the painting described in The Fifth Head of Cerberus or related Wolfe material does not match any publicized Apollo 11 photograph, so it may be a different moon landing or heavily reworked art. Responds to David Stockhoff's claim that the lunar module photograph refutes a later-universe theory by noting Alan Bean's paintings began only in 1982.
disputes · source-and-allusion · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Urth is a later universe run

We worked out earlier in 2008 that the composition of the picture described does not match any of the publicized Apollo 11 photos. If it is literally a painting, the artist took considerable liberties or it is some other moon landing. Alan Bean does similar paintings, but did not begin until 1982.

proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Apu Punchau picture is a painting not a photograph

I haven't read all the replies yet, so forgive me if this is redundant. but I've always figured it was a painting of the moon photo for a couple of reasons.
A) Rudesind cleans paintings and would know the difference between the surfaces of a photo and a paining.
B) Photo paper has a range of about 75 years before it begins to really deteriorate. It's hard to imagine photos surviving.
