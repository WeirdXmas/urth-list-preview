the JCdeck; fairies

Urth list · 1 messages · 2000-05-07 to 2000-05-07

Themes: Myth and folklore sources, Stories within stories

2000-05-07 Adam Stephanides #

Argues that the John Crowley card deck in Little, Big is a variant of ordinary Tarot rather than a truer original, and that the Drinkwaters have become fixed fairy-tale figures rather than a new fairy race. Responds to mantis's claim that the JC deck is the original Tarot, citing the regular Tarot's explicit mention in Something Horrific and differences from The Deep's deck.
disputes · close-textual-reading · myth-and-structure · theories: Little, Big deck is a variant of Tarot; Drinkwaters are fixed in fairy-tale roles; fairy history is not cyclical

Not that similar (at least according to alga's essay); TD removes the numbers and suits from the "people" cards, and cuts down the number of trumps much further. Aside from the features they both derive from the Tarot, the only similarity is the number of people cards.

Well, the LB deck is clearly based upon Tarot; its suits have the same names as do the Tarot suits, and in both the trumps are numbered starting with zero. Of your other two alternatives, the first is more likely than the second, for reasons I gave earlier. But it seems most likely to me that the LB deck is simply a variation of the Tarot deck, neither "initial" or "final" necessarily, and not "truer" than ordinary Tarot in any metaphysical sense, though specially adapted for explicating the Tale (whether by their inherent nature or because the fairies have "supercharged" them).

Yes; the regular Tarot is referred to explicitly in "Something Horrific," II, 1.

It seems kind of pointless to argue with you about whether I disagree with alga. But since my position is apparently unclear, I'll go over it again. The Drinkwaters have become fairies, but there is no sign that the original fairies (whom I will just call "fairies" for convenience) look upon them as of the same race as themselves. The fairies' goal is not the production of new fairies, which is what "reproduction" means to me. Nor would I call the Drinkwater-occupied outermost fairy world a "colony" of the fairies; at any rate, the fairies will have no contact with it once they themselves go further "in."

My assertion that there was only one kind of fairy (as far as we knew) was meant to refer to before the Drinkwaters moved in, and was meant as an argument against the idea of this event as cyclical. The Drinkwaters become fairies too (though I was late seeing this); whether they are really "the same type" as the original fairies I don't know.

Yes, in the sense that it's not cyclical; no, in the sense of non-humans moving into fairyland.

I don't see this as taking place. My sense is that the Drinkwaters are fixed in the roles the fairies have placed them in, and will not change except to act out the fairy-tale adventures planned for them. As in ES, they have "become parables." (You can see I lean towards the negative view of the ending.)

Yes. The latter rather than the former (which is my principal difference with alga).

In regards to these points, I do agree with alga. Do you agree also?

Yes, the fact that a fifty-two card deck also appears in TD does suggest something more is going on than my guesses in my earlier post. But I don't agree that JC built the house around the cards. The house has several calendric attributes, of which the fifty-two doors is only one.

Which would also argue against your "eternal cycle" theory, it seems to me: after five repetitions, the first group of fairies would be unable to go inward any further.

--Adam
