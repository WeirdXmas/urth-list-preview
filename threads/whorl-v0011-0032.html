Themes: Masks, disguise and shapeshifting

2000-08-01 Josh Levitan #

Argues that the inhumi of The Book of the Long Sun change their appearance mainly through physical means, molding their faces like clay and using makeup and wigs, with telepathy at most rounding out the illusion. Responds to Alga's claim that the inhumi are only partly shapeshifters, citing Horn's remarks about molding faces and the eyewitness account of arms turning into wings.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Inhumi mold their faces like clay and use makeup

Kevin J. Maroney wrote (quoting Alga):

But it's a mostly physical change. Horn tells Mora (and again, my poor addled brain can't remember the exact passage, and I'm too lazy to dig out the book and get a cite) that the inhumi mold their faces like clay, and then use makeup and wigs to cap off the illusion. That's a big part of how she can recognize inhumi (especially if it's a kid or a man wearing makeup). And we get eyewitness descriptions of the inhumi changing their arms into wings as well, a definite physical change. The telepathy, or magic, or whatever you call it, probably just rounds out the physical stuff, and they can apply that at will (like when Krait let Horn see him like an inhumi, not as a boy).

I hope it is something much bigger. Besides, I don't see how being kind to one another or to the inhumi would be a weapon "too heavy to wield," as Horn puts it.

Narwhal
