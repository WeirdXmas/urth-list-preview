Bee Wrangler

Urth list · 7 messages · 2006-08-28 to 2006-08-31

2006urthThe Book of the New SunBob MillerRex LycanthrosaurusTony EllisDan'l Danehy-OakesRoy C. Lackey
The thread: Participants debated whether the penultimate Autarch could be Appian, the Old Autarch who was Sancha's lover, by testing his age, reign length and time away in Yesod. Rex Lycanthrosaurus offered a bee quotation from Citadel as evidence of what Paeon taught him. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued relativistic time differences could hide a much longer reign and that absence would be harmless, while Roy C. Lackey countered with a timeline showing the Old Autarch was trained and kept bees about fifty years before death, leaving no room for a long absence. Bob Miller proposed Wolfe's deliberate time vagueness as a broader pattern. Tony Ellis rejected the long-absence theory, noting the text never remarks on the Autarch looking youthful, so the thread ended in impasse with the timeline argument the strongest against Appian.
  • Rex Lycanthrosaurus: bee quotation links the Old Autarch to Paeon's teaching and the House Absolute
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: relativistic travel could allow a much longer reign, so apparent age does not rule out Appian
  • Roy C. Lackey: timeline of training, bees and succession shows no long absence and an Appian identity is unlikely
  • Bob Miller: Wolfe's deliberate vagueness about time suggests the Autarch may have reigned longer than he looks
  • Tony Ellis: Predecessors could have returned him the day he left, and the text never notes implausible youth

Themes: Time loops and causality, Myth and folklore sources, Memory and forgetting

Supplies a quotation from The Citadel of the Autarch about bees and the House Absolute, offered to answer Roy Lackey's question about what Paeon teaches the penultimate autarch. The post adds the passage's mention of the hives, their sacredness, and steering by the sun, presenting it as evidence for what the lesson involves.

Found the one other bee-related quote I was looking for, and append it, if for nothing other than to answer Roy Lackey's question about what exactly it is Paeon teaches the penultimate autarch.

"The dead Autarch, whose face I had seen in scarlet ruin a few moments before, now lived again. My eyes and hands were his, I knew the work of the hives of the bees of the House Absolute and the sacredness of them, who steer by the sun and fetch gold of Urth's fertility. I knew his course to the Phoenix Throne, and to the stars, and back." (Citadel, Chapter XXIX)

2006-08-29 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that the Autarch may have reigned much longer than his apparent age suggests, because relativistic time differences during travel could have passed far more time on Urth than for the traveller. Responds to Roy C. Lackey's chronology argument about the Old Autarch's age by pointing to Severian's own return after years, which bears on whether the Autarch could be older.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Autarch reigned longer than apparent age

Jumping in to be a pain in the ass.

It ain't necessarily so, Roy. In fact ... I think you've just pried open an important argument for the pro-Appian camp.

How long was the Predecessor gone?

More accurately: how much time passed for him, and how much time passed on Urth, between his departure and his return? We know that Severian's journey didn't take too much subjective time, but he returned years after his departure.

Such considerations make it plausible to me that the Autarch has reigned longer than it seems, looking at him, he could possibly have done.

2006-08-29 Roy C. Lackey replying to Rex Lycanthrosaurus #

Argues that the Old Autarch's link to bees in Citadel does not make him Sancha's Appian, and that his age and reign length favor Roy's earlier list of the last four rulers. Responds to Rex Lycanthrosaurus by conceding the bee quotation, then reasoning from when the Old Autarch was trained, went to Yesod, and made his lament about succession.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: Old Autarch is not Sancha's Appian

Rex Lycanthrosaurus quoted:

"The dead Autarch, whose face I had seen in scarlet ruin a few moments before, now lived again. My eyes and hands were his, I knew the work of the hives of the bees of the House Absolute and the sacredness of them, who steer by the sun and fetch gold of Urth's fertility. I knew his course to the Phoenix Throne, and to the stars, and back." (Citadel, Chapter XXIX)

Damn! I forgot that and should not have, particularly in this context.

Still, while the quote establishes a link between bees and the Old Autarch, that does not make him Sancha's Appian. If the Old Autarch had been a boy when he was trained, and went to Yesod as a relatively young man a quarter-century before Paeon told him about Sev, then he would not have been particularly old when he made his lament about not thinking his replacement would be "so soon". If, however, he had held the throne for more than sixty years at that point, his complaint would be unreasonable.

I think my earlier list of the last four rulers makes more sense, and better reconciles the apparent with the actual age of the Old Autarch. Lomer's and Nicarete's longevity can't be helped, no matter who was autarch when.

-Roy

Grounds this in Severian's journey taking little subjective time yet returning years later, suggesting the Autarch may have reigned longer than his appearance implies.

2006-08-30 Roy C. Lackey replying to Rex Lycanthrosaurus #

Argues that the text gives no hint the Old Autarch was away from Urth for any significant time, so his reign was not longer than it looks. Grounds this in the timeline: he was trained by Paeon and kept bees about fifty years before his death, twelve years after Appian let Sancha go south, and questions whether he was autarch when he first knew Paeon.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Old Autarch did not reign longer than it appears

There is not the least hint in the text that the Old Autarch was absent from Urth for any significant length of time, whether measured subjectively or objectively. Whether he was in his fifties or his eighties doesn't change the fact that 50 years before his death he was being trained by Paeon and messing with bees. And that was 12 years after Autarch Appian let Sancha move south. I seriously doubt that the Old Autarch was autarch when he first knew Paeon, or that he would be learning all about bees at least a dozen years after coming to the throne.

And Maruthas closing the roads when Palaemon was 25 still has to be accounted for.

-Roy

2006-08-30 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes replying to Tony Ellis #

Argues that the penultimate Autarch could have been away for any length of time, so his apparent age does not prove he is not Appian, and that Father Inire could keep the Commonwealth running. Responds to Tony Ellis's claim that Tzadkiel would not risk the Autarch's absence by pointing out that time travel makes the absence harmless and that the Penult may be an Erebus double agent.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · puzzle-solving · theories: Penultimate Autarch could be Appian; Penult is a double agent for Erebus

No, you're quite right; he could have been gone from three seconds to three decades, or less, or more. But the point is not to argue that the Penult _did_ skip a couple of decades; it's simply that he _could_ have, and so his apparent age is not an overwhelming argument for his not being Appian, even if (as I believe) there's no life-extension technology available to the Commonwealth.

I don't particularly see this as a risk -- not only is Father Inire perfectly capable of keeping things going without the Autarch, the Penult is a double agent for Erebus (via Vodalus) ... or something.

2006-08-31 Tony Ellis replying to Rex Lycanthrosaurus #

Disputes the pro-Appian claim that the penultimate Autarch was gone for years in Yesod, arguing that the Predecessors could have returned him the day after he left. Cites the lack of textual support, noting that Severian never remarks on the Autarch looking implausibly youthful, and suggests he may have returned older instead.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: Autarch spent decades in Yesod; Autarch returned from Yesod older

Rex
(http://listserver.dreamhost.com/pipermail/urth-urth.net/2006-August/002 622.html) and Roy
(http://listserver.dreamhost.com/pipermail/urth-urth.net/2006-August/002 629.html) have already touched on this, I think.

Years *before*, surely?

In any case, as Roy says, the penultimate Autarch needn't have been gone any time at all. Why should Tzadkiel and co risk the Commonwealth falling to the armies of Erebus in his absence when they are masters of time travel, and can bring him back the day after he left?

My own problem with the old Autarch going to Yesod and coming back 20 years later or 20 years younger is that there's just no support for it in the text. If the old Autarch really has been in power for *at least* the last 62 years, why doesn't Severian comment on how implausibly youthful he looks?

If someone wants to argue that evidence isn't required, that this slight-of-time could have happened regardless of the fact that Wolfe doesn't say anything about it, I can just as easily argue the reverse. Who's to say the Autarch didn't come back from Yesod significantly older than when he left, as Gunnie says happen to some people who travel on the Ship?
