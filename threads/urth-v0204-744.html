2002-04-22 Adam Stephanides #

Argues that Paul Duggan's reading, in which Severian wrote his manuscript in a prior universe and Wolfe merely translated it, is hard to reconcile with the Shadow appendix's phrases about a tongue not yet existing and a posthistoric world. Notes that this reading would require artifacts and buildings to survive the singularity, and offers the Incarnation casting its shadow into the past universe as an alternative explanation.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theology-and-religion · theories: Severian wrote the manuscript in a prior universe; Incarnation casts its shadow into the past universe

I have a hard time reconciling this reading with the references in the SHADOW appendix to "a tongue that has not yet achieved existence" (unless we are supposed to believe that the timelines are so similar that the exact same languages will develop) and "the posthistoric world." Also, we would have to accept that some artifacts and even buildings had survived the singularity, as well as Sev's manuscript.

This, OTOH, could be explained as the Incarnation "casting its shadow" into the past universe.

--Adam

2002-04-22 Paul C Duggan #

Proposes that Severian wrote his manuscript in a distant past universe before throwing it into space, so that the book's references to "the future" are relative to that earlier timeline and Wolfe is merely the translator. Grounds this in the survival of the manuscript through a singularity and the similarity of the two timelines, while noting a theological objection to the repeated saving by the theoanthropos.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theology-and-religion · theories: Severian wrote in a prior universe; Wolfe is translator of Severian's manuscript; References to the future are relative to the past universe

But remeber, the text of the Book of the New Sun came into the hands of Gene Wolfe *after* Severian wrote it and threw it into space in the lead coffer. Wolfe is just the translator. So Severian wrote in the "distant past" prior universe and the manuscript survives the singularity (!) and Wolfe then gets it and tranlastes it

All references to "the future" should just be taken as refering to how Severian is in the future of where "we are" in the past universe's timeline (since the timelines are amazingly similar).

My only objection to this is theological, in that the theoanthropos then comes to save everybody at least 2 if not more times. But its just a novel...

Paul Duggan
