1998-09-28 Jay Lake #

Argues that Wolfe's The Fifth Head of Cerberus and Delany's The Einstein Intersection are nearly the same book, both telling stories by misdirection about displaced, resurrected cultures. Builds on an earlier post's Forlesen revival idea and mentions reading The Book of the New Sun aloud, then asks how large Severian's sabertache is.
proposes · genre-and-craft · theories: The Fifth Head of Cerberus and The Einstein Intersection are almost the same book

Having been lurking on the digest, I must mention something...

I have actually thought for many years that Delany's 'The Einstein Intersection' and 'Fifth Head of Cerebrus' were in so close a parallel as to be eerie. To me, they are almost the same book as written by two authors. I believe that I base this feeling primarily on the fact that both books are masterworks in the art of storytelling by misdirection, both are about severely displaced/resurrected cultures, and both are (nominally) about humans in stressful, regressed settings.

BTW, I am reading BOTNS aloud to my wife, a chapter per evening, which is doing amazing things for my own perceptions. We are keeping the excellent 'Lexicon Urthus' on the night stand for quick reference (although I won't let her see the entries until we're done -- no spoilers!). So I have come to realize a fundamental question about the book:

How big is that freaking sabertache anyway?

Jay Lake
Austin, Texas
