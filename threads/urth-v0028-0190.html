Tintin?

Urth list · 4 messages · 1999-10-08 to 1999-10-12

1999urthThe Book of the New SunMitchell A. BaileyNicholas GeversAlex David Groce
The thread: Participants traced the eclipse trick in Wolfe's Miracle of Apu Punchau to historical and literary sources. Nicholas Gevers linked it to Columbus's 1504 Jamaica eclipse hoax, which he saw as a shared source for Herge's and Wolfe's neo-Inca deceptions. Alex David Groce argued the rescue echoes Tintin's Prisoners of the Sun, citing The Secret of the Unicorn and a John 8:32 quotation. Mitchell A. Bailey asked whether the trick appeared in the 1931 film A Connecticut Yankee, then corrected himself: Twain's novel does have an eclipse chapter. The thread ended on that correction, with the Tintin link left unresolved.
  • Nicholas Gevers: Columbus's 1504 eclipse hoax is a likely historical source for the Apu Punchau and Herge deceptions
  • Alex David Groce: The Apu Punchau rescue echoes Tintin's Prisoners of the Sun, with Unicorn and Cross constellations as clues
  • Mitchell A. Bailey: Asked whether the trick is in the 1931 film, then admitted Twain's novel has an eclipse chapter

Themes: Myth and folklore sources, Power, rulers and politics, Stories within stories

1999-10-08 Nicholas Gevers #

Suggests that Columbus's alleged 1504 eclipse trick on the people of Jamaica may have influenced both Herge's and Wolfe's portrayals of neo-Inca people being fooled in this way. Builds on the earlier parallel between the Miracle of Apu Punchau in The Urth of the New Sun and the rescue in Herge's Prisoners of the Sun, offering a historical source for the deception.

portrayals of neo-Inca folk being gulled in this way.

1999-10-08 Alex David Groce #

Proposes that the rescue in the Miracle of Apu Punchau echoes how Tintin, Captain Haddock and Professor Calculus are saved in Herge's Prisoners of the Sun. Cites the previous Tintin adventure, The Secret of the Unicorn, and the chapter before Apu Punchau ending with the constellations Unicorn and Cross, then quotes John 8:32.
proposes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Apu Punchau rescue echoes Tintin's Prisoners of the Sun

This is purely coincidental, I'm sure, but I just noticed the resemblance between the Miracle of Apu Punchau and the means by which Tintin, Captain Haddock and Professor Calculus are saved in Herge's PRISONERS OF THE SUN. Incidentally, the previous adventure to the PRISONERS OF THE SUN is THE SECRET OF THE UNICORN/RED RACKHAM'S TREASURE, concerning the treasure of the ship Unicorn, hidden under St. John's Cross. The chapter before Apu Punchau in URTH ends with a mention of two constellations: the Unicorn and the Cross.

"And ye shall know the truth, and the truth shall make you free." John 8:32

1999-10-12 Mitchell A. Bailey #

Corrects the poster's own earlier claim by noting that Twain's novel does include an eclipse trick, in a chapter titled "The Eclipse". Also points out the windowless houses the protagonist sees in Camelot as a parallel to Thrax, the city in Wolfe's fiction.
informs · source-and-allusion

Whoops, my mistake, it WAS in the book, near the beginning, in a chapter entitled "The Eclipse", at that!

BTW, I noticed that among the protagonists initial impressions of Camelot, as he was brought as a prisoner, was a number of "windowless" houses. Shades of Thrax!
