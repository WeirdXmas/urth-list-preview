2000-02-08 Nicholas Gevers #

Recalls Wolfe's note on "Bluesberry Jam" in SPACE OPERA, saying a real traffic jam inspired that story. Notes that the "Hey, I'm dead!" ending of "Ain't You 'Most Done?" is a Wolfe habit that works as a redemptive close to the collection, suggesting Benson seems bound for Heaven.
informs · author-and-biography · genre-and-craft · theories: Benson seems Heavenbound at the collection's end

On the traffic jam stories in ST: I remember Wolfe
saying in his original note on "Bluesberry Jam" in
SPACE OPERA that an actual incident involving a
protracted traffic jam inspired the story. And re the
"Hey, I'm dead!" cliche in "Ain't You 'Most Done?":
Wolfe has done this rather often, but as a conclusion
to the collection, it has a usefully redemptive
significance after much preceding gloom: Benson seems Heavenbound.

2000-02-10 James Jordan #

Proposes that the protracted traffic jam in Wolfe's story "Bluesberry Jam" is purgatory, linking it to the purgatorial setting of Peace. Grounds this in Roman Catholic doctrine that purgatory is an anteroom to heaven where a heavenbound person makes peace with his past and sins, responding to a quoted note on the traffic jam stories and "Ain't You 'Most Done?".
proposes · theology-and-religion · allegory-and-typology · theories: The traffic jam in Bluesberry Jam is purgatory

A thought: The traffic jam is purgatory. This would link with *Peace,* where the character is also in purgatory. Remember that in RC thinking, purgatory is just the anteroom to heaven, and is not like hell at all. Purgatory is the place where a heavenbound person makes peace with his past and his sins. Just a thought....

Nutria
