The thread: Roy C. Lackey opened by arguing that the chronology of the white fountain and events before Severian's trial cannot be settled in a circular time-travel story, and read the Outsider's unchanging regard for Silk in Nightside the Long Sun as predestination. Alex David Groce answered that predestination theology, via Augustine's view of God's foreknowledge outside time, could resolve the time-loop puzzle, grounding this in Wolfe's Catholicism and rejecting a Calvinist reading of Silk. Michael Straight and Kevin J. Maroney disputed the predestination reading, calling the passage a promise of unconditional love that no act could alter, and Maroney allowed that the Outsider might foresee Silk's acts without them mattering. The thread ended in disagreement over whether the passage shows predestination or divine love, with no consensus reached.
  • Roy C. Lackey: time loops make the chronology unsettleable, and the Outsider's regard is predestination
  • Alex David Groce: Augustine's view of divine foreknowledge outside time answers the loop puzzle, not Calvinism
  • Michael Straight: the Outsider's regard is an unconditional promise of love, not foreknowledge of acts
  • Kevin J. Maroney: unchanging divine love, since foreknowledge would not alter the Outsider's regard

Themes: Time loops and causality, Free will and choice, Gods, angels and the Increate

Welcome Tony A. West!

Feel free to disagree; I think it's a requirement for posting here. And a healthy dose of scleroderma helps. <g>

BTW, you should turn off the html feature when you post here--plain text only--to avoid all that html garbage taking up Ranjit's valuable web space.

As for the logic of what came when; in a circular, time-travel story, one point on the circumference is about as good as another. If the circle is broken at any point, the whole thing collapses. I don't have a better answer than that; I don't know that there is one. If there is one, it may be related to Wolfe's views on predestination. I don't know whether or not the views expressed by Silk could be taken as Wolfe's, but on the second page of chapter V of _Nightside_ he has Silk thinking:

"...the Outsider had assured him that his regard for him was eternal and perfect, never to be changed by any act of his, no matter how iniquitous or how meritorious."

Now _that_ is predestination, pure and simple. FWIW

Roy

1999-04-06 Alex David Groce #

Argues that Roy Lackey's time-loop puzzle in the Long Sun books may be answered by predestination theology, using Augustine's view that God's foreknowledge lies outside time. Grounds this in Wolfe's Catholicism and his stress on free will, and rejects a Calvinist reading of Silk's views.
proposes · theology-and-religion · theories: Wolfe's view of predestination is Augustinian free will

Maybe. But notice the word "eternal"--which would imply the solution of St. Augustine, that "predestination" means that God's knowledge is outside time, which is a creation, and therefore perfect foreknowledge doesn't mean compulsion. And "regard" is a tricky point too--as far as I'm aware, orthodox theology of most varieties has God loving everybody always and perfectly--but people can reject the love. I would, given Wolfe's Catholicism and general emphasis on an almost existential freedom, be surprised if he believes in a Calvinist or similar form of predestination--I'd suspect he falls heavily, even within the Catholic schools of thought, into the free-will camp. --

1999-04-06 Michael Straight #

Disputes Roy C. Lackey's reading that the Outsider's unconditional regard is predestination, arguing it is instead a promise. Grounds this in the quoted line, which says the Outsider will love regardless of what one does, not that he foresees one's future acts.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: The Outsider's regard is a promise, not predestination

Predestination? It sounds to me more like a promise. The Outsider doesn't say, "I've seen the future and I know you're not going to do anything bad enough to make me stop loving you," but "I'll love you no matter what you do."

-Rostrum

1999-04-12 Kevin J. Maroney #

Argues that the Outsider's assurance to Silk in Nightside the Long Sun is unchanging divine love rather than predestination, since no act of Silk's could alter it. Responds to Roy's claim that the passage shows predestination, saying the Outsider may foresee Silk's acts without those acts affecting his regard.
disputes · theology-and-religion · theories: The Outsider's eternal love is not predestination

That's not predestination. That's a statement about the unchanging and unending love which god has for its creations. The Outsider might not know what Silk will do--though he probably does, given his role in the story--but no matter what Silk does, it cannot damage god's love for him.
