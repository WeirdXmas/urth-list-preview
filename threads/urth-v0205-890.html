PEACE: Morryster's _Marvells of Science_

Urth list · 5 messages · 2002-05-06 to 2002-05-10

2002urthThe Book of the New SunJerry FriedmanAdam StephanidesMichael Andre-Driussi
The thread: Michael Andre-Driussi traced the phrase "Marvells of Science" from Peace to a Bierce story and the Devil's Dictionary, suggesting the Morryster book might be a forgery layered on another. Jerry Friedman questioned how certain it was that the book was one of Gold's forgeries. Andre-Driussi argued it was as surely a forgery as Gold's Necronomicon and Kate's diary, and Adam Stephanides disputed the book's existence, citing its absence from library catalogues and Google. Friedman ended by accepting Stephanides's bibliographic clue as presumably, though not certainly, a Gold forgery.
  • Michael Andre-Driussi: the Marvells of Science is a double-layered forgery traceable to Bierce
  • Jerry Friedman: forgery status is uncertain, but Stephanides's clue makes it presumably a Gold forgery
  • Adam Stephanides: the Morryster book is almost certainly fictitious, based on catalogue absence and Bierce's invented authors
  • Michael Andre-Driussi: surely a Gold forgery like his Necronomicon and Kate's diary

Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Stories within stories, Mirrors, pictures and art

2002-05-06 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Traces the phrase "Marvells of Science" from Adam Stephanides's observation in Peace to Bierce's story "The Man and the Snake" and the Devil's Dictionary, suggesting a possible double-layered forgery. Points out that the quoted text in Bierce does not match the direct quote Weer reads, and speculates the quote may appear elsewhere in Peace, noting Wolfe's fondness for such stories.
proposes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Marvells of Science is a double-layered forgery

Does THE DEVIL'S DICTIONARY (1911) have some reference to that title? In any event, it shows up, with a little quote, in Bierce's short story "The Man and the Snake" (1891). See here
http://www.online-literature.com/bierce/174/
Yes, then Lovecraft used it later, but I've misplaced the note on which story that was.

<snipping a bit>

The quote included in "The Man and the Snake" does not match the direct quote that A. D. Weer reads in the bookstore. (But the quote in the story does sound rather familiar--maybe it is somewhere else inside PEACE?) Further research would be to check if Lovecraft created a quote that was used--this would be a nice "double-decker" forgery.

The indirect quote that Adam refers to is not included in the Bierce story, nor is it germane to that story, yet the story itself is rather germane toward PEACE as a whole, or so it seems to me, and it also seems like the sort of story that Gene Wolfe loves to the point of emmulation.

Argues that the Marvells of Science in Peace is as surely a forgery by Gold as his Necronomicon and Kate's diary, since Gold's bookstore holds books too rare to exist. Responds to Jerry Friedman's question about the Marvells' authenticity, and says the poster is still hunting for the source of the Venus di Milo story in Peace, then closes by plugging Sirius Fiction booklets and the out-of-print Lexicon Urthus.
supports · puzzle-solving · source-and-allusion · theories: Marvells of Science is a Gold forgery; Gold forged the Necronomicon; Gold forged Kate's diary

Well hmmm. It seems as certain as his forgery of THE NECRONOMICON and his forgery of Kate's diary. As Adam Stephanides noted before, it is a big pointer to the nature of Gold's bookstore as a place of books so rare as to be impossible: the rising music which crescendos a few pages later around the more famous and unspoken NECRONOMICON.

2002-05-10 Adam Stephanides #

Argues that the Morryster's Marvells of Science cited in Peace is almost certainly a fictitious book, probably another forgery attributed to Gold. Grounds this on the absence of the title from major library catalogues and Google, the forged status of other books linked to Gold, and Bierce's invented authors in the Devil's Dictionary.
disputes · source-and-allusion · theories: Morryster's Marvells of Science is a Gold forgery

Oops--I hadn't noticed this. Sorry, didn't mean to be redundant.

Given that:

1) neither the National Union Catalog (hundreds of thick volumes compiling the card catalogs of the Library of Congress and many other libraries), the British Library Catalog, nor Google show any trace of the book's existence;

2) all the other books named in connection with Gold are forgeries (I think); and

3) all the authors Bierce "cites" in the Devil's Dictionary seem to be his inventions,

it's a pretty safe bet that it's a forgery. And there's no reason to think that it isn't Gold's.

--Adam
