Pas, Silk and Horn

Whorl list · 3 messages · 2000-04-08 to 2000-04-10

2000whorlThe Book of the Long SunAlice TurnerJames JordanMichael Straight
The thread: Jim Jordan opened by proposing that Silk's ascension in The Book of the Long Sun mirrors the New Testament, with Kypris standing in for the Holy Spirit, and asked whether Pas is a good god akin to Typhon's evil counterpart, while admitting the question is unresolved. Alice Turner disputed that the two-headed bronze figure Kypris shows Silk proves he entered that body, noting no text confirms he accepted her offer, and rejected a clone theory as too easy. Michael Straight argued Silk had no real choice about his transcript entering Mainframe, possibly without consent through a sacred window, and suggested Kypris may already have done it and was talking to the version of Silk left in the physical world. The thread ends without resolution, with the debate over textual evidence for Silk's fate left open.
  • Jim Jordan: Silk's ascension parallels the New Testament, with Kypris as Holy Spirit and Pas possibly a good god
  • Alice Turner: The bronze figure does not prove Silk entered it; the text does not confirm his acceptance, and a clone theory is too easy
  • Michael Straight: Silk had no choice about Mainframe entry, and Kypris may have already transferred him and was teasing the physical Silk

Themes: Christ figures and messiahs, Robots, androids and artificial life, Gods, angels and the Increate

2000-04-08 James Jordan #

Proposes that Silk's ascension in The Book of the Long Sun parallels the New Testament, with Kypris as a surrogate for the Holy Spirit, and asks whether Pas is the good god of the Whorl akin to Typhon's evil counterpart. Grounds this in the Kypris window passage and biblical parallels such as the bronze-limbed Christ in Revelation 1:15 and Daniel 10:6, while admitting the question remains unresolved.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · source-and-allusion · theories: Silk's ascension parallels the New Testament narrative; Silk may be duplicated into Pas in Mainframe; Pas is the good god of the Whorl

seductively, "Silk, wouldn't you like to be like Pas? We'd be together then....They can scan you in at Mainframe. As I was scanned, Silk, with him. He held my hand....You'll go on with your life, Silk. Just as it is. You'd be Pas too. And he would be you. Look."

There's no evidence that he didn't either, of course, which is also most lupine. I suppose we'll eventually find out.
Given the "theology" of the narrative, which is never far from the surface, I've wondered about this. Silk's ascension to "heaven" (past the "border mountains," to quote an old hymn), following his death and resurrection and preceding his return in the final conflagration of (his part of) the Whorl, closely follows the New Testament narrative. In the cited passage, Wolfe at least alludes to Silk's ascension to "the right hand of the Father," with Kypris as a surrogate for the Holy Spirit. The question raised in my mind is this: Is the Pas of the Whorl the "evil Typhon" of earth? Clearly he originated there, but he actually seems to be the "good god" of the Whorl. If this be the case, then for Silk to allow a transcript of himself to be entered into Mainframe would not be out of accord with his character. On the other hand, this might be something like "The Last Temptation of Silk," which he should reject. I've never sorted this question out. I suspect that time will tell, and we'll find out eventually.
"Bronze limbed" is right out of the Bible, I suspect. The ascended Christ appears as a man of bronze in Revelation chapter 1:15, and compare Daniel 10:6.

2000-04-08 Alice Turner #

Argues that the two-headed bronze figure Kypris shows Silk at the end of The Book of the Long Sun does not prove Silk entered that body, noting no text confirms he accepted her offer. Responds to Adam's question about textual evidence, adding that the image could give the trickster a way to place Horn in Silk's body while keeping Silk around, and rejects a clone theory as too easy.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Silk is in Pas's two-headed body; Horn is placed in Silk's body; Young Newt is a clone

Adam asked:

.> Is there textual evidence from TBOTLS that Silk may be in a two-headed

Near the end of the last book Kypris appears in a Window and says seductively, "Silk, wouldn't you like to be like Pas? We'd be together then....They can scan you in at Mainframe. As I was scanned, Silk, with him. He held my hand....You'll go on with your life, Silk. Just as it is. You'd be Pas too. And he would be you. Look."

And she shows him the image of a "bronze-limbed man with rippling muscles and two heads." One is Silk's.

But there isn't the least textual evidence that he took her up on it. It would seem most un-Silk-like (though Hy would jump at it--and mantis sometimes seems to think that Hy is an aspect of Kypris--and he'd follow her anywhere). Still, it's intriguing enough to have prompted discussion. And it gives the lupine trickster an out if he wants to put Horn in Silk's body, but still wants to have Silk around when we Return to the Whorl. I don't buy young newt's clone theory: too easy, too trite.

-alga

2000-04-10 Michael Straight #

Argues that Silk had no real choice about his transcript being entered into Mainframe, since the process could be done without consent, perhaps through a sacred window. Suggests Kypris may already have done it and was only teasing Silk, talking to the version of him left behind in the physical world. Responds to Jim Jordan's point that Silk might reject a 'Last Temptation of Silk' scenario.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Kypris already transferred Silk's transcript before asking consent

I don't think Silk had a choice in the matter. It seems like the kind of process that could be done without consent. They might even be able to do it through a sacred window. Sure, Kypris asked Silk how he'd like it, but maybe she'd already done it, and was just teasing, talking to the Silk that had been left behind in the physical world.

-Rostrum
