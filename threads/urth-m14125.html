The thread: Readers debated how Dr Talos in The Book of the Long Sun could know about Mary Shelley's Frankenstein dream. Jeff Wilson argued a sleeping mind cannot select images of one event from all history, comparing it to a radio receiving every broadcast, and cited Clarke's Childhood's End as a case where non-linear dreaming made more sense. Tony Ellis countered that Talos only claims events influence dreams, not shape history, and offered Mary Shelley's Lake Geneva dream as a parallel. Jerry Friedman proposed two readings, depending on whether Urth lies in our future or in a past universe, with backward causation or linked stories as the mechanism. The thread ends with competing readings rather than agreement.
  • Jeff Wilson: a dreaming mind cannot select images of a specific event from all history, so the idea needs a singular event to make sense
  • Tony Ellis: Talos claims only that events influence dreams, and the Frankenstein dream shows how an influenced dream could be recorded and survive
  • Jerry Friedman: Talos's knowledge depends on whether Urth is in our future (backward causation by Talos and Baldanders) or in a past universe (linked stories ec

Themes: Ghosts, dreams and visions, Time loops and causality, The dying sun and the cosmos

2010-06-04 Tony Ellis #

Argues that Dr Talos in The Book of the Long Sun does not claim events in his time shape ours, but only that they influence our dreams, and suggests Urthian events may inspire dreams constantly. Grounds this in the historical case of Mary Shelley writing Frankenstein after a dream at Lake Geneva, offered as a parallel for how an influenced dream could be recorded and survive.
disputes · source-and-allusion · theories: Urthian events influence dreams rather than waking events

I'm not sure if that was a rhetorical question or not, but anyway:

Mary Shelley, famously, was inspired to write Frankenstein by a *dream* she had. At a villa on Lake (hint hint) Geneva. Dr Talos isn't saying events in his time influence events in ours, he's saying they influence our dreams.

They don't, necessarily. Maybe we're all having dreams inspired by Urthian events, all the time. It's just that in this case the dream was recorded and that record - somewhat miraculously - survived long enough for one of its instigators to identify it.

2010-06-04 Jerry Friedman replying to Tony Ellis #

Proposes two readings of how Dr. Talos knows about Mary Shelley's dream and Frankenstein, depending on whether Urth lies in our future or in a past universe. In the future case, Talos and Baldanders may have caused the dream backwards in time; in the past-universe case, three linked stories echo into Shelley's dream.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · source-and-allusion · theories: Urth lies in our future or past universe; Talos and Baldanders caused Shelley's dream; Shelley's dream echoes Urth's past universe stories

From: Tony Ellis <[email]>

Again I think it depends on whether Urth is in our future or in a previous universe.

If it's in our future, then the story is relatively simple. There was Mary Shelley's dream, which led to /Frankenstein/, which Dr. Talos somehow knows about and recognizes. He assumes, maybe correctly, that he and Baldanders caused the dream backwards in time.

If it's in a past universe, as Mr. Wolfe has said, then there are three stories. The one Dr. Talos knows is from Urth's younger days and may have been inspired by a dream. He and Baldanders replay it, we don't know how faithfully. Shelley's dream and /Frankenstein/ are an echo of those two stories in our universe.

Jerry Friedman
