Amanda Ros

Themes: Books, libraries and writing

2001-06-13 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Argues that the titles of Amanda Ros's books, including IRENE, DONALD and FUMES, check out as real and link to Peace, with a suspicion that Delina echoes A.D. Weer's mother. Grounds this on the Ros forgery in Peace, the publication dates, and a rationale tying her critic-hatred to the lawyer book.
proposes · puzzle-solving · source-and-allusion · theories: The Ros forgery in Peace is a deliberate fabrication; Delina echoes A.D. Weer's mother Adelina

Roy,

Okay, we have a genuine timeline problem, since O RARE AMANDA was first published in 1954.

How neat if our best anchor points (like Ludwig's biography of Napoleon) all come from publishing dates!

Back to Amanda:

These titles all check out. IRENE was her first book (a savage critique of it backfired and launched her, in a way), DONALD was aimed at literary critics, FUMES was her final published work.

I mentioned before HELEN HUDDLESON--it turns out she never finished the manuscript.

There is also ST. SCANDALBAGS, which slams critics.

Amanda Ros was famous for hating critics and lawyers; thus it makes perfect sense, seeing as how she wrote DONALD, that she would also pen a book blasting lawyers.

Hence the rationale for THE LUSTY LAWYER.

Looking over the titles, with an eye toward PEACE (please don't forget that the =only= occurance of the word "peace" in PEACE is in the Ros forgery!), I will admit that I'm a little uneasy about "Delina," since A.D. Weer's mom's name is Adelina, aka "Della."
