2000-03-25 Joe Leighly #

Proposes that the closing lines of Wolfe's The Claw of the Conciliator echo Borges' 'The Garden of Forking Paths', where a garden remark precedes a killing. Places the two passages side by side, quoting each about the garden and noting that in both the narrator then kills the other man, and labels the pairing 1:2::time:space.

Hello, I noticed this once. No doubt it's been considered and either rejected or accepted a long time ago, but I haven't read much = commentary.
=20
1. Borges, "The Garden of Forking Paths":
=20
"'I see that the worthy Hsi P'eng has troubled himself to see to=20 relieving my solitude. No doubt you want to see the garden?'" =20
[narrator is then shown a book, which is the garden, and subsequently kills the other]
=20

2. Wolfe, Claw of the Conciliator, end of Ch. XX:
=20
"and I said, 'Then you can show me where the garden is.'" =20
[ditto]
=20

1:2::time:space
=20
