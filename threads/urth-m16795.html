The Claw

Urth list · 12 messages · 2011-12-21 to 2012-01-02

20112012urthThe Book of the New SunJane DelawneyJerry FriedmanLee BermanGerry QuinnLarry MillerJeff Wilson
The thread: Readers debated how Severian's Claw works, mainly whether its healing power comes from a temporal anomaly, from the White Fountain's light reaching Urth, or from within Severian himself. Larry Miller proposed a space-time warp tied to the blood on the thorn, and Lee Berman added a stretched-time and higher-universe idea. Jeff Wilson argued that healing draws on Urth's daylight power, citing the Declan scene and the sickroom sequence, while Jane Delawney and Gerry Quinn disputed the White Fountain timing and the source of the power. Late in the thread Lee Berman argued the power is mostly internal and the Stone Town seance was staged, and no consensus was reached.
  • Larry Miller: the Claw warps space-time because of a temporal anomaly tied to Severian's blood on the thorn
  • Lee Berman: time is stretched across Severian's life and the power flows from a higher universe; later, the power is mainly internal
  • Jeff Wilson: healing draws on Urth's power in daylight, as the Declan and sickroom scenes show
  • Gerry Quinn: the source may be other than Urth, such as a white hole or Yesodi ship; the Claw held only a residual trace of Severian's power
  • Jane Delawney: healing isn't gated on the White Fountain light, since Severian's early miracles came before it

Themes: Time loops and causality, Robots, androids and artificial life, The dying sun and the cosmos

2011-12-21 Larry Miller #

Proposes that the Claw works through a temporal anomaly: Severian's blood on the thorn, which still lies inside him, warps space-time so the Claw can revive Dorcas. Grounds this in the anointing at the end of The Citadel of the Autarch and the Apu Punchau idea that two Severians cannot share a point in time.
proposes · myth-and-structure · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: The Claw warps time through Severian's blood; Severian's blood on the thorn causes Dorcas's resurrection

Alright so due to the discussion currently going on concerning the fantastical/magical elements in the BotNS I figured I should post this. Ive had a theory about the Claw for a long time now and Im not sure if this has ever been proposed before but I feel its relevant. Some people view the Claw as miraculous and that falls squarely in supernatural/magical territory (at least IMHO) but maybe its mysterious workings can be explained in a scientific (or science fictional) manner. Ive always thought that the Claw can do what it does through the warping of space-time around it because it is a temporal anomaly itself. We know that the Claw as a thorn was annointed for lack of a better word with Severians blood at the end of Citadel. After traveling back to the Conciliators era it becomes the Claw. When Severian obtains the relic it still has his blood on it. Now this same blood is still inside Severian as he hasnt been pricked yet. Now we know that two Severians cannot exist in the same point in time and space (ala Apu Punchau-why there was no implosion when they come into contact could just be that its not a whole other Severian just a drop of his blood ) so I believe that this creates some kind of anomalous effect around Severian. This causes the Claw to warp time back to the time when Dorcas was alive resurrecting her.

2011-12-22 Lee Berman replying to Larry Miller #

Proposes that the Claw's power comes from time being stretched across Severian's life, like a temporal rubber band, and links this to the idea that energy flows from a higher universe through the White Fountain. Responds to Larry Miller's theory by citing Dorcas's suggestion that the Claw warps time and by comparing Asimov's The Gods Themselves, whose higher universe is purely physical.
proposes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Claw powered by temporal rubber band effect; White Fountain leaks energy from higher universe; Severian is immortal

Dorcas does suggest the Claw works by warping time. So, maybe because Severian's life is stretched from primitive times to the far future, his energy source is sort of like a temporal rubber band always ready to snap? It would explain why Severian feels so drained after using his power.

I think the prevailing view is that his energy source is from another universe, the one higher than his own. There is an Asimov story (The Gods Themselves) from about the same time period which uses the same principle, though in that case (despite the book title) there is no spiritual aspect to the higher universe as there seems to be in BotNS. The higher universe is explained entirely in terms of physical laws- stronger nuclear force, weaker gravity, etc.

So, a leak (the White Fountain) from a higher universe to a lower one would allow an eternal, never diminishing energy source. Does that mean Urth's sun has become immortal? (I think Severian is immortal, so, perhaps).

Anyway, unlike Asimov, Wolfe ties travel to the higher universe to time travel in some sense. So perhaps your theory can be worked into the prevailing view in some manner?

2011-12-31 Jane Delawney replying to Lee Berman #

Argues against the theory that the Claw's healing powers work only after the White Fountain's light reaches Urth, citing Severian's early miracles in the primitive village. Responds to Lee Berman's quoted claim about Barbatus's hints and Apu Punchau's tomb, granting that the sunrise failure was the Yesodis's doing but noting Severian's own puzzlement at his earlier healings.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Miraculous powers are part of Severian himself; Claw's powers not tied to White Fountain light reaching Urth

Personally I've never been convinced by the 'powers only available after the White Fountain light reaches Urth / is above the horizon' theory (even though this is clearly posited in the text and appears for sure to be what [Wolfe tells us that] Severian believes) just because Sev / Apu Punchau performs miracles of healing (and resurrection? I forget. I don't have UOTNS in front of me now) from the time he arrives in the primitive village; it's part of why the villagers come to follow him in the first place.

Admitted that the most up-front 'miracle' of all, the failure of the sunrise, is caused by the Yesodis not by Severian; but Sev does elsewhere state explicitly that he performed healings and other works early in his stay in that era, and indeed I seem to remember he is actually puzzled by his ability to do this in the pre-White-Fountain era!

Perhaps the 'miraculous' powers are more part of Severian himself than even Severian realises.

jd

2012-01-01 Jeff Wilson replying to Jane Delawney #

Disputes the theory that Severian's healing powers work only when the White Fountain light is above Urth's horizon, arguing that his healing draws on power from the ground in daylight. Cites the Declan healing scene in chapter XXIX, where Severian's eyes glow rather than the Claw, then asks why the link works in the mines.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian's healing powers require White Fountain light above Urth's horizon

"The injured and the ill were brought to me for healing; some I cured as I had cured Declan, or as we of the guild had been taught to prolong the lives of our clients. Others died in my arms. Perhaps I could have revivified the dead as well, as I had recalled poor Zama; I never attempted to do so."

...but in CH XXIX "Among the Villagers", Declan's healing is described:

"After a moment I realized it was no longer quite so dark as it had been. A faint light played across him, moving with the movements of my eyes. My first thought was that it came from the thorn I kept in the little leather sack Dorcas had sewn for the Claw, though it seemed impossible that it could shine through the leather and my shirt in such a way. I took it out. It was as dark as it had been when I had tried to light the corridor outside my cabin with it, and I put it away again. "

"I pulled down the homespuns that had covered him and found he was naked beneath them. His right side was swollen, the lump the size of a child's head. I smoothed it away, thrilling to the power that surged out of Urth, through my legs and out my fingers."

So he's drawing on the power in the ground when he does this sort of healing, and his own eyes light up instead of the Claw.

It seems he has the gift to draw from different sources of energy to do his healing, at least. But if the Claw-White Fountain connection transcends time, it ought to work at any time of the day. If it can be blocked by the limb of Urth, why does it work in the mines on the ape?

2012-01-01 Jeff Wilson replying to Gerry Quinn #

Argues that Severian draws healing power from Urth itself rather than from the Claw, by walking through the sickroom sequence step by step. Responds to Gerry Quinn's doubt by citing the order of events: the room lightens with his eyes, the Claw is unlit, and the recovered man reports a glowing-eyed visitor.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Severian draws healing power from Urth not the Claw

No.

Yes, though this is apparently the first occasion.

The sequence is:

* Sev barricades the door and windows of the sickroom, which becomes dark.

* Sev notices the room is too dark for him to see the sick man.

* Sev notices the room lightens.

* Sev notices the light moves with his eyes.

* Sev checks to see if the Claw is somehow casting the light, but it is still in its bag in his shirt and when he peeks at it, it is not lit at all.

* Sev chats with the sick man.

* Sev smooths out the lump as he feels the power flow up through him.

* Later, the recovered man confirms he was visited in the sickroom by a figure with glowing eyes.

Sev's later healing and restoration of Zama to life is similarly accompanied by eye-glowing and visceral sensations.

From: Jeff Wilson

Do we really know he is drawing on a power from Urth? That is how he interprets it, but on what evidence? He doesn?t really feel the source, or he would not first have checked the Claw.

That said, he drained the batteries of the Ship at one point, so clearly he could draw power from there. But the power he has is connected with an ability to reverse the flow of time. This is plausible for a white hole, and perhaps also for a Yesodi ship.

I would speculate that he draws only on such sources, and the White Fountain is not truly blocked by Urth. But perhaps there are other sources. The Cumaean, for example, draws on the power of an ancient creature on the planet of a distant star to accomplish her own temporal delvings.

- Gerry Quinn

2012-01-02 Lee Berman replying to Larry Miller #

Argues that Severian's power comes mainly from within himself, and that the Stone Town meeting in The Claw of the Conciliator was staged so the Cumaean would draw on his nascent power to contact Apu Punchau. Points to Hildegrin's resurrection work, the Cumaean's lack of control, and Merryn's hint that Apu Punchau suggests the Conciliator, asking whether Severian's arrival at the seance is coincidence.
proposes · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Stone Town meeting was staged to draw on Severian's power; Apu Punchau is linked to the Conciliator

Yep, I think this supports Jane's contention that Severian's power resides mostly

within himself.

So perhaps he can draw from various celestial bodies (Tzadkiel's ship being a small

version of one) and perhaps his powers vary based on what body he is drawing from?

I have my doubt about this (though the being on Fomalhaut might really provide a temporal

touchstone). I think the Stone Town meeting was a set-up and that the Cumaean is really

drawing from Severian's nascent power, as planned. Her plan is to contact Apu Punchau, of

all people in history....and Severian is right there. Coincidence? hm....

She doesn't seem to be quite in control of the situation and I don't think she's had many

opportunities to do it before. Hildegrin says Apu Punchau is someone who can "make a

difference". It's to be the crowning achievement of Hildegrein's career of resurrecting the

dead. Merryn offers a vague explanation of him which kind of suggests he is The Conciliator.

Are we to think it is all a coincidence that Severian happens to stumble into a seance which

is meant to contact Apu Punchau and The Conciliator?
