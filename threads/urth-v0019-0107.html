The thread: The thread began as a dispute over whether the Ascian archive in The Book of the New Sun is guarded by librarians rather than taluses, and whether Ymar rather than Typhon founded it, with Alice Turner citing Ultan as a Borges tribute and Severian's own visits. Peter Stephenson questioned whether anything rules out the obvious library reading and raised doubts about Severian's family tree. David Duffy supplied dictionary entries for Greek words like apheta and offered folk etymologies for Peace and Excalibur. Kieran Mullen then drifted into a debate about the nature of science, invoking Feynman and warning against 'cargo cult exegesis' in Wolfe criticism. Michael Straight joked about that phrase and encouraged posting, so the thread ended in drift rather than resolution.

1998-10-13 Alice Turner #

Replies to a thread about the Ascian library in The Book of the New Sun, arguing that the archive is guarded by librarians rather than taluses and that Ymar, not Typhon, founded it. Supports the claim by pointing to Ultan as a Borges tribute, to Cyriaca's storytelling style, and to Severian's own visits to the library.
disputes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion · theories: Ymar founded the library, not Typhon; Ultan is a tribute to Borges

From Robert Borski

Well, I suppose flattery will coax me out of sulkdom for a moment. First, Mr. B., unlike prion, who is almost your equal in groundless invention, I must say that I think your recent geneological ventures go beyond the far-fetched into the realm where undines swim among the stars.

Second, no, I don't think for a moment that the archives/library is guarded by taluses, I think it is "guarded" by librarians, of whom the latest guardian is Ultan, who, I need hardly remind you all, is a tribute to Borges. And if you doubt that for a moment, please pick up a copy of the splendid new fiction omnibus and compare Cyriaca's style in telling this tale to that of Borges in many stories. I put "guarded" in quotes, because the library is only a legend to everyone but neighbors and the House Absolute, and would hardly need military protection.

Sev says that he knows more of it (the library) than she because, unlike her, he has been there. But he has not before heard its history. What has the Claw to do with that? And I would propose Ymar to have started the library, not Typhon--there are hints in that direction but not the other.

And the giant mechanical soldiers which recognize either a true autarch--even to come--or a true Concilliator (or do you argue that they are taluses?), guard Mt. Typhon. This seems to me simply a remote project that ran out of funds and political interest and has been forgotten. An interestingly similar picture of an abandoned early space project in (I think) Arizona ran in today's NY Times. Cf (and not by Wolfean accident) Ozymandius. The Saltus mine is something else again. If you really do lure me back, I'm only going to demand that you stay on track. Do you really want that?

-alga

1998-10-14 Peter Stephenson #

Asks whether anything in The Book of the New Sun shows that the place with the volumes, scrolls and succession of guardians cannot be the library, as the obvious explanation suggests. Responds to alga's claim that the archive is guarded by librarians, not taluses, and notes doubt about Severian's family tree while deferring a joke about the Inn of Lost Loves.
questions · close-textual-reading · theories: the archive is the library guarded by librarians

absolutely... even if I can't follow Severian's family tree [is there a pun about this at the Inn of Lost Loves? maybe I shouldn't start this one off] it does appear to be pretty clear this time. Is there any evidence in the story that the place with the volumes, scrolls and the series of guardians *can't* be the library, which is the obvious explanation? I worry about accepting obvious explanations, too, but you can't really dispose of them till they fail to fit.

corncrake

1998-10-14 David Duffy #

Reports dictionary entries for apheta and related Greek words, and offers two folk etymologies: brand-new in Peace and Excalibur's supposed derivation from Ex Calibor in Castleview. Cites the Shorter Oxford and an Arthuriana review of Castleview as sources, and notes that the dictionary includes many Book of the New Sun words including Severian.
informs · source-and-allusion

My Shorter Oxford has apheta as "M 17 Gk starter or sender off = HYLEG

hyleg E 17 Pers celestial indicator of the length of a newborn child's life. Astrol. The giver of life in a nativity.

Amusingly, the adjacent item was hyle Gk primordial matter of universe.

I have two possibly deceptive or just nice folk entymologies: In _Peace_, the brand new axe on the first page is suggested by Weer to be branded new. The SOD suggests it burning (brand) new. And in _Castleview_, Morgaine says the name of Excalibur derives from Ex Calibor, although it's origin is in fact as a corruption of the Welsh -- is this in Tennyson perhaps? M. is a pretty unreliable informant anyway, I guess. This latter nitpick comes from a review of _Castleview in the journal Arthuriana (1993 3:66), which has a few interesting thoughts.

BTW the Shorter Oxford has many BOtNS words and names, incl Severian.

David Duffy.

1998-10-14 Kieran Mullen #

Argues that science proceeds by critical rigor rather than mere guessing, citing the fractional quantum Hall Nobel prize as experiment-driven and Bose-Einstein condensation as hypothesis-driven. Responds to Kieran's earlier claim that science is a guessing game by invoking Feynman's rules of the game and warning against 'cargo cult exegesis' in Wolfe criticism.
disputes

Sometimes.

Hmm. Latest Nobel prize in physics (fractional quantum Hall effect) was not based on theoretical predictions, but pushing the experiments to a different regime of parameters. The one before (Bose-Einstein condensation) *was* based on a hypothesis.

Only if you know what you're looking for ahead of time (i.e. BEC).

I know that failures outnumber successes. I've had a lot of the form and few of the latter. But one skill one has to teach student's is the proper tweaking of the "bogo-meter": the ability to tell when a discussion or hypothesis has gone beyond the evidence. Feynman, who was not known for being an orthodox stick-in-the-mud, wrote about this as ``playing by the rules of the game''. To paraphrase him, You can be as inventive as you want - as long as you play by the rules.

I admit the rules of discourse are a bit blurry, especially in literary criticism. But surely there must be *some* bounds on hermeneutics?

No, I think my point is that part of the intellectual labor is being critical. As you point out, ideas are a dime a dozen. *Good* ideas are a bit more dear.

Of course not. I'm not sure how this follows logically from the above discussion.

This is weird, a complete inverse of how I see the discussion. I view some of the postings here as "Cargo cult exegesis" since they follow the *form* of interpreting a work, but don't seem to understand the standards that apply. How can a demand for a bit more rigor be turned into an exhortation to meaningless ritual?

Interesting allusions: cold fusion and Dean drives both were extraordinary claims that required extraordinary proof. Their eventual discrediting is a tribute to rational science and the danger of not being properly self-critical. Cold fusion is an especially interesting case of an "us vs. them" mentality developing in a research group.

Sorry to go on at such length, but the issue of science and pseudo-science is one about which I feel strongly.

1998-10-15 Michael Straight #

Jokingly praises Kieran Mullen's phrase "cargo cult exegesis" and off-topically suggests MacIntyre's After Virtue describes modern moral philosophy as cargo cult ethics. Also defends Mr. Borski's wild theories and urges readers not to be discouraged from posting, or to rebut criticism or ignore it.
chats

I love this sentence so much I think I'm going to print it out and sleep with it under my pillow.

"Cargo cult exegesis"--bwahahahaha!

To go further off-topic, anyone here read Alasdair MacIntyre's AFTER VIRTUE? It occurs to me that that's the perfect phrase for what he thinks has happened to contemporary moral philosophy: Cargo Cult Ethics.

For the record, Rostrum loves reading Mr. Borski's theories (the wilder the better) and hopes that he doesn't get discouraged by our criticism, and that other people don't allow him to discourage them from posting more "mainstream" discussion. If you think some of what you're seeing here is cargo cult exegesis, you can rebutt it or just ignore it and start a discussion you like better.

-Rostrum
