The thread: Aaron reported commercial CD audiobook copies of Wolfe's works, originally made for the blind, and asked how to inform Wolfe or his agent. Mark Millman argued the SFWA lacks authority to act for members and suggested contacting the literary agent, asking who it is after Virginia Kidd's death. Gwern0 questioned whether burned CDs were infringing given first-sale doctrine and urged caution about Aaron's uncertain account of the seller's format conversion. Aaron held that selling the copies is wrong whatever their origin and that the material is pirated either way. Matthew Keeley suggested the SFWA might handle it, relying only on its membership listing. The thread ended without resolution of the legal question or agreed contact.
  • aaron: Selling blind-edition Wolfe audiobooks on CD is piracy and exploitation regardless of first-sale arguments
  • Mark Millman: Contact Wolfe's literary agent, not the SFWA, which lacks authority to act for members

  • Matthew Keeley: The SFWA may handle it, since it lists Wolfe as a member

2008-02-05 Mark Millman replying to Matthew Keeley #

Argues that the SFWA is the wrong body to contact about pirated Wolfe audiobooks sold on CD, since it lacks authority to act for members and once acted against authors' wishes. Suggests Wolfe's literary agent should be notified instead, and asks the list whether anyone knows who the agent is now that Virginia Kidd has died.
proposes

Folks,

The SFWA probably are not the right contact for this. Not long ago they tried to act, as they thought, on behalf of some of their members with regard to some electronic rights issues. It turned out, if I remember correctly, that the SFWA were not empowered to act for any of those members, and for some authors--I recall in particular Cory Doctorow--their actions were in direct opposition to the authors' wishes.

Mr. Wolfe's agent would be a much better person to notify. The agent is responsible for protecting the author's rights, and will also know whether or not any particular publication is authorized. Regrettably, since Virginia Kidd's death, I no longer know who that may be. Do any of you have that information?

Best,

Mark Millman

2008-02-05 Gwern Branwen replying to aaron #

Argues that the seller's burned audiobook CDs may not be infringing, since a physical copy bought or given away can usually be resold under first-sale doctrine, and urges a measured tone until the facts are known. Responds to Aaron's post, which quotes a complaint about pirated Wolfe audiobooks originally made for the blind, by noting that Aaron's own account of the seller's conversion from MP3 to CD is uncertain.
disputes · genre-and-craft

On 2008.02.05 14:24:34 -0500, aaron <[email]> scribbled 2.8K characters:

I wouldn't go off half-cocked, if I were you. The situation is not 100% clear. Reading through it, it *seems* this fellow downloaded the MP3 files, converted them to WAV, and burned them to CD and is now offering the CDs for sale. If that's true, that's quite likely a copyvio.

But suppose he bought or was given the CDs, then he probably has every right to sell them. Physical objects don't really come with EULAs (first-sale doctrine and so on). So adopting a temperate tone until more is known would be a good idea.
