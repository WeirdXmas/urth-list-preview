Tale at the end of PEACE

Urth list · 1 messages · 1999-10-26 to 1999-10-26

1999urthMichael Straight

Themes: Stories within stories

1999-10-26 Michael Straight #

Recalls the tale of the Sidhe at the end of Peace as a favourite and asks how it would end if abstracted from Weer's narrative, whether the siblings die at once or wander old after baptism. Responds to Nicholas Gevers's note that the tale was published separately in F&SF, and says the poster retold it from memory and misremembered the ending.
questions · close-textual-reading

Speaking of tales out of context. The tale of the Sidhe at the end of _Peace_ is still one of my favorite things Wolfe has written. I tried to tell it from memory to some friends recently and got it mostly right (I couldn't remember the name of the hero whose arrows were plumed with the flocks feathers), but I mistakenly said that that the three siblings grew old and died at the end when Weer's narrative simply reveals that they had grown extremely old and then moves on (sort of).

The Sidhe are tied up symbolically with Weer, of course, but if you were to try to abstract this story from Weer's like I did, how would you end it? Do the Sidhe die immediately, or are we to imagine them wandering around for a bit in their old age after their baptism? Or would you just read it straight as Wolfe wrote it and let the listener cope with the abrupt ending?

The ideal solution, of course, is to pass out copies of PEACE to all my friends.

-Rostrum
