2001-04-18 Marc Aramini #

Argues that the four main characters of Free Live Free each embody one of the Seven Deadly Sins, with Stubb's sin disputed between pride and envy. Responds to James Jordan's recollection of a Wolfe interview naming Madame Serpentina as pride and Stubb as envy, and questions whether the Ziesing edition differs from the original text.
supports · myth-and-structure · author-and-biography · theories: Free Live Free characters embody the deadly sins; Stubb embodies envy mixed with pride

James Jordan wrote

As far as I remember from a Wolfe interview, I thought he identified Madame Serpentina as pride (she is a haughty wench) and Stubb as Envy. I think Wolfe waffled a little on identifying Stubb (little man complex seems like envy mixed with pride to me). (Is there a big difference in the Ziesing version of the text? I can't imagine editing a Wolfe work down by thousands of words. Who knows what subtle details were destroyed? Only Wolfe. Maybe not even him.) I readily agree with your analysis of the Wizard of Oz parallels. Marc Aramini

2001-04-19 Michael Straight #

Argues that Stubb's temptation in Free Live Free fits Pride rather than Envy, since it appeals to his wish to seem clever and essential, and that Serpentina's unseen temptation of supernatural power fits Avarice. Responds to maa32's recollection of a Wolfe interview by testing each character's temptation scene against the Seven Deadly Sins and asking whether all sins fit under them.
proposes · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: Stubb's sin is Pride rather than Envy; Serpentina's temptation is Avarice

I think the key has to be each one's temptation scene. Stubb's seems to be appealing to his desire to be thought smart and essential for an important case (and appealing to the young woman), thus Pride (Envy involves resenting someone else who has what you don't). We don't see Serpentina's but it seems to have been about giving her a shot at real supernatural power which seems to fit best under Avarice (maybe "lust for power"? Or do the Seven Deadlies put that under Avarice and reserve Lust for sexual sins? Do all sins fit under one of the Seven?).

-Rostrum
