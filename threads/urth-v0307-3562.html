The thread: The thread asked whether Severian is the original Conciliator and Apu-Punchau, and whether the Claw and the two Severians are linked by time travel or a time loop. Michael Andre-Driussi argued the Claw is a fragment of the first Severian and that the shadowy man ahead is Severian's future self, while Lisa Schaffer-Doggett first disputed the identity (suggesting an earlier failed Severian, Ymar) and later argued the first Severian was not erased. James Jordan held that the Claw is a sacrament derived from the present Severian, and Josh Geller argued the two Severians are one person split across timelines. The thread then shifted to the torturer argument, which Don Palumbo and Chris debated, and to time-loop comparisons with Heinlein and film. It ended without consensus, drifting from the Apu-Punchau question into Severian's torturer background.
  • Michael Andre-Driussi: the Claw is a fragment of the first Severian, and the shadowy man is Severian's future self in a time loop
  • Lisa Schaffer-Doggett: an earlier failed Severian, possibly Ymar, was the original figure; later argued both Severians share one timeline
  • James Jordan: the Claw is a sacrament derived from the present Severian, who could resurrect where earlier Severians could not
  • Josh Geller: the two Severians are one person split across timelines
  • Don Palumbo: Severian succeeds because he is or thinks like a professional torturer
  • Chris: Severian's torturer background is practically explicit but may not matter if he is an unconscious agent

Themes: Time loops and causality, Doubles, twins and clones, Robots, androids and artificial life

2003-07-05 Marc Aramini #

Proposes that Severian is both the original conciliator and the original Apu-Punchau, so his time travel made the later figures possible, and asks whether a vivimancer call links the two. Grounds this in the Cumean's description of Apu Punchau as a vivimancer in Claw and in Severian's dream of a fountain, river, and the witches in Citadel, which the poster suggests ties the witches to a flood.
proposes · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Severian is the original Apu-Punchau; Apu-Punchau is a vivimancer calling the living from death

In response to Don's comments, I have a brief question about synchronicity. So you don't think Severian is really the original Apu-Punchau? See, I always saw him as both the original conciliator and the original Apu-Punchau (ie - if he didn't go back in time, those guys would never have lived). The description of Apu Punchau in Claw led me to believe that he was doing the calling, which was facilitated by the Cumean, because she calls him a vivimancer: somebody who calls to the living from the realms of the dead. What else qualifies somebody to be a vivimancer? Of course, I recognize that the two callings would have to be at different points, and that the Cumean and Merryn are probably not present ... BUT there is that freaky, freaky dream of Severian's in Citadel of the autarch where a big fountain and a river shows up and Merryn and the Cummean are screwing around (surely you remember the dream - it was the most confusing part of the four books for me). Maybe that is a clue - I should find that dream and read it over again - especially since it directly ties those witches with a flood and a waterfall.

Do you think the ship causes the long night? And why did the moon get closer and become green?

I'm just trying to make sense of things ... Don't take this idea too seriously - I don't hold it as dearly as I maintain that Green is Urth, or that Horn is in Babbie (two convictions which I will probably take to my grave). This is just testing the water.

Take care.
Marc Aramini

2003-07-05 Lisa Schaffer-Doggett #

Disputes the idea that Severian is the original Apu-Punchau and original Conciliator, arguing instead that an earlier failed Severian, possibly Ymar, was the original figure while Severian was modified by the Hieros. Grounds this in the name Ymar meaning ox and Aleph, and suggests Severian's time-travel was unnecessary, a bid for power that failed.
disputes · puzzle-solving · source-and-allusion · theories: Ymar is the original Severian; Original Conciliator was a failed Severian; Severian contains Apu-Punchau and Conciliator

Don replies,

When I first read UotNS, before I read BotNS the second time and before delving much into Graves I did think that Severian was the original Conciliator and the original Apu-Punchau. And in a sense he is but I think also that he isn't because as he said himself he isn't the first "Severian" (I put this in quotation marks because I think the original Severian is in fact Ymar, the name meaning ox, which also means Aleph, which can also mean the first or the origin but that's a separate argument. One I can't resist bringing up, sort of my own little "Carthage must be destroyed!"). I think the original Conciliator and the original Apu-Punchau is the failed "Severian" (whoever he may be. Ymar. Ymar.) whereas our Severian is the one who has been modified by the Hieros to be the New Sun. Personally, I'm beginning to think that It was altogether unnecessary for our Severian to go back in time to fulfill a role as either of these two past figures. I think it may have been a fulfillment of some dark ambition to assume these roles, maybe to salvage something good for himself since his destiny seemed to be that of a tyrant and destroyer. Even this doesn't seem to work out so well for him though. But I'm slipping away from my point which is simply that no, I don't strictly believe that Severian is the original Apu-Punchau or the original Conciliator. I would rather say that he contains them both and then some.

2003-07-05 Brandon Mason #

Proposes that Gene Wolfe first meant someone other than the Severian of the later books, perhaps the first Severian or another figure, to be the Conciliator and Apu-Punchau, and that he changed this plan when writing The Urth of the New Sun. Offers no textual evidence, relying only on the poster's sense of how the books developed.
proposes · author-and-biography · theories: Severian 2 was not originally intended to be the Conciliator and Apu-Punchau

I have always just thought that Gene Wolfe didn't
originally intend for the Severian that we know
(Severian 2) to be the Conciliator and Apu-Punchau.
He might have intended these roles to be the first
Severian, or someone else entirely. But then when
Urth of the New Sun came along I think he changed his mind.

2003-07-05 Josh Geller #

Argues that the two Severians in The Book of the New Sun are one person split across timelines, as shown by his deaths and bifurcations at the duel with Agilus and on Tzadkiel's ship. Grounds this in Severian closing his old body's eyes on the ship and in the war scene where soldiers in formation face random shelling, linking it to Wolfe's own infantry service.
proposes · close-textual-reading · author-and-biography · theories: Severian's deaths split his timeline into parallel instances; Severian and Apu-Punchau are one split person

I don't think this is so.

I don't think you are understanding the diffference, and the sameness, between (amongst?) the two Severians. In fact, to talk of two Severians is in some fashion an extended metaphor, for all that there are at least two bodies that we are talking about. Severian dies several times in the story. In each case there is a bifurcation in his timeline. I say there are several instances, and I can call to mind two right away: At the duel with Agilus and on Tzadkiel's ship.

On Tzadkiel's ship (perhaps because of the special conditions which are inherent to the ship) he is able to close the eyes of his old body.

Not everyone experiences these things. To a person who has not experienced these things, it may seem that this is some kind of fictional strophe or analogy instead of a sober description of fact.

Remember the scene in the war, where Severian watches a formation of 'primitive' soldiers enduring bombardment, standing in formation, each doing his personal magick, oblivious to whether others live or die. The shells can go one way or another. If they go one way, one dies. If they go another way, another dies. Perhaps there in other worlds, other sets of them are alive and sound, or dead, or maimed. Perhaps there are a very large number of these worlds.

Gene Wolfe was an infantryman in Korea.

.

2003-07-05 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Proposes that the Claw is the hologramic, fragmentary presence of the first Severian, who did not possess the Claw and shapes the Severian we see by granting or denying miraculous powers. Responds to Brandon Mason's suggestion that Wolfe first meant Severian to be the Conciliator and Apu-Punchau, citing the passage where Severian refers to the first Severian.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: The Claw is the fragmentary presence of the first Severian; First Severian is semi-puppetmaster of the Severian we see

Because I have a hard time envisioning the Claw as either a dumb object or as a mere "focus" for Severian's current/latent power, I tend to think (insist) that the Claw is the (hologramic, fragmentary; blood magic) presence of the "first Severian" that Severian is referring to in the passage: that is, the first Severian who, I believe we are told, did =not= possess the Claw.

In this manner, by granting/denying miraculous powers at various junctures (revive this dead, fail to revive that one; cast a light now; etc.), the first Severian becomes, in part, author of the Severian we see. Or semi-puppetmaster.

2003-07-06 Lisa Schaffer-Doggett #

Argues that the Severian who died in the tomb and our Severian share one universe and timeline, so the first Severian was not erased by the later one, and that Wolfe deliberately blurred the question with the line "I am not the first Severian." Responds to Michael Andre-Driussi's claim that the Claw is a fragment of the first Severian by agreeing in general, then citing the Mausoleum's rose as a possible pun on the name.
supports · allegory-and-typology · close-textual-reading · theories: Ymar is the first Severian, who lived and died separately; Many Severians exist across the books, including Little Severian and Palaemon

Don repies, and then replies,

I agree with this in general. Regardless of whether Ymar is the first Severian (and I am unshakeable in my own belief in this) the original "Severian" and his timeline were not eradicated by the coming of the second Severian (or umpteenth "Severian" imho). His tomb exists and he's in it. He lived and he died in the same universe as our Severian who has his own separate memorial and who doesn't "die" in the sense that someone finds his body and puts it in a tomb. He dies and becomes an eidolon of the New Sun but it's still essentially our Severian. I think that Wolfe intentionally (and brilliantly) confused this issue by having Severian (ours) say "I am not the first Severian." There is no reason at all that the first "Severian" need go by that name any more than the Second Coming of the Messiah be named Jesus, Joseph, Joshua or whatever. "A rose by any other name. . ." Which leads me to wonder if the rose on the Mausoleum is a bit of a pun. Probably not. As I've said before, I think there are a lot of "Severians" out there, little Severian, the Mandragora, Palaemon, Ouen, and who knows how many others.

2003-07-09 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Argues that the shadowy man ahead of Severian is his own future self, and that the Claw is a sliver of the first Severian, sending hints through a time loop that overwrites and improves the narrator. Grounds this in the narrator's account of agents of Yesod retroactively altering the horserace after the test, and compares it to Heinlein's Door into Summer and the films Groundhog Day and Donnie Darko.
proposes · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Shadowy man is Severian's future self; Claw is a sliver of Sev1; Sev1 is overwritten by Sev2 through time loop

Who is that shadowy man striding always 15 years ahead of you? It is my future self, sending me hints for threading the maze that is being built up around me.

In TBOTNS, narrative Severian has a sense that there is a future self doing things for which narrative Severian receives goods and services. He says that once the first Severian passed the test, agents of Yesod came down the timeline to investigate, retroactively complicating the horserace after it was done.

The first Severian ("Severian the Cruel," as I say) passed the test, and then went on to become the Conciliator. At the moment he gave the blood-soaked thorn to the soldiers, the Claw was born (like a sliver or mini-shedding of Sev1 at that time -- like little Tzadkiel, the banished one). About 1000 years later this item becomes a teacher of Sev2 (our narrator), no less a teacher than Thecla and Dorcas and all the other "new" details thrown up to retroactively make the horserace more intense. The more difficult maze is such that the effort to get through it really improves Sev2 into the optimum version of himself, and Sev1 is overwritten.

Time travel "message to self" loop thingie. In Heinlein you have DOOR INTO SUMMER. In movies you have either "Groundhog Day," where the narrative hero is going through every loop from beginning to optimized end; or something like "Donny Darko," where it is the final loop only and thus more like DOOR INTO SUMMER (and Severian's Narrative).

=mantis=

2003-07-09 Don Palumbo #

Proposes that Severian succeeds in bringing the New Sun because he is, or still thinks like, a professional torturer, since ending the Old Urth requires a torturer's equanimity. Responds to James Jordan's point about Severian's resurrection powers and the Claw as a sacrament, and asks whether others have made the torturer argument.
proposes · myth-and-structure · theories: Severian succeeds because he is a professional torturer

One interesting possibility as to why Severian is successful in bringing the New Sun is that he IS a professional torturer, or at least still has the mind-set and sensibility of a professional torturer. I feel this is crucial to his task. Bringing the New Sun requires the destruction of the Old Urth, and only a professional torturer would have the necessary equanimity. New to the list, I wonder if anyone else has mentioned that only he can bring the New Sun BECAUSE he has been a torturer.

--Don Palumbo

Argues that Severian's background as a professional torturer is practically explicit in the text and suits his role in bringing the New Sun, though Chris doubts the torturer detail matters if Severian is merely an unconscious agent. Responds to a quoted post by working through the Kabbalistic references and questioning whether Severian understood the destruction he would cause.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theology-and-religion · theories: Severian is an unconscious agent bringing the New Sun

I think this is practically explicit in the text, though I don't recall if it's been brought up here. Just to play the devil's advocate for a second, however, it always struck me as a little odd that Severian did not seem to understand the destruction he'd be bringing for such a long time.
