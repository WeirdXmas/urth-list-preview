Science catches up to the New Sun

The thread: Participants discussed whether a news report of a cyclically contracting, bouncing universe illuminates the successive universes in The Book of the New Sun. David Stockhoff argued Briah is cyclical while Yesod is not, linking the model to the manvantara and Apheta's generation and countergeneration, and asked how species could survive between universes. David Duffy countered that a big crunch was once considered likely but the universe now looks flat, and offered Stapledon's Star Maker as a model of atemporal succession. The thread ended without resolution, with the science and the text pulling in different directions.

2008-12-12 David Stockhoff #

Argues that the cyclically contracting universe theory in the news clarifies the vague succession of universes in The Book of the New Sun, suggesting Briah is cyclical and Yesod is not. Builds on a quoted report of a bouncing-universe model, noting it resembles the manvantara model and Apheta's generation and countergeneration, and asks how species could survive universe to universe.
proposes · theology-and-religion · myth-and-structure · theories: Briah is cyclical while Yesod is not

Some kind of cyclically expanding/contracting universe theory has been around for at least several decades, if not more. But this is cool to read about.

It addresses something that has always bothered me when I read TBotNS:

That the various Hieros are rather vague on the concept of successive "universes." The word gets thrown around a lot, almost casually: you have stacked universes, sequential universes, and so on. How does one escape a universe when it implodes? Was Yesod always there, or was it created?

But if a universe is never totally destroyed, then you can imagine whole species surviving from one to the next with some predictability. Without that predictability, Apheta's theory of generation and countergeneration can't possibly go on forever. Eventually, the chain will break if every universe ends in a great melodramatic catastrophe.

Perhaps one could simply categorize Briah as a universe subject to cyclical contractions (thus, multiple bodies and a single consciousness, like Severian), and Yesod subject to none (like the Increate). The manvantara model dug up by Witz resembles this.

I'm pretty sure this isn't the first time this theory has been bandied about, but this sounds pretty much like the cosmology of the New Sun, except it's been posited as reality:

Most cosmologists believe that our universe emerged from a singularity during the Big Bang. But now physicists are exploring the possibility that our universe was created by the death of an earlier universe.

The researchers have found that when applying LQC, the universe does not revert back to a singularity as it contracts. Instead of seeing a big bang, the models indicate that the universe experienced a big bounce, with a predecessor universe contracting as it ended and then reemerging as our new, expanding universe. If the theory proves correct, it could mean that our universe does not have a finite beginning and end but is, instead, part of a chain of universes that expand and then contract to give way to a brand new universe.

2008-12-13 David Duffy replying to David Stockhoff #

Notes that a big crunch and restart was once considered likely when The Book of the New Sun was written, but the universe now appears flat, and suggests Olaf Stapledon's Star Maker as a model of progressive but atemporal cosmic succession. Responds to David Stockhoff's remark that cyclical cosmology has long existed and his question about how one escapes a collapsing universe in the Hieros' talk of successive universes.

2008-12-13 James Wynn replying to David Stockhoff #

Argues that because time collapses between successive universes, the idea of sequential universes in the Hieros must be either naive or mean something different, and that successive universes would be a theoretical model. Responds to David Stockhoff's question about escaping an imploding universe by suggesting the Hieros move between adjacent and distant parallel worlds, citing There Are Doors.
proposes · theology-and-religion · theories: Hieros move between parallel worlds

Since Time collapses with successive universes, the idea of "sequentiality" between universes would be either naive or would mean something totally different from what we mean by it. If we assume that the Hieros are "causality-based", then successive universes would be a theoretical model rather than an observed phenomena. It would mean that the Hieros have the ability to move between parallel worlds...some adjacent and others not...like those in "There Are Doors".
