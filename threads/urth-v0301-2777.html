2003-01-23 Andrew Bollen #

Proposes that the confusion at the end of On Blue's Waters is Babbie-into-Horn rather than Horn-into-Babbie, with a Vanished God or the Vanisher possessing Babbie and calling him away from the sleeping Horn. Grounds the reading in details of what Babbie takes and leaves behind, and in the inhumi's departure, while admitting it leaves the agency's identity and the reason for the move unexplained.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Babbie is possessed by the agency and moves into Horn; The Vanished God or VP shifts Babbie's spirit into Horn; Evensong is a Han spy

Just re-readring that enigmatic scene at the end of OBW, where Horn & = Babbie seem to become confused with each other.=20

Rather than this being Horn-into-Babbie, as I think Marc suggested, I = believe it is Babbie-into-Horn.=20

- Horn, exhausted, is in imminent danger from the inhumi. He has let the = fire burn down, he sleeps, the inhumi close in ...
- Some agency (perhaps VP, perhaps a "Vanished God") shifts Babbie's = strong, single-minded, Horn-devoted spirit into Horn's body. We know the = VP can do this kind of thing.
- The agency calls to Babbie in Horn's voice - presumably to get Babbie = to move Horn away from the dangerous place?
- He knows the inhumi are gone, thru some animal sense.
- Babbie feels a doggy compulsion to obey the call. He ignores sleeping = Evensong as irrelevant. He doesn't take boots & stockings (which of = course Babbie would think superfluous), or knife & sword (he already has = "two knives"!). He's wearing trousers already, and he does take tunic & = robe (why? maybe because it's cold? maybe because there's enough of Horn = functioning to select these, with the all-important pen case & = manuscript in the pocket of the robe?).
- He finds the agency in the forest, and goes for comforting in a doggy = kind of way.=20
- The possession ends when it's no longer necessary.

This doesn't explain why Horn must go to the agency, rather than the = agency to Horn, or really why it was so urgent that Horn go anywhere at = all, if the agency has the power to disperse the inhumi; or who the = agency is. But I think this is pretty much what was going on here.=20

(Actually, maybe the danger is more from Evensong at this point. She's = probably a Han spy. She's insistent that he tell her the Secret "this = night". Maybe she's acting under instructions to find out as much as = possible from him before killing him. With the inhumi all around, it's = probably time to finish things off & head home ... )=20

2003-01-23 Andrew Bollen #

Argues that the suicidal despair in the Babbie/Horn scene of On Blue's Waters belongs to the unhealed Silk rather than Horn. Grounds this in Silk's guilt and knowing risk-taking, and parallels Babbie's comfort to Horn's earlier strength, seeing Silk and Horn integrate by In Green's Jungles.
proposes · close-textual-reading · myth-and-structure · theories: Silk, not Horn, drives the suicidal despair in On Blue's Waters; Silk knowingly courts death near Evensong and the inhumi; Babbie's comfort recapitulates Horn's rescue of Silk after Hyacinth

Just a couple of other thoughts on this.=20

The second to last paragraph of OBW has Silk/Horn quoting his little = song about fair young girls living to deceive one, followed immediately = by a reference to Evensong. It seems to me pretty clear that Silk/Horn = knows, and always knew, that Evensong was deceitful. And surely in a = fairly large way also: why would have she come with him unless she had = some large designs? (BTW, I think she is a little gem of a character: = incredibely brave, resourceful & loyal to Han, when one thinks about = it.)

Returning to the Babbie/Horn scene, I think at this point, with Horn = exhausted, Silk starts to surface. (There has been a recent, = characteristic Hyacinth dream.) Whenever Silk/Horn becomes guilt-wracked = & despairing & suicidal I think it is mainly the unhealed Silk speaking, = more than Horn. He is certainly guilt-wracked at this point:=20

"By not returning to Gaon ... I had put her into deadly danger; and my = presence kept her there."

Knowing that Evensong is his enemy, yet still feeling as much for her as = if she were his friend, is surely Silk-think, not Horn-think.

Perhaps the SIlky part is hoping for death at this point, maybe from = Evensong, maybe from the inhumi, and he puts himself at risk = deliberately (fire burning down, half-hearted attempts to guard against = inhumi, gives Evensong sword & knife & valuables).=20

Just as the VP "lent" him the strength of tough Horn when he was = suicidal over Hyacinth's death, now they or somebody temporarily (?) = lend him the strength of brave Babbie. It's a neat parallel, = recapitulating the Silk/Horn story in miniature.=20

Now I think the comforting from the mysterious figure in forrest is very = important in Silk's "therapy". The "modulation" of the Silk/Horn = character changes greatly between OBW and the beginning of IGJ. To me, = it's not so much that Silk starts to emerge, as that Silk & Horn become = more integrated in their best characteristics, as I've said before. = Silk's charisma & goodness without his ... well, soppiness; and Horn's = toughness & practicality without his sour streak. *Something* about the = experience in the forrest did this.=20
