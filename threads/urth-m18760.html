The thread: The thread began when NPR's summer science fiction list omitted The Book of the New Sun, and Andrew Mason and others first argued over whether Wolfe's Sun cycle is SF or fantasy. Debate then turned to the best order for reading the Sun books: Daniel Petersen and Mo Holkar favored starting with On Blue's Waters, while Marc Aramini argued for reading Long Sun and New Sun first, and Stuart Hamm called Short Sun entertaining on its own but richer after the others. Petersen pushed back, saying the list overvalues puzzle-solving over wonder. Most later posts disputed the list's scope, which Jordon Flato, Lane Haygood and Nick Lee clarified as a roundup of new 2011 books rather than an all-time best-of, and the thread ended in jokes and a brief puppet-video aside.
  • Andrew Mason: Sun cycle is both SF and fantasy, so a genre split blurs on Wolfe
  • Daniel Petersen: Short Sun is a fine entry point despite spoilers; list overvalues puzzles over wonder
  • Marc Aramini: Read Long Sun and New Sun before Short Sun to keep the echoes
  • Stuart Hamm: Short Sun is the most entertaining standalone but rewards prior reading
  • Jordon Flato: NPR list is weak and omitting New Sun is ridiculous, later retracted after learning the scope
  • Nick Lee: NPR list recommends new 2011 books for summer, not an all-time best-of

Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Genre: science fiction, fantasy, both

2011-06-23 Daniel Petersen replying to Andrew Mason #

Reports the poster's own experience of reading On Blue's Waters before The Book of the Long Sun, then rereading Short Sun, and how this reduced the mystery they would otherwise have met. Mentions John Clute's review wondering whether starting Wolfe with Blue is the best approach, and says the poster has wondered the same.
informs

You know, I actually did read On Blue's Waters, then stopped and went back and read Long Sun before then re-reading Blue and the rest of Short Sun. It was indeed a very, very strange experience - but hugely enticing. Unfortunately it took away some of the mystery I think I would have encountered in reading Long Sun if I had had no or little clue what was coming.

I think John Clute wondered in a review whether jumping right into Wolfe at Blue might be the best way. I've always wondered the same.

2011-06-23 Mo Holkar / UKG replying to Daniel Petersen #

Recounts reading the four books of The Book of the New Sun out of order as a teenager from a library, expecting the later books' back-references to ruin surprises. Supports Daniel Petersen's point about reading On Blue's Waters before Long Sun by sharing a parallel experience, noting The Shadow of the Torturer still surprised him.
supports · genre-and-craft

I first read the four books of BotNS in the wrong order, as they became available in our local library (I was about 14-15 at the time). The later books are so packed with back-references to prior events, that I thought by the time I got to SotT there would be little to surprise me... how wrong I was!

2011-06-24 Marc Aramini replying to Daniel Petersen #

Argues that the Long Sun books and The Book of the New Sun should be read before the Short Sun books, since starting at Short Sun loses echoes like Horn's references to Silk and the ball-catching ending of On Blue's Waters. Responds to Daniel Petersen's account of reading Short Sun first and his remark that John Clute wondered whether starting at Blue is best, using specific Short Sun details as grounds.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Short Sun requires prior reading of Long Sun; Horn's narration carries traces of Silk

You could read Long Sun before New or Short Sun, but starting at Short Sun just ruins so many little cool things, like the whole "I caught the ball, I won the game" thing at the end of On Blue's Water, Horn talking about "his" dancing toy, things that undeniably let you know there is something of Silk running around in that narrator.

I like the published order, but Long and New should both be read before Short.? If you don't have a thorough understanding of the positive outlook of Silk and his "out of character" suicidal impulse Short Sun could never make full sense. ?

2011-06-26 Daniel Petersen replying to Marc Aramini #

Argues that starting with the Short Sun books is exciting and inviting despite spoiling some Long Sun links, and that the list overemphasizes puzzle-solving over wonder, awe, humanity and great writing in Wolfe. Responds to Marc Aramini's claim that Long Sun and New Sun should be read before Short Sun, citing spoilers like Horn's dancing toy and the ball-and-game ending of On Blue's Waters.
disputes

You're probably right, Marc. But being plunged into all that mystery through the wonders of Short Sun is still quite exciting and inviting. I sometimes feel people on this board are far more into solving little (and huge) puzzles than they are into a sense of wonder, awe, magic, thrill, humanity, divinity, great writing, and so on in Gene Wolfe. I know the puzzles are integral to his art - but I just don't feel like the other aspects get much air time.

DOJP
