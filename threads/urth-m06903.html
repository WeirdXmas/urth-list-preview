The thread: Posters debated whether Wolfe's Catholic faith and the Orthodox-Catholic divide illuminate the Severian and Christ reading, including a possible False Apocatastasis interpretation. John Watkins argued the Orthodox and Catholic churches see each other as schismatic rather than heretical, with differences mostly disciplinary and organizational, and corrected Son of Witz's terms. Son of Witz countered that Catholic doctrine is only one layer of how readers take Christian symbols, suggesting Wolfe as a convert may have fitted older views into his Catholicism, while admitting limited expertise on the heresy question. The thread drifted into anecdote about convert zeal and the Filioque, with no resolution on the theological points.
  • John Watkins: Orthodox and Catholic churches are schismatic to each other, not heretical; differences are mostly disciplinary and organizational
  • Son of Witz: Catholic doctrine is one layer of reading Christian symbols; Wolfe as convert may have fitted older views, possibly explaining a False Apocatastasi
  • Son of Witz (convert zeal post): converts can be zealous, illustrated by an Orthodox disputant who treated the Filioque as a serious doctrinal matter

Themes: Christ figures and messiahs, Catholic faith and sacraments, Gnosticism, heresy and other theologies

2008-11-24 John Watkins replying to Son of Witz #

Argues that the Orthodox and Catholic churches regard each other as schismatic rather than heretical, and that the differences between them are mostly disciplinary and organizational rather than theological. Responds to Son of Witz's remark that the Eastern Church sees Catholicism as heretical, correcting the terms and listing the issues he named as non-theological; the post is a side digression from the Severian and Christ thread.
disputes · theology-and-religion

I believe that the Orthodox Churches view Catholicism as a schismatic church, as Catholicism views the Orthodox Churches. Schism and heresy are distinct. There are very few theological differences between the churches (the majority of issues you names are not theological issues) and the ones there are, such as the Filioque, are relatively minor issues compared to the disciplinary and organizational differences between the churches.

But this is pretty far afield. I'll only add that in my experience, converts to the Catholic Church tend to be more orthodox and more ardent in their views than cradle Catholics.
