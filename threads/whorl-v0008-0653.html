Cards

Whorl list · 5 messages · 1998-07-09 to 1998-07-10

1998whorlThe Book of the Long SunKevin J. MaroneyJohn Gerstenmary whalenMichael Straight
The thread: Readers debated why the computer cards became currency in The Book of the Long Sun. Mary Whalen, followed by Michael Straight, argued that Typhon made the cards money so the crew could not misuse ship technology and that the Cargo would keep them for later use. Michael Straight also questioned how cards could become common money and represent a huge sum for peasants, admitting limited economic knowledge. Kevin Maroney disputed the premise, arguing the Cards are generic memory or power units that became currency through scarcity, durability, beauty and portability, as gold did on Earth. John Gersten asked whether broken cards can be reintegrated and mostly introduced himself. The exchange ended without resolution, with the programming-chip premise contested but no consensus reached.
  • Mary Whalen: Typhon made the cards currency to keep the crew from misusing ship technology, with the Cargo preserving them
  • Michael Straight: supports the Typhon-currency theory and finds the economics of cards as peasant money implausible
  • Kevin Maroney: the Cards are generic memory or power units, and they became money through gold-like qualities
  • John Gersten: asks whether broken cards can be reintegrated and agrees with the betrayal-prevention theory

Themes: Robots, androids and artificial life, Power, rulers and politics, Books, libraries and writing

1998-07-09 mary whalen #

Proposes that Typhon made the computer cards into currency in The Book of the Long Sun so the crew could not misuse ship technology, and that the Cargo would keep the cards safe for later use. Grounds the theory in the Whorl archives and in the logic of money keeping people from parting with the cards.
proposes · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Typhon made cards currency to disable the ship

This is Sean Whalen (prion).

I've just finished reading The Book of the Long Sun, and thought I'd give some of the thoughts I had after reading the Whorl archives.

The use of the computer cards as currency always seemed a little strange, and I have a theory about why this came about. Basically, Typhon didn't want the crew of the Whorl to be able to betray him by getting away/sabotaging/disobeying him by using the ships and/or other similar things before the Whorl arrived at its destination and the Cargo left to settle the planet and Typhon reappeared in day-to-day life.

By taking the cards out of the things he didn't want the Crew (who still retained their memories of technology use because they needed to be able to run the ship's functions) to use, he renders them useless. To keep the cards in a safe place that could be accessed when his plan neared conclusion he gives them to the partly-amnesiac Cargo to keep. He tells them to use it as currency. He thus spreads it around among the people who can each use their own cards to put into the machinery of the ship in which they leave the Whorl. It spreads it out over the whole Whorl, and because it's money, the people won't want to part with it. The Crew wouldn't be able to go around and collect the cards, and the Cargo wouldn't help at all, they don't want to give away their money. When Typhon wants them to leave, he just instructs them to bring their cards with them, which they'll probably do anyway. On the ship, he tells them to put the cards into the slots and the ship functions, taking them down.

prion

1998-07-10 Michael Straight #

Supports Sean Whalen's theory that Typhon made the computer cards into currency so the crew could not betray him, adding that breaking cards into bits is a tragedy Typhon did not foresee. Questions how plausibly cards could have become common money and then grown to represent a huge sum for ordinary peasants, admitting limited economic knowledge.
supports · myth-and-structure · theories: Typhon made cards currency so the crew could not betray him

I think this makes sense. I'll just add that the breaking of cards into bits is a tragedy unforseen by Typhon (not to mention a nifty pun). Except I don't know enough economics to guess how plausible it is cards could have once been common currency and changed so that for most peasants they represent a huge sum.

-Rostrum

1998-07-10 Kevin J. Maroney #

Argues that the Cards in the Long Sun books are probably not program chips but generic memory or power units, and that they became currency because of scarcity, measurability, durability, beauty and portability, like gold on Earth. Responds to Rostrum's question about how likely it is to find the right 100 bits to reassemble a given card, noting that if the Cards were not interchangeable Pas's currency idea would weaken.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: The Cards are interchangeable memory or power units; The Cards became currency like gold

Well, there's no guarantee that the Cards are program chips--they could just be pure memory, or power, or something else interchangable.

If they're not interchangable, then the whole idea that Pas set them up as currency weakens anyway--although I suppose if one Card holds an entire personality--"Majordomo Fred", say--then just bringing along a good set of them would give you enough personalities to make the system functional.

However, I don't think that's what is going on. I suspect the Cards became a unit of currency for the reason that gold did on Earth: they're scarce, measurable, almost indestructable, pretty, and portable.

--
Wombat
