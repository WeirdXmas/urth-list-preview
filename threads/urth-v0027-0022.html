The thread: Readers asked why the Green Man tells Severian that Agia is "above ground" in The Book of the New Sun, and whether this is a meaningful clue or an unhelpful non-answer. Patri10629 offered a tentative reading that it simply means she is alive, possibly with a jab tied to his plant nature. Timothy Reilly argued the advice is unhelpful and not a real bluff, rejecting proposed explanations and comparing underground hierogrammates from The Urth of the New Sun. Michael Straight shifted to a different point, arguing Severian gave the stone out of compassion, respect, or dislike for his captors rather than as mere payment, without citing scene evidence. The thread ends without consensus, with the "above ground" phrase left unresolved.
  • Patri10629: "above ground" likely just means Agia is alive, possibly an insult tied to the Green Man's plant nature
  • Timothy Reilly: the Green Man's advice is unhelpful and meaningless, not a real bluff; none of the proposed explanations fit
  • Michael Straight: Severian gave the stone out of compassion, respect, or dislike for captors, not just as payment

1999-05-27 Timothy Reilly #

Asks why the Green Man tells Severian that Agia is "above ground" in The Book of the New Sun, and argues that this answer seems unhelpful and oddly meaningless given the scene's importance. Responds to Michael Andre-Driussi's explanations by noting none fit, comparing the underground hierogrammates in The Urth of the New Sun, and finding the Green Man's advice unhelpful for escaping Agia.
questions · close-textual-reading

Michael

Thanks for the welcome. I'm an admirer of your book too by the way.

All of the suggested explanations you made had occurred to me, but none of them really seem right, do they? I have read TBNS several times (it is the Book of Gold for me too, as for many others), and have always found this exchange puzzling. Since the scene is a crucial one - Severian then gives the Green Man the stone that will help him escape his imprisonment, and the Green Man is later among those who saves Severian in repayment - it seems odd that this exchange should be so meaningless. For some reason I was reminded of it when Apheta and the other hierogrammates were said to live underground on Yesod in The Urth of the New Sun, only being allowed to venture to the surface as a reward (also puzzling, for me at least).

Maybe, as you say the Green Man is bluffing - but surely a bluff would be more specific and less unhelpful than telling Severian that Agia is exactly where she would be expected to be ie "above ground". Similarly, if the Green Man is trying to help, then his advice is not at all helpful to Severian escaping Agia's later attempts at assassination, or at least so it seems to me. And in any event, he is responding to a question as to where Agia is now, rather than at some time in the future. Yet presumably the Green Man's words have some positive impact on Severian, because he gives him the stone thereafter. So I remain perplexed!

Regards
Tim
