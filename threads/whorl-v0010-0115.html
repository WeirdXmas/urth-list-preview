more grist for the "enhanced Horn hypothesis"

Whorl list · 4 messages · 1999-10-15 to 1999-10-18

1999whorlThe Book of the Long SunDan ParmenterMichael StraightJames JordanAlex David Groce
The thread: Dan Parmenter proposed that Horn's view of Silk in On Blue's Waters may be enhanced by altered perception, tied to Patera Incus's hacker-priest improvements, and read Silk as a Christ figure with an unreliable narrator. Alex David Groce disputed this, arguing Silk is genuinely good and that Horn's close imitation of Silk and Nettle's role as scribe in Exodus from the Long Sun support that reading. Jim Jordan also disputed, contending Horn could not have drawn on the gospels since he lacks access to them, so the Christ parallels must come from the Outsider or Wolfe. The thread ended with the enhanced-Horn hypothesis opposed by two posters, with no sign of agreement.
  • Dan Parmenter: Horn's view of Silk is enhanced by altered perception, with Silk read as a Christ figure
  • Alex David Groce: Silk is genuinely good, not an exaggerated gospel-like figure; Horn imitates him closely
  • Jim Jordan: Horn cannot have used gospel templates, so Christ parallels come from the Outsider or Wolfe

Themes: Unreliable narration, Christ figures and messiahs, Myth and folklore sources

1999-10-15 Dan Parmenter #

Proposes that Horn's view of Silk in On Blue's Waters may be enhanced or exaggerated by an artificially altered perception, linking it to Patera Incus's hacker-priest improvements mentioned just before. Grounds this on textual proximity in the Marble and Mucor visit, the unreliable-narrator idea, and a Christ-parallel reading, with a passing comparison of Passilk to Tashlan in Lewis's The Last Battle.
proposes · close-textual-reading · allegory-and-typology · theories: Horn's view of Silk is enhanced

Last night I was re-reading the passage in OBW where Horn visits Marble and Mucor. Maytera Marble alludes to Hammerstone's deep fondness for Patera Incus, which as I recall, was a small "improvement" that Incus made, he being of the "hacker priest" class. A paragraph or two later, Horn laments the fact that the BOOK OF SILK doesn't convey just how great Silk really was. Hmmm! This may be a red herring, but given its textual proximity to Marble's observation and the fact that we have ample evdience of how human "software" can be re-jiggered by the will of the gods, it wouldn't surprise me to discover that Horn's perception of Silk has been enhanced.

Given the Wolfean "unreliable narrator," it makes you start to wonder if the Silk of the BOOK OF SILK is something of an exaggeration, which would certainly fit in with the Christ parallels. Silk is portrayed as a kind of Father Brown, but perhaps in "real life" he was more of a Father Ted.

Another random observation: "Passilk" immediately made me think of "Tashlan" from C.S. Lewis' THE LAST BATTLE.

Lex

1999-10-15 Alex David Groce #

Questions whether the Silk of the Book of Silk is an exaggeration in the way the gospels might be, and argues Silk's Enlightenment and the admiration of Marble and Mucor make him genuinely good. Points to Horn's close imitation of Silk and Nettle's secondary role as scribe in Exodus from the Long Sun as evidence.
disputes · author-and-biography · allegory-and-typology · theories: Silk is an exaggeration like the gospels; Horn's authorship of the Book of Silk

I'm a bit confused here--fits in with the Christ parallels in that Silk couldn't possibly be that Christ-like, or fits in that the _gospels_ are exaggerations? I would think the first would be a more likely approach for Wolfe than the second, but even then, there's Silk's Englightenment (which works two ways--(A) it might make Silk better than he would be and (B) maybe he got Enlightened because he had a tendency to be a Very Good person anyway). Also going against the exaggeration theory is that other people who knew Silk very well (Marble, Mucor, etc.) seem to have seen him as an extraordinarily good man.

I think a telling note in LONG SUN is that Horn is generally noticed much more than Nettle--is Nettle even listed as "Author of the BOOK OF SILK" in the Persons, Places & Things section in Exodus? It seems her role is secondary in the actual composition--scribe and organizer (given OBW, there is no telling what order Horn might have put things in...)

Horn does mimic Silk beautifully long before any possibility of possession or anything, so as usual in LONG SUN, any possession is well in keeping with his original tendencies.

1999-10-15 James Jordan #

Argues that Horn's narration of the Long Sun events cannot be shaped by gospel templates, since Horn lacks access to Luke, Acts or Revelation, so the Christ parallels must come from the Outsider and Wolfe. Grounds this in Silk's divine election, his predestined virgin birth, the imitator-all-along idea and Horn's knowledge of event-sequences he could not have learned.
disputes · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: Horn could not have gospel-shaped the narrative; Outsider and Wolfe shape the Christ parallels; Silk is a virtuous Mary-figure's son

Well, he's Brown in the first book, not much in the latter three.

Some kind of "divine election" is involved in his whole life, as well as the "scientific predestination" that resulted in his "virgin birth." I'm not sure how Horn as narrator could construe the whole flow of events to make them more gospellike than they in fact are. We certainly cannot assume that Horn had access to Luke, Acts, and the book of Revelation to use as templates. That would seem to be the doing of the Outsider and, behind him, Wolfe.

One would like to know more about his mother, who, given Wolfe's beliefs, would have to be a kind of virtuous Mary-figure.

Certainly the "imitator all along" business runs against any "special enhancement" view, and perhaps accounts for everything. Horn's ignorance might account for the seemingly truncated character of some sequences in the narrative. On the other hand, he seems to know a lot of things that it's hard to see how he could have learned -- not just details, but whole event-sequences.

Nutria
