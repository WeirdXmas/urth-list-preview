So Where DID the Guard Go?

Urth list · 16 messages · 2014-05-01 to 2014-05-07

2014urthThe Book of the New SunWilliam GrahamAntónio Pedro MarquesGerry QuinnDavid StockhoffMarc AraminiFred KiescheJeff WilsonDan'l Danehy-OakesAntonin ScriabinAndrew Mason
The thread: Participants debated where the gate guard went when Roche says he has gone at the start of The Book of the New Sun, and why Vodalus fires his weapon. William Graham worked through the patrol explanation and the dialogue, while Gerry Quinn offered possible explanations (the guard heard something, or Hildegrande removed him) and argued the gap works as narrative setup. Jeffery Wilson proposed a population-math reason, and Andrew Mason argued that In Green's Jungles answers the question but that Wolfe did not originally intend that answer, pointing to the citizen volunteers. Marc Aramini speculated that the question arose from Wolfe rereading while writing the Short Sun books. Gerry Quinn asked where In Green's Jungles addresses the guard on Urth, and Andrew Mason replied that Vodalus paying him off would fit the Short Sun account. The thread ended without consensus, with much of the later
  • William Graham: the guard's absence and Vodalus's shot can be worked out from the patrol positions and dialogue
  • Gerry Quinn: the unexplained gap is enough narrative setup; the guard may have investigated a noise or been removed by Hildegrande
  • Jeffery Wilson: the guard's absence is explained by Nessus's population mathematics
  • Andrew Mason: In Green's Jungles answers the question but was not Wolfe's original intent; Vodalus paying the guard fits the Short Sun account
  • Marc Aramini: the question may have arisen from Wolfe rereading the book while writing the Short Sun books

Themes: Unreliable narration, Stories within stories, Books, libraries and writing

2014-05-01 William Graham #

Asks where the guard went when Roche says he has gone at the start of The Book of the New Sun, and why Vodalus fires his weapon. Works through the patrol explanation, noting the volunteers' positions and the dialogue with Badger and Thea that suggests V did not shoot at them.
questions · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic

Hello folks, I've been reading the lists for ages- thanks for the help. I'm now one my second 'hard read' through BOTNS after half a dozen or so 'casual reads' since its publication.

I get tripped up on the fourth sentance.

Roche indicates, "The guard has gone".

The popular discussion on this page has historically been "Who asks, 'But why would the guard leave?' ?" in the next paragraph. While that discussion is very useful for introducing us to Wolfe's love of removing the net from underneath his readers' high wire, I cannot seem to find any place where those who have gone before have asked the more basic question:

"Yeah, where DID the guard go?"

The obvious answer is that he's patrolling, a possibility supported by the fact that Vodalus fires his weapon, but never indicates at whom. The issue however is that our apprentices are surprised to see the guard gone. If leaving his post to patrol were common, why the confusion over his absence?

V doesn't seemingly fire at the volunteers, the dialog between he and Badger and Thea support this, and only three of the volunteers (those pursuing Eata) could have possibly arrived at the site before Sev anyway.

So where's the guard, and why did V fire his weapon?

-WHG3

2014-05-04 Andrew Mason replying to William Graham #

Argues that In Green's Jungles answers where the guard went in The Book of the New Sun, but that this later answer is not what Wolfe originally intended when writing the earlier book. Notes that the presence of citizen volunteers suggests the guard is often absent or that his presence is ineffective, which points to Wolfe's original intent.
disputes · close-textual-reading · author-and-biography · theories: Wolfe's original intent differs from later Short Sun explanation

I'm sure that _Short Sun_ (specifically _In Green's Jungles_) does indeed tell us where the guard went; but I'm equally sure that this was not the answer Wolfe had in mind when he wrote _New Sun_; he wasn't planning _that_ far ahead. So we can still ask what his original intention was. (The presence of the citizen volunteers suggests that either the guard is quite often absent, or his presence is ineffective.)

2014-05-06 Andrew Mason replying to William Graham #

Explains that a guard leaving his post could fit the Short Sun account of Vodalus paying him off, without needing a change in Wolfe's plan. Walks through In Green's Jungles chapter 21, tracing the Rajan's party from Rigoglio's house to the river and the Bear Tower, and notes early readers linked it to the opening of The Shadow of the Torturer.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Vodalus paid the guard to leave his post

I suppose that's actually compatible with the _Short Sun_ explanation; if Vodalus had paid the guard to leave his post, he might have treated the arrival of the Rajan and party as a good excuse to do so. (So no actual change of mind needs to be posited, even though Wolfe wasn't actually planning _Short Sun_ when he wrote the original scene.)

IGJ Chapter 21, 'The Red Sun': the Rajan, Fava, Mora, Hide, Rigoglio and some extras travel to Urth, initially to Rigoglio's old house - from there they go to the river, and finally end up at the Bear Tower (escorted by a guard who has left his post), thus setting things up for the Rajan's later trips to the river and to the Citadel in RTTW. Severian does not appear in this episode; we have to wait for RTTW to meet him. Some people, though, when the book first came out, noted the relation of the episode with the first scene of _New Sun_, and hypothesised that the Rajan has just missed Severian, a theory confirmed in the next book when they do meet.
