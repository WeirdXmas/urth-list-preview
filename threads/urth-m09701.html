The thread: A thread with no subject line that turned from Wolfe to file sharing. Stuart Hamm argued that downloading shared copies of TV shows and books costs creators money, and Jeff Wilson had earlier said file sharing is not theft. Jordon Flato backed Hamm, saying film and TV workers are paid up front, unlike music piracy. Adam Thornton and James Wynn disputed the wording and stressed royalties and enforcement, while brunians argued that unpaid crews are not comparable to burglary. Gwern Branwen mostly posted about where ebooks of Wolfe's works could be found, including by torrent, and quoted Citadel of the Autarch on whether the dead outnumber the living. The thread ended in drift, with jokes and side debates and no return to the books.
  • Stuart Hamm: downloading shared copies harms creators, since writers and performers are paid from sales
  • Jordon Flato: film and TV workers are paid up front, so piracy differs from music piracy
  • Adam Thornton: illegal downloading is wrong but not the same as theft, and lost sales do not map onto downloads
  • James Wynn: piracy hurts future royalties, while the music industry's enforcement is excessive
  • brunians: unpaid crews are not comparable to burglary, and creators should find a new market model
  • Gwern Branwen: ebook copies of Wolfe are easy to find, and he got them via torrents

Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Stories within stories

2009-05-20 Adam Thornton replying to Stuart Hamm #

Argues that illegal downloading is wrong but not the same as theft, and that lost sales do not map one-to-one onto downloads. Responds to Stuart Hamm's appeal to buy first editions of Wolfe's new books, using the point as a counterpoint to Brunians' absolutist property-rights statement. Off-topic from the Wolfe books themselves.
disputes

Yeah.

My own position is that downloading media illegally is some-kind-of- bad (me, I still prefer to buy physical CDs and then rip them to iTunes, so that I have unprotected files which I can then copy to whatever music player I may have in the future that might not be an iPod), but it's not exactly *theft*. Copyright infringement is not, in my mind, the same crime as, say, stealing your TV.

I do not, however, think there's a one-to-one correspondence between lost sales and illegal downloads. That is, plenty of people download things that they're idly curious about, but nowhere near enough interested in to buy. Now, one can, quite correctly, make the point that they SHOULDN'T OUGHTA BE USING IT then. Fair enough.

But mainly I was just bringing it up as a counterpoint to Brunians' absolutist Property Rights statement, by pointing out that there are some things which some people consider legitimate property rights, and others do not.

Adam
