A living torturer

Urth list · 3 messages · 2003-06-06 to 2003-06-09

The thread: David Kirby shared a Washington Post gossip-column piece about a real torturer and linked it to Severian's casual attitude toward executions, arguing the account makes such acts look unsympathetic. Matthew Malthouse responded by linking a Guardian piece reprinted from Arab News and calling the Post's handling crass, since the story had passed through several hands. His third post shared a Post article on a beheading and a Guardian letters reply, and he said he was less impressed after reading a contributor's quip about cleaning the executioner's sword. The thread stayed light and did not develop a real argument about Wolfe's books.

  • matthew.malthouse: The Guardian letters-page quip about cleaning the executioner's sword is funny but unimpressive

Themes: Justice, torture and mercy, Beasts and the animal in us

2003-06-06 David Kirby #

Shares a Washington Post gossip-column article about a real-life torturer and reflects that it recalls Severian's casual attitude toward executions, noting how the account reframes his acts as unsympathetic. Grounds this in the torturer's quoted remark about sleeping well and Severian's own joking about his family cleaning his sword, and points to a reader chat about the piece.
informs · close-textual-reading

Folks,

There's an interesting article on a real-life torturer in the gossip column of today's WASHINGTON POST:

http://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-dyn/articles/A20993-2003Jun5.html

(I didn't bother to track down the original article, though a thorough look through OpinionJournal.com should let you do so.)

As I read the article, I of course couldn't help but think of Severian -- maybe the Severian of Earth-12, who lets his family "help me clean my sword."

A telling comment from today's Severian: "[T]here are many people who faint when they witness an execution. I don't know why they come and watch if they don't have the stomach for it. . . . Me? I sleep very well."

This is worth a quick glance. For me, it put into focus some of what Severian does, and not in the positive, sympathetic, or even neutral way much of Wolfe's writing portrays the acts. An eye- opener.

(If you're going to look at the article, do so quickly; the POST removes much of its Internet content from free public view after two weeks.)

And if you want some of the general public's reaction to today's Severian, check out the gossip columnist's weekly Web chat at:

http://discuss.washingtonpost.com/zforum/03/r_style_source060603.htm?nav=hpt oc_lol

(This should be available for more than two weeks, for those of you reading this message in the Urth archives.)

-- David
