2009-04-13 Robin Dunn #

Proposes a film adaptation of The Book of the New Sun in four two-hour films or eight one-hour episodes, and asks readers which dominant tone the book has, such as elegiac or melancholy mixed with wonder. Mentions the expensive Abaia scene and suggests the first thirty pages of The Shadow of the Torturer could fill a two-hour movie, plus asks whether Wolfe has refused film offers.
proposes

Am always so impressed with the level of thinking on this site- a real tribute to the depth of Wolfe's imaginary universe!

As an indie filmmaker, am daydreaming again today about how to make an appropriate film version of the book of the new sun.

it would definitely have to be four 2 hour films, or perhaps 8 1-hour episodes.

Peter Jackson did 3 Tolkien books for $150 mil , but I bet I could do 8 hours of Wolfe for 25 mil.

Any takers? ;)

The tone is one of the most important things: curious what some of you would say is the dominant tone of Book of the New Sun

Elegaic?

Melancholy mixed with wonder?

Something like that.

Abaia is definitely one of the more expensive scenes, as people have noticed. ;)

Part of me feels like you could do a 2 hour movie about the first 30 pages of Shadow . . .

You think Wolfe has turned many film offers down in his time? :)

best wishes,
Robin Dunn

2009-04-13 Jeff Wilson replying to Robin Dunn #

Proposes a film adaptation of The Book of the New Sun framed as Severian's reminiscences, with a grandfather-grandson frame in which the grandfather is Severian on Ushas. Replying to Robin Dunn's remark about making a film, it suggests the frame would allow inconsistencies, citing the Princess Bride frame and joking about Abaia's absence.
proposes · genre-and-craft · theories: Grandfather frame reveals Severian on Ushas

I think it might best be done as a series of Sev's reminiscences, with 2-3 plot threads a piece, and one or two continuing along the arc. You'd also have some freedom from continuity, with the chosen foil complaining in the framing sequences "that's not the way you described them before" and being placated. Perhaps even use a grandfather/grandson frame similar to the Princess Bride, but with with the grandfather being a bit sinister and playing on the child's short attention span to gloss over his inconsistencies. And of course, the grandfather is really Severian on Ushas.

It's one of things you can fill in yourself, it doesn't necessarily have a dominant tone.

Abaia doesn't even appear, does he?

they already made THE LORDS OF DISCIPLINE!

2009-04-13 b sharp replying to Robin Dunn #

Argues that Abaia does appear in The Book of the Long Sun, pointing to the magician's fable with the shiphead Naviscaput and his corn maidens, a story from Maxellinda's uncle about a ship on Gyoll, and the Mother and her sirens on Blue. Responds to Robin Dunn's claim that Abaia never appears, grounding the reading in the water imagery and undines linked to Abaia.
disputes · close-textual-reading · allegory-and-typology · theories: Abaia appears disguised in the magician's fable and Maxellinda's uncle's story

I think he appears in the magician's fable as the shiphead/Naviscaput with his corn maidens and roiling the bottom mud with his feet. Then we hear a story from Maxellinda's Uncle about a ship on Gyoll he encountered while shipping clams. I think manatees and deep voices from the water are involved plus we have that ship carrying pale warriors (minions of Erebus). All tied to what we know of Abaia and his undines. On Blue, The Mother and her siren(s) surely provide a glimpse of Abaia or something similiar also.
