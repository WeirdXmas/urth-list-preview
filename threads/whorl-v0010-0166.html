1999-11-12 Richard Horton #

Argues that John Clute's reviews are spoiler-prone, so a newcomer's interview with Wolfe is poorly suited for that purpose. Responds to a previous poster's defence of Clute's style by citing Clute's novel The Disinheriting Party and noting that On Blue's Waters spoils The Book of the Long Sun, including who narrates it.
disputes · close-textual-reading

That sort of sentence is very characteristic of Clute. It's just his style.

By the way, if anyone wants to read a novel more confusing (though, alas, less rewarding) than Wolfe at his most opaque, they could do worse than trying Clute's one novel, _The Disinheriting Party_.

Clute is the most spoiler prone reviewer/critic I have ever read. I really like his reviews, but sometimes he just forgets that his putative reader probably hasn't read the book yet.

And to be honest I think that while one =could= start with _On Blue's Waters_ and enjoy =it=, it is very much a spoiler for _The Book of the Long Sun_. Even knowing that the narrator of _On Blue's Waters_ wrote _The Book of the Long Sun_ is a spoiler.

1999-11-12 Alastair Reynolds #

Chats about John Clute, saying his reviews are frustrating yet sometimes brilliantly perceptive and that his Interzone review column is the first thing the poster reads. Responds to a digest post praising Clute's novel The Disinheriting Party as more confusing than Wolfe, and also mentions a rumoured new novel and a copy of On Blue's Waters arriving.
chats

Richard:

He's supposed to have a new novel out sometime - the last I heard was that it was to be called Appleseed.

The thing with Clute is (for me anyway), just when I'm ready to throw one of his reviews across the room, he comes out with something brilliantly perceptive. If he has a review column in Interzone, it's always the first thing I turn to.

Al, just got his copy of OBW...
