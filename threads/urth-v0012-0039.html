The Heads of Cerberus

Reports reading Francis Stevens's pulp novel The Heads of Cerberus and proposes that Wolfe may have read it and alluded to it in The Fifth Head of Cerberus. Grounds the suggestion in three parallels: a stasis-frozen tyrannical society, numbered people, and a forbidden library, while calling the book pulp and weak.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Wolfe drew on Stevens's The Heads of Cerberus for The Fifth Head of Cerberus

I've also been reading a book entitled _The Heads of Cerberus_, by "Francis Stevens" (a pseudonym for Gertrude Bennett). It was originally published in 1919, I would guess in magazine form, but the copy I read is a small-press reprint from 1952. It's about three people from the present who are transported by some strange dust into 22nd-century Philadelphia. It's very much the sort of pulp Wolfe parodied in "The Last Thrilling Wonder Story," and it's pretty bad, so much so that after I got halfway through I just skimmed the rest. But, though there are no thematic or structural similarities I can detect between _Heads_ and FHC, there are a few odd points of similarity which make it possible that Wolfe read _Heads_ and incorporated some allusions to it. These are:

1) A society frozen in stasis and ruled by a tyrannical government

2) People designated only by numbers (in _Heads_, everybody but the elite in future Philadelphia

3) A library which the protagonist(s) are forbidden to enter.

I can post or email a description of the book if anyone's interested.

--Adam
