2000-01-09 James Jordan #

Argues that Wolfe chose the name Yesod in The Book of the New Sun only as a symbol for the higher world, not to invoke the full Neoplatonic-Cabbalistic hierarchy. Reports a personal interview with Wolfe as evidence and notes that only three tiers appear in the books, mirroring the biblical heaven, earth and lower realm.

I asked Wolfe about this either in or around the time of my interview that is posted on Paul Duggan's website. I had supposed he intended to allude to the whole neoplatonic-cabbalistic chain, but he indicated that he had just picked the name Yesod as a good symbol for the higher world, without intending the whole Qabalistic scheme. Maybe he was just funning with me, but since only the three tiers show up in the NS works, I suspect that's all he intends. As in the bible, you've got a created heaven of angels, an earth, and a lower realm of fallen angels. Now, Yesod in NS is not the biblical heaven, but does correspond to it in some symbolic ways. At any rate, I don't think Wolfe intended to imply the entire neoplatonic heirarchy. Of course, this is always the problem. When Wolfe pulls a token from here and there to toss into the mix, how much of the baggage of that token is supposed to come with it?

Nutria
