Fuligin as Black Hole...

Urth list · 16 messages · 2010-07-30 to 2010-07-31

2010urthThe Book of the New SunJohn WatkinsRyan DunnLee BermanDavid StockhoffJames WynnJeff WilsonDan'l Danehy-Oakes
The thread: Ryan Dunn opened by proposing that the darker-than-black fuligin cloak in The Book of the New Sun foreshadows the dying sun and its black hole, a tentative idea he offered as a question rather than an argument. John Watkins backed the link, citing the contrast with the argent robes Severian wears at the end of The Citadel of the Autarch. Lee Berman extended it with a fuligin-black hole and argent-quasar pairing, grounded in Hethor's ship The Quasar and its mirror sails. The rest of the thread drifted into jokes, including a rap-style quip, a mock blurb, a TV pitch and a playful remix summary, with no further textual evidence offered. It ended without any real debate on the cosmic reading.
  • Ryan Dunn: fuligin cloak may foreshadow the dying sun and black hole, offered tentatively
  • John Watkins: fuligin's darkness links to the old sun's black hole, contrasted with the argent robes
  • Lee Berman: fuligin pairs with a black hole and argent with a quasar, tied to Hethor's ship The Quasar and its mirror sails

Themes: The dying sun and the cosmos, Deep time and ruins, Mirrors, pictures and art

Argues that the fuligin cloak's darkness may link to the dying sun and its black hole, rather than only to the torturers' guild in black. Grounds this in the contrast with the argent robes Severian is about to wear at the end of The Citadel of the Autarch, and in the guild's supposed ties to priesthood.
proposes · close-textual-reading · myth-and-structure · theories: Fuligin cloak foreshadows the dying sun and black hole

Or Gene, like Neil Gaiman and Tim Burton, thinks fair-skin-in-black looks cool.

If the coloring has some meaning beyond that particular to the torturers' guild (they torture people and seem to have some connection, literal or metaphoric, to Catholic priesthood, so are in black), then it must be read in connection with the robes of argent, the color more white than white, that Severian is about to put on at the end of Citadel. So I think a connection to the old sun and/or its black hole is probably intended.

2010-07-30 Lee Berman replying to Ryan Dunn #

Argues that fuligin, the darkest colour, pairs with a black hole as its cosmic counterpart while argent pairs with a quasar, and that the colour opposites in The Book of the New Sun carry physics and personal redemption as well as religious allusion. Builds the case on Hethor's ship The Quasar, its mirror sails, and the mirror's power to reveal good or evil, responding to John Watkins's reading linking fuligin to the old sun's black hole.
proposes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Fuligin and argent are cosmic opposites tied to black hole and quasar; Hethor's ship The Quasar links to quasar physics

Without disagreeing I'd want to point out that fuligin is the color which is darker than black. If black absorbs all colors of the spectrum then a black hole is even darker because it actively sucks all light (and everything else) out of this universe.

Conversely, argent is described as the color more pure than white. Argent can be used to describe a shade of white but its more basic definition relates to silver. White reflects all colors of the spectrum but (polished) silver does so in an even more pure way.

I don't know about now, but around the time of BotNS's creation, I think quasars were considered to possibly be the opposite of black holes, leaking energy into our universe. Hethor's ship is called The Quasar and was powered by (demon-haunted) mirror sails.

So the opposite of fuligin is argent. The opposite to a black hole is a quasar. The opposite to being a torturer is being Autarch/New Sun. Mirrors? Apparently they can be used to find good or evil. Depends on which way you pass through the looking-glass?

I dunno. I guess I am agreeing that in addition to religious allusion there is modern (at the time)physics and almost surely a conveyance of sense of personal redemption with regard to the opposites of fuligin and argent/mirrors that we find in BotNS.

2010-07-30 Ryan Dunn replying to Ryan Dunn #

Offers a playful remix summary of The Book of the New Sun, following Severian as an exiled torturer who becomes king of the world and reignites the sun. Lists its giants, androids, angels, time-travel and inadvertent resurrection, and compares the mix to Tolkien, Philip K. Dick and David Lynch, with cameo appearances by Neil Armstrong and Jorge Luis Borges.
chats

REMIX:

The Book of the New Sun is an epic journey following an exiled torturer with a photographic memory named Severian as he becomes king of the world and reignites the sun.

On the way, he encounters giants, whores, cyborgs, androids, Lovecraftian monstrosities, aliens, angels, aliens who might be angels, angel monsters, beastmen, sorcerers, witches, and futuristic nuns.

Also featuring inadvertent incest, inadvertent resurrection, time-travel (intentional and inadvertent), transubstantiation, and love.

Imagine J.R.R. Tolkien and Philip K. Dick collaborating with David Lynch, and you begin to approach the idea of Book of the New Sun.

(With guest appearances by Neil Armstrong and Jorge Luis Borges.)

...ryan
