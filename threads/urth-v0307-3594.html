Severian, memory, alzabo

Urth list · 8 messages · 2003-07-08 to 2003-07-10

2003urthThe Book of the New SunMatthew MalthouseCraig BrewerChris PDon PalumboJames Jordan
The thread: Readers asked whether Severian's near-perfect memory comes from the alzabo, especially the forebrain-eating and the Autarch's succession, and whether the first Ymar was Severian. Don Palumbo argued from the Autarch's own words that Autarchs do not pass memories through the alzabo drug, and James Jordan proposed that the memories of previous Autarchs come to life inside Severian, linking this to Thecla and the Eucharist. Matthew Malthouse objected that no textual clue shows Severian's inheritance differs from his predecessors, and Chris conceded he could not locate the passage he had relied on. The thread ended without resolution, with the disagreement over whether predecessors live on inside Severian left open.
  • Craig Brewer: questions whether the alzabo explains Severian's memory, citing memories predating his first encounter
  • Don Palumbo: Autarchs do not pass memory via the alzabo drug; the Autarch's vial is rarer and stronger
  • James Jordan: previous Autarchs live on inside Severian, with Thecla's revival as the clue
  • Chris: memories of past Autarchs fade each generation; Severian loses nothing
  • matthew.malthouse: no textual clue shows Severian differs from predecessors, so the alzabo effect works the same way for him

Themes: Memory and forgetting, Ghosts, dreams and visions, Names and their meanings

2003-07-08 Craig Brewer #

Questions whether Severian's near-perfect memory comes from the alzabo, noting he recalls events before his first alzabo encounter, and asks whether eating Thecla's memories or the Autarch's succession explains it. Responds to Chris's speculation that the first Ymar was Severian, and cites Severian getting lost in the tunnels before the Atrium of Time as a memory problem.
questions · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Severian's perfect memory comes from eating the alzabo; Ymar succession via the alzabo's memories

Chris offered:

I've wondered the same thing, especially since what
bothered me about the alzabo effect has always been
that I couldn't figure out if Severian only gains, for
example, the memories that Thecla remembered with her
"imperfect" (i.e., normal) memory or if he gained
*all* of her experiences which might have somehow been
"in" the part of her that he eats. That led me to some
wilder speculation towards wondering if the tradition
of the Autarch eating the previous Autarch began with
the first Ymar who was HIMSELF Severian (with, again,
a perfect memory). The only way, perhaps, for one
"incarnation" to share all of the memories of the
previous one would be to "ingest" those memories
through the Alzabo.

I'm not sure, tho, that the alzabo itself is
responsible for his supposedly perfect memory since
Severian speaks as if he perfeclty remembers the
events before his first experience with the alzabo in
Vodalu's forest.

Another piece of Severian-memory (or lack thereof)
that bothers me is how he gets lost in the tunnels
before the Atrium of Time. Granted it's dark and
confusing, but couldn't someone with a perfect memory
stop and retrace the whole thing?

Craig

2003-07-09 Don Palumbo #

Points out that the Autarchs do not pass on memories through the alzabo drug, citing the Autarch's own statement that the vial around his neck comes from a rarer and more powerful source. Responds to Craig Brewer's proposal that Severian's perfect memory arises from a recursive alzabo effect, offering a textual correction to that idea.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian's perfect memory comes from alzabo recursion

As a few people have said this, it may be worth pointing out that the Autarchs do not use the alzebo drug to pass on their memories. When the Autarch has Severian kill him and eat his forebrain, he points out specifically that the drug in the vial around his neck is not taken from the alzebo, but is a rarer, better, and more powerful drug.

--Don Palumbo

2003-07-09 James Jordan #

Argues that Severian does not merely receive the Autarchs' memories from eating the forebrain but that they come to life within him, an effect that helps him pass the trial. Notes a possible resemblance to Casher O'Neill in C. Smith's On the Sand Planet and reports that Wolfe said Smith was not an influence.
proposes · source-and-allusion · author-and-biography · theories: Autarchs' memories come to life in Severian

And Sev does not just get their memories, any more than he just got Thecla's. They come to life in him. This is part of how he passes the trial. I originally thought this was nod to Casher O'Neill in *On the Sand Planet* by C. Smith, who passes trials with the same benefit as gained in *On the Wind Planet,* but Wolfe told me Smith was not an influence on him.

2003-07-09 Chris P #

Argues that the memories Severian gains from the alzabo come to life within him uniquely, unlike the effect of the autarch's drug, and guesses that ordinary people lose such memories quickly while autarchs keep them. Responds to a post that quotes Wolfe's denial that C. Smith influenced the trial-passing device, and draws on the book's memory-acquiring trial.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Alzabo memories fade for ordinary people but persist for autarchs

This is true, though the fact that they come to life within him seems unique to Sev (regardless of which concoction he uses) rather than characteristic of the autarch's drug. If I had to guess I would say that the difference between the drugs is that *for a normal person* the memories gained from the alzabo fade quickly and are lost, whereas the autarchs must retain more of them and on more of a permanent basis. With Sev this difference doesn't seem to be so large.

Chris

2003-07-10 James Jordan #

Argues that the previous autarchs live on inside Severian, citing Thecla's coming to life within him as the clue that settles the matter. Responds to Matthew's claim that no clue shows Severian's inheritance differs from his predecessors, linking the idea to the Eucharist and Christ living in the believer.
disputes · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: Previous autarchs are revived inside Severian

I assume you refer to the possibility that the previous autarchs are brought back to life in Sev. I don't know of an explicit clue either, but Wolfe has said that he "only says things one time" in these books. The fact that Thecla comes to life inside Sev seems to me to be the only clue we need. Of course, since eating the Autarch is not by alzabo juice, there may be a difference. Yet, both of these things link to the Christian Eucharist, which is not merely symbolic (for Wolfe anyway), but has to do with "Christ living in you."
Hence, if they did not come back to life inside Sev, I'd want to know why not.

Disputes Chris's suggestion that alzabo memories fade for ordinary people but persist for autarchs, arguing Severian's memories of his predecessors work the same way. Cites the absence of any textual clue that Severian's inheritance differs, and Thecla's warning about sharing memories of loved ones, asking whether Thea avoided that for this reason.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian's autarchical memories work like his predecessors'

I can't recall any clue that suggests Sev's experience with autarchical inheritance was different from his predecessors - and he has his predecessors memories for guidance and comparison.

The other occasion when Sev eats of Thecla he (and we) have the warning that sharing those whom one knew well and were emotionally tied to already had dangers. Did not Thea avoid the experince for this reason?

2003-07-10 Chris P #

Argues that memories of past autarchs grow fainter with each generation, so Severian, who absorbed the autarch's memories, would lose nothing, and that Thecla's distinct experience supports this. Responds to a prior post citing Thecla coming to life inside Severian as the key clue for previous autarchs being revived, and admits he cannot locate the passage.
supports · close-textual-reading · theories: Previous autarchs are revived within Severian through absorbed memories

I do not know where to find it exactly, but I believe I got this impression from a passage that indicates that the voices/memories of the autarchs from the most distant past are more faint. There could be other explanations, I suppose, but I read it as an indication that a certain amount was lost on each passing generation.

With Severian (in theory) there would be no loss.

But in any event yes, this is probably to be inferred from the fact that Sev's experience with Thecla was distinctly different, with a certain amount of rationalization thrown in. It seems natural, and I can't think of any reason to think otherwise.

Civet
