2010-12-21 Lee Berman #

Argues that Typhon, despite his cruelty, has admirable traits like Alexander's, wanting to unite humanity and vanquish sea demons, and that Severian was set on a path by a stronger force. Responds to James's association of Typhon with Alexander and Spring Wind, and cites Typhon's beating of Severian as an echo of Severian's beating of Eata, which it reads as not life-threatening.
proposes · close-textual-reading · allegory-and-typology · theories: Typhon has admirable traits like Alexander; Severian was set on a path by a stronger force; Typhon's beating echoes Severian beating Eata

Forgot to address this. Often, in legends and life, familial hate is the deepest kind.

I used to find Typhon completely loathesome also. I think James' association of him to Alexander (and Spring Wind) has expanded my vistas on him recently.

We can understand Severian hating Typhon. He threatened his life and would enslave The Conciliator. But if Typhon is like Alexander perhaps he has some admirable traits as did Alexander/Spring Wind in younger years.

Typhon wants to elevate and unite humanity. He wants to vanquish the demonic beings in the oceans. He wants to make Severian his second-in-command. His beating of Severian wasn't really life-threatening, just an echo of Severian's beating of Eata. Typhon may be a sexually perverted megalomaniac but perhaps he isn't all bad?

FWIW, I think Severian was deliberately set upon a path to resurrect Dorcas, resurrect Typhon, defeat Baldanders, etc. But more than once force is manipulating the outcomes of these events. The more powerful one is working toward the New Sun and succeeds. But the purpose of the less powerful, Urthly, force is there to be detected also.
