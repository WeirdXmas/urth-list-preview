2001whorlThe Book of the Long SunBob Musser

2001-02-06 Bob Musser #

Argues that Nettle is still alive at the end of Nightside the Long Sun, citing the closing passage where she sails away with Silk and Seawrack, and reads Silk's violence as coming from others' fear of him. Responds to a poster's claim that Nettle was killed, using the final page and Silk's farewell to Daisy as evidence.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Nettle survives and sails away with Silk; Silk's carnage is caused by others' fear of him

No and Yes, I think. Last paragraph, last page, refering to the receeding Whorl:

"They are in it, I hope, he and his eerie young woman (Seawrack), Nettle, the old sibyl, and their bird, on course upon a greater sea to strange new islands."

On the previous page, Silk asks Daisy to make his goodbyes to Hoof and Hide. "Nettle is making her own, and cannot be bothered with mine."

I think the carnage which follows Silk around is one of the frightening aspects of his goodness. He doesn't instigate it; it flows from the responses of those who fear him. I'm starting to re- read the series and haven't revisited Green as yet, so I'm not sure if the same theory can be applied to Horn's actions there.
