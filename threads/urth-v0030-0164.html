Themes: Ghosts, dreams and visions

2000-12-07 Dave Lebling #

Jokingly casts Gene Wolfe as the Bridge of Death guardian from Monty Python, posing questions about Severian's grandmother, Apu Punchau and the ending of Castleview, with the reviewer answering wrongly. Responds to Michael Straight's quoted remark that critics are knocked into the moat by Castleview, playing on the idea that critics fail Wolfe's puzzles.
chats · theories: Dorcas is Severian's grandmother; Apu Punchau is Severian

Gene Wolfe: He who would pass over the Bridge of Death, answer me these questions three!
Reviewer: I will answer!

GW: Who was Severian's grandmother?
R: Dorcas!

GW: Who was Apu Punchau?
R: Severian!

GW: What was going on at the end of _Castleview_?
R: Um, uh, geez, no idea...

**BOING!**

With all due apologies to Monty Python and GW.

Dave Lebling

2000-12-08 David Duffy #

Argues that Peace is modernist in its use of dream logic, linking its illogical sequences to psychoanalytic influence on early twentieth-century writers. Responds to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's quoted point about modernist experimentation by connecting it to the ghosts-working-through-trauma reading of the novel's dream-like movement.
proposes · genre-and-craft · theories: Peace is modernist through dream logic

I think _Peace_ is definitely _modern_ in that sense. The logic, such as in the example above, is dream logic, and the modernists were all influenced by psychoanalysis. "Who knows what dreams may come" is relevant to the common model of confused ghosts working through all that difficult material before they "wake up".

David Duffy.
