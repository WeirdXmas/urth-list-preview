1998urthThe Book of the New SunMichael Andre-Driussi

Themes: Christ figures and messiahs, Power, rulers and politics

1998-05-16 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Argues that Severian, as the Sleeper, may have taken the throne of Abaia, so that the Christ figure seems to have become the tyrant he once opposed. Grounds this in a Christian reading of the Urth cycle and in Severian's ending musings about being a puppet of good or evil, and recalls a passage about Other Lords becoming gods of antiquity.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · theology-and-religion · theories: Severian as the Sleeper rules Abaia; Severian is a Christ figure turned tyrant

alga,

Even more disturbing is the thought that Severian as the Sleeper has taken the throne of Abaia as his own. "Yesterday's Hero becomes Tomorrow's Tyrant" deal. Especially if one is looking at the Urth Cycle as a narrowly defined Christian text, in a certain way--then it =really= looks like the "Christ" has backed into the throne of the "anti-Christ" or some lesser pagan idol!

Then again, it is perfectly in keeping with Severian's musings at the end of TBOTNS where he wonders if he has been a puppet of the baddies or a puppet of the goodies and resolves to resist puppeteering with every fiber of his being, more or less. So it remains unresolved in the end.

(Wasn't there something somewhere about "If we can't stop the arrival of the New Sun there's going to be a mass exodus [of us Other Lords and our followers] into the corridors of time [and into the past where we will be worshipped as gods of antiquity]"? Another one of those future-influencing-past tricks.)

=mantis=
