Rajan and Food

Urth list · 14 messages · 2009-04-28 to 2009-04-30

2009urthThe Book of the Short SunThe Book of the Long SunO'Donnell, Tim (BOSI)Jordon FlatoJerry FriedmanBrian LovelyJames WynnDavid StockhoffMatthew Groves
The thread: Readers debated whether the Neighbors and inhumi of Wolfe's Long Sun books are linked to trees and vines, and how inhumi can change into vines or into winged reptiles. Brian Lovely proposed a tree-to-vine-to-devil chain, and James Wynn, Tim O'Donnell and David Stockhoff pressed objections that the transformations are unexplained. Jordon Flato argued the Neighbors enslaved the inhumi and that Silk and Horn's adoption of an inhumi son is redemptive. Matthew Groves offered feeding as an alternative cause of vine-like form, and the thread drifted into a corn polyploidy aside. It ended without agreement, with the transformation problem left unresolved.
  • Brian Lovely: Neighbors are trees and inhumi are parasitic vines that rise to slugs or bat-winged devils by feeding
  • James Wynn: Neighbors are dryads whose souls take plant form; the inhumi's transformations are the fatal problem for the tree/vine theory
  • David Stockhoff: Inhumi evolved from parasitic vines and change shape with food; soul and body boundaries must loosen
  • Tim O'Donnell: Tree/vine theory cannot explain inhumi becoming vines or vampiric reptiles
  • Jordon Flato: Neighbors enslaved and were destroyed for it; Silk and Horn's adoption of an inhumi son is Christlike redemption
  • Matthew Groves: Inhumi on Green are vine-like because they feed on trees, not because they evolved from vines

Themes: Christ figures and messiahs, Beasts and the animal in us, Robots, androids and artificial life

2009-04-28 Brian Lovely #

Proposes that the Neighbors are trees and the Inhumi parasitic vines, with Neighbors having infected humans with Inhumi and Inhumi themes centering on sacrifice and one race raising another. Responds to James Wynn by contrasting Silk's fasting in the Long Sun books with the Short Sun, citing the Spring hunger.
proposes · puzzle-solving · allegory-and-typology · theories: Neighbors are trees; Inhumi are parasitic vines; Neighbors infected humans with Inhumi to study them; Crossbreed humans are higher than pure Inhumi

He's a tree, he's a tree! Neighbors are trees, Inhumi are parasitic vines. .or something. Not sure what the Neighbor-version of Inhumi are-maybe the vine with a face that is cut for Incanto's staff. Except that seems so quiescent. You'd think the Neighbor-Inhumi would be a bit more active. The Inhumi are somehow about sacrifice and one race raising up another. The Neighbors infected humans with Inhumi to see what human-Inhumi would be like.not sure where I'm going with this, but there's something here about being willing to suffer for the betterment of the Inhumi. So, a) the crossbreed would be a "higher" being than the pure Inhumi, and b) you'd rid yourself of them by being so passive.they'd take on your Christ-like willingness to sacrifice yourself.

Granted, but when it is combined with the onset of ravenous hunger in the Spring, we know more is going on here (incidentally, this is more of that Tree-Neighbor connection that I still don't understand). I do not recall an instance of LS Silk pretending to eat food set before him. He fasts, but when there is not a lot of food available anyway, it is easy to be holy in that way. Not the same thing as what happens in SS, I think.

--Brian Lovely

2009-04-28 James Wynn replying to Brian Lovely #

Posits that the Neighbors are dryads whose steady form is one of the sleepy trees of Blue, and asks how inhumi change into vines or back into winged, clawed reptiles. Replies to Brian Lovely's tree/vine theory, calling the inhumi's transformations the fundamental problem with it and inviting him to answer.
disputes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Neighbors are dryads; Neighbors are trees, inhumi are vines; Inhumi transform into vines and reptiles

Brian, let's posit that the Neighbors are dryads--that their steady physical form is one of the "sleepy trees" in the forests of Blue. How do the inhumi make the jump to become vines? Or how do they transform back to winged/clawed vampiric reptiles? That is the fundamental problem with any "tree/vine theory" regarding the Neighbors and inhumi.

J.

2009-04-28 Jordon Flato replying to James Wynn #

Proposes that the creature that boards Horn's ship in The Book of the Long Sun may be a Neighbor or inhumi remnant, and rejects the theory that Neighbors are trees who become inhumi vines. Argues the Neighbors infected the Whorl with inhumi to test human souls, and asks who the Neighbors befriended on the Whorl and whether an unidentified Neighbor exists.
proposes · close-textual-reading · allegory-and-typology · theories: The creature on Horn's ship is a Neighbor or inhumi remnant; Neighbors infected the Whorl with inhumi to test human souls; Neighbors gave Blue to humans because of human souls

I've wondered if the strange creature (not the leatherskin) that jumps on the ship with Horn when he first sets out, which is described much like a neighbor but faaaar scarier isn't maybe a nieghbor/inhumi remnant. That is what occurred to me.

I can't get behind the neighbors as trees/inhumi as vines theory at all. It seems like such a stretch. There is no denying a connection of some sort between trees and neighbors. I'm not certain it is a literal connection. In New Sun, there are instances of Wolfe anthropomorphising trees and such. With human qualities. Why wouldn't the same be true on another planet, but echoing the essense of the 'original' inhabitants, the neighbors.

And I think, in terms of the Neighbors infecting the inhumi on the Whorl, it was primarily to get a sense of what the souls of the humans were like. When they saw the result, they made their deciscion that they could give the world to Humans, as they had confidence that the humans would have a chance, that the neighbors did not, to end the cycle of inhumi/host extinction, due to the possible quailities of the human soul. I think, in Horn/Silk in particular (keeping in mind that when the neighbors gave Blue to humans through Horn, he was still Horn and not Silk at all), they saw something in humanity (in my opinion, not his being a neighbor doppleganger) that convinced them that they might be able, at some point, to make a peace with the inhumi. They wouldn't have known that if they had not first infected the Whorl with inhumi.

What is much more controversial to me is that the neighbors say they also spent time on the whorl, and befriended some there. What are the implications of this? Who did they befriend? We have an inhumi character in Long Sun. Is there a as yet unidentified Neighbor?

2009-04-28 Jordon Flato replying to Jordon Flato #

Argues that the Neighbors enslaved the inhumi and were destroyed for it, and that Silk and Horn adopting an inhumi son, loving one for the first time, is the Christlike act that offers redemption. Cites Hide's closing line about changing the rules to support the claim.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · close-textual-reading · theories: Silk's love for an inhumi redeems the cycle; Neighbors' enslavement of inhumi caused their destruction

Also, it seems like the Neighbors choose to enslave the inhumi, much like humans were doing to each other. They were wiped out because of it. I see no evidence of the neighbors having" rid yourself of them by being so passive?they?d take on your Christ-like willingness to sacrifice yourself." 2009-04-28 Brian Lovely replying to Brian Lovely #

Proposes that the Neighbors and Inhumi of Blue are linked by a chain of transformations: Inhumi vines rise to slugs or bat-winged devils by feeding, and crossbreeding explains the eight-legged beasts. Grounds this in the Horn corn quest, a remark about polyploidy, and questions about who crossbred whom and why.
proposes · myth-and-structure · source-and-allusion · theories: Inhumi rise through feeding into higher forms; Neighbors and Inhumi form one crossbreeding cycle

Well, whatever the Neighbors are to trees, the Inhumi are to vines. Maybe their base form is the slug-like thing that drops out of a tree on green, kind of an amphibian that lays its eggs in water. But if there base form is just a vine, then feeding on something higher pulls them up from vine to amphibian. It's all up. The uncrossed Inhumi are still vines, those who have managed to move up a level are slugs, those who have fed on humans are bat-winged devils.

Crossbreeding. The corn that Horn seeks on the Whorl. The underwater greenhouses, the strange doubling of limbs that many creatures on Blue exhibit. I read somewhere in the list a theory about polyploidy something or other--some biology that I don't know anything about--but the upshot was that you can cross corn in such a way that the genetic pattern is doubled...something like that. More to the point, like beasts with eight legs. Who was crossbreeding who, and why? Did the Neighbors have to change themselves in order to survive? Were the 8-legged animals experiments, to perfect the technique?

2009-04-28 David Stockhoff replying to Brian Lovely #

Argues that the inhumi evolved from parasitic vines, that the Neighbors are tree-related, and that the inhumi change shape with food, not as a fixed species. Replies to Brian Lovely's tree-and-vine theory, granting the Neighbor link but rejecting vine-to-reptile transformation, citing a recalled hint and the inhumi's changes when feeding.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Inhumi evolved from parasitic vines; Neighbors are tree-related; Inhumi change shape with food availability; Inhumi are reptile-like, not reptilian

I have no idea where it was, but I recall a fairly clear hint dropped that the inhumi evolved from parasitic vines. There is certainly no evidence that they are reptilian---merely reptile-like.

And I agree that the Neighbors are somehow tree-related. Wolfe never wastes a clue, so there must be something to that.

I don't see any problem with transformations from one form to another, although I wouldn't propose that the inhumi can change into vines and back any more than humans can change into monkeys and back. But they do have the capacity to winter underground just like plants.

The inhumi plainly are highly changeable, and do not need to conform to 20th-century notions of what a species is---especially since those notions are wrong. They change when they feed on sentient life; they change with the availability of food.

2009-04-28 Matthew Groves replying to Jerry Friedman #

Proposes that the corn polyploidy debate in The Book of the Short Sun cues the Neighbors as a corrupted, fading species, paralleling dying corn and the colonists' pure new seed. Responds to Jerry Friedman by setting aside literal extra limbs and citing the seasonal decay-and-rebirth image of The Book of the New Sun as a parallel.
proposes · myth-and-structure · theories: Neighbors are a hybridized, corrupted species; Neighbors parallel a fading corn crop, colonists the new seed; Decay-and-rebirth image is seasonal in TBotNS and generational in TBotSS

Yeah, for me, the take-away from the argument about corn polyploidy (or whatever it's called) is not that the Neighbors have double the normal chromosomes and therefore double the number of arms and legs. That's silly. The point (IMO), or the suggestion, is that the corn hybridization business is, among other things, a *cue* that the Neighbors are (in some sense I don't quite understand) a hybridized or somehow immanently corrupted species. Or maybe they're just fading away like successive generations of corn crops, and like the enigmatic ghosts of Mars in Bradbury's Martian Chronicles (not John Carpenter). And the colonists represent a necessary pure strain for the rebirth of civilization. At any rate, I think the case is very strong that Wolfe is constructing an analogy between the Neighbors and the diminishing corn crop, and the colonists and the new seeds. In TBotNS, the primary decay/rebirth image was seasonal; in TBotSS it's this corn generation thing. I believe it's Marc who deserves credit for this insight.

Argues that the boundary between soul and body and between plant and animal must be loosened, so an inhumi could be a soul-tree or a plant-based creature that is an animal only ecologically. Responds to Brian Lovely's suggestion that long-buried inhumi shrivel into vines, and to James Wynn's reply that inhumi are active and that a Neighbor-soul-tree connection needs explaining.
proposes · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: Inhumi are plant-based creatures that are animals only ecologically; Trees are the souls of Neighbors and inhumi

I think part of the answer must include some loosening of our terms, especially soul vs body and plant vs animal. For example, if a soul can be a tree, then it won't be a tree as we know it, but some kind of soul-tree or tree-soul.

And if a creature runs around on legs eating other creatures, it may still have evolved from plants, and thus be an "animal" only from an ecological point of view. It may still be a plant on the genetic and cellular level. (The Rajan can be right and wrong at the same time.)

And if "vine" is merely a morphological term, it doesn't indicate lack of sentience or other qualities---merely that an alien creature grows very tall and skinny, lacks support structures, and feeds on the alien trees (which also provide support).

And so on ...
