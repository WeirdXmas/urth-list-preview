TWK: The Aelf dilemma

Urth list · 24 messages · 2005-12-05 to 2005-12-07

2005urthstilskinthalassocratCliff JudgeAndy RobertsonBob MillerJames WynnChris PDan'l Danehy-OakesRoy C. Lackey
The thread: Readers debated whether the Aelf in The Wizard Knight have souls and can gain eternal reward, with Roy C. Lackey holding they are mortal and soulless and thalassocrat, transentient, and Chris arguing Disiri gained a soul or that the Aelf could be elevated. The debate turned to whether Able's blood gift is a sacrifice, a binding, or a reward, with James Wynn reading it as sacrifice and Roy disputing that the Aelf could rise by drinking human blood. A parallel quarrel between Dan'l Danehy-Oakes and stilskin over arrogance and bigotry in a one-line joke escalated into personal accusations, then Dan'l withdrew his harsh reading. The thread ended without agreement, drifting into speculation about semen as a further Aelf appetite.
  • Roy C. Lackey: Aelf are mortal and soulless, with no afterlife reward
  • thalassocrat: humans hold God-like power over Aelf, so they are not necessarily soulless
  • transentient: Aelf reward is gaining a soul and becoming human, as Baki wished
  • Chris: Disiri gained a soul when promoted, and Mani keeps an eternal spirit
  • James Wynn: Aelf blood-giving is a sacrifice, not a binding
  • Andy Robertson: Aelf want semen as well as blood, sexual union being the greater uplift

Themes: Christ figures and messiahs, Catholic faith and sacraments, Robots, androids and artificial life

2005-12-05 Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that the Aelf in The Wizard Knight cannot follow Disiri to Skai because they have no souls and die permanently, so they have no afterlife reward to motivate worship. Supports this with quoted lines from Able and passages about the Aelf's mortality, responding to a post by I. about Odin, Etela and Disiri's choice.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theology-and-religion · theories: Aelf have no souls and cannot reach Skai

[snip]

Again, I overlooked the obvious. Able said, when he was speaking to Leort about Disiri and indirectly about his time in Skai, "it's where she can never go." (W, 102) The reason she can never go there is that the Aelf have no souls, no "spirit". (W, 46) When they die, they die dead, kaput, unlike humans. Those humans who get to Skai have to die, just as Able had to die, to get there.

Given that the Aelf are not spiritually immortal, they have no hope of eternal reward (still less the denizens of Muspel). So why the hell should they worship humans -- or anyone or anything else? If there's no "pie in the sky when they die", where's the payoff for whatever passes as righteous living for an Aelf?

-Roy

2005-12-05 thalassocrat replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that humans hold God-like powers over the Aelf, citing healing, summoning, weather-working and promotion of heroes, and that Aelf are therefore not necessarily soulless. Grounds this in examples from The Wizard Knight: Toug's healing, Able's summoning and role as champion, and Able raising Dsiri, concluding that Love lifts all creation toward the One True God.
disputes · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: Humans are God-like to the Aelf; Aelf are not necessarily soulless; Able intends Dsiri to ascend to God

On Sun, 04 Dec 2005 23:59:55 -0800 "Roy C. Lackey"

Humans have at least some God-like powers with respect to the Aelf. For example: Toug's healing of <whichever one it was>. They can also be "supernaturally" powerful warriors in Aelfrice - why else would Garsecg & the Aelf groom Able for the role of champion?

In fact, I suspect that humans are probably God-like to the Aelf in pretty much the same way that Overcyn are God-like to humans.

The God-like powers we see in action as far as I can recall are:

- Healing. Toug => Baki (?); Able-as-Overcyn => a bunch of people at the end of Wizard.

- Summoning. Able => Aelf (I think this is a human power of Able's, given Micahel's comments at their first meeting); Michael => Valfather; Loki => eastern dragon warriors (maybe).

- Weather stuff: Able-as-Overcyn in Mythgathr; can humans do the same kind of thing in Aelfrice?

- Promotion: Valfather etc => human heroes; I believe this is paralleled by Able => Dsiri

The last surely indicates that Aelf are not eternally & necessarily soul-less. That would go against all of my understanding of Wolfe's metaphysics. With "divine" assistance, all creation can rise through the spheres and meet the One True God. We certainly have multiple indications that Able expects this for himself, and I believe the ending of Wizard is intended to indicate that it will happen - for Dsiri as well, despite her lower origins.

As always with Wolfe, Love is the force which propels and drags everything higher.

2005-12-05 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes replying to transentient #

Argues that motives for religious service need not be reward or punishment, and that in The Wizard Knight Able's gift of blood to Disiri supplies a real supernatural reward that parallels the Eucharist. Responds to transentient's remark about avoiding supernatural punishment, using Wolfe's Catholic framework to ground the comparison with Christ giving his body and blood.
disputes · theology-and-religion · allegory-and-typology · theories: Able's blood gift parallels the Eucharist

Don't parade your arrogance. There are those who simply
seek to serve something they perceive as higher, better, or more important than themselves, whether it be a god
or a nation or a cause.

There are, of course, those who seek to avoid penalties
by following the rules of a god or a nation. And there are those who seek to gain rewards by ditto. But -- "the fact that a thing can be used in illegitimate ways, does not
illegitimate its legitimate uses." Religion, like patriotism, is not _necessarily_ about gaining rewards and/or avoiding punishment.

That said: there is definitely the possibility of "supernatural reward" for inhabitants of Aelfrice who worship inhabitants of Mythgarthr. Able proves this when he gives his blood to Disiri and so "raises" her to humanity and, so, ensouls her.

In Wolfe's Catholic-Christian terms, this is precisely what the Christ does by giving his body and blood in the Eucharist -- enables humans to gain some of the nature of the divine.

--Dan'l

2005-12-05 Roy C. Lackey replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that the Aelf in The Wizard Knight have no eternal existence, a textual given rather than a deduction, since Able says the Aelf are gone completely when they die. Grounds this in the Toug-healing-Baki scene, where Able describes Aelf mortality, and in Mani's remark about the Seven World creation myth, comparing the Aelf to Mani.
supports · close-textual-reading

It was not my deduction that the Aelf have no eternal existence; it's a textual given. In the scene where Toug healed Baki, Able said the Aelf were long-lived, and goes on to say: "Much longer-lived than we are. But short-lived as we are, we're immortal. Our spirits don't die. It's not like that for the Aelf. Dead, they're gone completely." (W, 46)

Baki then gives a synopsis of the Seven World creation myth, and Mani concludes with, "The gods of each world are the people of the next one up. That's Skai for us, and us for Aelfrice." (49) The Aelf are in a situation much like Mani, whose ninth death will mark the absolute end of his existence.

-Roy

2005-12-06 James Wynn replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that the quantity of blood needed to make an Aelf human is not absurd, since it would nearly kill the donor and so would rightly be hard to supply. Responds to Roy C. Lackey's objection about a pint or quart by reasoning that the ritual and the freely given blood both matter.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Blood must be freely given as well as quantified

I don't see why it should be absurd. However much it took. it was *nearly* enough to kill Able. Able didn't know if it would require enough to kill him, and didn't care if it did. If one assumes that actual *blood* is required and not merely the *ritual* of the offering, then the quantity is obviously important. Anyone who has baked a cake knows that quantities are important for real recipes. A small person would probably not be able to offer enough, and an average sized person could not survive having offered enough. Reasonably the precise amount of blood required would depend on the Aelf, but clearly it is so much that even if all humans knew that it could be done (which apparently none of them did except for Able), few would do it.

But it is probable that it is not *merely* the physical act of giving blood that is significant, but also that the blood be freely given. Otherwise, I presume, some Aelf would have discovered the process and drained a human host with sufficient blood. I don't think there is any evidence that that has happened.

So, the quantity matters and so does the ritual.

The people in and around Toug's village were said to perversely worship the Aelf. The Aelf scared the hell out of them -- scared Ravd, too. Most of the Aelf who appear in TWK want humans to give them blood, if only "a drop". Given the dynamics of the situation, that village should have been a mecca for every Aelf in Mythgarthr. With willing blood givers and greedy recipients, it seems to me that sooner or later some theoretical 'critical volume' of blood ingested by some Aelf must be achieved and a new "soul" born -- provided that a finite quantity of blood given willingly by mere humans can do the trick.
