The thread: The thread asked whether Severian, the narrator of Wolfe's Book of the New Sun, is a rule-breaker. Nicholas argued he mostly lives by rules and breaks them only under severe duress, such as Thecla's torture and his aunt's execution, driven by guilt and higher loyalties. David Stockhoff agreed he is neither rebel nor automaton, noting his sympathy for Vodalus, his partial disobedience tied to the alzabo, and lapses like lying and rape that may be forgiven. Jeff Wilson disputed this, arguing Severian obeys moral rather than legal rules, which makes him a successful rule-breaker trusted by collaborators to commit necessary evil, and citing his admission in URTH of doing great evil and great good. The thread ended without resolution, with Nicholas jokingly asking to return to the Brunians discussion.
  • Nicholas: Severian lives by rules and breaks them only under severe duress, out of guilt and higher loyalties
  • David Stockhoff: Severian is neither rebel nor automaton; he mostly obeys, with breaks that may be forgiven
  • Jeff Wilson: Severian obeys moral rather than legal rules, making him a successful rule-breaker

Themes: Justice, torture and mercy, Free will and choice, Suffering and sacrifice

2010-08-06 Nicholas Jost #

Disputes the claim that Severian is a rule-breaker, arguing instead that he lives by rules and breaks them only under severe duress, such as Thecla's torture and the execution of his aunt. Grounds this in Severian's guilt over both events and his developing higher loyalties, then jokingly asks to return to the Brunians discussion.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian's rule-breaking happens only under extreme duress

I call bull crap. Severian lives most of his adult life following rules and only breaks them under the severest of duress. The first example is his first girlfriend being tortured to death. The second is what the community views as his aunt is ordered executed by strangulation for not loving her psycopathic husband and after Sevs had a blow out with his second girlfriend/mother. Both can be seen as Sevs development of higher loyalties. Despite that he feels bad about both. Can we have Brunians back now? His sort of crazy was at least humorous.

2010-08-06 David Stockhoff replying to Nicholas #

Agrees with Nicholas that Severian mostly obeys rules and breaks them only under extreme duress, arguing he is neither rebel nor automaton. Responds by noting his sympathy for Vodalus reflects moral thought, that he disobeys Vodalus only partly because of the alzabo, and that his lying and rape occur in situations where he may be forgiven.
supports · close-textual-reading

I agree. Severian obeys when he can. He's neither a rebel nor an automaton; the fact that Vodalus had his sympathies merely means that he thinks about morality. What thinking young man wouldn't want to overthrow the existing order in that world?

Nor does he follow Vodalus' orders blindly, but only because the alzabo made him.

He also lies and rapes, but in situations where he may perhaps be forgiven.

2010-08-06 Jeff Wilson replying to David Stockhoff #

Argues that Severian's obedience to moral rules, not legal ones, makes him a successful rule-breaker, since collaborators trust him to commit necessary evil and then stop. Responds to David Stockhoff's remark that Severian obeys when he can, citing Severian's admission in URTH that he is a doer of both great evil and great good.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian's obedience to moral rules enables his rule-breaking

That's not much of a restriction given Severian's gift of rationalization.

Of course Severian is mostly obedient to the written and unwritten rules of his society, but is also keenly aware that the occasions that he hasn't obeyed have been rather on the extreme side. Upon his return to the throne in URTH he admits that he is a doer of great evil like Baldanders as well as a doer of great good, and that most people are likewise creatures given to both.

It is probably Severian's adherence to moral if not legal rules that enable him to be such a successful rule-breaker, since his acquaintances and collaborators have good reason to believe he can be trusted to do what evil crime is necessary and then stop. Just make sure he isn't required to torture a woman he's had sex with.
