Why the Whorl left early

Whorl list · 4 messages · 2001-04-13 to 2001-04-17

2001whorlThe Book of the Long SunNicholas GeversAdam StephanidesMichael Straight
The thread: The thread asked whether the Whorl leaves early in Return to the Whorl as an emergency departure, and whether Silk and Mainframe engineered that departure so Pas would stay behind. Nicholas Gevers proposed the early-departure reading, citing Silk's reasons given to Remora and the Whorl's need for Pas and Mainframe. Michael Straight objected that if Silk-in-Mainframe could force an early departure he could also stop Pas escaping to Blue, which exposes a tension in Gevers's reasoning. Adam Stephanides then denied the Whorl leaves quickly and argued Mainframe could not be moved without its human inhabitants, doubting Horn's claim that Mainframe will reach Blue. Straight closed by reading Horn's line metaphorically, as gods arriving through possession, and argued that a sacred window on Blue would need Mainframe itself, so someone is misreading the setup. The thread ends unresolved, with the
  • Nicholas Gevers: the Whorl departs quickly as an emergency, so Pas stays put while Mainframe and Pas are needed in place
  • Michael Straight: an early departure would let Silk stop Pas escaping too, so the reasoning is inconsistent; Horn's Blue line is metaphorical
  • Adam Stephanides: the Whorl does not leave quickly and Mainframe could not be moved without human cooperation, so Horn's claim lacks grounds

Themes: Robots, androids and artificial life, Gods, angels and the Increate, Doubles, twins and clones

2001-04-13 Nicholas Gevers #

Argues that Wolfe intends the Whorl to depart very quickly after Silk and his companions return to it in Return to the Whorl, as an emergency departure. Grounds this in Silk and Silk-in-Mainframe not wanting Pas to descend to Blue with Mainframe, following Silk's reasons given to Remora, and in the Whorl needing Pas and Mainframe in place.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: The Whorl departs quickly after Silk returns in Return to the Whorl; Silk-in-Mainframe prevents Pas descending to Blue with Mainframe

Thanks to everyone who responded to my utopian
theorisings; I was testing the waters with some ideas
for a paper on TBSS...

One point: I think that GW intends us to understand
that the Whorl leaves very quickly after Silk and Co
return to it after RTW. Reason: Silk-in-Mainframe and
the corporeal Silk don't want Pas descending to Blue
with Mainframe and all, for the reasons Silk gave
Remora and because the Whorl needs Pas and Mainframe
where they are. Thus: emergency departure, far swifter
than M'to would have thought possible.

--Nick Gevers.

2001-04-13 Michael Straight #

Argues that if Silk-in-Mainframe can make the Whorl leave early, he would also be able to stop Pas from escaping to Blue, and that the name Passilk suggests the remaining Pas is largely under Silk's downloaded persona. Responds to Nicholas Gevers's claim that Silk and Mainframe wanted the Whorl to depart quickly so Pas would stay put, pointing out a tension in that reasoning.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Silk-in-Mainframe controls the Whorl's departure; Passilk means Pas is mostly controlled by Silk's persona

But if Silk-in-Mainframe is enough in charge that he can get the Whorl to leave early, he's probably enough in charge to prevent "Pas" from escaping to Blue.

But then I'm of the opinion that "Passilk" implies that the only Pas left in Mainframe is a Pas that is mostly controlled by Silk's downloaded persona anyway.

-Rostrum

2001-04-17 Adam Stephanides #

Argues that the Whorl does not leave quickly and that Mainframe could not have been moved off the Whorl without the cooperation of its human inhabitants, questioning whether Horn's claim that Mainframe will reach Blue has any grounds. Responds to Michael Straight's quoted point that Silk-in-Mainframe's power to make the Whorl leave early would also let him stop Pas escaping, and cites the emergency repairs and the state of technology on Blue.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: Whorl departs decades after Silk and Co. leave; Mainframe cannot be moved without Whorl inhabitants

Earlier, I posted an argument that the Whorl does not leave very quickly, but decades after Silk and Co. depart (to be pedantic, we don't know when they arrive at the Whorl, or if they do at all).

Indeed, how could Mainframe be gotten onto a loader and off the Whorl without the cooperation of the Whorl's human inhabitants? (If you argue that the godlings could do it, then conversely they would also have the power to prevent the emergency repairs.)

Also, given the level of technology on Blue, it will be a generation at minimum before the colonists there are capable of powering and maintaining Mainframe. If it were to come down now, they might not even be able to preserve its physical integrity, since they can't stop the landers from being looted and rendered inoperable.

In fact, I'm not at all sure what Horn means when he says that Mainframe will come to Blue. We don't know, either, on what grounds he says this; it's not in his conversation with the surgeon.

--Adam

2001-04-17 Michael Straight #

Argues that Horn's line about Mainframe coming to Blue should be read metaphorically, as the gods of Mainframe arriving on Blue by possessing humans or animals rather than a physical computer being moved. Responds to Adam Stephanides's doubt about what Horn means by suggesting that a sacred window on Blue would require bringing Mainframe itself, so one of them misreads the setup.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theology-and-religion · theories: Mainframe's gods arrive on Blue by possession

I read this as metonymy (like when Jews refer to G-d as "Heaven"); he means that the gods of Mainframe will come to Blue, presumably by posessing humans or animals, not that some (big?) physical computer will be brought to Blue.

And based on Horn's comments about the possibility of getting a sacred window working on Blue, at least one of us (Horn or me) drastically misunderstands how the things work. That *would* require bringing Mainframe itself to Blue.

-Rostrum
