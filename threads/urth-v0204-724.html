Starcrossers

Themes: The dying sun and the cosmos

2002-04-20 James Jordan #

Argues that the Long Sun series was originally titled "Starcrosser's Landfall", linking it to The Fifth Head of Cerberus, and speculates whether the books share one universe or whether Briah and the Fifth Head's setting are separate. Grounds the argument in the first-edition Lake data, Horn's closing remarks, and thematic parallels between the Fifth Head and Short Sun.
proposes · source-and-allusion · myth-and-structure · theories: Long Sun and Fifth Head share a universe; Long Sun's No. 5 descends from a different Wolfe

It just occurred to me that Long Sun was originally called "Starcrosser's Landfall" (LoC data in the first edition of *Lake,* and Horn's statement at the end of the series somewhere) -- and of course this is a link with 5HC. Thematically, 5HC is closer to Short Sun, so if Wolfe was thinking "starcrosser" for Long Sun, that would indicate that he was thinking of the same universe. Or else, that he just likes the word and considered using it again. Or maybe that we ought to look for more 5HC themes in Long Sun than seem to be there (seem to me to be there, anyway).
Same universe, then? Well, Briah is the Bang-gnaB of a previous universe, but the No. 5 seems to be a descendant of Wolfe, in this universe. So, "same universe" would not seem to be right. Yet, people from Lemuria/Atlantis building spaceships? That ain't OUR universe. So maybe No. 5 is a descendant of the Briah Wolfe, not of our Wolfe.
Not that it matters. But it's fun to fool around with.
