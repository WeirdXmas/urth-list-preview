Themes: Hidden identities and parentage

2002-08-04 Robert Borski #

Argues that Candy has been prostituting herself to Den Weer and that the ladies' man Mr. M may therefore be Den Weer in Carnies. Adds evidence to Roy's question about Candy's wish to hear from Weer, citing two quotes from Charlie T.'s letter about Candy trying to get Doris to turn tricks.
supports · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Mr. M is Den Weer; Candy prostituted herself to Den Weer

Roy again:

--But then, why does Charlie write: "Anyway, if you have any comments on the picture you
would like to make, go ahead and make them to me and I will pass them along to Candy; she will be glad to hear from you."? Why should Candy be glad to hear from Weer?--

Because she's been perhaps prostituting herself to Den, and now wants further contact with (or money from) him. Once again from Charlie T.'s letter: "...I heard that Candy was trying to get Doris to turn a few tricks when Mrs. M. wasn't looking, so she could make some getaway money..." Apparently, Candy knows enough about the business from personal experience to explain, er, the ins-and-outs of the process to "Little Cinder."

Yet further evidence that ladies' man "Mr. M." may be Den Weer.

Robert Borski
