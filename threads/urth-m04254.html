2007-11-04 Steve Taylor replying to mark mullis #

Asks whether an archive of discussion exists for Wolfe's short stories, or whether the list minds if he asks about individual stories. Says he has lurked for ages, feels he is not an analytical reader, and wonders whether Wolfe suits him.
questions

I've been mostly lurking here for ages, impressed by the level of erudition and analysis - I'm not a particularly analytical reader at the best of times, and I sometimes wonder whether I'm the sort of person who should be reading Wolfe in the first place. Most of the discussion has been centered on the novels though, and I tend to read his short stories more.

Is there any archive of discussion on his shorter works - or do people mind if I just ask "what the hell was that all about" about a particular story?

Steve

2007-11-04 Joe Bushong-Taylor #

Announces that Gene Wolfe won the World Fantasy Award for Best Novel of 2006 for Soldier of Sidon and praises the work's insight into loss and personal identity. Reports Wolfe's acceptance remarks, including that reader requests shaped the book and that he may continue Latro's story.
informs

Gene Wolfe has won the World Fantasy Award for Best Novel of 2006 for *Soldier of Sidon*. The presentation of the award came at the World Fantasy Convention awards banquet today in Sarasota Springs, NY.

I find it gratifying that the fantasy community has recognized the importance of this work and, indeed, of Gene's contributions as a whole. If you have put off reading the Soldier books, get to them. The unfolding story of Latro offers insight into how we deal with loss, and the nature of personal identity.

In accepting the award, Gene noted that his decision to write the novel was due, in part, to the requests of many readers that he continue the story of Latro. He also hinted that he may heed calls he has received already to continue the story even further.

2007-11-05 mark mullis replying to Joe Bushong-Taylor #

Congratulates Wolfe on winning the World Fantasy Award for a single work, calling it his most illustrious individual award apart from the earlier lifetime achievement honor. Argues the Latro books are becoming as significant to his legacy as the Sun books, and introduces the poster as a new list member anticipating discussion of Pirate Freedom.
informs

A big congratulations to Gene. If I reason correctly, this is the most illustrious award he's received for an individual work (in other words: discounting the "Lifetime Achievement" award he received from the same panel a few years back).

The Latro books are shaping up to be every bit as significant an aspect of his legacy as the trinity of Sun books.

By the way, this is my first contribution to the mailing list. I've been slowly working my way through the archives, reveling in the knowledge that there is a forum on the internet for Wolfe fans to interact. I'm also eagerly awaiting the spike in discussion that will inevitably follow Pirate Freedom's publication next week.

Mark Mullis

2007-11-07 Matthew Keeley replying to Steve Taylor #

Points Steve Taylor to the Urth archive, where many threads discuss individual Wolfe stories, and to the WolfeWiki as another resource. Responds to Steve Taylor's request for an archive of discussion on the short fiction, noting that such threads often start with readers asking what a story means.
informs

If you search the archive on urth.net, you'll find that there have been quite a few discussions of individual stories, most started by someone asking "What the hell was that all about?" 2007-11-07 Michael Straight replying to Steve Taylor #

Welcomes a newcomer, Steve Taylor, and encourages him to ask about the ending of a Wolfe short story. Says the list has helped readers move from confusion to appreciation of stories, and that members are generally willing to explain an ending.
chats

Welcome, Steve. Ask away. I've had more than one of Wolfe's short stories go from "What was that?" to "Wow, what a great story!" thanks to insight of people on the list.

No promises, of course, but people here are generally happy to respond to a "What's going on at the end of this story?"

Rostrum
