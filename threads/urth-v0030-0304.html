Themes: Unreliable narration

2001-04-14 William H. Ansley #

Reports that the poster listened to a lecture series on Chaucer, where the lecturer calls Chaucer the first unreliable narrator in English literature, and draws a parallel to Wolfe's narrators. Notes the lectures were published by a teaching company and says they were good for a reader without prior humanities training.
informs · author-and-biography

I have just finished listening to a series of taped lectures called _The Life and Writings of Geoffrey Chaucer_*. Somewhere in this series, the lecturer talks about how Chaucer inserts himself as a character, the narrator, in _The Canterbury Tales_. He questions how much we should believe the narrator and says, "Chaucer may be the first unreliable narrator in English literature." This gave me quite a start.

Well, well. Wolfe has an even longer and more distinguished literary legacy than I realized.

William Ansley

*These lectures were given by Professor Seth Lerer and published by The Teaching Company <http://www.teachco.com/>. I thought they were quite good, but I had a humanities deprived education. I don't know how much they would have to offer to someone who had already taken a course on Chaucer.
