1998-11-06 David Duffy #

Argues that Alden Weir's life is a series of wrong choices while Smart makes the correct ones, and that Weir's final financial success is accidental. Grounds this in his behaviour at the forking points on the stairs, outside the freezer room and in the creek-bed, which the poster finds forgivable and suited to Purgatorio rather than Inferno, and raises Cabell parallels such as Jurgen, Manuel and Felix Kennaston.
proposes · close-textual-reading · allegory-and-typology · theories: Weir makes wrong choices while Smart makes correct ones; Weir's final success is accidental; Weir and Smart as Jurgen and Manuel

(Maybe it should be Alder rather than Alden -- elm, though then you can't throw in Den)

I have the impression (coming back to the pillow story) that Weir's life represents a wrong choice at every key point in his life, while Smart makes the correct choices. Weir's final financial security/success is quite accidental. Furthermore, his behaviour at the forking points -- on the stairs, outside the freezer room, in the creek-bed -- is quite understandable/forgiveable (coming back to the idea of Purgatorio rather than Inferno).

Also I might raise the two allusions to Cabell (and isn't there one Cabell novel set in Florida?) -- Cabell saw Jurgen as the thinker and Manuel as the doer (Weir/Smart?). Felix Kennaston in the _Cream of the Jest_ is perhaps even closer, though Wolfe and Cabell's world-views are slightly different. (do I need a smiley?).
