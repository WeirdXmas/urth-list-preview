2011urthThe Book of the New SunJerry FriedmanLee Berman
The thread: Lee Berman asked why The Book of the New Sun's Commonwealth has so many South American references, suggesting they signal a link to South America. Jerry Friedman agreed the link exists but argued it is loose: Wolfe drew on South America for color and local detail because it was fresher than medieval Europe or Byzantium, and he admitted he had overstated the point earlier. Berman then argued for a close model, listing the pampas, the Ouroboros river, Gyoll, and Salto, and defended the Nessus-Buenos Aires link, though he held that the name mainly points to the Hercules myth. Friedman countered that grasslands and cold islands are common worldwide, that the Parana and Lake Diuturna do not match, and that South American geography is a poor key for reading the books. The thread ended in disagreement over how close the correspondence is, with no shared conclusion.
  • Lee Berman: Commonwealth is closely modeled on South America, with many matching geographic and name links
  • Jerry Friedman: South America is a loose source of color and detail, not a one-to-one model
  • Lee Berman: Nessus-Buenos Aires link is real, but the name mainly points toward the Hercules myth
  • Jerry Friedman: Shared features like grasslands and cold islands are universal, and the rivers do not match

Themes: Myth and folklore sources, Names and their meanings, Colonization and the native

2011-11-03 Jerry Friedman replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that Wolfe chose South America as a source for the Commonwealth's color and local detail because it is less overworked than medieval Europe and distinct from Byzantium, and that the connection stays loose rather than one-to-one. Responds to Lee Berman's question about why South American references are so numerous, citing the Tale of the Rose and Nightingale on fantasy and color and the Borges-like character, and restating the poster's own earlier overstatement.
answers · source-and-allusion · genre-and-craft · theories: Wolfe chose South America for color and detail rather than a one-to-one mapping

Color and flavor.? These things aren't incidental--you remember that BotNS started with a cool costume for a con, and the storyteller in "The Tale of the Rose and that Nightingale" says that "fantasy, color, and pathos are the chief" merits of a tale.? Writers have to give to airy nothing a local habitation and a name.

A good source of solid details is real places and cultures.? South America worked well--it's not overdone like medieval Europe, it's different from Byzantium, it ties in with the character who has resemblances to Borges.

I agree with something David Stockhoff said--we can see the continent as the result of a glance at a map of South America, not detailed study of the map.? I put it too strongly when I said I didn't think we were supposed to connect it with South America, but I still see no reason to connect the mountains with the Andes or Nessus with Buenos Aires.

Jerry Friedman

2011-11-04 Lee Berman replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that the Commonwealth in The Book of the New Sun and Long Sun is closely modeled on South America, citing pampas, cold southern islands, the Ouroboros river, Gyoll, Salto, the cataracts and floating-island lake. Defends the Nessus-Buenos Aires link, citing Borges and the name's sound, while holding that the name's main purpose is pointing toward the Hercules myth. Responds to Jerry Friedman's doubt that Nessus and the Andes connect to South America.
disputes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Commonwealth is modeled on South America; Nessus name points to Hercules myth

I wouldn't (of course) argue that the Commonwealth is an exact match to South America but I don't think you are recognizing how close the match is. The Commonwealth has pampas where S. America has pampas. There are the cold islands to the south. There is the mention of the large Ouroboros river just where we'd would expect the Amazon to be. Gyoll tracks quite nicely to the Rio de la Plata + Rio Uruguay. There is even a town named Salto on the route north. There is the mention of "the cataracts" in the vicinity of where Iguazu Falls would be. And what other mountain range in the world has a lake with floating islands other than the Andes? These are not incidental features of S. America but (except Salto) are all major, defining landmarks of the continent.

With regard to Nessus, it does track geographically with Buenos Aires but there is also the resident blind librarian there who surely can't be dismissed in evoking Argentinian Jorge Luis Borges.

The name "Nessus" sounds a bit like Buenos Aires and I'm sure Wolfe was aware of that in picking it. But I don't think that was his primary purpose. We are told the name Nessus is meant to suggest poison and this is an ironic twist to the "good air" meaning of Buenos Aires.

But I think the primary purpose for choosing the name Nessus and invoking poison was to focus a laser pointer on the Hercules story. I don't think Wolfe was successful in this for most of his readers, unfortunately. But I see much in the subtext of Severian's story which tracks to that myth. But that is a different discussion.

Responds to Lee Berman's list of parallels, including the pampas, Ouroboros river, and Gyoll tracking the Rio de la Plata, and disputes using South American geography to read the book.

Perhaps not "just" where.? It's around the same latitudes.

What about the Parana?? Did I miss another river?

As you see, I don't agree.

Except it's on the west coast, not the east.? Also, Master Ash says Nessus was once a city by the sea, not a river.? However, at Buenos Aires the Rio de la Plata has fresh water and isn't a very good choice for the metaphor for time that Ash is suggesting.
