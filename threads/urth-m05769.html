A Good Society

Urth list · 7 messages · 2008-07-11 to 2008-07-11

2008urthThe Book of the Long SunJeff WilsonPaul BLane HaygoodJohn Smith
The thread: John Smith asked whether Gene Wolfe ever portrays a good or decent society, pointing to the brutal or indifferent worlds of several novels. Paul B. blamed bad societies on secularism or misguided spirituality, citing Long Sun. Jeff Wilson offered There Are Doors as a tranquil liberal democracy and, later, backed Lane Haygood's view of a flawed material world using Wolfe's interview remarks. Haygood argued for a Gnostic undercurrent without calling Wolfe a Gnostic. The thread ended with Paul B. questioning the interview sources, and Wilson supplying a link, so it stalled on sourcing rather than resolving the question.
  • John Smith: asks whether Wolfe ever portrays a decent society, citing brutal worlds
  • Paul B.: bad societies in Wolfe tend to follow secularism or misguided spirituality
  • Jeff Wilson: There Are Doors shows a tranquil liberal democracy; Wolfe's interviews call the world depraved
  • Lane Haygood: Gnostic theology underlies the flawed material world, though Wolfe is not a Gnostic

Themes: Gnosticism, heresy and other theologies, Power, rulers and politics, Catholic faith and sacraments

2008-07-11 John Smith #

Asks whether Wolfe ever portrays a good or even decent society, noting that the planet Sainte Croix in The Fifth Head of Cerberus seems brutal on re-reading and that Urth, the Whorl and The Death of Dr. Island show cruel or indifferent societies. Raises the question of whether Wolfe writes utopias at all, inviting examples of a decent world.
questions

Can any one think of a Wolfe novel or story in which
he portrays a good society?

When I re-read The Fifth Head of Cerberus, I realized
that on the first reading I had missed how brutal and
nasty the planet of Sainte. Croix is. Urth and the
Whorl are also terrible places. Stories like The
Death of Dr. Island have a cruel or indifferent
society in the background.

Wolfe doesn't seem to write about utopias. But does
he ever show even a decent world?

Jack

2008-07-11 Paul B replying to John Smith #

Argues that Wolfe's bad societies often go with secularism or misguided spirituality, citing Long Sun as the clearest case and noting no spiritual society is portrayed. Responds to John Smith's question about whether Wolfe ever writes a good society, mentioning Fifth Head of Cerberus, Urth and the Whorl as brutal settings.
supports · myth-and-structure · theories: Wolfe's bad societies linked to secularism

Yeah, and the bad society often goes hand in hand with secularism (or wrongheaded spirituality). Long Sun is of course the most obvious example of this, but I haven't read him describe a spiritual society either, to date. Presumably because life there would be so good it'd be dull writing.

Paul

2008-07-11 Jeff Wilson replying to John Smith #

Argues that the Goddess's world in There Are Doors is a tranquil liberal democracy resembling 1950s Britain, where she reigns lightly and lets people govern themselves. Responds to John Smith's question whether Wolfe ever portrays a good society, offering this book as a counterexample to his claim that the Fifth Head, Urth and the Whorl are brutal.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: The Goddess's world in There Are Doors is a tranquil liberal democracy

The Goddess' world in _There Are Doors_ is a pretty tranquil liberal democracy similar to 1950's Britain - She reigns with the lightest of hands and suffers Her people to govern themselves as long as Her worship and personal property are respected.

2008-07-11 Lane Haygood replying to Jeff Wilson #

Argues that Wolfe's fiction draws on Gnostic theology for plot points, so the flawed material world in There Are Doors need not seem strange. Responds to Jeff Wilson's description of the Goddess's tranquil 1950s-Britain-style democracy by noting the Gnostic vibe he finds across Wolfe's work, while stating he does not think Wolfe is a Gnostic.
disputes · theology-and-religion · theories: Wolfe's fiction mines Gnostic theology for plot points

Given the strong Gnostic vibe I get from a lot of Wolfe's work (not that I think he's a gnostic, mind you, but I think he mines the theology for plot points), the idea that the material world is flawed and evil isn't so strange.

Lane

2008-07-11 Jeff Wilson replying to Lane Haygood #

Argues that Wolfe's interviews back Lane Haygood's view that the material world is flawed and evil, noting Wolfe's claim that the rest of the world is a depraved hellhole. Supports Haygood's reading with a remark that Wolfe is glad to live in a Christian society, contrasting it with places like Turkey.
supports · author-and-biography · theories: Wolfe's interviews reveal a flawed material world view

His interviews affirm this; he says we are lucky to live in a Christian society cos the rest of the world (like Turkey) is a depraved hellhole where people kill kittens for fun.
