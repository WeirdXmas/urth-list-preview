Themes: Love and loss, Names and their meanings

2003-08-21 James Wynn #

Proposes that the tomatoes Silk refuses Maytera Rose in The Book of the Long Sun stand for the love he denies her, since the tomato is called the love apple. Grounds this in the French name pommes d'amour and Silk's later regret, while admitting no confirmation of Wolfe's intent exists.
proposes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Tomatoes in Long Sun symbolize Silk's denied love for Maytera Rose

I have presumed that because the tomato is called "the love apple" from the French "Pommes d'amour", the tomotoes Silk denied her were a metaphor for the love he denied her. Later he regretted it.

But I have no confirmation that that is exactly what Wolfe had in mind.

2003-08-22 Tami Whitehead #

Adds that "love apple" was also an old name for mandrake, whose fruit was thought to act as an aphrodisiac and love charm, supporting a sexual-symbolism reading of the tomatoes. Builds on James Wynn's quoted reading of the Long Sun tomato dispute between Silk and Maytera Rose as a metaphor for denied love.
supports · source-and-allusion · theories: Tomatoes as metaphor for denied love

Dunno if it helps, or is relevent, but love apples are
also another name for mandrake, intimating its use as
an aphrodisiac and in love charms. The fruit of a
mandrake is supposed to be superior in these regards
to the root, whose virtue is that it lasts longer and
can be shaped to resemble the person charmed. The
fruit is yellowish, and in shape and scent resembles
strawberries.
