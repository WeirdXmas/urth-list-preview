What does Horn look like?

Urth list · 7 messages · 2004-10-02 to 2004-10-07

2004urthThe Book of the Long SunThe Book of the New SunGreggJames WynnDon DoggettChristopher CulverRoy C. Lackey
The thread: James Wynn asked what Horn and Silk look like, noting that the Short Sun covers show Horn differently across books and may conflict with the text. Christopher Culver argued from the characters' ages and Silk's hair length that Horn would be balding rather than white-haired at about thirty-five. Wynn then questioned whether the covers were an approved error, and Don Doggett dismissed the Return to the Whorl cover as a misreading of the godling passage. Wynn suggested the cover painter may have had instructions, and Gregg speculated Jim Burns worked from an early draft or without the text. Roy C. Lackey ended the thread by quoting On Blue's Waters, where Silkhorn calls himself nearly bald at thirty-five and later sees white hair and an empty socket, settling the textual question.
  • James Wynn: covers are inaccurate and may reflect instructions or a story behind them, and Horn's baldness matters to the narrative
  • Christopher Culver: Horn at thirty-five would be balding, not white-haired, based on ages and Silk's hair length
  • Don Doggett: the Return to the Whorl cover misreads the godling passage and is no evidence of Horn's looks
  • Gregg: cover artist Jim Burns may have used an early draft or not read the text
  • Roy C. Lackey: the text itself settles it, with Silkhorn nearly bald at thirty-five and white-haired with an empty socket later

Themes: Robots, androids and artificial life, Memory and forgetting

2004-10-02 James Wynn #

Asks what Horn and Silk actually look like, noting that the Short Sun cover art shows Horn differently across books: young with a beard, then balding with long white hair. Points out the covers may be inaccurate, since Horn is supposed to resemble Silk, and says the covers are poorly matched to the text.
questions · close-textual-reading

I got the idea while reading "The Book of the Short Sun" that Horn was (originally) balding. Is this accurate?

The cover of "On Blue's Waters" shows a Horn looking quite young, with lots of hair and a beard. He looks like Richard Chamberlain in "Shogun".

The cover of "In Green's Jungles" shows a Horn walking with a the Neighbor after he lands on Green. He has long white hair and is balding. This was my understanding of the way Horn looked.

The cover of "Return to the Whorl" shows the exact same character on the Whorl talking to the godling. Of course, this character *should* have looked like Silk.

So what's going on here. The covers of these books are unimpressive enough that they could have at least been *accurate* to the story.

So... what does Horn actually look like? What does Silk look like?

~ Crush

2004-10-02 Christopher Culver replying to James Wynn #

Argues that Horn in On Blue's Waters could not have had white hair at about thirty-five, so balding is more likely, and that Silk's long hair would also make him look old too early. Grounds the view in the ages of Horn and Silk and the stated length of Silk's hair in The Book of the Long Sun, replying to James Wynn's question about how the two look.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading

From: "James Wynn" <[email]>

Horn was only 35 years old or so at the beginning of OBW. While life was hard on the frontier planet of Blue, resulting in Horn having the tone of a crotchety old man, I don't think that by 35-40ish he could have had white hair. Instead, the picture of him as balding is more probable. Silk could have had white, long hair (in tBotLS we're told his hair is fairly long), but even he would have been pretty young, so life must have been really tough on the Whorl to make him look so ancient before the age of 50.

Christopher Culver

2004-10-03 James Wynn replying to Christopher Culver #

Argues that Horn's middle-age appearance is plausible for a frontier life, but that the cover art for In Green's Jungles and Return to the Whorl conflicts on whether Horn or Silk is balding. Asks whether the covers are a mistake Wolfe approved, or whether Horn and Silk look very similar, since Horn's baldness matters to the narrative.
questions · close-textual-reading

Well, life was no picnic on Blue, either. I remember reading "Angela's Ashes" as the author described his parents sitting by the fire clacking their false teeth, and I realized that at that point in the story they were in their late 20s or very early 30s. Yet I had already come to think of them well into middle age. They were worn out well before their time. So it is not inconceivable that Horn looked like a middle-age man by his mid-thirties. But the covers to IGJ and RttW describe very specific events in the story in which there is no real question whose body we are looking at. Yet it seems that one of them is a mistake. OTTH the character in the cover art is not the sort to make books fly off the shelves, so I'm thinking the design was not pure marketing trumping the author and story. Which makes me think Wolfe was involved.

I was hoping someone here would know definitively if the cover art were a mistake that was unapproved by Wolfe. Or......do Horn and Silk look verrrrry similar? Either way, I have always considered it an important narrative point that Horn was balding so how he looked...whether it was he or Silk that was balding is kind of important (to me).

~ Crush

2004-10-03 Don Doggett replying to James Wynn #

Argues that the cover art of Return to the Whorl is a misreading of the passage where Silkhorn is lifted by the godling, since the text describes the godling's thumb looking like a head in a curved hat. Responds to James Wynn's question about whether the cover shows Horn balding by dismissing the art as evidence of how Horn looks.
answers · close-textual-reading · theories: Cover art misreads the godling's thumb passage

Don replies:

I highly doubt the cover art was approved by Wolfe. The cover for RttW (besides being hideous) is the result of a misreading of the passage where Silkhorn is picked up by the godling. It describes the godling's thumb as looking like a head with a curved hat (thumbnail). So I wouldn't look to the cover art for any insight.

2004-10-07 Roy C. Lackey replying to James Wynn #

Answers James Wynn's question about whether Horn was originally balding by quoting On Blue's Waters: Silkhorn describes himself at thirty-five as nearly bald, and later sees his reflection with white hair and an empty socket. Dates the first passage to Silk's departure from Gaon and the second to his early forties, treating both as descriptions of Horn's body.
answers · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Horn's body is Silk's body

Crush queried:

Yes. Ignore the book covers. I don't remember Horn's hair color, but it isn't white. This is what Horn looked like when he began his quest (It is, of course, Silkhorn who wrote):

"They had brought food, tents, and trunks full of clothing, expecting to spend a week or more on Lizard; but in my books Silk was an account that had never been closed, one so large that it dwarfed all others. At fifteen, I had thought him the greatest of great men. At thirty-five, only a little taller, thick-bodied and nearly bald, I thought him a great man still." (OBW, p.65)

This is what Silk(Horn)'s body looked like, right after he left Gaon. He's then in his early forties.

"Saw my own reflection standing in the water holding up my spear, wild white hair and empty socket, lined and worried old face." (OBW, p.381)

-Roy
