AEG: Gideon

Urth list · 13 messages · 2009-01-04 to 2009-01-07

2009urthJohn WatkinsAdam ThorntonJosh GellerMarc AraminiDave TallmanRoy C. Lackey
The thread: Roy C. Lackey opened by asking who shot Gideon in An Evil Guest and whether Reis changed his mind after the poison bracelet was taken back, building the case on timing and Reis's lies. Dave Tallman argued the shooter was probably Diana Diamond of the Squiddies, then later proposed that the Takanga Ha'i assassin in Gideon the Ninth might be Donny Duke, a theory he admitted rested on thin evidence. Marc Aramini added a supporting suggestion about the D.D. initials, and Tallman's further posts on stage makeup, a healed axe wound and off-planet dealings for Reis drew Lackey's pushback on interplanetary phone plausibility. Lackey also argued Gideon put ambition above love for Cassie, which Adam Thornton disputed with a hypothetical plot; the thread ended in unresolved speculation with no consensus.
  • Roy C. Lackey: Gideon's ambitions outweighed his love for Cassie; questions who shot him and what Reis wanted
  • Dave Tallman: shooter was Diana Diamond, later Donny Duke; Reis pursued off-planet business via Woldercan
  • Adam Thornton: a low-born lover arranging a rich marriage is a plausible hypothetical, not from the text
  • Marc Aramini: the 'not a man' dancer with D.D. initials might be Diana Diamond, so a reread is needed
  • Lackey (on phone calls): real-time interplanetary calls are implausible, so Woldercan routing is doubtful

Themes: Hidden identities and parentage, Masks, disguise and shapeshifting, Love and loss

2009-01-04 Roy C. Lackey #

Asks who shot Gideon in An Evil Guest, or who was behind the shooter, and tests whether Reis changed his mind after the poison bracelet was taken back. Builds the case on the timing of Gideon's breakfast with Cassie, the shooting that afternoon, and Reis's later lie and his hiring of Gideon for an unexplained job.
questions · chronology-and-plot-logic · puzzle-solving · theories: Reis had a change of heart too late to stop the shooter; Reis lied to Cassie about the shooting

I agree.

I still wonder who shot Gid or, more to the point, who was behind the shooter. Gid had a late breakfast with Cassie the morning after the cast party. At about that time, Reis took the poison bracelet back from Margaret. Gid was shot early in the afternoon after dropping Cassie off at her apartment. Within 24 hours after being shot, he learned that Reis was looking for him to give him some help. So did Reis have a change of heart, as he did with Cassie, but too late to call off the shooter? Then why lie to Cassie about not being responsible for the shooting and tell her that he would find out who the shooter was? Also, Reis claimed that he preferred to win people over to him, rather than kill them.

On a related note, apparently some time during the three-month gap between plays, he hired Gid's services at an even higher fee than for a past consultation. Exactly what did he hire Gid to do? Deliver Cassie to him by buying off Gid's affection for her?

-Roy

2009-01-05 Dave Tallman replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that the shooter of Gideon in An Evil Guest was probably Diana Diamond of the Squiddies rather than the FBI or Reis, using Chase's reasoning on pages 203-204 and her poor marksmanship on pages 252-253. Responds to Roy C. Lackey's question about who shot Gid, citing Chase's statements about Cassie, the ATF, and the Squiddies.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Diana Diamond shot Gideon; Reis did not order the shooting

Chase told Cassie his best ideas on who shot him on pp. 203-204. 1) He was convinced it wasn't Reis, that Reis only wanted to get him medical help and enlist him. Chase must have been certain of this or he wouldn't have been working for Reis.
2) Chase didn't think it was the FBI because: they are better shots, they only use violence when they have no choice, and they want him alive and active.

That leaves two other major players, who may be the same: ATF and the Squiddies. Both use violence without caution: the deaths of Norma Peiper and Brian Pickens show that. The ATF might be poor shots (if Scott was lying and Cassie was the intended victim). The Squiddie assassin "Diana Diamond" was definitely a poor shot (p. 252-253), since she fired multiple times without killing anyone. She's a good candidate for Chase's shooter. She admits to killing Brian Pickens, so she was in Kingsport. I was hoping there would be a description of a facial scar that could have come from a cleaver, but no such luck. Cthulhu recruits may heal quickly.

Chase admitted to being hired to deliver Cassie, but not to loss of affection for her. He told her he loved her (p. 201). He just seemed to think Reis was winning in that department, and was surprised by Cassie's kiss and her offer to let him spend the night.

2009-01-05 Roy C. Lackey replying to Dave Tallman #

Argues that Gideon put his ambitions ahead of his love for Cassie, so his professed love was secondary to those ambitions. Cites his note, his excuses in Cassie's last conversation, and his claim to be a temporary consultant, then asks what Reis really wanted from him.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Gideon subordinated his love to ambition; Reis hired Gideon for a hidden purpose

But Gid saw the shooter and said it was a man. (203)

Yes, Gid professed his love for her in his note (216), and he may even have been sincere, in his fashion. What I meant was that, even at the surface level, Gid subordinated his regard for her to his ambitions. No man who really loves a woman is complicit in delivering her for marriage to another man. Cassie realized that in her last conversation with him (228), when he offered excuses for not coming to pick her up in the islands.

Reis didn't hire Gid, at great cost, to chauffeur Cassie to the islands. Anyone could have done that. Besides, Gid said that he had been hired as a temporary "consultant". (197) Gid had a reputation for getting things done that others couldn't do. What did Reis really want from him?

-Roy

2009-01-05 Adam Thornton replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Disputes Roy C. Lackey's claim that no man who truly loves a woman helps deliver her into another man's marriage, proposing instead a story where a low-born lover arranges his beloved's marriage to a rich man for later gain. Offers this hypothetical plot as a fun theme, citing a similar scheme in Stephenson's Baroque Cycle rather than any passage of An Evil Guest.
disputes · genre-and-craft

I take exception.

Even apart from polygynous cases, it strikes me that this could be a magnificent theme for a story, and one that I'm sure has been done (indeed, it's done in Stephenson's Baroque Cycle, after a fashion) many times. For example: the low-born lover sets up the object of his adoration so that she may marry into money or power, after which she may poison her husband (or indeed, allow him to die a natural death if he is much older than she) so that she may then marry the low-born lover and drag him into a higher social or economic sphere.

Sounds like it could be a fun read.

Adam

2009-01-05 Dave Tallman replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that the Takanga Ha'i assassin in Gideon the Ninth's Aeg-era story may be Donny Duke, weighing clues like the thick green drink, the "not a man" description and the shared D.D. initials. Responds to Roy C. Lackey's gender remark by listing textual matches and counterclues, such as Diana's denial of seeing Cassie's hornpipe performance, while conceding the theory rests on thin evidence.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: The assassin is Donny Duke

The gender of the Takanga Ha'i assassin is highly ambiguous. The doctor says it was once human and female, but that his opinion hardly matters. Chase may have been mistaken in thinking his attacker was a man.

The case for Donny Duke's connection with the assassin is good: 1) "In real life Donny Duke was small and swishy..." p. 53. He is later described as "not a man."
2) The assassin is a "little bit of a thing." p. 256.
2) Duke plays an assassin in "Red Spot" p. 63.
3) The actor who played the King Kanoa character in "Dating the Volcano God" played a character who hired Duke as Cassie's assassin in "Red Spot".
3) He likes thick green drinks (63,64). The assassin has thick green spit (252). 4) The common initials equals common identity game: both are known by D.D.

On the other hand:
1) "Diana" she has never tried to "tread the boards" as an actor. 2) "Diana" says she didn't see Cassie's performance in which she danced a hornpipe in a scanty costume (250). Cassie's partner in that dance was Donny Duke (131). One of her fellow cult members did see that performance, and that could well be Duke.

The assassin could be lying, of course. The connecting clues are so good that I hate to give up the theory on her say-so alone. Duke's face had red pimples, and the assassin's was very white, but this could be the result of cultish transformations.

2009-01-06 Dave Tallman replying to John Watkins #

Argues that Diana's line about treading the boards is not a flat lie, citing the old-fashioned theatrical phrase and its use by Vince Palma on p. 63. Suggests Donny may have picked up the phrase from Vince and lists the secret-identity suspicions the poster holds about the characters Cassie sits with at the party.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Donny picked up the phrase from Vince Palma; Vince Palma is possessed by the Volcano God; Margaret is a werewolf or spy; Donny is a Cthulhu cultist or assassin

The other statement is not exactly a lie either. "An actress with no more vocal control than that? Perhaps I may tread the boards myself someday. I couldn't be worse" (p. 250). It doesn't really say that "Diana" never acted.

The phrase "tread the boards" is old-fashioned theatrical slang. Vince Palma uses it on p. 63, "It was a privilege and an honor to tread the boards in your distinguished company." Perhaps Donny the assassin picked up the phrase from him. It's interesting that at the party Cassie ends up at a table with Vince, Margaret, Norma, and Donny, all of whom I have suspected of having plot-related secrets:

Vince (Possessed by the Volcano God? Wearing invisible headdress?) Norma (Ex-wife of Reis? Plotted with ATF?)
Margaret (Werewolf? Spy? Jewel thief? Seller of pictures to tabloids/internet?)
Donny (Cthulhu cultist? Assassin? Shooter of Chase?)

2009-01-06 Dave Tallman replying to brunians #

Argues that the actor Donny cannot be Chase's shooter because a facial axe wound would show in rehearsals, and that stage makeup could hide it. Offers a rebuttal: the assassin was transformed by the Squid God, which healed the wound and removed the pimples, and was a skilled mimic of voices.
disputes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Donny is Chase's shooter; The assassin was transformed by the Squid God

That's the biggest flaw I see with Donny as Chase's shooter. He has to appear in rehearsals for "Dating the Volcano God" very soon after the attack, and a big face wound would would be obvious. But he is an actor and would have access to advanced makeup technology. It's doubtful that his red pimples show in his stage roles, for example. You might have a pimply killer Seurat, but a pimply dancer in a musical would be strange.

The fact that Cassie doesn't recognize Donny as the assassin by face or voice is more easily explained. The assassin may have been transformed by the Squid God -- maybe this was necessary for finally healing the axe wound, and removing the pimples was a side-effect. The assassin was probably good at voices, since she critiqued Cassie's vocal control.

2009-01-06 Dave Tallman replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Proposes that Reis's reason for hiring Gid was to divide Gid's loyalties, and that Reis was pursuing something off-planet, possibly on Woldercan, which would explain his absence from Cassie on the islands. Grounds this on Reis's long absence after an early visit, Cassie's call to Chase saying he would be hard to reach, and a time-shift in the island phone calls.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Reis was off-planet on Woldercan; Phone calls routed through Woldercan

One advantage of hiring Gid was that it divided his loyalties. He couldn't deliver Reis to the FBI while taking his money (202). But there must be something big going on in the background, too. Reis had a strong motivation for joining Cassie on the island, but he let her stay there alone for a long time. Since he hopped from Europe to see her early on, it's hard to see what could be keeping him away. He could at least spend some nights. I suspect it was something off-planet, maybe even in Woldercan.

When Cassie called Chase he said it would be difficult to get to her (p. 228). Was he also off-planet? Maybe, since there's something strange about the phone service on the Takanga Islands (the time-shift we noticed for the call to Sharon). Perhaps calls are routed through Woldercan, and that's how Cassie was able to get the call through to him.
