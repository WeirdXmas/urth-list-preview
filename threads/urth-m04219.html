Teaching Wolfe?

Urth list · 4 messages · 2007-05-07 to 2007-05-08

2007urthCraig BrewerDan'l Danehy-OakesMatthew KeeleyJosh Geller
The thread: Craig Brewer asked the list for advice on teaching Gene Wolfe in a medieval and Renaissance fantasy course, weighing Castleview against The Wizard Knight for students new to Wolfe. Brunians replied with amazement that Wolfe has never held a university professorship, comparing him to Delany and Crowley, and wished for time to read. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes speculated that Wolfe's conservative politics clash with academia, offering no evidence. Matthew Keeley advised The Wizard Knight over Castleview because Castleview is out of print and bookstores may not stock enough copies, and asked for a syllabus if a course is taught. The thread drifted from pedagogy into an unsupported political explanation and ended without resolution.
  • Craig Brewer: asks which Wolfe book suits a fantasy course for newcomers
  • brunians: puzzled that Wolfe lacks a professorship like Delany and Crowley
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: Wolfe's conservative politics may explain no academic post, unsupported
  • Matthew Keeley: teach The Wizard Knight since Castleview is out of print

Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Power, rulers and politics

2007-05-07 Craig Brewer #

Asks the list for advice on teaching Gene Wolfe in a medieval and Renaissance fantasy course, weighing Castleview against The Wizard Knight for students who have not read him. Grounds the question in past class discussions of Castleview alongside Malory and Able, and in concern about the length of The Wizard Knight.
questions

Hello to the list!

I recently finished teaching a course on
medieval/Renaissance ?fantasy,? but I?m revising it
now to alternate old texts with new. I was considering
teaching either Castleview or the Wizard Knight. I
suggested both to a couple of ?fanboy/girl? students
who hadn?t read any Wolfe, and they went over quite
well. We had some great discussions on Castleview
contra Malory and Able contra just about everything
during office hours.

But I was wondering if anyone has ever taught Wolfe
before. Specifically, I was hoping for any tips on how
to deal with the all but inevitable confusion that
people being coerced into reading him might face. I?m
not sure if it would even work (simply for length with
TWK, at the very least), but both options just seem to
me like wonderful modern takes on old romance
themes/styles (apart from the glee of getting new
people to read Wolfe).

I would certainly appreciate any thoughts.

-Craig

2007-05-08 Matthew Keeley replying to Craig Brewer #

Suggests teaching The Wizard Knight rather than Castleview in a college course, because Castleview is out of print and bookstores may not stock enough copies for a class. Asks the teacher who posted the query to share a syllabus with the list if a Wolfe course is taught.
proposes

As a college student myself, I heartily wish that some of my professors would teach Wolfe, though I suppose it might be a hard sell for some students. I'd suggest teaching TWK over Castleview though, as I know that Castleview is out-of-print at the moment, and I imagine that even the best of college bookstores would have trouble getting enough copies for an entire class onto their shelves (Or at least that's my personal experience from working at a college bookstore). If you ever do teach a class with Wolfe texts in it, would you mind posting the syllabus to Urth? I'd be really interested in seeing it.

-Matt
