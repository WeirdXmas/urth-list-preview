Themes: Books, libraries and writing

2008-06-02 Christopher Hettinger #

Agrees with Dave Tallman that the manuscript Wolfe received is the second one, arguing Severian said he would copy the second exactly, including its mention of copying on the ship. Grounds this in the point that a future-tense reference does not show the event has not already happened, concluding Severian left his narrative unchanged.
supports · close-textual-reading · theories: Wolfe received the second manuscript

I agree that it is the second manuscript that Wolfe "recieved". Severian said he would copy the second the exactly the same as the first: which would include his mention of making a copy on the ship. Just because a book refers to an event in the future tense does not mean it hasn't happened already. It seems clear to me that Severian left his narrative exactly the same with no additions and nothing taken out.
