The thread: Readers of Return to the Whorl asked what the secret of the Inhumi is and what several plot points mean. Tim O'Donnell argued the secret is essentially the Golden Rule, since the Inhumi feed on and absorb human cruelty, while Jerry Friedman offered a parent-child parallel in which cruelty passes down to children, noting where it breaks down. Larry Miller repeatedly asked open questions about Scylla, Horn, the bird and the relaunched Whorl, and questioned Sergei Soloviev's Christian resurrection reading of Scylla's buried body. The thread ends unresolved, with several questions still unanswered.
  • Tim O'Donnell: the Inhumi's secret is the Golden Rule; kinder humans would make them harmless
  • Jerry Friedman: the animal inhumi mirror parent-child transmission of cruelty, with limits to the parallel
  • Sergei SOLOVIEV: Scylla wants her buried body back for a Christian soul-body reunion at the Last Judgement
  • Larry Miller: posing open questions and doubting resurrection and Horn-as-leader readings without offering answers

Themes: The dying sun and the cosmos, Beasts and the animal in us, Suffering and sacrifice

2011-10-11 Tim O'Donnell replying to larry miller #

Argues that the secret of the Inhumi in Return to the Whorl is essentially the Golden Rule, since they feed on human cruelty and deceit and absorb those traits from their hosts. Responds to Larry Miller's earlier post by proposing that if humans were kinder, the Inhumi would be no more dangerous than a snake or other dangerous animal.

2011-10-11 Jerry Friedman replying to James Wynn #

Argues that in Return to the Whorl the animal inhumi's feeding on humans mirrors parent-child relations, since children absorb traits from adults and so cruelty passes down. Responds to James Wynn's quoted remark about the inhumi and their offspring by offering this extended parallel and noting where it breaks down.
supports · myth-and-structure

For?me, this resonates with the trilogy's theme of parent-child relations.? Children absorb?traits to some extent from their parents and other adults, so if we were nicer to children--indeed to people in general--they'd be nicer to us. ?

Which would put those offspring in a world of hurt, incidentally.? Though /their/ offspring, if any,?might go back to feeding on us.? The parallel to children may break down here. ?
Jerry Friedman

2011-10-12 Sergei SOLOVIEV replying to larry miller #

Proposes that Scylla wants to return to her buried body because, in a Christian view, soul and body must be reunited at the Last Judgement, so the buried body is a chunk of her soul to be reunited. Grounds this in Catholic and Orthodox burial tradition and in how Scylla and Oreb are copies of the Long Sun original.
proposes · theology-and-religion · theories: Scylla must reunite with her buried body; Scylla-Cilinia soul fragment reunited with body

My personal opinion is that 1) It is related to the idea that for the final resurrection and Last Judgement the soul must be reunited with the body. For this purpose, by the way, the body must be buried and not burned, for example. I cannot remember, what is explicitly told on this in the Bible,
but it is a very strong tradition in Catholic Church and Ortodox Christianity.
GW gives it a sort of a twist - meaning that Scylla-Cilinia on the Long Sun Whorl, and even its "weak copy" in Oreb can be seen as having a "chunk of its
main soul" and it must be reunited with its body. 2) The deal is that the Scylla on the red Sun Whorl teaches Horn how to find/call Seawrack using its language and in exchange he brings a piece of soul of Cilinia who was connected with Scylla. 3) Because he thinks that Silk-Horn visiting the Red Sun Whorl as a sort of a ghost (in the Book of the Short Sun) is Malrubius. 4) I think that it has to return to the Urth (Ushas) to repopulate it after the flood.

Best

Sergei Soloviev
