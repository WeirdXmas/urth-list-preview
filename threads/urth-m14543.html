2010-06-14 James Wynn replying to Gwern Branwen #

Notes that the linked blog post identifies Asopus, the river-man and Swift God Latro mentions, as both a god and the river beside the battle that precedes the novel's opening. Confirms the blog's point but says he has found no reference to Asopus having horns.
supports · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Asopus is both a god and the river Asopus

"Asopus, whom Latro calls the ?river-man? (p. 6) and the ?Swift God? (p. 7) is both the name of a god and the name of the important river next to the battle scene that just precedes the opening of the novel."

All this is true. I haven't found any reference to him having horns, however.

2010-06-14 Eugene Zaretskiy replying to James Wynn #

Quotes a passage from a classical source addressing the River Asopos as a horned one, supplying the reference that the replied-to poster said he could not find. The post cites a website collecting myth about the river, answering James Wynn's remark that he had found no mention of horns.
informs · source-and-allusion

"[Adrastos addresse the River Asopos, whose waters refresh the parched troops of the army of the Seven Against Thebes :] O thou, whom no suns are wont to tame, O horned one, so lavish of never failing waters..."

Source: http://www.theoi.com/Potamos/PotamosAsopos.html
