The outsider (Lovecraft)

Urth list · 5 messages · 2014-06-30 to 2014-06-30

2014urthThe Book of the New SunThe Book of the Long SunFred KiescheAntónio Pedro MarquesDaniel Petersen
The thread: Posters asked whether Lovecraft's short story "The Outsider" has any link to Wolfe, prompted by the shared title and echoes of Severian's diction in the word 'strewed.' António Marques argued the Wolfean echo is real and that Poe could not have produced this specific story, answering Fred Kiesche's Poesque reading. Daniel Otto Jack Petersen then proposed that Wolfe's divine Outsider and Lovecraft's monstrous Outsider are both outcast, subterranean figures, with Wolfe recasting God as a Chestertonian monster. The thread is short and ends with the connection offered as valid but obscure, with no reply recorded.
  • António Marques: Lovecraft's story echoes Wolfe's prose and Severian's diction, and Poe alone would not have written it
  • Daniel Otto Jack Petersen: Wolfe's divine Outsider and Lovecraft's Outsider are both outcast monsters, with Wolfe inverting the Christian deity in a Chestertoni
  • Fred Kiesche: argued the piece is Poesque (quoted, not posting in this thread)

Themes: Christ figures and messiahs, Gnosticism, heresy and other theologies, Names and their meanings

2014-06-30 António Pedro Marques #

Argues that Lovecraft's short story "The Outsider" reads like Wolfe, with its wording and imagery echoing Severian, and wonders whether the title 'Outsider' links to Wolfe's Outsider. Grounds this on the word 'strewed' matching Severian's battlefield diction and on the story's overall language, and asks whether any discussion links it to Wolfe.
proposes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Lovecraft's The Outsider echoes Wolfe's Outsider

Hi.
For some reason, I'd never read this short piece. Now I'm trying to fill the blanks in some authors, I've come across it:

http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Outsider_(short_story)
http://www.hplovecraft.com/writings/texts/fiction/o.aspx

And you know what? The language rings very Wolfean. At first sight, it's a lame gothic tale. And there's really nothing hidden below that. But it can also be a lot of things if you want to read them into it. What I find interesting is that not only it could have been written by Severian, up to the last word, some imagery is very evocative. And then the words themselves. I'd have a very hard time believing the corpses that *strewed* Severian's battlefield came from anywhere else, even if the image is different, but what got me wondering was if, with such gleaning going on, the name 'Outsider' has got some connection with this story too. Yes, at first sight there's no relationship to be found.
I couldn't find any discussion of this story in connection to Wolfe - after all, why should there be?. Do you know of any? All I could find were analyses of 'An Evil Guest', which I have yet to read.

2014-06-30 António Pedro Marques replying to Fred Kiesche #

Argues that Wolfe's 'The Outsider' echoes Lovecraft's prose in a distinctly Wolfean way, while Poe's influence is real but would not have produced this particular story. Responds to Fred Kiesche's claim that the piece is Poesque, noting Poe could plausibly have written stories like 'The Music of Erich Zann' but not this one.
disputes · genre-and-craft

Yes, of course. My choice of words is due to the fact hat 1) I don't get this Wolfean linguistic ring from the rest of HPL and 2) tho I agree many of the piece's elements are quite inspired by Poe, I don't think it would have been written by Poe. Can't quite say why. I can see Poe writing 'The Music of Erich Zann', or any of the stories that revolve around corpses, but for some reason not this one.

2014-06-30 Daniel Petersen replying to Fred Kiesche #

Argues that Wolfe's divine Outsider and Lovecraft's Outsider are both outcast, subterranean, subversive monsters, and that Wolfe deliberately recasts the Christian deity as a monster in a Chestertonian upside-down way. Responds to Fred Kiesche's Lovecraft post, offering the connection as a valid if obscure link that supports Wolfe's theological aims and the 'mooreeffoc' reversal.
proposes · theology-and-religion · allegory-and-typology · theories: Wolfe's Outsider is a monstrous outcast God parallel to Lovecraft's Outsider

I'm glad you brought this up. I've always thought the most interesting thing about Wolfe's divine Outsider as compared to Lovecraft's Outsider is that both are monsters of sorts: non-canonical, subterranean, tenebrous, subversive. It's part of the devious theological genius of Wolfe that he casts his 'true God' as an outcast, a creature of the night as it were, a monster. I think part of Wolfe's theological impulse is to see the Christian deity anew in a Chestertonian 'upside down' sort of way and this very obscure (but valid, I think) connection to Lovecraft's Outsider is one of the ways he achieves that *'mooreeffoc'
<http://dictionary.reference.com/browse/mooreeffoc>* epiphany.

-DOJP
