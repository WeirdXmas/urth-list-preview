The thread: Lee Berman opened the thread by reporting Robert Borski's proposal that Father Inire, described as monkey-like, shapeshifts into a cynocephalus, a uakari, and other monkeys to watch Severian. Hammstu and Berman then argued the ape-like figures and Inire are Inhumi, linking Quetzal to Quetzalcoatl and to Roy Lackey's phylogeny idea. John Watkins countered that Wolfe reportedly ruled out the Cumaean and Merryn as inhumi but not Inire. James Wynn posted both for and against the Quetzal/Inire analogy, and against the inhumi identification on grounds of reproduction, Blue versus Urth, and Quetzal's politics. The thread ends unresolved, with the Inhumi reading contested and no consensus reached.
  • Lee Berman: Inire and ape-like figures may be Inhumi, supported by Quetzal and Shadow Children parallels
  • hammstu: ape-like figures on the ship, Inire especially, are Inhumi who reached the Whorl early
  • John Watkins: Wolfe ruled out Cumaean and Merryn as inhumi but not Inire
  • James Wynn: Quetzal is analogous to Inire without being him; Inire's absence is suspicious
  • James Wynn: inhumi and Neighbors are not Inire; they are absorbed in reproduction and passing as human, and Blue is not Urth
  • Robert Borski: Inire shapeshifts into cynocephalus and other monkeys to watch Severian

Themes: Beasts and the animal in us, Masks, disguise and shapeshifting, The dying sun and the cosmos

2010-08-09 Lee Berman #

Reports on a lively discussion, begun by Robert Borski, proposing that Father Inire, described as monkey-like, is a shapeshifter who takes the form of a cynocephalus, a uakari and possibly other monkeys to watch Severian. Notes that other monkey-like characters have been discussed in relation to Inire and that some list members reject the idea.
informs · close-textual-reading · theories: Father Inire is a shapeshifter who appears as monkeys

There has been some rather lively discussion, originating with Robert Borski (Solar Labyrinth) and elaborated in here, that Father Inire, called monkey- like in his first description, may be a shapeshifter who appears as the cynocephalus (a.k.a. baboon), a uakari and perhaps some other monkeys too. Just to keep tabs on Severian.

There has been discussion of some other monkey-like characters and their possible relationship to the elusive Father Inire. Some do not like the idea.

2010-08-09 Lee Berman replying to Lee Berman #

Supports the suggestion that the ape-like Inire are Inhumi, linking Quetzal to the Mayan Quezalcoatl and to pagan gods of knowledge and civilization. Builds on Roy C. Lackey's idea that Tzadkiel's growth recapitulates Hierogrammate phylogeny, and cites the shape-shifting Shadow Children in The Fifth Head of Cerberus as parallel evidence.
supports · source-and-allusion · myth-and-structure · theories: Inire are Inhumi; Tzadkiel's growth recapitulates Hierogrammate evolution; Inhumi descend from Green's shape-shifting primitives

I think it is a good idea. Quetzal bears some resemblance to Inire. Moreover using the bird-name Quetzal can't help but invoke the Mayan god Quezalcoatl. For me, this is yet another of the knowledge-and-civilization bringing sort of pagan gods which I think are alluded to in Urth's story. Moreover there is significant vampiric allusion to be found in the BotNS story.

In the recent, astute post of Roy C. Lackey, he suggests that the ontological evolution of Tzadkiel from shaggy blob to giant angelic being might (well, he doesn't say "might") recapitulate the phylogenic evolution of the Hierogrammate race. If so, we can guess that even the shaggy blob which has only the potential for intelligence does possess the capacity for shape-shifting. It does bring to mind the primitive, shape shifting beings on Green from which the Inhumi originated. (likewise the primitive origins of shape-shifting Shadow Children in Fifth Head of Cerberus).

2010-08-09 John Watkins replying to hammstu #

Reports that Wolfe himself reportedly ruled out the Cumaean and Merryn as inhumi, while noting he did not rule out Inire being one. Grounds this in a quoted Wolfe interview answer found online and a half-remembered comment that inhumi were absent from Urth during the New Sun books.
informs · author-and-biography · source-and-allusion · theories: Inire is an inhuma and arrived on the Whorl before orbit

I thought it had been suggested and officially debunked by Wolfe himself...but a quick google got me only this:

Are the Cumaean and/or Merryn inhumi? Possibly, or definitely? No. The Cumaean is an alien but not an inhuma. Merryn is a human being. http://www.sfsite.com/03b/gw124.htm

So the Cumaean, at least, is not an inhuma but Wolfe didn't rule out Inire being one in that quote. I still seem to recall seeing somewhere a more general comment by Wolfe that the inhumi were not present on Urth during Book of the New Sun, but I can't find it.

2010-08-09 James Wynn replying to John Watkins #

Argues that Quetzal/Silk is closely analogous to Fr Inire/Severian without being Inire, and that Inire's absence from the narrative is suspicious. Supports this by citing Wolfe's remark that the Cumaean is not an inhuma, noting Merryn's humanity, and drawing a Midas parallel for the neighbours' role in the Whorl plan sale.
proposes · puzzle-solving · source-and-allusion · theories: Quetzal/Silk is analogous to but not Fr Inire; Inire's absence is deliberate and suspicious

Was anyone else stunned that Merryn was human?

I believe that what he said was that Quetzal was "an recent arrival" which does not rule out the possibility that other inhumi were on Urth. Actually, I find it appealing that the neighbors had a hand in the sale of the Whorl plan to Typhon because I infer deliberate parallels in the story to the tale of how Midas got his golden touch from Dionysus. http://www.urth.org/whorlmap/midas.htm

However, while it can be said that Quetzal/Silk is highly analogous to Fr Inire/Severian, I'm not convinced that he IS Fr Inire. Actually, I suspect there is _a lot_ more overlap than just Mentor/Student, but I can't prove it because I don't believe we know anything important about Inire.

Inire is one of those characters that "shows up" so-to-speak in Wolfe stories--an important character that either makes no actual appearance or only brief cameo appearances. Or an important character that suddenly disappears from the active narrative although he is supposedly quite active in it. His absence is disturbingly fishy. You look for him everywhere because he _needs_ to be there.

2010-08-09 James Wynn replying to James Wynn #

Argues that Father Inire of The Book of the New Sun is not an inhume or neighbor, since the inhumi and Neighbors seem absorbed in reproduction and passing as human and in Blue rather than Urth. Cites Quetzal's Whorl politics on Green and Wolfe's ironic reply that Urth is Green as grounds for rejecting the identification of Blue with Ushas.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Father Inire is not an inhume or neighbor; Blue is not Ushas

What I meant to say was that I am not convinced that Fr Inire is an inhume or neighbor.

One reason is that the inhumi seem totally consumed with reproduction and passing as human. Quetzal, for example gets into Whorl politics on because he wants to establish a human colony on Green. That they would partake in some grand scheme for rejuvenating Urth seems beyond their ken.

Same with the Neighbors. Their interest (whatever it is) seems totally directed with Blue, not Urth.

And, no, I don't think Blue is Ushas either. When the subject was brought up, Wolfe --being ironic-- said "No! no! Urth is Green!". We can debate about what he meant by that, but he surely did not mean that Blue was in fact Ushas.
