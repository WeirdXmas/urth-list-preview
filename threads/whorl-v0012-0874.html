2001-06-21 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Proposes that the word "Duko" comes from Esperanto rather than Italian, fitting a pattern of base and foreign languages on the Whorl, and suggests other Trivigantes terms are Esperanto too. Explains that he named Sirius Fiction in 1994 for the Lexicon and a magazine, and that the domain names followed an ISP choice made as a joke.
answers · source-and-allusion · theories: Duko derives from Esperanto rather than Italian; Whorl uses base language plus foreign language for specialized terms

It was my impression that the word is from Esperanto rather than Italian. Fitting the pattern that seems to exist in some places on the Whorl (and Earth as well), where there is a base language supplemented by a second "foreign" language for certain terms (military ranks, city names, religious terms, science terms). (I thought the Trivigantes had a blend that was outside historical Islamic boundaries, but maybe not--since a blend of Arabic, Persian, and Indian terms might point directly to, say, Pakistan, or Sultanate of Deli. That is, the bounds of Islam are large indeed. Still--rani, or "ranee," is a Hindu queen; etc.) I thought that one or more other terms used by that group were also perfect Esperanto, but I could be mistaken.

I can't speak to all of them. I named Sirius Fiction in 1994, a publisher for the Lexicon and a magazine. There was a rash of Sirius namings around that time, and one of them was an ISP. I thought it would be funny to go with them, and years later, I did just that. Service went downhill and I left for another ISP, finally getting around to setting up siriusfiction.com, etc.

Asks whether the words from Grandecitta, the city in The Book of the New Sun, are mostly Esperanto, and reports that a dictionary lists "strego/a" as non-Esperanto Italian. Responds to Michael Andre-Driussi's point about the Esperanto origin of "duko" and the k spelling, with the poster mocking the mixed Esperanto-Italian language as strange.
