Themes: Names and their meanings

2000-03-08 Jeremy Crampton #

Asks whether Wolfe's work was influenced by Philip K. Dick, proposing a parallel between a door-bribery scene in The Urth of the New Sun and a doorknob-and-nickel scene in Ubik. Invites others to name further Dick echoes, noting the Ubik scene is well known and that the parallel has not been discussed before on the list.
questions · source-and-allusion

Hello,

I have a quick question: has anybody ever thought about possible influences on Wolfe's work by Philip K. Dick? I'm thinking of the scene for example in URTH where Severian speculates that the door may have been bribed, and the scene in UBIK where Joe Chip doesn't have a nickel to give the doorknob. It's quite a famous PKD scene so it might not be as obscure as it sounds.

I don't recall there being discussion about this before. Can anybody think of any others?

2000-03-08 Greene, Carlton #

Argues that Wolfe's use of translated proper names parallels Philip K. Dick's, offering Horselover Fat in Dick's work as a counterpart to Seven Lions in Soldier of the Mist. Responds to Jeremy's question about Dick's influence on Wolfe, citing the names themselves as the evidence for the parallel rather than the door scenes Jeremy raised.
supports · source-and-allusion

One quick and easy parallel is the use of proper names translated to reflect the original meanings of the words. Compare PKD's Horselover Fat with Seven Lions in Soldier.
