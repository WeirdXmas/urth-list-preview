Which order?

2003-04-27 Nigel Price #

Advises a newcomer to read the Long Sun books before the Short Sun books, arguing the two series are intimately related and that the Short Sun reader will want Long Sun anyway. Responds to Oren's question about reading order by likening the wrong order to reading The Urth of the New Sun before The Book of the New Sun.
answers

Oren asked...

It's up to you, but I really think that you'll get much more out of it if you read the books in sequence and read Long Sun before Short Sun. They are intimately related. To do it the other way round would be like reading _Urth of the New Sun_ before _The Book of the New Sun_. Once you start reading Short Sun, you'll want to go off and read Long Sun anyway, so why not take them in order in the first place?

Or let me put it another way. Long Sun is brilliant, so why miss out on a treat?

Nigel

2003-04-27 Gareth Jelley #

Argues that On Blue's Waters stands as a complete, excellent novel in its own right, so a reader can start with the Short Sun books without first reading the Long Sun. Responds to Nigel Price's remark that Short Sun readers will want to go back to the Long Sun, saying a reader's first encounter with the book felt complete and unbroken.
supports · genre-and-craft

From: "Nigel Price" <[email]>

This is true, and I do indeed want to read the Long Sun now I've started on the Short Sun; but the other point I'd make is that 'On Blue's Waters' is a beautiful novel in its own right, and reading it without any other knowledge of 'Urth' wasn't detrimental (for me, at least). The quality of OBW as a really marvellous work of literature, in its own right, shone through, and it never felt incomplete, as a novel. If that makes sense... And this, I think, is what was at the heart of my devil's advocacy :o)

Best,

G.
