Hierodules & time

Urth list · 9 messages · 2006-05-25 to 2006-05-25

2006urthThe Book of the New SunthalassocratMacronautDon Doggettb sharp
The thread: Don Doggett argued across several posts that the hierodules age forward yet travel backward through time by machine, arriving from the future to serve as guides for Severian's early reign, and disputed the claim that Wolfe dropped time travel in the Long and Short Sun books. Thalassocrat pressed objections: backward-moving hierodules could not converse coherently with Severian, their out-of-sequence meetings clash with a simple backward-travel explanation, and he asked Doggett to clarify whether his account matched thalassocrat's own. Macronaut and b sharp debated a chess remark, with Macronaut correcting the attribution to Vodalus and b sharp reading the chess imagery as casting hierodules as pawns on opposing teams. The thread ended without resolution, with Doggett's and thalassocrat's positions still apart.
  • don doggett: hierodules age forward but travel backward through time by device, sent from the future as guides for Severian
  • thalassocrat: backward-moving hierodules cannot coherently converse with Severian; time travel is an annoying device Wolfe dropped
  • Macronaut: the chess remark about playing black belongs to Vodalus, not Typhon
  • b sharp: hierodules are pawns moving opposite to Severian's time direction, and readers should pick a side

Themes: Time loops and causality, Gods, angels and the Increate, The dying sun and the cosmos

2006-05-25 Don Doggett replying to thalassocrat #

Argues that hierodules move backward through time along the corridors of time while aging forward like everyone else, so they come from the future to the past. Responds to thalassocrat's complaint that hierodules speaking to Severian is absurd if each instant runs backward, and disputes the claim that Wolfe dropped time travel in the Long and Short Sun books.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Hierodules age forward while traveling backward through time; Hierodules come from the future to the past; The New Sun and Long and Short Sun form one twelve-volume novel

There's a much simpler explanation. They age forward
(their personal timeline goes in the same direction as
anyone else's) but they are traveling backward via the
corridors of time. They are from the future moving
down the timeline to the past. And GW certainly didn't
drop time travel in the LS SS series, imo, but I'm of
the opinion that NS UotNS LS and SS are a single 12
volume novel rather than separate series set in the
same universe.

Don

2006-05-25 Don Doggett replying to don doggett #

Argues that the hierodules Famulimus and Barbatus age forward like everyone else but travel backward through time by machine, since their short lifespans make a device the way to spend time with Severian. Clarifies an earlier post by saying their backward travel is an assignment, so that Severian has knowledgeable guides during his early reign.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Hierodules age forward while traveling backward by machine

To further clarify this (how tacky on my part)
Famulimus, Barbatus only live for about twenty years,
so traveling by a machine is the way to maximize their
time with Sev. The reason they are travelling backward
is so they can be knowledgeable guides when Sev needs
it most, before he becomes Autarch and during his
early reign. There's really no need for them
(storywise) to age backward and I don't think that's
what's happening.

2006-05-25 b sharp replying to thalassocrat #

Argues that Tzadkiel's and Typhon's chess remarks in The Book of the New Sun cast hierodules as pawns moving in the opposite time direction from Severian, and asks which side readers should root for. Builds on Tzadkiel's pawn speech and Typhon's claim that Severian plays black, reasoning from the chess imagery itself to the opposing teams.
proposes · close-textual-reading · myth-and-structure · theories: Hierodules are pawns moving opposite to Severian in time; Severian plays black against a white team

Both Tzadkiel and Typhon (I think it is him) make chessboard references to the BotNS drama. Tzadkiel tells Severian that his is a race of pawns, who can only move in one direction in time, unless they reach the end (Yesod) and are brought back to the beginning to play as a new piece. Aren't the hierodules also pawns who can only move in one direction in time?

But they move in the opposite direction of Severian. Typhon says Severian plays for the black team, so his opposition must be the white team. Each side has players which are not pawns. Which side are we supposed to root for??????????

-bsharp

2006-05-25 thalassocrat #

Questions whether hierodules, who are said to travel backward through time, could converse with Severian, and calls the backward-time device an annoying plot device that Wolfe dropped in the Long and Short Sun books. Grounds the objection in the logic that backward-moving sentences would have to be spoken before the comments they answer, so the hierodules would have to be lying about their direction of travel.
questions · chronology-and-plot-logic

It is hierodules who are supposed to travel backwards through time,

right? I always get the various hiero-levels mixed up.

Whatever, it's a really annoying plot device & I'm glad that Wolfe dropped it in the (far, far better, IMO) Long/Short Sun books.

If they actually do move backwards in time then surely this must be

true of each instant. In which case how do they converse with Sev? It's fairly absurd to imagine them constructing sentences and then delivering them backwards. But it's even more absurd to imagine them delivering those backwards-sentences in response to, but before, comments made by Sev.

Unless they are lying for some reason about progressing backwards- thru-time. Which would be equally annoying.

Bah! Harumph.

2006-05-25 thalassocrat replying to thalassocrat #

Questions why the hierodules would travel backward through time on their mission, arguing that their repeated meetings with Severian out of sequence make a simple backward-travel explanation inconsistent. Cites the timeline of their encounters with Severian and Baldanders, and notes a suggestion from the archives that Ossipago reverses their time-flow when dealing with ordinary people.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Ossipago temporarily reverses the hierodules' time-flow

On Thu, 25 May 2006 17:34:05 +1000 don doggett

Yes, I certainly agree that's the best explanation, but I'm not sure it fits 100%. I *think* they say at some point that backwards- thru-time is indeed their nature - on the Ship? Need to rummage around to see if I can find the passage.

But mainly - why? It seems a very inconvenient way to plan a mission. I mean, I can see the point of them going back in time for (say) a first meeting with Baldanders, after which they move forward like everybody else. But that isn't what seems to happen: they've had interactions with Sev on Urth before (on their timeline) his first meeting with them (on his), which is also their last interaction with Baldanders (on *his* timeline) in his castle. So it would have to be something more complicated. Which would be, once again, annoying :)

A quick flip thru the archives shows somebody suggesting that Ossipago might temporarily reverse their time-flow for interactions with "normal" people. I guess that'd patch my conceptual hole here, if there were some evidence for it. But I still wouldn't like it.
