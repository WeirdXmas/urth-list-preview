Chung Kuo

The thread: Antonin Scriabin asked list members whether they had read David Wingrove's Chung Kuo series and in what order to read the 20-volume re-release that adds material to the original eight volumes. Scriabin, who began The Middle Kingdom, found it worth reading, balancing large science-fiction ideas with the personal stories of many characters. Entonio reported that the Wikipedia entry says the re-release keeps the original reading order while adding material and the author's intended ending, and asked whether the series offers ideas beyond action. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes mildly praised the first volume but never went on, while David Duffy, having read all eight volumes, gave a negative verdict citing padded plotting, implausibly limited technology, nasty scenes and a worry about stereotyping Chinese culture. The thread ends without resolution, split between Scriabin's qualified enthusiasm and Duff
  • Antonin Scriabin: early volumes are worth reading, balancing big SF ideas with many personal stories
  • David Duffy: read all eight volumes and found them padded, technologically implausible, nasty, and stereotyping Chinese culture
  • entonio: suggests the re-release keeps the original order and adds intended ending; asks if there are ideas beyond action
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: liked the first volume but never continued

2014-01-02 David Duffy replying to Antonin Scriabin #

Recounts why the poster was disappointed by David Wingrove's Chung Kuo series after reading all eight volumes, citing padded plot, implausibly limited technology and nasty scenes. Responds to Antonin Scriabin's question about reading order and the 20-volume re-release by offering a negative verdict and a worry about stereotyping Chinese culture.
answers

I did read all eight as they appeared at our local library, but I became a bit disappointed as things progressed, and was glad it finished. So maybe liking the first three volumes wouldn't be enough ;).

I didn't think think the plot justified the length, that the technologies needed seemed unnaturally constricted in scope (if one can do X, then Y would be just as easy - if it was fantasy then I wouldn't have this problem), and a few rather nasty scenes. The "meta" worry I had, which might seem weird to some, was whether Chinese culture was being adequately represented - I dislike obvious retellings of historical events (Napoleonic wars anyone?), and of stereotyping of non-European societies. There are a few set pieces from the series that stick in my memory though.

Just 2c,

David Duffy.
