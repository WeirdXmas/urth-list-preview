Is Free Live Free worth it?

Urth list · 4 messages · 2001-01-30 to 2001-01-31

2001urthPeter WestlakeRedhaven1James JordanMichael Straight
The thread: A newcomer, Redhaven1, asked whether Free Live Free is worth finishing, finding its early pages darker and grittier than Wolfe's other work and not enjoying it. James Jordan offered a one-line suggestion to keep The Wizard of Oz in mind, with no critical argument about the novel's merits. Michael Straight argued it is Wolfe's funniest book, a madcap adventure whose puzzle fits together at the end, ranking it best of his modern novels. Peter Westlake disagreed that the book is dark or gritty, citing its single profanity and urging attention to the Oz parallel and odd details about weather and music. The thread ended without a reported resolution, with the newcomer's doubts answered by encouragement to keep reading.

2001-01-30 Redhaven1 #

Asks whether Free Live Free is worth finishing, saying the early pages feel darker and grittier than Wolfe's other work and that he is not enjoying it. Seeks the list's advice on whether the book improves or is Wolfe's one dud.
questions

I'm just starting Free Live Free and it seems darker and grittier than anything by Wolfe I've read yet. Actually, I'm not really enjoying it at all. I would just give Wolfe the benefit of the doubt (he's never let me down yet) and read it trusting it will be better, but I'm coming to this list for advice. Maybe this book was his one dud. What did you guys think of it? Is Free Live Free worth it? (i.e. Does it get better?)

-Steve

2001-01-31 Michael Straight #

Argues that Free Live Free is Wolfe's funniest book, a madcap adventure with a puzzle that fits everything together at the end, and ranks it the best of his modern novels. Responds to a newcomer who found the book dark and gritty by recommending it and comparing it with Castleview, There Are Doors and Pandora.
supports · genre-and-craft

I think Free Live Free is easily Wolfe's funniest book. In fact the phrase "madcap adventures" comes to my fingertips. It's also got a humdinger of a puzzle to fit everything together at the end (I hope you've got the edition with the timeline in the back--BUT DON'T PEEK AT IT!)

I think Free Live Free is the best of Wolfe's "modern" novels, (Castleview, There Are Doors, Pandora).

-Rostrum

2001-01-31 Peter Westlake #

Argues that Free Live Free is not dark or gritty and urges a newcomer who finds it grim to keep reading. Responds to a poster who is struggling with the book, citing the book's single profanity as evidence of its lightness and advising attention to the Wizard of Oz parallel and to odd details about weather and music.
supports · genre-and-craft

It does. It isn't dark at all, and enthusiasts of grittiness wave it aside. You have probably passed the single instance of profanity in the entire book, for instance. Stick with it, is my advice. And as well as thinking about the Wizard of Oz, keep an eye open for anomalies. Odd things about the weather, music, anything at all.

Spectacled Bear.
