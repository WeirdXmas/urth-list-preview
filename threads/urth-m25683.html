The thread: Austin Hackett asked which of two editions of Free Live Free is better, the 1984 Ziesing text or the 1986 Tor text, and whether Tor was a publisher cut or the author's final revision. Antonin Scriabin urged the longer 1984 edition, arguing its extra roughly 4000 words are worth having. Marc Aramini catalogued textual differences, including changed chapter headings, a removed chapter altering Stubb, and Barnes' age changed from 41 to 34, and named the Tor version the preferred final text. Hackett leaned toward Tor as the author's final intention, and the thread ended with Aramini pointing him to the spoiler-laden list of revisions.

2014-05-09 Austin Hackett #

Asks which of two editions of Free Live Free is preferable, the 1984 Ziesing small press text or the 1986 Tor mass market text, and whether the Tor version is a publisher-truncated cut or the author's final revision. Notes the reported roughly 4000-word difference, the chronology added to Tor, a changed first chapter title, and possibly reordered parts, and asks whether anyone knows the backstory.
questions · author-and-biography

Hi, new member here. I joined up to find out which version of *Free Live Free* is preferable to read?

I haven't read *Free Live Free* but plan on doing so soon. It just so happens I have both the 1984 Mark V. Ziesing small press edition and the standard 1986 Tor mass market version on my shelf. Several places online say the Tor is shorter by about 4000 words. I also notice that it contains a chronology (which, it says, was requested by the publisher) and the title of the first chapter is different. The order of some parts may also be different between the two versions according to vague reports.

Does the Tor edition represent an editorially truncated text or the author's final revisions? Some might think "longer = better" but I don't want to assume anything. Maybe Wolfe shortened the book at the behest of the publisher, but he may have just revised it for his own artistic purposes. Even if the cuts were mandated, he may have taken the opportunity to make different changes as well. And none of these factors actually determine which version is *better*.

So has anyone read both, or at least knows a little about the backstory? I can't find any info online.

Thanks!

2014-05-10 Antonin Scriabin replying to Austin Hackett #

Recommends the 1984 Mark V. Ziesing edition of Free Live Free over the shorter 1986 Tor version, arguing that the extra roughly 4000 words are worth having. Suggests reading the longer text alongside the chronology included with it, and says the poster has just finished it and found it one of Wolfe's favourite standalone works.
answers

I just finished the 1984 version, and can't imagine doing without 4000 words. I haven't read the Tor version. I would say have the best of both worlds; read the longer version with the benefit of the chronology when you need it. It was fantastic by the way, one of my favorite of his stand alones.

Reports textual differences between the 1984 and Tor versions of Free Live Free, noting changed chapter headings and a character introduced differently, plus wording changes such as might to would. Words like might are changed to would, etc. I may read them side by side when I get to 1984 in my review of Wolfe's fiction... I don't plan on doing new sun unless the spirit strikes me and I have something new to say.

2014-05-19 Marc Aramini replying to Austin Hackett #

Reports that the Tor edition of Free Live Free is the preferred final text and lists some differences from the earlier version, such as a shortened scene, a removed chapter altering Stubb's character, Barnes' age changed from 41 to 34, and a hedged answer about communing with demons. Replies to Austin Hackett, who is reading the novel, and promises a spoiler-laden comparison post later.
informs · close-textual-reading

Yes I think the tor version is the one to go with as the final draft. There are a few scenes which are shorter in that version and one chapter taken out that changes stubb's character a little bit. I have jotted down most of the significant differences and when you are done with the novel you can read my upcoming post, maybe I will finish it by the end of this week, but it will have spoilers so wait until you are done with the novel.

A few of the interesting but minor changes: Barnes' age goes from 41 to 34. A character asked about communing with demons gives an unqualified yes in the ziesing text but in the tor version hedges them as having "other, and better, names."
