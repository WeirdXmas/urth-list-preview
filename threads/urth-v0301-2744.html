2003-01-21 Nicholas Gevers #

Reports receiving an uncorrected proof of The Knight, noting its length of about 200,000 words, its dedication to Yves Meynard, the Dunsany poem "The Riders" at the front, and a character and place-name list compiled by the narrator. Gives these physical and publishing details without analysing the story, so it is a news report for the list.
informs

I've just received an uncorrected proof of THE KNIGHT,
scheduled for January 2004 release. Comes in at 430
pages of fairly small print, so I'd estimate it runs
to about 200,000 words. It's dedicated to a past
member of this list, Yves Meynard, "with the greatest
respect", and a poem by Lord Dunsany, "The Riders",
appears at the front. There is a list of character and
place names at the beginning, as in the Whorl novels,
but in this case the narrator has compiled it...

--Nick Gevers.

2003-01-22 Kevin J. Maroney #

Expresses enthusiasm for the character list at the front of The Knight, noting that the narrator compiled it for a reader who is addressed as a brother. Quotes the list's prefatory note that it will make sense only after reading the rest, and admits the poster has not yet read the book.
informs · close-textual-reading

I haven't read it yet--it's next on the pile--but I love the character list. The entire novel is addressed to the narrator's brother (at least, I think it's the brother), and the "List of Characters" is prefaced with the note "This list won't make sense to you until you've read the rest of this, but you can refer back to it to keep track of people."
