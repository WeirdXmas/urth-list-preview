1997-10-02 Tony Zbaraschuk #

Argues that Silk's task to save the manteion in The Book of the Long Sun may be a smaller version of a larger spiritual mission, comparing it to St. Francis of Assisi's early vision to rebuild the church. Grounds the parallel in Francis's literal rebuilding of a decayed church before he understood God wanted spiritual reform, citing Chesterton's biography.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · source-and-allusion · theories: Silk's manteion task foreshadows a larger spiritual mission

Something that I didn't see _at all_ in the archives when I went through them (I just got onto the list):

St. Francis of Assisi, early in his career, had a vision telling him to "rebuild my church", and spent a while, first trying to beg stones from passer-by, and then actually working himself on rebuilding an old and decayed church building, before he figured out that what God wanted was a _spiritual_ reform, which become the Franciscan Order. (There's a fair amount about this in G. K. Chesterton's biography of Francis.)

The thought crossed my mind shortly after Silk started talking about how the Outsider wanted him to "save the manteion": "Boy, Silk, are YOU in for a surprise; the Outsider wants something much, much larger."
