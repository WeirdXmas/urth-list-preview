2013-10-11 Marc Aramini replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that the Long Sun and Short Sun books must involve time travel, citing the rajan and Oreb appearing repeatedly in earlier stories and the narrators' disorientation in Short Sun. Responds to Andreas Johansson's chronology point about Typhon's reign and the age of the Commonwealth by insisting that the series' time-bending logic makes such timeline arguments fit.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: Time travel occurs in the Long Sun and Short Sun books

Of course there is time travel or the rajan and Oreb would not show up in the past stories so frequently as themselves so obviously. Before they go to Urth the first time the narrator imagines Green before the corruption hit. It kind of befuddles me how a series with time travel all over the place and the bending of light, time, and space in its earliest entries suddenly wouldn't involve it in its final iterations.

The narrators don't know what's going on in Short Sun- they don't know who they are or where they are. Looking at the sky they are lost.

2013-10-11 Lee Berman #

Proposes that time dilation explains the gap between Typhon's reign and Severian's age, and that the masked Neighbors are evolved humans returning to their origin world Urth. Responds to Andreas Johansson's timeline argument, citing Hoof's remark in The Return to the Whorl about an artifact older than 300 years, and linking Echidna to Typhon.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · source-and-allusion · theories: Time dilation explains the Whorl's age gap; Neighbors are evolved humans returning to Urth; Typhon originated on Blue and sent worshippers back; Echidna is the Mother of All Monsters

.Andreas Johansson: Was going to ask also about the chronological work-out between

Yes, among those who scour the text for timeline clues, many arrive at a figure of around 1000 years of age for the Commonwealth, so Typhon's reign was at least that much earlier than Severian's time.

Moreover, at the end of RTTW, Hoof comments that some Typhonian artifact on Urth (the Red Sun Whorl) seems older than 300 years, and SilkHorn confirms that it is, indeed much older. Time dilation seems a likely explanation to me.

In BotNS it is implied that the human race expanded out from Urth and changed/evolved. So some, (perhaps all) of the alien "cacogens" are really evolved humans who have returned to their planet of origin. Thus B, F and O start with ill-fitting human masks which reveal horrific faces underneath. But when the horrific masks are removed, faces of superhuman beauty are revealed.

So, perhaps The Neighbors had a human origin?

Some wonder whether Typhon had his origin on Blue and was using the Whorl to send human worshippers (tribute of fair women and boys?) back to his home planet. Perhaps also back to the "Mother"?.

(The "Mother" representing Echidna in some fashion, the mythological Echidna being the "Mother Of All Monsters" while her mate Typhon was the "Father Of All Monsters". We do see electronic Echidna having a zest for human sacrifice in Long Sun and The Mother on Blue enjoying drowned sailors.)
