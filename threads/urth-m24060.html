For Your Amusement: On Twitter Today...

Urth list · 14 messages · 2013-04-30 to 2013-05-01

2013urthThe Book of the New SunAllan AndersonGwern BranwenAntónio Pedro MarquesDavid StockhoffMarc AraminiFred Kiescheaaron
The thread: Fred Kiesche relayed playful tweets from Felix Gilman and Max Gladstone posing questions about Severian, Apu-Punchau, the Short Sun and the ribbons on Agilus' mask, without offering analysis. Gwern Branwen and Marc Aramini joked about the Urth setting and political comparisons. Aramini then disputed Bob Borski's readings as inconsistent and too tenuous to be grounded in the text, and aaron agreed while praising Borski's work for spurring rereading. Aramini asked whether any critic had discussed the colour names in Peace, and aaron answered that Borski linked them to alchemy. The thread ended with a loose exchange rather than a resolved reading.
  • Fred Kiesche: relays Gilman's tweets as amusement, not argument
  • Marc Aramini: Borski's readings are tenuous and not grounded in the text
  • aaron: agrees Borski is tenuous but finds his work spurs rereading
  • Gwern Branwen: plays along with comic political riffs on the Urth setting
  • Aramini: colour symbolism in Peace may be too constant to read symbolically

Themes: Colonization and the native, Names and their meanings, Light, darkness and colour

2013-04-30 Marc Aramini replying to aaron #

Argues that Bob Borski's interpretations are inconsistent, imaginative but often too tenuous and not grounded in the text, and says the poster only pursues an idea once the text legitimately hints at it. Responds to Aaron Singleton's remark that Borski's explanation of the ribbons above Agilus' ears does not satisfy him, offering a general stance on Borski's method rather than a reading of the ribbons.
disputes · close-textual-reading

2013-05-01 Marc Aramini replying to aaron #

Asks whether a critic discussed the many colour associations attached to character names in Peace, such as peacock blue, olive, black and porter brown. Responds to aaron's remark about Borski's observations on The Fifth Head of Cerberus and Peace, adding that the constant colour symbolism could make it hard to read symbolically.
questions · close-textual-reading

Definitely, his work on Fifth Head is top notch. I am trying to remember if he did much with all the crazy colors associated with names in Peace ( blue ribbon around a little coffin, etc) since the characters seemed to be overwhelmingly associated with colors- peacock blue, olive, black, porter is specifically a darker brown beer (which color is bold? Yellow?)- but the ubiquity of color might make it a symbolist's nightmare in the small stories.

Thank you, by the way, for the congratulations. If not worthy, I hope at least that you will enjoy it a bit. Sent from my iPhone
