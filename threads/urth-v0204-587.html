2002-04-11 Andy Robertson #

Argues that "happy" is too childish a word for adult emotional life, saying the poster is happiest with children but moved most deeply by memories of a dead wife. Responds to Andy Robertson's quoted remarks about Wolfe's sadness and an essay in Castle of Days, adding that Wolfe's work has no happy endings.
disputes · author-and-biography

"Happy" is a rather childish descriptor. I don't mean that as a put-down: only that it may be often rightly used to describe children, but rarely rightly used to describe adults.

I am "happiest" when with my children. But I am most deeply moved by memories of my wife, now dead. I would not all my state "happy", but it includes deep joy.

In Wolfe's work there are no happy endings. The complex alloy of adult human emotional life is too strong and too various simply to be called "happy".

hartshorn
