Urth list · 12 messages · 2003-07-25 to 2003-07-28

The thread: The thread began with Lisa Schaffer-Doggett's rant that workshop critics of Gene Wolfe's manuscripts were juvenile and that Wolfe was mistreated when he left the Odyssey workshop. Josh Geller then proposed paid or free week-long gatherings with Wolfe, with Andrew Bollen willing to join a class, and silk tree asked for a Chicago-area WindyCon appearance, which Geller refused unless held in his own hometown. Chris urged understanding for Wolfe and proposed discussing the stories he praised, while Schaffer-Doggett and Bollen voiced doubts about workshops and cost comparisons were offered by matthew.malthouse. The thread ended without any agreed plan, drifting between workshop debate and logistics for a Wolfe event.
  • Lisa Schaffer-Doggett: workshop critics were juvenile and Wolfe was badly treated, and writing workshops are dubious
  • Josh Geller: proposed organizing a paid or free gathering with Wolfe and would only organize it in his own hometown
  • silk tree: asked that Wolfe appear at a Chicago-area event arranged through WindyCon
  • Chris: urged understanding toward Wolfe and proposed discussing the stories he praised in the workshop
  • Andrew Bollen: friend's blunt workshop criticism led to publication; willing to join an online Wolfe class
  • matthew.malthouse: offered Arvon Foundation course cost as a cheaper model for a Wolfe weekend

Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Fathers, mothers and children

2003-07-25 Lisa Schaffer-Doggett #

Rants that the workshop participants who criticized Gene Wolfe's manuscripts are juvenile and clueless, and that Wolfe was badly treated when he left the Odyssey workshop. Responds to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's quoted news that Wolfe left the workshop early, citing Locus's published explanation, and adds a general dismissal of writing workshops.
disputes

That is utterly unbelievable. What a pathetic lot of juvenile, ungrateful, UNKNOWING, clueless idiots. I don't even know where to start. How about why bother to take a writing workshop if you can't take honest criticism? It pains me, pains me deeply to read that Mr. Wolfe was treated that way. He is a writer of the first caliber, a truly unknown legend. Those hacks should be grateful he even bothered to read their most likely unreadable tripe. Personally, I don't have much use for writing workshops. I think writing is far too individual an art and the workshops are full of whiny idiots like these. I'll end this now with my paraphrase of Bukowski- Where is something interesting to read? Wait. Don't tell me. I know. You wrote it.

Sadly true
Don

2003-07-26 Andrew Bollen #

Recounts a friend's success after a writing workshop, where blunt criticism led to a rewrite and publication, and argues that handling criticism is a skill gained from experience. Offers this as a contrast to workshop members who struggled to take criticism, with no reference to Wolfe's work.
chats

I took a friend of mine to lunch yesterday to celebrate her first book being accepted for publication by Random. She found the workshop thing very valuable. Before the workshop, she had a draft manuscript and scattered comments on it from all over the place, but nothing very informed. The main value of the workshop was *useful reading*, to use her phrase. She was told the manuscript was totally unpublishable, for this reason and that, and that she would have to rewrite it. So she spent a couple of years rewriting from scratch, taking note of the specific criticisms, and ended up published.

She believes her work experience as a speech-writer, policy analyst etc for various senior politicians and bureaucrats was very helpful in being able to use criticism effectively: she'd spent years being told, brusquely, that pieces were no good & to rewrite them. But others in the workshop found it much harder to handle.

Being able to use criticism is one of those things you get from experience, if you're lucky. Sounds like these people have a way to go.
