Themes: Unreliable narration

1998-04-17 adam louis stephanides #

Points out that two Wolfe short stories, "Bluesberry Jam" and "Ain't You Most Done?", depict the same events from different characters' perspectives and give quite different pictures of what is really happening. Offers this as a lesser example of the related-stories effect praised in Ansley's post, noting the second appeared in a Sandman prose anthology.

A recent example of this (though on a lesser aesthetic plane than Fifth Head) is two short stories, "Bluesberry Jam" and "Ain't You Most Done?" The first of these appeared in an anthology whose title I forget, whose theme was music in science fiction IIRC; the second appeared in an anth- ology of Sandman (the Neil Gaiman comic character) prose stories, entitled "The Sandman: Book of Dreams" or something like that. On Thu, 16 Apr 1998 William H. Ansley wrote:

> One may excuse Wolfe (and I am well aware that he needs no excuse from me)
> by saying that his stories hold up by themselves and the references to
> other of his works are a reward for careful readers. This is certainly
> true, but Wolfe seems to have mastered writing a set of related pieces that
> complement each other so well that their whole is very much greater than
> the sum of their parts better than any other writer I can think of. (In
> fact, does anyone else know of *any* other writer who does this?) It seems
> a shame then that these stories or novellas are first published separately.
> _The Fifth Head of Cerberus_ is a perfect example of this at its best. The
> novellas all work separately quite well but together they form a most
> remarkable work.
