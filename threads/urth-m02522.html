The thread: Kieran Mullen opened the thread asking for favorite Wolfe quotes, arguing the prose works as a whole tapestry rather than through quotable lines. maru dubshinki shared a Shadow of the Torturer passage on people being shaped by the symbols they invent, and Bob Miller added favorite scenes, arguing memorable Wolfe lines come from buildup rather than standalone phrases. b sharp claimed Wolfe's humor is rare, which pthwndxrclzp disputed with a droll Dr. Talos passage. The thread ended with Mullen simply sharing two more unsourced quotations, with no real resolution of the humor or quotability debates.
  • Kieran Mullen: prose is a tapestry whose quality lies in word choice and cadence rather than quotable lines
  • maru dubshinki: offered the asimi-coin passage on people shaped by the symbols they invent
  • b sharp: Wolfe's humor is rare and effective, with few comic moments in The Fifth Head of Cerberus
  • pthwndxrclzp: Dr. Talos's gambling-show passage is a droll example against the claim that humor is rare
  • Bob Miller: memorable lines come from buildup, and the Idnn scene moved him to kneel

Themes: Humour in Wolfe, Books, libraries and writing, Power, rulers and politics

2006-05-19 Maru Dubshinki replying to Kieran Mullen #

Shares a favorite passage from The Shadow of the Torturer, where the narrator says people are shaped by the symbols they invent, using the asimi coin given to soldiers at their oath as the example. Replies to Kieran Mullen's thread on quotable Wolfe, quoting the full passage and crediting Lupine Nuncio's transcription, with no commentary beyond it.
informs · close-textual-reading

Well, I always was struck by one quote from Shadow of the Torturer: as a matter of fact, I first came to Wolfe when I was a kid, and I was casually browsing the first chapters of this odd looking book, and I read this:

"We believe that we invent symbols. The truth is that they invent us; we are their creatures, shaped by their hard, defining edges. When soldiers take their oath they are given a coin, an asimi stamped with the profile of the Autarch. Their acceptance of that coin is their acceptance of the special duties and burdens of military life--they are soldiers from that moment, though they may know nothing of the management of arms. I did not know that then, but it is a profound mistake to believe that we must know of such things to be influenced by them, and in fact to believe so is to believe in the most debased and superstitious kind of magic. The would-be sorcerer alone has faith in the efficacy of pure knowledge; rational people know that things act of themselves or not at all."
The Shadow of the Torturer, Gene Wolfe.

(Thanks to Lupine Nuncio
(http://mysite.verizon.net/~vze2tmhh/wolfeblog.html) for providing a transcription!)

~maru

2006-05-19 b sharp replying to Kieran Mullen #

Argues that Wolfe's humor is rare and effective, citing wry lines from The Book of the New Sun such as the Butcher and She-Wolf exchange about eating wolves, and claims The Fifth Head of Cerberus has almost none. Offers the late-series 'fuligin is the color which is' explanation as a favorite comic moment, responding to a thread of quoted lines from Jolenta, Severian and the Autarch.
informs · close-textual-reading

I think examples of Wolfian humor are especially effective because they are so rare and widely spaced and inevitably droll. 5th Head is so damned dark and foreboding I think there may be no examples. BotNS has a few such as:

The Butcher: "I have seen dead wolves"
She-Wolf: "Yes, and eaten their flesh, though it were too foul for the flies, I dare say. It may be you shall eat mine, if a falling tree kill me."

My favorite is toward the end after four books of continual explaining that "fuligin is the color which is..."

-bsharp

2006-05-20 pthwndxrclzp replying to b sharp #

Quotes a passage from The Book of the New Sun in which Dr. Talos describes his gambling show's unpredictable prizes, saying he enjoys the surprises. Offers it as a favourite example of the droll humour the thread is discussing, against b sharp's remark about rare humour in the books.
supports · close-textual-reading

This passage always brings a big smile to my face:

"It is a gambling proposition," Dr. Talos explained. "And I confess to loving them. The money in the hat is a sure thing--by the close of the first act I can predict to an orichalk how much it will be. But the dropsies! They may be no more than two apples and a turnip, or as much as the imagination can encompass. We have found a baby pig. Delicious, so Baldanders told me when he ate it. We have found a baby baby. We have found a gold-headed stick, and I retain it..."
