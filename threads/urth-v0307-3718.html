Wolfe Walks

Urth list · 5 messages · 2003-07-25 to 2003-07-28

2003urthMatthew MalthouseBrandon MasonAndrew BollenDan'l Danehy-OakesJames Jordan
The thread: Readers reacted to news that Gene Wolfe left the Odyssey workshop early, with Dan'l Danehy-Oakes simply posting the announcement and a link to Wolfe's published explanation. Brandon Mason then argued that students who objected to Wolfe's critique were childish and entitled, supporting an earlier post without offering evidence. James Jordan responded with indignation, insisting Wolfe would never have been harsh and suggesting a workshop in Wolfe's honour. The thread stayed at the level of reactions to the workshop incident and did not turn to the fiction; it ended with opinions about the students rather than any resolution.
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: announces Wolfe's early departure from the workshop and links his explanation, no opinion offered
  • Brandon Mason: students who objected to Wolfe's critique were childish and entitled to be grateful for the privilege
  • James Jordan: indignant that young writers treated Wolfe rudely; Wolfe would never have been harsh; proposes a tribute workshop but won't attend

2003-07-28 Brandon Mason #

Argues that students who objected to Gene Wolfe's critique in a workshop were childish and entitled, and that they should have seen being there as a privilege. Agrees with an earlier post on the matter, offering no textual evidence and relying only on the poster's view of the students' expectations.
supports

I agree that what the students in the workshop did was
more than a little childish. Wolfe's work speaks for
itself, and if they didn't want to be evaluated by him
then they shouldn't have attended the workshop. You
have to wonder what they expected really, a soft pat
on the back? I think these people probably thought
that they deserved to be there on account of the
quality of their manuscripts, I think they should have
looked at it as a privelage.

2003-07-28 James Jordan #

Expresses indignation at an account of Gene Wolfe being treated rudely by young writers at a workshop, insisting Wolfe would never have been harsh about their work. Reacts emotionally to a report of the event and encourages writers to hold a similar workshop honouring Wolfe, while declining to attend himself.
chats

I just read this.
Damn.
Here's an elderly gentleman being treated this way by novices, regardless of their age.
Gene apologizes to his host. Gentleman all the way. Anybody who knows Gene knows that there could not have been anything harsh, rude, or mean about his comments on their work. I'm not a fiction writer, and so would not try to come to the proposed workshop. But if those of you who are writers want do so something like this to honor Gene's skill and what he has to offer, I'm all for your doing it.
Damn, again. This is just appalling.
Damn and hell! And there are a lot of other even better God-given words that are appropriate for this situation, but I'll forbear, since this is the internet!
