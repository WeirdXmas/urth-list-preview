The thread: Michael Straight asked what sign Mr. Free hid the time machine under in Free Live Free, and Tony Ellis countered that Mr. Free said he hid it in a wall, suggesting it is built into the front door, and asked for a timeline of Ben Free's back-and-forth movements. Derek Bell backed the clue-hunt with a recollection of Wolfe's remark about flowers seen through a window that bloom out of season. Michael Bowman and m.driussi spent the thread on textual differences between the Ziesing and Tor editions, relaying bibliographic notes and John Clute's review about a dropped chapter, without offering readings of the novel. The thread ended without resolution, drifting between the ending puzzle and edition variants.

  • m.driussi: relays bibliographic notes and Clute's review on the dropped Chapter 43 and about 4000 words

1997-09-24 Michael Straight #

Asks what the sign is under which Mr. Free says he hid the time machine device in Free Live Free, noting that the final page seems to point readers to it. Also says the book is Wolfe's funniest novel, and wants help finding the clue.
questions · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving

Spoilers ho!

I asked this question a few weeks ago, but perhaps I was so brief that no one knew what I was talking about.

Mr. Free tells the salesman that he hid the device "under a sign." On the last page, after the salesman has retrieved his son and rejoined the others (who seem to have already been "improved" by the time machine), the salesman sees the sign, and it seems that Wolfe intends readers to say "Ah-ha! That's what Mr. Free meant."

I did not say "Ah-ha!" and would like to very much. What is the sign? Any help would be received with gratitude.

I really enjoyed this book and found it to be Wolfe's most humorous novel (right up there with his robot detective stories, in my book).

-Rostrum

1997-09-25 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Quotes John Clute's review of Free Live Free, noting that the limited edition was sharpened and had a dithery Chapter 43 removed, so the story flows smoothly from dream to reality. Shares the passage from Clute's STROKES as information about how the book's editions differ, without adding comment of its own.
informs · close-textual-reading

Reply: Item #9690381 from [email]@INET00#

Michael Bowman,

Re: the different editions, from Clute's STROKES:

"Even the limited edition of the book [FREE LIVE FREE] . . . was so clear and compulsive in the telling that it could be read at one long go. Slightly sharpened at points . . . and shorn of the dithery Chapter 43 of the original version, FREE LIVE FREE now flows with an unbroken liquid precision from this world to the last one, from dream to a magically-achieved reality" (p. 162).

=mantis=

Admits being unsure of the time-travel chronology and asks for a timeline to sort out Ben Free's back-and-forth movements.

1997-09-26 Derek Bell #

Supports the earlier poster's question about the ending of Free Live Free by recalling that Wolfe pointed out a clue about flowers seen through a window. Says the flowers bloom in the wrong season, implying the season outside the front of the house differs from the season seen through the window, which bears on the time-machine ending.
supports · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: time machine is built into Free's front door

Same here.

I don't think you're making a fool of yourself at all; I think Wolfe said somewhere that some people hadn't realised the significance of some flowers that a character saw through a window. In fact, Wolfe implied that the flowers were blooming in the "wrong" season, that it's a different season outside the front of the house to the season seen through the window.

I agree, provided it does not anger the great god K'op-eeriit.

Tercel
