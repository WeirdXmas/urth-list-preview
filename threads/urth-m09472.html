The thread: Fred Kiesche opened by linking a blog review of M. John Harrison's The Pastel City and comparing it to Vance and Clark Ashton Smith. Craig Brewer and James Crossley both praised Harrison's Viriconium stories and novels, with Brewer noting they may feel dated and Crossley recommending Light and Nova Swing. The thread drifted from Harrison to Roberto Bolaño, which Matthew Keeley raised and Crossley and Jordon Flato praised, and it ended without any real debate.
  • Fred Kiesche: The Pastel City recalls Vance and Clark Ashton Smith
  • Craig Brewer: Viriconium is worth reading, though dated, and not anti-genre
  • James Crossley: Harrison is an excellent stylist, and Bolaño is the most interesting new writer
  • Matthew Keeley: Has not read Harrison but wants to read all of Bolaño
  • Jordon Flato: Has read The Savage Detectives and greatly enjoyed it

Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Genre: science fiction, fantasy, both

2009-05-06 Craig Brewer replying to Matthew Keeley #

Argues that M. John Harrison's Viriconium stories are well worth reading, though they may feel slightly dated because he wrote them in frustration with 1960s and 1970s SF and fantasy. Notes that the stories are not anti-genre and that their innovations will seem familiar to readers of Vance, Wolfe and Moorcock.
informs · genre-and-craft

Virconium is WELL worth the time. It might seem a touch dated in a very elusive way, however, since Harrison has often said that he wrote the stories out of his own frustration as a sf/fantasy editor in the 60s/70s. They aren't anti-genre, by any means, but some of the things he does that were "new" then will seem more familiar to Vance/Wolfe/even Moorcock fans.

But if you're just up for the "dying earth" vibe, it's also a great exercise in that game.

2009-05-06 James Crossley replying to Matthew Keeley #

Recommends M. John Harrison's novels Light and Nova Swing as well worth reading, praising him as an excellent stylist in or out of genre. Also calls Roberto Bolaño the most interesting new writer in years and suggests an Urth-list-style discussion group for him, though finding his books resistant to analysis. Responds to Matthew Keeley's post about the Blog of the Fallen and his interest in Viriconium.
supports

I'll second the Harrison recommendation. He's an excellent stylist, in or out of genre. _Light_ and _Nova Swing_ are well worth a look. A heartier second here. Bolano is the most interesting "new" writer to come along in years, says I. I'd say it'd be great to set up an urth-list kind of discussion group for him, but his books are strangely resistant to analysis, I find.

James
