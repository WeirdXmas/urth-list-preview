2003-08-18 James Wynn #

Relays a post by Crush defending Wolfe's worldbuilding against the claim that the New Sun and Long/Short Sun books show seams. Argues the plot is deliberately hidden behind the scenery, and that a generation ship coexisting with the FTL ship Tzadkiel is realistic, comparing it to trains persisting after jet airliners.
disputes · close-textual-reading

Crush disagrees (he thinks):
Two things:
ONE - I'm not sure the seams show between the NS and LS/SS or anywhere else because I'm still not certain (nor do I think anyone else is) what kind of garments he is intending or who he had in mind to wear it. With Tailor Wolfe, it can be hard to tell a seam from a cuff. To change analogies, Wolfe's writing is so plot-oriented (the characters' behavior seem so precisely blocked), yet Wolfe has so much of the narrative taking place behind the scenery (whether we are talking about NS, LS, SS, Peace, Pandora, or The Fifth Head). I simply cannot imagine what audience Wolfe could have in mind when he is writing? "Look, the architect carved a little florish behind these baseboards! Wait! That no florish, that looks like a supporting beam!"

TWO - I think Wolfe's ease with a having a generation ship share the universe with a "post-Einsteinian" monstrosity like Tzadkiel is exemplary of Wolfe adeptness at World-building. He has criticized works that have a single culture on an entire planet (ala Star Trek); well, how realistic is it suppose that the existence of a FTL ship would render generation ships obsolete? Even a single one? That would be like saying that after the invention of the jet airliner, no one on the same planet would ever think or regularly moving freight by train, let alone horse and cart. But neither of these conveyances are on the verge of being eliminated.

I'm not sure this argument really even applies since Tz. moves in and out of Time and Space -- sharing the universe with an Urth before Europe discovered America, let alone Typhon's Urth.

-- Crush

2003-08-19 Chris P #

Argues that an FTL ship would not make generation ships obsolete on the same world, offering a jet-and-triplane analogy, and disputes linking Tzadkiel's ship to the Whorl. Responds to an earlier post's objection by saying such ships are unlikely to coexist rather than impossible, and states that arguments against the link have already been supplied.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Tzadkiel's ship is linked to the Whorl

I don't think that's the important point in the generation vs. FTL argument. I think a more accurate analogy might be that while the existence of jet airplanes do not preclude the existence of triplanes on the same planet, you are extremely unlikely to find a jet triplane.

In any event there is very little reason in my mind to equate Tzadkiel's ship to the Whorl, or even to posit any relationship between the two. Putting an argument together against the possibility seems unnecessary, but an ample number of them have been supplied.

-- Civet

2003-08-19 Matthew Malthouse #

Argues that Typhon's launch of a slow-boat colony clashes with his stated aim of regaining his empire, since the empire seems stellar rather than solar and the required technology differs. Answers Michael Buice's question about inconsistencies in science and technology across the Sun books, citing Typhon's meeting with Severian as Conciliator.
answers · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic

Typhon launching a slow-boat colony seems at odds with his intention of regaining his empire stated when Severian-as-Conciliator meets him.

Given that I'd always had the impression that this was a stellar empire, not just a solar one (which may of course be mistaken), the disparity in required technology jars. It also disrupts motive, but that wasn't your question.

Matthew
