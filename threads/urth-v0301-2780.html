The thread: The thread asks how Silk surfaces in Horn's narration in On Blue's Waters, and whether Babbie goes into Horn or Horn leaves his body and enters Babbie. maa32 argues Horn leaves and enters Babbie, citing Babbie's gestures toward his horns, his embrace of Horn's son in Return to the Whorl, and the three-horned beast Horn rides. Andrew Bollen disputes Marc's view that Horn wants to leave, pointing to the textual order of the forest meeting and questioning why Babbie's spirit cannot speak. cilluff1 answers that Scylla can speak through Oreb because a night chough can mimic words like a parrot. The thread ends without resolution, with Bollen's question about Babbie's silence left open.
  • maa32: Horn leaves his body and enters Babbie, which lets Silk surface at the end of On Blue's Waters
  • Andrew Bollen: the goodbye under the tree comes after the calling episode, so Marc's reading of Horn's wish to leave fails
  • cilluff1: Scylla speaks through Oreb because a night chough can articulate words like a parrot

Themes: Ghosts, dreams and visions, Doubles, twins and clones, Hidden identities and parentage

2003-01-23 Marc Aramini #

Disputes Andrew's claim that Babbie goes into Horn, arguing instead that Horn leaves his body and enters Babbie, which lets Silk surface in the narration at the end of On Blue's Waters. Cites Babbie's gestures toward his horns and his embrace of Horn's son in the astral visit at the end of Return to the Whorl, plus the three-horned beast Horn rides, as evidence.
disputes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Horn goes into Babbie; Silk surfaces when Horn leaves

I MUST disagree with Andrew on Babbie going into Horn - the reason that Silk surfaces in this scene is because Horn wants to leave and be done with his story - that's why he says goodbye under that big tree. He leaves, but the tree sends him into Babbie. Re-read the end of Return to the Whorl where Babbie is present in the astral visit - he continually points to his "HORNS" saying "huh-huh-huh" and hugs Horn's son vigorously, not letting him go and looking at him protectively. This is the action of a father seeing his son, but his son doesn't recognize him.

Silk comes to the fore here - the voice in narration changes right after Horn says goodbye. Then Silk tries to deny that a part of Horn had to leave for Silk to surface, and he represses his identity so that Horn may live, just as Horn tried to suppress his identity so Silk might live. This all occurs at the end of On Blue's Waters. Remember that Horn has to ride a beast with three horns, Babbie. There is too much evidence for Horn going into Babbie to think that Babbie goes into Horn/Silk.

Marc Aramini

2003-01-24 Andrew Bollen #

Disputes Marc's claim that Silk surfaces in Horn because Horn wants to leave, arguing the goodbye under the tree comes after the calling episode. Cites the textual order of the forest meeting and SilkHorn's account in On Blue's Waters of riding a three-horned beast, and questions why Babbie's spirit cannot speak.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Silk surfaces in Horn because Horn wants to leave; Horn's goodbye follows the calling episode; Babbie's spirit lingers in Horn

But SilkHorn saying goodbye to Nettle etc under the tree happens *after* the calling episode.

A problem with this remains: why can Scylla-spirit-in-Oreb articulate words, where Horn-spirit-in-Babbie cannot? I can buy a little of this, though: Babbie has been in Horn on my reading, so maybe some Horniness sticks?

But (textually, at least), the meeting in the forest happens immediately after the goodbye.

But SilkHorn says explicitly in his account of the first meeting with Mucor and Marble (OBW, end Ch 3): "On Green I rode a three-horned beast, as Maytera foresaw. Indeed, I was riding it at the time I was wounded fatally."
