Themes: Women, sex and gender

1998-07-01 Tony Ellis #

Argues that men and women differ substantially in brain function and testosterone-driven behaviour, so Wolfe's sharp polarisation of the sexes is acceptable in general. Responds to Jim Jordan's claim that the modern view sees the sexes as alike except for plumbing, and his Christian complementarity view, by rejecting the dismissal of biological difference.
disputes

That "plumbing" accounts for some pretty major differences, actually. It has been proved that men and women's brains work very differently, often using completely different parts to solve the same problems, and having wildly different areas of expertise. Greatly differing testosterone levels also make the sexes handle group and competitive situations very differently.

This is why I have no problem with Wolfe's polarisation of the sexes _in general_. The devil, as always, is in the details. :-)
