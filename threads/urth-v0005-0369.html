Cim glowing

Urth list · 3 messages · 1997-10-31 to 1997-11-03

1997urthPeter CashMichael Andre-DriussiJames Jordan
The thread: Peter Cash proposed that Cim, the glowing thing in The Island of Doctor Death, is an electrical phenomenon like St. Elmo's Fire or ball lightning, linking it to the electrical weapon named after the character. M.driussi countered that Cim in The Book of the Long Sun is a firefly, citing his glow, star-like look, and soft voice, and offered a Latin pun on cimex meaning bug. Jim Jordan backed the firefly reading through the Latin etymology and said he would keep it unless a good reason to reject it appeared. The thread ended with the firefly reading the only one defended, so it stopped at a one-sided endorsement rather than a resolved debate.
  • Peter Cash: Cim is an electrical phenomenon such as St. Elmo's Fire or ball lightning
  • m.driussi: Cim in Long Sun is a firefly, given his glow and star-like look and soft voice
  • Jim Jordan: Cim is a firefly, since Latin cimex means bug and 'cim glowing' means firefly

Themes: Names and their meanings, Light, darkness and colour, Beasts and the animal in us

1997-10-31 Peter Cash #

Proposes that Cim, the glowing thing in The Island of Doctor Death and Other Stories that moves from tree to tree like a soft star, is an electrical phenomenon such as St. Elmo's Fire or ball lightning. Grounds this in the quoted passage's description and suggests that Cim's totemic name gives the named character an electrical weapon.
proposes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion · theories: Cim is an electrical phenomenon; Cim's totemic name gives an electrical weapon

I hate to be always the contrarian (lie: I love it)...but I think "Cim" is an electrical phenomenon, like St. Elmo's Fire or ball lightning. Either could fit the description of a "soft star" moving from tree to tree. In addition there is Cim's weapon: perhaps the totemic naming conveys an analogous power on the named one, and thus Cim's weapon is electrical.

Sgt. Rock

1997-11-03 James Jordan #

Agrees with Mantis that Cim, the glowing thing in Wolfe's text, is a firefly, reasoning that Latin cimex means bug so "cim glowing" must mean firefly. Builds on Mantis's earlier tie-in to electricity and the weapon, and says he will keep this reading until someone gives a good reason to reject it.
supports · source-and-allusion · theories: Cim means firefly

Ya beat me to it, Mantis. After thinking more about snow, I settled on lightning bug/firefly, and was planning to post.... And, if "cim" is bug, then "cim glowing" is clearly firefly.

I'll ride with this one until somebody gives a good reason to depart from it.
