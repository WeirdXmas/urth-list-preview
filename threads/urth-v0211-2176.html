whorl in orbit

Urth list · 3 messages · 2002-11-18 to 2002-11-19

2002urthThe Book of the Long SunCharles ReedDon DoggettDan'l Danehy-Oakes
The thread: Participants debated whether the Whorl had been in orbit for thirty years, a claim tied to the meaning of the word 'longfather' and to the inhumi's arrival before Silk's enlightenment. Don Doggett disputed the claim, citing Lexicon Urthus and arguing that nothing in the texts is straightforward enough to settle the question. Charles Reed argued the Whorl must have orbited the Short Sun at sub-light speeds and that the narrator should be taken at face value unless the text forces otherwise. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes clarified that 'longfather' is an antique term for a distant male ancestor, not necessarily a culture hero, and said he was working from the word's ordinary meaning without the Lexicon. The thread ended in an impasse, with the textual evidence left disputed.

Themes: Unreliable narration, Time loops and causality

2002-11-18 Don Doggett #

Disputes the claim that the Whorl has been in orbit for thirty years, citing an entry on the longfather in Lexicon Urthus rather than a definitive reading. Argues that nothing in these texts is straightforward enough to support firm conclusions, adding a joking aside about L. Ron Hubbard as narrator.
disputes · close-textual-reading

Against the "Whorl has been in orbit for thirty years" possibly in the text and possibly said by Wolfe in an interview I lay down my "longfather, culture hero of Ushas who left for the stars" entry in Lexicon Urthus p.184 - 185. And I would argue most vehemently that nothing is straightforward enough in these texts to draw any definitive conclusions. Heck, for all I know, this is some elaborate joke by Wolfe, and the real narrator of the whole 12 book series is L. Ron Hubbard.

Don

2002-11-19 Charles Reed #

Argues that the Whorl must have been in orbit around the Short Sun at sub-light speeds, since the inhumi (Quetzal) boarded before Silk was enlightened. Rejects dismissing inconvenient evidence by calling the narrator wrong, and insists the narrator be taken at face value unless the text forces otherwise.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: The Whorl orbited the Short Sun at sub-light speed

In order for the inhumi to have come aboard before the Silk is enlightened -- and I don't think you can question that Quetzal was on board before Silk was enlightened -- then the Whorl has to have been in orbit around the Short Sun at necessarily sub-light speeds. I simply don't think there's any way around that conclusion.

Don, if this is true, then what's the point? If you can dismiss any piece of evidence that doesn't fit in with your theory by just saying, "Well, obviously the narrator is wrong." then you can twist the narrative into any shape you like. I don't see the joy in that, because then you're not working at interpreting the text, you're simply imposing your will on it. I certainly don't believe that the narrator is infallible, but I do believe that you have to take what he says at face value until something forces you to do otherwise. But that's just me.

Charles

2002-11-19 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Explains that "longfather" is an antique word for a distant male ancestor, not necessarily a culture hero, and notes Tolkien's use of it for the Dwarves. Responds to the Lexicon Urthus entry the earlier poster cited, saying he lacks the book and is working from the word's ordinary meaning.
informs · source-and-allusion

Okay, I don't have a copy of the Lex, so I don't know what it says there ... but ... "longfather" is simply an antique word for a male ancestor distant enough that you can't (or don't=20 want to bother) counting out the "greats" -- typically one who plays a patronymic or patriarchal role in the family history; Abraham would be "longfather" to the Jews. (Note that this does not exclude the concept of "culture hero," but does not
require it.) The only modern use of the word I know outside of Wolfe is -- who's surprised? -- Tolkien, who refers somewhere to the "longfathers of the Dwarves."
