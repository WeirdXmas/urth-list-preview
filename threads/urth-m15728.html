Dorcas the Forgetless or Baldanders the Forgetful?

Urth list · 5 messages · 2010-09-07 to 2010-09-07

2010urthThe Book of the New SunRyan DunnDavid StockhoffJeff WilsonLee Berman
The thread: Ryan Dunn asked whether a scene in The Shadow of the Torturer, where Baldanders and Dorcas meet the morning after the play, suggests Severian's perfect memory comes from Dorcas or instead shows Baldanders's forgetfulness, citing Dorcas forgetting her name and Baldanders's half-conscious, non-dreaming behaviour. Jeff Wilson proposed that Baldanders's question about who she is might be sarcasm, since Severian supplies her answer, and offered sleep disorder or distraction as explanations for his half-awake state. Lee Berman supported the half-awake reading, describing Baldanders as sleepwalking through the early books, and added that Dorcas's swallowing before giving her name hints she uses another name. The thread stayed on close reading of this one passage with no clear resolution beyond these readings.
  • Ryan Dunn: the scene may show Baldanders's forgetfulness rather than Dorcas as the source of Severian's memory
  • Jeff Wilson: Baldanders's question may be sarcasm, and his half-awake state may have ordinary causes
  • Lee Berman: Baldanders sleepwalks through the early books, and Dorcas hides another name

Themes: Memory and forgetting, Unreliable narration, Names and their meanings

2010-09-07 Ryan Dunn #

Asks whether a passage in The Shadow of the Torturer where Baldanders and Dorcas meet the morning after the play hints that Severian's perfect memory comes from Dorcas, or instead shows Baldanders's forgetfulness. Quotes the exchange in which Dorcas forgets her name and Baldanders praises her memory, and raises his non-dreaming and half-conscious behaviour as related clues.
questions · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Dorcas is the source of Severian's perfect memory

Hello All,

I have stumbled across another interesting exchange between Dorcas and Baldanders. The morning after the first play, just before they divvy up the coin from the crowd, Baldanders speaks to Dorcas. He asks who she is, and Dorcas reminds him.how they met the night before. It's worth noting that she forgets her name for a moment, though it could have been she was more hesitant to answer...

..

Though he seemed thoroughly awake, Baldanders stared at her. "Who are you?"

"I'm . . ." Dorcas turned toward me, frightened.

"Dorcas," I said.

"Yes, Dorcas. Don't you remember? We met behind the curtain last night. You... your friend introduced us, and said I shouldn't be afraid of you, because you would only pretend to hurt people. In the show. I said I understood, because Severian does terrible things but is really so kind."

..

At the end of this exchange, Baldanders has something to say to her...

..

Baldauders told Dorcas, "You have a wonderful memory. I wish I could recall everything like that."

..

I wonder, is there anything more to this exchange than what is said? Might this hint at or imply that Severian's perfect memory may come from Dorcas? Or is it meant to show how forgetful Baldanders is, that he could forget an encounter from the night before?

There's also the issue of him not dreaming (unless he's in proximity to Severian) and behaving like he is half-conscious even when he appears fully awake, but I'm not sure what those things mean.

I intend to investigate the text further, but thought this was worth positing to the group nonetheless.

...ryan

2010-09-07 Jeff Wilson replying to Ryan Dunn #

Suggests that Baldanders' bewildered question to Dorcas, asking who she is, may be sarcasm rather than real forgetfulness, since Severian supplies her answer. Offers alternative explanations for Baldanders' half-awake state, such as a sleep disorder or distraction by intellectual matters, after Ryan Dunn quoted the passage.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Baldanders' question to Dorcas is sarcasm

This could be Baldanders' saracsm, since Severian who has the wonderful memory answers for her, and generally fills her empty vessel (heh).

Baldanders' half-awakeness and dreamlessness could be really be a sleep disorder, or his distraction when his attention wanders to more intellectual matters.

2010-09-07 Lee Berman replying to Ryan Dunn #

Agrees with Jeff Wilson's idea that Baldanders is half-awake, arguing he moves through his life in the early books like a sleepwalker while his mind is on his own experiments and plans. Adds that Dorcas's swallowing before she gives her name suggests she goes by another name she cannot reveal yet.
supports · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Dorcas goes by a hidden nickname; Baldanders is half-awake and dreamless

I very much agree with the latter guess. I think Baldanders is sorta sleepwalking through the life we see him living in the first two books with Dr. Talos to tell him to eat, sleep, wake up, lift up stage equipment etc. His mind is back in his castle figuring out new biological experimentation and perhaps planning his eventual rule of Urth or establishing his god/megatherian-hood.

When Severian first meets Dorcas, she "swallows" before she says her name. I don't know if gut-level intuition has any advantage, but in both cases, on re-reading, my intuition told me that her hesitation is because she goes by another name. A nickname which, for obvious reasons, can't be revealed at this time in the story.

Not a very exciting answer but I lean toward that one.
