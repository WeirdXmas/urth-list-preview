The thread: James Wynn asked why Wolfe named the smallest boy at the palaestra Villus, proposing a double sense: a tiny intestinal hair and a hairlike plant growth, making the character's sex ambiguous and linking the name to Titi. Nigel Price supplied the Latin gloss, tying villus to pilus and shaggy hair, with a joking aside about hair. Che Monro played along lightly with the dictionary gloss and a quip about Shakespeare, so the thread ended in a brief, playful exchange rather than a settled reading.

2003-03-04 James Wynn #

Proposes that Wolfe chose the name Villus for its double sense, a tiny hairlike projection in the intestines and a tiny hairlike growth on plants, which makes Villus an ambiguously sexed smallest "boy" at the palaestra. Thanks Nigel for the etymology linking the word to Latin pilus, then compares the name's ambiguity to Titi, the male counterspy who impersonated women, asking why Wolfe chose it.
proposes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Wolfe chose Villus for its sexually ambiguous double meaning

Nigel lends Crush a hand:
It is related etymologically to "pilus", the Latin word for "a single hair".

Crush responds:
Thanks Nigel! This is very useful to me. The confusing thing about "villus" is that it is a sexually ambivalent name. In biology it is a teeny hairlike projection in the small intestines. In botany it is a teeny hairlike growth on the leaves or stems of some plants.

What's the same in both senses is the quality of smallness, and Villus is the smallest "boy" at the palaestra. But, there are lots and lots of more common words that carry that quality. Wolfe was sure aware of the ambiguity of this word and selected it at least in part for that ambiguity. Why?? This calls to mind Titi's sexually ambiguous name; Titi, the male counterspy whose specialty was female impersonation.

-- Crush

2003-03-04 Nigel Price #

Explains that "Villus" is a Latin word meaning shaggy hair, related to "pilus" meaning a single hair, answering a playful musing by Crush about a name. Responds to Crush's rhetorical ruminating with a dictionary-based gloss of the word's meaning and etymology, adding a self-deprecating aside about the poster's own hair.
answers · source-and-allusion

In a rhetorical mood, Crush ruminates...

Nigel, who, somewhat disturbingly, keeps a Latin dictionary by his side at all times, responds in a manner that is intended to be helpful, but which is probably intensely irritating...

"Villus" is a Latin word (2nd declension, masculine) meaning "shaggy hair". It is related etymologically to "pilus", the Latin word for "a single hair".

Nigel (who wishes he still had shaggy hair these days)
