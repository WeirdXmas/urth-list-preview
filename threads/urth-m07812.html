The thread: David Stockhoff asked about underwater greenhouse passages in Wolfe's On Blue's Waters, and Brian Lovely answered with a quoted scene of glass-walled underwater walls and plant life. Matthew Groves corrected the chapter reference to chapter 8, disputed that the glass blocks light one way, and suggested Lovely was confusing it with a glass detail in Engine Summer. Jordon Flato tentatively rejected the idea that Blue is Urth, citing parallel global floods as a pattern, and Stuart Hamm proposed that Wolfe recycles a limited stock of themes while jokingly raising the Abaia rumour. The thread ends without resolution, with the glass-light question and the Blue-Urth question left open.
  • Brian Lovely: the underwater glass walls in On Blue's Waters suggest a greenhouse, with light focused one way and blocked the other
  • Matthew Groves: the passage is in chapter 8, the claim about one-way light is wrong, and Lovely may be recalling Engine Summer
  • Jordon Flato: Blue is not Urth, but the global floods on both worlds reveal a shared pattern
  • Stuart Hamm: Wolfe repeats a limited stock of themes and motifs across books, with a joking revival of the Abaia rumour

2009-04-29 Brian Lovely #

Points out a passage in On Blue's Waters, chapter 10, where Seawrack describes underwater walls of masonry with broken glass that focuses light one way and blocks it the other, suggesting a greenhouse. Responds to David Stockhoff's request for the underwater greenhouse passages by quoting the scene and noting its plant life of differing ages.
answers · close-textual-reading

On Blue's Waters, Chapter 10 "The End": "I know places in the sea where there are walls like those," Seawrack told me. Her voice was hushed. "'Underwater' is what you say."

They've found an area with low curving walls of masonry. There are shards of broken glass around. Through one direction, the glass seems to intensify light, through the other direction it blocks it. Sounds like handy stuff to make a greenhouse from. There's also some talk about plant life inside and outside the circle and how it seems to be different ages.
