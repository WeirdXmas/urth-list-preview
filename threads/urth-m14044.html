On the allocation of blankets

Urth list · 3 messages · 2010-06-02 to 2010-06-03

2010urthThe Book of the New SunScott CooperTony Ellis
The thread: The thread asks what the blanket episode means: why Odilo refuses a blanket, why Thais speaks, and why Pega jabs her with an elbow. Scott Cooper argues that Thais's request would be too selfish, so she is really about to offer her own blanket, and that the elbow is a clue suggesting Thais is of exultant caste. Tony Ellis argues that Odilo refuses out of old-fashioned gallantry, that Severian's offer saves face, and that Pega cuts Thais off to stop her asking for a blanket. Tony Ellis then disputes his own earlier reading, saying Thais offers to share a spare blanket and that the elbow protects Odilo, citing the timing of her speech and the scene's effect. No shared conclusion is reached; the exchange ends in impasse.
  • Scott Cooper: Thais would be too selfish to ask for a blanket, so she is about to offer her own; the elbow is a clue to exultant caste
  • Tony Ellis: Odilo refuses from gallantry, Severian's offer saves face, and Pega silences Thais to stop her asking
  • Tony Ellis: Thais offers to share a spare blanket, and the elbow protects Odilo, per the speech timing and aesthetic effect

2010-06-02 Scott Cooper replying to Tony Ellis #

Disputes Tony Ellis's reading of the blanket episode by arguing that Thais asking for a second blanket would be too selfish, and proposes instead that she is about to offer her own blanket to Odilo. Grounds this in the principle that a minor detail signals a clue, citing the elbow-in-the-ribs moment, and suggests Odilo's irritation stems from suspecting Thais is of exultant caste.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Thais offers her own blanket to Odilo; Odilo suspects Thais is of exultant caste

Right: just because it's Wolfe doesn't mean it has to be convoluted. I strongly agree with that.

I hadn't thought of your interpretation, and it fits the text well. What's more, it explains why the first word out of her mouth was "I," and relates it to the foregoing passage where Pega is talking to Odilo in a low voice. On the other hand, asking for a *second* blanket would have been extraordinarily selfish. Do we really have any indication she's -- not just that selfish, but that indifferent to what people think of her selfishness? She'd be like Jolenta, in that case.

If we're going with a "not convoluted" explanation, then I'd go with my #3, i.e. what she's about to say is "You can share MY blanket any time, big boy" or words to that effect.

My reason for thinking there might be a "convoluted" interpretation was the following principle of Lupine exegesis: where there's a mystery, there's a clue. I think we can agree there's some mystery about Thais. The typical Wolfe clue is (I assert) a minor detail which, if you even notice it makes you ask "why did he even include that?" The whole Odilo/Pega/Thais episode only occupies a few pages, and, looking within it for such a detail, I found the elbow-in-the- ribs episode. That's why I thought it a question worth asking.

Also, incidentally:
Are you sure old fashioned gallantry would have prevented Odilo from accepting the blanket after Thais asked for it? What he's displayed towards her up to that point isn't gallantry -- it's irritation. I think a more likely explanation why he wouldn't accept the blanket if she asked for it is that he suspects she's of exultant caste (but suspects, too, that she's only pretending to be.) I think his irritation's provoked by that ambiguity: it's just the sort of thing that would get on his nerves.

2010-06-02 Tony Ellis #

Argues that in the blanket episode Odilo refuses the blanket out of old-fashioned gallantry, and that Severian's offer is a face-saving way to get him to take it. Reads the sequence closely: Thais falls silent only after Severian speaks, so Pega likely cuts her off to prevent her from asking for a blanket herself.
proposes · close-textual-reading · allegory-and-typology · theories: Pega silences Thais to stop her asking for a blanket

I really don't think it's that convoluted. Everyone except Thais can see that it is only Odilo's old-fashioned gallantry that is making him refuse the blanket he badly needs. Severian's "why don't you use it until I'm ready for it?" is transparently a way of getting him to take it without losing face.

Thais doesn't say anything until Severian has said this, so most likely she was about to say "I could do with another blanket, if it's going spare."

Odilo would never have accepted the blanket after that, so the compassionate Pega, who has already taken a shine to him, quickly shuts Thais up.

2010-06-03 Tony Ellis replying to Tony Ellis #

Argues that Thais's offer in the blanket scene is prompted by a wish to share a spare blanket rather than by pure dislike of Severian's refusal, and that Pega's elbow to Thais's ribs works as a payoff protecting Odilo. Responds to Tony Ellis's reading by citing the textual timing, that Thais speaks only after Severian declines his blanket, and by appealing to the scene's aesthetic effect.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Thais wants to share Severian's spare blanket

She's cynical, secretive... and a prostitute, with all the hardened self-interest that implies. Works for me.

Also a good explanation. But as I say, Thais doesn't start to speak until *after* Severian has said he doesn't need his blanket ? whereas if Wolfe only wanted to show that blanket-sharing was her agenda, he could have had her speak up before. The implication is that whatever she was about to say was prompted by the possibility of a spare blanket.

There's also the matter of aesthetics. Pega has taken a fancy to Odilo. Pega protecting Odilo, with a sharp elbow to the ribs of the self-interested Thais, is a nice payoff to this little vignette. Pega elbowing Thais simply for impropriety... is not.

That's how Odilo seems to me - but it's how Odilo seems to Pega that matters.
