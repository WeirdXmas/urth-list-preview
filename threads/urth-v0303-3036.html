Silk as Typhon

Urth list · 5 messages · 2003-03-08 to 2003-03-11

2003urthThe Book of the Long SunThe Book of the New SunLisa Schaffer-DoggettAndrew BollenChris PJeff WilsonRoy C. Lackey
The thread: The thread asks whether Silk is Typhon in disguise, a clone, or a future body for Typhon or Pas. Roy Lackey argues the four parents shown in Silk's vision from the Outsider rule out cloning from Typhon, and treats that vision as authoritative. Lisa Schaffer-Doggett counters that Typhon is indifferent to the genetics of the bodies he uses, and quotes Don, who proposes Silk as Typhon's son while admitting he must reread the series. Chris argues genetic manipulation could have produced Typhon's body from an embryo, and Andrew Bollen rejects the Pas-vessel idea on the grounds that no god would leave the mainframe Olympus for a fleshly body. The thread ends in disagreement without resolution, with the objections tied to the textual details of the parents and the Greek-gods analogy.
  • Roy C. Lackey: Silk cannot be a clone of Typhon, since the Outsider's vision shows four parents
  • Lisa Schaffer-Doggett: Typhon is indifferent to genetics, so the four-parents objection does not settle it
  • Don: Silk is Typhon's son and the four-parents idea is a red herring
  • Chris: Silk was an embryo meant as Typhon's future body, and genetic manipulation could explain it
  • Andrew Bollen: Silk is not a vessel for Pas, since no god would abandon the mainframe Olympus for a fleshly body

Themes: Robots, androids and artificial life, Gods, angels and the Increate, Fathers, mothers and children

2003-03-08 Roy C. Lackey #

Disputes the idea that Silk is Typhon in disguise, arguing that the vision Silk receives from the Outsider during his brief death shows four parents, which rules out Silk being a clone of Typhon. Grounds the objection in the text of that vision, cited from the Long Sun book, and treats it as authoritative because the Outsider granted it.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Silk is Typhon in disguise; Silk is a clone of Typhon

Then here is a somewhat more scientific objection. When Silk briefly "died" in that tunnel collapse, he was granted a 'vision' by the Outsider (and it _was_ the Outsider; Pas can't raise the dead) of his "parents". That 'vision' is treated in the text as every bit as valid as Silk's enlightenment. In that vision he saw _four_ parents, not three. The four were his "mother" (who gave birth to the embryo and raised him), his adoptive father (the old Calde Tussah), the "tall" "blue-eyed" man (who was his "biological" father) and the undescribed woman (who was his "biological" mother) (LS3:9). If that woman contributed biological material to the embryo that became Silk, then it is not possible for the embryo to have been a clone of Typhon, or of anyone else.

-Roy

2003-03-08 Chris P #

Argues that Silk was an embryo meant to become Typhon's future body, so the biological-mother objection does not rule out Typhon as the source, since genetic manipulation could have produced the body. Responds to an earlier post quoting Long Sun book three's mother passage, relaying Roy's objection and adding that Silk's scanned parents are unlikely to be Typhon's parents.
disputes · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Silk was an embryo intended as Typhon's future body; Typhon Pas used eugenics or genetic manipulation for Silk

Roy sensibly objected:

This is a good point, and I think it points back to an older theory that Silk was an embryo that was intended to be a future body for Typhon/Pas after the landing. We already know that Typhon is not fastidious about the genetics of the body his mind inhabits - if anything he considers it an accoutrement or tool, and would want as "souped up" a version as he could get. Whether this was accomplished through eugenics, or some kind of genetic manipulation of his biological parents is impossible to say.

I believe, as well, that this is not an insoluble problem in the grand scheme of Don's thesis. Thematically, it amounts to the same thing- Silk was intended to be Typhon, in either case.

Of course, there is always the possibility that the people Silk saw were actually *Typhon's* parents, but it seems highly unlikely that Typhon's parents would have been alive to be scanned into the mainframe; and if they had, they would have had a place in Pas' pantheon.

Chris

2003-03-11 Andrew Bollen #

Argues against the idea that Silk is a clone or vessel for Pas, reasoning that the mainframe is the gods' Olympus and no god would willingly abandon it for a fleshly body. Responds to Roy's claim by drawing on the Greek-gods analogy and closing with a joke about whether Passilk is homoousios or homoiousios.
disputes · theology-and-religion · myth-and-structure · theories: Silk is a vessel of Pas

The only reason I have seen for thinking that Silk might be a clone is the vessel-of-Pas theory. But why should Pas want such a vessel? It is clear to me that the mainframe is the Olympus of these "gods". Like the Greek gods, they may foray out in human or animal guise, but this is slumming (or desperation). Who would willingly abandon Olympus forever?

Pas' reign on Blue is to be heralded by the construction of a new mainframe/Olympus, not by the appearance of a fleshified Passilk.

(BTW - Passilk: homoousios or homoiousios?)
