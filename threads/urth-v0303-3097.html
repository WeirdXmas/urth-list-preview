Themes: Suffering and sacrifice

2003-03-18 Marc Aramini #

Proposes that the ending of Three Fingers shows the narrator biting off a finger of the villainous toon-executive, who has both real and mythic identities, rather than only his insanity. Grounds this in the glove being white and red with blood, explaining the differing finger counts, and asks if others see the bite, noting Wolfe's earlier cannibalism.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Narrator bites off the villainous executive's finger at the ending; Villainous toon-executive has real and mythic identities

I read the ending of Three Fingers not so much as a picture of the main characters insanity but as a picture of confusion - I think he acts to bite off the finger of the villainous toon/executive, who has both real and mythic identities, thus making the glove both white and red with blood, and accounting for the different number of fingers. Doesn't anyone else think he is biting a finger off at the end? It's not like we've never seen cannibalism in Wolfe before.

Marc Aramini
