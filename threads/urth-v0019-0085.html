Themes: Hidden identities and parentage

1998-10-10 Robert Borski #

Argues that Paeon and Palaemon are the same person, linking Appian's past as a honey steward to the Latin word for bee and the Greek word for honey. Builds the case on the name Melicertes given to Palaemon in Lexicon Urthus and on a claimed clue in the Citadel's chapter on Severian's visit to the House Azure.
proposes · puzzle-solving · source-and-allusion · theories: Paeon and Palaemon are the same person

Earlier this year prion (Sean Whalen) brilliantly observed the connection between Appian, the old autarch, and the Latin word for bee, apis. Why this works is because Appian was a honey steward before he became autarch. And who trained him in his duties as honey steward, the same way Palaemon trained Severian? Paeon.

But notice how in LEXICON URTHUS, mantis remarks how Palaemon was originally known as Melicertes. (I believe prion mentions this too.) Also now recall that the Greek word for honey is meli (Latin, mel).

This is but one clue of many that Paeon and Palaemon are one and the same.

(There's a huge clue in Chapter 25 of CITADEL, where Appian discusses Sev's visit to the House Azure.)

Robert Borski
