2007-06-17 Dave Lebling replying to Matthew Groves #

Corrects a spelling of his name and explains that the epigraph of Wolfe's Litany of the Long Sun-era book carries several meanings tied to Cutthroat's exile and wolf nature. Argues that Cutthroat parallels the Trojans, the she-wolf-raised Romans, the human-like animal people, and a Troy-to-Rome rebirth of humanity from animals. Responds to Matthew Groves, who cited an old archive post by Lebling on Cutthroat as a dog.
proposes · source-and-allusion · myth-and-structure · theories: Cutthroat parallels Trojan exiles and Rome's wolf-raised founders; Humanity reborn from animals as Rome from Troy

Thanks, Matthew. It's "Lebling," by the way, not "Liebling." I think the archives have various lacunae; my original post on this topic isn't there, just responses from alga and others. A little earlier in that archive is a post about problems with the database or the host, which may explain it.

The epigraph has multiple meanings. The simplest is that Cutthroat, like the Trojans, is an exile; he was expelled from the Great Sleigh, they were expelled from Troy. On another level, remember that the Romans, the descendants of Aeneas, were fostered by a she-wolf, as was Cutthroat by wolves. Third, consider the human-like animal people who have been approaching humanity for "seven years" and may now have reached it in the person of Cutthroat. Fourth, consider the destroyed Troy reborn as Rome, then a destroyed or at least diminished humanity (on the asteroid or even universally) reborn from the animals.

Dave Lebling, aka vizcacha
