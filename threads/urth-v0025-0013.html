Themes: Gods, angels and the Increate, Time loops and causality

1999-04-06 Kieran Mullen #

Disputes the claim that the Outsider's love in Wolfe's books amounts to predestination, arguing that unconditional love differs from it. Cites the idea that the future is fixed regardless of a person's acts and notes that universalism is not predestination except in a trivial sense.
disputes · theology-and-religion · theories: Outsider's love is not predestination

No, absolutely not. That is unconditional love. It is *not* predestination.

Predestination would be "the Outsider had assured him that the future was crystalline, never to be changed by any act of his, no matter how iniquitous or how meritorious," which Wolfe did not say.

Universalism (the doctrine or belief that all men will be saved, or made happy, in the future state) is also not considered a form of predestination save in a trivial sense.

Kieran "not a theologian, but I play one on TV" Mullen
