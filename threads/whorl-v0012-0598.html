The ending

2001whorlThe Book of the Long SunJames Jordan

2001-04-12 James Jordan #

Agrees with an earlier point that the ending of The Book of the Long Sun feels unprepared, with Horn suddenly becoming Silk and the characters abruptly departing. Notes that unlike Engine Summer the ending lacks emotional power, and suggests Wolfe's abrupt endings aim to prompt rereading, though rereading yields little here.
supports · genre-and-craft

While I did not "dislike" the ending as much as some here did, I think you have a good point. Suddenly "Horn" is "Silk." Suddenly they all go away. This has not been "prepared." And as you note, unlike Engine Summer, the ending is not emotionally powerful.
But Wolfe often does this kind of thing. It's his way of trying to get us to re-read the book or story with new eyes. Yet, the re-reading does not seem to give much in this case.
