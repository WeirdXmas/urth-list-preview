Undine's nature

Urth list · 17 messages · 2011-10-17 to 2011-10-19

2011urthThe Book of the New SunLee BermanGerry QuinnDavid StockhoffMarc AraminiLarry MillerJeff Wilson
The thread: The thread asked whether undines and related figures in Wolfe's books are natural beings, and whether Severian's attractions point to incestuous kinship. Lee Berman argued Baldanders grew unnaturally, Abaia may be his daughter-bride, and undines bud detachable parts, while Gerry Quinn and Marc Aramini rejected the incest-curse reading as too sweeping. David Stockhoff debated consummation with Juturna and offered jokey and speculative biology, and later proposed the Long Sun beans as DNA-absorbing or mind-transfer technology, which Jeff Wilson and Gerry Quinn engaged with. The exchange ended in impasse, with each side holding its reading and no clear agreement reached.
  • Lee Berman: undines bud detachable parts; Baldanders grew unnaturally; incest curse is plausible
  • Gerry Quinn: rejects incest-curse reading; sea monsters are extraterrestrial; Jolenta's appeal is her purpose
  • David Stockhoff: consummation with Juturna is doubtful; proposes beans as DNA-absorbing or mind-transfer tech
  • Marc Aramini: one incest case does not imply universal incest; genetic details undercut the curse
  • Jeff Wilson: beans may reproduce specific individuals' consciousness, making Scylla's threat a pretext

Themes: Unreliable narration, Fathers, mothers and children, Evolution and the future of humanity

2011-10-17 Lee Berman #

Argues that Baldanders is not originally a small man who became an undine, and that his size and gills point to an unnatural growth process rather than Idas-like origins. Supports the reading of Abaia as Baldanders' daughter-bride and possibly Great Scylla, citing the budding beings at the end of Return to the Whorl, and continues to back Jolenta as Severa over Merryn.
proposes · puzzle-solving · source-and-allusion · theories: Baldanders was not originally like Idas; Great Scylla may be Abaia; Jolenta is Severa; Typhon's daughter has a dual essence

There is some interesting convergence of a couple threads which might warrant a new thread title.

This is an interesting new idea I hadn't ever heard before. It is a bit cryptic how Baldanders started as a small man suddenly appearing lakeside a couple generations ago. Could he have been like Idas then? There is some other tangential evidence to support this theory I think, which can be seen in a further consideration of undines, below.

I also kinda lean against the idea. Idas is an adolescent undine but she still appears as a tall slender human. Moreover, the words of Dr. Talos suggest Baldanders created his doctor to maintain all the delicate hormonal adjustments which allow his growth to continue. Of course I think Dr. Talos is disingenuous on how Baldanders can breathe underwater but for some reason I am inclined to believe him regarding Baldanders' growth and the implication that it is unnatural.

I agree. Specifically Abaia, if we may believe the dream-undines' claim of being his daughter-brides. That term is surely meant to provoke our instinct for incest revulsion but when you have a giant creature who can reproduce asexually and who may be sequentially hermaphroditic like Tzadkiel, it starts to seem a bit more natural (as incest was the natural mating pattern of the pagan god pantheons).

Moreover I think the end of RttW supports this idea. Great Scylla is shown budding off a small army of female-shaped beings. What else could this be other than a production of undine-like beings. Actually I sort of think Great Scylla may actually BE Abaia. Scylla is associated with snakes and fish and Abaia is an eel. Fish-snake-eel provides a reasonable connection from which meaningless gender pronouns should not divert our attention.

A question which seem always danced around be never addressed is regarding who is Typhon's daughter really, Scylla or Cilinia? Though the process isn't clear to me, I do have a strong sense that his daughter has a dual essence. Perhaps there was cloning involved but I think one iteration of her died as a girl, but another entered the sea and kept growing into the monster which became Great Scylla (and perhaps Abaia). Somewhere in Long Sun there is mention of the planet Scylla herself ruled.

I've heard that theory before but it is quite a minority opinion I think. I think it has merit because there is the sexual attraction between Severian and Juturna which I find a necessary requirement for identifying Severa. I think the complete absence of sexual attraction, along with some other evidence, rules out Merryn .

So, I'd at least consider Juturna a likely relative of Severian's. I still think Jolenta is the best candidate for being Severa. We are conspicuously not given her name when she is a waitress and her lack of history allows her to have been an expelled witch. Jolenta could be a stage name and while she doesn't have much of a sex drive, she seems more attracted to Severian than to any other man. (it is indirect but something about the buxom, red haired appearance of Jahlee in RttW seems like a clue to Jolenta and away from Merryn also).

I don't find it a strong conclusion either but some do and my comments on the matter were meant for them.

2011-10-18 Gerry Quinn replying to Lee Berman #

Quotes Lee Berman's view that undines are detachable sea-monster organs and that Cilinia bargained with Scylla, then rejects dismissing the waitress as Severian's sister. Argues the name Jolie suggests someone made beautiful, notes she sleeps with Dorcas, and calls the sister-identification heuristic thin.
disputes · puzzle-solving · source-and-allusion · theories: Undines are detachable sea-monster organs; Juturna was spawned by Abaia; Waitress is Severian's long-lost sister; Waitress's name derives from Jolie

From: Lee Berman

Can they reproduce asexually? I don?t recall that we are told how they reproduce.

Actually I lean towards the idea that the undines are created by the sea monsters as detachable organs which are used for various functions: communicating with humans, going to war, and probably reproduction too. Perhaps there are male undines which serve as sea monster sperm, or perhaps the sea monsters directly fertilise the undines. Abaia may of course be capable of self-fertilising, which is a form of asexual reproduction I guess.

I?ve never seen the question asked. I?ll go with the one that was human and that Typhon had scanned. Cilinia obviously made a bargain with Scylla, probably looking for help overthrowing her father and ruling herself, and identified with her, even seeing herself as something like Scylla, though human sized.

Ruling out what most consider the most likely candidate for Severian?s putative sister, simply because he does not note that he is sexually attracted to her, seems a perverse reading in every sense of the word. (Most certainly the converse does not apply, or half the women of the Commonwealth must be Severian?s sisters.)

What need is there for it? Why would Severian know the name of the waitress anyway? I doubt they wear name badges in the inns of Nessus. And if Severian was speaking to the waitress, it would simply make it harder for Wolfe to have her appear later as another person without comment from Severian.

I think the name is probably derived from Jolie ? it suggests someone who has been made beautiful, or wants to become beautiful. Of course she might have changed it or taken a stage name.

At the risk of encouraging you, she also apparently sleeps with Dorcas, who would be her gran. But as a means of identifying long-lost sisters, the heuristic still seems thin.

- Gerry Quinn

2011-10-19 Marc Aramini replying to Gerry Quinn #

Disputes the idea that every woman Severian is drawn to is his relative under an incest curse, arguing that one case of incest does not imply universal incest. Grounds this in the varied social spread of the women, the genetic descriptions such as Pia's dark hair and pale skin, and Severian's black hair undercutting the golden-glow marker.
