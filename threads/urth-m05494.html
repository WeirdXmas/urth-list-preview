2008-02-06 David Duffy replying to David Duffy #

Reports on John Clute's one-page review of Pirate Freedom in the NYRSF, noting he calls the maze unicursal and finds the book Catholic. Quotes Clute's claim that emotionally excessive characters are likely the protagonist in disguise, and lists Severian-like traits shared by Ignacio, Latro and Able.
informs · close-textual-reading · allegory-and-typology · theories: Ignacio is Chris in disguise

Actually, he doesn't mention further iterations and says the maze is unicursal ;)

OK. This is in the NYRSF Jan issue, only one page. Generally liked it, though found a bit Catholic ;) "Angriest and most fun"

most amusing claim: "any character in any Wolfe novel whose emotional response to any event is deeper than seems exactly appropriate is fairly likely to be the protagonist of that novel in a guise we have not yet encountered". So, he implies that as he read it he was suspicious of i) Ignacio crying;
ii) "We are almost dead certain" that on P 23-24, the man whose chicken is stolen is Chris [see P 310].

comparisons: Severian - tall and thin, seen as dark; excel in fighting; charismatic; repressed extreme emotions; attractive to (untrustworthy) women; torturers; God speaks to them directly; inhumanly acure moral judgements; write confessions. It seems to me this equally applies to Latro and Able though.

difference: too certain in faith, "Savaranola-intense"

reason for confession: convince the reader that "life as a pirate [is] a good preparation for becoming a priest". Well, self-justification anyway.

And ends:

"The reader may envy the sea-foam and clarity of that past, and would be there with him in a flash of trance, if given entry. But the reader may also take [the] final sentence as a dreadful warning: Danger! We are the people. We are you." Well you either like Clute's writing style or you don't.

Cheers, David Duffy.
