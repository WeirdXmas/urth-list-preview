So who is the child in Baldander's bed? [was: Juturna, the Missing Sister]

Urth list · 8 messages · 2010-07-29 to 2010-08-01

The thread: Posters debated who the chained child in Baldanders' bed is, and whether the text points to a real child or a catamite. Lee Berman argued the child is Baldanders' own child, that the setting makes the sexual reading unavoidable, and later read Severian's denials as self-betrayal, while Jerry Friedman and Jeff Wilson focused on the word catamite and Severian's moral world. James Wynn and António Pedro Marques disputed whether Occam's Razor can settle such a puzzle, with Marques qualifying its scope. Jack Smith mostly echoed Berman. The thread ended without agreement, drifting into argument over the principle and Severian's supposed background.
  • Lee Berman: the child is Baldanders' real child, and Severian's denials suggest hidden homosexual or pedophilic experience
  • Jerry Friedman: Wolfe's use of catamite steers readers toward pederasty, and fantastic explanations should not be weighed first
  • Jeff Wilson: Wolfe hints at a homosexual background for Severian, and Severian could not have learned disapproval of pederasty in the Pelerine world
  • James Wynn: Occam's Razor cannot settle a Wolfe puzzle because necessity itself must be qualified
  • António Pedro Marques: Occam's Razor is not special to Wolfe and applies only in specific circumstances
  • Jack Smith: echoes Berman's catamite reading without adding a substantive argument of his own

Themes: Beasts and the animal in us, Fathers, mothers and children, Names and their meanings

2010-07-29 Lee Berman #

Argues that the child chained to Baldanders' bed is his real child, not a catamite, because the fancy setting and chains make the sexual reading unavoidable, and that Wolfe deliberately plants fantastic possibilities. Responds to Jack Smith's catamite reading by citing the word itself, the monstrous castle, and Severian's denial, and offers the VRT replacement of Dr. Marsch as a parallel case.
disputes · close-textual-reading · author-and-biography · theories: The child in Baldanders' bed is Baldanders' real child; Dr. Marsch was replaced by an aboriginal

No reason he can't be both (though Baldanders is dark haired). The thing is the word "catamite" is used. Severian is not a real person who might have meaningless random musings. This is a fictionally created person whose every word has been crafted with a purpose. As Jack suggests, we are compelled, purposefully, by the author, to think of pedophilia because that word is used.

Now if that big baby (it is a very large baby I think, not a retarded older child) had been found with a bottle and rattle in a giant crib, then perhaps we could doubt that Baldanders had sexual intentions. But this baby is adorned with fancy accoutrements and is chained to Baldanders' bed, which itself is decked out like a sybaritic 70's love nest. Severian may have doubts about the baby's catamite status, but we cannot.

Other parts of the story suggest Severian is in a constant state of denial when it comes to pedophilia. We may live in a Michael Jackson world where pedophilia jokes can be part of a comedian's monologue or a recurring character on Family Guy, but Gene Wolfe didn't. The Pelerines mention toleration of it, but it is a very sore subject for Severian, for some reason.

Pedophilia nicely conjoins with the ambience Wolfe builds regarding Baldanders' castle. The place is full of monstrosties, carved up human bodies experimented on and used for spare parts. The guy we had been led to believe was a dull, hulking servant has his evil masterhood graphically revealed (both Severian and we had trouble earlier believing that Baldanders was master of Dr.Talos, though the both assert it). For me the catamite simply reinforces Baldanders' monstrous persona. His real child is Dr. Talos.

I find the introduction of a rather fantastic, or unpleasantly outrageous concept then clouding it with doubt to be a recurring trope in Gene Wolfe's work. I think the best example is the suggestion that VRT has Dr. Marsch being killed and replaced by an abo. Perhaps an abo who forgets his own true identity. There are many seeds of doubt which are planted in the story to allow denial that this is what happened. Enough to spark lively debate in past years on this forum. But then it was discovered that Gene Wolfe, in a rare departure from reticence, openly acknowledges that the replacement of Dr. Marsch happened, in one of his interviews.

2010-07-29 Jeff Wilson replying to Jerry Friedman #

Proposes that Wolfe meant to hint at a homosexual background for Severian, reading the child in Baldander's bed against 1990s media sympathy for 'chicken hawks'. Responds to Jerry Friedman's remarks on the Pelerines' tolerance by asking where Severian learned that pederasty is disapproved, since visitors to the torturers rarely had a chance to absorb such moral teaching.
proposes · close-textual-reading · author-and-biography · theories: Severian was raised in a homosexual milieu

Gene Wolfe did live in a world where "chicken hawks" were occasionally treated sympathetically in the media, particularly when their exploitees turned out to be gay runaways, who easily could have had worse prospects in the drug- and mob-infested big cities they were drawn to.

Years ago I giggled about that sailing over GW's head, but it's starting to look like he meant it to suggest some homosex background.

I can see Severian not mentioning the sodomy because it's just the way things were where he was raised, and it explains the emphasis he puts on his hetero achievements, but where does he get his otherwise everyday "common" knowledge, like that people might disapprove of pederastry, or that there's a special potion that lets you relive the life of a dead person? Lots of people come to the torturers, but outside of Thecla, the circumstances don't seem to favor cultural inculation.
