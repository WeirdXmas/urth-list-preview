Key to the Universe

Urth list · 8 messages · 2010-12-17 to 2010-12-18

2010urthThe Book of the New SunLee BermanCraig BrewerDavid StockhoffJames WynnSon of WitzJeff Wilson
The thread: Readers debated the Jungle Room scene in The Book of the New Sun, especially Jonas's Alice and Theseus allusions and the Neil Armstrong reference, asking whether Urth borrows from Earth or shares its history. David Stockhoff saw Urth tracking Earth closely yet differing in the anachronistic historical figures, while Craig Brewer and James Wynn stressed the scene's effect on readers' sense of deep history. Lee Berman proposed linked universes through portals to explain Earthly material, and Son of Witz read the painting as Urth's own past. Jeff Wilson ended the thread with a manuscript-revision view of time and the Increate. The thread drifted from the scene to cosmology without reaching agreement.
  • David Stockhoff: Urth tracks Earth uncannily but borrowing from Earth shows in the anachronistic historical figures
  • Craig Brewer: the scene's pleasure is readers knowing more than characters and it builds Dying Earth history
  • James Wynn: the Armstrong reference belongs to Severian's own past, though he admits his preference clashes with his theories
  • Lee Berman: sequential universes linked by portals in a spiral or toroids carried Earth material to Urth
  • Son of Witz: the painting depicts Urth's own past from the same creation with no intervening deluge, history as a torus path
  • Jeff Wilson: the universes are book-like, revised draft by draft, with the Increate as a manuscript reviser

Themes: Stories within stories, The dying sun and the cosmos, Deep time and ruins

2010-12-17 Lee Berman #

Proposes that sequential universes form a spiral or stacked toroids linked by portals, suggesting Earth material reached Urth through them. Argues that Jonas's Alice and Theseus allusions show he has been to Earth or read true Earth books, responding to Adam Thornton's remark about missing Apollo photos.
proposes · myth-and-structure · source-and-allusion · theories: Sequential universes form stacked toroids linked by portals; Jonas has been to Earth or read Earth books

Heh, funny. Same here.

If we envision the sequential universes as an ascending spiral, or maybe stacked toroids, as Son ofa Witz suggested, it would be easy to envision a portal linking the adjacent coils or donut rings. Maybe like an Inire/Hethor mirror?

I don't think we're given enough information to know how, but it seems clear there is some way for stuff from Earth to have made it to Urth (I suppose some would argue it is simply longevity).

Some of the stuff could be just Urthly versions of Earth stuff, like the moon astronaut. But some seem too perfectly Earthian to be seen that way. I think Jonas' literary allusions are the best example. His Alice/Looking Glass quote and him supplying the Earth/Theseus ending to Severian's story "proves" to me that he (robot form) has either been to Earth or, more likely, spent significant time with true Earth books.

2010-12-18 Jeff Wilson replying to DAVID STOCKHOFF #

Argues that time in The Book of the New Sun runs mostly linearly but with loops and echoes, and that the universes it describes are book-like, revised draft by draft around a plot skeleton. Builds on David Stockhoff's remarks about the Alice allusion and the Jungle Room's anachronistic historical characters, using Wolfe's metafictional hints to support a view of the Increate as a manuscript reviser.
proposes · myth-and-structure · theories: The Whorl's universe is book-like, revised through drafts by the Increate

Remember, in the BOTNS, time proceeds only mostly linearly, with many loops and echoes backwards and possibly forewards. If we take other metafictional hints in the BOTNS, the universe(s) described in the Book are themselves book-like, proceeding from draft to draft with the best lines and tropes congealing around the plot skeleton. The Increate, outside of time, fiddles with manuscript, keeping some elements while rearranging others, and trying to work in His favored set pieces like the Flood where He can.
