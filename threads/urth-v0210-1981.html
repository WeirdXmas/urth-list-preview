PEACE: Gold hunt's end at the box in the hole

Urth list · 9 messages · 2002-10-29 to 2002-11-01

2002urthJeff VeyeraStone OxMichael Andre-DriussiRoy C. Lackey
The thread: Participants in this October-November 2002 thread argued over what the diggers unearth at the end of the Gold Hunt in Peace, a coffin or a strongbox, and whether the flat stone is a grave marker. Michael Andre-Driussi proposed a coffin and tied it to tomb-and-womb imagery. Roy C. Lackey disputed that the gold ever existed, that digging reached anything, and that the stone is a tombstone, citing the Bell Witch's unburied status. StoneOx17 pushed the coffin reading and then the theory that Weer killed Lois accidentally and buried her under a porch step, which Lackey rejected by pointing to the text's order. The thread ended in dispute without consensus.
  • Michael Andre-Driussi: the diggers unearth a coffin, the gold never existed, and the ghostly Kate meeting is real within the novel
  • StoneOx17: the object may be a coffin, and Weer laid the stone as a porch step over Lois's grave after an accidental shooting
  • Roy C. Lackey: the gold was invented by Lou Gold, digging stopped, the stone is not a tombstone, and Weer did not murder Lois
  • Jeff Veyera: Sherry's magic ring is vulgar in connotation, fitting her character
  • mantis: Weer keeps the gun against Lois's possible return (as reported by replies)

Themes: Hidden identities and parentage, Suffering and sacrifice, Ghosts, dreams and visions

2002-10-29 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Argues that the gold-hunt climax in Peace ends with the diggers unearthing a coffin rather than a strongbox, reconstructing how Weer and Lois's excavation would proceed after Lois pulls the gun. Grounds this in the author's denial that Weer found buried gold, and ties Sherry's magic ring to the tomb-and-womb imagery; it also retracts an earlier claim about the Bell Witch ghost.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: the buried container is a coffin not a strongbox; Weer does not kill Lois at the grave; the Bell Witch ghost is not really present

Ahem.

Damien Broderick voiced an opinion of many readers when he proposed to Gene Wolfe that Weer found buried gold in that expedition with Lois. Gene Wolfe said no he did not. (This was all in an issue of "The New York Review of Science Fiction" a few years back.)

So the probability wave for that possibility collapsed. I checked it off the list ("Weer's sudden fortune at age 50 due to finding hidden gold") and moved on.

But the fact remains that the diggers seem to have hit a container of some kind: if it was not the strongbox, then the next most likely item would be a coffin. In order to be sure that it was not a strongbox with gold, they had to excavate enough to determine its size.

Yet it is a tad difficult to imagine them doing further excavation after Lois has pulled the gun on Weer. Whatever happens, it is not in the spirit of easy cooperation. So maybe she tells him to keep digging, and he does, and then they figure out it is a coffin. Then he gets the gun from her. (I don't think he kills her, even though there is a ready-dug grave right there.)

Weer confronts Lou Gold, then Sherry makes her visit. Sherry speaks of her "magic ring," and aside from the link to the title of this message (the spooky link between tomb and womb), what she describes can also be termed a ring of gold.

On another topic: I wrote before that the Bell Witch under Margaret Lorn's porch was real. That was rash of me, and I take it back: what I meant was that if there is a mystery or action in PEACE which could be solved by a ghostly presence, then it =is= solved that way. The "witch under the porch step" thing is there to establish that ghosts can be pinned down (see also Gold's Necronomicon; elm trees planted on graves); the ghost itself plays no role, and thus probably isn't there.

=mantis=

2002-10-30 Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that the gold in Peace never existed because Lou Gold invented the hunt, and that the diggers never hit a strongbox or coffin. Responds to mantis by quoting the shovel-ring passage, citing the dry creek bed, wooden coffins, and the gun-pointing scene to show digging stopped.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Lou Gold invented the buried gold story; digging stopped when Lois pulled the gun

In the further cause of pedantry, I should point out that there doesn't seem to have been a coffin, any more than there had been a strongbox. The text reads: "My shovel had struck a stone, causing the blade to ring, and in a spot of moonlight I had seen the glint of metal in Lois's hand."

The gold never existed; Lou Gold made the whole story up, including the geographical reference points they were using to locate it. People don't locate graveyards in or near creek beds, for obvious reasons. Sugar creek had water in it at the time the gold was supposedly buried. It was a dry creek bed at the time Weer and Lois dug there, but would have been subject to flooding in the wet season. It is very unlikely that anyone would bury a body where it was commonly subject to being washed out, whether the grave site was hallowed ground or a clandestine burial. Also, in the 19th century, most coffins would have been made of wood, which would not cause a shovel blade to ring when struck.

I think the digging stopped when Lois pulled the gun.

Speaking of magic rings: did Lois ever employ hers in her quest for gold? Weer was certainly hoping so, and planned to "bring a flask, and make certain we had a blanket to spread upon the ground". They were two days digging that trench, and they were there by moonlight.

-Roy

2002-10-30 Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that the stone at the end of the gold hunt in Peace is not a tombstone, since the Bell Witch was never buried and so could have had no grave marker. Grounds this on a web source about the Bell Witch and on geography, noting Robertson County, Tennessee lies far from the Lorns' farm, and asks why a murderer would mark a victim's grave.
disputes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: The stone at the hole is not a tombstone

Correct. But, according to the web site mantis found, the Bell Witch was never buried, so there never was a tombstone. Margaret was just putting Den on. Not to mention that Robertson county Tennessee was at least the entire width of Kentucky from the nearest possible location for the Lorns' farm.

Why would a murderer mark the grave of his victim?

-Roy

2002-10-30 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Argues that the gold never being found does not invalidate the Kate and Quantrill meeting Weer witnesses, since ghostly memories channelled through Kate count as real within the novel. Builds on Roy's pedantic point and Stone Ox's claim that the stone pins a ghost down, adding Sherry's ring and Lois's sexual favours as supporting detail.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · close-textual-reading · theories: Kate's ghostly memories of Quantrill are real within the novel; Lou Gold channelled Kate while forging the diary; Weer uses the stone to pin Lois's ghost down

Fair enough.

Well now you're wrong: the gold historically exists and has never been found! (I know, you mean it was never where they were digging.)

I will go further and say that even though the gold wasn't there, still the meeting between Kate and Quantrill really did happen: when Weer sees Kate and Q it is as valid as when he sees Kate and Hannah. Ghosts are valid; Kate has already been established as one of the more coherent ghosts of the novel; any ghostly memory channeled via Kate is valid.

Furthermore, this means that Lou Gold, even while "forging" the diary, was in some sense channeling Kate. Seems outrageous, I know, but then the fact that his forged Necronomicon somehow "works" is a pointer; as well as the fact that, as I've written above, Ghost Kate is already established in the text.

Roy quoted Stone Ox:

While I argue against Weer murdering Lois, still, the text is clear that ghosts can be pinned down and this is why Weer would use the stone: not to mark the grave (though it would do that as well) but to pin the ghost down so she wouldn't come after him. Like a stake through the heart of a vampire (iirc the old tradition of pinning them down rather than turning them to dust).

More follow-up: while Sherry's "magic ring" can be riddled as "ring of gold," she pointedly puts down conventional marriage where the wife stays home and does chores -- she wants a life of adventure (rather like Olivia and Lois).

On to Roy's wondering about Lois doling out sexual favors. Maybe. Seems likely (shades of Olivia and Peacock in the cave). How ugly was their parting? Seems pretty ugly. He seems to be keeping the gun against her possible return.

=mantis=

2002-10-31 Stone Ox #

Argues that Weer laid the flat stone as a porch step over Lois's grave in Peace, so only he would know it marked her burial, and that the Bell witch passage was placed deliberately to parallel Lois's story. Builds on the earlier reply by supporting the murder reading, presenting a scenario where the gun went off during a struggle and Weer then had a body and a grave-stone at hand.
supports · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Weer murdered Lois after an accidental gunshot; Flat stone was a porch step grave marker; Bell witch episode mirrors Lois's story

Mantis quotes:

That's right. If Weer laid the stone down flat (like a porch step) only he would know it marked Lois's grave. Suppose he kills her (most likely, the gun went off accidentally while they were fighting for it). He now has a body, and a grave and stone at hand. I think Wolfe very deliberately placed that section about the Bell witch and the porch step "tombstone" in the middle of Lois's story (i.e., Weer's memories of Lois elicited his memories of the Bell witch episode) because of these parallels.

-- Stone Ox

2002-11-01 Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that Weer did not murder Lois in Peace, citing the text's order: she explains why she carries the gun only after he takes it from her. Responds to Stone Ox's theory that Weer killed her accidentally and buried her under a porch-step gravestone, and builds on mantis's remark that Weer keeps the gun against her possible return.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Weer did not kill Lois accidentally

Like mantis, I don't think Weer murdered her, and the way the text reads doesn't seem to indicate that he killed her accidentally, either. She told him why she had the gun *after* he took it away from her, or so I read it. Besides, as mantis wrote the other day: "How ugly was their parting? Seems pretty ugly. He seems to be keeping the gun against her possible return." And, as *everybody* knows, guns don't work against ghosts! <g>

-Roy
