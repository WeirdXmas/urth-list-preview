Berdanis asked why Mucor seems to be the only one of her kind in The Book of the Long Sun, given that Blood wanted her genetic talent, and why no one else tried to make another after her botched manipulation. 2000-02-10 Dennis G. Berdanis #

Asks why Mucor appears to be the only one of her kind in The Book of the Long Sun, given that Blood regarded her genetic talent as something he could use. Notes that Mucor was a product of genetic manipulation gone awry and questions why no one else deliberately tried to create another.
questions · chronology-and-plot-logic

Just one simple question really. Why does Mucor seem to be the only one of her *kind*? I know she was a product of genetic manipulation gone awry, but even if it was an accident, Blood thought it was a genetic talent he could use. Why wouldn't anyone else intentionally try to create another Mucor for their use?

Dennis/Endy

2000-02-10 Jack Lyons #

Argues that Mucor's inner nature, including her emotional, intellectual and spiritual life, could not be controlled by the scalpel and test tube, so her makers failed to produce the manageable subject their project required. Answers the earlier question of why no one else created a Mucor by pointing to this failed experiment and calling her a lab rat who is not manageable.
answers · close-textual-reading · theories: Mucor's inner nature could not be engineered

Perhaps because the experiment demonstrated that while one could manipulate the physical characteristics of a person, the emotional, intellectual and spiritual aspects of that person proved to be beyond the control of the scalpel and the test tube. Mucor may be a lab rat, but I wouldn't consider her "manageable" - a trait that I assume her manipulators had in their project requirements.

I'm sure that someone can provide a theist metaphor for this. :)

RJ
