Dan'l Danehy-Oakes offered biographical support by linking France to the Belloc and Chesterton circle that Wolfe admired. He then backed away, admitting the association was hard to account for and conceding that Chesterton may not have known France, though he kept Chesterton as a Wolfe model. The thread ended without resolution, with the link left as speculation.

2002-07-29 Jennifer Van Goethem #

Points out that Anatole France's novel Thais, the basis of the opera, contains details matching the Thais figure in Wolfe's work: an actress, an innkeeper's daughter, a Nubian slave friend tortured and crucified, and fear of aging. Suggests Wolfe may have read the novel or liked the opera, while noting the historical accuracy is doubtful.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Wolfe drew on Anatole France's Thais

In Anatole France's "Thais" upon which the opera of
the same name is based are found some interesting
tidbits about our mystery girl.

Thais is an actress.
She is the daughter of innkeepers.
The inn is frequented by sailors.
Her only childhood friend, father figure and spiritual
guide, a Nubian slave, was tortured/crucified for a
crime he didn't commit.
She is deeply afraid of growing old and losing her
beauty.

The historical or biblical accuracy of this rendition
is certainly questionable, but I wouldn't put it past
Wolfe to have read novel, or be a fan of the opera.

J
