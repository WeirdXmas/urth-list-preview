Juturna and the skull

Urth list · 2 messages · 2003-07-08 to 2003-07-08

2003urthThe Book of the New SunMarc AraminiChris P

Themes: The dying sun and the cosmos, Death and resurrection, Time loops and causality

2003-07-08 Marc Aramini #

Asks whether Juturna's promise to save Severian from drowning in the past implies that the Urth Severian ends up in a different timeline or earlier cycle, and whether the skull could be his if she never rescued him. Raises the problem of multiple lifelines, including how he could marry Valeria, and whether Ash and the Green Man could be separate figures on different worlds.
questions · chronology-and-plot-logic · puzzle-solving · theories: Juturna's rescue creates a separate timeline for Severian; Ash and the Green Man are separate figures on different worlds

One thing has always bothered me about Sev's meeting with Juturna in Urth of the New Sun: she doesn't know about saving him, but indicates that now that she has been told, she will save him in the past and not allow him to drown. Does this imply that the Urth Severian is on at the conclusion of Urth of the New Sun is a different iteration, a different timeline? Has he traveled even further back into the previous cycle of existence?

If Juturna never saved him in that past timeline could the skull be his? Then how was he alive to wed Valeria? The multiple lifelines are disturbing. Is there any way that Ash and the Green Man aren't mutually exclusive? If the future Urth is dragged into a past cycle, then one planet can have the green man and the other can have master ash. It is quite clear that in some time-lines Severian is not the New Sun, right? The timelines in which he died as a child? Or is that inconsequential?

Marc Aramini

2003-07-08 Chris P #

Argues that Ash and the Green Man are only mutually exclusive past a critical point in an event-tree cosmology, with Ash coming from a branch where things go badly and vanishing when he enters branches off his world's line. Offers this as separate from whether multiple Severians exist, which the poster doubts, and builds on the Ash–Green Man talk Severian has in The Urth of the New Sun.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theology-and-religion · theories: Ash comes from a branch where things go badly; Ash vanishes when entering off-line branches of the event tree

Ash and the Green Man are only mutually exclusive beyond a certain critical point of... for lack of something better to call it, the "event tree". The discussion Severian has with him indicates some kind of "many worlds" cosmology. Ash comes from an eventual branch of the tree where things do not go well. He is able to travel back anywhere along the path from his eventual end to the beginning, but winks out of existence when he enters any branch of the tree which is not along his world's line.

I think this is independent of the question of whether there are multiple iterations of Severian (though as I said I don't think there really are).

Chris
