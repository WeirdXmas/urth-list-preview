Themes: Memory and forgetting

2011-06-02 Andrew Mason #

Disputes James Wynn's reading of the 'enormous darkness overhead' passage, arguing that Marble's recollection of the airship recalls the transport that took her to the Whorl. Cites the soldier's 'Why don't you dust it?' exchange and the question 'Where was this?' as evidence that the memory is set on Urth.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Marble's airship memory is the transport to the Whorl

I'm not sure what you understand by 'blot'. I don't think it's impossible to say that something overhead 'blots' the fields by casting a shadow on them. (Not 'blot out', since they don't become completely invisible.)

I can't read it this way. The line of soldiers, on your reading, is a long way off, so that it just looks like a speck; Marble says 'It looks so dirty'; and a soldier replies 'Why don't you dust it?' How can he, if he's so far off?

And anyway, this doesn't explain the context. Marble sees the Trivigaunti airship, and the words 'Why don't you dust it?' arise spontaneously in her mind. Gradually, she recovers the whole scene. Why? Because the airship reminds her of another great object she once saw hanging over her in the sky. While we're not told in so many words what this was, the idea that it was the transport taking her to the Whorl seems a very plausible one. That also makes sense of 'Where was this?',a pertinent question if the answer is 'On Urth', less so of it is just some location on the Whorl.
