The thread: The thread began as b sharp and thalassocrat arguing that Agia worships a subterranean being called Hypogeon, tying her to the man-ape cave and the cult. Jeff Wilson answered that the Botanic Gardens, man-apes and Nessus wall questions point to the Autarch rather than Father Inire, while Roy C. Lackey pushed back on the contrast between the Autarch's secret acts and Inire's suspected ones. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes defended obsessing over minor characters as a useful reading tool. Jeff Wilson then proposed Inire as a double agent for the sun-stoppers, and Matthew King linked the double-agency theme to Chesterton's The Man Who Was Thursday. Adam Thornton ended by shifting to The Fifth Head of Cerberus and the Short Sun, with no clear resolution.
  • thalassocrat: Agia worships a subterranean being called Hypogeon
  • b sharp: Agia's cult ties her to the jungle sorcerers; Inire may return and lead the cult
  • Jeff Wilson: the Autarch, not Inire, answers the open questions; Inire is likely a double agent
  • Roy C. Lackey: questions whether Inire really worked against the New Sun and disputes the Autarch-versus-Inire contrast
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: obsessing over characters like Inire is a valid reading tool
  • Matthew King: double agency may echo Chesterton's Man Who Was Thursday, with the Autarch as Sunday

Themes: Gnosticism, heresy and other theologies, Doubles, twins and clones, Masks, disguise and shapeshifting

2006-07-23 thalassocrat #

Proposes that Agia worships a subterranean being called Hypogeon, linking her to the man-ape cave scene and the creature the Claw disturbs there. Grounds this in her quoting "high Hypogeon" in Shadow, her religious knowledge, her "Sacrilege!" shout during the avern combat, and a speculative parallel with Jonah's account of the notules.
proposes · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: Agia worships Hypogeon, the being under the earth; Hethor tutors Agia and Agilus in devil-worship

Besides "Theoanthropos", Agia calls on another entity in Shadow: "Well, high Hypogeon help you." (Ch 18)

"Hypogeon" presumably means something like "the one under the earth", and this neatly ties in to the man-ape cave scene and the being the Claw disturbs there - my guess is that Agia worships this being, and knows about the cave because of that.

A suprising thing about Agia is that she cares about religion. She's the one who tutors Sev in the history of the Claw and the Pelerines; she understands that the Conciliator is the "master of Power" and may transcend time; she knows that a particular building is the "mensal of the monachs"; when Sev fils to die in the avern combat, she shouts, "Sacrilege!" This last reminds me of Jonah's description of the notules; used on some far distant world for ritual murders - perhaps the averns have a similar story, and Hethor is Agia's and Agilus' tutor in devil-thing-worship?

2006-07-24 Jeff Wilson replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that Inire, who may be many people in many places, is likely a double agent working for the sun-stoppers, parallel to the Autarch spying on Vodalus and the BFO trio courting both black and saintly thaumaturges. Responds to Roy C. Lackey's question whether Inire was working to prevent the New Sun, extending the earlier point Jeff Wilson made about the man-apes in Citadel.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Inire is a double agent for the sun-stoppers

If Inire is really all those other people and in all those other places, he's likely a double agent posing as one of the sun-stoppers, the same way the Autarch is also a spy for Vodalus' revolution against the Autarchy, and the BFO trio court both black thaumaturgists like Baldanders and saintly theurgists like Severian. On which side his true loyalties lie would be hazy.

proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Autarch as Sunday and Severian as Thursday in Chesterton parallel

Could this be a Lupine elaboration on the double-agency in Chesterton's nightmare "The Man who Was Thursday"?

The Autarch would be Sunday, with Severian as Thursday. I don't recall any analogues for Inire, though.

2006-07-25 Adam Thornton replying to Matthew King #

Asks how The Fifth Head of Cerberus relates to the Short Sun books, weighing three hypotheses about Blue and Green as Ste. Croix and Ste. Anne, the inhumu, and the ruins on each planet. Raises a theological question about whether shapeshifting inhumu who become what they mimic have souls and when those souls arise.
questions · puzzle-solving · theology-and-religion · theories: Blue is Ste. Croix and Green is Ste. Anne; Inhumu are the Short Sun's shapeshifting beasts; Ste. Croix and Ste. Anne are earlier universe-cycle versions of Blue and Green; Fifth Head and Short Sun share one universe and planets

So, I have just finished _The Fifth Head of Cerberus_.

SPOILERS BELOW. Stop here if you haven't read 5HC.

Still with me?

OK.

So, it seems pretty clear that Blue is Ste. Croix, Green is Ste. Anne, and that the abos are the inhumu. What I don't know is which of these three hypotheses is right:

a) Wolfe just really likes the idea of shapeshifting creatures becoming what they mimic. So he recycled the central conceit from 5HC into the Short Sun books, but other than that we aren't supposed to see any connection between the stories.
b) Ste.-Anne and Ste.-Croix are Green and Blue from an earlier universe-cycle, even farther from the eventual Incarnation and thus end of history. Me, I'm not at all sure I buy the spiral-time theory, so I don't really like this one.
c) They're the same planets in the same universe. But in that case: if SS happens after 5HC, then the ruins on Green are explicable, but why are there no signs of prior human settlement on Blue? Contrariwise, if 5HC postdates SS, then why are there no signs in 5HC of prior human settlement on either planet in 5HC? And who are the Visitors anyway?

This also raises a troubling, to me, theological point, that exists in SS too:

If the inhumu are merely what they mimic, and before the arrival of humanity they are innocent beasts, but after they bite humans they can in most essential ways *be* humans....then do they have souls? If so, when do these souls come into being?

Adam
