2003-01-21 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Argues that the Gravesian thirteen-month lunar calendar, with its tree-based month names, is already complete in Robert Graves's The White Goddess, so it need not be read into There Are Doors. Responds to a point Tami Whitehead raised about Graves and states that the poster finds no details pointing to such a system in the novel.
disputes · source-and-allusion

Ah yes: "Post coitum omne animal triste."

Tami Whitehead pointed out the Gravesean 13-month lunar calendar in THE WHITE GODDESS. Yes, precisely: the thing is there, already made, with names (based on trees like "Wild Apple") and dates and spine-tingling festivals. Part and parcel of the big Graves "White Goddess" package. And I do not detect any details pointing towards such a system in TAD.

=mantis=

2003-01-22 Roy C. Lackey #

Disputes the claim that There Are Doors uses a Gravesean lunar calendar, arguing instead that the novel gives specific Otherworld dates with a month name and numbers. Supports the point by noting such dates appear several times and that the Otherworld calendar matches ours.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: The Otherworld calendar is the same as ours

Probably because it isn't there. Specific Otherworld dates (including month name, 294, and number, 128,130) are given several times in the book. The calendar in the Otherworld is the same as ours.

-Roy
