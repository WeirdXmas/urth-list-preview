Not using Quadrifons's name

Whorl list · 1 messages · 2001-02-18 to 2001-02-18

2001whorlThe Book of the Long SunDan Rabin

Themes: Christ figures and messiahs

2001-02-18 Dan Rabin #

Argues that the Long Sun and Short Sun books echo Torah prohibitions on speaking God's name, so that Silk's supposed communication with Quadrifons would parallel Moses speaking with God face to face. Grounds this in Exodus 20:7 and the substitution of 'the Lord' for the unpronounced name, while cautioning that such Biblical correspondences are associational rather than allegorical and may not all fit together.
proposes · theology-and-religion · source-and-allusion · theories: Silk's communication with Quadrifons echoes Moses speaking with God; Silk/Horn have Mosaic coloration; Quetzal as Aaron

There are injunctions in the Torah against using the name of God, including one in the Ten Commandments (Exodus 20:7). The name, which occurs in written form throughout the Torah, is not pronounced when reading the text out loud; indeed, it is not currently known how to pronounce it. In most English translations the name is given as "the Lord", which is a translation of the Hebrew word that is substituted for the name when read aloud.

This makes sense as a manifestation of the Outsider, especially in association with an exodus! If the principal narrator of _Short Sun_ indeed communicated with Quadrifons, this would echo Moses, who spoke to God face to face.

Aside: I myself tend to view these correspondences as associational rather than allegorical. Wolfe doesn't have to write the Bible--it's already written. We spend a lot of time here trying to make all the puzzle pieces fit together, but I'm not sure they're meant to. SIlk/Horn might still have Mosaic coloration, even if he wasn't denied entrance to Blue by the Outsider, and even if he didn't have a brother as high priest...hmm... Quetzal as Aaron? Naah.

-- Dan Rabin
