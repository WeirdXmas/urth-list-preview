The thread: Participants debated whether Severian's absorbed memories of Thecla and the old Autarch color his personality, with the question of who dominates his mind at the center. John Watkins argued Thecla's memories and personality prevail over the Autarch's and earlier Autarchs', since those rulers lacked the Claw and Severian's eidetic memory, attributing the imbalance to Severian's reticence about the old Autarch. Jeff Wilson supported this, arguing only his immediate predecessor and Thecla have enough presence to act independently and that Thecla appears most because she is the only passenger memory on view, citing the ten years of adjustment by The Urth of the New Sun. Son of Witz asked whether other absorbed figures' influence had been identified, and later drifted to a side question about Thecla's father, settling on the reading that Ouen was Severian's father.

2008-10-14 John Watkins replying to Son of Witz #

Argues that Severian's Thecla-memories and personality dominate over the old Autarch's and earlier Autarchs' memories, since those rulers lacked the Claw and Severian's eidetic memory. Responds to Duncan Truter's suggestion that other absorbed figures may color Severian's memory, attributing the imbalance to Severian's reticence about the old Autarch.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Thecla's personality dominates Severian's absorbed memories

It's my impression that the Severian and Thecla personalities and memories are far more prominent. Neither the old Autarch nor his predecessors had either the Claw, which made Thecla "alive" in Severian, nor Severian's eidetic memory, so the old Autarch's memories of Autarchs past would be dim and imperfect compared to Severian's Thecla-memories.

Certainly Thecla asserts herself in Severian in ways that the others do not, or rather, certainly Severian records Thecla's moments of self-assertion in ways he does not record such moments for others. But that could be in keeping with his general reticense about the old Autarch, whose name Severian surely knew.

2008-10-14 Jeff Wilson replying to Son of Witz #

Argues that only Severian's immediate predecessor and Thecla have enough presence in his mind to act on their own, and that Thecla appears often because she is the only passenger memory on view for most of the books. Grounds this in Severian's own statements about the faint autarch memories and in the timeline, noting that by The Urth of the New Sun he has had ten years to adjust, while also suggesting that his plural speech at the Citadel hints at the collective autarchs speaking through him.
supports · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Only Thecla and the immediate predecessor are active memories

Sev specifically says that the previous autarchs' memories are present but faint, only his immediate predecessor and Thecla are "there" enough to have initiative and identity of their own. I don't think the Claw would make a big difference, since Sev is the ultimately the source of the Claw's power. If anything, the conciliatory aspect of the Claw would have more of an integrating, assimilative effect, granting peace from such an internal division.

As for more Thecla-moments being recorded, that's largely because Thecla is the only passenger memory available for 2 1/2 volumes, with the a tiny snippet of Autarch-memory at the end of Volume IV. By the time URTH begins, Sev's already had ten years of time to adjust, so there's just not as much opportunity for other such takeovers to be recorded. Although, by the time Sev returns to the Citadel, he is speaking in the plural as more than just a royal affectation, as if the collective autarchs are speaking through him.
