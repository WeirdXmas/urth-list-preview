2005-12-01 Roy C. Lackey #

Asks what Abel's purpose was in telling Leort the Green Knight story, and how Abel could have travelled from Valhalla to Camelot, given that the Wizard Knight books never relate such a journey. Raises whether Abel as a possible Eternal Champion would explain his ignorance of knightly ways and affect questions about his parentage and brother Ben.
questions · myth-and-structure · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Abel is the Green Knight; Abel is an Eternal Champion

You are, of course, quite right. And I was staring right at the title of the story and didn't realize the obvious.

But what was Able's point in telling Leort the story? And how did Able get from Valhalla to Camelot, if that's the path he took? And if that wasn't the path, when was Able ever in Camelot? It's not related in TWK. If he was some sort of Eternal Champion, why was he so ignorant about everything knightly at the beginning of his career in Mythgarthr?

And how does that possibility color questions about his parentage? To say nothing of brother Ben.

-Roy

2005-12-01 Andy Robertson replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Proposes that Abel's time as the Green Knight took place in Skai between The Knight and The Wizard, likely a task set by the Valfather to lift Gawain toward Overcyn. Grounds this in the text's own wording and admits uncertainty over whether the episode occurs in a Camelot analogue in Mythgarthr or in Midgard, quoting a description of Abel's physique.
proposes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Abel's Green Knight stint occurred in Skai; Valfather set task to uplift Gawain to Overcyn

All good questions. Abel's stint as TGK, in my opinion, took place while he was in Skai - between THE KNIGHT and THE WIZARD.

I suspect it was just one of those tasks the Valfather put his assembled valiant ones to - an attempt to uplift Gawain into the status of Overcyn.

Whether it's meant to have happened in some Mythgarthyr analog of Camelot or whether it happened in Midgard (our world / "America,") which also lies under Skai, I do not know.

Sure sounds like Abel, though

his body as brawny as any can be,
so bull-necked, big-thighed, bulky and square,
so long-legged, large-limbed, looming so tall
I can hardly tell if he were half troll,
or merely as large as living man can be --
a handsome one too; as hearty a hulk as ever rode horse. His back and chest were broad as a barrel,
but he slimmed at the waist, with a slender stomach,
and his face was well formed, with features sharp
and clean --

2005-12-01 James Wynn replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that Able's Green Knight story in Skai is meant to dissuade Leort from taking his horse and Eterne, and suggests several readings of its point. Responds to Roy C. Lackey's question about Able's purpose by noting the story's meaning is unclear and that the truth Able tells is never credible to Leort.
answers · close-textual-reading · theories: Able's Green Knight story warns Leort off his horse and Eterne; Able is an Overcyn

I agree with hartshorn that this occurred in Skai in "the Age of Legends" (to borrow a "Time Bandits" term).

What was the point? Well, that depends to some degree on what Wolfe thinks the *meaning* of the Green Knight story is. It was intended to waive Leort off from taking his horse and Eterne as Able was warning him against, right? But as is often the case with Wolfe's heroes, the truth is useless because it is never credible.

Perhaps the story only "meant" that Able was an Overcyn and should be heeded...or that unless Leort is capable of having his head removed and still keeping an appointment the following year, anything he gets from Able will be more than he can handle.

J
