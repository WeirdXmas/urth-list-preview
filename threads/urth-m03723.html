The ending of Soldier of Arete

Urth list · 7 messages · 2007-01-29 to 2007-02-03

2007urthTom FosterthalassocratCliff JudgeJosh GellerBob MillerChris PJoel and Rachel Wilhelm
The thread: Tom Foster asked for an event-by-event explanation of the ending of Soldier of Arete, including Latro's dream, Polos's wish to love Pasicrates, the chariot race, and several specific plot questions. Thalassocrat answered by pointing to an earlier archive interpretation and focused on Pasicrates' infatuation with Polos, which they found troubling, and on why Pasicrates drives Latro's depression. Bob Miller disputed whether Pasicrates ever made Latro order Polos to obey, arguing this happened earlier in the book and that Latro could not extract Polos's account. Transentient argued from a textual passage that Latro is disgusted by Pasicrates' love for Polos and that such a relationship would not fit Latro's character. The thread stayed at the level of textual disagreement and did not reach a settled reading.
  • Tom Foster: asks for a full event-by-event account of the ending and its unexplained details
  • thalassocrat: points to the archive, finds Pasicrates' infatuation with Polos troubling
  • Bob Miller: the order to obey happened earlier in the book, and Latro never learned what Polos did
  • Transentient: a textual passage shows Latro's disgust at Pasicrates' love for Polos

Themes: Unreliable narration, Memory and forgetting, Love and loss

2007-01-29 Tom Foster #

Asks for an event-by-event explanation of the ending of Soldier of Arete, focusing on Latro's dream during Pindaros's recitation, Polos's wish to love Pasicrates, and the chariot race. Lists specific questions about Pharetra, the manumission betrayal, whether Anysia is Artemis, Elata's loyalty, and Pausanias's response to the race.
questions · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Anysia is an aspect of Artemis; Latro is cured because Pasicrates is forced to forgive him

A long-time lurker writes, though I have posted several times before in the past...

I've been re-reading the first two Latro novels in preparation for 'Soldier of Sidon', and have just finished 'Arete'. I remember finding the ending confusing on first reading several years ago, and had hoped it would be a bit clearer to me this time round, having read many posts in the urth archives, and some Robert Graves and Herodotus. However, I still don't really understand what happens, event-by-event so to speak (though much of the rest of the events of the two books as much clearer this time round, mainly thanks to past posts on this list).

The point where I start to lose my way is Latro's dream whilst Pindaros is reciting poetry for him (pp334 of the hardback). What exactly happens here? Why does Polos say that he "wants to love" and "will love" Pasicrates, who has treated him badly in the past? I believe Latro is cured of his moroseness because Pasicrates is forced to forgive him, but I thought that there was more to his depression than that - isn't it to do with Pharetra and the whole betrayal thing (the manumission ceremony)?

Is Anysia an aspect of Artemis? And is Elata on Latro's side or not? I always thought that she was, but in the final pages it seems like she turns against him. And what happens during and after the chariot race? Latro loses the race deliberately, right? Yet Pausanius is still honoured and allows Latro to leave? So many questions! As you can see, I'm really lost here! I know there are some very knowledgeable people on this list, so I'm hoping someone can take the time to clarify things for me a bit before I start on 'Sidon'.

Tom

2007-01-30 thalassocrat replying to Tom Foster #

Replies to Tom Foster's confusion about the ending of Soldier of Arete by pointing him to an earlier interpretation in the archives and then raising two specific points about Polos and Pasicrates. Describes Pasicrates' infatuation with Polos and Polos's offer to become his lover, calling it troubling, and says the poster cannot see why Pasicrates causes Latro's depression.
answers · close-textual-reading

On Tue, 30 Jan 2007 04:20:44 +1100 Tom Foster

The ending is pretty confusing. I posted my interpretation a few weeks ago - should be in the archives, fwiw.

On Polos: Pasicrates is pederastically infatuated with him and has molested him at least once in the past. Polos now says that he'll try to be Pasicrates' lover if he'll just let go of his anger at Latro. This is .... icky.

On Latro's depression: I've never understood why Pasicrates is the cause of this, either.

2007-02-03 Bob Miller replying to brunians #

Questions whether Pasicrates really got Latro to order Polos to obey him in Soldier of Arete, and argues that this happened earlier in the book, not at the ending. Reminds that Latro could not get Polos to say what had happened, so he never killed Pasicrates then. Responds to brunians's remark about dreams and Pasicrates's influence on Latro at the ending.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic

Didn't Pasicrates get him to order Polos to obey him?

I don't think pederasty sits too well with Latro, somehow. Pasicrates did get Latro to order Polos to obey him, but that was much earlier than the ending and Latro would have killed Pasicrates then except he could not get Polos to actually tell him what had happened. Much trouble avoided by many and maybe much trouble created for some by a few sword strokes.
