2007-05-13 John Barach #

Complains that a forthcoming 1008-page paperback edition, apparently of a Wolfe collection, will strain its spine, and asks why not a sturdy hardback. Grounds the complaint in the listed page count and a comparison with the single-volume paperback of Jeff Smith's Bone.
chats

Amazon says it comes out October 18 in the UK and that it runs to 1008 pages. It's a paperback apparently.

What in the world is the point of a paperback that large? The spine will break or at least be badly stretched by the time you're halfway through. What we need is a one or two volume hardback with sewn sheets, something that will last for a long, long time.

John
... who has been frustrated by the fact that Jeff
Smith's Bone is printed in one volume, but only
in one huge paperback.

2007-05-13 Jesper Svedberg replying to JBarach #

Replies to JBarach's remark about the size of a paperback omnibus by noting that UK publishers often release a hardcover alongside the trade paperback. Hopes the new title drops the 'Severian of the Guild' name, comparing it to a Lord of the Rings volume titled Frodo of the Shire, and speculates that The Wizard Knight sold poorly in the UK given few reviews.
chats

[email] skrev:

There will be a hardcover too. UK publisher often publish hardcovers and trade paperbacks at the same time, where the paperbacks seem to be aimed for the international markets.

Lets just hope that the "Abandonded" modifier means that they have decided to change the name. Calling this book "Severian of the Guild" is like collecting "The Lord of the Rings" in one volume and calling it "Frodo of the Shire".

(I wonder if this means that "The Wizard Knight" have sold well in the UK. I don't think I've seen more than one UK review of tWK and no real buzz, so I got the impression that it more or less sunk like a stone.)

// Jesper
