First Exodus

The thread: Ashley Crill proposed that the Inhumi reached the Whorl in an unremembered first exodus, with Pas launching cargo before Echidna's brood killed him, and argued Silk is Typhon's son bought from embryo thieves rather than a clone. Lee Berman and Gerry Quinn objected that the cities would have remembered or that the books never hint at such an exodus, and Quinn rejected the Silk-as-clone idea too. Marc Aramini argued Silk is Typhon's son, asking why Typhon would send his own son's embryo and disputing the timing of body theft. Quinn later offered a scouting-exodus variant and questioned Aramini's claim that Typhon would choose a biological son, with no clear resolution.
  • Ashley Crill: First Exodus of Inhumi from the Whorl, Pas killed by Echidna; Silk is Typhon's son bought from black-market thieves

2011-02-09 Ashley Crill #

Proposes that the Inhumi reached the Whorl during a first exodus in which Pas launched cargo until Echidna and her brood murdered him to keep their status. Grounds the idea in Pas's prepared embryos for rebodying, and in a Neighbors-arranged stowaway on a returning lander that could only depart without humans aboard.
proposes · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Echidna and her brood murdered Pas to halt the exodus; Inhumi arrived on the Whorl as stowaways on a returning lander; Pas planned to rebody himself as Typhon in embryos

Here's a theory on how Inhumi arrived in the whorl.
When the Whorl first arrived in the Blue/Green system, Pas put his plan into motion and the initial launchers full of cargo launched from the Whorl. Echidna and her brood panicked at this point, because they didn't want to lose their status and worshipers. They didn't think that they would be able to travel to Blue/Green and become deities there. They then murdered Pas, halting the exodus and keeping the rest of the cargo on the Whorl.

Pas wasn't worried about making the transition to Blue/Green because he had a number of embryos prepared to serve as potential hosts that he could download himself into and rule the new Whorls as a rebodied Typhon. Its possible that he didn't share this part of his plan with Echidna and the kids because he wanted to leave them behind on the Whorl. Its also possible that he had embryos prepared for each of the gods and goddesses but they still balked at leaving the Whorl. The Inhumi encountered humans during this first Exodus, probably on Green, and the Neighbors arranged for an inhumi to stow away on a returning lander. The lander would have returned to the Whorl because there were no humans on board. If humans had been on board, the lander would have refused to depart. Silk's exodus was a second exodus from the Whorl.
Apologies again if this idea has already been floated by someone else. I've been trying to read all of the relevant threads but you are a prolific lot of posters.

2011-02-09 Ashley Crill replying to Ashley Crill #

Proposes several reasons a first Exodus could have gone unremembered, including a scouting party launched from Viron or Mainframe, and suggests the real Patera Quetzal was sent and later impersonated by an inhumi. Answers Ashley Crill's objection about collective memory, and separately argues Silk is Typhon's son bought from black-market embryo thieves rather than a clone, citing Tussah's payment.
proposes · puzzle-solving · theories: Real Patera Quetzal sent on a scouting mission; Inhumi impersonated Quetzal using downloaded persona; Silk is Typhon's son, not a clone; Tussah bought embryo from black market thieves

There are a few possible reasons why nobody would remember a first Exodus: - It could have been launched from other cities in the Whorl, not from Viron. - it could have been launched from Viron, and the Calde knew about it but the general population was unaware.- It could have been a small scouting party.- It could have been launched from Mainframe, with representatives from different cities including Viron. As far as learning that Green is dangerous and Blue is good, nobody on the whorl would have learned that because the landers refuse to return while people are on board. If a scouting party was launched, nobody would have known their fate. A thought just occurred to me - Perhaps the real Patera Quetzal was sent as the Chapter's representative on such a mission. An inhumi on Green could have learned to imitate him (downloaded his digital persona via blood) and then returned to the Whorl as the only 'survivor' from the scouting party. The councillors and His Cognizance are aware that Pas is dead. Perhaps they kept knowledge of a scouting mission secret because they feared retribution by Echidna and her brood. I'm not sure if The Crew were aware of any prior launches to Blue/Green or not. I can't remember evidence for or against it. Also, I don't think that Silk is a clone at all. He is Typhon's son. Tussah probably bought embryo from black market traders who had ransacked the embryo chambers in the tunnels. Perhaps he even paid them to go down and do the ransacking. Perhaps the 'large sum' that he supposedly paid for the embryo was actual a batch of cards used to repair a lander.

2011-02-09 Lee Berman replying to Ashley Crill #

Argues that a first exodus from the Whorl would not need to be remembered by the cities, but that Wolfe should have foreshadowed it, citing the Chekhov's gun principle. Objects that Quetzal seems to have lived in the Whorl longer than a human lifespan, and that Inhumi appearing at the journey's end undercuts the mythic-religious underpinnings of the Long Sun series.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theology-and-religion · theories: Inhumi should have been foreshadowed earlier in the Long Sun series

I don't see that as a problem. Why would people remember an isolated lander leaving the Whorl? Each city seems pretty wrapped up in its own business.

Now if you are talking about *storytelling* that's different. I would expect Wolfe to make some mention of a first exodus if it happened. To reverse Chekhov's Law, if a gun goes off in Act III it must appear on the wall in Act 1.

My problem is that Quetzal seems to have been in the Whorl for a really long time. Longer than the normal lifespan of a human being I think. But mostly, having Inhumi just sort of show up at the end of the journey of the Whorl belies the mythological/religious underpinnings of the whole Sun Series.

Evil/demons don't just show up at the end of the world. They have always been around, since the beginning, most often in disguise. I know some see nothing more than a big space drama here and they could be right- perhaps that's all Wolfe ever intended. But I and some others see deeper religious/ mythic allegory around each and every turn.

2011-02-09 Gerry Quinn replying to Ashley Crill #

Disputes Ashley Crill's theory of a first exodus of the Inhumi from the Whorl, arguing that nobody in the books remembers such an exodus or the dangers of Green. Also rejects the idea that Silk is a clone of Typhon, citing the absence of any recognized resemblance from Kypris, Echidna, Scylla, Mamelta, Rigoglio, the Crew, and the perfect-memory character, plus the implausibility of buying a clone embryo.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: Silk is a clone of Typhon; Inhumi arrived in the Whorl through a first exodus; Echidna and her brood murdered Pas to halt exodus

I haven't seen that theory before. The main difficulty, as I would see it, is that nobody remembers such a first exodus. Not the Councillors, not the Crew. Nobody knows Green is dangerous and Blue is the place to go.

As for Silk being a clone of Typhon, James is fond of that theory, but it has a major problem in that nobody familiar with both of them mentions any resemblance! Not Kypris, Echidna or Scylla. Not Mamelta or Rigoglio. Not the Crew (whom I'd expect to have much better historical data). There's also this guy with a perfect memory who meets Silk first and Typhon later, and he notices nothing either!

Also there's the problem that Tussah is alleged to have paid a large sum for Silk's embryo. However you get hold of a Typhon clone, I'm pretty sure you don't just buy it.

- Gerry Quinn

2011-02-10 Gerry Quinn replying to Marc Aramini #

Questions Marc Aramini's claim that Typhon would use an embryo of his own son as a vessel, arguing Typhon would more likely choose a strong human among colonists or sleepers, or stored embryos. Grounds the objection in Piaton as the chosen slave and in Hierax and Tartaros as evidence that a biological son would not suit Typhon's purposes.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Typhon would not possess his own son's embryo

From: Marc Aramini

But why would Typhon send an embryo of his son to occupy himself?

Why not another Piaton, if that's what he plans to do? There is no reason to believe the children of Typhon were conceived of Piaton's body, since we don't have a good concept of how long after his ascension such a procedure became necessary, only that it predated the launch of the whorl. I can't imagine a megalomaniac not switching the testes as well if conception was not done prior to his little body theft.

My point was not about the testes. My question was: if we assume that Typhon wanted to possess one or more humans on the colonised planet or planets, why would he do this to an embryo of his own flesh and blood?

In the case of Piaton, he chose his strongest slave. Why wouldn't he simply wait and choose the best suited for his purpose among the colonists or sleepers? Or, indeed, have embryos stored that would be well-suited? But there's no reason to think that a biological son of his would be ideal. Indeed, he had evidence (in the forms of Hierax and Tartaros) that it would not.

- Gerry Quinn
