2011urthThe Book of the New SunThe Book of the Long SunLee BermanGerry QuinnDavid StockhoffMarc AraminiJeff WilsonJack SmithDavid Duffy
The thread: Thread began with a Guardian article quip about Wolfe and grew into a debate over the Whorl's Inhumi and Neighbors in The Book of the Long Sun, with Marc Aramini, Lee Berman, Gerry Quinn and David Stockhoff arguing whether Neighbors could have reached the Whorl unseen and whether Inhumi were present from the start. The discussion then shifted to the orbits of Blue and Green in the Short Sun books, where Jack Smith argued equal-period orbits cannot yield the stated six-year conjunctions and Gerry Quinn proposed eccentric, horseshoe, and dark-star models. Quinn's horseshoe and eccentric proposals were disputed by Smith, Stockhoff and Jeff Wilson, who used Cruithne and the Saturnian moons as analogies. The thread ended without agreement, drifting into side points about orbital mechanics and sources.
  • Gerry Quinn: Inhumi were left behind by the visitors and the Whorl orbit explanation is viable via eccentric or dark-star orbits
  • Lee Berman: Inhumi were present on the Whorl from the start and Neighbors-as-long-conflict is the better reading
  • Jack Smith: Blue and Green cannot orbit as described; equal periods would put Green about 13 million miles away
  • Jeff Wilson: Cruithne is too small to matter, so the horseshoe objection fails; Janus and Epimetheus are the real analogue
  • David Stockhoff: the Whorl is big enough to hide people from Neighbors, and the orbital puzzle may be clarified by Fifth Head of Cerberus
  • Marc Aramini: playful questions and half-serious speculation that Wolfe retconned notules into Inhumi

Themes: Robots, androids and artificial life, The dying sun and the cosmos, Evolution and the future of humanity

2011-02-08 David Stockhoff replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that the Long Sun Whorl is a large enough ship to hide people the Neighbors might contact, and that decades is ample time for the ship's arrival and the Whorl's breakdown. Grounds this in the ship's cargo of sheep and clones, the timing of Quetzal's detection, and a comparison with Rendezvous with Rama; also asks whether Blue and Green orbit each other or the sun.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: Neighbors contacted tribes near the Whorl's ends first

The Whorl is a large place. Like Tzadkiel's Ship, it seems almost to contain a universe, not a small planet. It has a cargo full of sheep, as well as bays of clones and embryos to shepherd them.

It also has a crew or crews in the form of people like the flyers, correct? Is it unreasonable to assume that certain tribes aboard the LSW, especially those housed near its fore and aft ends and/or closer to its skin, are people the Neighbors might prefer to contact, avoiding the sheep themselves? Such people are accustomed to knowing things they do not share with the sheep, but once the Neighbors made themselves generally known in the towns there would be no going back.

The timing of the LSW's arrival seems to encompass important events: (1) detection by the Neighbors and their boarding/insertion of Quetzal (2) the beginning of the Whorl's breakdown leading to its evacuation. I don't think several decades is a long time for a large
spaceship/asteroid to orbit a sun before these things happen, in a universe where there is no Lt. Uhura to announce incoming alien starships to Captain Kirk. This isn't Rendezvous with Rama.

BTW, is there a consensus on the exact relation of the planets Blue and Green? Do they orbit one another as a single system, or do they orbit the sun on opposite sides, as do Earth and Gor?

2011-02-08 Jack Smith replying to David Stockhoff #

Asks how the planets Blue and Green move relative to each other, noting they cannot orbit each other or sit on opposite sides of the sun given their distance changes and conjunction every six years. Grounds the question in On Blue's Waters chapter 7 and the observed conjunction interval, floating orbit-shape possibilities that the poster cannot make work.
questions · chronology-and-plot-logic

"BTW, is there a consensus on the exact relation of the planets Blue and Green? Do they orbit one another as a single system, or do they orbit the sun on opposite sides, as do Earth and Gor?"

They can't orbit each other or orbit on opposite sides of the sun because Blue and Green are farther apart and then get closer at conjunction. Conjunction occurs every 6 years (OBW, Ch 7), and Blue's year seems to be about the same as a year on the Whorl.

I'm not sure how Blue and Green move to produce this conjunction. If they are about the same distance from the sun but in different orbital planes, they could at times be closer or more distant but conjunction would occur twice a year. Perhaps Blue's orbit is circular and Green's is more eliptical, resulting in the 6 year interval, but I haven't been able to figure how this would work.

2011-02-08 Gerry Quinn replying to Lee Berman #

Replies to Lee Berman by working through how the Neighbors could have reached and infiltrated the Whorl unnoticed, proposing a long secret period before the Whorl entered the system. Grounds this in Windcloud's account, the absence of Neighbor contact in The Book of the Long Sun, and the vague rumours that Lemur and others knew.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: Neighbors infiltrated the Whorl about fifty years before arrival; Crew stood by while the gods warred

From: "Lee Berman" <[email]>

How do you mean "better than boring through the hull?" Windcloud said he was on the Whorl - he doesn't say how he and his team got in.

Maybe they obtained access by way of a lander or surface infrastructure associated with one - or maybe they just bored a hole and popped up in the tunnels. All we know is they got in somehow. Given that their mission (as we know from elsewhere) was to introduce inhumi, secrecy would probably have been paramount. In particular, it seems certain that they avoided contact with the Crew (in fact, perhaps they knew nothing of the Crew).

Pas died thirty years before the events of BotNS, but it's conceivable that the Whorl was in-system for some significant time before that. Perhaps while the Gods warred, the Crew stood by rather than initiate exploration missions etc.

According to Windcloud, the Neighbours infiltrated the Whorl before it reached the system.

Fifty years seems feasible.

Lemur evidently knew nothing of the inhumis as such (though he knew one personally). Rumours of their existence were pretty vague - and they were preying on humans, while the Neighbours presumably brought their own food.

We don't have any evidence from BotLS of the Neighbours talking to anyone - it's possible Wolfe simply didn't think to put it in. Still, many people saw various mysterious visions on BotLS, and perhaps rumours of strange eight-limbed beings would have been lost among the various ghosat stories and suchlike...

- Gerry Quinn

2011-02-08 Jack Smith replying to Gerry Quinn #

Disputes Gerry Quinn's proposed orbital arrangement for the planets Blue and Green in the Short Sun books, arguing that their periods cannot match at the given distances. Calculates from Kepler-style proportionality that Green would sit about 13 million miles from Blue, while In Green's Jungles gives conjunction separation of only 105,000 miles.

2011-02-09 Gerry Quinn replying to Jack Smith #

Argues that the horseshoe-orbit idea for Blue and Green in The Book of the Long Sun is problematic, because Cruithne's orbit has no room for six-year conjunctions, and proposes instead that Blue has a near-circular orbit while Green has an elliptical one with a period of 6/7 that of Blue. Offers dark stars as another way to produce the orbital motions, responding to Jack Smith's suggestion that paired horseshoe orbits could work mechanically.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Blue and Green have circular and elliptical orbits with 6/7 period ratio; Blue and Green orbit in a horseshoe configuration

I think horseshoes are a bit problematic. If you look at Cruithne's orbit, its actual distance from Earth varies on a short scale with a period of one year. Over a longer term, the average distance varies. Since Cruithne's orbital period is one year, there's no room for meaningful six-year conjunctions.

Hence I'm inclined to prefer the simpler concept: Blue has a circular orbit similar to the Earth's, while Green has an elliptical orbit with a period 6/7 that of Blue. Ignoring precession, this can give a conjunction every six years, while they are far apart the rest of the time.

As another possibility, we could imagine that there are dark stars of some kind (black holes or dense dark matter objects, for example) present in the system - these might induce interesting orbital motions in the hadronic planets.

- Gerry Quinn

2011-02-09 Gerry Quinn replying to Jeff Wilson #

Restates a point about horseshoe orbits: two co-orbiting bodies have nearly equal speeds, so their conjunctions recur only after many orbits, a claim the poster offers as a general property of horseshoe orbits. Replies to Jeff Wilson's earlier comment on the orbital period formula and notes the dual orbit discovery of 1978 predates the internet, making analytical sources hard to find.

From: "Jeff Wilson" <[email]>

I think it's going to be a general property of horshoe orbits that the period between conjunctions will be very long compared to the orbital period. The reason being that the two objects are essentially in the same orbit, albeit going a little faster or slower at times. So their orbital speeds cannot differ much, and can only catch up or fall behind over a period of many orbits.

- Gerry Quinn
