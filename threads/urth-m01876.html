Appian's Proxy?

The thread: Rex Lycanthrosaurus asked who ruled the Phoenix Throne while Appian was away on the journey to Yesod, and how long he was gone, noting Severian's vow to reclaim the throne as Appian had. b sharp argued Appian needed neither a proxy nor a long absence, suggesting he took the test quickly using his mirror book, citing the House Absolute scene with Tzadkiel and Tzadkiel's remarks in Urth. Roy C. Lackey answered that the Autarch was away only briefly, with Inire rather than Valeria left in charge, and that the departure came shortly before Severian's birth, supporting this with Urth and Citadel passages and time-travel logistics. The thread ends with competing textual readings rather than resolution.

2006-08-20 Rex Lycanthrosaurus #

Asks who rules the Phoenix Throne while Appian, Severian's predecessor, is away journeying to Yesod, and how long he stays gone. Cites a passage in Urth of the New Sun where Severian says he would reclaim the throne as his predecessor had, asking whom Appian reclaimed it from.
questions · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic

Colleagues:

When Severian goes to Yesod, he designates his wife Valeria to be his proxy, and she is even given the title of Autarchia. Apart from Ymar, she is probably the only person in a thousand years to occupy the Phoenix Throne without first taking the alzabo-laden derivative that contains all the memories of the previous autarchs. Or is she? After all, the autarch who is Severian's predecessor (and whose name never actually appears in either tBotNS or its sequel) also undertakes the same journey. But who is it that rules in his absence? And do we have any sense how long the former honey-steward is absent from Urth? Or at what stage in his own reign he leaves? Severian waits a decade before he journeys out and does not return for forty years. But surely his predecessor is not gone for a similar amount of time.

I ask this because there's a passage in _Urth_ where Severian writes "...I would now be permitted to reclaim the Phoenix Throne, as I had in the person of my predecessor" (XXIII, 4th paragraph from the end). But who is Appian reclaiming the throne from? Who has been his acting proxy or regent?

2006-08-20 b sharp replying to Rex Lycanthrosaurus #

Argues that Appian did not need a proxy ruler or a long absence, proposing instead that he traveled to Yesod and took his test quickly using his mirror book. Supports this by citing the House Absolute scene where Appian shows Tzadkiel to Severian and says "Go if you dare," and Tzadkiel's remarks in Urth about the test.
proposes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Appian traveled to Yesod via mirror book quickly; No proxy ruler needed for Appian's absence

Regarding Appian, Rex asks:

I don't think Wolfe intends to be clear on these issues. To even try to answer them I am drawn to the scene where Severian meets Appian in the House Absolute. Severian wants to go to "the garden". Appian misunderstands and says, "I will show you the way to the Garden, though I am by no means sure you will be permitted to enter". Appian pulls out his mirror book, shows Tzadkiel to Severian and says, "Go if you dare".

The Garden..hm..more Genesis allusions? Anyway I always took this to mean that Appian used that book to travel to Yesod and take his test. No need for a proxy ruler. He could have been there and back in hours (returned in altered form, unfortunately). Anyway, in Urth, Tzadkiel makes it clear Appian's test was a boondoggle intended only to help Severian, through acquired memory, later. (though I don't see how it did help Severian). So I think there was no need for 10 years of preparation or a long voyage through space for Appian's trip. If someone has to lose his manhood, best it be done quickly as possible, yes?

-bsharp

2006-08-21 Roy C. Lackey replying to Rex Lycanthrosaurus #

Argues that the Autarch who left for Yesod was absent only briefly, with Inire left in charge rather than Valeria, and that his departure came shortly before Severian's birth. Supports this by citing the Urth of the New Sun passage naming Inire and a passage in The Citadel of the Autarch about three pages in, plus time-travel logistics.
answers · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Inire ruled Urth during the Autarch's absence; Autarch left for Yesod shortly before Severian's birth

Not so. He left Inire in charge. (URTH, 239, chapter XXXIV, 3rd paragraph)

I assume that it was also Inire.

He need not have been gone any significant amount of time at all, what with time travel involved, and apparently wasn't. He evidently went to Yesod shortly before Severian was born. See CITADEL, chapter XXIV, about three pages in. Sev was gone from Urth for forty years only because the Hierogrammates deliberately returned him to Typhon's era to establish the Conciliator legend. That, and the fact that Sev wasn't very adept at using the Corridors of Time.

-Roy
