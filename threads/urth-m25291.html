Baptism and Confirmation: Shadow of the Torturer: Chapter I

Urth list · 9 messages · 2014-08-22 to 2014-08-22

2014urthThe Book of the New SunBrad HenryLee BermanmarcobadieGerry QuinnMarc Aramini
The thread: Participants debated whether the water, death and resurrection imagery in Shadow of the Torturer Chapter I is a sacramental pattern of baptism and confirmation. Marc Aramini argued the water mixes healing and death, that Severian's moral journey must not be undercut, and questioned whether the sacramental order is Eastern Orthodox rather than Roman Catholic, while marcobadie proposed baptism by immersion and confirmation via the Autarch's coin. Brad Henry supported Aramini's Christian reading using Catechism links between flood and baptism. Gerry Quinn doubted the dark-haired corpse links to Severian, while Lee argued she is his mother; the thread shifted toward Lee's mother theory and a source interview without a clear resolution.
  • Marc Aramini: water imagery mixes healing and death, Severian's journey is redemptive, sacramental order may be Eastern Orthodox
  • marcobadie: Undine rescue is baptism by immersion and the Autarch's coin is confirmation, grounded in solidus etymology and Eastern Orthodox order
  • Brad Henry: supports Aramini, baptism into Christ's death matches Catechism and New Testament
  • Gerry Quinn: doubts the corpse is linked to Severian, notes he was arguably sacramented but later abjures that faith
  • Lee: the corpse is Severian's mother, citing Vincula cleansing, the gown, Ultan's face and necklace torture

Themes: Catholic faith and sacraments, Death and resurrection, Water, the sea and drowning

2014-08-22 Marc Aramini replying to marcobadie #

Responds to Marc Aramini's baptism reading of Severian's resurrection by arguing that water in The Shadow of the Torturer carries a syncretic mix of healing, death and renewal, linking the flood of Genesis to apocalypse. Cites Severian healing at Thrax in the water cataract with terminus est, and questions whether Vodalus's false coin and a confirmation fit the sacramental pattern.
disputes · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: Water in the Book of the New Sun is both rebirth and death

I like it, but there is and always has been a syncretic symbolism in new sun between water, healing, and death that undercuts this baptism imagery a bit. It's not just baptism, it is healing coupled with death. He heals the sick at Thrax in the water cataract stumbling three times with terminus est - water imagery is turned into both healing and death- it is the flood and the crucifixion of urth, renewal through drowning. Over and over water is rebirth. Not for abaia, I don't think, but the link between the flood of genesis and the apocalypse of eschatology. Water is always linked to healing, death, and resurrection from the very start and presages the flood of renewal. I don't think Vodalus is important enough with his false coin to warrant a confirmation before sev gets his diabolical Eucharist. Usually confirmation is fully adult - I wouldn't expect it right after the baptism unless we had an adult convert. ( Wolfe may have been, BUT ...)

2014-08-22 marcobadie #

Argues that Severian's rescue from water by the Undine in Shadow of the Torturer is a baptism by immersion, and that his taking the Autarch's coin from Vodalus is a confirmation. Grounds this in the Latin etymology of soldier from solidus, the single chrisos Severian is paid, and the later flesh of Thecla as Eucharist, read against Eastern Orthodox sacramental order.
proposes · theology-and-religion · source-and-allusion · theories: Undine's rescue is Severian's baptism; Vodalus's coin confirms Severian's allegiance to Abaia; Thecla's flesh is Severian's Eucharist

My third post at the Urth list.
Thanks you, Gerry Quinn and Lee, for your comments and observations at my first posts.

My point: the double resurrection in Chapter I of Shadow of Torturer states a connection between Severian and the the dark-haired woman, just dead.

Next.
When Severian is resurrected from water by the Undine, he is baptised in the form of a baptism by immersion, unconsciously entering the congregation of the subjects of Abaia.? In the same day, Severian is confirmed in the allegiance to Abaia when he accepts the coin with the face of the Autarch from Vodalus, this time consciously making definitive the choice the Undine made for him at the baptism.? The Confirmation administered by Vodalus makes Severian ?a "Soldier of Abaia" (Severian thinks himself a Vodalarius). Soldier is a derivation of Solidarius, Latin meaning someone who works for money. Solidare in Latin means "to pay" and the Roman soldiers were paid in "Solidi". Severian instead is paid in chrisos (only one).

Later, in the Claw of Conciliator, Severian will receive the sacrament of Eucharist in the form of the flesh of Thecla. The succession of sacraments follows the tradition of the Eastern Orthodox Church rather than the Roman Catholic Church (where Eucharist is very close to Confirmation).

For what its worth, this description is very close to both the New Testament (baptised into christ's death, etc...) understanding of baptism and the Catechism (that develops more fully the connection between the noah-flood, the arc, and baptism).

2014-08-22 Lee Berman replying to marcobadie #

Argues that the corpse Severian and Vodalus's group encounter in the Shadow of the Torturer is Severian's mother, not a random commoner, citing the Vincula cleansing parallel, the gown, the face resembling Ultan's, and the necklace torture. Responds to Gerry Quinn's doubt about the link, offering evidence such as Vodalus and Thea's motives and Cyriaca's adultery sentence.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: the corpse is Severian's mother; Severian's mother is the Contessa Carina; Ultan's face resembles the corpse's

I think Severian's flooding of the Vincula and Silkhorn's cleansing of the sewer

on Green serve a similar connective function.

Moreover the mythological cleansing of the Augean Stables was done by an oft-

cited pre-cursor to Jesus: Heracles.

I'm good with the association of this corpse with Severian's mother, who is likely

represented in Dr. Talos' play as the Contessa Carina.

I think the evidence is against this corpse being a random commoner. Why would

Vodalus and Thea risk their lives just to be able to eat the flesh and live

the memories of a peasant's mother? The long, pale gown doesn't seem very

peasantish either.

Moreover, Severian later sees her face in the face of the exultant Ultan. Plus,

the corpse's livid face might indicate she had recently been tortured to death

by Allowin's necklace (which receives an otherwise inexplicably detailed description).

Given that Cyriaca was later sentenced to strangulation for the crime of adultery, I

suspect this was also this woman's crime. If the connection of this corpse to

severian's mother is valid, it adds insight to the brief relationship between

Catherine and Ouen.
