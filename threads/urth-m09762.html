Wizard-Knight sucks, or, is there any scholarly research on it?

Urth list · 6 messages · 2009-11-09 to 2009-11-10

2009urthThe Book of the New SunLane HaygoodJosh GellerGwern BranwenJames WynnDavid Duffy
The thread: A poster asked whether any scholarly research exists on The Wizard Knight, and the thread turned into a debate over whether the book is good. Lane Haygood argued it is heavy-handed in symbolism, weaker than The Book of the New Sun, and that Abel is a poor storyteller and an unappealing character. James Wynn countered that the storytelling flaws Haygood found also appear in The Book of the New Sun, so they belong to Wolfe's method rather than to Abel. Gwern Branwen mocked the scholarship question and said the book is not that bad but less enjoyable than the earlier novels. The thread ended with Haygood softening into a mixed verdict, without any real consensus.
  • Lane Haygood: Wizard Knight is heavy-handed, imitates New Sun prose, and Abel is a poor, unlikable narrator
  • James Wynn: the flaws Haygood names also appear in New Sun, so they reflect Wolfe's method
  • Gwern Branwen: not that bad, though enjoyed less than New Sun and Fifth Head of Cerberus
  • David Duffy: referenced by Haygood in a remark about a guy in Peace (his own post is not in the cards)

Themes: Unreliable narration, Books, libraries and writing, Stories within stories

2009-11-09 James Wynn replying to Lane Haygood #

Argues that the storytelling weaknesses Lane Haygood finds in The Wizard Knight also appear in The Book of the New Sun, so the flaw belongs to Wolfe's method rather than to Abel alone. Grounds this in the poster's own reading history, comparing the two novels with The Fifth Head of Cerberus and noting his earlier view that Wolfe is a great world-builder but only a so-so writer.
supports · genre-and-craft

Well, honestly, the part about being a bad story-teller is true of Severian, as well (to the extent that it IS true). The Book of the New Sun was the first Wolfe story I'd read. I told the guy who recommended it that I thought Wolfe was a great creator of worlds but only a so-so writer. Then I read The Fifth Head of Cerberus and decided that --based on the two novels-- he was the best science fiction writer I'd ever read. But the "weaknesses" in the story-telling in TWK (if they are weaknesses) are the same as those in TBotNS.

J.

2009-11-09 Lane Haygood replying to David Duffy #

Argues that Severian's voice and prose are engaging and that he may be a liar who shades his story, though underneath he is not truly bad, while the character Abel is a brute and a bully the poster cannot identify with. Responds to David Duffy's remark about the guy in Peace by offering a mixed verdict on the Wizard Knight book, saying its Christianizing theme of subverting heathen beliefs is heavy-handed compared to Wolfe's other work.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian is a self-serving narrator who shades the facts

See, I found Severian's voice and prose engaging. He's a liar (maybe), and certainly not above shading the story to paint him in a better light than the facts might justify, but I thoroughly enjoyed reading what he wrote. I got the sense that underneath, he's not really a terrible guy.

Unlike Abel. Abel is a brute and a bully. Didn't identify with him at all. I still liked the book, even if the "subvert heathen beliefs to Christianity!" theme was a little heavy-handed versus some of Wolfe's other books. Like I said, it's not Wolfe's most subtle work, but it's a damn sight better than most books out there.

-LH
