Whorl Name and a theory...

Whorl list · 5 messages · 1998-05-15 to 1998-05-18

1998whorlThe Book of the Long SunKevin J. MaroneyDan'l Danehy-OakesMichael Straight
The thread: Dan'l Danehy Oakes proposed that Long Sun's 'whorl' marks the Whorl as an artificial realm made by the gods, with God entering prophetic writings to enlighten Silk after Typhon tried to erase memory of God. Michael Straight disputed this, arguing Typhon sought to replace Pas's memory and that Pas is the true God. The main debate then turned to the source of the Outsider's altars to unknown gods: Kevin J. Maroney pressed Pausanias and an Allah parallel, while Straight argued for St. Paul's Areopagus speech in Acts 17. Maroney ended by maintaining the Pausanias comparison as the more apt one, so the exchange stopped at impasse rather than agreement.
  • Dan'l Danehy Oakes: Whorl is an artificial realm made by the gods; the Outsider made the true World
  • Michael Straight: Typhon tried to erase Pas's memory; Pas is the true God; altars echo Acts 17
  • Kevin J. Maroney: Outsider's name parallels Pausanias's unknown gods and Allah

Themes: Gods, angels and the Increate, Gnosticism, heresy and other theologies, Myth and folklore sources

1998-05-15 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Proposes that the Long Sun books' use of "whorl" for "world" signals that the Whorl is a separate artificial realm made by the gods, while the Outsider made the true World. Suggests that Typhon and company tried to erase memory of God from the starcrossers but failed, so God entered the prophetic writings and enlightened Silk.
proposes · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: The Whorl is isolated from the World by the gods; Typhon tried to erase memory of God from starcrossers

Rostrum Rote:

-- in fact, in the language of the Long Sun, the word "whorl" has replaced the word "world" entirely; they talk about Urth as the "Short Sun Whorl", ask questions like, "What in the Whorl are you talking about?", etc.

I think there's something more going on here than meets the eye (With Wolfe? Heaven forfend!), but I'm not quite clear on _what_ it is.

A thought does occur, of a sudden, though ... the Outsider (= God, the one-and-only God of Abraham and Moses, of Jesus and Peter) made the World; the gods (= Typhon et famille) made the Whorl. The Whorl is not the World but isolated from it, a place where Typhon et alia can _be_ gods (because God is specifically _excluded_).

But they couldn't _quite_ pull it off. This (if this theory proves out -- I think it warrants a good look) is where one of the most interesting pieces of narrational twisting in all of Wolfe takes place. We are told (by whom? Can't recall!) that Typhon and company were unable to completely erase memory of _them_ from the starcrossers' brains. But they wouldn't want to do that; it's totally against Typhon's utterly self- aggrandizing style.

Rather, I suggest, they tried to erase memory of God from the starcrossers -- and were unsuccessful (as they were also unsuccessful in keeping Him out), so that He made it into the prophetic "chrasmologic" writings (as He also made it into the Whorl by enlightening the sacrificial vessel Silk).

Just a thought,

--Roach

1998-05-18 Michael Straight #

Proposes that Typhon and company tried to erase the starcrossers' memories of Typhon and replace them with Allfather Pas, to be incarnated as their divine ruler. Responds to Dan'l Danehy Oakes, arguing that erasing Pas's memory was necessary and only partly succeeded because Pas is the true God.
disputes · puzzle-solving · theology-and-religion · theories: Typhon meant to implant memories of Pas; Pas is the true God the starcrossers forgot; Silk's body was meant for Pas's incarnation

The way I read that business is that Typhon et. al. wanted to erase the starcrossers' memories of Typhon the Tyrant and replace them with memories of Allfather Pas the Omnibenificent who, when they arrived at their destination, would be incarnated into a human form (the original purpose of Silk's body?) and be their Divine Ruler.

Erasing all memory of the true God would be a necessary part of this plan and (because he *is* the true God) was not entirely successful, as you point out.

-Rostrum

1998-05-18 Kevin J. Maroney #

Argues that the only other name given for the Outsider in Wolfe's books is Allah, and notes Pausanias's account of altars to unknown gods as a likely source. Responds to Dan'l's reply equating the Outsider with the God of Abraham, Moses, Jesus and Peter, and points to a classical source behind the name.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theology-and-religion · theories: The Outsider's only other name is Allah; Wolfe drew the unknown gods altars from Pausanias

Strictly speaking, I believe the only other name we're given for the Outsider is Allah.

I was interested to discover Pausinas's description of altars to "the unknown gods" in his tourbook of 2nd-century c.e. Greece--obviously a major source for Wolfe.

1998-05-18 Michael Straight #

Argues that Wolfe's altars to unknown gods draw more directly on St. Paul's Areopagus speech in Acts 17 than on Pausanias's second-century Greek tourbook. Supports the point by quoting Acts 17:22-23 on the altar inscribed TO AN UNKNOWN GOD, while conceding Wolfe may also have read Pausanias.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Wolfe's unknown gods derive from Acts 17

Although I would not be surprised if Wolfe had read Pausinas, I think a more obvious source is St. Paul in the New Testament:

Paul then stood up in the meeting of the Areopagus and said: "Men of Athens! I see that in every way you are very religious. For as I walked around and looked carefully at your objects of worship, I even found an altar with this inscription: TO AN UNKNOWN GOD. Now what you worship as something unknown I am going to proclaim to you" (Acts 17:22-23).

-Rostrum

1998-05-18 Kevin J. Maroney #

Argues that the Outsider invoked in the standard prayers of The Book of the Long Sun works like the Unknown Gods of Pausanias, a catch-all for deities that may be forgotten or never known. Responds to a prior post's suggestion that Wolfe drew on St. Paul's Areopagus speech, offering the Pausanias parallel as the more apt comparison.
disputes · source-and-allusion · theories: Outsider modeled on Pausanias's Unknown Gods

I'd forgotten that one. But the use of the Outsider in the standard prayers in _Long Sun_ is quite similar to the Unknown Gods in Pausinas--as a catch-all for those Gods who are possibly forgotten or possibly never known but still deserve mention because, hey, they're *gods*.
