Has Anybody Read "Has Anybody Seen Junie Moon?"

2000-07-17 Alex David Groce #

Reports reading the novel and calls it entertaining but unremarkable, seeing it as a tribute to Lafferty with a militia-minded undercurrent. Asks whether he missed anything, and floats a guess that an old man with a stick and a woman with a baby hint at a Pendragon's son sent to our time, as in Susan Cooper.
questions · theories: Old man with stick and baby hint at Pendragon's son sent to our time

I read this recently, thought it was a entertaining, a nice little tribute to Lafferty with an interesting "Gene Wolfe, militia man" undercurrent, but nothing special. Did I miss anything? There were some bits about an old man with a stick ("Laffer") and a woman with a baby that I thought might be hints of the Pendragon's son being sent to our time (a la Susan Cooper) but maybe that was just my imagination.

2000-07-17 Adam Stephanides #

Adds to Alex Groce's remarks on the novel by noting it is based on a specific Lafferty story, "You Can't Go Back", which also features a small, low-flying moon. Says he cannot make sense of the old man with a stick and the woman with a baby, and calls the Pendragon's son idea plausible but unsupported by evidence.
supports · source-and-allusion · theories: Junie Moon is based on Lafferty's You Can't Go Back

I read it and posted a brief message in April of this year or thereabouts. The only thing I have to add to what you say is that it's based on a specific Lafferty story, "You Can't Go Back," which is also about a small, low-flying moon with the same or a similar name (I forget which). I couldn't make anything of the old man and the woman with the baby. Your suggestion about the Pendragon's son sounds plausible, but I don't have any evidence to back it up.

--Adam
