1999-11-16 Alice Turner #

Argues that Seawrack, the mysterious gift in The Book of the Short Sun's On Blue's Waters, is not a villain and that her missing arm is probably real. Supports the view by citing Krait's distrust, Horn's lack of longing for Seawrack, and the multi-limbed native fauna, responding to William H. Ansley's question about the arm.
supports · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Seawrack is not a villain; Seawrack's arm is really missing

[snip]

At the end of OBW, I pity Seawrack, and think no ill of her,

I'm in this camp. To start with, Seawrack was a gift from a mysterious entity, and surely we've learned to beware of such gifts. Krait's suspicion is a real clue too--in his maunderings, Horn speaks of future deeds on Green with a real love for Krait, but I don't recall anything of the sort re Seawrack. Now that Wm. points it out, I too find that arm thing odd, though I do think it's really missing. (Perhaps she can grow it back?) Especially since native fauna all seem to have multiple limbs.

-alga
