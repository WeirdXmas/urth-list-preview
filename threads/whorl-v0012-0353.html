2001-02-26 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Complains that Wolfe narrates around key events in The Book of the Short Sun, leaving many critical moments offstage, including the trial in Dorp, and that this left Return to the Whorl disappointing. Grounds the complaint in the sense that the omissions are deliberate rather than lazy, citing Wolfe's skill with dramatic scenes, and asks what purpose the technique serves. Responds to Patrick O'Leary's request for comments on the Dorp trial episode.
disputes · genre-and-craft

Only that it's yet another example of Mr Wolfe's occasionally annoying, clever-boots technique of narrating all around an event, dropping bits of it here and there, but never actually narrating it.

If you stop to think about it, how many of the critical
events of tBotSS take place offstage? The writerly, critical aspect of my personality wants to say "too damn many."

I think this is one of the reasons I found RttW disappointing, good though it is: I mentioned wanting the moon, sun, and all the stars? Well, I think that they were there, but Wolfe didn't bother to show them to us.

Aaaargh.

And it isn't laziness, and it isn't incompetent writing -- he certainly knows how to narrate a dramatic scene; he's clearly doing it on purpose. But to what purpose?

--Dan'l

2001-02-26 Patrick O'Leary #

Argues that Pig is not plausibly a godling despite the textual hints of ears and claws, since his character and behavior do not match the one known godling. Also proposes that Nettle and Seawrack return to the Whorl with Silk because they want to stay close to Horn, and that Silk will become Seawrack's mate, citing Horn's mermaid song and the Hyacinth misnaming.
proposes · close-textual-reading · allegory-and-typology · theories: Pig is a godling; Silk will become Seawrack's mate; Horn lives on within Silk

Pig as Godling:

Yes, the reference to "ears" and "claws" is probably the best evidence I've seen. Good fishing! Yet, for me, it still doesn't ring true. I guess I'd believe it if I saw Pig fulfilling the function of a godling--for aren't they just clarions for the gods of mainframe? "Leave the Whorl!" or "Stay on The whorl!" It's not a question of size. Yes, the text says they come in extra large and large sizes.
But compare the character/behavior of the one definitive godling that we know and the character of Pig--and I see a chasm unbridgeable by ears or size. Can anyone help me with this?

Seawrack's and Nettle's motivation:

Perhaps it *is* somewhat mysterious why Nettle and Seawrack choose to return to the whorl with Silk/Horn. But is it really so difficult to imagine
that they would want to remain as close as possible to whatever remnant of their lover
they could cling to? And, really, who can resist Silk? Robert Borski's summary of Seawrack's story really misses the point: Seawrack and Horn were in love. As were Nettle and Horn. Still, the way I read the ending is: Silk will become Seawrack's mate. Remember the way Horn is haunted by the mermaid's song? And how he sometimes substitutes/misnames "Hyacinth" for "Seawrack."

Horn Lives:

Ok, Horn does indeed exist somehow in Silk at the end. Alga's comment on Wolfe's PC model of possession is very sound, I think. But I love the irony: We think we've been reading a book written by Horn denying he is Silk. And, really, it is a book written by Silk denying he is Silk.

The Trial in Dorp:

Very few comments about this episode. Any thoughts?

Patrick O'Leary

2001-02-26 Dennis G. Berdanis #

Disputes the claim that Seawrack and Horn are in love, arguing the text shows only Horn's lust and enamorment and that Seawrack's mission, preference to stay with Horn, and fondness for Horn and Babbie do not show love. Grounds the objection in a short list of what the text says about Seawrack and admits a rereading might change the view.
disputes · close-textual-reading

I don't see this. I see Horn lustful and enamored towards Seawrack. The only thing I know about Seawrack from the text is that
1. See was fulfilling a mission for the Mother
2. See would prefer to stay with Horn rather than return to the Mother 3. See acted fond of Horn and Babbie

I saw no evidence that she loved Horn. But maybe I missed something. I'll look for it when I get around to rereading.

Dennis/Endy
