Urth is a colonised planet

Urth list · 12 messages · 2017-09-29 to 2017-09-30

2017urthThe Book of the New SunCharles GillinghamMarc AraminiAb de VosEric BourlandDan'l Danehy-Oakes
The thread: The thread asks whether Urth in The Book of the New Sun is a colonised planet. Ab de Vos argues it is not colonised in the imperialist sense, since the autarch shows no sign of foreign rule, and notes the Old Norse meaning of 'urth' as fate. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes disputes this, citing the cacogens' guidance of the Old Autarch and linking Urth to the Norns. Eric Bourland offers no argument of his own and mostly asks about publication, while Marc Aramini drifts into the status of his Light and Shadow book rather than the text. The thread ends without resolution, having drifted off the colonisation question.
  • Ab de Vos: Urth is not colonised in the imperialist sense; no sign of foreign rule, more like expatriates
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: Urth is one of the Norns, and the cacogens may direct the Old Autarch, which suggests foreign power
  • Eric Bourland: admits he never considered the idea, offers no argument
  • Marc Aramini: reports on his second Light and Shadow volume and its publication status

Themes: Power, rulers and politics, Colonization and the native, Myth and folklore sources

2017-09-29 Ab de Vos #

Argues that Urth is not a colonized planet in the imperialist sense, since the autarch shows no sign of foreign rule, and comparing the situation to ex-pats rather than the Dutch East Indies. Also notes the Old Norse meaning of 'urth' as fate or turning, and responds to Jeffery Wilson's suggestion that Abaia's undines are colonists.
disputes · source-and-allusion · theories: Urth is not colonized in the imperialist sense

They may be colonists in the sense of random settlers in another country, like ex-pats. But not in the sense of a imperialistic colonization scheme such as in Cerberus. There is I believe no indication that the autarch is subjected to the kakogens which would have pointed to a foreign power ruling. A foreign power ruling indigenous elites is a sure sign of colonization, like the Dutch East Indies.

By the way. Yesterday I read in a piece about Lovecraft that Urth means? 'fate' and also 'turn' or 'bend' in Old Norse. The word 'weird' is derived from it. I was always thinking 'urth' was a simple bastardization of earth. Anybody knew this? It was a surprise to me. Fate and the turning of deep time.

2017-09-29 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes replying to Ab de Vos #

Argues that Urth is one of the three Norns, with the future Mars and Venus named Verthandi and another Norn in tBotNS, and that the cacogens may be directing the Old Autarch. Disputes Ab de Vos's claim that no evidence shows the autarch ruled by a foreign power, citing the cacogens' guidance of the Old Autarch in Book Two.
disputes · source-and-allusion · puzzle-solving · theories: Urth is one of the three Norns; Cacogens direct the Old Autarch

Yes, Urth is one of the three Norns - as are the names given to the future Mars and Venus in tBotNS (Verthandi and ... duh, something else...).

And there is, I think, some evidence that the cacogens are telling the Old Autarch what to do...at least, it looks that way to me in Book Two.
