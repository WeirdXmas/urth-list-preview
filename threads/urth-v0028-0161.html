1999-10-02 Greene, Carlton #

Proposes that Jolenta died from her dependence on hormones and drugs used to sustain her glamour, not from the blood bat bite. Grounds this in the claim that blood bat bites are rarely fatal, and adds that Dorcas's relationship with Jolenta may count against Dorcas being Innocence, replying to Jim Henley.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Jolenta died from drug dependence, not the blood bat bite; Dorcas may not be Innocence because of her relationship with Jolenta

Many thanks to Roy Lackey for the welcome and to Jim Henley for the very fine analysis. I particularly like the notion of survivor's guilt as the driving force behind Dorcas' apparent embarrassment about her second lease on life. It had always struck me as odd that she didn't dance around and see it as a chance to begin again, and I'll have to go back and investigate what troubled her so about her life before.

One related point about Jolenta: I always assumed that Jolenta died, not from the bite of the blood bat, but because she had become dependent on the hormones and other drugs used to sustain her "glamour." Having spent some time around blood bats myself, it was my understanding that their bites are rarely if ever fatal. It is for this reason, I believe, that the Cumean (or was it Merryn?) states her expectation that Jolenta would recover from the wound (and perhaps she does, but other problems kill her). It also makes Dr. Talos' actions in casting her off particularly cruel.

One final note: One reason to doubt Dorcas as Innocence may be her apparent sexual relationship with Jolenta, which Sev alludes to. Here again, as Jim suggests, I may be applying 20th cent. morality out of context -- pederasty and sexual slavery seem reasonably common practices in Sev's times.

C/
