1999whorlThe Book of the Long SunAlice TurnerMichael StraightAlex David Groce
The thread: Readers debated whether Horn's account in On Blue's Waters is factual or embellished, using the Gospels as a comparison. Alice Turner argued Horn, like Matthew, invented events out of evangelical zeal and so does not amount to fact, and asked Alex what conversion means. Alex David Groce countered that the account is inspired like a Gospel, so the Holy Spirit or the Outsider may have guaranteed its accuracy, citing Wolfe's pattern of arranged secular events and Silk's enlightenment. Michael Straight argued that leaving the question open strengthens The Book of Silk, since a skeptic can side with Dr. Crane, and noted that readers still disagree about Matthew's details. The thread ended without resolution, with the disagreement about reliability left open.
  • Alice Turner: Horn invented events out of evangelical zeal, so his account is not fact
  • Alex David Groce: Horn's account is inspired like a Gospel, so the Outsider may guarantee accuracy
  • Michael Straight: ambiguity about Horn's embellishment strengthens the novel and the Gospel parallel holds

Themes: Unreliable narration, Myth and folklore sources, Gnosticism, heresy and other theologies

1999-10-17 Alex David Groce #

Argues that Horn's account in On Blue's Waters is inspired like a Gospel, so the Holy Spirit or the Outsider may have guaranteed that he got the facts right despite his evangelical zeal. Responds to Ratty's point that Matthew's contradictions are only apparent, and grounds the claim in Wolfe's pattern of secular events seeming arranged by the Outsider and in Silk's direct enlightenment.
proposes · theology-and-religion · source-and-allusion · theories: Horn wrote with supernatural help from the Outsider; Horn's account is inspired like a Gospel; Mainframe helped Horn get events right

Ah, but Rat & I would attribute to the Holy Spirit the result that Matthew never actually _gets it wrong_--contradictions are only apparent, resulting from reading things in the wrong sense. How exactly this applies to things like "who WAS the high priest at time X?" and such I forget at the moment and is probably out of the list's scope, anyway.

Which is highly relevant to OBW/The Book of Silk if we (as I would assume we all do) think Horn & Nettle writing are meant to be analogous to writing a Gospel. In other words, sure Horn wanted to get it right out of "love and the drive of evangelism"--but if you take the model Wolfe's probably using, he really DID get it right. Which, as far as I know, just the desire doesn't guarantee. That's where we seem to differ. Given Wolfe's penchant for having "secular" things act suspiciously as if at the behest of the Outsider, this strongly suggests Horn had help (from Mainframe or something) to guarantee he got it right. On the other hand, since in Long Sun the Outsider does directly Enlighten Silk it can't be ruled out that Horn really does have direct supernatural aid in writing the Book of Silk.

1999-10-18 Michael Straight #

Argues that leaving open whether Horn is embellishing strengthens The Book of Silk, since a skeptic can side with Dr. Crane on Silk's enlightenment and the parallel to the Gospels holds. Responds to Alex David Groce's claim that Horn and Nettle's writing is meant to be like writing a Gospel, citing how readers still disagree whether Matthew fudged details.
disputes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Horn may be embellishing Silk's story; Horn and Nettle's writing parallels the Gospels

On the other hand, I think it's still possible for a skeptic to read the Long Sun books and side with Dr. Crane on the question of Silk's enlightenment. For that reason, I think it strengthens the books to leave open the question of whether Horn is embellishing or not. It makes for a stronger parallel with the Gospels. As this list illustrates it's possible for smart people to disagree about whether Matthew fudged any of the details or not.

I would prefer to assess the Book of Silk based on what we learn of the character of Horn and Nettle than to learn that Mainframe was the de facto omnicient narrator. How much fun is a Wolfe story where you know you can trust the narrator?

-Rostrum
