Themes: Women, sex and gender

1998-10-22 Alice Turner #

Answers a question about supernatural evil in Judaism by naming Lilith as the most famous Hebrew demonic figure, linked to the Sumerian Liltu. Recounts Lilith's legend of leaving Adam over sexual position and her later demonization, then links her to Babylonian Jahi from Dr. Talos's play.
answers · source-and-allusion · theology-and-religion · theories: Lilith parallels Babylonian Jahi

I've vainly been digging around to find my copy of -Hebrew Myths- by Graves and someone else--not a patch on -Greek Myths-, but still....can't find it. Anyway, the most famous (and popular too, in the feminist millennial crowd) supernatural Hebrew figure is Lilith, akin to the Sumerian Liltu, and Adam's first wife. She ditched him because he insisted on the missionary position during sex while she wanted to be on top from time to time. (This was edited out of Genesis--the rib story follows.) She was then demonized into a) the mother of a huge breed of demons b) a demon who preys on newborns. Typical male revision. Anyway, she is quite similar to Babylonian Jahi (literally Revelation's Whore of Babylon), whom we met in Dr. Talos's play.

-alga
