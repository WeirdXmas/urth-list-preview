Next Wolfe collection: title change

Urth list · 4 messages · 2003-02-19 to 2003-02-19

The thread: A short thread sharing news about Wolfe's next story collection. Nicholas Gevers posted three near-identical notes that Tor's Moshe Feder scheduled the book for June 2004 under the new title Innocents Aboard, replacing Wraiths, Waifs, and Wolves, and expressed mild dismay at the change. Richard Horton added news of a new short Wolfe story, "My Name is Nancy Wood," in Weird Tales, calling it under 2000 words and fairly minor, with confusion that lacks the rewarding Wolfean kind. No replies appear, so the thread ended without any debate.
  • Nicholas Gevers: reports the title change to Innocents Aboard with mild dismay, offering no analysis
  • Richard Horton: finds the new Weird Tales story fairly minor, its confusion not the rewarding Wolfean kind

2003-02-19 Nicholas Gevers #

Informs readers that Tor's Moshe Feder says Wolfe's next story collection is scheduled for June 2004 under a new title, Innocents Aboard, replacing Wraiths, Waifs, and Wolves. Gives the news in one brief line, with a wry expression of regret about the change.
informs

Moshe Feder at Tor indicates that GW's next collection
is scheduled for June 2004, and will now be titled,
not WRAITHS, WAIFS, AND WOLVES, but INNOCENTS ABOARD.
Oh dear.

--Nick Gevers.

2003-02-19 Richard Horton #

Informs list members of a new short Wolfe story, "My Name is Nancy Wood," in the upcoming Weird Tales, and gives a brief opinion. Describes it as under 2000 words and fairly minor, with a nurse exhumed after a suspected murder and a buried letter about her relationship with a patient, and says its confusion lacks the rewarding Wolfean kind.
informs

Incidentally, Nick, I don't recall if you've mentioned Wolfe's new story in the upcoming Weird Tales, Spring 2003. It's called "My Name is Nancy Wood". It's quite short, under 2000 words, and to my mind it's fairly minor Wolfe. It's about a woman, a nurse, exhumed because people suspect she was murdered, and a letter she wrote that was buried with her, detailing her curious relationship with a patient.

I was confused by some bits of the story, but the confusion did not, for me, have the overtones of Wolfean confusion -- that is, confusion that on further thought and rereading will be dispelled to be replaced with some form of wonder or admiration. However, maybe further thought and rereading is what is required.
