Themes: Memory and forgetting, Death and resurrection

1999-01-23 C R Culver #

Asks whether the patients in Dr. Van Ness's waiting room in Peace are memories recreated from Weer's mind or other dead people revived by the storm that resurrected Weer. Grounds this in their lifelike small talk and the narrator's detached treatment of them, which suggests he knows they are dead.
questions · close-textual-reading · theories: Waiting-room characters are recreations from Weer's memory; Waiting-room characters are other resurrected dead

How quiet this list has been lately. We have not discussed everything in the non-LS works of Wolfe, so their is not reason no one is talking.

I'm re-reading PEACE, and this time I am approaching it with the knowledge that Weer is dead. I am puzzled, however, by the other characters of the book in the office of Dr. Van Ness in the novel's early pages. I do not know whether these characters are recreations out of Weer's memory, or perhaps fellow deceased persons awaked by the storm which resurrected Weer.

The way these characters engage in small talk, as if they actually were alive and waiting to see the doctor, make me think they are resurrected just as Weer is. On the other hand, the way Wolfe is aloof from them seems to mean that he knows they are dead and are merely recreations of the townspeople he knew.

I have some more questions to raise about Weer's status, but I shall save those for after I know people are still out there <g>

Cheers,
