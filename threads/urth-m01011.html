2005-01-18 Jim Raylor #

Presents a playful fictional dialogue in which the poster's imagined Anne of Green Gables-style character, Anne of Cerberus, discusses the spelling of 'anise' and the title of The Fifth Head of Cerberus. Mirrors Wolfe's own wordplay and the Anne-as-Anne-of-Green-Gables joke through invented characters, with no argument about the text itself.
chats

Anne of Cerberus

Anne had her own Shadow Children : Katie Maurice (her
reflection in the glass of the bookless bookcase) and
Violetta (the little echo girl).

I was speaking of anise and Anne asked me how it was
spelt. I told her and she looked disappointed.
'Wouldn't it look better spelt "Annese" with an 'e'
like my name?' she suggested, a little vainly I
thought.
'No it distinctly says "anise"...' I showed her the
phrase in the book.
'Yes I know it... it is a smell and a taste... like
those sweets that I didn't like much and that odd
drink those French artists like too much... Ugh!' She
pulled a most unbecoming face, but brightened up
almost immediately...
'Let me show you some fine plants that smell of
Annese?' She begged and I did not have the heart to
correct her spelling.

'Later, child... I must continue reading this book.' I
announced at last.
'The writer must be a very happy man, now!' She
announced as she read the cover.
'Why do you say that?' I asked, frankly puzzled.
'He was sad when he was just 'Gene Wolfe, The fifth
head', but now he is Gene Wolfe the Fifth Head... OF
CERBERUS.' She clapped her hands as she positively
shouted the last two words.

'And to think we almost sent you away beacuse you were
not quite a boy!' I smiled at her.
She looked pensive and was silent for a surprisingly
long time.
'Is that what abo means?' she asked ingenuously.
