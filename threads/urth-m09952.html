The Fat Magician

Urth list · 4 messages · 2009-09-21 to 2009-09-22

2009urthAndreas MueggeDave Tallman
The thread: Andreas Muegge asked whether the fat Ernst of "The Fat Magician" survived the Nazis and what became of him, offering no argument. Dave Tallman proposed that Ernst transformed himself into the young woman Gertrun, citing the shared family name, the ending's remark about a man in a dress and werewolves, and her becoming Dr. Cooper's lover, while admitting age and appearance problems. Tallman then suggested Gertrun might be a ghost, pointing to Cooper's opening remark about a ghost story and his earlier supernatural affair in "The Nebraskan and the Neriad." Muegge questioned the evidence for the transformation and proposed that the waiter's steering of Cooper to the Romantic Hotel signals an engineered arrangement to produce an offspring. The thread ends without resolution, with the ghost and transformation readings left open.
  • Andreas Muegge: asks what became of fat Ernst and questions the transformation evidence
  • Dave Tallman: Ernst became Gertrun, and possibly Gertrun is a ghost
  • Andreas Muegge: the waiter engineered Cooper's affair to produce an offspring

Themes: Ghosts, dreams and visions, Doubles, twins and clones, Masks, disguise and shapeshifting

2009-09-21 Dave Tallman replying to Andreas Muegge #

Proposes that the fat Ernst of "The Fat Magician" did not die at Nazi hands but transformed himself into the young woman Gertrun. Grounds this on shared family name, the ending's remark about a man in a dress and werewolves, and noting that Gertrun becomes Dr. Cooper's lover, while admitting age and appearance problems.
proposes · puzzle-solving · theories: Fat Ernst transformed into Gertrun

If the obvious answer that the Nazis took him and killed him isn't good enough, there is one other possibility -- he has somehow transformed himself into the young woman, Gertrun.

There are obvious issues of age and appearance, but Gertrun is of the S___ family, and so was Ernst. There's something at the end about "What's more disgusting, a man in a dress or..." and mention of werewolves. It's barely possible. It looks like Gertrun and Dr. Cooper became lovers, though...

2009-09-22 Dave Tallman replying to Andreas Muegge #

Proposes that Gertrun in "The Fat Magician" may be a ghost, citing the narrator Cooper's opening remark that it would be a ghost story and his claim to have personally encountered it. Responds to Andreas Muegge's suggestion about Ernst's werewolf remark and family link, and supports the idea with Cooper's earlier supernatural affair in "The Nebraskan and the Neriad."
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Gertrun is a ghost

Maybe Gertrun was a ghost. In the beginning Cooper said it would be a ghost story, and that he personally encountered it. He said he would not tell the whole truth for the sake of a promise he made to an attractive woman who was kind to him. It's not the first time Cooper had a fling with a supernatural being -- there was "The Nebraskan and the Neriad."

Hi Dave,

Hmm. But how is this possibility supported? By the "werewolf" remark, which might imply that Ernst is able to change into something different? Or maybe he does not "transform" into another person but is rather linked in another way (?) to the family S_____. The grandfather was a conjuror and the picture not only showed Ernst but also another person in the background...

One more thing. I was puzzled by the fact that the waiter in the caf? recommended the Romantic Hotel for lunch. He obviously tried to lure Dr. Cooper to this place. When Dr. Cooper arrived in the hotel, he immediately felt attracted by Gertrun and they quickly became lovers. This looks like a too pleasant arrangement to ensure that an offspring is born...

-Andreas
