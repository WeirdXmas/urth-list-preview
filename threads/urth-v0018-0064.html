Hierodules and Sev and Time

Urth list · 2 messages · 1998-08-30 to 1998-08-30

1998urthThe Book of the New SunJon Camfield

Themes: Gods, angels and the Increate, Time loops and causality

1998-08-30 Jon Camfield #

Argues that the Hierodules' repeated appearances in The Urth of the New Sun reflect a gap between their perceived time and absolute time, so their 'last' meeting with Severian is not really final. Grounds this in the Tzadkiel's time-travel and the Hierodules' rescue of Severian at Apu-Punchau, answering a poster who noticed the contradiction.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: Hierodules' perceived last meeting differs from absolute time; Tzadkiel's time travel explains Hierodule reappearances

I took this (and their appearance in the time of Apu-Punchau, as well) as time perception vs. time reality. Sev met the three for his first time at Baldander's Castle, and since the Hierodules see that Sev does not know them, but they him, they believe that this will be the last time (for them) to meet with Sev. (I here refer to time relatively, not absolutely. The first time Sev remembers meeting them)

Severian then meets them for the first time, Hierodule time, on the Tzadkiel, which is travelling between times quite a bit. When the Tzadkiel returns to Urth, it is at a point in time for the Hierodules (who must have gotten separated from Severian's time-flow) in the future from their first meeting with Severian, as it is in the past history of Urth.

We see this mismatch again when the Hierodules 'rescue' Severian from his people when he is Apu-Punchau. It is a point in time in the future of the Hierodules from when they (mistakenly) perceived their last meeting with Sev to be, because it is (absolutely) a point in time before the rest of Sev's life, though further down the line in his personal timeline.

Just a bit complicated, huh?
