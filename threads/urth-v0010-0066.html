1998-05-02 Charles Dye #

Argues that Agia is not generally educated but has picked up odd historical factoids, and proposes that she heard the Conciliator's age from someone alive thousands of years ago, speculating that Hethor might be the unseen soldier who betrays the chiliarch. Responds to William H. Ansley's remark that Agia seems too well educated for her class, citing her 'thirty thousand years ago' line in Shadow, Chapter 19 and her phrasing 'thousand years' rather than 'chiliads' as evidence.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Hethor is the unseen soldier who betrays the chiliarch; Agia heard about the Conciliator from someone alive long ago

I've never seen Agia as being generally educated. Rather, she seems to have picked up a lot of odd historical facts, or factoids. Her offhand reference to the Conciliator living "say, thirty thousand years ago" (in Shadow, Chapter 19) is unusually precise for a Commonwealth citizen. And the odd phrasing "thousand years" instead of "chiliads" or "millenia" suggests to me that she heard it from someone who is not a Commonwealth citizen. Perhaps someone who was alive that long ago? I see one obvious suspect.

(Wild speculation: could Hethor have been the (unseen) soldier who betrays the chiliarch freed by Severian?)

1998-05-09 William H. Ansley #

Retracts Ansley's earlier complaint that Agia seems too educated, saying the real problem was that she looks like a mouthpiece for exposition. Accepts Charles Dye's explanation that Agia's odd facts come from Hethor, and adds that she may be showing off to Severian, which resolves his objections to the passage.
supports · close-textual-reading · allegory-and-typology · theories: Agia's facts came from Hethor; Agia shows off her knowledge to Severian

Now that I look back on what I wrote and your reply, I think I understand what really bothered me about Agia. I wasn't that I thought she was too educated, it was that she seemed to be too obviously a vehicle for exposition. It seemed to me that Wolfe was letting the seams show in this passage.

But you have answered my objections nicely already. Undoubtedly most of what Agia tells Severian she picked up from Hethor, which explains the "earthly" flavor of her facts. If I also assume that Agia wanted to show off her store of knowledge for Severian to let him know he "wasn't so smart," which seems likely enough, just about all of my problems with this passage of TBotNS disappear.

William Ansley
