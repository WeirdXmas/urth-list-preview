1998urthThe Book of the New SunTony A. West

Themes: Buildings, cities and labyrinths, Time loops and causality

1998-08-30 Tony A. West #

Asks whether the apparent meetings of Ossipago, Barbatus and Famulimus with Severian in Urth of the New Sun are two first meetings or a sign of non-linear time. Suggests that Yesod's spiral architecture may reflect a spiral model of time, contrasted with cyclical time in Hinduism and spiral time in Tantric Buddhism, and invites group responses.

Hello everyone; having perused the Archives of this discussion group, I = must say you all have shard wonderful ideas about TBotNS, quite = stirring! Without any further ado, and without making myself seem more = a fool than I already must seem (!), I have a question to pose to you = (whether or not it has already been addressed, I don't know; I wasn't = able to read all of the Archives!):

In Urth of the New Sun, Severian meets Ossipago, Barbatus and Famulimus = for what is apparently his last time, and their first. However, as much = sense as that may make, it seems to be contradicted just as Severian and = Burgundofora are about to exit the Ship, in the "hall made for crying," = when the three Hierodules appear again! This then must be their first = meeting; or were both meetings somehow the first in the apparent lack of = "linear" time? Some Eastern religions, most remarkably Tantric = Buddhism, believe in a sort of "spiral" time, whereas a religion like = Hinduism and many others believe in "cyclical" time.... might the rather = "spiral" architectural tastes of Yesod reflect a distinct alteration in = the flow of time, i.e. spiral time? Just some sophomoric ideas I wanted = to throw out, I would love to hear what you all have to say!

Confused on a small tropical island in southern Japan,

Tony West
