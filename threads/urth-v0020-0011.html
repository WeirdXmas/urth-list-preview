The thread: Alice Turner praised the 1913 Catholic Encyclopedia as a source of 19th-century scholarship and invited volunteers to transcribe it, noting its Severian entry was not yet posted. Alex David Groce and Peter Stephenson replied with jokes about checking a library copy and about Protestant readers not needing the merit of transcription. No one offered a substantive reading of Severian or his Catholic background, and the thread ended in playful banter rather than debate.

1998-10-15 Alice Turner #

Praises the 1913 Catholic Encyclopedia online as a source of 19th-century scholarship and invites volunteers to transcribe it, noting the Severian entry is not yet posted. Her interest in Severian's Catholic background is only a passing pretext; the post is mainly an appeal for volunteer help with the transcription project.
informs

I went to the Catholic Encyclopedia- http://www.knight.org/advent/ - to see more about Severian, but the article has not yet been transcribed.

Bear with me a moment while I rhapsodize about the 1913 Catholic Encyclopedia, now, lucky for us, in public domain, and in the process of being transcribed in its enormous entirety onto the web. Compared to this treasure of 19th century scholarship, the New Catholic
Encyclopedia--feh!--is a pallid thing, shorn of weirdness and wonders, and ever so politically correct.

The transcription is done entirely by volunteers. If any of you has access to a university library with the CE (the site helpfully lists locations for you) and a scanner, you will build up merit in the next life by helping them out. Not only that, they will list your name at the end of each article (they have a list of these waiting too). You can be immortal as the one to transcribe Severian!

-alga

Jokes that a Protestant reader would not need the merit that transcribing the Catholic Encyclopedia offers, a reply to Alice Turner's note about its Severian entry not yet being transcribed. Suggests Gene Wolfe and Borges would appreciate the joke, but offers no argument about the books themselves.

(Well, maybe GW would appreciate the joke. I'm sure Borges would.)
