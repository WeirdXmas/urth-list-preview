The thread: Readers debated who Quadrifons, the cherub-like figure in Wolfe's Book of the Long Sun, really is. Christopher Culver proposed he is the historical Christ, citing Horn's quasi-Eucharist and the Neighbor's altar scene, and noted that the older Severian-apotheosis theory had been dropped. 2001-02-14 Christopher Culver #

Asks who Quadrifons, the god of crossroads, really is and proposes that he is the historical Christ, the Son. Grounds this in Horn's quasi-Eucharist in Olivine's quarters and the Neighbor's altar scene in IGJ, and notes that the earlier theory of Quadrifons as apotheosized Severian from OBW has been discarded.
questions · puzzle-solving · allegory-and-typology · theories: Quadrifons is the Son, historical Christ; Quadrifons is the apotheosized Severian

Hello all,

My first post after reading RttW. I'll admit that I'm somewhat disappointed; the BotSS is no BotNS, but it's certainly much better than the BotLS. Tormented first-person accounts are really Wolfe's forte, and I missed them in the BotLS.

One of my biggest hopes was that RttW would clear up the mystery of who Quadrifons is, but I admit I'm pretty stumped. My suspicion in OBW was that he was the apotheosized Severian, who now is enjoying his own gifts of teleportation. But IGJ totally discarded that theory.

Is Quadrifons the Son, i.e. historical Christ? Being the god of crossroads, its understandable that he could also be the god of crosses. Horn's performance of a quasi-Eucharist in Olivine's quarters supports this idea.

In IGJ, Horn mentions Quadrifons "whispering in his ear" in the Whorl. I didn't catch any reference to this in RttW. What is it supposed to mean?

Reading again Horn's Eucharist scene at the Neighbor's altar in IGJ, which I believe is one of the most moving things Wolfe has ever written, I was suprised to see him mention that he "sacrificed for Olivine." I think it will be interesting to go back through the first two books and put together all the pieces now that we have RttW.

Christopher CULVER <[email]>

2001-02-17 James Jordan #

Argues that Quadrifons, the cherub-like guardian in Horn's Book of the Long Sun, is a cherub from Ezekiel and thus a sphinx, and that his garden scene reads as a full Christian liturgy with washing, scripture, sermon, communion and sacrifice. Grounds this in Ezekiel chapter 1, the Garden on Mount Olivet, and the bath, communion and reunion with Horn's father, and says Wolfe's remark that Quadrifons is an aspect of the Outsider fits the minority view that cherubim are theophanies of God.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · theology-and-religion · theories: Quadrifons is a cherub and theophany of God; Quadrifons is an aspect of the Outsider; Olivine's garden scene is a Christian liturgy

Quadrifons is a cherub. (Ezekiel chapter 1). Also, thus, a sphinx. The cherubim guard the gate of Eden, which is now the Church. Passing into the Edenic garden, we pass by the cherub Quadrifons. It is Olivine's garden, the Garden on Mount Olivet, where Jesus so often met with His disciples.
Then we have a full liturgy. We must wash ourselves, a symbol of cleansing, so Horn takes a bath, removing his old soiled clothing and receiving new ones. We have the opening of the word (the writings) and a sermon. Then we have holy communion. Also, since we are present with all the saints, living and departed, we have reflections on Horn's encounter with his father, whom he had thought was dead. Finally, moved and transformed a bit by the liturgy, Olivine is enabled to make a sacrifice of her own as a response.
Now, who or what is a Quadrifons? Well, there are two basic theories regarding the cherubim. The first is that they are mighty angels, and this is the majority read. But a minority maintains that the cherubim are theophanies of God, and not creatures. Wolfe says that Quadrifons is an aspect of the Outsider, so it seems he is using this second theory.

Patera Nutria
