Is Severian A Real Person?

Urth list · 21 messages · 2010-08-01 to 2010-08-02

2010urthThe Book of the New SunLee BermanJosh GellerDavid StockhoffJames Wynnaaron
The thread: The thread opened with Lee Berman arguing that Severian is as real as any reader, since both have authors, and reading Master Gurloes as a tragic figure drawn partly from Wolfe's family. It quickly collapsed into a personal quarrel between David Stockhoff and brunians over scruples, civility, and whether to answer hostile posts. James Wynn advised taking hostility offline, and brunians disagreed. Late posts drifted to a quotation about St Ignatius's visions and a Cumaean parallel, offered without argument. The thread ended in impasse, with the original question left largely unanswered.
  • Lee Berman: Severian is as real as the reader, since both have authors; Gurloes is a tragic figure drawn from Wolfe's family
  • David Stockhoff: Declines to answer silly questions merely because they are phrased civilly; defends his own standard of courtesy
  • brunians: Presses Stockhoff for serious answers and argues engaging hostile posters is worthwhile
  • James Wynn: Advises taking hostile exchanges offline so the other poster loses interest

Themes: Unreliable narration, Ghosts, dreams and visions, Books, libraries and writing

2010-08-01 Lee Berman #

Argues that Severian's status as a real person is no different from that of the reader, since both have an author, and reads Master Gurloes as a tragic, complex figure. Supports this by comparing the author of Severian to a vast, inhuman creator and by claiming Wolfe draws on his own family in describing Gurloes.
supports · author-and-biography · theories: Gurloes is based on Wolfe's own family

I presume the Author of you and I is to be considered of an intelligence too vast, penetrating, universal, inhuman and mysterious to ever be comprehended by us.

How true is that of the intelligence of the author of Severian?

I agree. There are places in Wolfe's work where I am sure he is referring to his own wife and children in addition to his fictional characters. The part where he describes Master Gurloes as being tragic, a complex man trying to appear simple, someone who eats too much and not often enough, seems to be one of those sort of places.
