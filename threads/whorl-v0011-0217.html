BEATING AN EIGHT-LEGGED HORSE

Whorl list · 4 messages · 2000-09-02 to 2000-09-04

2000whorlThe Book of the Short SunJohn CluteUrash, TomAlastair Reynolds
The thread: Tom Urash opened by complaining that the Short Sun covers, especially for In Green's Jungles and On Blue's Waters, were weak, and recalled choosing The Shadow of the Torturer for its Maitz cover. John Clute called the New Sun covers abominable and the Short Sun design philistine, arguing that Jim Burns's art fails to capture Wolfe's elusive world and that it resists illustration more than earlier volumes did. Alastair Reynolds asked whether Clute meant the Bruce Pennington UK paperbacks, which define his own mental picture of the series, and contrasted them with the Maitz US hardcovers. Clute narrowed his earlier claim to the US hardbacks, conceded Pennington's art may be memorable, and promised to check the editions, so the exchange ended without a real impasse.
  • Tom Urash: Short Sun covers are poor and lack soul; Maitz cover drew him to the first book
  • John Clute: New Sun covers were abominable; Short Sun art fails to render Wolfe's world
  • Alastair Reynolds: Pennington UK covers define the New Sun world; Maitz and later UK covers miss the tone
  • John Clute (clarifying): earlier claim applied only to US hardbacks; Pennington art may be memorable

Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Mirrors, pictures and art

2000-09-02 Urash, Tom #

Complains that the Short Sun covers, especially those of In Green's Jungles and On Blue's Waters, are poor, comparing the artist to white Texas blues guitarists lacking soul. Recalls buying The Shadow of the Torturer for its Maitz cover and speculates Wolfe had no say in the cover choice.
chats

Having just received IGJ I just can't help myself. The cover sucks! As does OBW's, imltho. Shadow was the first Wolfe book I ever bought: an impulse buy spurred by the Maitz cover, and, Jesus MacGod!, am I ever grateful. Now, like most you, I can't get enough. That first purchase was twenty some odd years ago, and I'd never even heard of Gene Wolfe at the time. The young man I was then, or so I surmise, wouldn't take a second glance at either of the first two volumes of Short Sun based on the covers. I live in Texas and the artist who paints the Short Sun covers reminds me of the myriad white Blues guitarists in this state who, not uncommonly, are technically quite proficient but lack that special something -- soul? -- that so many Black Blues artists seem to evoke so effortlessly. Or, like Twain is reputed to have told his wife upon her trying to shame his habitual swearing by doing a little swearing of her own, "... you have the words, honey, but you don't have the music."

It says more about me than Wolfe, but I just hate the idea that he could have been on board with the decision to use those covers. I'd rather imagine some sort of evildoings that left him no choice but capitulate in the matter.

2000-09-04 John Clute #

Argues that the New Sun covers were abominable, that the Short Sun cover design is philistine, and that Jim Burns's Short Sun art is well meant but fails to fix the book's visually elusive characters and world. Asks whether any good visual representation of Wolfe exists, offering the judgment that the Short Sun world resists illustration more than its predecessors did.
proposes

In-Reply-To: <[email]>
A couple comments:
1) I thought the _New Sun_ covers were abominable. But this made no difference.
2) I thought the _design_ of the _Short Sun_ covers was philistine, and destroyed any virtues the covers might possess. 3) I think Jim Burns usually does very well with books he illustrates, and that these covers--if the art can be abstracted from the design of the whole--are meritorious but failed attempts at doing illuminations of a text of quite extraordinary visual difficulty. None of us on this list seems really to know what any of the main characters in the book look light at any one moment. Fixing this sort of thing into a visual representation is something Jim was not able to do. He was probably the wrong kind of artist for the job, though he is unusual in the fact he actually reads the ms he's illustrating, with great care. But find the right artist. The world of _Short Sun_ is immensely less fixable than the world of its predecessors, all of which have execrable art. Is there any _good_ Gene Wolfe visual?

Best,
John C

2000-09-04 Alastair Reynolds #

Asks John Clute whether his criticism of the New Sun covers refers to the Bruce Pennington UK paperbacks, and says those covers define how he pictures the New Sun world. Contrasts them with the Don Maitz US hardcovers and later UK covers, which he feels missed the right tone.
questions

Do you refer to the Bruce Pennington covers of the New Sun books, John, as used on the UK editions (paperback at least)? It's strange because for me these absolutely define how I see the world of the New Sun. Neither the Don Maitz (US hardcover editions, I think) or the later UK covers seemed to hit the right note. Funny things, covers...

Al

Clarifies that the poster was referring only to the US hardback editions of The Book of the New Sun when making an earlier claim, and retracts the fullness of that statement. Responds to Al's comment about reprint editions and cover art, noting that Pennington's illustrations may be memorable and promising to check the editions.
answers

Al,
I was referring to the US hardbacks, which is how I encountered the Book initially. I should retract the fullness of my utterance (I was writing late at night, not my best time), as I'm not very familiar with reprint editions, and I know some of the UK art differs from the US (some I think is the same). Pennington might well have done something memorable for me--I'll give the editions a look, thanks.

Best,
