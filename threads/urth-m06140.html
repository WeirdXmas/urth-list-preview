The thread: The thread asks whether the eclipse in The Urth of the New Sun swapped the planet Aphetas with the moon, which would explain the tides and the moon's size. Marc Aramini proposes the swap and argues that the different-universe problem need not block the story, though he cites no text. Roy C. Lackey repeats the swap, citing Severian feeling watched from the moon while swimming, and notes Aramini's objection that Aphetas lies in a different universe from Lune. Jeff Wilson offers a rival reading, that the moon-like figure is Yesod's sister-world or Thea's presence, tied to the portal scene. The thread ends with no recorded resolution.

  • Jeff Wilson: the moon-like figure is Yesod's sister-world or Thea's presence, linked to the portal scene near the Ship

Themes: The dying sun and the cosmos, Doubles, twins and clones

Proposes that during the eclipse a celestial body was swapped, with the planet Aphetas exchanged for the moon, explaining the fiercer tides and the moon's apparent enlargement. Grounds this in the scene where Severian, swimming, feels watched from the moon, and says he needs to reread Urth.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Aphetas was switched with the moon at the eclipse

I have had the suspicion that some cosmic body was being switched around at the eclipse -that aphetas planet was actualy switched with the moon, producing the increasingly fierce tides and making the moon seem bigger. There is a scene when severian is simming and looks up to feel watched from the moon. Unfortunately I really need to read urth again.

2008-06-10 Roy C. Lackey replying to Marc Aramini #

Argues that at the eclipse the planet Aphetas was switched with the moon, causing the rising tides and making the moon look bigger, and cites a scene where Severian, while simming, feels watched from the moon. Replies to Marc Aramini, whose point is that Aphetas's planet lies in a different universe from Lune, so the proposal fails on that objection.

2008-06-10 Jeff Wilson replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that the moon-like figure Severian sees during the flood in The Urth of the New Sun is Yesod's sister-world or Thea's dimensional presence, linked to the portal scene near the Ship. Grounds this in the earlier portal sequence, where Severian wonders whether gravity or escaping air pulls him through, and in page references to Urth's late chapters.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Thea appears in place of the moon; Yesodis bodily portal planets for gravity

She does use of a sort of dimensional portal to deposit Severian back on the Ship, already back in Briah, and is visible to him for a while after. (URTH, late Ch 22 - early Ch 23) He records seeing her in place of the moon while he scrounges among the wreckage of the flood in way similar to that earlier appearance. (can't get Amazon's "look inside" to let me check the chapter, but it's p. 340 out of 386 in the edition they offer just now)

In the earlier sequence, Severian is unable to avoid entering the portal and speculates it could be gravity or escaping air that forces him. If it's gravity, then it's possible that the Yesodis can pipe in their gravity as needed; if it's "just" air, they still may be able to bodily portal in planets to provide gravity, though I doubt they'd remove their own planet from Yesod.
