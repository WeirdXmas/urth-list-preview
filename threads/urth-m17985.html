Themes: Myth and folklore sources

2011-01-14 Lee Berman #

Supports David Stockhoff's point that irrigation cultures passed on more mythic material than slash-and-burn cultures, adding that writing arose in river civilizations while oral traditions are less durable. Responds to Stockhoff's quoted remark about fertility, drowning and fire, and speculates that swidden tribes' mythic fire would contrast with irrigation societies.
supports · myth-and-structure

Excellent anthropological point. And so true. Oral traditions are nice, but for longevity and accuracy, they are inferior to writing systems. And writing always originated in river-based civilizations not jungle-based tribal cultures (well, there are the seaside Phoenicians but they needed to develop writing for their trade between river civilizations).

I'm not aware of studies of the mythical supremacy of fire among swidden agricultural tribes but I bet there are some interesting contrasts to irrigation-based societies.
