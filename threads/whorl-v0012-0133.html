Themes: Names and their meanings

2001-02-06 Dan Parmenter #

Argues that the Who song "Pictures of Lily" fits Silk's infatuation with Hyacinth, because "Lily" suits the Vironese naming convention and Thieves' Cant and Hyacinth is partly Kypris. Responds to Dennis's remark by extending the song's lyrics, and jokes that Oreb's version of "No Woman, No Cry" would be the perfect Vironese song.
supports · source-and-allusion · theories: Lily fits Vironese naming convention and Thieves' Cant; Hyacinth's partial Kypris nature makes the song apt

From: "Endymion9" <[email]>

Dennis, that's brilliant! And how about the rest of the song?

"She's been dead since 1929"
Oh, how I cried that night
If only I'd been born in Lily's time
It would have been alright

For me and Lily are together in my dreams
And I ask you, "Hey mister, have you ever seen"
"Pictures of Lily?"

It's almost too perfect. "Lily" fits the Vironese naming convention as well as the Thieves' Cant; and of course since Hyacinth is partly Kypris, this fits the subject of the song - falling in love with the image of a woman long-dead.

And here I thought that the perfect Vironese song would be Oreb's take on the Wailers' classic "No Woman. No Cry".

Shellac
