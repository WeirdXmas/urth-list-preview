Godling size

Whorl list · 4 messages · 2001-04-19 to 2001-04-19

2001whorlThe Book of the Long SunGeorge TerryDan'l Danehy-OakesJames Jordan
The thread: Readers asked whether low gravity could explain the unusually large godling in the Whorl, a question that came up after Ratty Rote asked whether centrifugal gravity permits such a creature. George Terry proposed that giant godlings might come from near the pole, where gravity is weaker, pointing to Pig's origin in the Mountains that Look at Mountains. James Jordan suggested gravity might drop with altitude so a huge godling could live high up, offering this tentatively. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes objected that the Whorl is too large for such an effect and that the airship crew did not moonwalk, and he compared the godling's size to the sea-dwellers of the New Sun books. The thread ended with the physics left unresolved and an open invitation for scientific readers to test the idea.
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: low gravity fails given the Whorl's size and the airship evidence; the godling is smaller than large dinosaurs
  • George Terry: giant godlings may come from near the pole where gravity is weaker
  • James Jordan: centrifugal gravity may weaken with altitude, allowing a very large godling high up

Themes: The dying sun and the cosmos, Robots, androids and artificial life

2001-04-19 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that a proposed low-gravity explanation for the large godling fails given the Whorl's size and the lack of moonwalking on the airship, while noting the godling seen is smaller than large dinosaurs. Responds to the quoted post's suggestion by reasoning from the book's physical scale and the sea-dwellers' size in the New Sun books.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic

Ratty Rote:

Given (1) the size of the _Whorl_, and (2) the fact that people don't seem to moonwalk on the airship, I'd say this won't work. OTOH, the godlings (at least, the one we see) isn't even as big as, say, an apatosaur of the larger varieties, so I don't see any inherent problem with them. The impression I get about the seadwellers in NEW is that they are more like the size of young mountains.

--Dan'l

2001-04-19 George Terry #

Proposes that the giant godlings of the Whorl could be large because they come from near the pole, where gravity may be weaker, citing Pig's origin in the Mountains that Look at Mountains. Responds to a post by Ratty Rote that asked whether the Whorl's centrifugal gravity lets the large godling exist and how high one must go for less gravity.
proposes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Godlings are large because they come from near the pole where gravity is weaker

However, Pig does state his origin as being in the Mountains that Look at Mountains. This is much closer to pole, potentially an area of decreased gravity in this system...the other godlings, having come from the pole areas may also "benefit" from this effect.

2001-04-19 James Jordan #

Proposes that the Whorl's gravity, being centrifugal, might weaken with altitude, so that a very large godling could exist at great height, and asks whether this could explain the creature. Responds to a quoted objection that the Long Sun godling defies physics without any stated explanation, offering the idea tentatively and inviting more scientific readers to test it.
proposes · theology-and-religion · theories: Whorl gravity weakens with altitude, allowing giant godlings

The gravity on the whorl is caused by centrifugal force. It is possible that the higher up you go, the less gravity there is? And how high up do you have to go? Is it possible that the large godling can exist because of this? I doubt if this is good enough, but maybe one of your more scientific types can address this.

Nutria
