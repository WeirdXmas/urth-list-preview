The thread: Newcomer Matthew Wisbey asked where the lines Ultan recites in the library about the trail of ink come from. Gerry Quinn found no quick match and suggested an obscure source or invention, while Adam Thornton and Lee Berman proposed Borges as the likely origin, with Thornton suggesting Wolfe translated it from Spanish and Berman pointing to Borges's "Mirror of Ink". Thornton also argued Ultan is meant to be Borges, and Berman argued Ultan carries traces of Wolfe himself, with Ouen blending into a master-apprentice figure. Son of Witz only welcomed the newcomer and did not answer the question. The thread never confirmed a source, so it ended unresolved.
  • Adam Thornton: quotes likely from Borges, possibly Wolfe's own translation; Ultan is meant to be Borges
  • Lee Berman: quote may allude to Borges's "Mirror of Ink"; Ultan carries traces of Wolfe himself
  • Gerry Quinn: quoted lines not found by quick search; likely an obscure source or invented by Wolfe
  • Son of Witz: welcomed newcomer Wisbey without answering the question
  • Matthew Wisbey: asked where the library quotations come from

Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Unreliable narration, Names and their meanings

Argues that quotations Wolfe used are likely to be from Borges, and that since Borges wrote in Spanish, the English wording may be Wolfe's own translation, which would explain why searches failed. Someone with a much more encyclopedic knowledge of Borges than I might be able to say whether it's a quotation or just something that sounds kinda like Borges.

2011-01-01 Adam Thornton replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that Ultan in The Book of the New Sun is meant to be Borges, citing his blindness and role as National Librarian to support identifying Nessus with future Buenos Aires, and that Cyby is Wolfe's self-insertion. Responds to Lee Berman's remark on Borges and the devil/demiurge, reading Ultan as Author-as-Demiurge and the book as a Borges-inflected Dying Earth.
proposes · puzzle-solving · author-and-biography · theories: Ultan is Borges; Nessus is future Buenos Aires; Cyby is Wolfe's Gary-Stu insertion; Ultan is Author-as-Demiurge

I'm quite sure that:

1) Ultan is meant to be (FSVO) Borges. In fact it's Ultan that convinced me that Nessus was intended to be the future Buenos Aires, because after all how many south-of-the-Equator blind National Librarians can there be?

2) Cyby is the Gary-Stu insertion of Wolfe into the story (thanks to someone--maybe Borski? Maybe someone else? at ReaderCon '98 for pointing this out to me).

3) So of course Ultan is Author-as-Demiurge, and as acknowledged mentor to our actual authorial demiurge. But then I think of BotNS, in some sense, as "What if Borges had written _The Dying Earth_?"

Adam

Argues that Ultan may carry traces of Gene Wolfe himself, pointing to Ultan's reading on the 49th floor over a rosemary bed as echoing Wolfe's age and wife Rosemary when he published The Shadow of the Torturer. Adds that Ouen's physical description matches Wolfe's appearance at that time, so master and apprentice, father and son, fuse into one figure.

I agree. Although...I think it might be more complex. Ultan mentions reading on the 49th floor, overlooking a bed of rosemary; while Wolfe published SotT at age 49 having wife Rosemary. Might be a bit of WOlfe in Ultan also.

I think this is paralleled later. I'm not the only one to find Severian's ostensible Dad, Ouen having a physical description which matches Gene WOlfe's at that time. Master and apprentice combined.
