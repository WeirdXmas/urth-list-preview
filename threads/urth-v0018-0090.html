Backwardsness

Urth list · 1 messages · 1998-09-02 to 1998-09-02

1998urthRobert Borski

Themes: Memory and forgetting, Time loops and causality

1998-09-02 Robert Borski #

Corrects a poster's title to Martin Amis's Time's Arrow and praises its reversed-life structure and Holocaust framing, then lists other works using backward time. Points to Wolfe's In Looking Glass Castle, where Daisy McKane lives backward and remembers her drowning, and suggests Mercedes and her mother in Castleview may also live backward.
informs · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion · theories: Daisy McKane lives life backwards in In Looking Glass Castle; Mercedes and her mother in Castleview live backwards

mantis having written earlier:

"I understand that Merlin is said to be living backwards in time. And this device has since been used by writers. So I don't know . . . it is certainly possible, but I think it usually manifests in certain quirky elements--people knowing their future/past but not the past/future which shaped them (I'm thinking maybe of THE ARROW OF TIME here, which I only read of in reviews, but the hero has these mysterious scars and as he gets younger he ends up in a concentration camp where the scars are inflicted/removed), etc."

Actually, I think you mean TIME'S ARROW by Martin Amis. And it's a powerful book, well worth seeking out, not only for its time-lived-backwards execution (Tod Friendly is "born" at the end of his life and "dies" when he is born), but also the concentration camp tie-in, which still blows me away. (You think the Holocaust is horrible enough already, wait'll you experience it in reverse.)

Comic-book-writer Alan Moore's "The Reversible Man" explores similar terrain.

As for Gene Wolfe, "In Looking Glass Castle" is a shorter work of his where a character lives life backwards and thus remembers the future--clearly modeled here on Lewis Carroll's White Queen, who does the same. In this case, however, it's Daisy McKane, who, although she's naturally confused, remembers her own drowning.

I also believe there is some support for the notion that the Merlin characters in CASTLEVIEW--Mercedes and her mother--may be experiencing life backwards.

Robert Borski
