Themes: Unreliable narration

2001-02-15 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Argues that the narrator of The Book of the Short Sun is a separate writer, "N", whose writing creates every Horn, and lays out a numbered timeline of Horn and Silk's journeys across Blue, Green, the Whorl and Urth. Offers this diagram in reply to Adam's claim that Horn cannot be impersonating Silk, and compares the layered narration to the VRT/John Marsch problem.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: litHorn of TBOTSS is a literary creation of narrator N; every Horn in TBOTSS is created by N's writing

If you had written "litHorn of TBOTLS is the literary creation of the narrator Horn; litHorn, of TBOTSS, is a literary creation of the narrator `N'" then I'd know we were on the same page. As it is, I sense continuing Time Frame problems.

Here, let me make a simplified diagram:

1) Horn goes out on Blue.
2) Horn goes out on Green.
3) Horn/Silk goes out on the Whorl.
4) The rajan of Gaon begins his book about steps 1, 2, and 3. 5) OBW: the narrator on Blue and on step 1-Blue (same place, different person).
6) IGJ: the narrator on Blue, step 2-Green, on Green (ditto), and on Urth. 7) RTTW: the narrator on Blue, step 3-Whorl, and on Urth.

If it helps to visualize that Escher drawing of the two hands drawing each other, then by all means do so!

Remember: just as our sense of Silk in TBOTLS is created by the writing of Horn, so our sense of all the Horns in TBOTSS is created by the writing of "N."

It is rather like the VRT/John Marsch problem.

2001-02-15 Adam Stephanides #

Argues that the Horn of the Whorl in The Book of the Short Sun resembles the Silkified Horn more than the pre-Silk Horn, since all three narrators portray him alike. Responds to Michael Andre-Driussi's claim that the Horns are created by the narrator N's writing, granting his basic theory while rejecting it because Silk's presence in the Whorl's Horn rests on other grounds.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Silk was present in the Whorl's Horn

True; but this is irrelevant to the point I was trying to make, which is that the representation of the "Horn" of the Whorl resembles any representation of Silk, or of the Silkified "Horn" of Gaon and after, more closely than it resembles any representation of Horn before he was put into Silk's body. Actually, my memory of TBOTLS isn't that great, so I'm really going by the representations of Horn and the Silkified "Horn" in TBOTSS.

It's not quite the same, because there are three different narrators who portray "Horn" in TBOTSS: the Rajan, the group narration by the editors, and Hoof (I'm not counting Daisy, since Horn is gone by then). But I'm not aware of any differences between their portrayals of the "Horn" who's in Silk's body. This actually could support your basic theory, if not your details: you could argue that since the editors only saw Horn-in-Silk after he'd been reinfused with Silk, they projected that personality onto the Horn-in-Silk of the Whorl. But I would still disagree, mainly because there are other reasons (which I've stated) for believing that Silk was present in the "Horn" of the Whorl.

--Adam
