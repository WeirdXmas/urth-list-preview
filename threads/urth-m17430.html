Themes: Humour in Wolfe, Names and their meanings

2011-01-03 Lee Berman #

Argues that the word "saltimbanque" in Wolfe's text may be a pun on "clone" but leans toward the reading of a performer or trickster, pointing to the author's name inserted into the story. Responds to Jerry Friedman's dismissal of a clown-clone pun by rejecting the image of Wolfe as an elitist divorced from popular culture and cheap humor.
disputes · source-and-allusion · author-and-biography · theories: Saltimbanque means performer or trickster in the story

Erm..are we talking about the Gene Wolfe who wears a Texas bolo tie, a Chicago moustache and quotes Roger Rabbit and enjoys Pinnochio, the Disney movie? Some here have attempted to paint Gene Wolfe as an academic elitist, divorced from pop culture and cheap humor. I think they succeed only in painting themselves that color.

Anyway, clown might be a pun on clone. But since Gene WOlfe inserts his name into the story as an author, I lean toward the "saltimbanque" interpretation as performer/trickster. With the not- so-subtle allusion to The Great Deceiver. But could be all of the above.
