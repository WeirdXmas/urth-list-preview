Violet=Plutonium?

Urth list · 5 messages · 2011-11-14 to 2011-11-14

2011urthThe Book of the Short SunDavid StockhoffJames WynnLee Berman
The thread: The thread opened as a question about Violet's nickname "Plutonium" but quickly drifted into a debate about Krait, Horn, Silk, and the Neighbor in The Book of the New Sun. James Wynn argued that Krait becomes Horn's son by acting like one and that Silk and the Neighbor are fully present together in Silk-Horn, comparing this to the Trinity. Lee Berman questioned whether the text supports a Neighbor component in Silk or Horn and pointed to the text's statements that Silk is a component of Horn. Berman also argued that "Plutonium" is an endearment linked to "heart" and "core," and defended the speculative idea that Violet might be a chem. The thread ended without resolution, with the two posters talking past each other.
  • James Wynn: Krait becomes Horn's son through acting like one, and Silk and the Neighbor are fully present together in Silk-Horn
  • Lee Berman: Questions whether the text shows a Neighbor component in Silk or Horn, and says "Plutonium" is an endearment for a reactor-powered being
  • Lee Berman: Defends David Stockhoff's idea that Violet may be a chem and urges the list to keep speculation open

2011-11-14 Lee Berman replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that the name Violet's nickname "Plutonium" in The Book of the New Sun is meant as a term of endearment, a "sweetheart" for a being powered by a nuclear reactor, since "heart" and "core" are linked. Defends David Stockhoff's idea that Violet may be a chem, urging the list to keep speculative discussion open rather than dismissing it.
supports · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Violet is a chem powered by a nuclear reactor; Plutonium is a pet name meaning sweetheart

As David demonstrates, he is not married to his theory to the point of blind rage if he is gainsaid. He tossed out an idea which eventually led to a point of interesting discussion. I think your quotes combined with David's insight suggest that the appellation "Plutonium" is meant as the equivalent to "Sweetheart" for a being powered by a nuclear reactor (especially considering a synonym for "heart" is "core"). Surely a connection is worth noting and identifying, yes?

Do we really want to discourage such disucussion? How interesting would this board be if all that was discussed was as certain and obvious as rock solid granite foundation.
