Themes: Christ figures and messiahs, Gods, angels and the Increate, Hidden identities and parentage

1997-01-16 James Jordan #

Proposes that Quetzal, the Pope-like figure in Exodus from the Long Sun, may be a converted inhumi, linking him to the inhumi named in Teasel's entry in the Dramatis Personae and to his alliance with Silk. Grounds the idea in Catholic typology of a good and bad Pope as vicar of Christ or Antichrist, and asks readers to find textual evidence of Quetzal changing his behavior.
proposes · puzzle-solving · allegory-and-typology · theories: Quetzal is a converted inhumi; Quetzal may be the serpent in the garden; The inhumi are the serpent in the garden

AN inhumi! AHA! Maybe there are more than one? Maybe Quetzal is indeed a "converted" inhumi, and thus his alliance with Silk? Maybe....

Those re-reading the quartet can do us a favor by paying close attention to this question. Is there evidence that Quetzal has altered his beliefs/behavior at some time in the past? We know from Book 4 that he is familiar with the Jesus of this universe. On the other hand, he advises that the settlers go to the wrong planet -- or so it seems (never try to guess where Wolfe will go -- maybe Quetzal's advice was right!)

Theologically, with Quetzal as "pope" of the local church situation, we are in the realm of good Catholic (RC) thought. The Pope is the vicar of Christ, and therefore a bad (serpentine) Pope is the vicar of Anti-Christ. The Protestant Reformers said that the whole Papacy, and thus all Popes, were involved with Anti-Christ, but long before then Medieval theologians had said that some Popes had been agents of Anti-Christ. --- Yet Quetzal might be a "converted" serpent. The ambiguity is certainly there in my mind at present. Can we find enough clues to decide for sure? I'll be interested to see what the re-readers find for us on this one!

Or: Is Quetzal the serpent in the garden? Or: Are the inhumi in general the serpent in the garden? The latter seems clear enough, and probably can be expanded by a theologically-sensitive reading of the quartet. (I can't wait until I find the time to do it myself!)

Jim Jordan

1997-01-17 CoxRathvon #

Asks about the inhumi in the Long Sun books, tracing Quetzal's attacks on Teasel and Mattak and noting Patera Pike's remark that devils infiltrated the Whorl. Also reports that the introductory list of gods, persons and animals in Exodus hints that Horn wrote the Book of Silk, which is the Long Sun book itself.
questions · close-textual-reading · theories: Quetzal is an inhumi who preyed on Teasel and Mattak

Thanks to everyone posting to this group! I feel I get a little insight from every message.

As I re-re-read the quartet of "Long Sun" books, I'm keeping my eye on Quetzal. It's pretty clear that he preyed on Teasel and Mattak; I can't find a hint about any other such attack, nor do I find a reference to any other inhumi aboard the Whorl--except for that revealing remark by Patera Pike that "devils" had infiltrated the Whorl against the wishes of Pas. Very mysterious comment, that.

Like a total dope I first read "Exodus" without browsing the introductory list of "Gods, Persons, and Animals Mentioned in the Text," so I missed a couple huge hints about Quetzal and the inhumi. I did accidentally bump into the reference to Horn as "the colonist who undertook to write the Book of Silk"--but I figured "The Book of Silk" would get mentioned near the end of the story; I wasn't prepared for that book to be Wolfe's "Long Sun" itself!!!

--Henry Rathvon
