Themes: Gods, angels and the Increate, Water, the sea and drowning

2009-04-06 David Stockhoff #

Argues that Wolfe's Abaia need not cause the floods his myth associates him with, and considers whether a water monster would back Severian's mission or want civilization kept intact. Responds to a quoted remark on whether the Divine's intentions were ambiguous, and to b sharp's suggestion that Idas's attempts on Severian are a set piece toward a flood. Grounds this in Lovecraft's views and the novella Silhouette.
proposes · myth-and-structure · source-and-allusion · theories: Abaia is associated with flooding but does not cause it; Abaia wants humanity enslaved but keeps technology

Abaia causes flooding; he is a magical eel.

But that doesn't mean Wolfe's Abaia causes flooding. It only means that he is associated with flooding.

However, you would think a water-dwelling monster would be a member of the pro-flooding party, so you have a point---he should support Severian's mission.

OTOH, if Abaia wants to enslave humanity AND drive it back to its star-empire peak, then he may not want the entire planet swept clean and all civilization and technology destroyed. Even if he retains his own technology, he's back to square 1 on the humanity-enslavement project. Maybe decadent technologists are more likely to get on board than people who barely know how to grow crops. Lovecraft apparently thought differently, but see the novella Silhouette for a possible Wolfean perspective.

OTOH yet again, maybe it's a matter of whether the balance of power between angels and monsters is knocked sideways. Abaia would lose the Ascians, but the Commonwealth would lose itself. Who will resist Abaia in Ushas?
