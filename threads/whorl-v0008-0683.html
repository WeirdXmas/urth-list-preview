Themes: Names and their meanings

1998-07-29 mary whalen #

Identifies the real-world meanings behind the names Elodia, Ermine and Erne in Wolfe's text, linking Elodia to the water herb Elodea and Erne to the sea eagle. Suggests Elodia's swamp association makes her name unsavory, ties Ermine's hotel to the costly fur of the ermine weasel, and links the eagle to Roman military symbolism.
informs · source-and-allusion · theories: Elodia's name meant as unsavory swamp association

This is Sean Whalen (prion).

Elodia. Probably Elodea, a genus of water herbs in the family Hydrocharitaceae. Gelada's former mistress, so the name is probably meant to be unsavory with its swamp association.

Ermine. Any weasel that gets white fur in winter, especially Mustelidae Mustela erminea. This fur is expensive. Ermine runs the most expensive hotel in Viron, which probably relates to the expensive fur of the ermine.

Erne. Also ern. Any eagle, especially the white-tailed sea eagle (Haliaeetus albicilla). Erne is a commander in the civil guard. The eagle has military associations via the Romans.
