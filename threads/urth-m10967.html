Themes: Catholic faith and sacraments

2010-08-17 Dave Tallman #

Asks whether the outdoor procession Nadan watches in "Seven American Nights" is a Good Friday or an Easter Sunday procession, since the answer shapes the story's symbolism. Supports the question with the procession's details and with Ardis's remark that the theater closes for Easter the next night, reasoning that a secular America makes Easter more likely.
questions · close-textual-reading · theology-and-religion

I had a question about the day of the week of the final day described in "Seven American Nights." The main clue is an outdoor procession Nadan supposedly witnessed on that day:

*A very old man -- I suppose a priest -- carried a cross on a long pole, using it as a staff, and almost as a crutch. A much younger one, fat and sweating, walked backwards before him swinging a smoking censor. Two robed boys carrying large candles proceeded them, and they were followed by more robed children, singing, who fought with nudges and pinches when they felt the fat man was not watching them.*
*
*
*...the entire procession, from the flickering candles in clear sunshine, to the dead leader lifted up, to his inattentive, bickering followers behind -- seemed to me to incarnate the philosophy and the dilemma of these people... I realized that its ritualized plea for life renewed was more foreign to them than to me.*

Borksi says this is a stations of the cross procession for Good Friday. Others think it was an Easter Sunday procession. I'm not sure, and I'm not familiar enough with Catholic ritual to tell the difference. It matters for the symbolism of the story.

We also have the statement by Ardis, "Tomorrow night the theater will be closed for Easter, and you can take me to a party." She said that the day before the procession. It's barely possible the theater would be closed for Good Friday and she would still say the same thing, but the fallen America doesn't seem that religious.

A theater could be closed "for Easter" on a Saturday. That would make it a Good Friday procession. And Saturdays are party nights.
