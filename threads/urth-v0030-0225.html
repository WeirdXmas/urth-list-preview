2001-01-04 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that the confusing sequences in Castleview are glamours, illusions faeries project so that mortals see what they expect, which makes the book straightforward. Grounds this on a reread, and says only the question of whether certain characters are mortal or fay remains open, asking what the problem is.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Castleview's confusing sequences are faerie glamours

Okay... Having now reread CASTLEVIEW under somewhat better circumstances (i.e., at home, not lacking massive quantities of sleep, etc.), I'm still puzzled by the puzzlement. In some ways, this seems to be one of the most straightforward things GW has written -- an account of some mortals caught up in the affairs of Faerie, one of whom turns out to be the brother of the Faerie Queen. Most of the "Huh? What's going on here" sequences strike me as (in retrospect)
glamours -- mortals facing faeries see a combination of what they expect and what the faeries want them to see. The only major puzzles I think left by the book's ending are whether certain characters are mortal or fay.

So what's the problem here?
