Brother Cook and Brother Porter: Robots or Crappy Torturers?

Urth list · 10 messages · 2010-07-03 to 2010-07-04

2010urthThe Book of the New SunJerry FriedmanLee BermanAntónio Pedro MarquesDavid StockhoffJeff Wilson
The thread: Readers asked whether Brother Cook and Brother Porter in The Shadow of the Torturer are robots or simply poor torturers, and whether they ever became journeymen. Lee Berman proposed they were fully trained torturers ranking above journeymen, while Jeff Wilson argued from Severian's account that they declined the fuligin and stayed to serve the Order, and floated midshipman-style ranks. David Stockhoff suggested they were ordinary full brothers who merely directed the portage, and António Pedro Marques argued the titles name administrative posts that can be held without torturing. Jerry Friedman reported that Lexicon Urthus and Andre-Driussi describe them as guild torturers who do not torture, undercutting his Flambeau and Mannea theory. The thread ended with the non-torturing-title reading gaining ground, though no firm consensus was reached.
  • Lee Berman: fully trained torturers ranked above journeymen, below masters, by analogy to monastic stages
  • Jeff Wilson: declined the fuligin and stayed to serve the Order; ranks may resemble midshipmen
  • David Stockhoff: not really torturers; full brothers who direct the portage, Cook's cooking not a real duty
  • António Pedro Marques: titles are administrative posts held by members who may never torture
  • Jerry Friedman: guild torturers who do not torture, per Lexicon Urthus and Andre-Driussi, refuting his Flambeau/Mannea theory

Themes: Justice, torture and mercy, Slavery and service, Books, libraries and writing

2010-07-04 Lee Berman replying to Jeff Wilson #

Proposes that Brother Porter and Brother Cook are fully trained torturers ranking above journeymen but below masters, comparing the guild's ranks to monastic stages. Grounds this in the "Brother" title, Drotte's remark about recognizing brothers by their eyes, and a suggested parallel with the wizard school in Le Guin's A Wizard of Earthsea.
proposes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Brother Porter and Brother Cook are fully trained torturers; Torturers' guild ranks mirror monastic stages

My guess is that they are a bit higher in status than journeyman but (of course) below masters. They go by the title of "Brother". Journeymen seem to call each other by first name.(though Drotte does say to Severian after his elevation and masking, "can't you recognize all the brothers by their eyes....").

Monks call each other "Brother" but they must first pass through the stages of postulant then novice. Very few go on to become Father.

I wouldn't be surprised if Brother Porter and Brother Cook are fully trained and capable torturers who are ready to perform those duties if needed. There are some similar characters in the wizard school in Le Guin's 1968 A Wizard of Earthsea. Wolfe surely read that story before writing BotNS.

2010-07-04 Jeff Wilson replying to David Stockhoff #

Proposes that Brother Cook and Brother Porter declined the fuligin yet asked to remain and serve the Order, based on Severian's account of them. Responds to David Stockhoff's remark that the names could be titles, citing passages where Cook leaves cooking to apprentices and where old Porter lounges by the corpse door.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Brother Cook and Porter declined fuligin and stayed to serve

Brother Cook doesn't even do all the cooking, though. "Soon there is scrubbing and kitchen work. Brother Cook performs such cooking as might be interesting or pleasurable, and the apprentice is left to pare vegetables, serve the journeymen, and carry an endless succession of stacks of trays down the stairs to the oubliette."

And Brother Porter is *old*. "But, oh, how much it cost me to walk past the open tower door and looking out see the corpse door in the curtain wall, and old Brother Porter lounging there."

Looking back over Severian's elevation, I think it might be that they declined the fuligin but asked to remain and serve the Order.

2010-07-04 Jerry Friedman replying to David Stockhoff #

Reports that Lexicon Urthus and Michael Andre-Driussi call Brother Porter and Brother Cook Guild torturers who do not torture, which refutes Jerry Friedman's theory that Cook is Hercule Flambeau and Porter is Mannea. Cites Lexicon Urthus page 288 and Andre-Driussi's note on a Brother Porter in Urth of the New Sun chapter 36 as evidence.
informs · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Brother Cook is Hercule Flambeau; Brother Porter is Chatelaine Mannea

"According to Wolfe he [Brother Porter] is a regular torturer of the Guild who does not actually do any torturing."

/Lexicon Urthus/, 2nd ed., p. 288. Michael Andre-Driussi says the same thing, but without the "according to Wolfe", about Brother Cook, Brother Aybert (who may be Brother Cook), and Brother Corbinian the apothecary. He also notes the Brother Porter in Typhon's time in TUotNS, ch. 36.

This shoots down my theory that Brother Cook is really Hercule Flambeau and Brother Porter is really the Chatelaine Mannea.

Jerry Friedman

2010-07-04 António Pedro Marques replying to David Stockhoff #

Argues that the titles Brother Cook and Brother Porter in The Shadow of the Torturer name administrative posts within the torturers' guild rather than the actual duties of those who hold them. Responds to David Stockhoff's view by suggesting such titles could be held for decades by members who never torture.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Brother Cook and Porter are administrative guild offices

My view on this has always been similar to David's.
There should be someone directing the kitchen and the parlour. Whoever do so might be called Cook and Porter. It wouldn't actually expected that they do torturing, but they must be members of the order anyway. Such titles could be held for decades.
