The thread: Stilskin asked what event at the end of Soldier of Arete throws Latro into depression, suggesting a betrayal tied to his lost memory and his feelings for Io. Dave Lebling pointed to Jim Jordan's interview with Wolfe as the answer, and Stilskin said it confirmed his assumption that the cause was a Spartan episode. Adrian Robert disputed that a single event explains the depression, citing the loss of Phareta, parting from friends, and aimless drift, and read Latro's persistence as spiritual strength. Bob Miller pointed readers to the interview link and told them to search for Sparta, without summarizing it. The thread ends with the interview as the reference point and no full agreement on the cause.
  • stilskin: depression stems from a deeper personal betrayal tied to memory loss and Io, out of proportion to earlier crimes, confirmed by the Jordan interview
  • Adrian Robert: depression has many causes beyond the Spartan ceremony, including Phareta's loss and parting from friends
  • Dave Lebling: the Jim Jordan interview answers the question about what happens at the end
  • Bob Miller: directs readers to the interview and says to search it for Sparta

Themes: Memory and forgetting, Suffering and sacrifice, Love and loss

2006-07-10 stilskin replying to Dave Lebling #

Thanks Dave Lebling for a link to Jim Jordan's interview with Wolfe and says the interview confirms what he had assumed about the event. Argues that Latro's deep depression after a Sparta episode seems out of proportion to the worse crimes he witnessed earlier, suggesting a deeper personal betrayal tied to how he lost his memory and to his shifting feelings for Io.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Latro suffered a deeper personal betrayal at Sparta

Very helpful, Dave -- many thanks!

That's what I thought happened, actually, but I
couldn't square it with the depth of Latro's
subsequent depression based on his reaction to similar
and indeed even worse, in a more personal sense,
tragedies and crimes that he witnesses and is involved
in prior to that event. That disparity of affect made
me suspect that I was missing a deeper and more
personal betrayal, perhaps something related to the
circumstances under which Latro lost his memory in the
first place. It also seemed to be accompanied by, or
succeeded by, a definite shift in his feelings for Io,
one, again, that made me suspect there was more going
on than just her having finally finagled her way into
his bed.

Paul

2006-07-10 Adrian Robert replying to Dave Lebling #

Argues that Latro's depression in Soldier of Arete has many causes beyond the Spartan ceremony Wolfe names in an interview, citing the loss of Phareta, parting from friends, and his aimless drift. Responds to Dave Lebling, who had pointed to the Jim Jordan interview as answering a question about Latro's depression, and reads Latro's persistence as a sign of spiritual strength.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Latro's depression has many cumulative causes

Well regardless of what Wolfe himself says there about the Spartan ceremony being the main antecedent, in real life there is rarely a single cause of depression, and the text certainly doesn't require Latro to be different. There are a number of events that despite his weakened memory cannot have failed to have a cumulative effect. The loss of Phareta, parting from various friends, the futile purposelessness with which he is tossed about by the winds of fate, ... It is a tribute to his strength of spirit (and of the human spirit) that he carried on as long as he did.
