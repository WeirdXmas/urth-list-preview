An Evil Guest - An Object Lesson

Urth list · 5 messages · 2018-03-20 to 2018-03-21

2018urthThe Book of the New SunRobert PirkolaEric BourlandMarc AraminiJeff WilsonPaul Rydeen
The thread: Readers debated whether Cassie Casey in An Evil Guest is pregnant. Robert Pirkola argued that Eric Bourland was filtering out textual evidence about Cassie's body by refusing to treat her as a flesh-and-blood person, and used a Madame Bovary analogy to press the point. Eric Bourland replied that his instinct says she is not pregnant because the story does not need it, and promised to review Pirkola's notes and clues while stressing he simply enjoys the story. Paul Rydeen supported the pregnancy reading by citing the menstrual supplies Cassie packs for Woldercan as a concrete clue, with a joke about red herrings. The thread ended without agreement, with Eric holding his skeptical view.
  • Robert Pirkola: Cassie is likely pregnant, and Eric is filtering out textual evidence about her body
  • Eric Bourland: Cassie is probably not pregnant because the story does not need it; enjoys the story
  • Paul Rydeen: the menstrual supplies Cassie packs are a concrete clue supporting a pregnancy reading

Themes: Women, sex and gender, Love and loss

2018-03-20 Robert Pirkola #

Argues that reader Eric was wrongly filtering out textual evidence about Cassie Casey's body in An Evil Guest, deciding not to measure her as a flesh-and-blood person. Responds to Marc Aramini's comments on reader freedom by using a Madame Bovary analogy and a 'pregnancy thing' remark, while conceding Eric's earlier careful work.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Cassie Casey's body shows she is pregnant

I agree with your above-quoted points, Marc, and do not intend to spoil anyone's fun. I foisted the book on my mother to see what she would think of the thing and she was bemused and amused about equally, but she didn't go on a quest to figure anything out beyond what stuck with her in her first (and likely only) read-through. The point I hoped to make about Eric's approach was that he was actively putting on blinders to what the text was communicating about CC's body. And he was apparently doing so because he had decided that it would be unfair to measure Cassie Casey if she were in fact a flesh-and-blood person. I'm reading Madame Bovary at the moment. If I were strongly opposed to adultery as a commandment violation worthy of the death penalty, would I:

(A) read the book and filter out those bits that offend me, or (B) not read a book about adultery?

I certainly don't mean to suggest that you shouldn't be free to read the book, stick it up your nose, or paint with it. But reading someone advocate for the conscious exclusion of textual evidence here on this forum was a bit like finding a tortoise at the top of a flag pole -- there it is, but *how* in the *world* . . . ?!

It would be unfair to Eric to classify his views and approach by only the last post which I disagreed with, however. I know from following this list for years that Eric has posted several observations and questions that indicate the opposite of what I accuse him -- that is to say, he has shown a willingness to pry into obscure corners of Wolfe texts and get down with the minutiae. I hope he will continue to do the same and tell me why I'm all wet with this pregnancy thing.

2018-03-20 Eric Bourland replying to Robert Pirkola #

Replies to Robert Pirkola's suggestion that Cassie in An Evil Guest may be pregnant, saying the poster's instinct is that she is not, since the story does not need it. Says they will review Pirkola's notes and clues before deciding, while stressing they simply enjoy the beautiful story.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Cassie is pregnant with a demon child

Dear Robert,

Actually I don't think you're all wet. Maybe she is pregnant. Maybe with twins. ;-)? I need to go back, read the materials that you have suggested, and read some or all of the book again -- all of which will be pleasurable. My instinct as a reader is, she's not pregnant, mainly because she does not have to be, to make the story work. Then again, maybe I have a limited view of how AEG works as a story. I'm ready to think more about it.

I think the AEG story is already doing a lot. It's a busy story. Would Wolfe, as a writer, add yet another component -- Cassie being pregnant, possibly with a demon child? Maybe so. But she's already got a lot to do.

I'm delighted to be wrong, and learn something interesting. If GW has left clues that Cassie is, or was, pregnant, then I would like to review them. I'll go back through your notes when I get some time, this evening.

In any case, it's a beautiful story, which is, I admit, what's most important to me. ;-)

Eric
