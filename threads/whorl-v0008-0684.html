Names, animals, and meanings (F)

Glosses the names Feather, Feeler, Femur and Fulmar from The Book of the New Sun, giving dictionary definitions and noting the boy Feather's falling during Silk's enlightenment and the fulmar's place among the black mechanics. Reports these name meanings as a list, with the Latin plural of femur linked to Incus and the Latin root of fulmar linked to lightning and electricity.
informs · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading

This is Sean Whalen (prion).

Feather. A light shaft with many thin barbs protruding from it along one (imaginary) plane that contains the shaft. Biological, used by birds, usually to help in flight. Feather is a boy at Silk's palaestra. The only relation he seems to have with his name is that he's falling when Silk is enlightened and Silk's perception of time slows, so he would seem to be falling slowly, like a feather.

Feeler. A sensory limb or projection of an animal, such as a tentacle or antenna.

Femur. Plural femurs or femora. The thighbone, from the hip to the knee. The largest and longest bone in humans. Incus' older brother. It is a bone with an irregular Latin plural, like Incus.

Fulmar. Procellariidae Fulmarus glacialis. Also called the fulmar petrel or the northern fulmar. A seabird living in the North Atlantic Ocean. About a foot and a half long. Eats fish and floating debris (they have increased in numbers due to getting more of this debris in the form of waste from fishing). Hunted for prized feathers, eggs, and oil. This is a member of the black mechanics. The name was probably chosen partly because of the unpleasant association of the bird feeding on garbage. However, fulmar is very close to fulminate, which comes from the Latin word fulmen, lightning, which has close associations with electricity.
