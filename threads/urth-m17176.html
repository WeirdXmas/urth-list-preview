2011-02-11 Lee Berman #

Argues that events and objects in Wolfe's fiction exist simultaneously at several levels, physical and spiritual, so no level need be ranked above another. Grounds this in an interview with Wolfe about paper and hydrocarbons and in a reply to Craig Brewer's question about materialist versus religious readings of The Book of the New Sun.
proposes · theology-and-religion · theories: Wolfe's events exist simultaneously at several levels

First, I enjoyed your conversation on materialism with Lane. Thanks.

I think what you wrote above is in the spirit with which Wolfe is writing. Any object or event exists simultaneously at several levels. Borrowing from the Wolfe interview I recently cited, paper is not reduced by considering that it is made of hydrocarbons. Hydrocarbons are elevated because they serve a useful purpose for a human being. Paper is elevated when it is put to the purpose of discussing and expanding understanding of God. God is elevated when the hydrocarbons he created are used at every level for every purpose. Elevation eventually leads from "highest" back down to "lowest". Circularity. Which is the most important, defining portion of a circle?

I think that's the principle. That we needn't choose one level as superior or subservient to another. They exist simultaneously, and all elements in a Wolfe story can be interpreted in this simultaneous, multi-level way.
