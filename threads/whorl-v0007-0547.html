A couple of points

Whorl list · 4 messages · 1997-09-02 to 1997-09-03

1997whorlThe Book of the Long SunKevin J. MaroneyKieran ClearyAlastair ReynoldsMichael Straight
The thread: Readers asked how long the airship Whorl had been in flight, since Rose and Marble estimated about 300 years while Kevin Maroney suggested it might be closer to 3000, arguing that a ship falling apart so soon after its expected voyage would be implausible. Michael Straight offered several explanations for the Whorl's poor condition, including tampered memories, stripped parts, sabotage by Pas's children, and a self-destructing sun, and he also proposed that Silk and Chenille were bioengineered hosts. Alastair Reynolds argued the voyage must have lasted longer than the story's timeframe, and Kieran Cleary read Kypris's picture of Pas and Silk as Silk's head ruling Typhon's body. The thread ended without resolution, with the longer-voyage view gaining some support but no consensus.
  • Kevin J. Maroney: the Whorl was probably in flight about 3000 years, since a ship decayed this badly so soon is implausible
  • Michael Straight: the Whorl's decay has active causes such as sabotage or a dying sun, and Silk and Chenille were engineered hosts
  • Alastair Reynolds: the voyage must have lasted longer than the story's timeframe because Quetzal arrived after the ship had already been in place
  • Kieran Cleary: Kypris's image suggests Silk's head ruling Typhon's body, and Pas could have used personality downloading

Themes: Time loops and causality, Robots, androids and artificial life, Hidden identities and parentage

1997-09-02 Kevin J. Maroney #

Raises two points: whether Wolfe ever answered which egg held the hallucinogen in Seven American Nights, and whether the airship Whorl was in flight for about 3000 years rather than the 300 years Rose/Marble estimates. Asks if anyone recalls the passage and argues that the ship's dilapidation is more credible over 3000 years, since engineers would not design a ship falling apart so soon after its expected travel time.
questions · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: The Whorl was in flight for 3000 years

I do not regularly contribute to Whorl, but I've been reading the archives, and I had some comments.

First, re: Seven American Nights and the egg: I believe that I have read a description of Wolfe answering the question about which egg contained the hallucinogen, though I do not remember the specific answer. The description of Wolfe's answer indicate that he seemed somewhat saddened by the fact that people felt they had to ask him; I sometimes suspect that Wolfe is upset by the degree to which we the readers attribute to him both infalibility and infinite cleverness. Generally, there are answers to any question we might have, and we just have to ask the text correctly.

Second, I have a question about how long the _Whorl_ was actually in flight, and have not been able to find any discusson of this particular piece, nor is my memory of the text good enough to confirm it. Sometime in the portion of the novel in which Rose/Marble sees the airship, she thinks about how long she's been around, and comes up with an answer of three hundred years. Then she realizes that she must have misplaced a decimal somewhere.

Does anyone else have any memory of this? The dilapidation of the _Whorl_ is much more credible if it has been in flight for 3000 years. Certainly the engineers of the _Whorl_ would not have designed a ship that would be completely falling apart less than 10% beyond the expected travel-time of the ship?

Yours,
Wombat

1997-09-03 Michael Straight #

Proposes several explanations for why the Whorl is in poor condition after only 300 years, including tampered memories, stripped components, sabotage by Pas's children, and a self-destructing sun. Also suggests that Silk and Chenille were bioengineered as host bodies for Pas and his children, and speculates that Kypris may merge Silk with Typhon.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · puzzle-solving · theories: Pas's children sabotaged the Whorl; Memories of cargo were tampered with; Pas set the sun to self-destruct; Silk and Chenille were intended as host bodies

Some points that might explain the condition of the Whorl after only 300 years:

1) When Silk meets the woman in the tunnels who had been in suspended animation, I seem to remember her making some comment to the effect that the "cargo" had had their memories tampered with, which would explain how knowledge of the Whorl's nature had expired.

2) For some reason, people are using as currency computer cards that are necessary to run the Whorl. Vital components may have been raided by people in search of "money."

3) The children of Pas have "killed" (or at least temporarily disabled) him, apparently so that their toys (the people) won't be able to leave the ship. They may have dammaged the ship in their battle with him, or purposely sabotaged it to keep people from leaving.

4) The Whorl may be set so that the sun starts to fail when it reaches its destination so that the colonists are forced to disembark. Or maybe Pas, knowing that he was being hunted, in addition to saving bits of himself in various places, set the sun to self-destruct so that even if he was killed, his children couldn't keep the colonists in the ship forever.

Does anyone else agree with the idea that bioengineered people like Silk and Chenille were originally intended to be permanent host bodies for Pas and his children so they could continue to rule when the colonists disembarked?

When Kypris shows Silk the picture of Pas, with Silk as one of the heads, I asked "which head"? Is this a picture of the original plan where Typhon's "head" rules Silk's body? Is Kypris turning it around, offering to download Silk's personality and use Silk's "head" to rule the partially assembled Pas program? I wouldn't be surprised if she did that with or without Silk's consent and that in the next series we find that "Pas" is now an amalgam of Silk and Typhon.

-Rostrum

1997-09-03 Alastair Reynolds #

Argues that the voyage of the Whorl in Wolfe's Urth-era text must have lasted longer than the story's apparent timeframe, since the ship had arrived well before Quetzal could board and infiltrate it. Responds to a quoted question about the Whorl's flight length and Rose/Marble's three-hundred-year estimate, agreeing that the ship's rundown state and forgotten origins suggest a longer voyage.
supports · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: The Whorl arrived a century before Quetzal boarded

Wombat:

it didn't seem long enough to me either - not just from the point of view of how rundown everything was, but how comprehensively the origins of the Whorl had been forgotten.

Was it not also the case that the Whorl had arrived at its target system at least a century earlier, long enough for Quetzal to get aboard and infiltrate things?

1997-09-03 Kieran Cleary #

Supports Cleary's reading that Kypris's picture of Pas with Silk as one head may show Silk's head ruling Typhon's body, and asks why Pas never used personality downloading. Grounds this in the two-headed figure of Pas in the later book and in Kypris's ambiguous depiction of Silk's head sharing a body with Typhon.
supports · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Kypris's picture shows Silk's head ruling Typhon's body

Cool. I hadn't really thought of that. Godly duplicity again. Seems more than reasonable, despite Typhon's apparent rehabilitation, that Silk, already accepted as Calde, would merely be used as a mount. Although, as I quibbled (much) earlier, I have trouble with Pas having to graft his head onto some hapless subject for locomotive and presentational reasons when the technology for personality downloading existed. His appearance as the two-headed one in tBotNS indicates that he never availed of this technology. Why not? Although, quibbles aside, I agree that Kypris' depiction of Silk's head sharing a body with Typhon is, at the very least, ambiguous.

lasrach
