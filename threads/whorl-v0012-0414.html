2001-03-07 Sheila Herndon #

Proposes that children wrote the third-person sections of the Long Sun narrative, and that these read more stylishly than a child would write because later, better writers reinterpreted Horn's story over the years as a religious text. Concedes that this explanation does not account for the 'Pig's h'ears' passage, which the poster admits is a problem for the idea.
proposes · genre-and-craft · theories: Children wrote the third-person sections, later polished by better writers

Hi,

I was just wondering if it could be possible that
the kids *did* write those sections - but, in fact,
not as stylishly as we're reading them. and the reason
they're as stylish as we're reading them is becuase
the story of Horn has turned into a religious text,
and has been "intepreted" over the years by better
writers? (Though, I guess not a good explanation for
Pig's h'ears.)

mimosa

2001-03-07 Alex David Groce #

Replies to Shiela Herndon's suggestion that the children wrote the Horn sections, but in a less stylish way than the text reads, and that later interpreters polished it. Argues that the explanation is weak given Hoof's distinct section, yet still proposes that the children were raised and taught by the authors of The Book of Silk, so their education can explain the style.
disputes · author-and-biography · theories: Hoof and Hide were raised by the authors of The Book of Silk

Well, it leaves a lot to be desired as an explanation (since Hoof's section is so different) but even though these kids haven't had a strong literary education, Hoof and Hide were raised by (and Daisy taught by one of) the authors of _The Book of Silk_.
