1998urthThe Book of the New SunCraig Christensen

Themes: Power, rulers and politics

1998-07-16 Craig Christensen #

Proposes that the "holy words" in The Citadel of the Autarch are not just old machinery triggered by voice, but a means of controlling or winning allegiance from the populace. Grounds this on the scene where Severian opens the Autarch's door with a word and on the homunculus exchange, where Severian mentions the words and seven continents.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Holy words control or win allegiance of the populace

This was my thinking, too- the Autarch's oldest personalities remember how to operate the voice-recognition machinery. The episode I remembered was near the end of CITADEL (Father Inire's Letter) where Severian opens the door to the Autarch's quarters with a word. I scanned this at home last night, and I noted a mention of 'words' a little further on, at the beginning of his conversation with the homunculus. The homunculus opens by saying (something like) "Why have you interrupted my contemplation of your world?" and Severian replies (something like), "I now know this world to have seven continents, of which only part of this one honor the holy words."

Where I had thought that the use of words was a simple use of old technology appearing magical in this age, I think that this last tidbit indicates something more. It seems that the use of 'words' elicits control over the populous. Or maybe just voluntary allegance.
