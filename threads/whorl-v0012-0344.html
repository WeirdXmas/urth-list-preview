Themes: Memory and forgetting, Hidden identities and parentage

2001-02-23 Robert Borski #

Corrects an earlier reply by noting that the man met later in Requiem for a Mermaid is Horn in Silk's body, not the same man, and that Horn keeps his transferred memories of Seawrack. Points out that Seawrack is thereby sent off on a second blind date with a stranger, and that Silk has one good eye.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Horn is now in Silk's body

Several minutes ago, while waiting for the caffeine to suffuse its way

<Then a couple of years later when this same man--or maybe it's not the same man, he sort of looks the same, but he seems to have undergone radical changes in personality and is missing an eye>

Actually, he looks nothing the same, of course, since Horn is now in Silk's body (although his transferred memories of Seawrack are probably intact)--adding yet one more layer of offense: Seawrack's now compelled to go off with still another stranger on blind date number two (well, not totally blind, cause Silk has one good eye, but you know what I mean.)

Now for some carbohydrates and protein...

Robert Borski
