The thread: Readers compared Melville's Moby-Dick to Wolfe's Book of the New Sun, starting with John Bishop's claim that the narrator's name Ishmael carries biblical allusion and that Queequeg's origins and shrunken-head technique were misstated. Mark Millman extended the parallel, proposing Baldanders as Severian's whale and Severian as both Ishmael and Ahab, with redemption distinguishing him from Ahab, and added Maori and Samoan head-taking practices. Peter Stephenson supported the comparison but narrowed it to individual scenes rather than the books as a whole, noting Ishmael and Severian have no overt connection. The thread ended without resolution, with the parallel left as a loose, scene-level resemblance.

1999-02-05 John Bishop #

Argues that the narrator's name Ishmael in Moby-Dick alludes to the biblical son of Abram and Hagar, signalling a denial of his real name and a biblical, symbolic framing. Supports this with a cited professor's view and corrects a claim about Queequeg's origin, noting whalers' diverse backgrounds and the Jivaro shrunken-head technique, while ending by asking who would be Ahab.
proposes · source-and-allusion

Cute, but no cigar:

o "Call me Ishmael" is a reference to the son of Abram and his concubine Hagar (search for "Ishmael" in
http://www.tagnet.org/freebies/KJBible/01_GEN.HTM).
His family splits off before the Jews become Jews.

So "Ishmael" is not the narrator's name, but his method of:

1) denying the reader his real name,

2) claiming that real names are not the point,

3) saying he is a "wanderer" and cast out from his people and

4) telling the reader that what follows is going to be portentious and symbolic (biblical) rather than realistic and direct.

Dan McCall, author and English professor, told me this some years ago; as the professor pointed out, any reader of the time would have instantly caught the reference.

Of course, "Severian" is meant to make us think of "severe" but it's also his name.

o Quequeeg is probably not Andean; many of the whalers were Native Americans from New England (e.g. the "Gay Head Indians" who were in the news a few years ago when they had a run-in with Jackie Kennedy over land on Martha's Vineyard), others came from all over; but the makers of shrunken heads are the Jivaro [http://users1.ee.net/pmason/shrunk2.html], who are from Brazilian or Ecuadorian low-land forests. On the other hand, he may just have picked up the technique as a way of making money from other people's prejudices.

Personally, given his tatoos, I suspect he's a Pacific Islander--Hawaiian, Tahitian, Samoan or Maori: the whalers were active in the Pacific and recruited anywhere they could get cheap, tough workers.

o And who would be Ahab?

-John Bishop

1999-02-05 Mark Millman #

Proposes that Baldanders takes on the role of the whale in Severian's life, and that Severian is both Ishmael and Ahab, with redemption setting him apart from Ahab. Builds on John Bishop's shrunken-heads note by adding that the Maori also took trophy heads and that the Samoans may have had a similar tradition, and cites the shared bed with a stranger and the water-borne fight in Lake Diuturna.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · source-and-allusion · theories: Baldanders takes on the whale's role; Severian is both Ishmael and Ahab

On Friday 5 February 1999 at 2:08 pm GMT,

As it happens, the Maori also preserved trophy
heads (part of the object was to preserve the
victim's tattoos), although I don't belive that they
shrunk them. It certainly is the Jivaro who are
best known for the practice and who made the
stereotypical shrunken heads. I don't know
whether the Samoans, who were notoriously
belligerent, had a similar tradition.

Like Rostrum, I noticed that Severian was
sharing a bed with a stranger whom he at
first finds threatening but later grew to trust
(and also like Rostrum, I didn't get any further
correspondences at the time). Of course,
unlike Ishmael, Severian eventually finds
himself fighting his former companion.
Though it is, interestingly, a water-borne
assault that results in a fight with dreamlike
elements. Perhaps Baldanders--who disap-
pears into Lake Diuturna and eventually comes
to inhabit the ocean--actually takes on the
whale's role in Severian's life for a time.

Maybe Severian is Ahab as well as Ishmael--
but Severian, unlike Ahab, achieves redemption.

Mark Millman

1999-02-05 Peter Stephenson #

Clarifies that the proposed parallel is between individual scenes, not the books as a whole, and that Ishmael and Severian have no overt connection. Defends the comparison by pointing to Queequeg's poncho, described in detail by Ishmael, as placing the association in the right part of the world.
supports · close-textual-reading

I just wanted to suggest the scenes were parallel. I'm certainly not implying the books are parallel, and the subject line was just a throwaway, there's certainly no overt connection between Ishmael/Severian. Queequeg's poncho (`doormat') is referred to in some detail by Ishmael, which puts him, or his associations, entirely in the right part of the world for me, anywhere in Ecuador would do as far as I'm concerned. I'd have thought that was enough for one little cigar. I'm not asking for a Havana, and I'll go and smoke it outside if you like.

corncrake
