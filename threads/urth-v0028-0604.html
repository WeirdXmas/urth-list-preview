1999-12-09 Mitchell A. Bailey #

Argues that the Good Ship Tzadkiel cannot be a conventional lightsail craft and must instead teleport itself through the exotic properties of its specula, which resemble Father Inire's mirrors. Supports this by noting Inire's explanation would produce an interference pattern rather than hyperdimensional travel, and by quoting Severian's remark that the specula reflect reality rather than merely light.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Tzadkiel teleports using its specula

Previously, while discussing Hethor’s mirrors I mentioned that the Good Ship Tzadkiel did not appear to be a lightsail craft in anything like the conventional sense. Lightsails would be better suited to generation ships like the _Whorl_ since the acceleration would be so slight.

I had argued that if the sails resembled the mirrors of Father Inire, as indeed they must to apport creatures as described, then the Ship must actually be teleporting itself using the exotic properties of those sails.

The explaination Father Inire offered Domnia about their principle of operation is indeed nonsensical for conventional mirrors; the process he describes would result in an interference pattern, not hyperdimensional travel. But then again, Wolfe signals that the specula are not merely perfect mirrors, but something much more. Severian, although he is not an expert on these things, muses after Jonas’ departure: “How foolish to call them mirrors... They reflect light indeed, but that, I think, is no part of their true function. They reflect reality...”
