The thread: The thread opened with Andrew Mason proposing that Severian's lovers embody the Commonwealth's social classes, using the class list from the appendix to The Claw of the Conciliator, and Son of Witz supported it while adding Thecla's khaibit as a separate enslaved class. Jack Smith then argued Dorcas fits the optimate class better than a Roman reading, citing her refinement and her father's success. Jeff Wilson disputed Dorcas's family decline, using a cloisonné portrait and her artistic activity to argue they were comfortable, and in a separate post argued hierodules wear monster masks on Urth and that cacogen is Urthspeak for bug-eyed monster. The thread shifted from class allegory to Dorcas's family and then to hierodules, ending without resolution.
  • Andrew Mason: Severian's lovers stand for Commonwealth social classes, with Dorcas as optimate and Pia and Gunnie as outside groups
  • Son of Witz: supports the class allegory and adds Thecla's khaibit as an enslaved prostitute class
  • Jack Smith: Dorcas fits the optimate class via wealth and refinement, and her marriage lowered her standing
  • Jeff Wilson: Dorcas's family stayed comfortable, shown by the portrait and her artistic work
  • Jeff Wilson: hierodules disguise themselves with monster masks and may be aristocrats among underpeople, and cacogen means bug-eyed monster

Themes: Slavery and service, Power, rulers and politics, Masks, disguise and shapeshifting

2011-01-14 Andrew Mason #

Proposes that Severian's lovers in The Book of the New Sun stand for the social classes of the Commonwealth, linking him to each rank, with Thecla as exultant, Valeria as armiger, Dorcas as optimate, and so on. Grounds this in the classification listed in the appendix to The Claw of the Conciliator, and extends it to Pia as autochthon and Gunnie as off-Urth.
proposes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion · theories: Severian's lovers represent the classes of the Commonwealth

I suspect that Severian's lovers represent the different classes of the Commonwealth, linking him to all of them.

Thecla - exultant.
Valeria - armiger.
Dorcas - optimate.
Jolenta - commonalty.
Cyriaca - (former) religious.
Daria - servant of the throne.
Apheta - cacogen. (She would certainly be called that if she came to Urth.)

(The first four are explicitly listed as examples of those classes in the appendix to COTC. The others have not yet been introduced.)

Thecla's khaibit represents an anomalous group who don't really fit into the classification. Pia represents autochthons, a group which hasn't been highlighted when the classification is given. Gunnie/Burgunadofara can be seen as representing off-Urth humans, and the tall woman was born millions of years before the Commonwealth existed.

There is also a fair chance that Casdoe is a relation of his - likewise.

2011-01-15 Jack Smith replying to David Stockhoff #

Argues that Dorcas, from The Claw of the Conciliator, fits the optimate class more closely than a Roman-republic reading suggests, citing Wolfe's appendix note that optimates were wealthy traders. Supports this by pointing to Dorcas's refinement, her successful father, and how her marriage to the old boatman lowered her economic standing and led to her death.
proposes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion

Dorcas did not seem like an optimate to me, since optimates were the "best" people of the Roman republic and Dorcas never struck me as being what we might call upper or upper middle class. What Wolfe says in the appendix is that "optimates appear to be more or less wealthy traders...there are some hints that Dorcas originally belonged to this class." (CotC, appendix) Now that sounds more like it. Dorcas seems somewhat refined (more than Agia) and her father appears to have been successful. But her marriage to the old boatman brought her down in the world economically, and then led to her death. A sad figure.

2011-01-16 Jeff Wilson replying to Jack Smith #

Argues that Dorcas's family was financially comfortable before her death and that her father only later fell into poverty, working as a boatman. Bases this on the cloisonné portrait of his mother that Ouen has and on Dorcas's artistic activities, reasoning that a family able to afford several portraits was not doing badly.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Dorcas's family was prosperous before her death

I got the impression that they were doing okay until she died, then he was okay for a while more until he went into a depression and ended up working as a boatman so he could keep looking for her body every day. I'm judging this on the basis that Ouen has a cloisonne of his mother, apparently a self-portrait since the painting business comes down her side of the family and Dorcas does various little artisty things while she's with Severian. The obsessive boatman wouldn't have wanted to part with his last picture of her esp to a child who might lose it, so there must have been another for him to keep somewhere. Ergo, if they could afford to devote time and materials to multiple portraits of family members, they weren't doing too badly.

2011-01-16 Jeff Wilson replying to Son of Witz #

Argues that hierodules disguise themselves on Urth with monster masks, so Apheta as a hierarch needs no mask, and that they may be aristocrats among the underpeople. Qualifies the claim that Severian rejected Daria by glossing 'cacogen' as Urthspeak for bug-eyed monster, responding to Andrew Mason's remark that the Khaibit are enslaved prostitutes.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Hierodules wear monster masks when visiting Urth; Cacogen means bug-eyed monster in Urthspeak; Apheta passes as human because she is a hierarch; Hierodules are aristocrats of the underpeople

Didn't Sev pointedly reject Daria?

Yea and no; "cacogen" is Urthspeak for Bug Eyed Monster, and hierodules wear scary monster masks when they visit Urth undercover. Apheta, being a hierarch, doesn't need a disguise because she appears outwardly human and if she reveals herself to be an exalted being, well, "divine messenger" is right up there just under the Autarch.

They could be the aristocrats of the underpeople, made to pass for human unlike the cat women, mastiff men, anpiels, etc.
