Themes: Myth and folklore sources

1998-07-08 mary whalen #

Argues that Cutthroat's exploits in Tracking Song parallel a Sioux folktale, "The Rabbit, and the Bear with the Flint Body," in which a rabbit takes flint from a bear under a hill, kills bears and traps the rest. Draws the parallel by matching the bear under the hill to the underground Min and the horned staff, and cites the rabbit's fast escape as explained by low gravity.
proposes · source-and-allusion · myth-and-structure · theories: Cutthroat's exploits mirror the Sioux rabbit and bear tale

This is Sean Whalen (prion).

I recently looked through a book called _Myths and Legends of the Sioux_ by Marie L. McLaughlin to try to find insight into the story Tracking Song, and came across a story within it called "The Rabbit, and the Bear with the Flint Body." It's about a rabbit who wants to make flint arrowheads and goes to get flint from the bear that lives under the hill. The bear's body is half flint. The rabbit chips off flint, but then chips too hard and the bear comes apart. The rabbit flees, and is pursued by bears, but he runs and leaps so quickly that he escapes them. He calls a blizzard and the bears get trapped. He then kills the bears.

This seems to parallel much of what Cutthroat does in Tracking Song. The bear under the hill is like the underground cyborg (or anti-cyborg) Min. He kills some Min and gets a weapon (the horned staff). He is able to run and jump faster than anyone else because (probably) of the low gravity. He seals the entrance to the cave and traps the rest of the Min.
