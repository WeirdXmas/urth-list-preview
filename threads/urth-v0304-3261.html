Agia: Space Babe

Urth list · 3 messages · 2003-04-21 to 2003-04-22

2003urthThe Book of the New SunMatthew MalthouseBrett M. GraceJames Jordan
The thread: Readers debated whether Agia, the space-born figure in The Book of the New Sun, is Hethor's paracoita or an artificial companion. James Jordan argued she is thematically the Enemy, a fallen heavenly being like Satan in Eden, while allowing she could be a human from space. Brett M. Grace asked whether she is Hethor's paracoita, saying his disordered mind cannot rule this out, but admitted he could not recall enough of Hethor's ramblings to settle it and found the alliance hard to believe. Matthew Malthouse answered that Agia was a substitute for Hethor's lost paracoita, possibly an inferior one from his view, which may bear on his mental disorder. The thread is short and ends without resolution.
  • James Jordan: Agia is thematically the fallen heavenly Enemy, though possibly a human from space
  • Brett M. Grace: Agia may be Hethor's artificial paracoita, and his disordered mind leaves this open
  • matthew.malthouse: Agia is a substitute for Hethor's lost paracoita, possibly inferior, tied to his disorder

Themes: Robots, androids and artificial life, Unreliable narration, Mirrors, pictures and art

2003-04-21 James Jordan #

Proposes that Agia in The Book of the New Sun is thematically the Enemy, comparable to Satan in the Garden, a fallen heavenly being. Concedes she need not be a cacogen and may be a human from space, arguing the fall-from-heaven reading suits the theme.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · theology-and-religion · theories: Agia is thematically the Enemy, a fallen angel

Thematically, anyway, Agia is Enemy. Compare Satan in the Garden. Satan is a fallen heavenly being. That doesn't mean Agia is a cacogen; she may be a human from space. But thematically, it makes sense for her to have fallen from heaven.

2003-04-21 Brett M. Grace #

Asks whether Agia is Hethor's paracoita, an artificial companion, and argues that Hethor's disordered mind means his raving cannot rule that out. Admits he does not remember enough of Hethor's ramblings to support or disprove it, asks readers with fresher memories to weigh in, and says the deep alliance between Agia and Hethor seems hard to believe.
questions · puzzle-solving · theories: Agia is Hethor's paracoita, an artificial companion

Is she Hethor's paracoita? Wait! Yes, I know what you're thinking. BUT. He is delusional. Given his disordered mind, it wouldn't necessarily mean that Agia was artificial.

I don't remember enough of his ramblings to disprove or support this. Somebody with a fresher memory? I've always found the deep alliance between Agia and Hethor to be a little incredible.

2003-04-22 Matthew Malthouse #

Proposes that Agia was a substitute for Hethor's lost paracoita, a possibly inferior one from his point of view, and that this may bear on his mental disorder. Responds to Brett M. Grace's question about whether Agia is Hethor's paracoita, offering a brief answer without quoting specific scenes.
answers · close-textual-reading · theories: Agia was a substitute for Hethor's lost paracoita

Hethor lost his paracoita, potentially one cause of his mental disorder. It seemed to me that Agia was a substitute, possibly an inferior one as far as he was concerned.

Matthew
