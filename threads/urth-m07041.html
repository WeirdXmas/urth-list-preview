"My father's mounted guard"

Urth list · 7 messages · 2008-10-08 to 2008-10-14

2008urthThe Book of the New SunMatthew KeeleyMark MillmanBennewitzJames WynnStanisław BocianJeff Wilson
The thread: Bennewitz asked whose father is meant by the mounted guard in Claw of the Conciliator chapter 25, where Severian's and Thecla's memories seem to blend and a sentence shifts mid-way. Jeff Wilson proposed that Thecla is a typo for her sister Thea, while James Wynn disputed that a typo could persist across editions and argued the memories stay layered throughout. Mark Millman explained the hoof-dodging as a small child underfoot or a youthful horse-infatuation and questioned Thecla's age relative to Severian. Wilson then asked why Severian's pebble-playing memory entwines with Thecla's, since they never knew each other as children, and Stanisław Bocian replied with a close parse arguing the sentence moves from Severian's memory into Thecla's point of view as his artificial memories merge with his own. No consensus was reached, and the thread ended with the close parse still contested.
  • Jeff Wilson: the name Thecla is a typo for Thea, Thecla's sister, explaining the memory
  • James Wynn: a typo would need to persist across editions, so the layered memories are intended
  • Mark Millman: the hoof-dodging is a small child's or youthful horse-infatuation, and Thecla may be older
  • Stanisław Bocian: the sentence moves from Severian's memory to Thecla's and then into her view, showing merged artificial memories
  • Bennewitz: asks whether the shift signals a narrator change and whether the guard belongs to Severian's father as Autarch or another family

Themes: Memory and forgetting, Unreliable narration, Doubles, twins and clones

2008-10-08 Bennewitz #

Asks who the father is in a passage from Claw of the Conciliator chapter 25 where Severian's memories seem to blend with Thecla's, and whether a sentence shifting mid-way signals a change of narrator. Questions whether the mounted guard points to Severian's father as Autarch or another Exultant family, noting the passage's confusing mid-sentence shift.
questions · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian's father may be the Autarch

In Claw chap XXV, Severian is having life memories before he falls asleep. while there is obviously the potential for Thecla memories to intermix here, this following passage is still confusing:

"I played again with pebbles in the courtyard beside the fallen curtain wall, as Thecla dodged the hooves of my father's mounted guard."

Who's Father?

Do any of the other 'interruptions' from Thecla's memory happen mid sentence like this? I searched the archives and found that this was suggested. I find it problematic that the narrative voice would change in mid sentence. Even if the memory is Thecla's why would she be dodging the feet of her father's guard?

IS this tidbit pointing to Severian's father as Autarch or some other Exultant family?

2008-10-09 Jeff Wilson replying to Bennewitz #

Proposes that the name Thecla in Claw chapter XXV is a typo for Thea, Thecla's sister, to explain the confusing mid-sentence memory about the father's mounted guard. Notes that Thecla's memory surfaces in Severian's first-person reverie and again years later during the writing of the manuscript, answering Bennewitz's question.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Thecla in the passage is a typo for Thea

I think it's likely that Thecla here is a typo for her sister's name Thea. But the really interesting thing is not only is Thecla's memory roused to the first person at the time of the original reverie, but apparently manifests once again years later during the writing of the manuscript.

2008-10-09 James Wynn replying to Jeff Wilson #

Disputes Jeff Wilson's claim that Thecla is a typo for Thea, arguing that a typo would have to persist across several editions and that a far stronger case is needed. Argues instead that Thecla's memory and identity remain present throughout, comparing the layered authorship to the multiple narrators of The Book of the Short Sun.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Thecla is not a typo for Thea; Thecla's identity persists within the manuscript

If it were a typo, then it has been replicated across multiple editions. I think one needs a significant evidence before settling on the typo explanation.

Thecla's memory (and as Wolfe seems to see it, her conscious identity) is always there. As in The Book of the Short Sun, the author is not a single person.
J.

2008-10-09 Mark Millman replying to Jeff Wilson #

Argues that Thecla dodging the hooves of her father's guard in Claw chapter XXV is explained by her being a small child underfoot, or by a youthful horse-infatuation if she is older. Responds to Jeff Wilson's confusion about the passage by proposing that the memories may be Severian's and Thecla's from the same age or the same time, and questions whether Thecla is older than Severian.
answers · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Thecla is the same age as Severian; Thecla is possibly older than Severian

Dear Bennewitz,

It's because she's a small child and has gotten underfoot, as small children do. Presumably it depends on whether the memories are from Severian and Thecla at the same age, or Severian and Thecla at the same time. (Has anybody raised this point before? Is it even interesting for our purposes? Do we know how much, if at all, older Thecla is than Severian? I've always assumed that she is older, but on examining that assumption I find that there's no real support for it. She could easily be Severian's age.) If they're the same age, she's probably too young to know better. If the memories are from the same time and she's older, she may have been acting under what in our day is the common horse-infatuation of young girls--I imagine it still holds with Urth girls and destriers.

Mark Millman

2008-10-14 Stanisław Bocian replying to Mark Millman #

Argues that the sentence about pebbles and dodging hooves moves from Severian's own childhood memory to Thecla's, then into her point of view, showing that his artificial memories have merged with his own. Offers this close parse of the sentence in reply to Mark Millman's question about whether the childhood memories of Severian and Thecla are from the same age or the same time.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian's artificial memories of Thecla have mingled with his own

Hello!

"I played again with pebbles in the court-
yard beside the fallen curtain wall, as
Thecla dodged the hooves of my father's
mounted guard."

That sentence is perfectly constructed. Severian has in his head memories both his own and of Thecla. He begins by recalling his own childhood (I played again with pebbles in the court- yard beside the fallen curtain wall), which transits seamlessly into memories of Thecla's childhood, but still recalled from the point of view of Severian (as Thecla dodged the hooves). Finally, it transits into Thecla's memory seen from her point of view (of my father's mounted guard). This shows that the artificial memories have become mingled with his own.
