Interpreting Peace [ Digest urth.v009.n013]

1998urthJohn Bishop

Themes: Ghosts, dreams and visions

1998-04-22 John Bishop #

Proposes that the memos nailed to Weer's desk in Peace show the office is preserved and fixed, a memory rather than a living space, hinting that Weer is dead. Grounds this in the nails as cruelty, linking them to Marley's chains in A Christmas Carol so that Weer is nailed to his memos. Answers Paul C Duggan's question about the desk.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · source-and-allusion · theories: Weer is dead and his office is a preserved corpse; Weer is nailed to his memos like Marley's chains

Paul C Duggan <[email]> asks:

[6] What's with the memos and such nailed to Weer's desk?

I saw this as:

1) saying the office is unchangeable, fixed--it is a memory, not the real thing: another hint to the reader that Weer is dead. It is a preserved corpse of an office.

2) the nails are also somewhat cruel, reminding us of "A Christmas Carol"'s ghost Morely, who says he "wears the chains he forged in life". Weer is nailed to his memos.

-John
