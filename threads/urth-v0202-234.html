Wolfe filk from Wolfe folk

Urth list · 3 messages · 2002-02-26 to 2002-02-26

2002urthThe Book of the New SunDan'l Danehy-OakesMichael Andre-Driussi
The thread: A light-hearted thread in which Michael Andre-Driussi and Dan'l Danehy-Oakes joked about producing Wolfe-themed filk, the folk-song parody form. Andre-Driussi cited an existing parody of Cordwainer Smith's "Miyameefla" as proof the list had the talent, and later offered a sample reworking of Greensleeves with lines about Thecla and a knife. Danehy-Oakes argued that Wolfe's work resists being turned into songs, though he called that resistance a challenge, and proposed a pun on the Age of Severian. No one offered a critical reading of the books, and the thread stayed playful, ending with the joke still running.
  • Michael Andre-Driussi: list has the talent for Wolfe filk and parody folk-song lyrics are a fun way to do it
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: Wolfe resists being turned into filk, which is a challenge, and suggests a pun title on the Age of Severian

Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Humour in Wolfe

2002-02-26 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Jokes that Wolfe's work resists being turned into filk songs, though that resistance is a challenge, and proposes a pun on the Age of Severian as a parody title. Responds to a earlier post by Mantis about filking Wolfe, offering no critical reading of the books.
chats

My dear Mantis,

Actually, Wolfe's work doesn't in general seem all that amenable to filkification, but really that should be perceived as a challenge.

If nothing else, I'm amazed that nobody has yet written anything using the obvious natural pun (like unto Yankovic's "this here Anakin guy") "the dawning of the Age of Severian." But I don't think I will.

2002-02-26 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Proposes writing parody filk, setting lyrics to old folk songs, and offers a sample reworking of Greensleeves with lines about Thecla and a knife. Responds to Blattid's joke about a pun on the Dawning of the Age of Severian, using the same sort of parody playfully.
proposes · genre-and-craft

My thoughts were more toward using old folk songs. Turning "Greensleeves" into something like:

"Alas, Thecla, you do me slight
Your final friend, your last boy-slave
When I have loved you from first sight
And will love you beyond your grave.

Chatelaine of Matachin
Chatelaine of my heart bright
Chatelaine of traitor kin
Escape with my shiny knife."
