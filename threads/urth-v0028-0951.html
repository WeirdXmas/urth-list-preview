Little, Big: the magic goes away

Urth list · 1 messages · 2000-05-01 to 2000-05-01

2000urthAdam Stephanides

2000-05-01 Adam Stephanides #

Disputes Alex's claim that the disappearance of the fairies impoverishes the world in Little, Big, arguing the decline comes from the civil war the fairies caused. Grounds this in the book's final section and calls the closing paragraph's comparison nostalgia, likened to Russell Eigenblick's nostalgia in "Fifty-two Pickup".
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Final-section poverty comes from the civil war, not magic's absence

I agree with the first of these sentences, but not with the second. I don't recall any indication that the presence of the fairies brought any benefits to the world at large, either directly or through the diffusion of enchantment. The world of the final section is impoverished not because of the absence of magic, but because of the real destruction brought about by the civil war that the fairies themselves caused.

The book's final paragraph is beautiful, but it's not an objective comparison between now and then. It's an expression of nostalgia, sharpened by material deprivation; and (I would suggest) a nostalgia as misplaced as the nostalgia for Russell Eigenblick described in "Fifty-two Pickup" (Book Six, Chap. Four).

--Adam
