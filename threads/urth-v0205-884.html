Boy Called Frog and interplanetary war

Urth list · 3 messages · 2002-05-06 to 2002-05-07

2002urthThe Book of the New SunDan'l Danehy-OakesMichael Andre-DriussiJosh Geller
The thread: Michael Andre-Driussi disputed Blattid's claim that the Boy Called Frog story depicts an interplanetary war, arguing the Frog-Fish conflict is urban versus rural and that only Spring Wind's storyline reaches interplanetary scale. Josh Geller and Dan'l Danehy-Oakes both reported a recollection that one or both brothers call on Spring Wind's people for help, which suggests an offworld link, though both admitted uncertainty and neither had rechecked the text. The thread ended without resolution, with the question left to a fresh reading of the story.
  • Michael Andre-Driussi: the Frog-Fish fight is urban versus rural; only Spring Wind's storyline is interplanetary
  • Josh Geller: one or both brothers likely received help from Spring Wind's people, unverified
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: recalls Fish calling on Spring Wind's people for help, but unsure after rereading

Themes: Colonization and the native, War and soldiers

2002-05-06 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Disputes Blattid's claim that the Boy Called Frog story depicts an interplanetary war, arguing that the fight between Frog and Fish is urban versus rural rather than planet against planet. Cites the Romulus and Remus parallel and the offworld vocabulary around Spring Wind's mother and the World of Shadows, concluding that only Spring Wind's storyline reaches interplanetary scale.
disputes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion · theories: Fish and Frog's conflict is urban versus rural; Spring Wind's war alone is interplanetary; World of Shadows is Pluto, planet Dis

Umm, that's the Romulus and Remus bit, iirc.

When I read Romulus and Remus, I don't see "interplanetary war" . . . do you? Fish and Frog are certainly urban vs. rural, without question, but I myself cannot see planet vs. planet.

The tale has ways of saying "offworld." The mother of Spring Wind clearly lives offworld, "beyond the shores of Urth." "The World of Shadows" is also offworld, and if it has a real location in the solar system it would be our Pluto, the planet Dis of the Commonwealth.

The vocabulary is there to speak of interplanetary war in the story of Fish and Frog, but I do not see it being spoken of in the story. Well wait: I see it in the part about Spring Wind--is that the point that Josh Geller was trying to make? Spring Wind is out there, battling across the solar system--yes. But this is far and above beyond the level that Fish and Frog are at, fighting first together for their lost legacy on/of Urth, then fighting each other over it.

=mantis=
