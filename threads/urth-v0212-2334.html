2002-12-02 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Defends the use of 'objective' and 'subjective' time for relativistic time dilation, giving the formula t = t(o)/√(1−V²/c²) and rejecting the claim that the terms are merely a pedantic quibble. Responds to Blattid's objection that time is equally objective in every inertial frame, mocking the dispute over terminology as overly fussy.
disputes · close-textual-reading

Unless you are trying to tell me that there is no time dilation at relativistic speeds, which would be an interesting claim, then this looks to be another flea circumcising exercise on the terms Objective and Subjective. Worthy of litcrit, and literary theory, and philosophy (where these terms are Big Terms indeed).

As I was using it,

"Objective time" = t(o) = "interval of time experienced by an observer in the coordinate system (at rest)"

"Subjective time" = t = "time recorded by a clock moving in the rocket at a constant velocity V across space"

c = "velocity of light in a vacuum"

t = t(o)/Square root(1-(V^2/c^2))

Would you prefer I use "t" and "t(o)" instead of Subjective and Objective? I mean, criminy, you make it sound like I'm selling the Dean Drive here! Gimme a break!

=mantis=

2002-12-02 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that in relativity neither the Urth nor the Whorl coordinate system is privileged as "at rest," so the question of whose clock runs slow depends on frame choice. Responds to Mantis's objective and subjective time definitions by rejecting the luminiferous aether assumption and noting the Whorl's motion relative to Urth after boost stops.
disputes · genre-and-craft
