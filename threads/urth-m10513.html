The thread: The thread began as a debate over whether Yesod and Briah are sequential or separate planes, given the Brook Madregot passage in Wolfe's New Sun cycle. Jerry Friedman, Lee Berman, Roy Lackey, Jeff Wilson and James Wynn argued over the brook's topology and the Corridors of Time, with Lee Berman and Wynn pressing a meta-universe model. Later the discussion shifted to whether Urth is our future, with Gerry Quinn arguing it is and Friedman and Jeff Wilson pointing to the Botanic Garden fauna and the flood against it. A large share of posts concerned Craig Brewer's spam-filter problem and the shift to Latin inire and Inuus/Pan, which drifted into sexual readings and did not resolve.
  • Jerry Friedman: Yesod as a shotgun-house roof above a linear Briah sequence, later arguing Urth is a different world
  • Lee Berman: Brook connects Yesod and Briah in one line, later a meta-universe model and Inuus zoo reading
  • Roy C. Lackey: Brook flows from Yesod down to Briah, so they lie on separate planes
  • James Wynn: The passage makes a Yesod-in-chain-of-universes model untenable, Yesod a meta-universe
  • Jeff Wilson: Flood breaks Noahtic covenant, so Urth is a previous cycle; later calls that a belated retcon
  • Gerry Quinn: Time may not compare across universes and Urth is our future

Themes: Time loops and causality, The dying sun and the cosmos, Myth and folklore sources

2010-08-11 Jeff Wilson replying to Lee Berman #

Proposes a model of Yesod's place in the cosmology of The Book of the New Sun, placing it above Briah's cycles like the Corridors of Time above the Commonwealth's roads. Grounds this in Severian's visit to a machine-planet with a simulated sun, the word "manvantara" and Hindu nested cycles, and Tzadkiel's power for travel.
proposes · close-textual-reading · myth-and-structure · theories: Yesod is a neighbor universe with its own cycle

Yesod is above the cycles of Briah the way the Corridors of Time are above the roads of the Commonwealth. Expansion/inflation of space makes Briah it's own neighbor in finite but unbounded space; the successive cycles are neighbors in time, though separated by the unassailable fences of creation and destruction of all matter. Yesod and Abaddon are neighbors in yet another dimension, possibly the same as the Corridors of Time, but of a distance of much greater magnitude, requiring the exalted power of Tzadkiel to allow safe travel between them within a human lifetime.

The use of "manvantara" brings in the concept of Hindu myth's nested cycles, so possibly Yesod does have its own bang-gnab cycle, but one very different from ours/Briah's. Yesod the universe is said to contain many consturcted worlds, but Severian visits one machine-planet that seems to be embedded in a small universe with a simulated sun and stars for cosmetic reasons. (There are no tides, so the sun of Yeasod is not anything like a star or even a moon.) The machine-planet itself has various levels inside of it, so perhaps they count as various constructed worlds.

The simulated day in Yesod is very short compared to the subjective length of URth's day. This may also mean that the Yesod cycle stretches over many Briah cycles, even if the billion-trillion years per cycle in Briah seem like a few decades to the Hierodules. It also may be that this is arbitrary, based on actions the Yesodis take rather than Yesod having a fixed flow of time like Briah.

2010-08-11 Craig Brewer replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that Briah's cyclical renewal under a New Sun differs from ascent to Yesod, so Yesod cannot be what Briah is meant to become. Supports this with thematic reasoning about cycles and renewal, and by analogy to The Wizard Knight's levels of Asgard and the human world, while noting he probably agrees with Roy's textual reading.
disputes · theology-and-religion · myth-and-structure · theories: Yesod is not what Briah becomes

I'll add something to the discussion, noting from the beginning that I don't have a personal stake in the argument, although I probably agree with how Roy is reading the textual passages in question.

My difficulty with the Yesod-as-later-Briah theory is thematic. What happens in the Briah universe is largely cyclical. Similar stories get played out over and over. The entire idea of a "New Sun" is about Urth/Earth's renewal, it seems, rather than complete transformation and evolution. Or, at the very least, when the New Sun arrives, Urth is transformed rather than overcome or left behind.

Yesod seems "higher" than Briah in a number of senses, but primarily spiritually. It's not exactly heaven, but it is a place where judgment gets passed on the "lower" realm. It might be a higher dimension (however we understand that word), but it doesn't seem to me like the kind of thing or place that is ever a realistic goal for Urth-ers to achieve. (A Christian wants to become a better or saved Christian, for example, but it's not precisely a Christian's goal to become an angel, if that analogy makes sense.)

If Yesod is actually what Briah is supposed to become, then I don't really understand how all of Severian's struggles and adventures are supposed to lead to that goal. I completely understand how he's supposed to transform Briah/Urth and bring about its renewal, "save" it, etc. But if that's the case, then Briah is going to go through evolution on its own path, not turn itself into a higher spiritual mode of existence. In other words, if Yesod is what Briah is supposed to become, then doesn't that make the entire drama of simply bringing a New Sun within the Briah-universe something pretty trivial?

It always seemed to me that Yesod/Briah were on levels in similar-but-not-identical ways that the levels worked in TWK: Briah is like the human level. Yesod is like the Asgard level. Asgard is "better" than where Able spends most of his time. And Able can work to make humans better people. But the point is never really to turn all humans into angels or all elves into humans or what-not. The levels are each valued differently, but that doesn't mean that the lower ones are supposed to become the higher ones, even if they can learn from what happens in the higher ones. Individuals, like Able, may well be able to move through and "evolve" through the different levels, as similar in the way that Severian learns quite a bit from his experience in Yesod, but there are still meaningful horizontal stories even if the ultimate metaphysical story is vertical.
