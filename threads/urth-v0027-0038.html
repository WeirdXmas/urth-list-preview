Jonas in the Antechamber

The thread: Dave Lebling argued that Jonas's distress in the Antechamber stems mainly from realizing how much time has passed on Urth since the Fortunate Cloud left, citing the misplaced spaceport, the black sails in the Theseus tale, and the Kim Lee Soong descendants as shocks of long time spans. Kieran Cleary supported this, noting that relativistic travel gives no easy way to gauge elapsed time and citing Palaemon's remark that the Autarchs kept society in stasis since Ymar. Peter Stephenson replied in a joking vein, describing the Matachin citadel as the place Jonas's ship can no longer land and proposing that the tower is the ship after a time-warp. The thread ended with supporters agreeing and the joke treated as a playful challenge rather than a real counterargument.

1999-06-01 Dave Lebling #

Argues that Jonas's distress in the Antechamber comes mainly from realizing how much time has passed on Urth since the Fortunate Cloud left, with four spans of time behind it. Cites his remarks on the misplaced spaceport, the black sails in the Theseus tale, and the Kim Lee Soong descendants as shocks of long time spans.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: Jonas's distress stems from realizing elapsed time

mantis asks:

My take on this, ever since first reading _New Sun_, is that a major part of Jonas' distress is the realization of how much time has passed on Urth since his departure. He tells the fairly straightforward story of the _Fortunate Cloud's_ return, remarking that the spaceport wasn't where they expected it to be (bad info, continental drift, or what?). Then on hearing Severian's tale of the Magician and his son, he realizes that the story of Theseus has mutated almost beyond recognition (his remark about the black sails). Then he discovers that the prisoners may be the descendants of people from his ship or another like it which crashed generations before (the Kim Lee Soong stuff). All three of these shocks have in common the apprehension of long spans of time.

Finally, of course, he fears that he may be imprisoned for a long time, and will never see Jolenta again.

So, we have a span of tens of thousands of years back to the loss of knowledge about the spaceport; a span of thousands of years in which the culture of his age was lost or changed; a span of hundreds of years in which the prisoners have lost the common culture of humanity; and a span of decades of possible imprisonment in which Jonas will lose Jolenta.

Last there is the span of seconds in the room of mirrors in which he regains the stars (or does he?).

Dave Lebling
(aka vizcacha)

1999-06-01 Kieran Cleary #

Agrees with Dave Lebling that Jonas's distress in the Antechamber comes from realizing how much time has passed on Urth. Explains that relativistic travel gives no easy way to gauge elapsed time, and cites Palaemon's remark to Severian that the Autarchs have kept society in stasis since Ymar.

True, and I suppose this was a dawning realisation as there probably isn't an easy way to find out how much time the average relativistic space traveller has been away simply by looking around, particularly as the Autarchs have been trying to keep society in stasis until the New Sun comes ever since the time of Ymar (as Palaemon remarks to Severian at the beginning of his journey).

Excellent work Ranjit, thank you!

1999-06-01 Peter Stephenson #

Jokingly describes the Matachin citadel, with its torturers, witches, magic atria and homunculi, as the spot where Jonas's ship can no longer land, and proposes proving the tower is the ship after a time-warp. Responds to a quoted point that Jonas's distress comes from the time elapsed on Urth, treating the missing spaceport as a playful challenge to readers.
