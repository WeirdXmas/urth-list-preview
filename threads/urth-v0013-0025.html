1998urthThe Book of the New SunAlice TurnerPeter WestlakeMichael Straight
The thread: Readers debated the Fifth Head of Cerberus setting and whether the taluses seen in the factory scene will matter later. Alice Turner praised Neal Smith's colonist explanation and argued the taluses will return in the Short Sun books, since Wolfe lingered on their factory. Michael Straight disputed that foreshadowing reading, arguing the factory is background because the talus Blood was a significant character, invoking Chekhov's gun. Peter Westlake turned to the Ascian war machines Severian sees from the Autarch's flyer, proposing they are giant walking towers like taluses, possibly intelligent, rather than tracked vehicles. The thread moved from the taluses to the machines without clear resolution.
  • Alice Turner: taluses in the factory scene foreshadow their return in the Short Sun books, and colonists may explain the Fifth Head setting
  • Michael Straight: the factory is background because the talus Blood matters as a character, not foreshadowing (Chekhov's gun objection)
  • Peter Westlake: the Ascian war machines are walking, possibly intelligent towers like taluses, not tracked vehicles

Themes: Robots, androids and artificial life, The dying sun and the cosmos

1998-06-12 Peter Westlake #

Argues that the Ascian war machines Severian sees from the Autarch's flyer are giant walking towers like taluses, possibly intelligent, rather than tracked vehicles. Grounds this in Severian's description of machines as big as fortresses and in the implausibility of soldiers riding a machine left down a mine for centuries.
proposes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Ascian war machines are walking towers like taluses

It's a bit bigger than that, isn't it? And it walks, too, rather than having caterpillar tracks. When Severian looks at the Ascian armies from the Autarch's flyer (why don't flyers float right off into space, BTW?) he see "machines as big as fortresses", which I take to be the same thing. They look like towers walking.

They might be intelligent in the same way as taluses, of course. That would make more sense than having soldiers on board if you were going to leave one down a mine for centuries.

(Would you really want one of those things aboard the Whorl? "Stamp! Stamp! Crunch, crack, wheeeeeee...")

Sbear.

1998-06-12 Michael Straight #

Disputes Alice Turner's claim that the factory scene foreshadows taluses in The Book of the Short Sun, arguing instead that the factory background is there because the talus Blood was an important character. Supports this by invoking Chekhov's rule that a gun shown in the first act must go off later.
disputes · close-textual-reading · genre-and-craft

I'm not sure I know any such thing. My impression in reading that bit was that Blood's talus had been an important enough character that Wolfe (Horn?) wanted to give the reader some background about where taluses come from.

Like Chekov says, if a gun goes off in the first act, you better make sure someone finds it on the mantle in the third act.

-Rostrum
