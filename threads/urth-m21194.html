The thread: The thread began as a dispute over whether Typhon, in Wolfe's Long Sun and New Sun books, wanted to destroy human books, with Gerry Quinn arguing he valued learning and David Stockhoff seeing a book-burning monarch. It quickly sprawled into a fight over a misquoted Severian passage, then over whether Typhon and Echidna are siblings or sacred-incest partners in the Long Sun books. Gerry Quinn and Marc Aramini argued that the text gives no grounds for reading classical myth into the family, while Lee Berman and David Stockhoff pushed the sibling and incest reading. A later branch on the short story Suzanne Delage debated whether the narrator's memory is unreliable and whether the Proust name matters, with Gerry Quinn dismissing the name and Marc Aramini and Jerry Friedman pressing the memory and supernatural readings. The thread ended in impasse with no consensus.
  • Lee Berman: Typhon and Echidna are siblings linked to classical myth, and Severian consistently chooses good
  • David Stockhoff: Typhon is a book-burning archon, and implied incest is a gothic device worth pursuing
  • Gerry Quinn: Typhon valued learning and technology, and the mythic family and Proust name are raw material, not meaning
  • Marc Aramini: Wolfe's myth mapping needs explicit textual support, and Suzanne Delage's narrator is unreliable
  • Jerry Friedman: The Suzanne narrator's memory need not be unreliable, and the Proust resemblances may matter
  • Jeff Wilson: Lee Berman misquoted him, and the altered punctuation reverses the meaning

Themes: Unreliable narration, Memory and forgetting, Doubles, twins and clones

"It is a revelation to compare Menard?s Don Quixote with Cervantes?. The latter, for example, wrote (part one, chapter nine):

Argues that Typhon of The Book of the New Sun is an archon-like ruler who built a tapestry of gods to hide the Outsider and the universe from colonists, and that his telepathy is neither electronic nor genetic. proposes · allegory-and-typology · theology-and-religion · theories: Typhon is an archon who created the pantheon to hide the Outsider; Typhon's telepathy is neither electronic nor genetic; Typhon's mindnet is beyond analysis, close to magic

Jerry, I'm glad I brought up PL and that you corrected me on the passage. Milton is probably the original fantasist (as well as propagandist) of English literature. Even a Catholic wouldn't dare to ignore him in building a mythology!

Thinking a little further: Echidna's spawn and Sin's spawn seem to be roughly parallel---one may even be an imitation of the other---but one is based on a clear moral system and the other is not, or at least not on one familiar to us.

I'm not sure what Greek monsters really do except entertain us and present us with puzzles about the intractability of the struggle of human nature vs itself and the unforgiving universe---gods too. Maybe the gods were supposed to humble us by depicting exaggerated human weakness and simultaneously inflicting always-inventive punishments for them.

But what Wolfe appears to have done is synthesize from these and possibly other sources to get a mythology that is comprehensive within a narrow context, and as well developed as Roman state paganism in 2000 A.D. It's flexible enough to serve the goal of stability, in terms of social engineering, and just moral enough to highlight the inadequacy of its morality and ethics. It's ripe for the Outsider to reveal himself to and bring down.

(1) Given that assumption, it is not odd to find Tartarus as Typhon's son and the unions of Satan + Sin, Sin + Death, and Typhon + Echidna a bit blurred.

(2) Typhon of New Sun shows no sign of becoming Pas, but he was a good candidate as an exemplary tyrannical Ruler of Worlds. If we take him as Archon of whatever plane of existence Urth is in (Puritans used to see Satan as almost the jailer/landlord of the filthy world outside church, and Death can be seen as the single truly immutable law of the mortal plane, hiding the truth of Eternal Life), then it fits for him to have created a kind of tapestry of gods to wrap around the colonists and hide from them the true nature of the Outsider and the universe.

(3) Even if this pantheon and the society described by Mamelta were not fleshed out at the time New Sun was written, my guess is that they were at least conceived of. Typhon's "I send my thought" which is so opaquely suggestive without this added information becomes much easier to read with it. I no longer see any need for Typhon to be a telepathic sport. Telepathic, yes, but not in the way I guessed before Long Sun. Purely electronic, definitely not, nor purely genetic.

And yes, it does matter! Typhon's mindnet is as close to magic as the series gets; it's beyond analysis. His genes are still superior but not to the degree that the Mule's would have been. (IIRC, the Mule was sterile anyway.)
