1997-06-10 Michael Straight #

Argues that the Whorl's shaking in Lake of the Long Sun was the ship's engines firing for the first time in years, and that Horn's love-goddess remark in Exodus rebukes romantic love. Replies to an unnamed critic who calls Kypris a concubine's digitized personality, arguing possession grants abilities beyond the original, citing Chenille's knife-throwing and Auk's machine repair.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theology-and-religion · theories: The ship arrived at its destination recently; Horn's remark rebukes romantic love; Possession grants abilities beyond the original person

As others were confused about this, I'll mention we're talking about the scene in _Lake_ where Silk is down in one of the landers and sees the stars and the outside of the Whorl. He then sees a bright light at one end of the Whorl and then the entire Whorl is shaken in a manner that makes Silk think the hand (or just the finger) of the Outsider has shaken it. Silk's description of the shaking makes it seem like a singular event, not something Whorl inhabitants are used to experiencing, which is what made me think that the ships engines were firing for the first time in many years. Which made me think "The ship has arrived at its destination!" Which puts a kink in the idea that the Whorl has been orbiting for a long time (an interpretation that otherwise appeals to me).

Mantis continues:

I think the key passage (and I don't know where it is - either Exodus or the end of Calde) is where Silk speculates that the more Kypris becomes a true love goddess, the more she will become like the Outsider. Which makes me think that Horn's comment at the end of Exodus about a love goddess not being able to make Mint a warrior was meant to highlight the difference between a "Love Goddess" conception of Love (being all about romance) and a Christian conception of Love (exemplified by one who endures suffering for his beloved). Christians sometimes picture God's love as a consuming fire which devours evil.

So, I read the Horn's comment as a poke by Wolfe saying, "Ha! Were you thinking like Horn that Love is all perfume and roses? Think again!" It certainly goes along with Silk's disillusionment when he realizes Hyacinth can be a nasty fighter when she needs to be.

Someone else speculated that Kypris is just the digitized personality of a concubine, who would know very little about being a military leader, but I think that there's a lot of evidence that possession grants the possessed person abilities far beyond what the original digitized person was capable of. Are we to believe that Kypris the concubine could throw a knife as Chenille does? Or that Typhon the tyrant was able to understand and repair any mechanical device as Auk does?

-Rostrum

1997-06-11 Derek Bell #

Proposes that the deities of the Whorl were programmed with differing abilities so each would hold an advantage in one area, discouraging the cities from uniting against Mainframe. Responds to Derek Bell's suggestion that a shaking felt in The Light Outside might be a ship's engines firing, offering instead that the engines could merely adjust the orbit, and admits the scenario is speculative.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Whorl deities programmed with differing abilities to prevent war

Even if it were a ship's engine firing, it could be to change the orbit slightly. (I include retro-rockets in "engine", as they would have to be huge to alter the orbit and certainly capable of shaking Silk's Whorl as well as his whorlview.)

<speculation>Maybe the different deities were programmed to have abilities the originals didn't so each deity would have an advantage over the others in one area - after all the cities were designed to fairly isolated from each other, both geographically[1] and culturally to prevent them uniting against Mainframe, so it could be that the deities were programmed to discourage them fighting, at least until they reached their desination.</speculation>

Of course, that's not proven, just a (IMHO) plausible scenario - and we know how many of Silks' plausible scenarios were wrong! ;-)

Tercel

[1]I know, it's not the most appropriate world - maybe
"whorlographically" would be better.
