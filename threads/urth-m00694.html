2005-02-05 Chris P replying to Jim Raylor #

Argues that Wolfe's only arguably pro-slavery point is the ancient practice of enslaving a conquered city instead of slaughtering it, and that this cannot be read as an endorsement of slavery. Responds to Jim Raylor's claim that Wolfe argues in favour of slavery when the slave is willing, offering a narrower reading of the point.
disputes · close-textual-reading

The one and only argument that might be considered pro-slavery that I can recall from Wolfe offhand is the reminder that *for the ancients* a certain amount slavery was actually the humane way to eliminate a conquered city as a potential threat - the alternative being wholesale slaughter.

I don't think that could be held as a pro-slavery argument in anything like this context.
