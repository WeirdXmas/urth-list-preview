Severian of Gabala

Urth list · 3 messages · 2018-01-17 to 2018-02-07

2018urthThe Book of the New SunKeith SadlerAntónio Pedro MarquesAb de Vos
The thread: Newcomer Keith Sadler asked whether Severian's name in The Book of the New Sun links to the historical bishop Severian of Gabala, or whether the overlap is coincidence, noting Wolfe seems not to leave things to chance. Ab de Vos answered briefly that there is little connection beyond the shared name and reassured the newcomer not to worry about errors, offering no textual evidence. António Pedro Marques disputed this, arguing Wolfe's world-building is tightly structured and that sharing a historical name does not make a character that person, and speculatively linked the bishop Chrysostom to Gurloes, Palaemon or Severian. The thread ended without further replies, leaving the name question unresolved.
  • Keith Sadler: asks whether the Severian name is a deliberate link, doubting coincidence
  • Ab de Vos: little connection beyond the shared name; brief dismissal without evidence
  • António Pedro Marques: Wolfe's structure is tight; shared name is not identity; speculative Chrysostom link

2018-01-17 Keith Sadler #

Asks whether Severian's name in The Book of the New Sun carries meaning linked to Severian of Gabala, a historical bishop, and whether the overlap could be coincidence. Notes the poster is a newcomer halfway through In Green's Jungles and has a friend who suggests coincidence, while the poster doubts Wolfe leaves anything to chance.
questions · source-and-allusion

Hello all! This is my first post on this mailing list, and thus I apologize in advance if I've made an error, or offered a silly question. I've been following and reading through for a while.

Is there anything to be read into Severian sharing a name with Severian of Gabala? I'm currently half through 'In Green's Jungles' in terms of the Solar Cycle series and I've read through a number of his other books.

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severian_of_Gabala

One rather unhelpful friend suggested that Gene Wolfe was writing in a pre-Google era and thus it might be coincidence? I think I can be fairly certain that there is very little happenstance that Gene Wolfe offers in his worlds.

2018-01-18 Ab de Vos replying to Keith Sadler #

Tells newcomer Keith Sadler not to worry about errors and says there is little to connect Wolfe's Severian with Severian of Gabala beyond the shared name. Responds directly to Keith Sadler's question about the name, offering reassurance and a brief dismissal rather than textual evidence.
answers

Hi Keith,

Probably you will make a lot of errors as everybody does who tries to make sense of Wolfes books. They have an impenetrable character almost esoteric so don't worry about it.

There is not much to connect between the two Severians except the name.

2018-02-07 António Pedro Marques replying to Keith Sadler #

Argues that Wolfe's world-building is tightly structured with little accidental randomness, and that sharing a historical person's name does not make a character that person. Responds to Keith Sadler's remark on Wolfe's research by suggesting the bishop (Chrysostom) inspired Gurloes, with a speculative link to Palaemon or Severian.
disputes · source-and-allusion · theories: Chrysostom inspired Gurloes; Chrysostom may have inspired Palaemon or Severian

Very, very little is how I?d put it. Whatever is random is there just for the purpose of having a tiny bit of randomness in an otherwise stiflingly structured ball of thread.
