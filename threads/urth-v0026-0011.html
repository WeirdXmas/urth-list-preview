1999-04-23 Alice Turner #

Argues that Tzadkiel in The Urth of the New Sun is an angel of divine justice, though not an archangel like Michael, citing Gustav Davidson's A Dictionary of Angels. Responds to Ori Kowarsky's denial of a link between Tzadkiel and Michael and to Christopher Culver's suggestion that Tzadkiel is divine.
disputes · source-and-allusion · theories: Tzadkiel is the Angel of Divine Justice

Now wait a sec here. Tzadkiel is not Michael but s/he certainly is an angel, not an archangel like Michael, but a sufficiently great and important being for Urth/Ushas. S/he is the Angel of Divine Justice. (My info comes from Gustav Davidson's -A Dictionary of Angels-, Free Press 1967, which is not one of those idiotic gift-books of angels, but a scholarly work). I don't have the book to hand, so cannot tell you more, but I do have a xerox of the list of the 30 top angels (and the 103 fallen angels).

-alga

1999-04-23 Jack Lyons #

Disputes Alice Turner's claim that Tzadkiel is an angel, arguing that a fictional being cannot be a supernatural one and citing Gustav Davidson's scholarly dictionary of angels. Ends with a joke that actor F. Murray Abraham is an angel, mocking the supernatural framing rather than the Wolfe text itself.
disputes · source-and-allusion · theories: Tzadkiel is not a supernatural angel

:Now wait a sec here. Tzadkiel is not Michael but s/he
:certainly is an angel, not an archangel like Michael, but a :sufficiently great and important being for Urth/Ushas.

Angels are supernatural beings. Tzadkeiel (acknowledging that this is fiction) is not a supernatural being.
