Themes: Death and resurrection, Unreliable narration

1997-10-23 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Lists eleven Wolfe works in which a narrator is dead or dead-seeming, from 'The Dead Man' and Peace through The Urth of the New Sun to 'V.R.T.' in The Fifth Head of Cerberus. Replies to a thread quip that three or four dead heroes is obsessive, asking whether the count is overstated or missing any stories.

Re: "two dead heroes are okay, three or four are obsessive" or whatever the numbers were, heh-heh, not to be too much of a mischief maker, but let's count:

1. "The Dead Man"
2. PEACE
3. "The Other Dead Man"
4. THE URTH OF THE NEW SUN
5. "The Changeling"?

7. "Forlesen" (another of my top favorites)

11. "V.R.T." of FIFTH HEAD? (More a case of assumed identity?)

Did I leave any out? Or overstate? Surely I must be overstating it!

1997-10-24 adam louis stephanides #

Questions whether narrators who die and are resurrected belong in the same category as narrators dead throughout the story, arguing the count is overstated. Distinguishes UotNS, where the protagonist dies within the story, from V.R.T. in The Fifth Head of Cerberus, where a living man impersonates a dead one, responding to a list of dead narrators.
disputes · close-textual-reading

I have to admit, I hadn't realized there are so many, but it does seem to me you've overstated a bit:

Should having died and been resurrected be considered in the same category as being dead throughout the story?

I would tend to doubt this, though I could be wrong.

The protagonist dies in the course of the story, so perhaps again shouldn't be in the same category.

It's not exactly the hero who's dead here.

I'd say the latter, since it is a living man impersonating a dead man.

--Adam
