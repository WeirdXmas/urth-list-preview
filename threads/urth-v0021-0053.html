1998urthThe Book of the New SunCharles Dye

Themes: Books, libraries and writing

1998-11-09 Charles Dye #

Supports Mantis's idea that Severian read Canog's Book of the New Sun before composing it, arguing that parts of Severian's own autobiography also appear in the brown book Wonders of Urth and Sky. Cites Claw of the Conciliator chapter XXVIII, where wind riffles the brown book's pages and Severian notices phrases, including 'by noyade' from Sword of the Lictor chapter XXIX.
supports · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian's autobiographical writings appear in the brown book; Severian read Canog's Book of the New Sun before composing it

I've gratefully received your latest, Mantis. Thanks. I take it Father Inire helped with the cover.

On page 5, under "Book of the New Sun" you mention the idea that Severian has read Canog's "Book of the New Sun" before living and helping to compose it.

Part of Severian's own autobiographical writings also almost certainly appear in the brown book, "Wonders of Urth and Sky." In Chapter XXVIII of "The Claw of the Conciliator" there's a scene where he's idly watching the wind riffle the pages of the brown book, and mentions some of the phrases that strike his eye. One of them is "by noyade" from Chapter XXIX of "The Sword of the Lictor." Cheers.
