Themes: Gnosticism, heresy and other theologies

2006-07-20 Jonathan Laidlow #

Argues that Wolfe is being disingenuous when he says he borrowed Kabbalah only for ideas and names, suggesting he used it the way other writers borrow from Tolkien for what works in the story. Responds to BSharp's quoted remark that Wolfe never believed in the cabala and treated it as a source of names and ideas, adding a general warning not to trust Wolfe's statements about his own work.
disputes · author-and-biography · source-and-allusion

If there's one thing I've learnt from reading Gene Wolfe it is to never trust the author or anything he says....

But I think GW is being disingenuous here because he borrowed the Kabbalah much as other writers might borrow from Tolkien - not as something he believes in, but something that worked well in the story.

J
