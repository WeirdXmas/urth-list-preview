Black Hole Sun

Urth list · 4 messages · 1999-11-30 to 1999-12-01

1999urthThe Book of the New SunJim HenleyMichael StraightGreene, CarltonDaniel Fusch
The thread: Readers debated how the White Fountain and Erebus work in the Book of the New Sun and whether the White Fountain truly restores the sun. Jim Henley proposed that Erebus is a leftover machine intelligence and the White Fountain a white hole adding matter, while Daniel Fusch asked how the sun could be rebuilt without growing brighter and suggested the giants are ancient dormant beings, admitting no textual support. Michael Straight argued the White Fountain only delays the sun's death since all stars die, and Carlton Greene held it is a temporary fix that fuels the worm at the star's heart, undercutting the allegory. The thread ended in impasse, with the physical mechanics left unresolved and the disputants split on whether the fix is permanent.
  • Jim Henley: Erebus is a leftover machine intelligence and the White Fountain a white hole that adds matter
  • Daniel Fusch: the giants are dormant ancient beings and the New Sun's brightness is an unresolved problem
  • Michael Straight: the White Fountain only delays the sun's death, not a permanent solution to entropy
  • Carlton Greene: the White Fountain is a temporary cure that feeds the worm and undermines the allegory

Themes: The dying sun and the cosmos, Deep time and ruins, Myth and folklore sources

1999-11-30 Jim Henley #

Speculates that Erebus is a chem or giant machine intelligence left over from the first empire, and that the White Fountain could be a white hole that adds more matter than the black hole removes. Responds to Carlton Greene's question about why a White Fountain would stop a black hole, reasoning from the opposite-wormhole concept and the need to rebuild the sun, not merely halt its consumption.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Erebus is a chem or giant machine intelligence; White Fountain is a white hole adding matter

I tend to think of them as home grown - Abaia as maybe the first giant (or maybe just the guy who was _meaner_ than the first giant, if you get my drift), and I'm really taken with the idea that Erebus is a "chem"/giant robot/machine intelligence now. He might then be a leftover from first-empire days. Why is he in Antarctica? Why, so the chips don't overheat of course! <g>

Scylla and Arioch? Oh who cares. Let's face it - they just don't have the Q Rating the other two do. It was a bad career move on Arioch's part to leave Moorcock - he was a star there.

I can see it. A "white fountain" is a wormhole, which is (or was, I'm hardly current) conceived of as the opposite of a black hole. On the theory that black holes are drains into other universes and white holes are drains _from_ other universes, one could imagine some physical interaction whereby the white fountain adds more stuff than the black hole takes away. (At first I thought they might just "cancel out," but that wouldn't be enough because the sun needs rebuilding, not just an end to its consumption.)

Best,

Jim

1999-11-30 Daniel Fusch #

Asks how the White Fountain can rebuild the dying sun without making the New Sun grow steadily brighter, and proposes that the giants Abaia and company are ancient beings dormant in the sea or on Mt. Erebus. Replies to Jim's remark on Scylla and Arioch, builds on a quoted black-hole drain idea, and admits no textual evidence.
proposes · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: White Fountain makes the New Sun grow brighter; Black holes drain into other universes, white holes from them; Abaia and the giants are dormant in our age

Hello!

Jim said...

(snip)
"Scylla and Arioch? Oh who cares. Let's face it - they just don't have the Q Rating the other two do. It was a bad career move on Arioch's part to leave Moorcock - he was a star there."

That's pretty funny. I suppose we could also say that Scylla should have stuck with the Odyssey!

I like the north/west/east/south hypothesis that's up in the air, by the way....

(snip)
"On the theory that black holes are drains into other universes and white holes are drains _from_ other universes, one could imagine some physical interaction whereby the white fountain adds more stuff than the black hole takes away. (At first I thought they might just "cancel out," but that wouldn't be enough because the sun needs rebuilding, not just an end to its consumption.)"

This is how I've always pictured it, too. However, this does raise the question--if the White Fountain adds more than the black hole can take, then won't that build up, causing the New Sun to grow constantly brighter at a steadily increasing rate? That doesn't sound very hospitable! Hmmm...any thoughts on how this is supposed to work? (Physics isn't my strong point, I fear.)

One final conjecture: when reading TBOTNS, I always received the impression that the giants/entities/whatever were immeasurably ancient (of course, all of Urth is immeasurably ancient). They had an "eternal" feel to them. Certainly, there is never any mention in the Book of how these entities arrived. Granted, at the moment, I have no textual evidence to give you, but here's a speculation: what if Abaia & Co. are presumed to be already living in the sea (or on Mt. Erebus, if that is taken as Erebus' physical throne) in our own age--simply inactive (because they are young, or because they are waiting, or for some other reason). Only a hypothesis--but perhaps an interesting one.

Well, that's all I have for now.

--Daniel

1999-12-01 Michael Straight #

Argues that the White Fountain is not a permanent answer to entropy, since all stars eventually die, so it only delays the sun's end rather than solving the cosmic problem. Responds to Carlton Greene's question about whether the white hole merely outshines a black hole, and offers the reading that the white hole prevents a premature death of the sun.
disputes · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: White Fountain only delays the sun's death

On the other hand, it doesn't make much sense to assume the white hole is an eternal solution to entropy. All the stars are gonna die eventually (unless this is a gnab gib universe). The white hole prevents the premature end of the sun.

-Rostrum

1999-12-01 Greene, Carlton #

Argues that the White Fountain cannot truly cure the Old Sun's decay, since it only supplies more fuel for the worm gnawing at the star's heart. Responds to an earlier quoted point about black holes by saying the cosmic fix is temporary, which undermines the allegorical sense of the New Sun burning away corruption.
disputes · allegory-and-typology · theology-and-religion · theories: White Fountain only delays the Old Sun's decay

<<On the other hand, it doesn't make much sense to assume the white hole is an eternal solution to entropy. All the stars are gonna die eventually (unless this is a gnab gib universe). The white hole prevents the premature end of the sun.>>

Certainly the White Fountain will not prevent the eventual fizzle or re-crunch of the universe. What disturbed me is the notion that the White Fountain may not "root out" the worm gnawing at the heart of the Old Sun. It just gives the worm more to gnaw on -- "chew all you want -- we'll make more!" From an allegorical standpoint, it takes away much of the punch of the New Sun burning away the old corruption, etc.

Carlton
