Themes: Books, libraries and writing

1998-06-18 Pedro Jorge Romero #

Argues that Ultan's line about being made blind so he would know who keeps the keeper echoes a Borges poem about receiving books and darkness, so Ultan is really a stand-in for Borges. Cites the parallel passage in the poem and a similar sentence in Borges's Autobiographical Essay as evidence, and recommends a Borges biography.

Alice Turner escribi=F3:

Ok, Here it goes:

At one point of TOOT Ulan tells about becoming blind and adds: "He who had given all books into my keeping made me blind so that I should know in whose keeping the keeper stand" (chapter VI).

That's very similar to a poem by Borges ("Poem of the Gifts", my own translation of the title) where talking about God and commenting about been made head of the National Library he writes: "...with great irony/he gave me both the books and the darkness". I am not sure, but in his "Autobiographical Essay" (in THE ALPHA AND OTHER STORIES) there seem to be a similar sentence commenting on the irony of receiving 800,000 books and darkness.

So, to me it has always been pretty obvious that Master Ulan is really Brogues.

A good biography of Brogues: James Wood's The Man in the Mirror of the Book.

Best

Padre Gorge
