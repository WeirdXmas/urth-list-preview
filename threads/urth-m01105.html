Similarities between TWK and "Golden City Far"

Urth list · 2 messages · 2005-07-18 to 2005-07-20

2005urthRoy C. LackeyDustin Frost

Themes: Genre: science fiction, fantasy, both

2005-07-18 Dustin Frost #

Asks whether the short story "Golden City Far" is a condensed extract of The Wizard Knight, a jumping-off point for it, or related to Castleview. Says the similarities seem too many to be coincidence, and notes the story's appearance in the Flights anthology and Starwater Strains.
questions · source-and-allusion · theories: Golden City Far is a condensed extract of The Wizard Knight; Golden City Far was a jumping-off point for The Wizard Knight

I haven't seen any mention of it (I've been lurking since the beginning of The Wizard postings), and I couldn't pull up anything in a search of the archives, so I thought I'd bring it up here. For any who might have missed it, "Golden City Far" is the last story in the 'Flights: Extreme Visions of Fantasy' anthology, which I think was just published in trade paperback, as well as another upcoming Wolfe collection, Starwater Strains. Anyway, the similarities are too many to be coincidence. Is "Golden City Far" the extract version (like condensed, but moreso)? Was it a jumping off point for TWK? Is there any relation to Castleview? Or am I just reading too much into it?

2005-07-20 Roy C. Lackey replying to Dustin Frost #

Argues that Wolfe's short story "The Golden City Far" does not share a dream world with The Wizard Knight, though it resembles it, and suggests tracing how Bill, like Art, is transformed from an ordinary teenager into a hero. Responds to Dustin Frost's question about whether the story is an extract or a jumping-off point, noting its 2004 copyright and its links to Castleview.
disputes · close-textual-reading · genre-and-craft · theories: The Golden City Far and The Wizard Knight do not share a dream world

Far" the extract version (like condensed, but moreso)? Was it a jumping off point for TWK? Is there any relation to Castleview? Or am I just reading too much into it?<<

I hadn't read the story before, but now that I have I agree that the correspondence with TWK is striking. Still, I don't believe the works are related; that is, I don't believe the "dream" world entered into by Bill is the same world as that entered into by Art in TWK. GW may have been riffing off one of his own ideas when he wrote the short story, which was copyrighted in 2004. The story is also like CASTLEVIEW in so far as the supernatural elements intrude into our "real" world.

What might be profitable to pursue are the stages whereby Bill, like Art, is transformed, with very little effort on his part, from a nondescript fifteen-year-old student into a Hero. The personages he encounters in the process might also inform the relationships Art makes with similar characters along the way in TWK. Then again, maybe not. Could Gylf talk for the same reason that Shep could? I don't know, but I doubt it.

Welcome to the list.

-Roy
