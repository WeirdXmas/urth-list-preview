Seven American Nights explanation wins "award"

Urth list · 4 messages · 2010-08-26 to 2010-08-26

2010urthThe Book of the New SunDavid StockhoffJames WynnLee BermanDave Tallman
The thread: Lee Berman proposed that Seven American Nights reads as anti-colonial, paralleling the accepted solution to the V.R.T. mystery. James Wynn backed a postcolonial reading and claimed Gene Wolfe is a populist right-winger, citing a remark about America being a republic. David Stockhoff disputed the postcolonial label, arguing the story is postrepublican and shows imperial evils only as a dictatorship abusing its own people. Dave Tallman thanked the list for moving him from skepticism to near conviction about the explanation and said he will reread the story to check for remaining problems. The thread ended without resolution, with the explanation still contested and the discussion drifting toward Wolfe's politics.
  • Lee Berman: story is anti-colonial, parallel to the V.R.T. solution
  • James Wynn: postcolonial reading; Wolfe is a populist right-winger
  • David Stockhoff: postrepublican, not postcolonial; dictatorship abuses its own people
  • Dave Tallman: nearly convinced by the explanation, will reread to check problems

Themes: Power, rulers and politics, Colonization and the native

2010-08-26 David Stockhoff replying to James Wynn #

Argues Seven American Nights is postrepublican rather than postcolonial, since the government seizing Nadan no longer claims to be a republic. Disputes James Wynn's postcolonial reading, noting the story shows imperial evils only as a dictatorship's abuse of its own people, and contrasts Nadan's tourism with British tourism in Egypt.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Seven American Nights is postrepublican rather than postcolonial; Nadan's tourism reverses Orientalism

Well put.

I'm not sure how SAN is postcolonial, exactly. If anything, it's postrepublican: the government that seizes Nadan no longer has any pretense to being even a republic.

Nadan's presence in the US is more of a simple literary reversal of Orientalism. The story doesn't show the evils of imperialism except as practiced on one's own people, as dictatorship. If a foreign government were supporting that dictatorship, it might be different---but that doesn't appear to be the case. Nadan's tourism reveals those evils, not the evils of submission to foreign empire that allows such tourism as the British once practiced in, say Egypt, though they are superficially similar.
