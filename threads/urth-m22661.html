2012-06-13 Tony Ellis #

Disputes Mo Holkar's reading of the Gazelle balloon in the Mathoms story, arguing that a crumbly soda label could be Wolfe's deliberate time inversion and that the woman is alone. Cites the balloon's tethering, its historical rail-car towing, and Vance's balloon-rail system in The Faceless Man as grounds, calling the WWI setting the bigger problem.

I don't know... GW might argue that in the context of Civil War era America, a stoppered bottle of sticky pop would more realistically have a crumbly label than a pristine one. He does enjoy that kind of inversion.

If it is supposed to reflect that the balloon has come adrift in time, I can?t say I have a big problem with that. Not in a fantasy story. I have a much bigger problem with someone flying a replica US Civil War balloon in WWI France, with a bottle of 50-year-old soft drink for company.

He says that there was a basket, and ?someone was in it? - singular - so no, she?s alone. An observation balloon doesn?t have a pilot in any case: they?re tethered, you just winch them up and down. Googling the Gazelle I see that they actually used to pull that one along with a rail car. (Which makes me think of Vance?s wonderful balloon-rail transport system in The Faceless Man.)
