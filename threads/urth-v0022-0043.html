Severian's Lies

Urth list · 6 messages · 1998-12-17 to 1998-12-18

1998urthThe Book of the New SunKevin J. MaroneyDave LeblingDerek BellMichael Andre-DriussiMichael Straight
The thread: Participants debated whether Severian's statements in The Shadow of the Torturer are deliberate lies, centering on Greg Feeley's list of Severian's lies. David Lebling argued the river passages contradict each other, while Kevin Maroney and Michael Straight questioned whether the Tower of Torment claim is false, since prisoners may die naturally or some sentences involve torture without death. m.driussi defended Feeley's claim as technically true because torturers pass in and out daily without dying in agony. Derek Bell asked whether the contradictions are lies or just inconsistencies from writing at different times, and suggested suicide as a way a prisoner could die before sentencing. The thread drifted into jokes and a link to a quotations page without resolving the question.
  • David Lebling: Severian contradicts himself about returning to the river, which is evidence of lying
  • Kevin J. Maroney: the Tower of Torment fable may not be a lie if prisoners die of natural causes
  • Michael Straight: the Tower may refer only to the execution wing, so non-fatal torture sentences undercut the lie reading
  • m.driussi: Feeley's claim holds technically because torturers enter and leave daily without dying
  • Derek Bell: contradictions may be inconsistencies from different writing times, or lies; suicide may explain a prisoner's death

Themes: Unreliable narration, Memory and forgetting, Stories within stories

1998-12-17 Dave Lebling #

Points out a contradiction in Severian's narration: he says he never went back to the river where he nearly drowned, yet later describes taking apprentices to swim there. Cites the two passages, one about his near drowning and one from a later chapter, as the evidence.
informs · close-textual-reading

Another one. After describing his near drowning on the day he rescued Vodalus, he says "I never went back" to the river. Yet a few chapters later, ("The Last Year", I think) he describes taking the apprentices down to swim in the river, "though I could never dive in deep water without fear."

1998-12-17 Michael Straight #

Asks whether the Tower of Torment refers only to the execution wing of the torturers' building, since many clients are sentenced to torture without death. Responds to Michael Straight's quote of Greg Feeley's claim that Severian lies in calling the Tower's death warning a fable, suggesting that non-fatal torture sentences undercut the reading.
questions · close-textual-reading · theories: Tower of Torment is not only for executions

Forgive me if I'm misremembering and the "Tower of Torment" refers specifically to the execution wing of the torturer's building, but aren't there a lot of "clients" whose sentence includes some form of torture but not death?

-Rostrum

1998-12-17 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Adds to Viscacha's list of Severian's lies by citing Greg Feeley's example from chapter four, where Severian tells Valeria the Tower of Torment is a fable though it kills all who enter. Notes Feeley's own qualification that the claim holds technically because torturers enter and leave the tower daily without dying in agony.
supports · close-textual-reading

Viscacha,

You've missed one already--what are you trying to do, cover for Severian? <g>

One of Greg Feeley's list of "Severian's lies" is in chapter four, when Valeria mentions the Tower of Torment "where all who enter die in agony."

Quote Severian: "I told her that, at least, was a fable."

Then again, Feeley was flexible enough to note that the lie was based on the technicallity that the torturers themselves entered (and exited) on a daily basis without dying in agony. FWIW!

=mantis=
