Hero as Werwolf

Urth list · 4 messages · 2009-09-25 to 2009-09-25

The thread: Jonathan Goodwin asked whether 'optional' in the opening of 'Hero as Werwolf' is a misprint for 'optimal' and what it implies about the masters' intentions. He later argued it is a deliberate, characteristically Wolfean word, citing the OED on 'werewolf' and its etymology. Mo Holkar argued 'optimal' was intended in both places, since the literal reading breaks the logic between the two sentences, while conceding the question cannot be settled. James Wynn offered an untested guess that the extra 'e' in 'werewolf' comes from Old English pronunciation, and conceded that no alternative to the man-wolf derivation is compelling enough to drop. The thread ends without resolution, with the misprint question and the etymology left open.
  • Jonathan Goodwin: 'optional' is deliberate and Wolfean, supported by the OED entry on werewolf
  • Mo Holkar: 'optional' is a misprint for 'optimal' because the literal reading breaks the logic between sentences
  • James Wynn: the extra 'e' likely comes from Old English pronunciation, offered only as a guess
  • Jonathan Goodwin: the OED doubts the wer- (man) derivation of werewolf

2009-09-25 Jonathan Goodwin #

Asks whether the word 'optional' in the opening recording of 'Hero as Werwolf' is a misprint for 'optimal' and what it implies about the masters' intentions. Reports the class's discussion of a colonization reading and of a woman's line about 'another year' before Paul kills her.
questions · close-textual-reading · theories: Optional development signals a deliberate colonization plan

I'm teaching Island of Dr. Death, etc. in an introduction to short fiction class this semester. We covered "Hero as Werwolf" yesterday, and I had some questions about it that came up during class.

I've looked as best I could through the archives, though I'm sure I must have missed several things. What I saw didn't address these questions, though again I'm sure it's been discussed. Anyway, here's the first one:

When the recording starts, here is what Paul hears:

"I am sorry to detain you, but there is reason to think that you have undergone a recent deviation from the _optional_ development pattern. In a few minutes, it may be useful for us to review what is meant by '_optimal_ development.' Look at the projection." (emphases mine)

It is such in both the Island collection and the Best of. I didn't check the original. I asked the class whether they thought it was a misprint, and, if not, why the two words were used. Most seemed to agree that it was not a misprint but were unsure of the implications. The possibility was raised that the masters intended to use homo sapiens (the "optional" development) to colonize the moon or similar (this being their "optimal" use) for resource extraction perhaps:

"Later one's peer group becomes, for a time, all-important--or nearly so. The boys and girls you see are attending a model school in Armstrong. Notice that no tint is used to mask the black of space above their airtents."

But then we remembered the woman that Paul kills speaking of how in "another year, everything would have been alright." Some thought that implied that she would be leaving, though others assumed that the masters had not created a needless egalitarian society and that she may have been supporting herself via prostitution.

We also discussed why it was Hugo De Vries, of all people, in four-dimensional projection, though this seems relatively clear.

2009-09-25 Jonathan Goodwin replying to Mo Holkar / UKG #

Argues that the word "optional" in the Hero as Werwolf text is not a misprint and makes sense as a characteristically Wolfean touch. Supports this by quoting the OED entry on "werewolf" and its etymology, noting the earliest 1000 entry spells the word "werewolf", while replying to Mo Holkar's view that "optimal" was intended.
disputes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading

If someone has the original printing, or could check the other reprints, and they all match, well, it's not final evidence that it's not a misprint; but I would imagine that someone would have queried him about it along the way. I think it makes sense as is, personally, and is a characteristically Wolfean touch.

Also, the OED entry on "werewolf" notes: "The first element has usually been identified with OE. wer man WERE n.1, but the form were- in place of wer- (cf. however were- and wer{asg}ild WERGELD), and the variants in war-, var-, makes this somewhat doubtful.
Evidence for the real currency of the word (chiefly in the {beta} and {gamma} forms) is rare, and confined to Sc., after the 17th cent. In modern use it has been revived through folk-lore studies, and until recently the most usual form has been werewolf, and occas. wehrwolf from German.] "

The first entry from 1000 has "werewolf" as the spelling.

2009-09-25 James Wynn replying to Jonathan Goodwin #

Proposes an untested hypothesis that the extra "e" in "werewolf" comes from Old English pronunciation difficulty with the "ehr" sound before a "w", offered as a guess rather than proof. Responds to Jonathan Goodwin's quoted OED entry, which doubts the wer- (man) derivation, by conceding no alternative is compelling enough to drop the man-wolf reading.
proposes · source-and-allusion

I'm going to hypothesize that the reason "werewolf" has the extra "e" is because the pronunciation requirements for an Old English speaker to say the "ehr" sound in front of a "w". I'm not offering any proof of this. It is just that the translation "man-wolf" is just too compelling in absence of an alternative.

J.

2009-09-25 Mo Holkar / UKG replying to Jonathan Goodwin #

Argues that the word "optional" in the quoted passage is a misprint for "optimal" in both places, since the literal reading makes the second sentence fail to follow logically from the first. Grounds this in the judgment that "optional development" is not meaningful enough to be authorial, while conceding the question cannot be settled.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: the word optional is a misprint for optimal

Hmm, I tend to believe that it is a misprint, and that it should be "optimal" both times. The literal reading would be
uncharacteristically clumsy: the second sentence wouldn't flow on logically from the first. I don't think "optional development" is sufficiently meaningful to be genuine.

I guess we'll never know, though!

Mo
