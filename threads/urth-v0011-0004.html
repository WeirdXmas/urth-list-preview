The thread: Alice Turner opened a playful game of casting The Book of the New Sun as a dream film, inviting readers to name actors for Severian, Dorcas, Talos and others. Most replies were light fancies: William H. Ansley offered a 'worst case' casting, Robert King cast Seinfeld characters, and Paul C Duggan proposed a Terry Gilliam-directed list. Dan Parmenter objected that the novel is too tied to its literary form to film and needs epic length, while Peter Stephenson and Robert Borski made more reasoned picks, Borski grounding his in the Robert Graves link to I, Claudius. The thread ended with no conclusion, just a drift into comic pairings such as J. Schultz's Happy Days parody.

1998-05-12 Alice Turner #

Proposes a playful exercise: readers name actors they would cast in a dream film of The Book of the New Sun, starting with Severian, Dorcas, Talos, Baldanders, the Autarch, Vodalus, Thecla, Jolenta and Agia. Offers its own casting choices and invites others to disagree and add, noting the same game could run on the Whorl list.
chats

Okay, we've got lots of serious subjects cooking. How about a frivolous one? Surely all of us have an an occasional fantasy of a dream production of these books. Who would you cast? Because we're all of different ages (I presume), let's say that the actor doesn't have to be his or her current age--usually, that would mean that s/he would be a younger version. I'd start with:

Severian: Daniel Day-Lewis
Dorcas: Sissy Spacek. Claire Danes might do as a younger version. Dr. Talos: Dustin Hoffman
Baldanders: that guy who's in the Billy Crystal movie, though it could be a computer-enhanced Arnold, I guess.
The Autarch: Alec Guinness. If you want to go younger, Kevin Spacey (who could also do Talos).
Vodalus: Willem DaFoe
Thecla: Most of you are probably too young to have seen Vanessa Redgrave as drop-dead in -Morgan- or -Blow-Up-, but she would have been divine. Sigourney would have been pretty terrific too.
Jolenta: Well, Marilyn, but can we think of someone still alive? Agia: I draw a blank, but it should be somebody pretty vibrant.

Okay, that's a start. Please disagree, and add. This would also be fun with the whorl list.

-alga-

Jokingly proposes casting Seinfeld characters in The Book of the New Sun, with Jerry as Severian, Elaine as Dorcas, Kramer as Dr. Talos, and Newman as Baldanders. Notes he is rereading the book for the first time in 15 years and hears Richard Dreyfus's voice when Talos speaks.

1998-05-13 Dan Parmenter #

Playfully rejects the idea of filming The Book of the New Sun, arguing the novel is too tied to its literary form to adapt, and that a film would need epic length. Responds to the earlier casting suggestions by agreeing with some, proposing Mia Farrow and other actors, and floating an animated version.
disputes · genre-and-craft · theories: BotNS is unfilmable because it comments on its literary form

From: Robert King <[email]>

Bingo!

Okay, so these are the ones I agree with from other people.

God, if Charlton Heston were a little taller. I mean let's face it, this movie would have to be pretty much an epic on the scale of BEN-HUR. And about 12 hours long! If I were to play devil's advocate, I'd call it unfilmable, just for its length, but I've heard that some brave soul is putting together a new production of LORD OF THE RINGS in New Zealand, so one never knows these days. Back when there seemed to be a less-than-remote chance that WATCHMEN might be made into a film I vehemently argued against, arguing that it was far too tied into the comic book form and that indeed, its plot was almost secondary to the subtle mechanics of its manipulation of the syntax of comics. This is one of the reasons that I think BOTNS is unfilmable: it's too tied into the literary form itself and indeed, seems to almost comment on it. In a sense, Gene Wolfe has given us the perfect SF story: time travel, spaceships, robots, giant bugs (Hethor's), scantily-clad girls, the works, but to simply present these elements in the usual Hollywood way might leave the less imaginative members of the auidence scratching their heads.

I wouldn't mind seeing an animated version of it.

D

1998-05-13 Robert Borski #

Proposes casting actors from the BBC series I, Claudius in an imagined film of The Book of the New Sun, matching Patrick Stewart to Severian, Brian Blessed to Typhon, and Sian Phillips to Agia. Grounds the idea in the Robert Graves influence shared by both works and in Graves's own doubts that Claudius was filmable.
proposes

Haven't been to a movie in years and don't own a television (I'm hearing-impaired and loathe close-captioning), so I'm a little out of touch when it comes to current actors and actresses, but I still bear an enormous fondness for the BBC production of "I, Claudius."

Indeed, if anyone could break down the Book of the New Sun into manageable episodes, I believe it would be the late Jack Pulman, screenwriter for the I,C series.

I also believe that most of the I,C cast would be appropriate in one way or another for roles in any cinematic version of New Sun. To wit:

Severian -- Patrick Stewart
Baldanders or Typhon -- Brian Blessed
Agia -- Sian Phillips
Appian, the old autarch -- Sir Derek Jacobi
Dr. Talos -- John Hurt
Jolenta -- Sheila White

Of course, given the influence of Robert Graves on New Sun, perhaps it's only natural to find correspondences between the paired works, fictive and cinematic, even if one half of the latter will likely never be.

Then again, after an earlier production of I, Claudius starring Charles Laughton and Merle Oberon failed, Robert Graves himself, citing the possibly hostile spirit of the original Claudius, opined his work might not be filmable--so who knows?

Robert Borski
