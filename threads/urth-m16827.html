2011urthThe Book of the New SunLee Berman

Themes: Genre: science fiction, fantasy, both

2011-12-22 Lee Berman #

Argues that Wolfe's 1983 Thrust denial of fantasy magic must be read in context: it excludes Harry Potter-style magic lacking any source, while The Claw of the Conciliator has an explained power source. Replies to Jerry Friedman's quotation of the Thrust article in LU, and cites a Robert Frazier interview from before Urth of the New Sun.
disputes · close-textual-reading · author-and-biography · theories: Wolfe's no-fantasy-magic remark targets Harry Potter-style magic only; Inhumi space travel is deception rather than magic; The Claw has an explained power source

Well, of course you should not ignore it! But I think reading in context is important. By saying BotNS has no "fantasy" sort of magic, I think he is saying there is no Harry Potter/Merlin stuff going on, where magic comes out of a stick with no discernable power source nor recognition that one is needed. The Claw does have a (sort of) explained power source. And the same is true of most other wonders in the Sun Series.

I'm glad you pointed out that Wolfe is quite aware of the deception aspect of some "magic" as well (since I tend to think the inhumi space travel is more deception than magic).

Also, I'm glad you got me steered toward some older Wolfe interviews. The one by Robert Frazier, (found in the collection below) is very interesting, having been done after the first four books of BotNS were published but before UotNS. Wolfe was a bit less guarded back then also.
