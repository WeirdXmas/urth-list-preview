Burns redeems himself

Urth list · 5 messages · 2000-03-23 to 2000-03-26

2000urthThe Book of the New SunNicholas GeversJesper SvedbergKevin J. Maroney
The thread: Nicholas Gevers reported that Millennium had issued the first half of The Book of the New Sun in the UK as Shadow and Claw, praising its production and Jim Burns's cover. Jesper Svedberg attacked that cover as ugly and preferred Pennington's older covers, and Gevers defended the Millennium edition's cover, type and format against the Orb edition. Kevin J. Maroney added that the Orb covers are cropped versions of Don Maitz's originals and named his own favorites. Svedberg then pressed his objection that the Millennium cover is technically inadequate and clichéd, while Maitz's Orb covers are better. The exchange ended in an impasse over taste, with each side keeping its preferred cover artists.
  • Nicholas Gevers: Millennium's UK Shadow and Claw is well produced, readable and a stronger cover than Burns's Short Sun work
  • Jesper Svedberg: The Millennium cover is ugly, clichéd and technically weak; Pennington, Maitz and Frazetta are better
  • Kevin J. Maroney: The Orb covers are cropped Maitz originals, and Maitz's Torturer and Citadel covers are his favorites
  • Gevers: Tastes differ, but the Millennium text and format are superior to the Orb edition

Themes: Books, libraries and writing

2000-03-23 Nicholas Gevers #

Reports that Millennium has issued the first half of The Book of the New Sun in the UK as Shadow and Claw, with the second half to follow, and praises its production and text. Notes that the cover illustration by Jim Burns is stronger than his Short Sun covers and shows Severian against an archaic urban backdrop.
informs

Millennium has just released the first half of THE
BOOK OF THE NEW SUN in the UK under the title SHADOW
AND CLAW, as the leadoff title in their Fantasy
Masterworks series; SWORD AND CITADEL follows in
December. SHADOW AND CLAW is a nicely-produced trade
paperback, reprinting the UK hardcover text, which is
much more readable than that in the Tor Orb edition.
What's more, Jim Burns has provided a fairly
impressive cover illustration, far superior to his
SHORT SUN covers; it shows Severian somewhat in
Pennington mode, against an effectively archaic urban
backdrop. A welcome change.

2000-03-25 Jesper Svedberg #

Criticizes the cover of Millennium's UK Shadow and Claw edition as ugly, praising Pennington's older Shadow of the Torturer cover instead. Asks how the UK hardcover text differs from the Orb edition and what format the Fantasy Masterworks titles are in.
questions

Redeems himself? That cover easily plants itelf on my "worst cover ever" list, it's amazingly ugly, his _OB'sW_ cover is much nicer. I think they should use Pennington's old Shadow of the Torturer cover, it pictures almost the same scene, but is infinetly more atmospherical and beautiful.
What's the difference between the UK hardcover text and the test in the Orb edition?
Are all the titles in Millennium's Fantasy and SF Masterwork series trade paperbacks? (There are generally three different sizes of paperback's in the UK it seems, one is the size of a normal massmarket paperback, one is the size of a normal hardcover and the last one is somewhere in between. Are we talking about the hardcover sized type here?)

// Jesper Svedberg

2000-03-25 Nicholas Gevers #

Defends the Millennium edition's cover, text and format against the Orb edition, saying Millennium's type is more readable and its books use format B. Replies to Jesper Svedberg's cover remark and to questions about editions, listing upcoming Fantasy Masterworks omnibus titles including Time and the Gods and The Worm Ouroboros.
answers

--- Jesper Svedberg

Tastes differ; and compare the Millennium cover art to
the Sword & Sorcery bilge on the Orb cover!

As for the Millennium text and format: the hardcover
text they've reprinted is of a much more readable size
than that of the Orb edition, which was reduced to a
painfully small dimension to minimise the number of
pages, I suppose; and the format is B, the one between
mass market paperback and hardcover. And all the
Fantasy Masterworks will be in B format: already out
is TIME AND THE GODS by Lord Dunsany, an omnibus of
six early collections; and in April there's Eddison's
THE WORM OUROBOROS and TALES OF THE DYING EARTH by
Jack Vance, an omnibus of all four Dying Earth volumes.

2000-03-26 Kevin J. Maroney #

Notes that the Orb covers are cropped versions of the original Simon and Schuster and Pocket editions by Don Maitz, and states preferences for his covers of The Shadow of the Torturer and The Citadel of the Autarch. Names Free Live Free's Gollancz hardcover as a favorite, though it wrongly carries cover art meant for Disch's On Wings of Song.
informs

The Orb covers are cropped versions of the original Simon and Schuster/Pocket editions by Don Maitz. I have always disliked Maitz's cover for _Citadel_ and regret seeing it return on the Orb _Sword and Citadel_, but I have always loved his cover for _Shadow of the Torturer_ and am glad to see it used on the Orb _Shadow and Claw_.

Tastes differ.

My favorite Wolfe cover is the Gollanz hc edition of _Free Live Free_. Unfortunately, it's a cover for Tom Disch's _On Wings of Song_ which found its way on to the wrong book.

2000-03-26 Jesper Svedberg #

Disputes Nicholas Gevers's praise of the Millennium edition by arguing its Book of the New Sun cover is technically inadequate, clichéd and ugly, while Don Maitz's Orb covers are better. Responds to Gevers's point that tastes differ by citing the Fifth Head of Cerberus cover and naming Bruce Pennington, a Swedish edition artist, Jeffrey Jones and Frank Frazetta as preferred artists.
disputes

I won't argue about taste but I just want to say that I'm not Don Maitz' greatest fan either, his BotNS covers range from good (the two that Orb uses on the omnibuses) to pretty horrible (the other two), but they're all technically well crafted and the good ones are IMO atmospherical and not at all as clichéd as their reputation.
The Millennium cover on the other hand is technically inadequate, clichéd, totally lacking in atmosphere and plain ugly. (The guys at Millennium must really have something against Wolfe, the cover of _The Fifth Head of Cerberus_ is also horrible buyond belief.) Bruce Pennington's cover art and the art on the Swedish edition by [Something] Gordon are really good though, and could have been used instead. (But if I could choose, then I'd hire Jeffrey Jones or Frank Frazetta to do the covers, both are truly fabulous artists that would really catch Urth's atmosphere.)

Mm, format B, my favourite. :)
I really don't have a problem with reading small print, I even like it of some reason.
The Fantasy Masterworks series looks REALLY interesting, (I'd buy the entire series if the BotNS cover hadn't been so ugly, I think I'm going to buy the Orb edition instead :)) all of the books planned for release so far have been on my to-buy list for ages, especially Jack Vance's TALES OF THE DYING EARTH, which is almost as good as Wolfe himself.
Also planned for release so far are _Little, Big_ by John Crowley and Robert E. Howard's Conan series collected in two volumes, I've also heard rumors of M. John Harrison's Virconium series.
Other titles that I wouldn't mind seeing would be _The Broken Sword_ by Poul Anderson, something by Avram Davidson (perhaps _The Phoenix in the Mirror_) and _Nifft the Lean_ by Michael Shea.

// Jesper Svedberg
