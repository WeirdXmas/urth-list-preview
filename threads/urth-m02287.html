2006-06-14 PMorris33 #

Expresses unhappiness with Gwyneth Jones's fantasy series "Bold As Love" after admiring her science fiction and criticism, saying the fantasy has gone lifeless on the page. Asks whether other list members read Jones and invites their opinions on the series.
questions

I've long been an admirer of Gwyneth Jones. Her first science fiction book "Divine Endurance" was a perfect gem. And her book of criticism, "Deconstructing The Starships" is simply the best science fiction criticism I've ever read, pace Kingley Amis and John Clute.

But I'm having trouble with her fantasy series "Bold As Love". What's happened to that beautiful Englishwoman? She's gone dead on the page.

Is anyone else in the group a reader of Gwen Jones?

I'd be interested in hearing your opinion.

Thanks to all of you
Pip

2006-06-15 Cliff Judge replying to Rob Thornton #

Recommends Alexander Jablokov's A Deeper Sea and Nimbus. Replies to Rob Thornton's Jablokov thread by calling A Deeper Sea a fun story carrying an allegory of the fall of the ancient world, and Nimbus an edgy sf-mystery-thriller.
supports

I rather thought _A Deeper Sea_ and _Nimbus_ were quite excellent. The former was a fun little story on the surface, but carried an allegory to the fall of the ancient world through it which continually jumped back up in surprising new forms, and the latter I thought was just an excellent, edgy sf-mystery-thriller.

2006-06-15 Rob Thornton replying to PMorris33 #

Recommends Alexander Jablokov's first novel Carve the Sky, noting its immersive worldbuilding and in medias res opening that feeds information gradually instead of infodumps. Responds to PMorris33's mention of Jablokov and David Herter as Wolfean writers, admitting he has not heard of Herter.
supports · genre-and-craft

Did someone say Alexander Jablokov? I strongly recommend his first novel "Carve the Sky" and now that Wolfe is invoked, I can see some connections. For example, "Sky" has a very involving worldscape and Jablokov takes some relish in tossing you in medias res and continously stirring in information rather than habitually using SFnal "infodumps."

Unfortunately, none of his later efforts really matched "Carve the Sky" but I strongly recommend "Sky"

Now who is this Herter fellow? Actually, I haven't heard of him....

back to the lurk,

rob t.
