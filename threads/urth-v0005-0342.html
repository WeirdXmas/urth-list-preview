Wolfe.con

Urth list · 6 messages · 1997-10-22 to 1997-10-23

1997urthThe Book of the New SunScott DalrympleMichael Andre-DriussiDoug EigstiJason Voegele
The thread: Members of the Urth list exchanged practical and social notes about Wolfe's convention appearances, leather-bound editions, and collecting. Doug Eigsti asked about Wolfe's upcoming convention schedule, the leather-bound Shadow of the Torturer, and a hardback of Island of Doctor Death. Jason Voegele and m.driussi answered that the leather-bound Shadow books come from Easton Press and listed Wolfe's convention appearances, while Voegele also relayed Wolfe's report on his Short Sun rewrite. Scott Dalrymple, who posted several times, mainly shared his collection and corrected his own earlier misreading. The thread ended amicably as a set of factual exchanges rather than a debate.
  • Doug Eigsti: asked for convention schedule, leather-bound Shadow edition source, and a Island of Doctor Death hardback
  • Jason Voegele: reported Wolfe's Short Sun rewrite progress and named Easton Press as publisher
  • m.driussi: listed Wolfe's upcoming convention appearances and the Easton Press source
  • Scott Dalrymple: shared prized Wolfe collection and corrected his misreading about the Fifth Head of Cerberus hardcover

Themes: Books, libraries and writing

1997-10-22 Doug Eigsti #

Asks whether anyone knows Gene Wolfe's future convention itinerary and who published a leather-bound edition of The Shadow of the Torturer. Also comments that a new trade edition of The Island of Doctor Death and Other Stories is an improvement over the paperback but a hardback is still wanted.
questions

Jason,

Congratulations on meeting Mr. Wolfe. I would like to meet him someday too. Does anyone have information about his future itinerary? Who published the leather-bound copy of SHADOW? Many of his works deserve fine editions. The new trade edition of THE ISLAND OF DOCTOR DEATH AND OTHER STORIES AND OTHER STORIES, I just love saying that, is a step up from the paperback 1st ed. but a hardback is needed.

=Talon=

1997-10-22 Scott Dalrymple #

Asks whether other list members own fine Wolfe collections, and lists the poster's prized items: a paperback of Operation Ares, a signed Plan(e)t Engineering, a hardcover Devil in a Forest gifted by Wolfe, and a signed Cheap Street Empires of Foliage and Flower. The post is a social note about book collecting, responding to an earlier remark that the Island of Doctor Death trade edition deserves a hardback.
chats

Of course there is a hardback edition-- I'm lucky enough to have a first edition. But it is a bit old, and not very findable. :)

On that note, I'd be interested in discussions with anyone who believes they have a fine Wolfe collection. Mine is probably decent, though some of you may have better. My prize possessions are a paperback of Operation Ares, a signed copy of the original Plan(e)t Engineering, a hardcover of The Devil in a Forest (which Wolfe was kind enough to send me as a gift), and a signed Cheap Street edition of Empires of Foliage and Flower, among others. Any other ardent collectors out there?

Scott

1997-10-22 Jason Voegele #

Reports a personal conversation with Wolfe in which he said he was about 150 pages into a second rewrite of the first of three Short Sun books, due about a year later. Also answers the question about the leather-bound Shadow by naming the Easton Press Masterpieces of Science Fiction series, which also issued Claw of the Conciliator.

When I talked to Mr. Wolfe I asked him if he was working on the Short = Sun books. He said he was about 150 pages into the second rewrite of = the first (of three) books. He said to expect it about this time next = year :(=20
I didn't ask him any details about the work, except to confirm it is = actually three books. I should've at least got the titles out of him, = but I didn't want to push the issue.

As for the leather-bound copy of Shadow, I got that from the Easton = Press as part of their Masterpieces of Science Fiction series. They = also currently have The Claw of the Conciliator as well, and I believe = that they will eventuall go ahead and publish the final two (three?) = volumes of The Book.

informs

As so often happens to me, when I read through my post as it was sent to the list I found an error. Specifically--I guess I must have read the original post (to which I responded) sloppily--I meant to say that I had a hardcover of 5th Head, not Island of Dr. Death &tc. &tc. I don't have a hardcover of the latter, and I'm not even sure that there was one.

Scott

1997-10-23 Scott Dalrymple #

Reports that Easton Press has published The Sword of the Lictor in leather binding and that The Citadel of the Autarch is not yet out, and complains that the Sword volume is noticeably taller than the first two volumes of the set. Praises the books' beauty otherwise, and responds to a quoted earlier post about the publisher's leather-bound editions of the Book of the New Sun.
informs

Easton has also published Sword of the Lictor, which I purchased last year. I don't think Citadel is out yet. I'm very disappointed in Sword, because it's quite a bit larger (height-wise) than the first 2 volumes. Hey-- I'm picky when I spend a lot of money for a leather bound set! I expect the volumes to match! I will say the books are quite beautiful in all other respects, though.

Scott
