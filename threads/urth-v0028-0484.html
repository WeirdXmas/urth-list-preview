Themes: Catholic faith and sacraments, Gods, angels and the Increate

1999-11-19 Nicholas Gevers #

Argues that Ori's blunt reading of whether the Hierogrammates serve the Increate or themselves is too crude, and that Wolfe's Catholic-style theology would route divine influence through mediating intermediaries. Responds to Ori by agreeing with him on the Increate's cruelty toward the Commonwealth but faulting his unsubtle method, noting that Wolfe presents such hierarchy only by remote implication.
disputes · theology-and-religion · theories: Hierogrammates mediate Increate's influence like Catholic sacraments

I agree with Ori in some ways, in particular re the
inexcusability of many of the Increate's actions, such
as the immolation of the Commonwealth's people that
Ushas may arise. But Ori, aren't you approaching the
matter of whether the Hierogrammates serve the
Increate or themselves rather unsubtly, with a
curiously RELIGIOUS hammer-and-tongs zeal? Surely a
striking attribute of Catholicism, in contrast, is its
theological subtlety, its emphasis on the mediation of
all Divine influences through sacraments, priests,
etc.; so why not aliens? Wolfe is hardly going to show
us the Increate giving Tzadkiel his instructions for
the day; such a process is necessarily presented by
remote implication only.

1999-11-20 Ori Kowarsky #

Concedes a point that Ori Kowarsky's hammer-and-tongs approach to the Hierogrammates may be too blunt, suggesting that Catholic readers feel a bridge to the book that non-Catholics lack. Argues that the book works equally as a critique of a holy institution committing worldly crimes and as a defense of it, depending on the reader's view of material versus metaphysical guilt.
disputes · theology-and-religion · theories: The Book of the New Sun critiques or defends a holy institution, depending on reader

Nicholas Gevers:

Hmmm ... well, you may have a point. I'm not a Catholic, and so I've never had to reconcile myself to the worldly actions of an institution which I believe to be holy. As I wrote in a previous post, perhaps that is the "bridge" which some readers bring with them to the book and some others don't. As a result, then, if you do not believe that crimes on the material plane are exculpabale or even laudatory on the metaphysical plane, then TBOTNS serves equally well as a critique of such an institution as it does a defense for those who do.

Ori
