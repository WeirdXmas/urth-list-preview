Spoiler on the connection between New Sun/Long Sun

Whorl list · 5 messages · 1999-02-16 to 1999-02-17

1999whorlThe Book of the Long SunCharles DyeKevin J. MaroneyKieran ClearyRobert Patterson
The thread: Readers asked whether the Long Sun and New Sun books share a universe. Kevin J. Maroney argued the link is explicit, since Lake of the Long Sun says Pas was once named Typhon, the autarch Severian meets, and he also argued it is implicit through Pas's two-headed depiction in the Nightside mural. Kieran Cleary raised the two-headed figure in Kypris's temptation of Silk from Exodus, admitting his evidence was memory-based and thin. Charles Dye and Robert Patterson then turned to the taluses matching Spenser's Talus, citing Wolfe's Onomastics and Dr. Talos in Sword of the Lictor, Chapter XXXV. The thread drifted from the New Sun connection to Talus without a clear resolution.
  • Kevin J. Maroney: the New Sun link is explicit via the Typhon name in Lake of the Long Sun, and also implicit via Pas's two heads
  • Kieran Cleary: the two-headed Pas in Exodus links the series to the New Sun, though the evidence is thin
  • Charles Dye: the Long Sun taluses match Spenser's Talus, supported by the Onomastics entry and Dr. Talos in Sword
  • Robert Patterson: the Talus connection is a shared literary source, joked about Wolfe and Spenser reading the same books

Themes: Doubles, twins and clones, Myth and folklore sources, Robots, androids and artificial life

1999-02-16 Kevin J. Maroney #

States that Lake of the Long Sun explicitly links the Long Sun books to The Book of the New Sun, since a character says Pas was once named Typhon, the autarch Severian meets. Responds to Robert Patterson's question about whether the two series share a universe, noting that this is the only explicit connection and other links remain unproven guesses.
answers · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Pas was formerly named Typhon

The link is explicit, but like so many things in Wolfe's works, is mentioned only once, and you have to make the connection yourself.

Somewhere in _Lake of the Long Sun_, one of the characters (Cypris, if I recall correctly) mentions that before he entered Mainframe, Pas's name was Typhon.

So it is clear that the _Whorl_ was built and launched by Typhon, the two-headed tyrant autarch of long-ago, whom Severian meets in _Sword of the Lictor_.

That's the only explicit connection between the two works that I've seen or heard. Other links have been guessed at, but as nearly as I can tell, there is no conclusive evidence about them.

1999-02-16 Kevin J. Maroney #

Argues that Pas is depicted as two-headed throughout the Long Sun books, citing the mural in the brothel auditorium in Nightside, and so the link to the New Sun's two-headed tyrant is implicit rather than explicit. Responds to Kieran's point about Kypris's temptation of Silk in Exodus, adding that readers would likely have noticed the connection regardless.
disputes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion · theories: Pas's two heads link Long Sun to New Sun

Pas is depicted as two-headed throughout _Long Sun_--I believe that the description of the mural in the auditorium in the brothel in _Nightside_ mentions his two heads (and two penises, since the mural is a blasphemous ribaldry).

Pas being two-headed would not be an *explicit* link to _New Sun_. But people probably would have made the connection even without Kypris's comment--I can't imagine Mantis not having noticed it eventually, for instance, and I distinctly remember that when I read that sentence in _Lake_, I slapped myself on the forehead (literally!) for not having made the connection myself.

Grounds this on that single image from the late Exodus scene and admits the poster may also recall a mural, so the evidence is memory-based and thin.

1999-02-17 Charles Dye #

Agrees with Robert Patterson that the Long Sun taluses match the Spenserian figure Talus, and adds that Wolfe's Onomastics entry on Talos mentions guard robots. Points out that Dr. Talos plays a guard-robot role in Chapter XXXV of The Sword of the Lictor, citing the book itself as evidence.
supports · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Long Sun taluses derive from Spenser's Talus

Not bad. Not bad at all -- this fits the Long Sun taluses nicely!

But doesn't Mr. Wolfe explicitly mention guard robots under the Talos entry in "Onomastics?" A role Dr. Talos actually plays in Chapter XXXV of _Sword_ -- there's one small connection for you.
