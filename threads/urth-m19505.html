Sard

Urth list · 4 messages · 2011-11-05 to 2011-11-06

2011urthDavid StockhoffAndrew Mason
The thread: Andrew Mason reported that Sard, the pawnbroker in the Long Sun books, is a chem, citing Calde of the Long Sun's example of chem names and arguing chems can run businesses though it is unusual. David Stockhoff supplied a dictionary definition of sard as an orange-red chalcedony, suggesting the gem as a possible source for the name. Mason agreed the word primarily names the mineral, adding that readers hoping Sard is human have read it as a fish instead. The exchange ended with the mineral sense accepted, with no further dispute recorded.

  • David Stockhoff: the name likely derives from the gem sard, an orange-red chalcedony

2011-11-05 Andrew Mason #

Reports that Sard, the pawnbroker in the Long Sun books, is a chem, citing the opening of Calde of the Long Sun where Sard is given as an example of a chem name. Concludes that chems can run their own businesses, though this is unusual; the post follows up on the poster's earlier question about Sard's status.
informs · close-textual-reading · theories: Sard the pawnbroker is a chem

A while ago I raised the question whether Sard, the pawnbroker in _Long Sun_, was a chem. I've found positive evidence that he is; at the beginning of _Calde_, 'Sard' is given as an example of a chem name. So it seems chems can have their own businesses, though it's unusual.

2011-11-06 David Stockhoff replying to Andrew Mason #

Provides a dictionary definition of sard as a translucent orange-red variety of chalcedony, also called sardius and sometimes equated with carnelian. Responds to Andrew Mason's point that the Long Sun pawnbroker Sard may be a chem, offering the gem's etymology as a possible source for the name.
informs · source-and-allusion

Sard: "A clear or translucent, deep orange-red to brownish-red variety of chalcedony. Also called sardius. [Middle English sarde, from Old French, from Latin sarda...."

Also apparently another name for carnelian.
