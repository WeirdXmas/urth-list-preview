The thread: Kieran Mullen passed along Paul Witcover's Locus review of The Sorcerer's House, which called Wolfe's late novels hermetic puzzle boxes for expert solvers, and joked the line would make a poor blurb. James Wynn disputed the charge, arguing that Wolfe's novels since The Fifth Head of Cerberus are no harder on a first read than The Book of the New Sun, and offered a personal reading anecdote and a Hellraiser comparison. Fernando Gouvea suggested the review's complaint was partly political rather than literary, said he stopped reading after two sentences, and closed with a joke. The thread ended without resolution, with the exchange shifting from the review's substance to its motives and tone.
  • Kieran Mullen: passes on the Locus review and treats its puzzle-box charge as a counterpoint worth noting
  • James Wynn: late Wolfe is no harder than the New Sun on a first read; the reviewer's complaint is misplaced
  • Fernando Gouvea: the review's objection is partly political, and it did not merit a full reading

2010-03-10 Kieran Mullen #

Passes along a Locus review by Paul Witcover that calls The Sorcerer's House hermetic and compares it to finicky puzzle boxes admitting only expert solvers, and jokes that the line would make a poor cover blurb. Responds to James Wynn's post sharing a Lawrence Person review and a photo of Wolfe at ReaderCon, quoting the Locus critique as a counterpoint.
informs

FWIW, Locus review Paul Witcover panned "The Sorcerer's House" "hermetic" and "like exquisite but finicky puzzle boxes that deny entrance and pleasure to all but a few aficionados who are adept in the strategies necessary to solve them."

How about putting that on the back of a book cover as a blurb....(!)

Kieran

2010-03-11 James Wynn replying to Kieran Mullen #

Argues that Wolfe's novels since The Fifth Head of Cerberus, including An Evil Guest, are no harder than The Book of the New Sun on a first read, so the Locus reviewer's complaint about puzzle-box obscurity seems misplaced. Responds to Kieran Mullen's quotation of Paul Witcover's Locus review of The Sorcerer's House, which called the books hermetic and exquisite puzzle boxes, by offering a brief personal reading experience and a Hellraiser comparison.
disputes

This is probably true for all Wolfe's novels going back to Fifth Head, so I'm not sure I understand Witcover's beef. Even AEG was like The Book of the New Sun in that on the first read, I thought I generally understood what was going on.

Sometimes Wolfe's novels are more like the puzzle box in Hellraiser.

J.

2010-03-11 Fernando Q. Gouvea replying to James Wynn #

Suggests that Witcover's complaint about Wolfe's novels is at least partly political rather than a matter of literary judgment. Replies to James Wynn's quoted remark by saying he stopped reading Witcover's review after its second sentence, and closes with a Monty Python quotation.
disputes

Reading past that first sentence it becomes clear that Witcover's beef is at least partly political. I'm afraid I didn't read past the second sentence.

Fernando
