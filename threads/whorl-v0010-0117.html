The thread: Alice Turner doubted that Horn and Nettle could have written The Book of the Long Sun without supernatural help and compared the narrators to the New Testament evangelists, with Thomas as a fifth. Jim Jordan argued that authorship questions should be settled by Wolfe's own statements and that the books are theological, so Wolfe's intent matters. Jordan then proposed that the Long Sun tracks the Gospels, so an analogue of the Holy Spirit's inspiration likely appears in the Silk narrative. Alex David Groce offered a secular analogue, with mainframe possession standing in for the Outsider, and cited Catholic teaching on late Gospel dating. The thread stayed on the Gospel parallels and Wolfe's intent without clear resolution.
  • Alice Turner: Horn and Nettle could not have written the Long Sun without supernatural help; narrators resemble late gospel writers
  • Jim Jordan: Authorship is settled by Wolfe's own statements, and the books are theological so his intent matters
  • Jim Jordan: The Long Sun tracks the Gospels, so an analogue of Holy Spirit inspiration is likely in the Silk story
  • Alex David Groce: Silk's story is a secular analogue of Gospel making, with mainframe possession as the Outsider's work
  • Alex David Groce: Catholic teaching and Wansborough accept late Gospel dating, so a brain tumor explanation seems unlikely

Themes: Unreliable narration, Books, libraries and writing, Christ figures and messiahs

1999-10-15 Alice Turner #

Doubts Horn and Nettle could have known and written The Book of the Long Sun without supernatural help, and asks if anyone has re-read the Quartet from Horn's view. Responds to 'But I digress. The Rat' by comparing the narrators to the New Testament gospel writers, who wrote decades after their subject's death, and Thomas as a fifth.

An Enraged Enormous Rat is something to see, all right, horrid red eyes aglow, lice-ridden fur bristling, slimy fangs dripping...

How dare you imply that I'd forgotten Nettle's help? Don't forget that Nutria's eat Vizcachas alive bite by bite (whatever Vizcachas are)! Hyoomr aside, I still find it hard to understand how either of them could have known many, many of the things in TBLS. Inventing "typical" dialogue is a long way from the large stretches of material they had to learn from other people, and I'm having trouble thinking of how they might have learned all this stuff, and from whom. But what I really need to do is re-read the Quartet through Horn's eyes, and see if it is really possible that he and Nettle could have written it without "supernatural" help. Has anyone done this?

Yes, indeedy-doody. The four gospeleers of the NT come to mind instantly, writing decades, and dubiously, after the death of their biographee. If you throw Thomas in, five gospeleers.

-alga

1999-10-15 Alex David Groce #

Argues that Wolfe may be presenting the making of the Gospels through a secular analogue in Silk's story, with mainframe possession doing the Outsider's work, and that a brain-tumor explanation seems unlikely because Silk walks the pattern of Matthew, Mark, Luke and John. Responds to Alga by citing Catholic teaching that the Gospels were written later under hard circumstances and Father Henry Wansborough's view that orthodox Catholics accept a 70+ AD date for John.
proposes · theology-and-religion · allegory-and-typology · theories: Silk's Gospel pattern is a secular analogue of gospel composition; Mainframe possession does the Outsider's work in Silk's story; Dr. Crane brain-tumor explanation is ruled out by narrative logic

Since BLS and OBW are highly theological novels, its important to assess what

Ah, but as someone actually going through the conversion process right now, I think formal Catholic position is "sure the gospels were written later, under difficult circumstances, etc." but that, of course, they overcame this by aid of the Holy Spirit. So Wolfe could be giving us an analogous process here-- with "mainframe possession" or something secularly explicable doing the Outsider's work--which is a favorite Wolfe tactic. The Dr. Crane hypothesis COULD hold, but a kind of narrative Occam's Razor makes it seem ridiculous-- if the explanation is just a brain tumor, it's very odd that Silk walks in the pattern of Matthew, Mark, Luke and John.

From Father Henry Wansborough, general editor of the New Jerusalem Bible, in Oxford a few years back I found out that very orthodox Catholics are much more comfortable saying John was written in 70+ AD than many similarly orthodox Protestants. The historical approach to the Gospels and the Theological approach aren't necessarily opposed, because Christianity is quite concerned with historical events--the claim of the Incarnation is a historical as well as metaphysical claim. So, perhaps zapping "dubiosly," what Alga said might match Wolfe's views. In fact, that the canon had to develop and the Gospels didn't just instantly appear after the resurrection is part of the Church's fundamental argument about the Magesterium and Sacred Tradition.
