2008-06-21 Dave Tallman replying to b sharp #

Argues that the "he" in Nod's line about Meschia refers to Meschia's son rather than Meschia, linking it to the Bible's puzzle of Cain's wife. Supports this by citing Genesis on Cain's flight to the land of Nod and knowing his wife, and suggests exultants used alien-derived genetic engineering rather than literal interbreeding, tying this to khabits and giants in Talos' play.
proposes · source-and-allusion · puzzle-solving · theories: Meschia's son is the "he" who dwells in Nod's land; Exultants used alien technology for genetic engineering and cloning; Khabits are royal jelly that keeps exultants young

this garden. I will give him one of my daughters to wife.

Before this line, Nod asks "Have you seen Meschia's son?" I believe the "he" in this line refers to the son, not Meschia himself. It's a reference to the old Biblical puzzle of Cain's wife. After Cain killed Abel, he fled to the Land of Nod, east of Eden, and "knew his wife." Her name and origin are not given in the Bible, so some say she came from the Nephilim or Rephaim.

I don't think Wolfe literally means that the human race is corrupted by mating with aliens. The DNA would be so different that there would be no possibility of offspring. The exultants might have adapted alien technology to do genetic engineering and cloning. The "royal jelly" idea about khabits makes some sense; exultants have to grow to maintain their youth. That could be the reason Talos' play refers to giants.
