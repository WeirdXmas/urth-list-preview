The thread: Posters on a December 2000 thread about Peace and Wolfe's narrative and metaphysics debated Weer's reliability and nature. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued Weer is a ghost narrating from beyond the grave, and that Wolfe uses modernist and postmodern tools selectively rather than as a whole. Adam Stephanides pressed on Weer's noticing of strange events and on the memory-house, and disputed the conservative label and the storytelling reading of the Arabian Nights frame. Alex David Groce argued Wolfe's metaphysics work in a Newman-like way rather than as social construction, and that Lafferty is weirder than Wolfe. The thread shows exchanges and disagreement without a clear resolution.
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: Weer is a ghost narrating from beyond the grave; Wolfe uses modernist and postmodern tools selectively
  • Adam Stephanides: Weer notices many odd things and his memory-house tracks when he remembers; Wolfe is not simply conservative
  • Alex David Groce: Wolfe's metaphysics are Newman-like, not socially constructed; Lafferty is weirder than Wolfe

Themes: Unreliable narration, Ghosts, dreams and visions, Memory and forgetting

2000-12-08 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Mostly a set of brief replies to several list members, with Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's own argument that Peace's narrator Weer is a ghost narrating from beyond the grave, reasoning through five possible readings of his time-shifting. He also says Wolfe uses modernist and postmodern tools selectively, and that the book's mystery-like withholding is not a standard genre convention. Responds to Adam Stephanides and William Ansley about Peace's structure, the narrator's affect and the mystery genre.
proposes · close-textual-reading · genre-and-craft · theories: Peace narrator is a ghost narrating from beyond the grave; Weer travels back in time to his past self; Purgatory option explains Weer's time-shifting; Peace as a stage in a longer cycle

Does Weer ever seem to think that _anything_ is unusual...? (Serious question; with the text not before me, I don't recall.) I mean Weer- the-narrator, not Weer-the-memory. 1. He imagines going back in time and talking to these people. I find this a dull option.
2. He hallucinates same. Duller still.

I guess I got carried away -- I'm semi-desperate to work out why and for whom Weer (believes he) is writing. I think this is as important as the "Time of Narration," which I hold as settled beyond reasonable argument: He's narrating From Beyond The Grave.

2000-12-08 Adam Stephanides #

Argues that Weer, the narrator of Peace, notices many things as unusual, including the falling elm, his stroke, and crossing the Persian room, and that his memory-house location tracks when he is remembering. Responds to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes by admitting loose use of 'deconstruction,' questioning his claim that Wolfe is conservative, and disagreeing that the Arabian Nights frame is mainly about storytelling.
disputes · close-textual-reading

He thinks his the elm tree falling is unusual, I think; he thinks his "stroke" is unusual; he thinks coming across the Persian room is unusual. And he is definitely agitated when he thinks he hears a door close, at the point where he breaks off retelling Julius Smart's story (162 in H & R).

Nice call.

Truth to tell, I haven't read Derrida. I was using the word "deconstruction" in the loose way it's used by people who haven't read Derrida--slipshod of me, but I couldn't think of anything better at the time.

"The Grail lies in the aaaarrrggh...."
"In the aaaarrrggh...."
"He must have died while he was writing it."
"Don't be ridiculous. If he was dying, he wouldn't write aaaarrrggh, he'd just say it."
"Maybe he was dictating."
(Sorry, couldn't resist.)

Well, you were the one who contraposed "using pomo tools" with "putting the story first." At any rate, I'm now more confused than ever about what point you were making in asserting the Wolfe is a conservative writer and "puts the story first." If postmodernists put the story first to, and are really conservative, what writers do you consider to not put the story first and to not be conservative?

While I left this out of my earlier post, because I'm not an expert on the subject, I disagree with you about the Nights. I don't think the compiler or compilers were primarily interested in the process of Scheherezade telling stories; I think they were interested in telling a lot of stories, and the frame-tale and stories within stories are largely pretexts to tell lots of stories.

--Adam
