2002-03-25 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Asks whether a specific children's book series about a boy who raises a talking dinosaur from an egg lies behind Wolfe's story "Petting Zoo," comparing it to how Dr. J/Mr. H may match "Walking Sticks." Offers the premise of Roderick and Rex and a Barney-like tone as grounds, and invites children's literature experts on the list to identify the source.
questions · source-and-allusion

Everybody has different opinions about ranking stories. Nick Gevers dislikes "Walking Sticks," but I like it, in fact I probably like it better than "Hour of Trust."

But now I'm talking about "Petting Zoo."

In short, Roderick and Rex: a boy and a dinosaur. And the dinosaur is a tyrannosaurus rex. Who can talk and is kind of purple in color.

So yes, there is some Barney <tm> in there. But there is also something else, I think--something at the edge of my memory.

I turn to the Children's Literature experts we have around here (I know we have a few). What was that book, or series of books, maybe by HarperCollins now, that had illustrations similar to "Curious George" but was about a boy who grew a dinosaur from an egg and then rode around on its neck? A boy in a polka-dot shirt.

Sure, there are probably a bunch. But I wonder if there is one in particular that matches the back-story of "Petting Zoo" like Dr. J/Mr. H matches "Walking Sticks"?

=mantis=

2002-03-25 Fernando Q. Gouvea #

Identifies the children's book mantis asked about as Oliver Butterworth's The Enormous Egg, in which a boy named Nate hatches a triceratops called Uncle Beasley. Notes a possible distant connection to Wolfe's "Petting Zoo" but says the fit is not quite right, answering mantis's query on the list's spoiler thread.
answers · source-and-allusion

mantis> I turn to the Children's Literature experts we have around here (I mantis> know we have a few). What was that book, or series of books, maybe mantis> by HarperCollins now, that had illustrations similar to "Curious mantis> George" but was about a boy who grew a dinosaur from an egg and mantis> then rode around on its neck? A boy in a polka-dot shirt.

The description sounds like "The Enormous Egg", by Oliver Butterworth. The man character is a boy called Nate who finds a large egg from which a live triceratops hatches. The triceratops is named "Uncle Beasley" and various adventures ensue. There may be some distant connection, but it doesn't seem to quite fit the Wolfe story.

Fernando
