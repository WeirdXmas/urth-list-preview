2013-07-25 Brian Lovely #

Proposes that Severian's replacement bodies are drawn from other timelines in which he did not die, reading much of the books' magic as combined technologies that include considerable time manipulation. Responds to the earlier thread on cloning, offering this as an alternative explanation to cloning for how his bodies return.
proposes · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Severian's replacement bodies come from other timelines

The discussion of cloning a while back was of interest to me, because I always thought that Severian's replacement bodies were pulled from other timelines, where he hadn't died--and in fact considered most of the "magic" in the books to be combinations of technologies, including quite a bit of time manipulation.

2013-07-25 Jeff Wilson replying to Brian #

Argues that Severian's replacement bodies and his perception of time stem from an inborn gift rather than Yesodi machinery, though the Yesodi do use time-spanning devices like aquastors and eidolons. Responds to Brian's suggestion that the replacement bodies come from other timelines' Severians by raising the questions of why the survivor was not kept and why rival Severians do not struggle.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Replacement bodies come from other timelines' Severians; Severian's time perception is an inborn gift

Yes, but....

The Yesodis do seem to have machinery that spans time, and they can use long-forgotten Urth technology across times, like the projection of aquastors and eidolons, but Severian seems able to perceive across time and to bend the time-like accumulation of entropy by some natural or at least inborn gift, rather than by gadget.

You suggestion that the replacement bodies come from other timelines' Severians is interesting, though it raises the questions of why they didn't just go with the survivor entirely, and why doesn't a struggle with the other Severian ensue?
