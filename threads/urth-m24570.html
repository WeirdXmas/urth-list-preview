Marsch an assassin?

Urth list · 9 messages · 2013-03-24 to 2013-03-25

2013urthJeff Wilsonnate jarvisMarc Aramini
The thread: The thread asks whether Marsch in The Fifth Head of Cerberus was really a hired assassin, with his ballistic chart as evidence. Marc Aramini proposes the assassin reading and repeatedly floats a rifle-conversion or shared-gravity explanation for the chart. Nate Jarvis questions how ballistic tables could fit two planets, and Jeff Wilson argues that gravity alone cannot make them match because planetary radius matters, so the worlds would need to be identical twins. The exchange turns to whether the Field Guide and the journal are separate books and whether a missing journal would look suspicious to V.R.T. Aramini eventually answers Wilson's assassin question only with a joke, and the thread ends without agreement, drifting into the physics and the book's details.
  • Marc Aramini: Marsch was a hired assassin, and the ballistic chart may be a rifle conversion for St. Anne or St. Croix
  • Jeff Wilson: Gravity alone cannot match ballistic tables, since planetary radius changes curvature; the journal was Marsch's important working record
  • nate jarvis: Gravity differences should alter range, so the ballistic tables puzzle needs explaining
  • Marc Aramini (second stance): The field guide is a separate published volume that Marsch used as scientific equipment

Themes: Hidden identities and parentage, Masks, disguise and shapeshifting, Robots, androids and artificial life

2013-03-25 Jeff Wilson replying to nate jarvis #

Argues that identical gravity would not suffice for two planets to produce the same ballistic tables, since differing radius alters the apparent curvature of the surface, so St Croix and St Anne would need to be literally identical twin planets. Responds to nate jarvis's question about gravity by working through the physics and then asking what Homeric Greek science has done.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: St Croix and St Anne are identical twin planets

Yes, and even if surface gravity were essentially the same, as for Mars and Mercury, the difference in radius changes the perceived rise due to curvature of that surface. St Croix and St Anne would need to be literally identical twin planets.

Which I suppose is within the theme of the stories, but we must note that the only exact duplicates of people spring from the experiments by Number 5's predecessors. What has Homeric Greek science done?

Yes. Marsch's journal shows him hunting but there is no sure indication that the ballistic chart is a rifle conversion for st. Anne or st. Croix from the earth values Marsch was used to.

How much of Marsch' personality is left in VRT? The saliva of the shadow children switch the perceptions of Sandwalker and eastwind. VRT takes that book even though heavy items cost a lot to transport from planet to planet, and little else.

Also, st Anne and st croix have a similar year, perhaps their gravity is closer to one another than to earth.

Maybe VTT just got the surface feeling that the book was important and his understanding is just animal mimicry, as he picks up speech.

2013-03-25 Jeff Wilson replying to Marc Aramini #

Argues that planetary year is independent of planetary gravity, so a shared year on St Anne and St Croix does not show closer gravitational ties, and that the journal was Marsch's important working record. Responds to Marc Aramini's claim that V.R.T. only absorbed the journal's importance, reasoning that a spy-catcher would find a scientist's missing journal more suspicious than a retained one.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · puzzle-solving · theories: Marsch's journal entries may not be genuine

Planetary year is independent of planetary gravity.

The book *was* important to Marsch, it was his journal. If any of the entries are genuine, VRT would have seen Marsch spending time frequently with the book.

It should be more suspicious to a spy-catcher for a scientist *not* to have retained his journal or other working papers.
