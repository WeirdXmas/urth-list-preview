2012-01-18 Brian Lovely #

Proposes that W. Y. Evans-Wentz's study of Celtic fairy belief is one of the sources Wolfe drew on for his research. Asks whether others have read the book and agree, noting many striking similarities seen while reading without taking notes.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Evans-Wentz's Fairy-Faith is a source for Wolfe

I've been reading Evan Wentz' "The Fairy-Faith i Celtic Countries," and I'm fairly convinced it's one of the sources Wolfe uses for his research. I've noticed many striking similarities, but have just been reading through rather than stopping to take notes.

Has anyone else read this book, and do you agree with me as to it's being a likely source for Wolfe?

2012-01-19 David Duffy replying to Brian #

Suggests that Evans-Wentz's Fairy-Faith in Celtic Countries may be a source for Wolfe, but proposes that Andrew Lang's writings are a likelier one, since Wolfe names Lang as a childhood favorite. Points to Lang's introduction to The Secret Commonwealth, which covers similar ground, and offers Dunsany's Curse of the Wise Woman as another possible source.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Andrew Lang is a major source for Wolfe

I've looked through it, and agree it might be a source. But Wolfe always mentions Andrew Lang's books as a childhood love. Lang also edited _The Secret Commonwealth_, and his intro to that covers similar ground to Evans-Wentz. Another source might be Dunsany eg _Curse of the Wise Woman_.

Cheers, David Duffy.
