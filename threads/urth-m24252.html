Themes: Ghosts, dreams and visions, Hidden identities and parentage

2013-12-11 Nicholas Jost #

Argues that The Land Across is a Little Englander story about the dangers of foreign involvement, and theorizes that Grafton is the son of Leader, a US-backed post-Cold War figure with dual citizenship. Also suggests Grafton is possessed by his country's patron saint, Vlad, and admits being unable to explain the opening train scene.
proposes · puzzle-solving · author-and-biography · theories: Grafton is Leader's son; Grafton has dual citizenship; Grafton is possessed by Vlad; The Land Across is a Little Englander story

I'm going to posit that this, as the appendix makes clear is a "Little Englander" story. A fight between two peoples and a story about why America has to be careful about foreign involvement.

(Spoiler)
-I think Grafton is Leaders son. Graftons father worked for State...but I don't think he was a US citizen. I think the US set him up as a post Cold War leader. Grafton has dual citizenship which is why he can get a national ID card. -Can't figure out the opening train scene.
-Grafton is possessed, possibly by the Patron Saint of his country, Vlad.

> It's possible Grafton is seeing things. There is obviously "someone" operating as the third border guard / Leader, but only Grafton seems to think they look similar. Confusion on his part? Grafton doesn't recognize the man on the posters right away either.

> Ever see the movie Cypher? (Spoilers) A guy finds himself in increasingly odd circumstances, trapped in an ever growing conspiracy, and then he ultimately figures out he was orchestrating the whole thing all along; he had given himself an amnesia-inducing treatment so that his previously laid plans for world domination could come to fruition without any interference. Including his.
> 
> I get this vibe from Grafton. At the beginning of the novel, he knows nothing about the country. By the end, he is getting its highest award, speaks the language, is a member of the secret police, etc. Almost like he was a tool all along of the country's plan to oust the Unholy Way.
> 
> This makes sense in regards to Volitain and the "treasure" as well. Are we supposed to believe that one hundred years' worth of treasure hunters didn't think to look in the chimney? I think Martya and Volitain made up the story and planted the treasure, in order to add to the "game" Grafton has to play / solve. This makes sense also considering how quickly they enlist his help in their scheme. Within an hour of meeting them both, Grafton is brought in on the treasure hunt.
