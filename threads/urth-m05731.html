Juturna

Urth list · 4 messages · 2008-07-09 to 2008-07-10

2008urthThe Book of the New SunJeff Wilsonb sharpAnthony Davis
The thread: Thread asks whether Juturna's relationship to Severian can be explained by family ties, and what Juturna is. b sharp proposes that Juturna, Severian and the undines are all pinched-off versions of Abaia, citing the five coffins and the incestuous attraction. Jeff Wilson counters that Juturna and Severian are probably half-siblings, linking their attraction to a longevity-by-growth theme involving undines and sea creatures, with size differences among Idas, Juturna and Merryn as support. Anthony Davis then asks who Juturna is, admitting he has just read the books for the first time and confused her with Jolenta. The thread ends with no response to his question and no resolution of the sibling dispute.
  • b sharp: Juturna, Severian and the undines are pinched-off versions of Abaia, explaining the coffins and attraction
  • Jeff Wilson: Juturna and Severian are likely half-siblings, with attraction tied to undines and sea creatures growing
  • Anthony Davis: asks for a reminder of who Juturna is, confused with Jolenta

Themes: Fathers, mothers and children, Water, the sea and drowning, Love and loss

2008-07-09 b sharp #

Proposes that Juturna, Severian and the undines are all pinched-off versions of Abaia, arguing that this explains the five coffins in Severian's mausoleum and the family's incestuous attraction. Grounds the idea in Juturna's "father-husband" remark, the Naviscaput Corn Maidens parallel, and Idas's large size as a hurdle the theory must overcome.
proposes · puzzle-solving · source-and-allusion · theories: Juturna is Severian's sister; Severian and undines are pinched-off versions of Abaia; Undines are large by nature

One support for Jeff Wilson's idea that Juturna is Severian's sister could be their shared pale white skin. In my view, another would be their bizarre sexual attraction for each other, since I suspect Severian's family is so cursed to feel that way about each other.

This story is full of liars but I tend to believe Juturna when she describes Abaia as her (and her sisters') "father-husband". If there is any correspondence between the undines and the Corn Maidens of the Naviscaput story, we may believe the undines aren't really very loyal to their incestuous father, Abaia and might lean toward Severian and the New Sun.

If Severian is Juturna's brother, is he to be considered a son of Abaia? I think the most plausible scenario within that theory would be for Juturna and her sisters and Severian to all be pinched off versions of Abaia. It would explain (for me) why there are 5 coffins in Severian's mausoleum, two opened. The first opened would be Severian 1 (Reechy?) and our Severian the second. The other three go unused.

I think a real hurdle for this theory to overcome could be Idas. She is so tall as a child it implies that undines are large by nature, not as a result of being sea-dwelling. If siblings why would Juturna be so big and Severian normal sized? Also, if undines were pinched off we would think they could be of any size. But Severian's encounters with Juturna and Idas also suggests that undines are extremely large by nature, not subject to choice.

-bsharp

2008-07-09 Jeff Wilson replying to b sharp #

Argues that Juturna and Severian may be half-siblings rather than full ones, and that the attraction between them fits a longevity-by-growth theme in which undines and sea creatures keep growing. Supports this with lobster growth, Baldanders joining the undines, and the size differences of Idas, Juturna and Merryn, answering b sharp's sister theory.
proposes · theology-and-religion · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Juturna and Severian are half-siblings; Undines grow indefinitely like lobsters; Baldanders joined the undines from weight

uncanny attraction between long-lost siblings is fairly common in literature and not unknown in RL

This could be a habit of speaking for an adopted sister. It may also be that Jut and Sev are half-siblings rather than full.

Unlikely, or he wouldn't be at a disadvantage underwater even the first time.

It's part of the longevity-by-growth theme. Many sea creatures live and grow for an indefinite period; lobsters, for instance, grow about 1 lb a year and have been found up to a couple hundred pounds. Baldanders redsiscovered this principle and ended up joining the undines under the water because his weight became prohibitive.

There is also the property of aquatic life to limit their growth according to the size of the habitat.

So...Idas live underwater, growing taller than a man by the time she has noticeable breasts, and then is sent onto The Ship. Juturna is larger because she's been living underwater longer. Merryn, an undine changeling many generations descended from the corn maidens is brought up on land and is stunted because of it, but still has the youthful face from undine longevity.
