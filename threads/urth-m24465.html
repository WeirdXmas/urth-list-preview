Time, Not Cloning

Urth list · 5 messages · 2013-07-29 to 2013-07-30

2013urthThe Book of the New SunJeff WilsonBrian LovelyDavid Duffy
The thread: Participants debated how the Yesodis replace Severian's dead bodies in The Book of the New Sun: by cloning, or by borrowing a living Severian from another timeline. Jeff Wilson argued the aquastors, including those of Triskele, Malrubius, and Master Ash, show cloning, and questioned why a privileged timeline would be chosen and a living Severian moved rather than preserved in place. Brian defended time-borrowing, using a "borrowed cup of sugar" analogy and pointing to time technology such as vertical travel and the Last House, and answered that no coexisting Severian exists since a new one is only drawn when one dies. David Duffy offered Blish's Jack of Eagles superposition model as a possible source, comparing component timelines fading with Master Ash. The thread ends without resolution, with the cloning and time-borrowing readings still opposed.
  • Jeff Wilson: replacements are clones (aquastors and khaibits), so time-borrowing is unnecessary and the choice of timeline is unexplained
  • Brian: the Yesodis switch Severian's timeline and borrow a body from a timeline where he did not die, a no-coexistence arrangement
  • David Duffy: Wolfe may have drawn on Blish's Jack of Eagles, with weighted timeline superposition and fading timelines comparable to Master Ash

Themes: Robots, androids and artificial life, Time loops and causality, Doubles, twins and clones

2013-07-29 Jeff Wilson replying to Brian #

Questions why the Yesodis would privilege one timeline and transplant a living Severian from another Urth to replace a dead one, rather than keeping the original alive. Points out that other timelines would presumably have their own watchers, and that a living Severian in identical circumstances could simply be preserved in place.
questions · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Yesodis replace dead Severians with Severians from other timelines

I'm still not seeing why a particular timeline is privileged above the others and made the recipient of their Severians when he keeps dying. Do the other timelines not have Yesodis watching over them? If it's the same gang of Yesodis watching over several Urthly timelines, why do they need to move a live Severian from Urth A to replace the one that died on Urth B ? Why don't they just concentrate on keeping the one from Urth A alive there, since he is in the identical circumstances until that moment?

2013-07-29 Jeff Wilson replying to Brian #

Argues that the Echopraxia prostitutes are khaibits, clones, and that the Yesodis can make aquastors without borrowing from other timelines. Cites the appearances of aquastors of Triskele and Master Malrubius, and the trial in The Urth of the New Sun where aquastors of Master Ash appear, as evidence against a time-borrowing explanation.
proposes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion · theories: Echopraxia prostitutes are clones; Yesodis make aquastors without borrowing from other timelines

The girls at the bawdy house called Echopraxia are said to be khaibits, which is supposed to be the Egyptian word for "shadow", and echopraxia is a medical term for the involuntary repetition of another's actions, so yeah, clones.

But the Yesodis do seem have the ability to make aquastors (duplicates of beings) without borrowing from other timelines, we see aquastors of Triskele and Master Malrubius appear and disappear and dissolve into their component energies, and then at the trial in URTH they pull up aquastors of people who wouldn't have an alternate timeline counterpart, like Master Ash.

2013-07-29 Brian Lovely replying to Jeff Wilson #

Argues that the Yesodis replace a dead Severian by switching the protagonist's time line rather than by cloning, offering a "borrowed cup of sugar" analogy. Responds to Jeff Wilson's objection about why a privileged timeline is chosen, citing the time-related technology in the books such as vertical travel, the Last House, and the House Absolute garden displays.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: Yesodis replace dead Severian by switching timelines; Cloning is less likely than time travel

I don't honestly know how time would work for the Yesodis. I'm imagining a situation where you are in your "prime" timeline when Severian dies on you. So you get a replacement from a thread where he hasn't died. Why not switch yourself to that thread? I don't know. Maybe your current timeline is right where you want it except for the unfortunate demise of Severian. So you borrow a cup of sugar from your neighbor and bring it back to your house, not move into your neighbor's house because they don't happen to be out of sugar. I would accept cloning, except there seems to be a lot of time related technology in the books. Vertical motion through certain spaces seems to cause travel through time, the Last House seems to be a very "time-volatile" locale, the displays in the House Absolute Garden where you seem to be in two times at once, etc. I suppose the prostitutes (boy I can't remember any of the exact terms from the books) imply cloning, and that's enough to allow for cloning to be the method by which Severian is replaced when necessary.

2013-07-30 David Duffy replying to Brian #

Suggests Wolfe may have drawn on the timeline model of Blish's Jack of Eagles, where the perceived timeline is a superposition weighted toward likely histories rather than full many-worlds branching. Notes that in one episode a component timeline fades as its probability falls, after the protagonist's actions shift the whole ensemble, comparing this to Master Ash.
proposes · source-and-allusion · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Wolfe drew timeline model from Blish's Jack of Eagles; Timeline is superposition weighted by likely histories

It's possible Wolfe was recalling the model in Blish's _Jack of Eagles_, where the "single" main timeline we perceive which is actually a superposition dominated by the most likely histories. So it's not quite what I think of as a "full" many worlds where all outcomes are equally real, and any actions you perform are reversed in the other branch.

MINOR SPOILER

Like Master Ash, there is one episode where a component timeline fades as its probability falls, as a result of actions of the protagonist that cause the whole ensemble to shift in his favoured direction.
