AEG spoilers - Gid as Timelord?

Urth list · 4 messages · 2008-10-28 to 2008-10-28

2008urththalassocratDave TallmanAdam Thornton
The thread: Posters debated whether Gid in Wolfe's Exodus/An Evil Guest is a real child or a time-traveler, perhaps an imposter sent to avoid time complications like Cassie's ethermail. Dave Tallman argued Gid is shaped by two fathers, the Woldercan biological father and adoptive father Robert Chase, who might have been a dreamer, wizard or time-traveler, and linked this to Lovecraft and Finney. Adam Thornton extended the Lovecraftian reading, casting Gid as a son of an Outsider-like god drawing on old-wizard motifs. Mr Thalassocrat pressed the time-travel reading using birth and ethermail discrepancies, the watch, ages and ambassadorship lengths, and the speed of Gid's leg healing. The thread ends with four proposals and no recorded replies, so no agreement emerged.
  • Dave Tallman: Gid is not a true time-traveler but shaped by two fathers, a Woldercan biological father and adoptive father Robert Chase
  • Adam Thornton: Gid may be a Lovecraftian son of an Outsider-like god, tied to the old wizard theme
  • Mr Thalassocrat: Gid may be a time-traveler, with Bob as a possible earlier link to Heinrich Pirtle
  • Dave Tallman: birth announcement may have been sent to avoid time complications, so Gid might be an imposter

Themes: Time loops and causality, Doubles, twins and clones, Ghosts, dreams and visions

2008-10-28 Dave Tallman replying to Mr Thalassocrat #

Proposes that the birth announcement message, like Cassie's ethermail, may have been sent to avoid time complications, so Gid might be an imposter rather than a real child. Grounds this in the time discrepancies around Gid's birth and ethermail, while noting the President treats him as the genuine son of the ambassador.
proposes · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Gid is an imposter, not the ambassador's son

If the birth announcement message was treated like Cassie's ethermail (sent in order to avoid time complications) then it's possible that there was no child at all and that Gid is an imposter. On the other hand, the President treats him as the genuine son of the ambassador (p )

2008-10-28 Dave Tallman replying to Mr Thalassocrat #

Argues that Gid in An Evil Guest is not a true time-traveler but a being shaped by two fathers: a Woldercan biological father and adoptive father Robert Chase, who may have been a dreamer, wizard or time-traveler. Grounds this in textual details such as Gid breaking a door, his dancing after losing a leg, his time-sense, and Ambassador Klauser's sighting of him on Woldercan, plus links to Lovecraft and Finney's Dr. Lao.
proposes · puzzle-solving · source-and-allusion · theories: Gid was born on Woldercan; Robert Chase was an old dreamer or time-traveler; Chase modeled on Lovecraft's Randolph Carter; Lao came from Woldercan and brought crossbreed monsters

Gid cannot be too powerful. A time-traveler with the ability to freely change the past would not allow himself to be shot and lose a leg, for example. I don't think he can consciously warp reality enough to account for Cassie's address changes, for the same reason. Still, he is very capable:

1) He's strong, since he can break through a door with one lunge (p. 82). 2) He's agile, since he can do professional-level dancing even after losing a leg.
3) He has a time-sense, though there are places it doesn't work (p. 75). Perhaps when he's in space?
4) He may have matured more quickly than usual, allowing him to appear older than he really is.
5) He has a remarkable range of knowledge, including physics, Earth arcana, and old sea shanties.
6) He can enter a locked room through a one-foot-square hole.

I think these can be accounted for by two factors: his biological father and his adoptive father. I think we should believe he was born on Woldercan. Ambassador Klauser saw him there as a young boy (p. 297). Klauser also says that Wolder males try to seduce Earth females, and Chase tells us that they can reproduce with other species. This probably accounts for his physicial traits.

His adoptive father is Robert Chase, who seems to have known Lovecraft (p. 75). If Lovecraft used him as the basis for his stories of Randolph Carter, we know that he is an "old dreamer" (Dream Quest of the Unknown Kadath) who is well-aquainted with the mysterious beings of Earth (who are aliens, at least in this novel). Bob Chase was either an old wizard who had some means of prolonging his life, or a time-traveler. He probably taught his son a great deal.

There is also the connection to Charles Finney and the "Circus of Dr. Lao" to account for. Bob Chase became the first ambassador to Woldercan. Is it too much to speculate that he used some knowlege he gained through his arcane research to get a clue to the planet's location from the old space-faring races that are hidden on Earth? He may have collaborated with Chuck Finney, a descendant of the Charles Finney who wrote a novel based on his encounter with Dr. Lao and his circus, in order to get the planet discovered. Lao himself may have come from the planet Woldercan, and brought some of the humanoid-animal crossbreed monsters from there (there was a centaur in his circus, for example).

2008-10-28 Adam Thornton replying to Dave Tallman #

Suggests that Gideon in An Evil Guest might be a Lovecraftian figure, a son of the Outsider-like god, linking him to the old wizard theme in Lovecraft's fiction. Grounds this in references to 'The Thing on the Doorstep' and 'The Dunwich Horror' and the idea that old wizards never die, responding to Dave Tallman's claim that Robert Chase knew Lovecraft.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Gideon is a son of Yog-Sothoth

Oh, there's a whole Lovecraftian thread on how Old Wizards Never Die...see "The Thing on the Doorstep," for example.

But it wouldn't be exactly a shock to find that Gideon was a Son Of Yog-Sothoth (or whatever they call him on Woldercan) either (cf. "The Dunwich Horror").

Adam
