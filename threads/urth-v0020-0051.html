1998-10-16 Robert Borski #

Asks why an aquastar of the dead torturer Malrubius would carry more authority than a letter from a living figure, questioning whether aquastars keep only memories or also a trace of the Hierodule machine. Grounds the question in Severian's remark about the letter in the Quasar cabin and Idas's later confirmation, and briefly notes that Miles is also an aquastar.
questions · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Hierodule machine adds itself to each aquastar

When Severian's cabin aboard the Quasar is broken into by Idas and plundered, Sev has only one big worry: "Whatever he had sought, I had possessed only one treasure: the letter Master Malrubius had given me, identifying me as the legitimate Autarch of Urth." (URTH, p.42)

Malrubius, being dead of course, he means the aquastared version of Malrubius, as Idas later confirms ("Your letter, the one that the aquastars of Urth gave you for the Hierogrammate...")

But hold on a second here. How is it that an aquastar, and of Malrubius no less, has this much cachet with a grand Pooh Bah of Briah? Aren't aquastars true to the memories held of them? And if they are, why would the Hierogrammate find a letter from Malrubius--a dead Master of the Torturers--more compelling and legitimate than one from, say, Father Inire? Or does the Hierodule animating machine put a little of itself into each aquastar--kind of a secret sharer, co-intelligence (in which case a letter from Triskele would have as much value as one from Malrubius)? I have my theories, of course; but rather than be laughed off this list once more time, I'd love to hear anyone else's thoughts first.

Oh yeah. While I'm talking about aquastars, Miles is one.

Robert Borski

1998-10-17 Alex David Groce #

Suggests that Severian's belief that the aquastored Malrubius's letter certifies him as autarch is a circular proof, since he regards himself as self-ruling and only someone who has used the phallus and gained memories would picture an aquastor endorsing him. Offers this as a reading of Severian's self-regard rather than of the letter's authority, closing with a John 8:32 epigraph.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theology-and-religion · theories: Severian's autarch-hood is a circular proof

Perhaps, since they reflect Severian's ideas of the person, it's a circular proof--because Severian feels that Malrubius would certify him as autarch, that seals his autarch-hood--after all, the autarch is the "self-ruler". Perhaps only someone who's used the phallus and gained the memories can conceive of an aquastor (especially a mentor such as Mal.) as viewing him as the proper autarch?
