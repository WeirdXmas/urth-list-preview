This Week in Google Alerts: Gene Wolfe podcast interview

The thread: A June 2013 thread shared a podcast interview with Gene Wolfe, with Gwern Branwen posting a topic outline and timestamps covering genre, Borges, Chesterton, Dickens and The Land Across. The discussion turned to a remark in the interview that Wolfe's wife, Rose, has Alzheimer's, which Marc Aramini connected to the high cost of medical care. Gwern said he could not be certain the speaker said Alzheimer's because of his hearing, though it sounded that way, and invited others to listen. Fernando Gouvea shared a comparable family experience with home care costs. The exchange was personal and informal, with no critical debate about the fiction, and it ended without any further argument.
  • Marc Aramini: personal reaction to the interview's mention of medical bills and his wife's long illness
  • Gwern Branwen: shared the interview link and outline and checked what was actually said about Rose's illness
  • Fernando Gouvea: family spends similar amounts on a sick father's home care

2013-06-26 Gwern Branwen #

Shares a link and detailed topic outline of a 2013 podcast interview with Gene Wolfe, listing discussion points such as science fiction versus fantasy, Borges, Chesterton, Dickens, Pringles and The Land Across. Provides timestamps and a quoted passage in which Wolfe recounts his plot summary of that novel and a personal story about Dickens.
informs

http://www.tommerritt.com/2013/06/25/sl-podcast-134-interview-with-gene-wolfe/ "S&L Podcast ? #134 ? Interview with Gene Wolfe"; from the Nebula Awards

http://www.podtrac.com/pts/redirect.mp3/archive.org/download/SlPodcast-134-InterviewWithGeneWolfe/134SlPodcast-134-InterviewWithGeneWolfe.mp3 (47M; 34:28)

Interview starts 02:04, ends 33:37.

Topics:

- 'science fiction' vs 'fantasy' and 'science fantasy';
- "Beach Hill";
- on another Latro book ('I don't know if I will ever write another Latro book; I may. Those take a lot of research.');
- ancient Egyptian society, the Persian empire;
- at ~12m, Borges (Wolfe 'went through a period in which I read all the Borges I could find and I also went to a lecture of his. I never actually got to meet him but I did see him in the flesh. I was sitting in the audience and [inaudible] he was just the most marvelous man...2 young people from the Spanish department, and they say 'we are from the Spanish department [University] and we're here to interpret to interpret for Signor Borges as necessary, and he begins to speak and he has English down absolutely pat - he has a Spanish accent, yes, but he has a bigger vocabulary than 90% of the audience and I never heard him make a grammatical mistake...He was great.'), Wolfe mentions 'The Garden of Forking Paths';
- Chesterton's _The Napoleon of Notting Hill_ & _The Man Who Was Thursday_; - Charles Dickens, where Wolfe brings up a personal story about reading _Pickwick Papers_ at 14, a friend starting to talk like Mr. Jingles, who went on to become an Air Force psychologist, met a 'tragic' end, Wolfe misses him, and he brought the friend back in a story whose name he couldn't recall;
- the Pringle machines and the clever way the Pringle shape assists manufacture; Wolfe claims that when the fresh Pringles come out of the salter, they are 'unspeakably delicious';
- screenrights (_Shadow of the Torturer_ options sold '2 or 3 times'); - on the upcoming _Shadows of the New Sun_ story anthology ('some of my characters as handled by other people, but not as handled by me'); to help with money issues related to Rose being in the nursing home (arthritis, Alzheimer's) and difficulties in reimbursement (at ~30m) - _The Land Across_; "it's about a young American to travels to an obscure liberal eastern European country who gets into all sorts of trouble; and as I told someone, there are two attractive young women and there's an attractive old woman; there's a crazy cult; there's a truly evil cult; and much other stuff stuff - boats and so on, and when I got through giving the whole description I thought 'my god I forgot dogs and motorcycles! No dogs and motorcycles.' He goes to prison and he ends up as a member of the secret police of this little east European dictatorship and - and I had a lot of fun"
