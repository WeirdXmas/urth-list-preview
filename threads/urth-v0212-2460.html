The thread: Posters debated whether Silk's enlightenment in The Book of the Long Sun lies outside ordinary time. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued it is non-temporal, citing Silk's plea to Blood, relativistic time between Whorl and Urth, and a gnostic reading. ArchD'Ikon Zibethicus disagreed, describing an out-of-time view of past and possible strands and urging close textual study. Josh Geller proposed Silk moves at right angles to his timeline through an extra dimension we perceive as time. Timothy Reilly's post complained about typos and voices in Return to the Whorl and asked about a Mint/Marble correction; the thread ended without resolution.
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: Silk's enlightenment is non-temporal, and the Pike prayer vision is a real past moment
  • ArchD'Ikon Zibethicus: transcendence gives out-of-time access to past and possible strands; time does not stop
  • Josh Geller: Silk moves through an extra dimension at right angles to his timeline, which we perceive as time
  • Timothy Reilly: complains about typos and voices in the Tor edition and asks about a Mint/Marble fix

Themes: Time loops and causality, The dying sun and the cosmos, Gnosticism, heresy and other theologies

2002-12-18 ArchD'Ikon Zibethicus #

Argues that Silk's transcendent moment in The Book of the Long Sun is an out-of-time experience of past and possible strands rather than a stoppage of time, and that Marble's decay and Nessus's aged street show time works non-linearly. Responds to earlier posts by quoting the Pike rabbit vision in Nightside and the Duko Rigoglio chapter in In Green's Jungles, and urges close textual study of Wolfe's clues.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Silk's experience shows past and possible strands of time; Nessus time elapsed corresponds to Urth time

Don:

<snip>

Pardon my obtuseness, but is it anywhere specified that Marble's memories of her life as a secular chem are located on the Whorl? She might have had an earlier life on Urth, as did many people who were forced aboard.

Nutria:

Well, when trying to establish these matters with some sort of definitiveness, I believe that an ounce of study of the actual text in question can sometimes be worth a ton of theorising in one direction or another. For that reason, allow me to quote briefly from NLS Ch1 (are there standard abbrevations for titles here?); during his enlightenment, Pa. Silk "watched outside a clockwork show whose works had stopped", and saw (among other things):

"- dead Patera Pike mumbling prayers as he slit the throat of a speckled rabbit he himself had bought."

This seems to me to represent a vison or perception of a moment in Pa. Pike's past life, rather than the appearences of his shade elsewhere in the book.

If this is the case, it would tend to support Nutria's perception of the experience as a transcendent - or transtemporal - one for Silk himself, rather than a stoppage of time for the entire Whorl, as _Silk_ is being shown things from various strands of time, the past and possible the future. That's how I read it, anyway...

Also;

Of course such a thing is _possible_, but I don't myself know just how _probable_ it is. I base this opinion especially on IGJ Ch22, 'The Barbican and the Bear Tower', which relates the visit/return of Horn/Incanto's party to Nessus. Duko Rigoglio is one of those inhabitants of Nessus who were forced to board the Whorl. He returns to the street outside his house because of his emotional attraction to the spot (as I understand it). Throughout the chapter, it becomes obvious that the spot has aged greatly since the Duko lived there; the party guess anywhere from 1 900 to 2 500 years.

If what is involved in this sort of non-physical travel (call it 'astral' if you _must_) is a passage from one BangGnab to another, why would there be a linear progression in the local timeframe of Nessus? It seems to me that something altogether more intricate is involved here...

If this amount of time has elapsed since Rigoglio's involuntary departure, how does it synchronise with the general consenus that the Whorl has been travelling for three hundred years? And, further, this visit of the party to Nessus occurs some years before Severian brings the New Sun, which destroys Nessus and all else. If Rigoglio was abducted during the reign of Typhon, is it possible that he was kept in storage on Urth somewhere for an appropriate period of time?

It would appear likely that the lapse of time in Nessus as experienced by Rigoglio on his return actually corresponds (in Urth time) to the period for which he has been gone on the Whorl. Otherwise, why have the elapsation at all?

Blattid (tip o' the hat to archy!):

Mundus vult decipi!

Moreover, it occurs in an instant in the middle of the prosaic activity of a ball game, which is not interrupted by it. I suspect that Mr. Wolfe is relating something corresponding to the classic 'mystical' experience, which occurs _outside_ time rather than as a consequence of time somehow 'stopping'. Mr. Wolfe specifies that what Silk retains of the experience which he can consciously relate is far smaller and less profound that the totality of the experience as it was originally granted to him. Whether he relates it sequentially, or whether it occurs sequentially, it is impossible to explain it in a fashion conformant to its nature, which is, if you like, superhuman. It certainly doesn't resemble the experience of an embolism!

*

4WIW, I read a long I/V with Mr. Wolfe shortly before I joined this forum, in which he said something to the effect that _all_ the 'clues' were present in the books, but that they were only placed there _once_, as he didn't intend to insult the intelligence of his readers. Something like that, anyway. I would tend to the view that, which I would of course be greatly interested to hear anything which Mr. Wolfe had to say concerning the planning and nature of his works, he is a skilled professional author, who inserted everything necessary to have his works understood TO THE EXTENT TO WHICH HE INTENDED in the works themselves. Therefore, I support the call to closer textual study of works which are being theorised about...speaking as a hypocrite, to be sure!

2002-12-18 Josh Geller #

Proposes that Silk's enlightenment happens at right angles to his normal timeline, so that his experience is a motion through an extra dimension that we perceive as time. Builds the argument on a layered plenum of spacelike and timelike dimensions, with angels or small-g gods moving involuntarily in a seventh dimension and the figure called Mr Big Nose beyond all levels. It answers Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's point that enlightenment takes no objective time.
proposes · myth-and-structure · theories: Silk's timeline is turned ninety degrees; Enlightenment occurs at right angles to normal time

It happens at right angles to Silk's normal timeline.

We are three dimensional creatures moving involuntarily in a fourth dimension. This motion, and this dimension are seen as time. The plenum we inhabit is thus four dimensional (three spacelike and two timelike dimensions). This is embedded within a plenum that consists of these four, one other spacelike and one other timelike dimension, and a seventh dimension within which creatures of this order are in involuntary motion. This is 'time for them' ('them' are the creatures that I generally refer to as "angels or small-g gods"). Mr Big Nose, of course, is beyond this level as much as he is beyond our level.

Silk's timeline has been turned 90 degrees.

.
