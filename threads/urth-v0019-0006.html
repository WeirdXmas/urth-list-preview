Sev and Thecla in childhood

Urth list · 3 messages · 1998-09-21 to 1998-09-28

1998urthThe Book of the New SunMatthew MalthouseTony EllisJon Camfield
The thread: Jon Camfield proposed that Dorcas is Severian's paternal grandmother and Thecla his half-sister, reading the 'father's mounted guard' detail as pointing to Ouen and an affair. Tony Ellis disputed this, arguing the phrase is a brief slip into Thecla's first-person voice within one sentence rather than Severian's father speaking, citing other Thecla first-person moments and locating the transition after 'wall'. Matthew Malthouse supported Ellis, arguing the sentence's syntax already marks the shift without needing an added 'and'. The exchange ended with Ellis and Malthouse aligned against Camfield, and no further reply from Camfield appears in the thread.
  • Jon Camfield: Dorcas is Severian's grandmother and Thecla his half-sister, so 'my father's mounted guard' points to Ouen
  • Tony Ellis: 'my father's mounted guard' is a slip into Thecla's first-person voice, not Severian's father speaking
  • Matthew Malthouse: Ellis is right; the sentence's syntax already signals the shift, so no added 'and' is needed

Themes: Memory and forgetting, Unreliable narration, Fathers, mothers and children

1998-09-21 Jon Camfield #

Proposes that Dorcas is Severian's paternal grandmother and that Thecla is his half-sister, so the 'father's mounted guard' detail in Claw points to Ouen and an affair with an exultant. Grounds the theory in the passage about pebbles and dodging hooves, the memory-mixing after the House Absolute performance, and the missing-sister problem.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Dorcas is Severian's paternal grandmother; Thecla is Severian's half-sister; Ouen had an affair with an exultant; Severian's pebble memory comes from Thecla's childhood

mantis, you've already seen this, but as I don't have my books nearby and the list is as quiet as a convent, I figured I'd post it:

Last time I reread TBotNS, this passage in Claw struck me as very odd. Story-line wise, this is right after the troupe's performance in the House Absolute, when Severian has become separated, walks to exhaustion, and has memory-mixed dreams of himself and Thecla.

"...I played again with pebbles in the courtyard beside the fallen curtain wall, as Thecla dodged the hooves of my father's mounted guard" (II, XXV, 204)

The first part, up to 'curtain wall', is Severian, that's easy, he said at some point that the pebbles memory is his earliest (until he recalls his mother before the meeting with the undine the next night).

"as Thecla dodged the hooves" could also be Sev, having syncronized his and Thecla's memories; Sev was piling pebbles in the Citadel, while, at the same time, Thecla was dodging hooves.

"of my father's mounted guard" is really troublesome. It's not Thecla, because Sev is (presumably, no hints to the contrary) still talking in the first person, and describing Thecla in the third. This implies it is Severian's father. Going out somewhat on a limb, I propose this: Dorcas is Severian's paternal grandmother, mother of Ouen, as you say. The mother of Severian is or is not Katherine, but _is_ the mother of Thecla as well (Thecla then becomes the half-sister of Sev, with a smidgin more of evidence only than Merryn). Ouen seems to have had an affair with en exultant, who already had a daughter (Thecla). Sev was given to the torturers (being somewhat of a bastard) (possibly the exultant had, since giving birth to Thecla, gone to be one of the bride's of the Autarch?). Ouen and Sev were shipped off to the citadel, Sev to be given to the Torturer's, Ouen to be delivered back to Nessus, maybe even tortured (hence the mounted guard) before being set free. This would explain the origin of the memory, as well as Sev's bastard-exultant lineage, and the missing-sister problem.

Fire at will.

1998-09-22 Tony Ellis #

Argues that the phrase "my father's mounted guard" in Severian's childhood pebble-playing scene does not show a shift to Severian's father speaking, but a brief slip into Thecla's first-person voice within one sentence. Responds to Jon Camfield's objection by pointing to other moments in The Book of the New Sun where Thecla's personality surfaces in the first person, and locating the transition after "wall".
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Thecla's voice briefly intrudes into Severian's narration

<snip>

I really think you're seeing complications that aren't there with this one. TBOTNS is full of moments where Thecla's personality surfaces and speaks, or rather writes, in the first person. All that's happening here is that it's happening within the confines of one sentence for effect. The transition presumably takes place after "wall", but anywhere before "my father's mounted guard" would do.

1998-09-28 Matthew Malthouse #

Agrees with Tony Ellis that the phrase "my father's mounted guard" in the Thecla pebble-game passage should be read as Severian's father's guard, not Thecla's. Says the sentence's syntax already signals a shift from Severian to Thecla, so an added "and" is unnecessary to clarify it.
supports · close-textual-reading

I'm inclines to agree. Making the sentence ...the fallen curtain wall, and as Thecla... would have remove the ambiguity but it isn't actually necessary to add the "and" for it to be read as a change of view point from Sev. to Thecla.

Matthew
