Sundial with "multitudinous faces"?

Urth list · 25 messages · 2011-05-12 to 2011-05-17

2011urthThe Book of the New SunJerry FriedmanGwern BranwenDavid DuffyJames WynnSon of WitzDan'l Danehy-OakesJeff WilsonJason HAntonin Scriabin
The thread: Beet asked how the multi-faced sundial in the Atrium of Time, described as giving multitudinous faces, could show different times, and Son of Witz and Antonin Scriabin answered with photos and links showing that real multi-faced dials show the same time on every face unless configured for different ranges or places. Beet and Son of Witz offered symbolic and configuration explanations, while Gwern Branwen proposed that the dials might track times on other worlds. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes and Jerry Friedman objected that a sundial covers only a 24-hour cycle and cannot serve another planet's day, and the thread drifted into relativity, wormholes, and negative mass, with Tipler's cylinders and Hawking's proof debated. It ended without agreement, with the physics talk largely unconnected to the text.
  • beet: the multi-faced dial would not show different times on each face, so it is either deliberately configured or decayed, and the contradiction symbolizes the
  • Son of Witz: each face could show a different time range covering its own hours, and the cube-like dial makes sense as a real design
  • Antonin Scriabin: real multi-faced dials show the same time on every face, though faces could be set for different places
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: a sundial is limited to a 24-hour cycle and cannot show time on other worlds; relativity and time travel are one matter of spacetime navigat
  • Gwern Branwen: the far-future sundials may be relics of an interstellar empire telling times on different worlds
  • Jerry Friedman: sundials on other worlds could keep time without conflict, but a sundial cannot be a regular clock for another planet's day

Themes: Time loops and causality, The dying sun and the cosmos, Deep time and ruins

Asks how a faceted sundial in the Atrium of Time with multiple faces and gnomons could work, since the text describes its multitudinous faces giving different times. Also remarks that the motto "Aspice ut aspiciar" is an appeal to the sun rather than to a human bystander.
questions · close-textual-reading

Hi folks-- first-time poster. Doing a pretty detailed re-read of New Sun.

I'm having trouble visualizing the sundial in the Atrium of Time. It's described as "one of those old, faceted dials whose multitudinous faces give each a different time." And later, there is a reference to "one of its gnomons", implying it has more than one. What kind of a sundial has multiple faces and gnomons? I'm having trouble visualizing it. Any thoughts?

btw, it's pretty cool that "Aspice ut aspiciar", translated as "Look on me that I might be looked upon", is an appeal made to the sun (not to a human bystander).

-Jason

Argues that the multi-faced sundial Severian describes would not show a different time on each face as he claims, and suggests it is either deliberately configured that way or has decayed after billions of years. I wouldn't quite say of these sundials that each face tells "a different time" as Severian does; I wonder if they're purposely configured that way (as Antonin suggests), or if perhaps they just don't work very well billions of years in the future.

2011-05-12 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes replying to James Wynn #

Argues that relativity makes space travel and time travel one thing, movement through a four-dimensional spacetime, and that the open question is why time cannot be navigated at will like the other dimensions. Responds to James Wynn's point that relativity undermines synchronous time across distant locations, citing worm-holes and FTL travel as the context; the post is a general physics discussion rather than a reading of Wolfe's text.
disputes

Well, that was my point...

If there's FTL travel then some aspect of relativity is untrue.

But: taking relativity at face value, there is no such thing as "time travel" -- or for that matter "space travel." There is only spacetime travel through a four-dimensional manifold. To move to another point in space is to move to another point in spacetime, and so to travel in spacetime. To move to another point in time while standing still, as one does at a rate of one second per second, is also to move to another point in spacetime. The problem is _why_ time is different and can't be navigated at-will the way the other three dimensions can, and no satisfactory answer to that has yet been proposed.

Argues that time travel in Wolfe's Urth books could be explained by relativity, since time's rate depends on frame of reference, so an instantaneous trip can be re-framed to return before departure. Supports this with the physics of negative mass, citing Robert L Forward's novel TIMEMASTER as an example of a relativity-compatible model.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Time travel possible via relativistic frame switching

Is it the same time everywhere on Mongo at once? :)

Since the rate of time's progression is relative, you can't say travel is instantaneous without saying in which frame of reference it is measured as instantaneous. Since other, equally valid frames of reference measure the time between your departure and your arrival as having finite, you can switch over to a frame where it was negative, and do the instantaneous thing in that one, then find one where that was negative, and do it again, etc., and that's how you end up returning before you left.

There are numerous relativity-compatible physics models that seem to allow for time travel, but explain the lack of of observed time travel by positing it requires improbable arrangements of nigh-unobtainable resources. Robert L Forward's negative mass as dramatized in his novel TIMEMASTER is once example: matter whose density is negative causes a negative curvature of space-time, opposing the positive curvature from positive mass, among other weird things, but complies with all known physical laws.
