Themes: The dying sun and the cosmos

1997-09-07 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Works out the Whorl's flight time, estimating from the subjective and objective years given in the books that the ship travelled at about 95 percent of light speed. Then reasons about when the Whorl arrived in the Blue/Green system, using the theocide and Quetzal's rise to head of the chapter as markers, and asks for a bibliography of Wolfe articles in NYRSF.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Whorl travelled at about .95 c; Whorl arrived about thirty years before Long Sun

Reply: Item #1260418 from [email]@INET#

Wombat,

Re: Whorl flight time. It appears to have been 332 years subjective time (III,173) and 1000 years objective time (III,242); which in turn suggests an average speed of around .95 c (95% of light speed). (Time compression, doncha know.)

Re: arrival time. It seems that the time of the theocide would be the best marker of when the Whorl arrived in the Blue/Green system, and that would be about thirty years before the action of THE BOOK OF THE LONG SUN. OTOH, Quetzal became head of the chapter thirty-three years ago (see character lists), so maybe this is a better marker? Start at shipboard year 299 and count backward enough for an anonymous beef-tea sipping augur to rise through the ranks to become head of the chapter. (The last guy died at his desk, you say? My, my, my!)

Re: NYRSF, maybe you could post a bibliography of Wolfe related articles published in NYRSF through the years? Along with ordering information!

=mantis=

1997-09-08 Kevin J. Maroney #

Asks whether a remembered detail about Rose and Marble's decimal point rings a bell, citing Wolfe's statement that Pas did not know of the inhumi. Reports that the magazine is planning internal ads on Wolfe after Delany and Dick, and praises an essay titled Autopsy of SF.
informs

I'll have to check those references out. Does my memory about Rose/Marble's decimal point ring any bells?

I would think so, given Wolfe's explicit statement that Pas did not know of the inhumi.

Actually, we're preparing a series of internal ads along that exact idea. The only one we've done so far is Delany, with PKD the obvious next; Wolfe should be right after that, since I can't think of anyone else who has received so much coverage in the magazine.

I love your "Autopsy of SF", by the way--that'll be in 110.
