2008-01-04 Marc Aramini replying to Matthew Keeley #

Argues that the weight figure in Matthew Keeley's 'seventeen stones' remark cannot fit Severian, citing Wolfe's interview where Severian is described as about 6'1" and roughly 170 pounds. Responds to Keeley's suggestion that the stones may be a weight clue, concluding that Severian is far lighter than seventeen stones while Baldanders would be far heavier.
disputes · close-textual-reading · author-and-biography

As someone who obsesses about weight unnaturally from
years of boxing at 126 lbs, I found it interesting
when Wolfe described Severian in an interview, saying
he was about 6'1'' or taller and maybe 170 pounds. So
we've got a Bernard Hopkins style Severian. Sev
certainly isn't a heavyweight.

Seventeen stones is almost certainly too heavy to fit
Sev and far too light for Baldanders.

Proposes that the name "Seventeen Stones" in The Book of the New Sun may be a pun on weight, since one stone equals fourteen pounds. Notes that the figure 238 pounds leads nowhere useful, and suggests instead that Wolfe might be joking about a friend's size rather than encoding a name.
