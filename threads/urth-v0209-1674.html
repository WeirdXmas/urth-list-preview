Was Jonas a Chem?

Urth list · 8 messages · 2002-09-06 to 2002-09-09

2002urthThe Book of the New SunFilipe SilvaMatthew MalthouseJerry FriedmanAndrew ReevesRoy C. LackeyMichael Andre-DriussiMichael Straight
The thread: Readers debated whether Jonas in The Book of the Long Sun is a chem partly rebuilt with human parts, as Marble is. Andrew Reeves proposed the chem idea from his odd personality and Marble resemblance, and Roy Lackey disputed it, citing Jonas's light weight and the absence of biological parts in chems. Michael Andre-Driussi argued Jonas is a different robot type, a hollow-man descended from pressure suits, and Matthew Malthouse said his nature cannot be simply determined. Filipe Silva proposed the 'Jo' prefix reads as 'IO' to hint at chem automation, and Jerry Friedman asked for evidence behind the pseudonym and twinning claims. Michael Straight's Hammerstone fusion-plant point was a side dispute. The thread ended without consensus.
  • Andrew Reeves: Jonas is a chem partly repaired with human parts, like Marble
  • Roy C. Lackey: Jonas is not a chem, since he is light and chems use no biological parts
  • Michael Andre-Driussi: Jonas is a separate robot type, a hollow-man from pressure suits, partly rebuilt with biological parts
  • Matthew Malthouse: Jonas's nature cannot be simply settled; chems were a cruder product modeled on creations like Jonas
  • Filipe Silva: the 'Jo' prefix reads as 'IO', hinting that Jonas is automated like a chem
  • Jerry Friedman: asks for textual evidence for the pseudonym and twinning claims

Themes: Robots, androids and artificial life, Beasts and the animal in us, Names and their meanings

2002-09-06 Andrew Reeves #

Proposes that Jonas in The Book of the Long Sun may be a chem partly repaired with human parts, like Marble, citing his odd personality and its resemblance to Marble's merging with Rose. Grounds the idea in personality oddities rather than textual detail, and excuses technological gaps in the far future via Clarke's Law.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Jonas is a chem partly repaired with human parts

The recent discussion on chems and their nature has caused me to weigh in with a few brief thoughts. The first of these is that though there are a lot of technological questions involving the chems and their function, we ought to remember that this is getting on 50k to a million years hence, at which point Clarke's Law ought to be in full effect. So I think that Wolfe can engage in just about any sort of handwaving in that regard.

Something else that may or may not have come up in years past, but which I haven't noticed, is the question as to whether or not Jonas in not a chem who (like Marble) was repaired partially with human parts. Indeed, there is a lot of weirdness going on with his personality that rather reminds me of what happened when Marble merged with Rose (not in the particulars, just that things are... odd). Still, these are just random thoughts, and _New Sun_, having been written before _Long Sun_ would not take a future work into account.

Still, what do others think?

Andrew

2002-09-06 Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that Jonas was not a chem like those on the Whorl, citing his light weight when Severian lifted him in the Antechamber against the greater weight of chems. Contends that Marble took only man-made replacement parts from Rose, not biological ones, and that no chem in the Long Sun and Short Sun books uses biological parts.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Jonas was not a chem; Marble took only man-made parts from Rose

Yes, Jonas's nature has been discussed before, and I'm still waiting for an answer to my question of just what the hell he thought he was going to do with Jolenta, even if she was willing. <g>

But, no, Jonas wasn't a chem, at least not one like those on the _Whorl_. Jonas was very light weight, as Sev noticed when he picked him up in the Antechamber. Male chems are _much_ heavier than humans. Even female chems are heavier than same-sized humans. Rose was a human with some man-made replacement parts, including human-looking hands. It was some of those man-made parts that Marble appropriated for herself. So far as I am aware, _none_ of Rose's biological parts were taken. Marble ran test routines for each part she tried, rejecting some. For one thing, Rose's biological parts were pretty much spent, due to her age. Even if it were possible, which I doubt, I don't think there is any instance of biological parts being used by a chem in the LS/SS books.

-Roy

2002-09-06 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Argues that Jonas in The Book of the Long Sun is a robot of a different line from the chems, a hollow-man type descended from pressure suits, not a chem, while agreeing he was partly rebuilt with biological parts. Grounds this in his smaller size compared with Sidero, the silvery command-class robots, and parallels with Jolenta, Marble and Roy's remark about robotic love.
disputes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion · theories: Jonas is a hollow-man robot, not a chem; Jonas's personality is a blend of software from prosthetics and bio parts; Jonas's body in Yesod was rebuilt into a full biological body

Andrew asked if Jonas was a chem.

I will add my voice with those saying that Jonas is not a chem. Yes, he is a robot, but of a different evolutionary line of descent: he is a "hollow man" type, which developed from pressure suits and powered exoskeletons. Because he is of smaller size than the "iron man" class represented by Sidero (who is big enough that a man like Severian can climb inside him and wear him like the hard suit that he is), Jonas is presumably of the silvery command class seen in passing on the starship _Tzadkiel_.

From another angle: Jonas is a sailor, not a soldier.

Having writ that, still I agree (most others will too) that Jonas has been repaired with biological parts. And yes, this is a very Oz-like bit of magic which is not like the Rose/Marble situation (where robotic hardware is recycled from a dead bio to a living chem).

Half-facetiously Roy asks what Jonas would do with Jolenta if he could find her; which relates to robotic love (rather than sexual congress--I believe the text makes it clear Jonas has no external male reproductive organ), if I read it right. That is, Roy questions Jonas's love, because Jonas is a robot (Roy does not question the love of a man who has no external male reproductive organ; nor the possibility of Platonic Love).

My sense is that Jonas's prosthetics have actually made him part-human in software (as mirror to how a cyborg is "part machine" in hardware), making him capable of loving (if robot sailors cannot love) or loving a human (if robots can love robots but not love humans). And every man loves/desires Jolenta! (So in this sense I agree that the exchange of parts has had a corresponding software effect as might happen in the Rose/Marble case, even though the parts are of different categories.)

In addition, Jonas might well love Jolenta for the things they have in common: both are part artificial (her cosmetic implants, his prosthetics); both are part robot (she is Talos's hypnotic slave); and both have pseudonyms (that begin with "Jo-," hinting at twinning or alike-ness) they received after their traumatic reinventions.

Jonas steps into the mirrors after speaking of being repaired. When next we see him, he is fully biological. I have often speculated that in Yesod his biological prosthetics were expanded into a full biological body into which the Jonas-software was integrated . . . along with the biological software that was present-yet-dormant in the prosthetics, now full-blown in the reconstructed bio body.

(A somewhat cleaner version is that the Jonas-spirit was drawn from Yesod by the Claw to ride the body of the annonymous dead soldier.)

In any event, two personalities in one body.

So I agree as to possible software weirdness in both Marble and Jonas being a result of their use of external hardware replacements; that the weirdness involves a degree of personality transfer ranging from "dilute" to "potentially overpowering." OTOH Jonas is not a chem of the Whorl.

Side note: re chem power systems, iirc the talus had a fusion power plant, not a chemical power plant.

2002-09-06 Matthew Malthouse #

Argues that Jonas's nature as robot, android or chem cannot be simply determined, suggesting the Chems were a cruder product of Typhon and Pas's limited technology modeled on more sophisticated created persons like Jonas. Responds to Andrew Reeves's question about whether Jonas is a chem repaired with human parts, offering a view rather than settling the question with textual evidence.
answers · myth-and-structure · theories: Jonas is a chem repaired with human parts; Chems were cruder copies of created persons

My view is that Jonas' nature, beit robot / android / chem, cannot be simply determined, if at all.

The impression I have is that the Chems were the product of Typhon/Pas' relatively limited technology. They concept may have been based on the much more sophisticated created persons such as Jonas but no where near matches it in execution.

Matthew

2002-09-06 Michael Straight #

Argues that Hammerstone's remark about using water or water vapor as fuel is shorthand for a standard science-fiction fusion plant that takes in water and outputs hydrogen, oxygen and power. Responds to Michael Andre-Driussi's note that the talus had a fusion plant rather than a chemical one, offering the poster's recollection of Hammerstone's comment as support.
supports · close-textual-reading · theories: Talus powered by fusion plant

Offers no textual evidence beyond the sound of the name and a keyboard analogy, and admits limited familiarity with Wolfe's work having read only The Book of the New Sun.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Jonas was a chem; the prefix Jo reads as IO
