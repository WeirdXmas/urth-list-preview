Wolfe in music?

Urth list · 14 messages · 2011-05-23 to 2011-05-25

2011urthThe Book of the New SunLane HaygoodJerry FriedmanCraig BrewerJames WynnMarc AraminiStuart HammDaniel Petersen
The thread: The thread began as a playful question about whether Gene Wolfe's work reaches into music, prompted by a punk song quoting 'The Detective of Dreams.' Daniel Petersen asked whether Wolfe's books mention music and what Wolfe himself likes, and Jerry Friedman noted folk songs in the Severian books. Participants linked Severian's cosplay appeal to filk and metal subcultures, with James Wynn claiming Wolfe said his tastes ran to folk and filk, though no source was given. Lane Haygood mostly joked about the costume image, and Petersen admitted discomfort with the sadomasochistic undercurrent in Wolfe. The thread drifted from textual music to fandom and ended with hammstu recalling a blues passage in Endangered Species, without a settled answer.
  • Daniel Petersen: Wolfe's gothic, baroque taste links him to cosplay, metal and alternative subcultures, and the Solar books feel full of unnamed song
  • James Wynn: Wolfe deliberately built Severian as a cosplayable figure and said his own music taste was folk and filk
  • Jerry Friedman: asks what music the fiction actually contains, noting folk songs and a dancing reference, and repeats an unverified claim about heavy metal
  • Craig Brewer: asks whether the Sun books describe much music and links convention cosplayers to filk
  • Lane Haygood: mocks the shirtless masked swordsman costume idea rather than engaging the argument
  • hammstu: praises the blues music written into Endangered Species' opening story

Themes: Robots, androids and artificial life, Books, libraries and writing, Masks, disguise and shapeshifting

Fewer clothes = easier costume.

Metal and folk don't often go hand in hand, do they? Or do they? I don't know much about metal.

2011-05-23 Daniel Petersen replying to James Wynn #

Argues that Wolfe's baroque, gothic taste overlaps with cosplay, metal and alternative art subcultures, and that this helps explain why gothic and punk-leaning writers like Gaiman and Mieville admire him. supports

Yeah, I've always heard that too. So yeah, cosplay and so on for sure. Gene seems to definitely have some kind of baroque, gothic taste that overlaps with these subcultures (from costumed convention-goers to metalheads). At times the guy's practically a goth-metal lyricist! Hilarious. But I love how these aspects of Wolfe relate so widely to 'alternative' art-culture. That's one part of why I think more gothy/punky writers like Gaiman and Mieville love him so much. And it just adds to the enigma of the disparate parts he brings together into one man. What a guy.

-DOJP
