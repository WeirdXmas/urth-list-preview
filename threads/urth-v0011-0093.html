1998urthThe Book of the New SunAlice Turner

1998-05-23 Alice Turner #

Complains that the sound Severian wakes in the cave of the Man-Apes is never explained, despite Jonas saying the monsters are water-bound and have servants. Cites Severian's later remark about what the Claw had awakened as an evasion, arguing it breaks Chekhov's rule about the unfired gun; it sets aside J. Shultz's questions.
disputes · close-textual-reading · genre-and-craft

J. Shultz asks about pedophilia--I don't think so--and about Sev's double dose of wounds, which I think will be answered by several, so I won't get into it, and also this:

We never get an answer to this, and it is *most* annoying. The sound is scary and portentous. He asks Jonas about it when he tells him of the adventure, and Jonas tells him that the monsters are water-bound but that they have "servants." And then later Sev comes out with one of those sententious statements--"I know now what the Claw had awakened" (I paraphrase, being too lazy to look it up)--and that's that, unless I've missed something. It breaks Chekhov's rule (If you brandish a gun in the first act and have not discharged it by the third, the audience will never forgive you). Grrrr.

-alga-
