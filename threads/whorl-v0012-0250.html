2001-02-16 Adam Stephanides #

Argues that the Silk-in-Horn character behaves much like the Silk-possessed Pig in the mansion, both fixated on Hyacinth despite different surface reactions. Supports this with the passages where Horn wants to enter alone, deceives Pig, and finds Hyacinth's dressing chamber by 'certain' knowledge, answering Michael Andre-Driussi's claim that their reactions differ.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Silkhorn is Silk denying his identity

I may be missing your point completely, but it seems to me that their reactions aren't so different. In fact, I'd say that "Horn"'s behavior supports my position. He first tells Pig he wants to enter the mansion alone, with no explanation (118, 119). When Pig insists on coming with him, he deceives him so as to be able to go in alone (121). Once inside, he gets "a sort of itch" to find Hyacinth's bedroom, although he tells himself it's foolish. He decides to try to return to Hound, hoping to blunder into Hyacinth's bedroom on the way. And he does eventually find himself, by chance, in Hyacinth's dressing chamber; although he's never been there before, he "knew with absolute certainty" that that's what it is (134-35). From this evidence "Horn" is just as fixated upon Hyacinth as the Silk-possessed Pig is, though better able to rationalize it.

(I wouldn't expect Silk-in-Pig and Silkhorn to behave identically, in any case. One is filtered through Pig, so to speak, the other blended with Horn (Silk-in-Pig tells "Horn"'It was Pig who was angry'" (175)); plus Silkhorn is engaged in denying to himself that he is Silk.)

--Adam
