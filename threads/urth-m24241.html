The Land Across Discussion (Spoilers!)

Urth list · 22 messages · 2013-12-07 to 2013-12-27

2013urthAuspex RexAndrew DubbeldMatthew KnightMatthew KeeleyVasa CurcinMatthew KingMarc AraminiPiotr SzczęsnyAntonin ScriabinStephen HoyDaniel Petersen
The thread: Readers of The Land Across puzzled over unexplained details: who owns the severed hand, what Leader is, and whether Grafton can be trusted. Matthew King and Christopher Simon proposed Grafton as unreliable or as orchestrating the conspiracy, while Matthew Keeley and Antonin Scriabin floated narrator-language and vampire readings. Piotr Szczęsny, Andrew Moon, Antonin Scriabin and Stephen Hoy converged on the hand belonging to the girl behind the mirror, with competing identifications of her, and Vasa Curcin argued possession. The thread drifted into spoiler-warning complaints and a Third Man allusion, and ended without consensus.
  • Matthew King: Butch was killed for Russ Rathaus's voodoo doll, and the open puzzles about Leader and the hand remain unresolved
  • Christopher Simon: Grafton is unreliable and may be the conspiracy's orchestrator with amnesia
  • Matthew Keeley: Grafton's name and the vampire invitation rule suggest he is a vampire-like count
  • Piotr Szczęsny: the severed hand belongs to the girl behind the mirror, buried by Grafton
  • Andrew Moon: the ghost girl's body was turned into a hand of glory by Volitaine or Zenon
  • Vasa Curcin: Grafton is possessed and Leader may be Dracula

Themes: Unreliable narration, Ghosts, dreams and visions, Masks, disguise and shapeshifting

Answers Christopher Simon's question about who cut off Butch's head by reasoning from the doll plot in The Land Across, and raises doubts about why the killers displayed the body to Naala or Graft-on.
answers · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Unholy Way killed Butch to recover the voodoo doll

Why they chose to announce this to Naala (or was it to Graft-on?) using the Colonel Kurtz method I cannot discern.

Perhaps more of a tutelary spirit?

2013-12-08 Antonin Scriabin replying to Christopher Simon #

Proposes that Grafton in The Land Across may be unreliable, possibly being the orchestrator of the whole conspiracy with amnesia, comparing him to the film Cypher. Argues the treasure story was planted by Martya and Volitain to set Grafton a game, and raises questions about the hand's ghost owner and the girl with the red pen.
proposes · puzzle-solving · theories: Grafton orchestrated the conspiracy himself under amnesia; Martya and Volitain planted the treasure as a game

It's possible Grafton is seeing things. There is obviously "someone" operating as the third border guard / Leader, but only Grafton seems to think they look similar. Confusion on his part? He also seems to change his assessment of this individual ... sometimes the third border guard / Leader looks only reminiscent of his father, sometimes very similar ... Grafton doesn't recognize the man on the posters right away either.

Ever see the movie Cypher? (Spoilers) A guy finds himself in increasingly odd circumstances, trapped in an ever growing conspiracy, and then he ultimately figures out he was orchestrating the whole thing all along; he had given himself an amnesia-inducing treatment so that his previously laid plans for world domination could come to fruition without any interference. Including his.

I get this vibe from Grafton. At the beginning of the novel, he knows nothing about the country. By the end, he is getting its highest award, speaks the language, is a member of the secret police, etc. Almost like he was a tool all along of the country's plan to oust the Unholy Way.

This makes sense in regards to Volitain and the "treasure" as well. Are we supposed to believe that one hundred years' worth of treasure hunters didn't think to look in the chimney? I think Martya and Volitain made up the story and planted the treasure, in order to add to the "game" Grafton has to play / solve. This makes sense also considering how quickly they enlist his help in their scheme. Within an hour of meeting them both, Grafton is brought in on the treasure hunt.

Definitely Evil Dead 2. That is one mean, murderous hand, most of the time. Only Grafton sees its nice side.

Not sure who the ghost owner of the hand is, but there is a type of juxtaposition between her and the man in black. A white, helpful, sometimes friendly female character on the one hand, and a dark, sometimes terrifying male character on the other. And both ghosts.

Another question: are we supposed to know who the heck the girl with the red pen is?

2013-12-10 Matthew Keeley replying to Matthew King #

Proposes several speculative readings of The Land Across, including that the name Grafton hints at vampire 'counts', that the Willows may belong to the man in black, and that the narrator may not be writing in English at all. Grounds these in the Old English and German meanings of Grafton, the invitation rule for vampires, and the narrator's odd voice and the unexplained translation machines.
proposes · puzzle-solving · source-and-allusion · theories: Grafton family has vampire ancestry; The Willows belongs to the man in black; Narrator is not writing in English

A few thoughts: (spoilers)

-
-
-
-
1. A quick Google search shows me that "Grafton" is "derived from the Old English graf meaning 'gorve' and 'tun' meaning enclosure." However, with all the Germanic stuff in this book, as well as the apparent presence of vampires and Vlad the Impaler, I was reminded that "Graf" is also German for "Count." Perhaps there are fangs in the Grafton family tree, perhaps I am reading too much into it.

2. When the the Legion kidnaps Grafton, they first ask themselves in, but Grafton refuses, steps outside, and is kidnapped. There's no indication that they ever enter The Willows. Could this have something to do with the rule that vampires may only enter places into which they've been invited?

3. On a related note, we see the man in black at a castle (his?), in the prison cell (apparently invited by Russ), and in The Willows (without invitation). Perhaps The Willows belongs to him? On the other hand, Grafton has a vision of Eion Desmartes' death and describes the miser; he doesn't recognize him as being the man in black.

4. The Willows: Are we supposed to be thinking of the Algernon Blackwood story? Or the association between "weeping" and "willows?"

5. (This last one is the most out-there)
When excerpts from the book first appeared, there was some discussion here about the oddness of the narrator's voice. His vocabulary certainly seems more limited than a lot of Wolfe's narrators, and, especially at first, his voice sounds a little off. I half-wonder if this is a clue, as per Christopher Simon's suggestion that the narrator has forgotten he's involved in a plan. There's mention of the Legion possessing translation machines, but it's never really followed up on. Is it possible that, contrary to what he tells us, Grafton isn't writing in English, in Massachusetts, for an editor in New York? But, if that were the case, what purpose is being served by the ruse?

Thoughts?

-Matt

2013-12-11 Andrew Dubbeld replying to Matthew King #

Proposes that the ghost girl is the body found behind the mirror in the willows, turned into a hand of glory by Volitaine, Papa Zenon or Magos X, and asks who Magos X really is. Grounds this in the unobserved funeral, Papa Zenon's remark that the dead repay them with prayers, and the blurred prayer tattoos that suggest they were applied to a living hand.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Ghost girl is the body behind the mirror; Body was turned into a hand of glory; Magos X is a hidden identity

Feels like the implication is that the ghost girl is the body they find behind the mirror in the willows, transformed into a hand of glory by either Volitaine and/or Papa Zenon, or Magos X who presumably has the capability, and whose real name Grafton knows but deliberately omits from the text while making a point of doing so.

That the funeral was not observed by Grafton (or anyone else?) which gives opportunity for mutilation of the body, and the fact that Papa Zenon says: "We do not charge for funerals. The dead repay us with their prayers." (Kobo epub, pg 206.7/586) is interesting given the prayers tattooed on the hand.

Though the tattoos are blurred, which a couple of characters (Archbishop, Papa Iason) indicate means that they were applied to the living hand. Not sure how that would fit in.

Any ideas on who Magos X actually is? Are there any hints? Surely not the Leader again?

2013-12-12 Antonin Scriabin replying to Piotr Szczęsny #

Proposes a literal reading of a cafe scene in The Land Across: Martya used sewing scissors from her purse to turn Volitain away. Builds on Piotr's and Andrew's idea that the hand belongs to the girl behind the mirror, and that Zenon's burial was a ritual enchanting the hand, which is protective toward Grafton.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: the hand belongs to the girl behind the mirror; Zenon's burial enchants the hand; the hand is protective toward Grafton; the girl with the red pen is a patron

Welcome Piotr!

I took that scene literally ... that Martya had sewing scissors or something like that in her purse, and used them to turn Volitain away!

I think your and Andrew's suggestion makes a lot of sense: the hand belongs to the girl behind the mirror, and that Papa Zenon's burial wasn't *just *a burial, but some ritual intended to enchant the hand. The hand (or more accurately, the spirit it is attached to) is friendly and protective towards Grafton because he put an end to the wait behind the mirror and got her a proper burial.

The girl with the red pen wasn't a waitress, she was a patron in the cafe I believe. I'm not sure what to think of her.

2013-12-18 Auspex Rex replying to Stephen Hoy #

Lays out a series of puzzles about The Land Across, including whether Leader is visible only to Grafton, whether the house named Nuts is possessed, and whether the severed hand belongs to Yelena. Supports these with quoted passages about the invisible driver's passenger, Grafton's sense of being inside the house, and the hand's apparent friendliness. Also proposes that multiple overlapping versions of the same people, such as Leader as an older Grafton, may explain the clues.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Leader is visible only to Grafton; Grafton is possessing or possessed by the house; The hand belongs to Yelena; Leader is an older Grafton

I'm about 80% through my second reading of TLA and I have some other observances and questions that I'm interested in hearing others' thoughts on.

The Invisible Leader
First, as far as I can so far determine, until the end when they all meet in Leader's mountain house, when Grafton sees Leader, no one else does. Or they pretend not to. And when others see Leader, Grafton does not. From Chapter 9, "Free Almost", when Grafton is being taken from the prison to the warehouse:

'Every once ina while our driver said something. He was not talking to me or to the screws on each side of me'

later:

'After I had listened awhile I got the idea that our driver was talking to somebody sitting next to him who was too short for his head to show over the back of the seat. Either that, or he was invisible.'

This, along with the custom of police leaving the front passenger seat unoccupied, really has me stumped.

Home Possession
I'm fairly sure someone or something or multiple things are possessed, but I'm not sure how it all works. I started thinking how people go into homes and 'possess' them the way a spirit might go into a person, which got me thinking about how in TLA the streets are not named, but houses are (The Willows, for example). I think that Martya and Kleon's house may be named Nuts due to the nut trees they have. From Chapter 9:

'But thinking "Nuts!" reminded me that there were those nut trees all around Martya's house. They had nut trees ...'

And later, in the final chapter, Grafton feels as if he's become the house for a moment, or a part of it:

'Martya was on her knees blowing them out before I understood that she and Volitain were inside me somehow, and it was Volitain who had told me to blow them out.

Here is what I think. I think that he and Martya were really there, but I could not see them. The only Volitain and Martya I could see (the Volitain and Martya I thought I saw) were my idea of Volitain and Martya. Does that make any sense? Either that, or I was spread out all over the room somehow.'

No, Grafton, that does not make any sense. Not me anyhow. Anyone have any idea what's happening here, seems like a pretty big clue. Is Grafton a time ghost, is he possessing the house or vice versa?

Is the hand Yelena's?
I feel as though the hand might be Yelena's, which would require some time weirdness and/or Grafton's sketchy narration. In the book, I believe the hand first appears when it runs over Martya's foot (like a rat) in The Willows. This is before the dead woman is removed from behind the mirror. But the sort of fetal position of the corpse is such that it might not be obvious that one of her hands is missing. We have a another dead woman in the story, Yelena. And the descriptions of Yelena, the corpse, and the ghost attached to the hand are compatible, with the possible exception being that the ghost is described as tall while Yelena is not. I'm stretching here, but some possible clues are here in Chapter 15, "Revisiting":

'"... she said, "I would die standing, if I could. Take my hand."

I did.

"You are good man. I meet you so late. There are so few good men."'

Grafton prays after she dies. Later Papa Iason says of the hand that "prayers of the righteous might free such spirits, or at least assist them in gaining their freedom". Later when talking about the incident with Papa Zenon and Naala, Grafton says he held her hand while she died. The hand seems friendly to Grafton, jealous perhaps of Naala, and hateful of Ferenc Narkatsos. Also the distinct mention of her body position as she dies is strange. Grafton assists her in going from a laying position to sitting up, closer to the fetal position of the dead girl in the house. At Magos X's house when he sees the ghost girl 'just for half a second maybe', she is described as 'a tall girl with long hair and a good face'.?

Multiple Version of People
I think there may be a case of multiple versions of the same people in TLA. Is Leader just an older Grafton? Is Russ just an older Papa Iason. There is a religion in TLA built around keeping things the same, which may help describe why so much of the country seems outdated, but maybe there are multiple time tracks overlapping in TLA. We have a train that appears to go from a modern style to a steam engine, for example. Lots of seeming fathers and sons, as I've mentioned. Possibly some time tracks move slower than others, like the conveyer belts in the first chapter.

We have two people who pull out magnifying glasses from a drawer to look at the hand. Maybe no big deal, but it feels a little too much like a clue to me. I mean, it's a big magnifying glass, sort of a universal symbol for a clue. The archbishop and Volitain. The first time we have a magnifying glass in the book Volitain is using it to look at Grafton's bee stings. The next time, the archbishop pulls out a magnifying glass to examine the hand. Finally, Voltain uses a magnifying glass to examine the hand. Note that Papa Zenon does not require a magnifying glass to read the tattoos on the hand when Grafton shows it to him.

Grafton jokes about Butch and Aegis being the same guy until he meets them both in the same room.

The raw-boned, dark-haired lady who wears black and white polka-dot in one scene and quickly, it seems, changes to black and red stripes with a different hair-do.

So Many Questions
What was Ferenc Narkotsos talking about when he said "She wouldn't sign. Everything I say to her, it is still no. She wants the police."

The chapter Getting Ahead, in which Grafton possibly lifts the shrunken head from the Narkotsos' shop, and Butch's severed head is tossed into Naala's apartment, got me thinking about The Land Across. The Land, A Cross? I don't know.

Russ told Grafton about Rosalee's aunt Lily who lived with her disabled husband. Then there's The Lily & The Civet shop(s). I don't believe we ever see Abderos Narkotsos, Ferenc's father, but perhaps he is Rosalee's purported uncle. Of course, the story Russ tells Grafton doesn't exactly stand up to Grafton's later theories about how and when Russ got into the country.

When Grafton is following the dark man in the woods with three wolves, the man stops and points and the wolves run off and kill something or someone. There's a little scream. Who did they kill?

I don't even know exactly where to begin with the dark man with fire in his eyes (who I assume is Vlad), Magos X, and the girl with the red pen.

Any thoughts are appreciated,
Jeff
