Severian and the corridor of time.

Urth list · 25 messages · 2010-08-03 to 2010-08-04

The thread: Jason Walker asked whether Severian's time travel to the ancient past in The Urth of the New Sun is consistent, since his powers depend on the New Sun yet he is stripped of them before its light reaches Urth, and whether the corridor of time lies outside time and space. Jeff Wilson answered with a theory that Severian needs the White Fountain's power to travel in a higher spatial dimension, that Juturna is secretly related to him, and that he overshot the point where the light reaches Urth, landing in the early Andes, in an analogy to a ship running aground. The rest of the thread drifted into jokes and off-topic chat, including a Jehovah's Witness joke and a real-world identification of Tim Powers, and it ended without resolving the question.

2010-08-03 Jason Walker #

Questions whether Severian's time travel to the ancient past in The Urth of the New Sun is consistent, since his powers come from the New Sun yet he is stripped of them in the ancient era before its light reached Urth. Asks whether the corridor of time lies outside time and space, which would permit travel even before the new sun was created, and invites others' input.
questions · chronology-and-plot-logic

In Urth of the new Sun, Severian travel back through the corridor of time to the ancient civilization and becomes Apu-Punchau. He goes to that time because that is the time when the new sun is created and cannot go back any further his powers come from the new sun and cannot go back any further than it was created. But then once he gets there he does not have any of his powers and the Hierodules say that this is because the light from the New Sun gives him his power and this light has not yet reached Urth. According to this, he should only of been able to travel back to where the New Sun's light has came to Urth.
I am missing something or do this not make any sense to anyone else as well. Is the corridor of time outside of time/space and only needs the power from the New Sun to access it, but then he should be able to travel to anytime he wants, even before the new sun was created.
I am just looking at someone else's input on this.

2010-08-03 Jeff Wilson replying to Jason Walker #

Proposes that Severian needs the White Fountain's power to move through the corridors of time in a higher spatial dimension, and that Juturna is secretly related to him. Grounds this in the light-related means of travel used by other corridor travellers, such as the Green Man, mirrors and reflective sails, and the Hierodules' saucers, and suggests Severian writes Urth because he is stranded in early time.
proposes · close-textual-reading · myth-and-structure · theories: Severian needs White Fountain power to travel corridors; Juturna is secretly related to Severian; Severian writes Urth while stranded in early time

We don't know for sure. The business about moving in a direction not seen before suggests that movement om higher spatial dimension is involved. The power of the New Sun may not be needed in general, but it may be that *Severian* needs the power of the White Fountain to achieve that movement. All the other folks that travel the Corridors seem to do it in ways that are light-related in some way (mirrors, reflective sails) or the travelers themselves are light-related (the Green Man, the Hierdules presumably make both the time-spanning saucers, the White Fountain, and the sun of Yesod), except for Juturna, which is why I think she is secretly related to Severian.

Once in the Corridors, he may be able to travel any distance on either Fountain-power or human muscle power, and then step down into the normal world of four dimensions. But without the White Fountain or a similar power source to draw on, he can't go back up to the Corridors. It may be that he decides to write URTH at all because with the White Fountain contained, concealed, or even consumed by the new sun, he is stuck in the early time centuries of Ushas , and is making the best of his forced retirement from saviorhood.
