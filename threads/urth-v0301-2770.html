2003-01-22 James Jordan #

Proposes that Wolfe's Pas, a Pan figure, echoes the Ovidian myth in which Pan challenges Apollo to a musical contest and loses. Cites Ovid and Bach's cantata The Contest of Phoebus and Pan, and notes that a recent mention of Apollo in reference to Severian prompted the post; says Wolfe likely knows Ovid but not Bach.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Pas echoes Pan's challenge to Apollo

The recent mention of Apollo in reference to Severian reminded me of something I meant to share earlier. In Ovid, and in Bach's cantata *The Contest of Phoebus and Pan,* we have a story of a musical battle between Pan (Pas) and Phoebus (Apollo). Pan's music is earthly; Apollo's heavenly. Pan challenges Apollo, seeking to usurp the god's position. He loses, of course.

Sort of interesting grist for the old Lupine mill, isn't it? I doubt if Wolfe knows the Bach, but I'll bet he knows the Ovid.

2003-01-23 James Wynn #

Argues that the Long Sun books draw on the Greek report of Pan's death, recorded by Plutarch, to shape Pas's reputed death and possible return. Points readers to the closing note on Pan in Graves's Greek Myths and contrasts Pan's permanent death with the resurrected Zeus whose tomb was shown at Crete.
informs · source-and-allusion · myth-and-structure · theories: Pas's death echoes Pan's death in Plutarch

Also, different from the resurrected Zeus whose grave(s?) was displayed to pilgrims at Crete. I believe **this** comes into to play in "The Long Sun" with Pas' reputed death.

The report of Pan's death comes from Plutarch. See the final note on "Pan His Nature and Deeds" in Graves' "The Greek Myths" for details on this. -- Crush
