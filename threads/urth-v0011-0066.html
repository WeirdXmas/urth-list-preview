1998-05-18 Dan Parmenter #

Argues that Vodalus is a shallow rebel figure, a rich idler whose stock phrases make his revolutionary pose unconvincing, and notes that Baldanders, like Severian, misreads the Hierodules' bowing as a sign of his own victory. The post supports these points by quoting Vodalus's dialogue and recalling the Lake People conflict, and it follows an Alice Turner message about the Autarch's claim that the undines seek to enslave mankind.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Vodalus is a patsy rather than a real revolutionary

From: "Alice Turner" <[email]>

But the old Autarch tells us that they seek to enslave mankind. Not just those in boats, one assumes.

From: [email]

I'm inclined to think of Vodalus as a total patsy. His dialogue is filled with stock phrases like "that mewling fool, the Autarch" which seem too trite to be believed. He seems like a charismatic rebel figure, somewhat taken with himself, but really just another rich idler who might otherwise be torturing the prisoners in the antechamber, trysting in the Secret House and tripping on Alzabo in the woods on the weekends with his lowlife friends. He has about as much to do with real revolution as Mick Jagger.

BTW, it strikes me that Baldanders, for all of his mad science is just as capable of "not getting it" as Severian is. When the Hierodules bow down before Severian, Balanders assumes that it is due to the fact that he will "win" the immediate conflict (the angry mob of Lake People vs. the Mad Scientist/Monster Baldanders). He's not thinking very globally, which is generally Severian's problem too.

D
