2014-08-19 Marc Aramini #

Proposes that the bison pierced by an arrow in Wolfe's "To the Dark Tower Came" may represent the slain astral bull of the sun from Mithraic cult, linking it to the archer Sagittarius. Raises further questions about whether the fire-eyed women are sibyls or Delphic pythias serving Apollo, and whether a bison can count as a Taurus.
proposes · source-and-allusion · allegory-and-typology · theories: The bison in To the Dark Tower Came is the bull of the sun; The fire-eyed women are sibyls or Delphic pythias

doing some research for my "Solar Labyrinth: write up and was quite pleased to rediscover that the killing of the astral bull of the sun was every bit as important to the syncretic Mithras worship in the late Hellenistic and Roman cults as the execution of Christ is in the Christian cosmology. I earlier wrote that "To the Dark Tower Came" was a semiotic study of slippage in the letter s, but was confounded a bit by the symbolism of the bison and the penned whale.

Could that bison, pierced by the arrow, possibly from the archer Sagittarius, represent the bull of the sun? (Also, are the women with fire in their eyes who flank the young king sybils, or Delphic pythians? Seers for the god of light and the sun, Apollo? (In addition to healing, Apollo brought plague and sickness, too - thus the rats, besides representing senility eating at the mind of the king). Can we safely call a bison a bull? They are definitely bovines like cattle, but might not be a valid Taurus. (Tarus is a species name for cows)

2014-08-20 Jeff Wilson replying to Marc Aramini #

Argues that a bison can be treated as a bull for the Taurus constellation question, provided it is not obviously female, and suggests an Ethiopian bull might fit if the aim is to reflect Hellenic differences in appearance. Responds to Marc Aramini's objection that bison are not a valid Taurus and that Taurus is a cow species name, offering a playful, tentative counter in a thread about the Dark Tower and the bull of the sun.
disputes · source-and-allusion

As long as they are not obviously female, a honed bison makes a fine bull. Perhaps even an Ethiopian bull, to Hellenize the difference in appearance.
