Themes: Hidden identities and parentage

2001-03-12 James Jordan #

Asks whether the list has ever settled what the Big Inhumi Secret in The Book of the Long Sun is, noting that the revelation to Nettle about inhumi intelligence coming from human blood is already known. Guesses the Secret is the actual way inhumi travel from Green to Blue, which the book never states.
questions · close-textual-reading · theories: The Secret is how inhumi travel from Green to Blue

Maybe I'm having a Senior Moment (more and more since I passed 50), but did we ever "decide" what the Big Inhumi Secret is? Surely it's not what Jahlee tells Nettle, that they get their intelligence from our blood; we've known that all along.
My guess would be that The Secret is the actual and true way that inhumi get from Green to Blue, though we're never told what that is. But what have I forgotten.

Patera Nuttier and Nuttier

2001-03-12 Adam Stephanides #

Argues that the Big Inhumi Secret told to Nettle in the Long Sun books is only the inhumi getting their minds from human blood, and that Horn could not have substituted a fake secret. Responds to James Jordan's question by noting that the inhumi know Horn knows the secret, so any omission or fake would expose him, and that a fake secret would not fit the Krait revelation scene in In Green's Jungles.
answers · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Horn substituted a fake secret to protect Nettle; Inhumi get their minds from human blood

Though Jahlee's words aren't entirely clear, it seems to be a little more than just that: each inhumu/a derives its "mind" from a specific human whose blood its parent drank.

I agree it's a disappointment, and somewhat inconsistent; but I think we're stuck with it. The theory that Horn substituted a fake secret to protect Nettle from the inhumi doesn't hold up: the inhumi know that Horn knows the secret, and they would be able to figure out what he had done. If he thought he would be endangering Nettle by writing down that she had been told the secret, he would have omitted it completely. (When he wrote these words, he would have expected to be able to excise anything dangerous from the manuscript if it ever was published.)

This doesn't work any better as the secret. It doesn't fit the revelation scene with Krait in IGJ.

--Adam
