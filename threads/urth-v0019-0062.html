The Book of Wonders of Urth and Sky and other Urth tales

1998urthThe Book of the New SunC R Culver

Themes: Myth and folklore sources, Stories within stories

1998-10-07 C R Culver #

Asks where the tales told in the Urth cycle come from, after noting that Foila's tale "The Armigette's Daughter" seems to derive from a Latin American folktale, "El Pajaro de Siete Colores." Requests help identifying the folklore sources of the other tales in The Book of Wonders of Urth and Sky and those told at the lazaret.
questions · source-and-allusion · theories: The Armigette's Daughter derives from El Pajaro de Siete Colores

I know from reading a reading in a Spanish textbook once that Foila's tale, "The Armigette's Daughter," comes from a Latin American folktale called "El Pajaro de Siete Colores," or "The Bird of Seven Colours." What I would like to know is: where do the other tales told in the Urth cycle come from? The several tales of the Book of Wonders of Urth and Sky and the other tales told at the lazaret?
Any help would greatly illuminate me on the folklore of Urth.
