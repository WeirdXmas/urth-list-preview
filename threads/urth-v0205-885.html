2002-05-07 David Duffy #

Argues that the Australian Aboriginal and Dreamtime allusions in Wolfe's story "A Story" are a nod to Bradbury rather than a real link, and that Marsch's shadow-child claim should not be taken at face value. Proposes that the narrator of The Fifth Head of Cerberus could have written "A Story", listing parallels such as the twin, the father figure, and the cultural details, and responds to alga's fairy-tradition reading.
proposes · source-and-allusion · puzzle-solving · theories: The narrator of The Fifth Head of Cerberus wrote "A Story"; "A Story" alludes to Bradbury rather than Dreamtime

And are polymorphic: short and ugly, taller and fair.

It is the obvious link, given the "abo" terminology, which I think is unique to Australian vernacular, but there is little material specific to indigenous Australian traditions in "A Story". The Dreamtime ended long before 1788, though it is a nice conceit to imagining stopping with the introduction of European time. I think it is a nod to Bradbury, not _The Exiles_ but a similar short.

To be tricksy and ambiguous ;) I can't believe anyone would be gullible enough to believe Wolfe when he tells an interviewer Marsch was a shadow child! Unfortunately he is not so fervent a modernist as to provide a key to his works cf Joyce (writing to mates to tell them what each chapter of Ulysses is *really* about, and almost btw what David is reading ;)).

Of course, we are allowed to have multiple worldviews simultaneously "true" in this postmodern era.

_"A Story"_ is pretty Wolfean in its' equation of simplicity and godliness versus the more technologically adept and nasty Mars(c)hmen, see the epigraph from StJotC. Aside from _VRT_, one could easily believe that No. 5 wrote it:
1) killing and replacing a twin -- the twin in this case is the other No 5 personality that runs things while the narrator is blacked out. It is presumably been inculcated by the father as in

2) The castration, and nightly examination of dreams and visions by Lastwind, a surrogate father.

3) cultural details: flails, ordeal by fire, mating with trees, drowning to honour rivers, stone tools not being needed
4) Etruscans, Atlantis and Gondwanaland
5) Veil: in where did the slavery and cruelty come from -- Marshmen

David Duffy.

2002-05-07 Adam Stephanides #

Adds to David Duffy's point that Wolfe's stories set simple, godly figures against technically adept, nasty Marshmen by noting a parallel opposition in The Fifth Head of Cerberus. Points to the individualistic Sandwalker versus the collectivist Marshmen and Shadow children, and to Victor calling the mountain-dwelling Annese the Free People and claiming to belong to the Laissez-Faire Party.
supports · close-textual-reading

--Adam
