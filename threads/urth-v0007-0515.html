Green Book

Themes: Books, libraries and writing

1998-02-04 James Jordan #

Proposes that the two books Severian describes at the Library, a huge genealogy of Thecla's family and a small green book of prayers and icons, form a contrast between the old world and the world to come. Grounds the reading in Severian's wording about the books and the phrases "exalted, exultant" and "humbly satisfied," linking the green book to the green man and Nutria.
proposes · close-textual-reading · allegory-and-typology · theories: Green book represents the world to come

Severian describes two of the books he collects at the Library. One is a huge book, 40" x 15" in size, that is a genealogy of Thecla's family. The other is a small green book, the size of one's palm, that consists of prayers and ikons.
Two families. Two groups of "exalted, exultant" people. One inflating itself with a huge book, but representing the old world; the other humbly satisfied with a tiny book, but representing the world to come. Green book. Green man. The world to come.
