The thread: Participants traced the voice-actuated transcriber "voisrite" through Wolfe's 1970s stories into the 2004 story "Monster" and wondered why he revived it after thirty years. Stockhoff argued it is a generic tool name rather than a brand and reported his puzzlement without offering a theory, while Aramini noted a trademark sign appears beside it only in "Monster." Aramini also questioned whether the foreign law in "The Blue Mouse" is literally alien, suggesting a shared universe with "Silhouette" would justify that reading. Stockhoff's card names "The Detective of Dreams" where the others name "The Blue Mouse," a discrepancy left unresolved, and no one answers the shared-universe proposal, so the thread ends without resolution.

2014-05-13 Marc Aramini #

Notes that the voice-actuated transcriber brand "voisrite" appears in two 1970s stories, "The Blue Mouse" and "Silhouette", and has now reappeared in the 2004 story "Monster". Expresses puzzlement that Wolfe revived the same device after about thirty years, pointing to the shared brand name as the connecting detail.
informs · close-textual-reading

I noticed that Wolfe used a voice actuated transcriber in two stories from the 70s with the brand name voisrite, connecting "the blue mouse" and "silhouette", but just noticed that it also appears in 2004's "Monster". I am kind of baffled that he brought it back after 30 years.

2014-05-13 David Stockhoff replying to Marc Aramini #

Argues that the voisrite voice-actuated transcriber is a generic tool name rather than a brand, and notes it recurs in two 1970s stories, "The Detective of Dreams" and "Silhouette", and again in the 2004 story "Monster". Expresses puzzlement that Wolfe brought the device back after thirty years, reporting the observation rather than arguing a theory.
informs · close-textual-reading

I never thought of it as a brand but as a generic name for a tool. Thus the "unbreakable" spelling. Think of it as a "phone."

2014-05-13 Marc Aramini replying to DAVID STOCKHOFF #

Points out that the trademark sign appears beside voisrite in the 2004 story Monster but not in the earlier stories, and questions whether the foreign law in The Blue Mouse is literally alien. Suggests that a shared fictional universe for The Blue Mouse and Silhouette would make the alien reading easier to justify, citing the lads' complaint about foreigners.
proposes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion · theories: The foreign law in The Blue Mouse is alien; The Blue Mouse and Silhouette share a fictional universe

Sure, but in "Monster" it does have the trademark sign next to it though (not in the earlier texts, however). In "The Blue Mouse" I have never been confident about whether the foreign law comprises an alien contingent or just a jingoistic representation: "our lads are only wanting to be free of the foreign law, and the greasy dark foreigners followin' your blue rag half round the world to suck our blood." Sometimes I like to take things quite literally in Wolfe. Easier to justify aliens if The Blue Mouse and Silhouette occur in the same fictional universe.
