Argues that the New President's succession in the story was a real inauguration, and that the two-term limit allows a workable timeline with the murder and 'Copperhead' in 2009. Replies to Roy and the quoted critique by citing the two-term rule and a dated 2008 timeline, then jokes about an Old Sun series set there.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: The New President's succession counts as an inauguration

Roy wrote that the NP

Ummmm... that _was_ his inauguration. There was no big hoo-hah with both houses of Congress looking on reverentially, and no inaugural ball (which would have been decidedly inappropriate under the circumstances), but it was an inauguration.

Further, if he'd been the VP of a president who died or left office any time after the two-year mark, he could have served the remainder of his predecessor's term and still run for the office twice -- the so-called "two-term limit" is actually two pieces: you can't be elected more than twice, and you can't serve a total greater than ten years.

If I recall, there was a ceremony before congress, but no inaugural ball. (Would've had to compete with the parties in the streets all over the country anyway after Tricky resigned.)

This is all workable, as follows. I'm going to use the 2008 election to avoid implicating any specific living persons:

Winter 2007/8: The Old President dies/resigns/whatever, the NP succeeds to office (with minimalist inauguration)
Spring 2008: The trouble in Spokane
Summer 2008: The crash
Late Summer/Early Fall 2008: Lilith appears
November 2008: The NP is elected for the first time
January 2009: The NP is inaugurated. He is now in a meaningful sense a New President -- he will have the "honeymoon"
period with press & Congress that any new administration gets.
February-March 2009: The murder
March-April 2009: "Copperhead." (It is Spring, and about three years before the beginning of the 2012 campaign season.)
