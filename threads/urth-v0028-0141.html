1999-09-29 Michael Straight #

Argues that Severian's survival of swallowed lead shot may not be judged by modern toxicology, because human physiology may have changed between his time and ours. Replies to Carlton Greene's doubts about the lead shot by noting the chronology could run either way, so the ingested shot's effects cannot be judged by modern standards.
supports · chronology-and-plot-logic

One wrinkle is that we don't know how much human physiology has changed between Severian's time and ours (or between ours and Severians, depending on how you want to think about the chronology).

-Rostrum

1999-09-30 Peter Stephenson #

Jokingly argues that the lead shot weighting down the dead woman in The Book of the New Sun would not harm a corpse, especially if the Claw had revived her. Responds to Carlton Greene's suggestion that the Claw caused the strike on the lead weight, adding a tongue-in-cheek remark about the dead not minding lead poisoning and a warning against serious fan fiction.
chats · theories: The Claw of the Conciliator revived the drowned woman

Perfectly reasonable --- she was, after all, already dead when she was weighted down with lead shot. (Well --- I hope so.) If the Claw brought her back to life, presumably a bit of lead poisoning isn't going to be much of a problem. To put it another way, I don't suppose the other corpses lie around thinking `Ugh, this lead shot's not doing me any good'. Before this becomes serious fan fiction...

corncrake
