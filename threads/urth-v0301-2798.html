2003-01-24 Thomas Bitterman #

Compares Tom Wolfe's accessible, contemporary journalistic novels with Gene Wolfe's dense, decayed far-future and distant-past fiction, after a list member asks about Thomas Wolfe. Draws on the poster's reading of The Electric Kool-Aid Acid Test, Bonfire of the Vanities, and other titles, a brief off-list comparison rather than textual analysis.
informs

I've read (very little) Tom Wolfe: _The Electric Kool-Aid Acid Test_, _Bonfire of the Vanities_, _From Bauhuas to Our House_, and _The Kandy-Kolored Tangerine-Flake Streamline Baby_. Based on that he's an easy read, good style, interesting topics. He started in journalism and his subjects tend to be contemporary people in interesting or extraordinary situations.

In some ways he's almost the opposite of Gene Wolfe. Gene writes very dense, difficult, rather pessimistic stories about times and places far away where everything is strange and decayed. Thomas writes brighter stories in contemporary settings where people cope pretty well. He'll probably be remembered as one of the best American novelists of the 20th century.

- tom
