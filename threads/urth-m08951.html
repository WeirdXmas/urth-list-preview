The thread: The thread began as a question about whether readers should keep going with Pynchon's Gravity's Rainbow, given its dense detail and disturbing content. Son of Witz asked whether to read past page 300 and compared Pynchon unfavourably with Burroughs, while Matthew Keeley recommended The Crying of Lot 49 as a shorter, funnier entry point. Adam Thornton corrected the record by separating two distinct disturbing scenes, and brunians and Son of Witz exchanged remarks about the sexual content involving young girls. The discussion drifted into joking and never returned to Wolfe's own fiction, ending without any resolution.
  • Son of Witz: doubts Gravity's Rainbow is worth reading past page 300 given its density and explicit content
  • Matthew Keeley: recommends The Crying of Lot 49 as an easier, funnier Pynchon starting point
  • Adam Thornton: distinguishes two separate disturbing scenes rather than defending the content
  • brunians: notes the explicit scenes involving young girls and disclaims any interest in readers who find them erotic

2009-03-18 Matthew Keeley replying to Son of Witz #

Recommends Thomas Pynchon's The Crying of Lot 49 as a shorter, funnier entry point than Gravity's Rainbow, based on the poster's own reading of Lot 49 and Against the Day. Responds to Son of Witz's complaint about Gravity's Rainbow's detail, mentioning a comic scene in Against the Day that readers may recognize.
chats

I haven't read GR; the only Pynchon books I've read are The Crying of Lot 49 and Against the Day (which is actually a lot longer than GR). I think you might enjoy Lot 49 - it's less than two hundred pages, there's not nearly as much detail, and there are several scenes that made me laugh out loud.

There's also one scene in Against the Day that had me laughing for about five minutes on end, though it was a variation on one of Pynchon's kinky sex scenes, so YMMV. I think people who have read AtD may know the scene I refer to?

-Matt
