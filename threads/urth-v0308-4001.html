Asks whether the Dorplanders' Yoda-like syntax in the Long Sun books, who bookend the series, comments on a story told backwards. Builds on a quoted remark by Mr. Borski that the Dorplander dialect is less warped than Pig's and Remora's, offering the idea tentatively and apologizing for possible repetition.
questions · genre-and-craft · close-textual-reading · theories: Dorplanders' dialect mirrors the story being told backwards

Thank you, Mr. Borski. Looking forward to your further thoughts. Just a quick dirty little tangent I simply have to vocalize:

"...though to be fair none of the dialect is as bad as Pig's; nor is it as syntactically warped as the Dorplanders of Blue, nor as inflected as
Remora's. Still..."

(Apologies if this has been run over several times)
Did the Dorplanders' (who bookend the series) Yoda-esque dialect strike anyone else as being a strange commentary on a story which is essentially told backwards? Perhaps that's over-simplifying, but you get the idea.
