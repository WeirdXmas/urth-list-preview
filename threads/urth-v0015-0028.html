Little Severian, Things in Jars, and Martians

Urth list · 1 messages · 1998-06-27 to 1998-06-27

1998urthThe Book of the New SunJohn Moar

1998-06-27 John Moar #

Argues that the Little Severian sequence in The Book of the New Sun uses shock rather than foreshadowing, so rereading it knowing his death felt flat. Also asks for information on a homunculus and cites Stanley G. Weinbaum's Valley of Dreams, where Tweel chases a ratlike creature from a library, as a parallel.
proposes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion · theories: Little Severian's death is shock rather than foreshadowing

Thanks for the responses so far, although I'd still like some information on the homunculus.

As for my not 'getting' the Little Severian sequence: I originally read parts of tBotNS when I was far too young to understand it properly. I did, however, remember how Little Severian died.

This sequence is, IMO, one of the simplest uses of shock in tBotNS, not following the usual methods of foreshadowing or retrospective inevitability.
Little Severian's death seems to be unusually timed, part-way between leaving him a bit-player and expanding him to a secondary character. So, when I reread this knowing when he would die, it fell flat.

BTW, do Big and Little Severian remind anyone else of Batman and Robin? <g>

On the subjects of the rats in the library. I was reminded of something in a story by Stanley G. Weinbaum. The story, _Valley of Dreams_, is the sequel to _A Martian Odyssey_. In this story, the Martian Tweel, upon entering a library, angrily chases away a ratlike creature which is attempting to read the books stored there. This story may be rare enough that this is nothing, but still...
