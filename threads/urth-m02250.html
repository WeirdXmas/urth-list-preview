2006-06-11 JWillard replying to Kate Savage #

Quotes Gene Wolfe from Castle of Days recommending the Compact OED, the American Heritage desk dictionary, and Merriam-Webster's Unabridged, while noting the Random House alternative. Responds to Kate Savage's request for dictionaries to decode Wolfe's obscure words, saying the poster favors the OED and Webster's Second but finds both unwieldy.
answers · source-and-allusion

Wolfe, from p. 250 of Castle of Days: "The best dictionary I have found is The Compact Edition of the Oxford English Dictionary. I also recommend a small book called The New York Times Everyday Reader's Dictionary of Misunderstood, Misused, Mispronounced Words... A good desk dictionary, such as the American Heritage, is always of value. So is a good Latin-English dictionary.
Be warned that anybody can call a dictionary Webster's - there's no copyright on that name. The real McCoy...is Merriam-Webster's unabridged, but the current (third) edition has fallen among the flakes; you can get better value for your money by buying the Random House..."

OED and W2 are my favorites, but they're both unwieldy. (Good exercise, though!)

2006-06-12 Mark Millman replying to Kate Savage #

Recommends reference books for obscure Wolfe vocabulary, including Byrne's Mrs. Byrne's Dictionary, Wolfe's essay The Castle of the Otter, and Andre-Driussi's Lexicon Urthus, noting its scarcity and price. Responds to Kate Savage's request for dictionaries and offers to send definitions from vocabulary lists she provides.
answers · source-and-allusion

Kate,

You might also want to get a copy of _Mrs.
Byrne's Dictionary of Unusual, Obscure, and
Preposterous Words_, by Josefa Heifetz Byrne.
Wolfe has referred to it in, I believe, an
essay or an interview, and it has some words
that are tough to find even in the OED.

Then there's always _The Castle of the Otter_,
collected in _Castle of Days_ but frequently
available on the secondary market, and
Michael Andre-Driussi's _Lexicon Urthus_,
though this latter is very difficult to find,
and usually quite costly when found. I have
a copy of _Castle_ I can give you--let me
know by private e-mail whether or not you al-
ready have a copy--but the three copies of
the _Lexicon_ I found are $225 (abebooks),
$250 (Amazon), and $495 (!--abebooks again),
respectively.

Or you personally may send me vocabulary
lists if you like, and I'll send definitions.
(This offer available only to Kate Savage.)

Mark

2006-06-15 Maru Dubshinki replying to Macronaut #

Disputes Macronaut's suggestion of the OED DVD, arguing that the printed Compact OED is the better buy because it costs little more, lasts longer, and is not limited to Windows. Responds to Macronaut's recommendation by citing price, the poster's own use of the disc versions, and the question of future-proofing.
disputes

To be honest, I'd have to disagree. I own the Compact OED and I've used the disc versions, and the COED is a better choice. The price differential doesn't really justify one over the other (for such a long-term purchase, anyway), and even worse, the disc version is Windows-only. In ten or 20 years, which one do you think will still be as useful as the day they were bought? (And that's assuming one even uses Windows, which isn't a safe assumption). No doubt there are other reasons other than simply future-proofing things.

~maru
