Miles, Jonas and that sailor guy

Urth list · 3 messages · 2011-02-09 to 2011-02-10

2011urthThe Book of the New SunGerry QuinnAndrew Mason
The thread: Readers asked whether the soldier Miles is a returned Jonas, a question prompted by Severian's Miles-Jonas theory. Andrew Mason first disputed the link, arguing that the Wellerisms Severian ties to Jonas are spoken by several sailors, and asked when Severian's claims should be trusted given his dismissed Typhon and witch claims. He then reversed course to propose that Miles may be Jonas, grounding this in Long Sun parallels about beings existing in two places at once. Gerry Quinn questioned whether Miles is Jonas's resurrected body or another figure, floated a dead-crewman theory, and left the Wellerism point unresolved, so the thread ends without agreement.
  • Andrew Mason: Miles is not Jonas, since the Wellerisms are shared by several sailors, and Severian's claims need scrutiny
  • Andrew Mason: Miles may be Jonas, since mirrors are non-spatial and Jonas never left for a distant galaxy
  • Gerry Quinn: Miles may be a different figure, perhaps a dead crewman whose parts repaired Jonas's ship

Themes: Unreliable narration, Doubles, twins and clones, Death and resurrection

2011-02-09 Andrew Mason #

Argues that the soldier Miles is not a returned Jonas, since the Wellerisms Severian ties to Jonas are spoken by several sailors, including Maxellindis' uncle, Tzadkiel's crew and Eata. Replies to Marc Aramini's doubts about Severian's Miles-Jonas theory and asks when readers should trust Severian, noting that his Typhon and witch claims are generally dismissed.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Miles is not a returned Jonas; Wellerisms are common sailor idiom; Miles' parents were sailors

No, I've always doubted it. Severian thinks Miles is Jonas because he uses Wellerisms, and even works out a theory which would explain why robots do so. But later we discover this way of speaking is common among sailors, used by Maxellindis' uncle, several sailors on Tzadkiel's ship, and even Eata in later life.

(I suppose Urth-bound sailors might have got it off interplanetary sailors, who got it off robots. But it may just be that Miles' parents were sailors.)

It's a puzzling question when we should trust Severian and when we shouldn't. People seem very ready to accept his Miles-Jonas theory ,and his 'first Severian' theory - which is proved to be at least partly wrong, in connection with Apu-Punchau. Yet his claims that Typhon is lying, and that Merryn and the Cumaean are _not_ the two witches he met as a boy, are generally dismissed.

2011-02-09 Andrew Mason replying to Andrew Mason #

Argues that Miles may be Jonas and that Severian's theory of mirrors as non-spatial devices means Jonas never left for a distant galaxy. Grounds this in Long Sun parallels where Typhon and Silk seem to exist in two places at once, and offers that split spirits share an origin.
proposes · close-textual-reading · myth-and-structure · theories: Miles is Jonas; Mirrors are not space-travel devices; Spirits can exist in two places

It's part of Severian's theory that the mirrors are _not_, as he first thought, simply space-travel devices. So if Miles is Jonas - which I'm not sure about - he presumably never was in a distant galaxy. (I'm not sure where he was, as Severian doesn't elaborate.)

In any case, in the light of _Long Sun_, it seems that in that universe a spirit can be in two places at once - Typhon both on the Whorl and on Urth, Silk both in Mainframe and in his body. (Once the separation has taken place, they may really be two spirits, but they have the same origin.)

2011-02-10 Gerry Quinn replying to Andrew Mason #

Questions whether Miles is Jonas's resurrected body or some other figure, offering a theory about a dead crewman whose parts were used to repair Jonas's ship. Responds to Andrew Mason by noting the robots' status and why Miles would share Jonas's personality, and the post treats the Wellerism and sailor-speech point as unresolved.
questions · puzzle-solving · theories: Miles resurrected the body of Jonas's dead crewmate

From: "Andrew Mason" <[email]>

He also dreams of a woman with red-gold hair, whom Severian identifies with Jolenta.

I certainly don't dismiss the latter. I'm not sure how he'd know anything about Typhon, though.

As for Miles, I really don't know. One idea that has occurred to me is that he resurrected the body of the guy who was killed when Jonas's ship crashed, and whose parts were taken to repair Jonas. But then why would he have anything of Jonas's personality?

On the other hand, Jonas is a machine, so shouldn't he have been resurrected as one, if indeed he is resurrectable?

It's a puzzler.

- Gerry Quinn
