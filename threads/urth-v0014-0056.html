Gorgeous Borges

1998-06-19 Michael Straight #

Proposes Borges's short story "The Immortal" as a precursor to Wolfe's work, noting its narrator's journey, its fictitious manuscript, and its confusion about the narrator's identity. Asks list readers to articulate what is distinctly Wolfean about the story, and also mentions Bester's "Fondly Fahrenheit" as similar.
proposes · genre-and-craft · theories: Borges's "The Immortal" is a precursor to Wolfe

I submit Jorge Luis Borges's short story "The Immortal" for the those looking for Lupine precursors.

The narrator's journey reminds me of tBotNS in ways I can't put my finger on. The fictitious manuscript and the characters from antiquity remind me of the Soldier books. But most of all, there is that confusion about the narrator's identity that Wolfe loves so much ("Fondly Fahrenheit" by Alfred Bester also seems like an important story in that regard).

Is anyone able to better articulate what is so Wolfeish about this story?

-Rostrum

P.S. -- Yes, I know how to pronounce 'Borges'. It was just a joke.

1998-06-22 Michael Straight #

Notes that the immortals Borges's narrator meets in 'The Immortal' resemble an episode of Stanislaw Lem's Cyberiad, where a constructor visits the most powerful planet in the universe. Asks whether Wolfe has ever given a public opinion of Lem and whether he is a fan, adding a joke that the Cyberiad is wittier and does not rhyme with 'gorgeous'.
questions · source-and-allusion

Also, I forgot to mention that the disposition of the immortals when the protagonist meets them reminds me somewhat of the episode of Stanislaw Lem's _Cyberiad_ where one of the constructors visits the most advanced and powerful planet in the universe.

Has Wolfe ventured a public opinion of Lem's work? Is he a fan?

Mmm. Good one. It's also more witty, if I remember correctly.

i.e. it doesn't really rhyme with 'gorgeous'.

-Rostrum
