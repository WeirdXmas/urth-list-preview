Where did Abaia go?

Urth list · 17 messages · 2010-07-18 to 2010-07-27

2010urthThe Book of the New SunJohn WatkinsLee BermanJosh GellerAdam ThorntonDavid StockhoffJames WynnJeff Wilson
The thread: Readers asked where Abaia went after the flood that ended the Urth of the New Sun, and whether her kind could escape. Lee Berman argued Abaia is a freshwater eel living in Lake Diuturna, later revising to a splitting survivor theory, while Jeff Wilson objected that Juturna and Baldanders end up as sea-dwellers. John Watkins proposed Abaia was crushed or trapped by rising land, citing the Eschatology and Genesis dialogue. James Wynn questioned whether the Megatherians, including Typhon, could leave Urth, and Wilson speculated they were stranded and that star-swimming undines might carry them away. The thread ends with Wilson shifting to Juturna's possible link to Abaia's line in the Long Sun books, with no consensus reached.
  • Lee Berman: Abaia is a freshwater eel in Lake Diuturna, and she may have survived by splitting into many bodies
  • Jeff Wilson: Juturna and Baldanders are sea-dwellers, and Typhon was stranded by pride and his fleeing commanders
  • John Watkins: Abaia was destroyed by a foundered or rising continent, based on the demon dialogue
  • James Wynn: Megatherians had no reason to stay and seemingly could not leave after the sun's heat declined

Themes: Myth and folklore sources, The dying sun and the cosmos, Deep time and ruins

2010-07-18 Lee Berman #

Proposes that Abaia is a freshwater eel rather than an ocean creature, so a flood could kill it through salt water, and that Lake Diuturna is its home. Supports this with the name Juturna, Baldanders living under the lake, the lake people's fish god, and the town name Murene meaning moray eel.
proposes · source-and-allusion · myth-and-structure · theories: Abaia is a freshwater eel living in Lake Diuturna; Murene means moray eel and points to Abaia

In my last post under Patera Inire (which has a typo- should start- "I don't agree") I mention an allegorical answer I think best for explaining how an ocean-bound creature like Abaia could be destroyed by a flood.

But for those who don't see Judeo-Christianity in this story, I will offer a more textual explanation. Perhaps Abaia doesn't really live in the ocean. Abaia is the name for a magical *freshwater* eel in Melanesian mythology. So perhaps a flood could kill off Abaia simply via contact with salt water.

Abaia's most likely freshwater home would surely be Lake Diuturna. We have the similarity of the name to Juturna, the daughter-bride of Abaia. We have (perhaps aspiring megatherian) Baldanders choosing to live near and eventually under the waters of this lake. The Lake/Island people have their belief in a fish god beneath the waters. But perhaps the best evidence for the presence of Abaia is the Lake/Shore people's town which is named "Murene". This Latin derived, Germanic word basically means "moray eel" (which does include freshwater versions).

2010-07-18 Jeff Wilson replying to Lee Berman #

Disputes Lee Berman's proposal that Abaia lives in Lake Diuturna, arguing that Juturna and Baldanders end up as sea-dwellers by Severian's return. Grounds the objection in Juturna's claim that her kind swim between the stars, the varied salt and pH conditions, and the upheaval caused by Ushas' coming.
disputes · close-textual-reading · myth-and-structure · theories: Abaia lives in Lake Diuturna

The problem with this is that Juturna and Baldanders are both sea-dwellers by the time of Sev's return. Juturna says her kind swim between the stars and supposedly Abaia's seed came from off-world as well, so they would presumably have to cope with all kinds of salt levels, pHs, alkalai content, etc.

The upheaval of Ushas' coming might result in very low fish stocks for a long time, until the dispersed populations regroup and currents and thermoclines re-establish themselves in a stable fashion. Meanwhile, the hungry undines gotta eat something....

2010-07-18 John Watkins replying to Lee Berman #

Proposes two textual explanations for how Abaia, an ocean creature, could be destroyed, namely being crushed by a foundered continent or trapped on a rising new continent and collapsing under its own weight. Grounds this in the Eschatology and Genesis demon dialogue about continents sinking and rising from the sea, and in the claim that Abaia's kind die out of water, as Juturna does.
proposes · close-textual-reading · myth-and-structure · theories: Abaia destroyed by foundered continent or mountain; Abaia collapsed under own weight on rising continent

I think there are a couple of textual possibilities, actually. From Eschatology and Genesis:

First Demon: The continents themselves are old as raddled women, long since stripped of beauty and fertility. The New Sun comes--

Autarch: I know!

First Demon: --and he will send them crashing into the sea like foundered ships.

Second Demon: And from the sea lift new--glittering with gold, silver, iron, and copper.

So Abaia could easy have been crushed by a foundered continent or mountain, or trapped on a new continent as it rises from the sea and then, assuming he could survive the upheaval itself, collapsed under his own weight. I think the latter is more probable, simply because we're told in the text that Abaia and his kind can't survive outside of water long (perhaps Baldanders is the reference, I can't recall) and we actually see Juturna dying in this manner.

Argues that Abaia may have survived the drowning of Lake Diuturna by splitting into many bodies, and that Juturna is a surviving remnant of a demon. Answers John Watkins's suggestion that Abaia was crushed or trapped by rising land, citing gravitational waves, Tzadkiel's sponge-like account of multiple bodies, and Seawrack and The Mother as pinched-off agents.
proposes · puzzle-solving · allegory-and-typology · theories: Abaia survives as multiple pinched-off bodies; Juturna is a surviving piece of Abaia

Yes, considering the "waves of gravitation" emitted by the onrushing New Sun that could be the force which kills off the giant monsters. Or perhaps some newly risen continent has mountain sized gelatinous masses on it baking in the warm, golden radiance of Ushas.

I still think the evidence of Abaia in Lake Diuturna is too compelling to completely ignore. Doesn't matter though. As a giant shape-shifting alien who reproduces asexually and sexually (daughter-brides) the guy can easily be in two or many more places at once.
