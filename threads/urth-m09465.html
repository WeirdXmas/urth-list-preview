The thread: Brian Lovely opened the thread proposing that the Neighbors hide inside multi-limbed animals on Blue, and that the baletiger in In Green's Jungles is a host, citing Horn's eight-legged wolves and other clues. He then extended the idea to the Long Sun books, arguing the domes are undersea shelters the Neighbors engineered, and asked about Seawrack's reactions to blood sharing. James Wynn offered alternatives: the domes might signal a drying sea or a floating island raising a dome above the water. Lovely replied that the domes may be shelters against the Inhumi, since Krait swims to Horn's boat, and reiterated the drying or floating-island options. The thread ended without resolution, with Lovely and Wynn testing competing readings of the domes.
  • Brian Lovely: Neighbors hide inside multi-limbed animals, including the baletiger as a host
  • Brian Lovely: Long Sun domes are undersea Neighbor shelters, not greenhouses
  • Brian Lovely: Domes may be shelters against the Inhumi, since Krait swims to Horn's boat
  • James Wynn: Underwater domes may signal a drying sea rather than a world flood
  • James Wynn: A floating island may lift a dome above the waters

2009-05-04 Brian Lovely #

Proposes that the Neighbors hide inside multi-limbed animals on Blue, and that the baletiger in In Green's Jungles is a Neighbor host. Cites Horn's eight-legged wolves, Hide's question about the baletiger, a Dorp trial remark about Horn's house, and Babbie's extra limbs and Horn's miscounting of creatures near Maytera Marble's island as evidence.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Neighbors hide inside multi-limbed animals; The baletiger was a Neighbor host; Babbie is a Neighbor host; Horn once stayed in Babbie's house

"They're hiding in animals" Maybe this applies to the Neighbors as well. Not all animals on Blue have extra limbs. Somewhere in the books, Horn talks about eight-legged wolves and regular wolves. ...I think it's wolves. Anyway, what if the Neighbors decided to hide from the Inhumi in animals, and the animals they are inside are the multi-limbed ones. It may be a kind of defense against the Inhumi in that if the host animal is attacked, the Inhumi only gets non-sentient animal blood.

Perhaps the Inhumi mimicked Pas also by hedging their bet with more than one survival strategy: hiding in animals, hiding in trees, maybe hiding in undersea domes, and possibly hiding in "the spaces between". Or maybe the "spaces between" part is when they come out of their various hiding places.

At the end of "In Green's Jungles" Silkhorn and Hide see a baletiger. Silkhorn has been doing a lot of communing with the Outsider. Hide asks about the baletiger something like "It that...?" and Silkhorn replies "I think so". ...or something very much like that. There are at least two interpretations of what Hide was thinking. One that the baletiger was a messenger of the Outsider, or at least currently under the direct influence of the Outsider. Another is that the baletiger was a Neighbor host.

During the trial in Dorp, a Neighbor says that Horn has stayed in his house. Assuming you accept the theory that at some point Horn has been in Babbie, that could be when this occurred. Babbie has extra limbs, marking him (according to this theory ) as a Neighbor host. Also, Horn first sees Babbie jumping from Maytera Marble's island, he has trouble determining how many creatures he is seeing. This confusion of number is a very Neighbor-like trait.

FWIW, my impression of the message of multi-limbs on Blue's creatures is the
opposite
that some others get. Having four limbs is not a universal ideal or baseline
(as our arthropod
cousins show); it is just an evolutionary by-product of the basic earth
vertebrate pattern.
 
So rather than seeing multi-limbs as an alteration, I see them as a way that
Wolfe marks
certain creatures on Blue as natives; others are are alien invaders. This
could be a
clear message that Blue is not Earth or Urth or Ushas.
 
Also, I suppose it has been mentioned before but I just re-noticed Wolfe's
description of 
Inhumi as not being good with tools.  Add this to their parasitic and
mimicking nature and
I think the connection to Shadow Children becomes quite powerful. This might
make Fifth Head
of Cerberus a very fruitful place for understanding Inhumi origins and how
they think and feel
as they assume a new identity.

2009-05-05 Brian Lovely replying to Brian Lovely #

Speculates about the Neighbors in Wolfe's Long Sun books, proposing that the domes are undersea shelters rather than greenhouses and that the Neighbors engineered animals to host them. Also asks why Seawrack reacts to blood sharing in the He-pen-sheep and Pajarocu scenes, and whether the Mother made her a siren through blood transfer.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Neighbors used Marc's eat-spit trees to create hosts; Seawrack was transformed into a siren by blood transfer; The Mother made Seawrack a siren

Some more thoughts about Neighbors, domes and blood. If the domes are greenhouses, it seems strange that the one Horn and Seawrack find has no old growth in it. Where did the plants go? Were they removed for the purpose for which they were grown? The glass of the domes intensifies light, which I have interpreted as meaning that the domes are greenhouses. Seawrack says she has seen structures similar to the ruined one she and Horn find on the weed island. If they are just shelters, maybe the light intensification is to allow sunlight into an undersea dome.

As to the Neighbors hiding in animals, I think maybe the Neighbors used the Marc's eat/spit out hybrid behavior of the trees to create animals capable of hosting them.

What's up with blood? We know that Wolfe uses blood transfer as a mechanism of personality/physical attribute transfer. Why does Seawrack react at the mention of blood sharing? I just re-read the encounter with He-pen-sheep in the Land of Fires. When He-pen-sheep says he shared blood with a shearbear, Seawrack reacts with surprise/shock. This happens again in Pajarocu. Was Seawrack transformed from a vanilla human to a siren through some blood transfer mechanism? The Mother likely made her a siren in the first place. Could the Mother have altered the Neighbors so that they could escape the Inhumi?

2009-05-05 James Wynn replying to Brian Lovely #

Proposes that the underwater domes Seawrack has seen may signal the drying of the sea rather than a world flood, or a floating island raising a dome above the waters. Responds to Brian Lovely's suggestion that the domes are shelters whose light intensification admits sunlight, building on the dome Horn finds in the Book of the Short Sun.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Domes signal drying of Blue rather than flood; Floating island raises dome above waters

This is something I have been thinking recently. It is common to think of the underwater domes Seawrack has seen as a sign of a world flood. But the dome Horn found might be a sign of the *drying* of Blue, or of the floating island raising the dome above the waters.
