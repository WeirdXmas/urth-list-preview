Corrects the poster's earlier claim by explaining that the letter in question is a lowercase eth, not a thorn, and that capital eth looks like Ð while capital thorn is Þ. Admits the error after Mark Millman privately pointed it out, and apologises for the confusion.
informs · source-and-allusion

Actually I'm still wrong, as Mark Millman politely pointed out to me in a private e-mail. The character I was thinking of is actually capital eth. It looks like Ð, if you have the same character set as me, whereas capital thorn is: Þ. The character mantis was describing is actually a lowercase eth, ð, and not a thorn as Mark and I initially thought.

Apologies for any confusion caused.

1999-03-31 Derek Bell #

Adds that Croatian may have a letter resembling the capital eth, while noting that its pronunciation is unknown to him. Responds to a correction by Derek Bell's earlier post, which Mark Millman had pointed out, by describing the eth's shape for readers with different character sets.
informs

I think Croatian has a similar looking character - I don't know how it is pronounced, though.

(For those with a different character set, the capital eth looks like a capital D with a small horizontal line through the vertical line on the left side.)

Derek
