The thread: Newcomer David A Baird asked which Wolfe books to read next and how far in the future the New Sun novels are set. Michael Andre-Driussi and Dan'l Danehy-Oakes gave reading orders, and James Wynn cited Lexicon Urthus for a 97,000-year date. Danehy-Oakes argued that uplifted ruins would need millions of years, citing carved mountain figures, while Baird expected millions of years too, citing a black hole and an oscillating universe. Mark Millman then argued that 'The Cat' belongs among the New Sun connections, and the thread drifted toward other stories. No agreement on the date was reached.
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: the 97,000-year date is implausible, since uplifted ruins would need millions of years
  • David A Baird: expects millions of years, citing the hierodules' black hole and an oscillating-universe idea
  • James Wynn: Lexicon Urthus places Severian about 97,000 years in the future
  • Michael Andre-Driussi: gave the full reading order of the Wolfe books and welcomed the newcomer
  • Mark Millman: 'The Cat' belongs among the New Sun connections because a Steward Odilo appears in it

Themes: Deep time and ruins, The dying sun and the cosmos, Books, libraries and writing

2002-07-16 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Recommends The Book of the Long Sun and The Book of the Short Sun as the next reads, the former tangentially related in plot, the latter a direct sequel. Answers Dave Baird's question by also suggesting Free Live Free, Peace and The Island of Doctor Death and Other Stories for readers wanting to leave the Briah Cycle.
answers

Mantis replied...

Second that.

Actually two questions, of which only the first is "easy" (and even that somewhat quibblesome):

... plus various short stories scattered across a variety of anthologies, collections and magazines ...

... plus THE FIFTH HEAD OF CERBERUS, which, if very unlikely to be set in the same universe, is at least thematically related to the Books of the <Foo> Sun, a/k/a the Lupiverse, a/k/a the Briah Cycle ...

... none of which, however, addresses Dave's _second_ question, "what should [he] read next?"

Dave, if you want to continue with the main line of the Lupiverse, you should definitely move on to the Books of the Long Sun and the Short Sun, in that order.

tBotLS is set in the same universe, but in terms of plot, is related only tangentially to the adventures of Severian.

tBotSS is a direct sequel to tBotLS, and also relates more directly to tBotNS in terms of plot than does its predecessor.

(At least one short story, "The Map," deals with a character from New Sun. At least one, "The Night Chough," deals with a character from Long/Short. I believe -- though I haven't tracked all of them down -- that the other short stories set in the Lupiverse are simply that -- "set in the same universe.")

***HOWEVER***, this assumes that "what should I read next" is a simple question of continuing in the same universe. Wolfe has written a number of other novels ranging from "superb" to "Huh? What was _that_?" (and one which he himself considers an utter stinkeroo and will not allow to be reprinted, OPERATION ARES, though I rather like it). Which descriptions fit which of these novels is a matter of some debate.

If you want to branch out of the Briah Cycle, I would highly recommend either the short story collection, "THE ISLAND OF DOCTOR DEATH AND OTHER STORIES" AND OTHER STORIES, or the novels FREE LIVE FREE and PEACE. Others will have other
ideas on the subject.

--Blattid

2002-07-16 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that the 97 thousand year figure for the dying sun in The Book of the New Sun is implausible, because ruins of ancient civilizations would need millions of years to be uplifted into new mountains. Responds to David A Baird's quoted remark that he had imagined a much later date, and cites the carved mountain figures of Urth's rulers as the evidence.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: The sun is far older than 97 thousand years

The 97 Kyear figure is _very_ debateable, and I for one hold it to be completely implausible ... we're talking a sufficient amount of time so that the ruins of ancient human civilizations have been uplifted in the bodies of new mountains (which, of course, are then carved into the figures of Urth's vain rulers). That doesn't happen in less than multiple millions of years.

--Blattid

Street" is probably the easiest approach.
