In the Sewer

Urth list · 1 messages · 2002-12-03 to 2002-12-03

2002urthJames Jordan

Themes: Power, rulers and politics

2002-12-03 James Jordan #

Proposes that the sewer-cleaning task in Horn's story is a test of humility, linking it to Jesus's saying that rulers must be slaves of all and to Horn's later political rise. Offers a Herculean allusion to the Augean Stables as a possible source, asking others to supply details, and compares the cleaning to David's lion and bear before Goliath.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · source-and-allusion · theories: Sewer cleaning tests Horn for rulership

1. Seems Herculean, and given Wolfe's interest in and knowledge of Greek myth, such might be at least one allusion. But I'm not up on legends about Hercules/Herakles, so I'll toss this ball to anyone who wants to bat at it. Is there anything specific about the Augean Stables Cleansing that would fit in here?
2. Is this a test of sorts? Jesus said, "Those who would be great among you must be slaves of all." Horn (Horn Plus) is on his way to becoming a ruler, in different ways, in three cultures. He's "rewarded" with a sword, right? Cleaning a toilet seems not only Herculean, but also a very humble work. "If you're willing to do this, then you are ready to rule." "Only those who are willing to do this kind of thing are fit for rule." Is that part of it?
2b. Is cleaning out this sewer analogous to Horn Plus's later political activities? (I love the line in *Last Action Hero* that goes something like "There's something rotten in the state of Denmark, and Hamlet's here to take out the trash!") David killed a lion and a bear to prepare him to kill Goliath. Horn Plus cleans out a sewer to prepare him for political office.
Well, them's my thoughts. Maybe someone has some others.

Nutria
