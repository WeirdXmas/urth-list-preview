1999-09-28 Kuhlmann, Kurt #

Argues that the word "strike" on the lead shot Dorcas vomits up in The Shadow of the Torturer is ordinary sling ammunition marking, not a judgment on Dorcas. Responds to Carlton Greene's ostracism comparison by offering a bomb-inscription analogy and noting the shot was also used to weight corpses.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Strike inscription is ordinary sling ammunition marking

I took it as simply the same thing as painting "Dear Adolf" on a bomb during WWII -- the lead shot is inscribed with the word "strike" to help send it on its way and make it a more effective missile.

In other words, the lead shot wasn't specifically intended for Dorcas. It was simply sling-stone ammunition that was also handy for weighting corpses before throwing them in the lake.

1999-09-28 Greene, Carlton #

Proposes that the word "strike" stamped on the lead shot Dorcas vomits up in The Shadow of the Torturer reflects the Greek ostracism ritual, where symbols on pottery voted for banishment. Suggests this judgment explains Dorcas's dreams of shopping while people look at her with hatred, as though she were unclean and had no right to be among them.
proposes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Strike on the lead shot is an ostracism judgment against Dorcas

Has anyone discussed the message "strike" imprinted on the lead shot that Dorcas vomits up in III? It reminded me of the greek ritual of ostracism, where people mark symbols on a piece of pottery to indicate their vote for or against banishment. Is it possible that the word represents a judgment of some sort? If so, that might explain the dreams Dorcas reports in which she is shopping and realizes that everyone looks upon her with hate -- I think she even reports that the looks of the people express the idea that she has no right to be there among them, and is something unclean.
