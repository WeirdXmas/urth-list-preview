Themes: Beasts and the animal in us

2012-01-10 Lee Berman #

Disputes Gerry Quinn's claim that the Urth-era megatherian and the undines lack an animal motif, arguing that a sentient beast can have a biography and that the megatherian's size and characters like the dog men, cat women and man apes point to beastly figures. Responds to Quinn's point that similes do not make a motif, insisting the discussion should concern beings in the story, and floats manatees and Tzadkiel's beastly traits as further examples.

It isn't too hard to figure out which notable character is really large and

named for an earth-inspired animal.

Heh. Funny. I know there is more than one definition for the word "character" but

cat women and man apes. And Tzadkiel, who obviously has a beastly component or two.

Heh. I find that a rather fishy conclusion. ;-)

Or perhaps we should consider the Order Sirenia (manatees)?

2012-01-10 Gerry Quinn replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that the title "Lives of the Seventeen Megatherians" is wrong for a biography of Abaia, Erebus and the others, since they are never called Megatherians. Replies to Lee Berman by noting that megatheres are at large in the grounds of the House Absolute, so the animal name is misleading.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Lives of the Seventeen Megatherians is an incorrect title

From: Lee Berman

?Lives of the Seventeen Megatherians? is simply *wrong* as a possible name for a biography of Abaia, Erebus, and the rest, for a multitude of reasons. They are never referred to as ?the Megatherians? either. And we also know that there are even animals called megatheres at large in the grounds of the House absolute.

- Gerry Quinn
