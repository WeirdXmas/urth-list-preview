Themes: Unreliable narration

2001-04-15 Alice Turner #

Quotes William Ansley distinguishing a deliberately unreliable narrator from an unreliable author, and argues that John Crowley's Daemonomania shows the author himself unreliable in third-person narration. Responds to a quoted lecture claiming Chaucer was the first unreliable narrator, and separates this deliberate unreliability from the carelessness the poster found in Return to the Whorl.
proposes · genre-and-craft · theories: Unreliability in an author differs from unreliability in a narrator

Quoth William Ansley:

It is important to distinguish between the unreliable narrator, a creature of the author, and the unreliable author. In Chaucer's case, the lecturer may well be correct. (It's been quite a while since I've read!) But the fascinating thing, for the few of us who have been following John Crowley's Aegypt series, is that the *author*, especially in -Daemonomania-, appears to be unreliable, in a *third-person* narration. Unreliable does not mean careless--I have been complaining about carelessness in RttW and I don't want the two
confused--unreliability in a narrator or an author is deliberate. But different.

-alga
