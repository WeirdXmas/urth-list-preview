Short Story 3 title correction: The Grave Secret

Urth list · 10 messages · 2012-03-24 to 2012-05-02

2012urthDavid StockhoffMarc AraminiFred KiescheJeff WilsonDan'l Danehy-OakesDaniel Petersen
The thread: A short thread began as a logistical correction: Marc Aramini and Fred Kiesche both gave the corrected title of Wolfe's third short story as "The Grave Secret" and listed "The Dead Man" as story four, asking requesters to get in touch. David Stockhoff then gave a dismissive verdict, calling the story unserious, clumsy, and silly, and questioned how its ghost performs magic. Later, Stockhoff proposed that Clark Ashton Smith's "The Return of the Sorcerer" may have been the source of the title, quoting an Arabic Necronomicon passage about a sorcerer's will reanimating his body that the protagonist undergoes. Aramini agreed, adding a playful note about Lovecraft's necromantic fiction. The thread ended in agreement on the source idea, with no close textual argument.

  • David Stockhoff: suggested Clark Ashton Smith's The Return of the Sorcerer as the title's source

2012-05-01 David Stockhoff replying to David Stockhoff #

Proposes that Clark Ashton Smith's story The Return of the Sorcerer may have been the origin of Wolfe's The Grave Secret. Quotes a passage from the Arabic Necronomicon in that story about a dead sorcerer's will reanimating his body, and notes that the protagonist of The Grave Secret undergoes exactly this.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: The Grave Secret originates from Clark Ashton Smith's The Return of the Sorcerer

A Cthulhu Mythos story by Clark Ashton Smith, THE RETURN OF THE SORCERER, may have been the origin of this one. Working as a translator for a John Carnby, the narrator reads from the original Arabic Necronomicon:

'It is verily known by few, but is nevertheless no attestable fact, that the will of a dead sorcerer hath power upon his own body and can raise it up from the tomb and perform therewith whatever action was unfulfilled in life. And such resurrections are invariably for the doing of malevolent deeds and for the detriment of other's. Most readily can the corpse be animated if all its members have remained intact; and yet there are cases in which the excelling will of the wizard hath reared up from death the sundered pieces of a body hewn in many fragments, and hath caused them to serve his end, either separately or in a temporary reunion. But in every instance, after the action hath been completed, the body lapseth into its former state.'

This is of course exactly what happens to the protagonist of The Grave Secret.
