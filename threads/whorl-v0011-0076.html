IGJ: altar scene

Whorl list · 5 messages · 2000-08-09 to 2000-08-24

2000whorlThe Book of the Long SunAdam StephanidesUrash, TomJames JordanPeter Westlake
The thread: Adam Stephanides asked whether the plural 'them' in Horn's altar-scene sentence in In Green's Jungles, describing the Outsider's manifestation, is a typo and what it means if not. Tom Urash had suggested the plural refers to the Outsider as a Trinity. Adam disputed this, arguing Horn never shows belief in a multiple Outsider and that an unexplained singular-to-plural shift would be odd even if both pronouns meant the Outsider. Jim Jordan replied that the Outsider arrives with a retinue of angels and dead saints, so a Trinitarian hint cannot be excluded, and pointed to a similar one-and-many pattern in Nightside's opening. The thread ends without resolution, with the reading left open.
  • Adam Stephanides: 'them' is probably not a typo but an unexplained shift, and the Trinity reading is unsupported by Horn's beliefs
  • Tom Urash: the plural 'them' refers to the Outsider as a triple god, the Trinity
  • Jim Jordan: a Trinitarian hint cannot be excluded given the Outsider's retinue of angels and dead saints; the singular-plural shift matches Nightside's opening

Themes: Catholic faith and sacraments, Gods, angels and the Increate, Names and their meanings

2000-08-09 Adam Stephanides #

Asks whether the word "them" in Horn's sentence about the Outsider's manifestation in the altar scene of In Green's Jungles is a typo, and what it means if it is not. Quotes the line "I knew that he was there, that if I turned, I would see them" (285) as the basis for the question.
questions · close-textual-reading

I was reviewing the altar scene, and I just noticed the following sentence, where Horn is narrating the manifestation of the Outsider: "I knew that he was there, that if I turned, I would see them." (285)

Is that "them" a typo? If not, what does it mean?

--Adam

2000-08-10 Adam Stephanides #

Disputes Tom Urash's suggestion that the plural 'them' in Horn's altar-scene narration refers to the Outsider as a triple god, the Trinity. Argues that Horn never elsewhere shows belief in the Outsider's multiple nature, and that the unexplained singular-to-plural shift within one sentence would be odd even if both pronouns meant the Outsider.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: 'Them' in the altar scene refers to the Outsider as Trinity

Perhaps, but I don't recall anywhere else where Horn indicates a belief in the Outsider's multiple nature. And even if he did, the unexplained shift from singular to plural in the same sentence would still be very odd if both referred to the Outsider.

--Adam

2000-08-10 James Jordan #

Argues that the Outsider arrives with a retinue of angels and dead saints, and that a Trinitarian hint cannot be excluded. Responds to Adam's objection about Horn's singular-to-plural shift by pointing to the same one-and-many pattern in the first paragraph of Nightside.
disputes · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: The Outsider comes with angels and dead saints as retinue; A Trinitarian hint lies behind the Outsider passages

The same one and many is seen in the first paragraph of Nightside. I'd say that the Outsider comes with His retinue, which is the angels and the dead saints. But a hint of the trinity cannot be excluded.

Pat. Nut.
