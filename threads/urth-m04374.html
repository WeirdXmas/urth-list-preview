The Tomb of the Unknown Severian

Urth list · 28 messages · 2007-11-29 to 2008-01-09

The thread: Readers debated which Severian the tomb in Citadel XXXVIII was built for and whether the first Severian (Sev1) failed or passed the Yesod test. Tony Ellis proposed an unmet Severian and later argued Sev1 likely passed, while Joel Sieh and Roy C. Lackey held that Sev1 failed and that time-walking and drowning paradoxes made the timeline incoherent. Roy Lackey and others pressed on who saved Sev1 from drowning, with gwern0 arguing an outside rescuer like Inire and Jeff Wilson proposing the Green Man as a derailer. Later posts drifted to e-book availability, the Claw as symbol versus power (Matthew and Pedro Pereira), Doctor Who comparisons, and the Autarch selection process, ending without agreement.
  • Tony Ellis: tomb belongs to an unmet Severian; later argues Sev1 likely passed Yesod and time-walks
  • Roy C. Lackey: Sev1 failed Yesod, diverged after drowning, and time-walking creates paradox
  • Joel Sieh: original Severian failed, went back in time, and altered events so Sev2 succeeded
  • gwern0: outside rescuer like Inire must have saved Sev1, since the loop needs it
  • Jeff Wilson: Green Man lurked in the Gyoll to derail Severian; later argues Autarch selection was planned
  • Pedro Pereira: Claw is only a symbol or old thorn; Severian's power came from within

Themes: Time loops and causality, Doubles, twins and clones, Unreliable narration

2007-12-18 Joel Sieh replying to Tony Ellis #

Proposes that the tomb in Citadel XXXVIII could have been built by an original Severian who failed the test in Urth of the New Sun, went back in time, and altered events so he later succeeded. 2007-12-18 Roy C. Lackey replying to Tony Ellis #

Asks who saved the first Severian from drowning as a boy, since the narrator says Juturna saved his life and that the first Severian also did not drown. Builds the question on the timeline in The Book of the New Sun, citing the throne room talk with Juturna in Book V and the remarks about Sev1 near the end of Book IV.
questions · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: Juturna saved the first Severian from drowning

I don't care about the mausoleum stuff, but the Juturna part intrigues me. The problem with the above is that it was Sev2 (our narrator Severian) who spoke with Juturna in the throne room near the end of Book V about the boyhood near-drowning incident. Near the end of Book IV is where Sev2 spoke of Sev1 (the "first Severian"). There, Sev2 said that Sev1 hadn't drowned either, because someone was already messing around in his (their) life by that point. So, when Sev2 spoke to Juturna about her having saved his life when he was a boy (though she had not yet saved him in her own backward personal timeline, but never mind), he was referring to the incident at the beginning of Book I when he shot out of the river propelled by her hand, obviously. Yet, if Sev1 didn't drown either -- and he obviously didn't, since he went on to become autarch -- who saved *him*? You can't say that time-walking Sev1 saved himself as a boy -- he wouldn't have been alive to become a time walker. Care to speculate?

-Roy

2007-12-20 Roy C. Lackey replying to Tony Ellis #

Argues that the Green Man likely did not take part in the Antechamber episode, and that Sev1 may never have met him, since Sev1's life diverged from Sev2's after the near drowning. Grounds this in differences the poster lists: Sev1 lacked the Claw, so the tent-cathedral burning and Pelerine flight differ, and Dorcas, Jonas's intrusion and the man-apes episode are absent.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Green Man did not appear in Antechamber; Sev1 may never have met Green Man

I don't think that the Green Man was involved in the Antechamber episode, but in any event it is far from certain that Sev1 ever even met the Green Man. Though the general outline of the life of Sev1 was the same as Sev2's between the time of the near drowning and becoming autarch, the details varied considerably. Sev1 didn't have the Claw, so everything associated with it, including the burning of the tent-cathedral and the Pelerines' flight from the city, didn't happen the same way for him. No Dorcas, no excuse for Jonas to intrude himself on the theater troupe, no blue-light special for the man-apes, etc. And the Green Man came from a sunny future -- Ushas -- that Sev1 did not bring.

Any other candidates?

-Roy

2007-12-20 Jeff Wilson replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Proposes that the Green Man was lurking in the Gyoll to derail Severian rather than save him, because an agent of the New Sun may have engineered Sev1's drowning to restore a different timeline. Builds the case on Juturna's motives and the Green Man's reaction to the whetstone, which he reads as sensing the New Sun's arrival.
proposes · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Green Man lurked to derail Severian; Pro-New Sun agent drowned Sev1; Juturna saves Sev2 to restore timeline; Green Man senses New Sun through whetstone

Given that Sev1 succeeded in becoming Autarch but failed in Yesod, a pro-New Sun agent may have arranged for a change in history to drown him, clearing the way for another candidate to become Autarch and win Yesod. Then Juturna has a justifiable reason to save Sev2, hoping to restore the timeline and serve Abaia by preventing the New Sun.

This would mean that the Green Man may have been lurking about, hoping to kill or otherwise derail Sev2, rather than save him. His greeness is pond scum after all, making him likewise adapted to lurking camoflaged under yucky Gyoll water. This also explains his exceptional change of mood at being given a whetstone by Sev2; touching his hand, he is able to feel that presence extending through time into Ushas, affirming the arrival of the New Sun.

2007-12-20 Roy C. Lackey replying to Tony Ellis #

Argues that neither Severian drowned in either timeline, so Juturna's promise to save Sev2 would not stop the New Sun, and that the Green Man is a far-future being. Responds to Tony Ellis by citing the flooding timing of Nessus and the House Absolute and the Green Man's rescue of Sev2 in the northern jungle.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: Neither Severian drowned in either timeline; Green Man is a far-future child of the New Sun

But neither version of Severian drowned in either historical timeline. At the time Juturna promised to save Sev2 in his past, Nessus had been underwater for two days and the House Absolute was minutes away from inundation. The white fountain was at most hours away from rendezvous with the Red Sun and it was unstoppable. Sev2 was near the peak of his god-like powers when she spoke. She knew for a fact that Sev2 had succeeded in bringing the New Sun. Saving Sev2's life when he was a boy would not be the way to stop him.

The Green Man helped to rescue Sev2 from the Ascians in the northern jungle. He didn't want Sev2 dead. He came from the far future where mankind had evolved to make use of sunlight more directly than using the medium of the usual food chain. He owed his very existence to the New Sun. As he told Severian in chapter XXX of CITADEL, speaking of his kind, "We are your children . . ."

-Roy

Argues that Severian exists as a single self whose time-walking produces two divergent Urths, with Sev1's judged, fountain-empowered life reshaping the world Sev2 inhabits. Supports this with the claim that Sev1 would never have rescued Triskele, found Dorcas or acquired the Claw, and with the meeting of men from two futures as evidence the future is unfixed.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · puzzle-solving · theories: Sev1 wanders time as Apu-Punchau and the Conciliator; Sev2 lives in Sev1-altered divergent Urth; Sev2 judgment as fail-safe against Sev1

I think that in one sense, there is only one Severian and this is his way of describing the paradoxes involved in passing into his own past.

On the other hand, it is an apt metaphor for the two divergent histories of Urth which are set up, where everyone experiences the alterations made by Sev1, thus everyone lives alternative existences.

If I may be excused for quoting a popular British Science Fiction program, "it is a big ball of wibbly-wobbly timey-wimey," in the sense that neither the future nor the past are set in stone.

When considering the world of Sev1, in his journey to the Phoenix Throne and eventual judgment, it would have been a world where the Conciliator never existed and he himself would have possessed no special abilities. If we remove both of these from his history (and other instances of Sev1 in the past), that should give us an idea as to his adventures. It should be obvious that he would never have rescued Triskele, found Dorcas, or acquired the Claw. On the other hand, short of giving up Terminus Est and fleeing, I do not see how he would have survived his duel with Agillus... To make a long story short, when he was eventually judged favorably, and acquires access to the power of the white fountain for the entire term of his life including retroactively. He then goes on to wander the corridors of time to be various important people such as Apu-Punchau, the Conciliator and, no doubt others who have not been named.

Sev2, whose story we are familiar with, thus lives in the world created to some degree by Sev1 including the power to occasionally access the power of the white fountain. Though, clearly the future is still not written and he can fail when his time comes to be judged. (This is evident by his meeting men from two disparate futures.)

As to why does Sev2 need to be judged when Sev1 was successful, perhaps it is to determine whether the initial judgment was correct, or as a fail-safe against Sev1 abusing such power as he was given.

Todd
