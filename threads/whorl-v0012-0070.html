IGJ: altar scene *Spoiler*

Whorl list · 3 messages · 2000-12-29 to 2000-12-29

2000whorlThe Book of the New SunJames JordanE DorianPeter Westlake
The thread: Readers asked who the plural 'them' in the altar scene refers to, and whether Severian's shifting pronouns mean he is more than one person. E Dorian proposed that Severian is the Outsider, citing the page 356 passage about Valeria, Thecla and the old Autarch. Jim Jordan argued Severian is an agent of the Outsider rather than the Outsider himself, tying his plural 'we' to his role as Conciliator and to the Eucharist. Peter Westlake disputed the identification, saying 'them' refers to the Outsider and the baletiger, and that Severian speaks of the Increate and Pancreator as a man would of a deity. The thread ends with three competing readings and no agreement.
  • E Dorian: Severian is the Outsider, shown by his shifting pronouns and the page 356 passage on Valeria, Thecla and the old Autarch
  • Jim Jordan: Severian is an agent of the Outsider, his 'we' comes from being Conciliator, and Thecla and earlier Autarchs live inside him via the Eucharist; bale
  • Peter Westlake: 'them' means the Outsider and the baletiger, and Severian's talk of the Increate and Pancreator treats the Outsider as a deity

Themes: Unreliable narration, Catholic faith and sacraments, Gods, angels and the Increate

2000-12-29 E Dorian #

Proposes that Severian is the Outsider, arguing that the shift between 'I', 'we', 'he' and 'them' in The Urth of the New Sun and In Green's Jungles shows Severian is more than one person. Responds to Adam Stephanides' question about the word 'them' in the altar scene, citing the page 356 passage where Severian recalls Valeria, Thecla and the old Autarch.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Severian is the Outsider

Hi Everybody,

So, recently I decided to read all of the Sun books in order, timing it so that when I finished IGJ, RTTW would come out and I would jump to that. Well, I am behind; I am about to finish Urth of the New Sun. But I may have discovered something interesting. I was on page 356 of Urth:

"I felt as real as I ever have; and when I searched among my memories, I found Valeria there still, and Thecla and old Autarch, and the boy Severian(who had been Severian only). "No," I said. "We will live."

Adam's quote from IGJ: "I knew that he was there, that if I turned, I would see them."

Severian stopped being "one" person, so to speak. He uses "I" throughout his entire narrative, but sprinkles the "we's" around on occasion.

Severian uses "I" and "we", the IGJ passage uses "he" and "them", so, have I unurthed evidence that Severian is The Outsider?

Your thoughts?

2000-12-29 James Jordan #

Argues that Severian is not the Outsider or the Increate but an agent of it, and that his plural "we" comes from his role as Conciliator who revives those he eats. Grounds this in the Eucharist, where bread unites believers to Christ and to the whole Church, so Thecla and the earlier Autarchs live inside him; also proposes linking Horn's baletiger to Blake's tyger as a Christ symbol.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · theology-and-religion · theories: Severian is an agent of the Outsider, not the Outsider; Eating the Autarchs revives them inside Severian; Horn's baletiger is Blake's tyger, a Christ symbol

Also:

No. Severian is not the Outsider, but an agent thereof. When Severian is "we" it is because, being the Conciliator, he has the gift/curse of actually bringing back to life, in some sense, anyone he takes into himself. That's a symbol of the way the sacrament works in the Church, uniting believers not only to Christ as an individual, but also to all the other people in the Church (the bread represents both Jesus and all of his "body"). Thus, when Severian eats Thecla, he not only gets her memories for a brief time, like everyone else, but she actually comes to life inside of him, and is with him from then on. In the same way, he eats all the preceding Autarchs, and they are all alive inside him. But he is NOT the Outsider/Increate/Pancreator.
Additionally, I suggest that the baletiger that helps Horn in his encounter with the Outsider should be linked to Blake's tyger, who like the lamb he is compared with, is a symbol of Christ.

Nutria

2000-12-29 Peter Westlake #

Argues that the "them" in the altar scene refers to the Outsider and the baletiger that helps Horn, not to the figures the earlier poster suggested. Grounds this in Severian's talk of the Increate and the Pancreator, which he uses as a man would speak of a deity, and identifies the Outsider as that deity.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theology-and-religion · theories: The Outsider is the deity Severian calls the Increate or Pancreator

I see what you mean, but I think "them" refers to the Outsider and the rather large baletiger that helps Horn. Severian talks a lot about "the Increate" and "the Pancreator" in very much the terms of a man talking about a deity, and that's who the Outsider is, I'm sure.
