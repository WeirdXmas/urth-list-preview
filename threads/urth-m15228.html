is Terminus Est really just a simple piece of hardware?

2010-05-17 Cliff Judge #

Proposes that Terminus Est may be an artifact of far higher technology than Severian admits, pointing to Dorcas's account of the sword's inner channel of liquid metal. Grounds the idea in the Mataquin Tower's lostech devices and plans to transcribe Dorcas's description of cutting the avern during the duel.
proposes · close-textual-reading · genre-and-craft · theories: Terminus Est is an artifact of advanced technology

I plan on keeping my eye on descriptions of Terminus Est throughout the New Sun books, but something about how Dorcas described it when she was recounting to Severian what happened when he went down during the duel was interesting.

I am wondering if the sword might be an artifact of much higher technology than Severian gives it credit for. Certainly it must be cleaned, oiled, and sharpened at various times in the book, but the description of a channel of liquid metal inside the blade seems to ring with the general "lostech" descriptions of many of the devices in the Mataquin Tower. When I get my hands on the book I will transcribe the passage where Dorcase describes using it to cut the avern that was about to bite Severian, it sounded like more was going on then simply a transfer of weight inside the blade.

2010-05-17 Jeff Wilson replying to Cliff Judge #

Argues that Terminus Est is more than simple hardware, reasoning that its mercury-filled channel and threaded stopper imply machining beyond medieval means. Responds to Cliff Judge's suggestion of higher technology by pointing to Dorcas's description of the blade in the duel aftermath and to the general survival of old technology on Nessus, such as mechanical limbs.
supports · close-textual-reading · genre-and-craft · theories: Terminus Est is an artifact of higher technology

The preparation of the blade's metal and the mercury is within the means of a medieval society, but drilling the channel down the finished center and fitting it with a threaded stopper would require something like a drill press, which would not be particularly unexpected in a place like Nessus, where enough old technology remains useful that mechanical limbs are not particularly remarkable.
