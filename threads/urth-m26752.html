The thread: Marc Aramini drove a long run of speculative readings of A Borrowed Man, linking the Owenbright sandwich scene, temperature shifts, the blue-bound equation book, and colour symbolism to a hidden plot about Arabella and Colette. Robert Pirkola pushed back with textual checks, notably reading hot and cold as sorting characters into camps and collecting the red references with page numbers. Ab de Vos raised further speculative links (Point Blank, saints' names, water imagery) and questions about the world's sun and time. Aramini kept revising his claims, such as Mars via March rather than Juno, and his readings grew more tangled, while the thread ended without agreement and with the main claims left open.
  • Marc Aramini: colour, temperature and bird imagery encode a hidden plot about Arabella and cloning, with Chick and Cob financing the clones
  • Robert Pirkola: hot and cold imagery sorts characters into camps, with Summer and Spring Peters placed in different cities
  • Ab de Vos: water rather than fire-and-ice is the book's central motif, and the world's time and sun are unclear
  • Ern's burning: Pirkola reads it as humanity's fear of living rather than of dying

Themes: Colonization and the native, Names and their meanings, Mirrors, pictures and art

Proposes that the Owenbright library scene in A Borrowed Man, where Chick Bantz offers Ern sandwiches, shows Cob financing the Arabella clones, with Chick a form of the mother or of Cob. Grounds this in bird imagery (cob, swan, canary, chick), the sandwich Ern gives away, Arabella's pencil-grasping stare echoed in Colette, and Ern's Chatterton mistake.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Cob financed the Arabella clones; Chick Bantz is a form of the mother or Cob

Well, gonna have to add this realization in there somewhere. Sometimes when I glance through these things afterwards a particular image clicks into place. When ern first sees Arabella at the owenbright library, she complains that he brought her chocolates that ?greased the skid to Hell?. Chick Bantz, the androgynous thug in blue, then comes and offers ern three hundred creds, hot chocolate, and three sandwiches, trying to get him to leave with him. When ern gives the third sandwich away, he sees Arabella staring into space and unconsciously grasping a pencil. Ern told chick he had a card for a different library that would not unlock the owenbright doors, though they would let him out of Spice Grove. Ern will meet three Arabellas and will just leave one with Colette, whom he finds staring off into space just as Arabella was in this scene. I don?t know why sandwiches should serve as symbolic stand in for the Arabellas but they seem to. The library card he mentions as a means of egress, but not for every door, might also be related to the method he leaves the jungle planet door the first time: a key he already has which only works to exit the right side of the door. Chick?s short stature and blue clothes might link him to the deceased mother, but perhaps this set piece actually metonymically illustrates who was responsible for buying the Arabella ?sandwiches.? We should remember cob (a swan - chick offers ern a swig of swan-n-sweetheart here) is a bird as his car Canary - chick, too might be in line with this. The Arabella clones may have been financed by Cob, with his murder on mars (mom) forgery. Whether chick IS a form of the mom or of Cob, this scene illustrates who put things in motion through the connection of bird (and food) imagery - cob-swan-canary-chick.

That's a key scene because it is also where Ern makes his Chatterton mistake, the young forger who poisoned himself, thinking that instead the boy choked on a biscuit. Tied to the presence of Chick ...

2018-10-02 Marc Aramini replying to Ab de Vos #

Argues that the colours red, blue, yellow and black in A Borrowed Man follow a seasonal and personal pattern, with the yellow car canary and the black car cat marking the pre-Ern drama, and that Blue may stand for Mars. Grounds this in the yellow car, Chick's blue and later black overcoat in July, Ern's women's clothing, and images of water, a grave on Mars and a choking snake.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Blue is Mars; Chick is a metonymy for the pre-Ern drama; Colours follow a seasonal and personal game

Here, paradise is water and the desert with its sands of eternity impede progress, like arabella?s Poem of feet dragging across the desert with boots that can never be free from the sands of time .

Yellow - the car canary is yellow. It seats six and ern and Arabella take it to taos towers at the end. While chick wears blue at first, later he wears a black overcoat in JULY (summer) when ern gets the drop on him in women?s clothing. The car Cat is black. So Wolfe is playing a seasonal game, a color game, and maybe a ?person? game here. Should chick be at all counted in the yellow group for a time because of his (or her) name? I still think he is a metonymy rather than an actual player in the pre-ern drama which took place - the colors and the symbols tell us what happened before ern showed up since he can?t know.

Now ... wrestling the man headed and ape headed being of many arms while a grave on mars filled it up with water might be more than a clue that Blue is Mars. The red planet ... death .... water fills it up. The red planet is choked by a snake on the cover. Cob is choked by someone with strong hands.
