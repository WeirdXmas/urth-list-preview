Themes: Christ figures and messiahs

1997-07-09 Alice Turner #

Argues that the story "Hues" is heavy-handed in its antichrist motif, citing its title, shadow imagery and explicit hell references, and disagrees with Ron on the 230011 and Marylin=Mary readings. Responds to Ron by noting Baldanders and Dr. Talos, suggesting Ossipago leads the three cacogens, calling Polyaris a simple Polaris take, and remarking that a name Kyle recurs in another Endangered Species story.
disputes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion · theories: Hues carries an antichrist motif; Marylin is not Mary; Ossipago leads the three cacogens

Ron, I'm sort of amazed that you don't see the antichrist motif for "Hues"---to me it seems laid on with a trowel, from the title through all the "shadow" imagery through the explicit references to hell. I agree with you about 230011, but not about Marylin=Mary; to me that's Wolfe being not nearly as subtle as usual. It *is* a pulpy story!

Re "I am the master": let's not forget Baldanders and Dr. Talos. Also, there's some indication that Ossipago is the leader of the three wise cacogens.

I would vote for Polyaris simply being a cute take on Polaris, and suggest that we are trying too hard to make more of the name.

On Kyle: Wolfe used Kyle as a name in another of the stories collected in Endangered Species. I'm not at home, so can't check which. But I did note that the stories were written years apart, so it might just be coincidental.

-alga-
