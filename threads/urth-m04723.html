Got a spare grand-and-half?

Urth list · 7 messages · 2008-12-01 to 2008-12-03

2008urthThe Book of the New SunGwern BranwenStuart HammMatthew KeeleySon of Witz
The thread: Gwern Branwen shared news of a $1500 Centipede Press limited edition of The Shadow of the Torturer with Alexander Preuss illustrations, and the thread turned into a dispute over whether Preuss's plates match Wolfe's text. Son of Witz argued most plates do not conform closely to the book and singled out the floating city as the worst, which Matthew Keeley echoed with examples such as the too-futuristic city in Leaving Nessus. Gwern mocked Preuss's claim of fan-assisted research and quoted the artist's own defence, while the joking about the price and the fuligin being left out kept the tone light. The exchange ended without resolution, with the critics and Gwern still divided over how faithful the art is.
  • Gwern Branwen: Preuss's illustrations are worth seeing and his fan-research claims are laughable
  • Son of Witz: most Preuss plates don't match the text, especially the floating Pelerine city
  • Matthew Keeley: the art misses Urth, the cathedral city looks wrong, and Severian is shown poorly

Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Buildings, cities and labyrinths, Humour in Wolfe

2008-12-01 Matthew Keeley replying to Son of Witz #

Matthew Keeley replies to Son of Witz that the illustrations in Preuss's Book of the New Sun art do not match his picture of Urth, singling out the floating city he reads as the Pelerine cathedral. He backs this with specific images: Leaving Nessus is passable but its city looks too futuristic, and the artist avoids showing Severian in a good light, which sidesteps the question of his chest hair.
disputes · close-textual-reading

Well, I think that at various times we've all missed the New Sun boat in one way or another. I don't think any of the illustrations conform to my view of Urth, though "Leaving Nessus" isn't too bad (though the city is too futuristic by far). I think the biggest failure is the "floating city", which I take it is Preuss' illustration of the Pelerine's "cathedral".

Like you said, I could nitpick for hours, but won't.

By not showing much of Severian, and never showing him in decent light, Preuss did at least solve the vexed problem of Sev's chest hair...

-Matt
