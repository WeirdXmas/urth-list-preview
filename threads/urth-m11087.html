The thread: A thread titled 'GUTs and so on' began with Dan'l Danehy-Oakes complaining that some list members chase elaborate, implausible theories about a lupine matter in the Short Sun books, prizing difficulty over plausibility. Craig Brewer argued hidden puzzles matter only when solving them changes the thematic reading, while James Wynn defended his theory that the Neighbors are at least some trees, citing the Rajan's 'sleepy' trees and Hy being Fava. Stuart Hamm and Ryan Dunn floated their own speculations, and David Stockhoff joked about confusion rather than arguing. Lee Berman proposed that Green's shapeshifters began as trees and vines that imitated advanced creatures, possibly arriving from space, and Gerry Quinn later restated and extended that reading. The thread drifted from critique of theorizing into a sprawling set of competing speculations with no resolution.
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: some members prize baroque, implausible theories over plausibility
  • Craig Brewer: hidden puzzles matter only if solving them changes the thematic reading
  • James Wynn: the Neighbors are at least some trees; Hy is Fava
  • Lee Berman: Green's shapeshifters began as trees and vines that imitated advanced beings, perhaps from space
  • Gerry Quinn: Shadow Children are degenerate human discoverers; natives took human form before colonists
  • Ryan Dunn: Dorcas is Severian's grandmother, and the list is a place to float undecipherable ideas

Themes: Time loops and causality, Evolution and the future of humanity, Colonization and the native

2010-08-24 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that some list members compete to build the most elaborate and implausible theories about a Lupine matter, valuing difficulty over plausibility, with praise as the only prize. Frames this as a recurring pattern rather than a complaint about any one poster, and names no specific theory or person.
disputes

Sometimes ... and I want to be clear that I'm not singling anyone out here ... nor is this a recent but rather a recurring thing ... I get the feeling that there's a competition going on, whose rules I am unaware of, but the objective of which seems to be the erection and successful support of the most baroque and prima facie implausible theory concerning some Lupine matter; the prize being simply the gasps and awed admiration of our fellow Wolfeans, and that this competition is rather like a circus act, in that the value is not in the thing done but in its difficulty.

2010-08-24 Craig Brewer replying to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that Wolfe's hidden puzzles matter only when solving them changes the thematic reading, and that the theory-heavy approach should reflect on why its details matter. Responds to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes by praising Borski's occasional reflection and dismissing minor genealogy guesses, then asks how identifying Fava as Hy would change Silk's prophet status.
proposes · puzzle-solving · allegory-and-typology · theories: Fava is Hy

I think (almost) all of us have been guilty of that at some point, myself included. (I know I've got some weird ideas about TWK and Sorcerer's House.)

But one thing that I always want to know is how the details or the baroque theories do more than point out some hidden plot point. As much as I disagree with MOST of Borski's stuff, say, one thing I appreciate about him is that he will occasionally take a step back and reflect on why his theories matter and how they might change our general understanding and even thematic interpretations. One thing I like about Wolfe is that the puzzles that I'm pretty sure are there and are there to be solved are usually there for a pretty interesting reason, rather than just being puzzles to be solved. That Severian may have died and come back (either resurrected or as an eidolon) multiple times in BotNS, say, has all kinds of philosophical and theological ramifications for the story. Guessing that some minor character may be his second cousin once removed by hidden marriage on his step mother's side, however...not so much.

In the most recent case, I'd want to know not just IF Fava is Hy, but I'd also want to know how that changes what we're supposed to think about Silk/Silkhorn in terms of his prophet-status, etc.

2010-08-24 James Wynn replying to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Responds to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes by defending his own theory about a lupine matter in Wolfe's Short Sun books and rejecting the charge of baroque, implausible reasoning. Says he will first gather the textual references, including a clue about Hy in In Green's Jungles, before laying out the whole argument at Dave Tallman's request.
disputes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading

I am feeling a little singled out.

Look, it's not really a baroque or complicated explanation. It's not intuitive, but it's founded on one pretty well-known event in text. And lots of little clues that many people have agreed were clues without an answer to unify them into anything. It doesn't require supposing vast histories that are undescribed or unalluded to. I'm surprised large parts of it was even a big mystery to anyone who has read to In Green's Jungles, even though it was a couple years after I first read "Short Sun" that the the bit about Hy occurred to me. But I can be a little slow.

The reason I'm not just laying it out on the fly is that I do know in advance that some people will _hate_ it -- on purely philosophical grounds, regardless of what the text says. And I want to make it clear that I'm not singling anyone out here. ;-)

So, I am at least going to get the textual references down before present it as a whole--although I mentioned parts before. Dave Tallman asked me to explain it and that's why I'm explaining it. If I'm performing a circus act, I'm performing on request. Wait. Then throw eggs.

J

2010-08-24 Lee Berman replying to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Proposes that the shapeshifting beings of Green, including Neighbors, Inhumi and Shadow Children, began as simple trees and vines that evolved by imitating more advanced creatures, perhaps arriving on Green from space. Responds to James Wynn's claim that the Neighbors are trees and that the Rajan's vine staff is an inhumi, offering metaphorical and scientific grounds such as parasitism and the Shadow Children's landing on St. Croix.
proposes · close-textual-reading · myth-and-structure · theories: Neighbors and Inhumi evolved from simple trees and vines; Shadow Children gained humanity from a crash landing

Sleepy trees? Hm, sounds a bit like a nod to Ents.

Anyway, I don't find this theory overly fantastic at all. To me it makes sense both metaphorically and scientifically. Trees are multi-limbed creatures. Vines are parasitic to them.

I think we are given evidence that all these lupine shape-shifting creatures, Shadow Children, Inhumi and Hierogrammates had their origins in very simple, almost formless organisms. They gained their eventual shape and intelligence by successive imitation of other, progressively more advanced creatures. Perhaps Neigbhors and Inhumi started out as simple trees and vines.

I think the Shadow Children of St. Croix got their first taste of humanity and intelligence and shape with a single crash landing. This allowed them to turn into abos. Later, with colonization, they just hit the jackpot with regard to people to imitate.

Perhaps something similar happened on Green during Urth's First Empire exploring out in space. What impact would the presence of human beings have arriving on a planet of shapeshifting trees and parasitic vines? (keeping in mind that humans parasitize each other).

Not sure how Blue might fit into that. Inhumi can't fly through space. But could tree seeds or vine spores make the trip, somehow? Black beans?

2010-09-04 Gerry Quinn replying to Lee Berman #

Restates Lee Berman's reading that the Shadow Children of St. Croix are degenerate descendants of the original human discoverers, while the hillmen and marshmen are native aborigines who took human form before the French colonists arrived. Builds on the crash-landing and imitation idea by adding that the natives merged with colonists and replaced some, such as Marsch.
supports · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Shadow Children are degenerate original human discoverers; Hillmen and marshmen are aborigines in human form; Natives merged with and replaced some colonists

From: "Lee Berman" <[email]>

As I read it, the Shadow Children are the degenerate remnants of the original human discoverers of St. Croix. The hillmen and marshmen are the St. Croix aborigines who took on the discoverers' original form. They were already of human appearance when the psychic shield surrounding the twin planets was removed and the French colonists came.

To some degree they merged with the incoming colonist population and in select cases such as Marsch they replaced individuals.

- Gerry Quinn

St. Croix aborigines who took on the discoverers' original form.  They were 
already of human appearance when the psychic shield surrounding the twin 
planets was removed and the French colonists came.

To some degree they merged with the incoming colonist population and in 
select cases such as Marsch they replaced individuals.

- Gerry Quinn
