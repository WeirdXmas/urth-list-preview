2001-02-05 George Terry #

Argues that Horn's death and return as Silk in The Book of the Long Sun is a Christ-like resurrection and incarnation, citing Horn's self-sacrifice on Green and the offering of bread and wine to the Outsider at the Calde's palace. Responds to a trinity reading of Silk, Horn and Oreb by suggesting Horn merely incarnates Silk and that any trinity must include the Outsider.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · theology-and-religion · theories: Horn's death and return is Christ-like resurrection; Horn incarnates Silk; Any trinity must include the Outsider

Long time list observer, first time poster...

One of the things that struck me in RTW along this vein is the idea that Horn's death/re-incarnation/resurrection is very Christ-like. He dies on Green pretty selflessly, and is re-incarnated/resurrected as Silk. This is especially seen in the scene with Olivine at the Calde's palace where Horn/Silk makes the offering of his body and blood in the form of bread and wine to the Outsider.

Perhaps this movement of Horn->Silk mirrors the essence of the spiritual journey for Gene Wolfe? I don't think that Horn is stupid...he knew Silk, and even says at one point in RTW that he realizes he bears a physical resemblance to Silk since returning to the Whorl. I think it represents a basic humility in Horn. Horn had previously immersed himself in Silk, certainly viewing him as an model of what is 'right' and 'good'. In his journey, learning to love, learning the inhumi secret, he becomes more and more like Silk, until at the moment of his death, dying for a mission of compassion for New Viron, he actually becomes Silk. Would this not be the goal that Wolfe would seek in his own life?

Ultimately I think that Horn merely 'incarnates' Silk, in much the way that Jesus incarnated the divine logos. Any theory that looks at a 'trinity' must include the Outsider, as well.

George Terry

2001-02-06 Jeffrey Meyers #

Proposes that Juganu gained his leadership skills by secretly drinking Horn's blood during the boat trip, mixing Horn's leadership with Hoof's impetuous youth. Builds on Joel Priddy's remark that Horn's death was arranged by Juganu's recruiting rather than by the inhumi, and reads the blood-drinking as the explanation.
proposes · close-textual-reading · allegory-and-typology · theories: Juganu drank Horn's blood for leadership

For him to have had such highly-developed leadership skills Juganu must have sneaked a little more of Horn/Silk's blood than we might have guessed. As humble and self-effacing as Horn/Silk was, it would make sense that he would not make a big deal of Juganu's drinking a bit a night or two, especially while they were stuck for three days on the boat. (After all, Horn/Silk was planning his own suicide. What was the harm?) He can't be faulted for not adequately thinking through the implications of the mixture of his blood (leadership) with Hoof's (impetuous youth), which Juganu was surely tasting now and then.

Patera Bunny
