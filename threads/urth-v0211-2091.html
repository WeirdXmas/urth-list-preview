2002-11-13 James Wynn #

Relays a suggestion that the Green Man in Wolfe's New Sun may not be one figure with the peyote and Islamic green men, then has Crush argue that al-Khidr is probably the main source of the Green Man. Crush supports this with Robert Graves's The White Goddess, which describes him as a former sun-hero who drank from a pool of youth and is linked to sea travelers.
supports · source-and-allusion · theories: The Green Man derives mainly from al-Khidr

Josh Geller notes:
There's a green guy who is important in Islam also.
And there's the one that's associated with peyote.
Maybe they're not all the same green guy. It's interesting to contemplate what the World would have to be like for them all to be the same one

Crush responds:
Actually the green fellow in Islam ("El Hidr" or "al-Khidr") is probably the primary derivation of the NS's Green Man. Robert Graves in "The White Goddess" identifies him as a "former Sun-hero". Apparently he drank from a pool of youth in Hyperborea and gained immortality. He encounters wandering holy men to impart divine knowledge. He is also a patron of sea travelers so perhaps, like Apollo (another Sun divinity) and all the Moon divinities (according to Graves), one his early aspects was a sea-god.

But alloying of conceptual cognates is very Wolfean.

-- Crush

2002-11-13 Andy Robertson #

Disputes a proposed link between the Green Man, a pagan god who became a northern fay, and a character in The Book of the New Sun. Argues the character is never said to be chained or imprisoned, so the parallel fails, and admits he had never made the connection.
disputes · source-and-allusion · theories: Green Man parallels a chained TBOTNS character

Interesting. I had never made this connection, I confess.

The Green man (or Green Knight) is a god figure that later became an elf/fay/sidhe in Northern legend. I am not at all sure that I see any linkage with the character in TBOTNS: for example, he is never spoken of as chained or imprisoned.

hartshorn
