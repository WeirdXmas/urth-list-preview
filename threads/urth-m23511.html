2012-11-26 Larry Miller #

Asks whether the play in Talos's Short Sun context corresponds to the Short Sun story, proposing that Silk returned to Urth and inspired the original book, and that the Inhuma Jahlee inspired the character Jahi. Prompted by a comparison of the names Jahi and Jahlee from an earlier thread, it raises whether anyone in the play could be Silk. The post offers no textual evidence beyond the name parallel and is a set of speculative questions.
questions · puzzle-solving · source-and-allusion · theories: Silk returned to Urth and inspired the play; Jahlee inspired the character Jahi in the play

Just reading the the Claw thread I took note of Lees comparison of the name Jahi and Jahlee and wondered if there were any correspondences between the Short Sun story and Talos' play. Could Silk have returned to Urth after the Whorls departure and somehow inspired the original Book with stories of his past? Could the Inhuma Jahlee be the inspiration for Jahi in the play? Is there anyone in the play that could be Silk?

2012-11-27 Lee Berman replying to Larry Miller #

Argues that the Short Sun's Jahlee and Silkhorn's astral visits to Urth link to the Talos play, with Meschia, Meschiane and Jahi as Adam, Eve and Lilith, bearing on Severian's sister. Grounds this in the Eden, serpent and vampire references, Green and Blue tied to Urth, and Wolfe's note to Marc that Green is Urth, while suggesting Wolfe intends an intuitive rather than concrete understanding. Responds to Larry Miller's questions about Jahi, Jahlee and Silk.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · source-and-allusion · theories: Meschia, Meschiane and Jahi as Adam, Eve and Lilith; Jahlee's astral visits to Urth link to Talos play; Wolfe intends intuitive rather than concrete understanding

Well, Silkhorn does astral travel to Urth with Jahlee, where she takes real human form and basically spends all her time copulating. Aside from making the connection of busty redhead Jahlee to Jahi and Jolenta, I'm not sure if there are further connections to Dr. Talos' play established with the astral visits. I do tend to think the question of Severian's sister is answered though.

In the play, Meschia, Meschiane and Jahi are persian correspondants to Adam, Eve and Lilith. There are jealousy and vampiric and serpent references that swirl around the Biblical characters as well as the BotNS characters.

By bringing Jahlee to Urth, I think he does reinforce the Eden references already established for Green and the Inhumi. By associating Green and Blue to Urth and Ushas via the connection to Dr. Talos' play, Wolfe is telling us something about the Short Sun story and how it relates to human origins and forests and vampires and snakes and flooding. Then there is Wolfe's cryptic note to Marc that "GREEN is Urth!"

Perhaps we are not meant to get a concrete, nuts and bolts understanding of what Wolfe is trying to get at. He may be deliberately attempting to install an intuitive, "right at the tip of my tongue" understanding which defies being placed within the constraint of clearly understood wording.
