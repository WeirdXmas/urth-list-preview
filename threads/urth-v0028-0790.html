Guesses that alga likes "The Ziggurat" because it moves from realism into dreamlike inner depth, while "The Woman Who Loved the Centaur Pholus" and "Castleview" stay on the surface. Responds to alga's remarks on Wolfe's stories by projecting alga's reactions onto the hospital Dr. Death tale and Castleview's Arthur, closing with a Fellini traffic jam aside.
proposes · theories: alga prefers The Ziggurat for its inner depth

Okey-dokey. (THE DEVIL IN THE FOREST as realistic and contemporary? <g>) So I would posit that you like "The Ziggurat" better than most of the contemporary lot because you sense that it does deviate from "realistic" into some form of fantastic (surreal, dream, etc.); that it leaves behind the outer world of surfaces to dive deeply into the/an inner world. (Not to spur on more talk of the Zig, just to see how it crosses your internal boundary from a badland into the verge of likeable.)

"The Woman Who Loved the Centaur Pholus" probably leaves you cold because it is stuck in the outer world of surfaces, making a Greek myth concrete in contemporary terms. (In part based upon the guess that you don't like "the hospital one" of the Dr. Death tales because it has a technological source for the magic.) Likewise I'd guess that you feel CASTLEVIEW plays fast with Arthur on its surface, yet lacks true depth (or even the illusion of depth) on either Arthur or anything else; all (dizzy) surface, no (inner) depth.

Re: traffic jams in foreign cinema, I thought there was a Fellini film that started this way.
