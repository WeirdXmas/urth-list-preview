Suzzane Delange

Themes: Memory and forgetting, Hidden identities and parentage

1998-05-08 Craig Christensen #

Proposes that the narrator of Suzanne Delange once met Suzanne and that the young woman he sees, unable to be photographed, is his own daughter by her. Grounds this in the story's opening remark that everyone has a forgotten, overwhelming event, and in the phrase about being unable to be photographed, offered as a reply to Peter's earlier reading.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: The young woman is the narrator's daughter; Narrator met Suzanne and forgot her

This is my first post. I have been working my way through the archives for several weeks, and I am finally caught up.

Peter,

I read Suzanne Delange again last night and when nothing unmistakable jumped out at me I reread it with the story's opening statement in mind. That is, the narrator was struck by the passage he had read which stated that every person has probably had at least one event so momentous and so inassimilable (somebody's making good use of his thesarus, huh?) that he has wiped it from his memory. I took that to mean that the narrator had not only met Suzanne in his past, but that she was very important to him. I read the "Unable to be photographed" to mean that Suzanne was pregnant then and that the young lady he saw was his own daughter.

The vampire angle never occurred to me. Neither have many other aspects of Mr. Wolfe's writings revealed to me in the urth.list archives.

Craig Christensen
