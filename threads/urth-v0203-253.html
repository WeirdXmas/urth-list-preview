2002-03-01 Seth Lombardi #

Proposes a possible link between Frank Black's song "The Swimmer" on Dog in Sand and The Book of the New Sun, based on the word "pelagic" and the Undine image in its lyrics. Asks whether the song is a love song to an Undine, arguing that a sci-fi musician would not pick pelagic at random.
proposes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Frank Black's The Swimmer is a love song to an Undine

Here's a possible GW pop music connection:
(on the subject of music from a few posts back)
Anyone listen to Frank Black? Former lead singer of the Pixies? This guy has a strong science fiction flavor: some of his songs paint melancholy post-apocalyptic or extraterrestrial landscapes with great skill. He also has an obsession with Ray Bradbury, even going as far as to name an album after him (The Cult of Ray).
Anyway, I got his newest, Dog in Sand a few months back. I was dumbstruck by one song that, well. . . it reminds me a LOT of the BOTNS. Mainly because it has the word "pelagic" in it. It's hard to believe a sci-fi freak pop music star could pick that word randomly. Anyway, here are the lyrics. I had to find them on the web as I do not have the album of the liner notes with me, and it does not designate the chorus or which parts are repeated. I highly recommend you get this album or anything else by him.

You know, now that I think about it...tell me this isn't a love song to an Undine!! :)

2002-03-01 William H. Ansley #

Argues that Frank Black's use of the word "pelagic" in his song is not random but a correct use meaning open-sea or mid-ocean, so it need not derive from Wolfe. Responds to Seth Lombardi by quoting Merriam-Webster's definition and noting the song's lyrics share little resonance with The Book of the New Sun.
disputes · source-and-allusion · theories: Frank Black's pelagic is not borrowed from Wolfe

[...]

[...]

Frank Black certainly may be a Gene Wolfe fan, but even if he has never read Wolfe, his use of "pelagic" could hardly be considered random. The song you quote (I have omitted the lyrics from my quote of you message, above) refers to the mid-ocean. To quote Merriam-Webster's Collegiate Dictionary, pelagic means:

"of, relating to, or living or occurring in the open sea : OCEANIC"

So Frank Black was merely using an uncommon word appropriately. Since Gene Wolfe has practically made a career out of this, Black may have been imitating him, but I find it far from certain, especially since the rest of the song doesn't seem, to me at least, to have much resonance with TBotNS.

William Ansley
