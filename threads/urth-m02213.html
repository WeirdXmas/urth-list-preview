The thread: Members discussed which Gene Wolfe stories depict families, with the thread opening on whether Wolfe's families look normal but are cracked. Jonathan and Rachel argued that families in Pandora by Holly Hollander and Innocents Aboard are fractured or destroyed beneath a traditional surface. Jordan Dennis listed short stories that explicitly depict families and offered PDF copies, while Dan'l Danehy-Oakes asked for the listed stories and whether any remained uncollected. 2006-06-07 Jonathan Laidlow #

Argues that Wolfe's stories show families that look traditional but are cracked, citing the Holly Hollander family in Pandora by Holly Hollander and the investigation with Aladdin. Adds The Tree Is My Hat and the Friendship Light in Innocents Aboard as further examples of fractured or destroyed families, offered as a reply to the thread on normal families in Wolfe.
proposes · close-textual-reading

Well I've been away from the list and Wolfe for a
while, but my recent reading can tell you this:

Pandora by Holly Hollander. The family is set up as
traditional (Holly and her parents) but the cracks are
revealed through Holly and Aladdin's investigation.

The Tree is my Hat and the Friendship Light in
Innocents Aboard also show fractured/destroyed
families.

2006-06-08 Jordan Dennis replying to Jonathan and Rachel #

Lists Wolfe short stories that explicitly depict families, including divorced mothers, a boy whose sister has died, a travelling-salesman father, and a princess with many siblings. Offers PDF copies of several stories to list members who want to read them.
informs

My readings have been by no means exhaustive, but I can point out a few stories that explicitly mention families.

"Hunter Lake" (Science Fiction and Fantasy, Oct/Nov 2003) features a divorced mother and her daughter. I have this in .pdf if anyone wants it.

"The Peppermill Man" (Fantasy & Science Fiction, Oct/Nov 1996) is about a boy who just had his sister die. His mother is divorced.

"Houston, 1943" features an intact family, but the father is a travelling salesman.

In "Under Hill," Princess Apple Blossom has 526 sisters and 610 brothers. I really don't know if this could be counted, though, because she is a princess from the Chi'in dynasty.

By the way, I have "Bea and her Bird Mother," "The Little Stranger," and "The Gunner's Mate" saved as .pdfs as well, if anyone would like to read them if they have not done so already.
