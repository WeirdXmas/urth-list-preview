2000-06-12 Jim Russell #

Proposes that the single starship in The Urth of the New Sun has a non-linear internal history, so Jonas may have served on it and his crash-landing could be the end of Tzadkiel's ship. Builds the argument from Jonas stepping into Father Inire's mirrors and promising to return when he is sane and whole, reading that as a return to his own past aboard the ship.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: Jonas served on Tzadkiel's ship; Jonas's crash-landing ends Tzadkiel's ship; Jonas returns to his past aboard the ship

Ok, Im not sure if this has been dealt with or not, but
referring back to the Jonas, Hethor, Tzadkiel stuff...

At several points in UotNS characters note that there is only one ship travelling between the stars, but because it ignores standard temporaral physics it seems like many. So the ship has a linear, internal history which does not obey the laws of normal time (just like Sev). Bearing in mind the high possibility that Jonas had served on this ship (at some point in history), can we assume that the crashlanding he talks about is the end of Tzadkiel's ship?

When he then steps into Father Inire's mirrors, claiming that he will return when he is "Sane and whole" isn't he talking about returning to his own past aboard the ship
(The asumption being that he will emerge between the sails which pick up the apports that plague the ship).

Obviously this poses some (whopping) problems with regards to the Jonas/Miles situation, but I'd appreciate hearing anyone else's thoughts on this.

Feel free to shout me down at will.
Jim Russell
University of Exeter
