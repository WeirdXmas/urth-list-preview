arguing JC heroic duties

2000urthThe Book of the Long SunMichael Andre-Driussi

Themes: Hidden identities and parentage

2000-06-29 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Questions whether Reynard can be read as a heroic figure, calling the Judas-like reading grim and comparing him to Machiavelli, and asks what duty he performs beyond reproduction. Responds to David Duffy's remark about Rush's choice at the end, and endorses Duffy's suggestion that Rush's name means book while adding the Rush/Rushmore link and a parallel with Silk as the nearest JC-style hero.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Rush's name means papyrus that speaks, i.e. book

Eek, Reynard as the hero? Even more horrifying than anything I saw. <g> (Not really, I mean I saw that: just that it seems like the grimmest reading. Judas, yes; a necessary-for-the-book Judas, certainly; but Judas as hero? In truth "Judas" doesn't capture the Machievellian aspect of Reynard at all: Machievelli as hero?) Please remind be what duty Reynard is doing, besides his own reproduction: I can't see the manipulative masks for the masks of manipulation.

Right, well which end of his narrative? The human end (hi, let me make this brain tape of you--it won't hurt at all) or the recorded end (did I really know what I was agreeing to when I said yes)? [Where is my copy of ES, anyway?]

Yes, definitely his name means book. (I think it would be "Rush That Speaks" = papyrus scroll = book). There is also a weird link to Mount Rushmore, with Rush's head in the Teddy Rooseveldt spot; but this is probably just another artefact of my overactive reading.

And yes, thanks for mentioning Silk, because that is what I was going to write in my previous post: that Silk is the closest thing to a JC-style hero that Wolfe has written. Or so it has seemed to me.

They probably just take an i-v drip in between chapters <g>. (Shades of Delany's NOVA, come to think of it.) But no, I myself wouldn't try to argue the rationale on this one.

=mantis=
