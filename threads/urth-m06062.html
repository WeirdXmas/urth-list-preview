Sev the Murderer

Urth list · 4 messages · 2008-06-05 to 2008-06-05

2008urthThe Book of the New SunthalassocratDave TallmanJohn Smith
The thread: The thread asks whether Severian is a murderer whose early killings define him or a flawed hero. Thalassocrat argues he is a murderer: the killing of an innocent volunteer to protect Vodalus sets up his later choice to side with the eidolons against the sailors defending Urth, and later argues the killing of Vodalus was unnecessary and that Severian may be a charm against Christ. Dave Tallman disputes this, conceding Severian's immorality but rejecting the claim that the series inverts morality and defending his side because the sun was damaged by a black hole. John Smith offers a middle reading: Severian kills the volunteer from romantic illusions about Vodalus as a Robin Hood figure, which he only later outgrows. The thread ends with the sides still apart.
  • Dave Tallman: Severian is a flawed hero, not a villain, and his side is justified by the damaged sun
  • John Smith: the early killing reflects Severian's romantic illusions about Vodalus, which he later loses
  • thalassocrat: Severian is a murderer whose early killing sets up his later choice of the eidolons
  • thalassocrat: Severian's killing of Vodalus was unnecessary and criminal, and the Hierarchs' goals are unexamined

Themes: Suffering and sacrifice, Power, rulers and politics, Christ figures and messiahs

2008-06-05 Dave Tallman replying to thalassocrat #

Argues that Severian is a flawed hero rather than a villain despite his killing of the second volunteer and his role in Yesod's sham trial. Responds to thalassocrat by conceding Severian's immorality, rejecting the claim that the series inverts morality, and defending the side Severian joins on grounds that the sun was damaged by a black hole.
disputes · myth-and-structure · theories: Severian was on the right side despite foolish motives

Sev was raised to kill without pity, so this is not surprising. Probably if he had not helped Voldalus that night he would have been mistaken for a grave robber and killed himself, but I agree about the immorality of what he did.

The real defenders? Now you are arguing for a complete moral inversion of the entire series, and I can't agree. The Sun wasn't dying of natural causes; someone put a black hole into it and shortened the life of the entire biosphere by millions of years. Was it worth catastrophic global warming to put the Sun back to normal? The choice of futures was the world of the Green Man or the world of Ragnorak. I believe Sev was on the right side, however foolish his motives. He was a flawed hero, not a villain.

2008-06-05 John Smith replying to thalassocrat #

Proposes that Severian kills the second volunteer in BOTNS because he romantically sees Vodalus as a Robin Hood-style outlaw fighting an evil king. Responds to thalassocrat's objection by arguing Sev only later learns Vodalus's real aims, so the killing reflects his early illusions.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian sees Vodalus as a Robin Hood outlaw

Vodalus is like Robin Hood. He lives in the woods and
battles the evil king. At least this is what
Severian believes at the time. He has a romantic view
of the outlaw-rebel, which explains why he kills a man
to protect Vodalus.

Of course, Vodalus is not really fighting for freedom
and justice. But Sev only learns later what Vodalus
really stands for.

2008-06-05 thalassocrat #

Argues that Severian is a murderer whose early killing of an innocent volunteer in The Book of the New Sun, done to protect Vodalus, sets up his later choice to side with the eidolons against the sailors defending Urth. Supports this by citing the struggle scene, Severian's admiration for Vodalus and attraction to Thea, and the Urth of the New Sun battle, with Gunnie's contrasting view offered as a counterpoint.
proposes · close-textual-reading · allegory-and-typology · theories: Severian's trial tests his willingness to sacrifice others for causes he does not understand

One of the first things Sev does in BOTNS is kill an innocent man who is fighting to prevent the violation of his dead, who presents no danger to him and with whom he has no quarrel.

"The second volunteer rasied his ax, then hesitated ... he circled to get a clear stroke ... The ax rose; I grasped the helve just below the head almost by reflex, and found myself at once in the struggle, kicking, then striking. Quite suddenly it was over. The volunteer whose bloodied weapon I held was dead."

The context makes it clear that the blood is the "second volunteer's", and that Sev has killed him. Only later does Sev come out and say explicitly that he did so. Perhaps somewhere he expresses some kind of remorse or shows some empathy, but I don't recall it.

Sev does this to protect Vodalus, of whom he knows practically nothing except that he is a grave robber, apparently just because he likes Vodalus' style: " .. it was that willingness [to die] that kindled my admiration for him." And because he is attracted to Thea.

The culminating point of Sev's sham "trial" in Yesod is when he joins battle on the side of the eidolons against the sailors fighting to prevent the destruction of Urth. (UOTN XXI) The eidolons are weak at first, then appear to gain strength as he focuses on them, and when he joins the battle, they triumph at once.

There is a resonance here; once again he takes sides against the real defenders in a cause he knows almost nothing about. He has no idea who the Hierogrammates and Hierarchs really are, or what their agenda is, or how it is to be accomplished. He had meant to question Apheta the night before, but was distracted by sex. At the crucial point, he chooses figments and fantasies over the lives of real people.

This, I believe, was the point of his "trial" - willingness to countenance the death of his fellows in a cause which he does not understand, and which he makes no real effort to understand. But right at the start of his story he demonstrates the characteristics which led to his selection for the role.

(Part of Gunnie's role in the story is I think to give us a viewpoint not infected with Sev's woolly mysticsm. She, like the other sailors, doesn't seem particularly impressed with the Hiero- dudes; should she be, after experiencing Tzadkiel's apparent inability to maintain good order even on the Ship?

At any rate, she calls Apheta a whore. (UOTNS XXIII)

Apheta: "On Urth, women who look as I do, do no work at all - or so I have heard."
Gunnie: "Some do.")

2008-06-05 thalassocrat replying to thalassocrat #

Argues that Severian's killing of Vodalus was unnecessary and criminal, and that the Hierarchs' goals are unexamined and may not be good, suggesting Severian himself may be a charm against Christ. Responds to a thread where thalassocrat defended Severian's act, citing Severian's training and the Urth of the New Sun Apheta conversation and the mutilated cock charm.
disputes · allegory-and-typology · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian is a mutilated charm against Christ; The Hierarchs' goals are not necessarily good

On Thu, 05 Jun 2008 22:03:17 +1000 Dave Tallman

I don't think Sev was in any danger. The volunteers had seen him before, at the gate. If he had to intervene, it could have been on their side, and he could no doubt have killed Vodalus, given the position.

His training was in judicial punishment and execution, not private murder. What he did was certainly criminal, even in this benighted society.

I've argued at length previously for just that inversion. Was it worth killing almost everybody on Urth to restore the Sun, just one star among many with human-populated worlds? If it was so necessary to restore it, could not humanity have been evacuated, as it was in Master Ash's timeline? What exactly were the Hiero-dudes seeking to accomplish? We really don't know, beyond some vague puffy story about repellant cycles of pain and evolution. Why assume that the Hieo-dudes and their plans are *good* things?

Anyway, the ovrwhelming majority of humans present in Yesod were in no doubt of what they thought about it. But their vote didn't count. They had been tricked - told they were to be spectators, dragooned into being matyrs.

Just on the topic of what exactly the Hiero-dudes goals are ... because Severian never really questions and examines them, we don't really know. To become like the Hieros? But how, and anyway, where are those Hieros?

I take as a hint this comment from Sev to Apheta in UOTNS XX: "You Hierarchs are magicians. You're more powerful than those I once met, but magicians still."

Before he meets those magicians, he finds a mutilated cock on the path, a charm against the coming of the New Sun. Perhaps Sev himself is a mutilated charm against the coming of Christ.
