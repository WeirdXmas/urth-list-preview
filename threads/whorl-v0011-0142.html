Limp biz (cut)

Whorl list · 4 messages · 2000-08-21 to 2000-08-23

2000whorlThe Book of the Long SunJohn CluteAlice TurnerAlastair ReynoldsMark Millman
The thread: Readers discussed why lame figures recur in Wolfe's fiction and myth, focusing on whether the limp motif is a single Claudius-type trait or a wider pattern, and whether Oreb and Horn fit it. Akt supported Patera Rat by listing classical and Long Sun lame figures and predicted Horn will become Odin. John Clute asked for further thoughts on Oreb's identity and on why heroes are so often lame, noting Nutria's Christian typology reading. Mark Millman added Odysseus's healed thigh wound and Vidar's single shoe as possible Norse parallels. The thread ended without resolution, with the posts mostly adding examples rather than settling the question.
  • akt: The limp is a widespread motif across myth and Long Sun, not only Claudius's, and Horn will become Odin
  • John Clute: Asks for more on Oreb's identity and why heroes are lame; notes Nutria's Christian typology reading as possibly central
  • mark millman: Odin may have no limp, but Vidar's single shoe and Odysseus's healed thigh wound offer possible parallels

Themes: Myth and folklore sources, Suffering and sacrifice, Christ figures and messiahs

2000-08-21 Alice Turner #

Lists lame figures from classical myth, the Fisher King and Wolfe's Long Sun, including Oedipus, Jason, Telephus, Sev, Silk, Oreb and Horn, arguing the limp motif is widespread. Supports Patera Rat's point that the limp is not only Claudius's, citing Jason's single sandal and Achilles' heel, and predicts Horn will become Odin.
supports · myth-and-structure · source-and-allusion · theories: The limp is a prophetic mark across myth and Wolfe's characters; Horn will become Odin

Patera Rat put forth (on another subject altogether):

That limp doesn't go just with Claudius. That limp is important to classical mythology in general. There's Oedipus--"swollen-foot"--of the dire prophecy. There's Melampus--"blackfoot," also a prophet. There's Jason, who arrives wearing just one sandal, as was prophesied. There's some other odd stuff about sandals, including the sandals of Theseus (under the stone) and the drag-footed lame Crane Dance and the prophetic fall that followed. Telephus was wounded in the left leg by Achilles, and we all know of Achilles and his heel. I cannot remember if Odin was eventually lame, but I wouldn't be surprised, since Horn is certainly fixing to be Odin in his accoutrements. There's the Fisher King, wounded in the thigh.

And there's Sev. And Silk. And Oreb (wing). Eventually (I'll bet; he's already got a staff), Horn. And for the Crowleyites among us, there's Pierce walking up Mount Randa with one black shoe and one brown shoe, first seen as a pilgrim in a shamanistic trance by Beau Brachman. Tres, tres Jason, IMO.

On another subject, Ian, mantis isn't putting you off--we did get pretty intense about Suzanne. If you read the earlier stuff and still have questions, please ask them.

-alga

Asks for further thoughts on Oreb's identity and on why so many heroes in fiction are lame, referencing Tim Powers and a body of theory on the subject. Notes Nutria's Christian typology reading of Oreb as one pattern of meaning, possibly the central one, and apologizes for reviving an old thread.

2000-08-23 Mark Millman #

Adds to the discussion of limping figures in myth by noting Odysseus's old, healed thigh wound and searching for any lameness or leg wound in Odin. Reports that Vidar's single giant shoe in the Ragnarok fight might explain a limp, and tests this against the classical examples raised earlier.
supports · source-and-allusion · myth-and-structure

Odysseus, too, has a thigh wound, though it's old and healed and apparently doesn't cause him to limp. It does, however, serve to identify him to members of his household upon his return.

I've been unable to find any reference to Odin's becoming lame or receiving a wound in his leg or foot. I did, however, come across one interesting foot fact about Odin. During Ragnarok, Odin is swallowed by Fenrir and dies. His son Vidar avenges him by kicking Fenrir in the mouth with a giant shoe until the wolf's jaws are torn asunder--just one giant shoe, not one of a pair. I suppose that could cause a limp.

Nacre
