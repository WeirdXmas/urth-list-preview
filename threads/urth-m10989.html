António Marques argued Valeria may be Severian's missing twin via Valeria Severa, and later that she is a recurring, possibly alternate figure; David Duffy proposed Dune and Empire Star as sources, reading Valeria as an Irulan analogue. The thread ended in impasse: a quarrel between David Stockhoff and António Marques about the tone of replies spilled onto the list, displacing the Wolfe content.

2010-08-18 Lee Berman #

Proposes that the upper levels of the Last House and Valeria's tower exist in the future, linked by an Inire-built portal called the Path of Air that lets a woman be taken back in time. Grounds this in Severian's Path of Air scene, the invisibility of the structures from the present, and the unchanged face of a woman from his youth, and links her to the dark-haired woman in the opening grave.
proposes · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Upper Last House and Valeria's tower exist in future; Path of Air is an Inire-built portal; Woman on Path of Air is Catherine; Valeria is Severian's missing twin sister

Ah, now I understand your reluctance to invoke time travel. That kind is a huge undertaking. But how much effort did Severian spend to travel to the future to see Ragnarok? A flight or two of stairs. He actually WAS in the future, not just observing it, since the upper levels of the Last House, are in the future, making them invisible from the present.

Valeria's tower, off the Atrium of Time, is also invisible from the present, suggesting its upper levels exist in the future. About 60 years in Severian's future, if I am correct. Valeria lives in the lower levels of her tower, thus in the past, hence her antique appearance.

To understand the Path of Air I think we not only have to connect it to the Last House but to other Commonwealth structures, specifically Ultan's library and Rudesind's art gallery. Both are said to extend from the Citadel all the way to House Absolute. A while back someone suggested that these structures truly physically extend for 100 miles or whatever in underground tunnels. I find it more plausible that Father Inire-built portals are involved.

I think bogus suppositions of sleigh rides, etc. are meant to lead us to wonder how khaibits are able to travel back and forth between House Absolute and House Azure in Nessus. Inire-portals would fit the bill nicely and it makes sense that the Autarch would have access to them.

I am suggesting that there is a similar portal which connects House Absolute to the top of Valeria's tower. It is called the Path of Air because one end is underground but the other is, well, in the air providing a breeze. Hence it is a loggia or breezeway as Jeff has suggested. (a loggia is provided breeze from the side rather than its terminus but, hey, no theory is perfect).

I find this the best explanation for why Severian sees an unchanged face from his youth appear 60 years in the future. It allows for a reason for why she seems to be under arrest and being taken back to the past. Whatever crime Catherine committed it was surely in the past from the Path of Air scene.

Since the woman on the Path of Air has dark hair and a pale gown I connect her to the woman in the grave at the beginning of the story. If that is right and Dave Tallman is right she was brought back in time to give birth to Severian and then died 20 years later.

I'm not sure. Nor am I sure which of these various dark haired women is the orginal (Contessa?) Catherine and which is a khaibit. I tend to think the woman on the Path of Air was not pregnant with Severian (and Severa) but had already given birth and was trying to escape her deferred execution by escaping to the future.

I'm not sure how the monials fit in, but if her manner of execution was Allowin's necklace I must guess her crime was infidelity, mirroring the strangulation sentence for that other dark haired, oval faced, failed Pelerine, Cyriaca.

The connection of this Catherine personage to Valeria's time travelling tower might be Valeria herself. I think Valeria remains a possible candidate as Severian's missing twin sister. The wife of Roman emperor valentinianus was named Valeria Severa.

2010-08-18 Jeff Wilson replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that the Last House and Valeria's tower are better understood as monitoring structures than as causal tools, since Severian never changes history in the Corridors of Time. Responds to Lee Berman's claim that Severian travels into the future via the Last House and that Valeria's tower is invisible from the present, offering instead that the Hierogrammates and sea-beasts direct changes and that the tower's invisibility may reflect curved tunnels rather than time-bending.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Valeria's tower is entirely in the past; Hierogrammates direct historical changes; Tunnels curve through space to hide the tower

This sort of transcendental architecture may be more suitable for research and monitoring than meddling with causality, especially regarding Master Ash's fade from existence and the subsequent detachment of the lower stories from the altered timeline. The Corridors of Time likewise seem unsuitable on their own, as Severian never changes history despite randomly blundering into new eras repeatedly, he only ever confirms what was already suspected. It seems to be the Hierogrammates and the sea-beasts that have the superhuman perception of time required to judge which changes can be made and how best to make them, and to direct their minions accordingly.

I'm in agreement on the existence of physical corridors that bridge time and space like the Corridors of Time, but I see no evidence that Valeria's tower itself is a time-bender. Since it's immediately adjacent to the Atrium of Time and the tunnels passing the dial-filled rooms, you get the same effect if the tower is entirely in the past, or if the tunnels curve through space so that the tower seems to be in one place rather than another.

It's also the sort of thing you'd find on a certain university campus, but that's a theory for another time.

supports · source-and-allusion · puzzle-solving · theories: Valeria is Severian's missing twin sister

2010-08-18 Jeff Wilson replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that the Atrium of Time is a secluded area beside the Corridors of Time rather than a place of time travel, and that Valeria's tower can resemble other towers because the time tunnels let it sit in an unusual spot. Responds to Lee Berman's claim that Master Ash's presence and the Last House comparison support a time-travel reading, rejecting the idea that the Last House's upper stories imply vertical time travel.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Atrium of Time is a secluded area beside the Corridors; Valeria's tower is like other towers

Where? You mean Master Ash is really from the past instead of the ice-age future?

An atrium is an enclosed or partially enclosed area adjacent to some larger structure or feature, usually open to the sky though glassed-over and skylit atria are popular these last few centuries. Thus, the Atrium of Time can be this secluded area adjacent to the Corridors of Time, associated with their function but not part of it, accessible through time but outside of it. Mantis speculates that it and the dial-filled rooms nearby might be part of some abandoned time patrol headquarters.

Her tower can be just like all the other towers, only the place it is resting is unusual. There are other towers shown on the map not in evidence, and towers visible that are not on the map. The time tunnels allow her tower to be both.

That's "speculating on", not "noticing." The Last House has to have its upper stories in the future of its lower stories, because the ice accumulates over the ages. If time travel had a strictly vertical, up-to-the-future-down-to-the-past character, the Green Man would seen climbing the Stairs of Time, or the Chutes and Ladders of Time, and Tzadkerbell would flutter alongside the Cataract or the Geyser Madrigot.

proposes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion · theories: Woman on the Path of Air resembles Holy Katharine maid; Valeria linked to Roman empress Valeria Severa

They are still about the same age when they married I think. I am not interested in parsing out exact timelines but I think those who do estimate Severian's journey from graveyard to Autarchy lasted somewhat under two years. Or was it somewhat over... I forget.

2010-08-19 Jeff Wilson replying to António Pedro Marques #

Argues that Severian's pull toward Valeria after he becomes Autarch rests on nostalgia for their youthful meeting, their near-equal standing, and his own desire-through-distance. Grounds this in Severian's remarks on desiring women he cannot have and on love being bound up with lust, and in the apparent age and status parity between them.
proposes · close-textual-reading

There are several reasons: he still feels a fondness for her, having met her under unusual circumstances on his final juvenile adventure. She is a connection to a simpler time, his last hurrah without adult responsibility.

They met as near-peers, both obscure servants of the Autarchy. Valeria's technically an armigette of a landed family while Severian has no pedigree at all, but his guild still actively serves while her outfit hasn't been called upon in generations. They are apparently peers in age as well.

Severian met and knew her before his infamy and ascenscion, so he knows she did not seek to spend time with him for those reasons. This is an important advantage she has in that it rules out her being a golddigger or a plotter, or otherwise seeking to exploit their relationship, at least initially. (Alas, Claudius and Messalina...)

Severian remarks on how he never desires a woman more than when he cannot have her - their considerable separation counts here. Then there his reflection that love and lust are intertwined, since men love women for giving themselves to them, even if it is only in their minds; so after months or years of unconsumated separation and months of fantasizing on his un-conjugal travels, his crush on his original adolescent sweetheart might be very strong indeed.

And there is her dark hair and light skin that Severian overtly finds attractive, and if the metal trim on her dress is zippers up the sides, that doesn't hurt either.
