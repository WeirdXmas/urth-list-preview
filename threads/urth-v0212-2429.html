Time must have a stop

Urth list · 3 messages · 2002-12-16 to 2002-12-16

2002urthThe Book of the Long SunJerry FriedmanChris PDon Doggett
The thread: Readers debated whether time stopped on the Whorl during Silk's enlightenment, and if so why. Jerry Friedman argued the stopping was a direct act of the Outsider's will rather than the starship's faster-than-light drive, asking why an omnipotent Outsider would not simply stop time itself. Don Doggett defended the time-stopping theory, citing Marble's internal clock being about 2,700 years off, and conceded the evidence is circumstantial but still compelling. Chris also favored a direct Outsider cause, possibly an embolism, and said Don had not answered that alternative, while noting that proving FTL would only show the theory possible, not true. The thread ended without agreement, with the exchange stalling on the same points.
  • Jerry Friedman: time stopped by the Outsider's direct will, not FTL; a miracle would be simpler
  • Don Doggett: time stopped during Silk's journey via FTL, supported by Marble's clock being about 2,700 years off
  • Chris: the Outsider directly caused Silk's experiences, possibly through an embolism, which is more believable than the FTL explanation

Themes: Time loops and causality, Gods, angels and the Increate, The dying sun and the cosmos

2002-12-16 Jerry Friedman #

Argues that the stopping of time on the Whorl during Silk's enlightenment was caused directly by the Outsider's will rather than by the starship's FTL drive, since a miracle to stop time would have been simpler. Responds to Don Doggett's question by restating his view, granting it for argument, and asking whether an omnipotent Outsider would not simply stop time itself.
disputes · theology-and-religion · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: The Outsider directly stopped time for Silk's enlightenment; Time stopped because of the Whorl's FTL drive

[snip]

Because the Outsider wanted it to.

Since I don't have the text here, I'll accept for the sake of argument your claims that time stopped rather than something else happening and this is separate from Silk's enlightenment. Your contention is that this happened because of some action of the _Whorl's_ space drive, maybe coming out of FTL (I don't quite remember what you wrote), and the Outsider miraculously exempted Silk from this stoppage. Right?

I would assume--but correct me if I'm wrong--that the Outsider's choosing this moment wasn't a coincidence. For some reason the stoppage of time was a favorable circumstance for his enlightenment of Silk. Do I have that right?

But if stopping time was favorable for enlightenment, than the Outsider (who is omnipotent) could have stopped time on his own. And would have--why not? So we're choosing between the Outsider's taking advantage of an event in the _Whorl's_ trip to work a miracle, or the Outsider's working one miracle to create the right set-up for another.

If we had some independent evidence that the _Whorl_ did have FTL, and (better) that Wolfe's imagined FTL involved time stopping at some point, then your suggestion would tempt me. But as it is, the possibility that the Outsider is directly responsible for everything Silk experiences seems much easier to believe.

2002-12-16 Chris P #

Argues that the Outsider directly caused Silk's experiences, possibly through an embolism, and that this is more believable than the faster-than-light explanation for the Blushas theory. Responds to Jerry's point and says Don's reply left his earlier alternative unaddressed, noting that proving FTL would only show the Blushas theory is possible, not true.
disputes · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Outsider directly responsible for Silk's experiences via embolism; Blushas theory via faster-than-light travel; Ship circled or never left solar system

Thanks Jerry, this is, essentially, not far from what I proposed in my post as a reasonable alternate explanation, and which Don didn't address in his reply. Basically, the Outsider is directly responsible, possibly by means of Silk having an embolism. The embolism is much easier to believe than the FTL explanation, if we're looking only for the most believable/possible answer.

I am not a crusader against the Blushas theory, though I do not believe it. I just think there are more fertile grounds to pursue the question than this particular line. In the absolute best case, if one proves beyond reasonable doubt that FTL did occur, one merely proves that the Blushas theory is *not impossible* - it does nothing to establish its truth. And there are much easier ways to show that Blushas is not impossible, without resorting to FTL. You could say that the ship went in a circle, or it might never have even left the solar system in the first place. There are arguments against both of these, too, I grant, but they both require less justification.

Chris
