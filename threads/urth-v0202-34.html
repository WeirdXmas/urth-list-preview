"It's all right there in Chapter 1"

Urth list · 3 messages · 2002-01-31 to 2002-02-01

2002urthThe Book of the Long SunDan'l Danehy-OakesJames JordanMichael Straight
The thread: Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued that the Short Sun books, like Wolfe's earlier series, are holographic, with most of the story already present in the first chapter, and compared the openings of the New Sun and Long Sun. Michael Straight agreed the book is holographic but said its hidden key is plants and hybrids, and explained that Silk and Horn must combine for hybrid vigor, which fades so Silk must return to a pure strain as corn does. James Jordan extended the hybrid idea, calling the Catholic Eucharist the ultimate plant-human hybrid, and asked whether bread and wine figure in the books. The thread stays at the level of proposals and questions, and no reply settles the points.
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: Short Sun is holographic like the earlier books, with most of the story in Chapter 1
  • Michael Straight: plants and hybrids are the hidden key, and Silk must return to a pure strain as corn does
  • James Jordan: plant-human hybrids are a theme, with the Eucharist as the ultimate example

Themes: Christ figures and messiahs, Catholic faith and sacraments, The dying sun and the cosmos

Argues that the Wolfe books share a holographic structure in which most of the story is implied or stated outright in the first chapter, and asks why the Short Sun should differ. Builds the point on Marc's earlier remark and on the openings of The Book of the New Sun and The Book of the Long Sun, comparing the series to one another.

2002-01-31 Michael Straight #

Argues that SS is holographic like the earlier books, with plants and hybrids as its hidden key rather than the genetics Horn never discusses. Replies to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes by explaining that Silk and Horn must combine for hybrid vigor, which fades, so Silk must return to a pure strain as corn does.
proposes · puzzle-solving · theories: Silk and Horn are hybrids that must combine; Silk must return to a pure strain like corn

SS is also holographic, but I don't think Horn talks about genetics because plants are the key. Hybrids are the key. Silk and Horn must be combined to get the benefits of hybrid vigor, but it won't last. Eventually (corn) Silk must return to being a pure strain in order to be healthy.

-Rostrum

2002-02-01 James Jordan #

Argues that plant-human hybrids are a theme of the Short Sun books, pointing to the Catholic Eucharist as the ultimate example of a plant-human hybrid. Responds to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's claim that the Short Sun is holographic and that Horn's plants and hybrids are key, asking whether the bread and wine figure in the books.
proposes · theology-and-religion · theories: Transsubstantiated bread and wine as plant-human hybrid

If plant-human hybrids are a theme of the book, then consider the ultimate plant-human hybrid: the Roman Catholic transsubstantiated bread and wine. And this does seem to figure in the books, doesn't it?

Nutria
