2003-02-14 James Jordan #

Argues that conventional science fiction, like Wolfe's work, is best understood as myth rather than as settled theory about the universe, and that today's scientific paradigms will look dated later. Reacts to an unnamed remark that Wolfe is out of date, offering a general defense of SF as literary and mythical instead of citing any book or passage.
proposes

Well, so Wolfe is now apparently out of date!
I think 100 years from now, all our SF will be seriously out of date. Conventional SF works in terms of theories about the universe that are just that: theories. Better: myths. Can anyone seriously believe that humanity has done more than just scratch the surface of the reality of the universe? We cast out grids, paradigms, myths, that seem to make the most sense of the data we know. How much don't we know? We've not been at this very long.
That's why I like SF much more than I like PBS. SF is literary, fun, mythical. PBS always seems to think the latest word is also the last word!

Nutria

2003-02-14 Chris P #

Notes that an open-universe finding from cosmic microwave mapping was long anticipated, since scientists hoped dark matter might supply the missing mass for a recollapse. Questions how researchers claim to know the amount of dark matter, doubting the article's precision; the post responds to a quoted note about cyclic universe theories and Wolfean cosmology.
questions

This has actually been theorized for quite some time. IIRC some scientists were holding on to the idea that there may be enough dark matter in the universe to account for the difference in mass needed to bring everything back together.

What confuses me about this article is that they seem (or claim) to know just how much dark matter there is in the universe - I'm not sure how they're coming up with those numbers.
