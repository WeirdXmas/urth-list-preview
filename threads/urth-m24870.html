Excerpt from "The Land Across"

Urth list · 11 messages · 2013-10-22 to 2013-10-26

2013urthGwern BranwenGerry QuinnThomas BittermanFred KiescheAntonin ScriabinDaniel Petersen
The thread: Readers discussed the Tor.com excerpt from The Land Across, with Gwern Branwen proposing that it blends a Cold War anti-Communist potboiler with the Dracula myth and objecting to the jarring iPhone and an unlikely narrative voice. Antonin Scriabin countered that the journal voice resembles The Sorcerer's House and that Wolfe's later novels include modern technology, such as Able's Mac in The Wizard Knight. Daniel Otto Jack Petersen compared the tone to Swift, Kafka and Mieville, suggested the narrator has moved into a parallel world, and linked the excerpt's terror to the holy in angels, a point Scriabin and he developed together. The thread ended without any real dispute, drifting into blog pointers and the 'Horror of the Holy' idea.
  • Gwern Branwen: excerpt mixes Cold War potboiler with Dracula myth; iPhone and voice are jarring
  • Antonin Scriabin: journal voice echoes The Sorcerer's House; modern tech appears in later novels
  • Daniel Otto Jack Petersen: tone suggests a parallel world; angels and God are horrifying to mortals
  • Fred Kiesche: posted the Tor.com excerpt link

Themes: Ghosts, dreams and visions, Gods, angels and the Increate, Robots, androids and artificial life

2013-10-22 Gwern Branwen replying to Fred Kiesche #

Proposes that the excerpt from The Land Across mixes a Cold War anti-Communist potboiler with the Dracula myth, citing Transylvania and national fear of the Turks. Notes the jarring iPhone mention and a narrative voice no contemporary American would use, and asks what to make of the Kleon/Cleon naming, replying to Fred Kiesche.
proposes · genre-and-craft · source-and-allusion · theories: The Land Across mixes Cold War thriller with Dracula myth

Curious. Comes off as a mish-mash of a Cold war anti-Communist potboiler and the Dracula myth (Transylvania, national paranoid fear of 'the Turks') with some jarring contemporary features like mention of an iPhone (an iPhone? in a Gene Wolfe novel? I never thought I'd see the day) - jarring because the tone of the narration is so Wolfe and no young or even middle-aged American talks/thinks/writes like the narrator does. I also don't know what to make of the Kleon/Cleon naming.

2013-10-22 Antonin Scriabin replying to Gwern Branwen #

Points out that Able of the High Heart recalls his Mac early in The Wizard Knight, when he remembers his earlier life in the real world. Responds to Gwern Branwen's objection to an iPhone in The Land Across by offering this and other modern-technology references in Wolfe's later novels as a counterpoint.
informs · close-textual-reading

If I remember correctly, Able of the High Heart thinks back to his Mac towards the beginning of *The Knight, *when he is remembering his previous life in the "real world"*. *They may be few and far between, but references to modern technology can be found in some of his more recent novels.
