2000-08-02 Adam Stephanides #

Corrects Josh Levitan's recollection by showing that the speaker in the Long Sun passage is Fava, not Jahlee, and quoting her line about the inhumi's marvelous powers. Argues she means the powers the inhumi gained from the Neighbors rather than moral improvement, and that enslaved inhumi would not remember the Neighbors as benevolent.
disputes · close-textual-reading · myth-and-structure · theories: The Neighbors enslaved the inhumi

I found the passage. It's actually Fava and not Jahlee, and her exact reply is "'I've wondered about that. It must have been marvelous. Miraculous...For them [the inhumi], I mean.'" (214) She seems to be referring to the powers the inhumi would have acquired from the Neighbors, not to any moral improvement, and the context of the conversation supports this (Horn goes on to suggest that present-day inhumi may have retained a trace of these powers). And if Horn is correct in his conjecture (and so far it is only a conjecture) that the Neighbors enslaved the inhumi, the inhumi would be unlikely to remember the Neighbors as especially benevolent or spiritual, although they seem that way to us.

--Adam
