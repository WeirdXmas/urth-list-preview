2007-12-15 Ashley Crill #

Proposes that Typhon's mountain in The Book of the New Sun is the real peak Illimani in the Cordillera Real, and that a clockwise drift of South America would fit Folia's geography. Grounds this on Severian's route up the Parana and Pilcomayo, Typhon's mountain facing west toward the coastal plain, and Lake Titcaca lying northwest of it.
proposes · source-and-allusion · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Typhon's mountain is Illimani; Clockwise drift of South America explains Folia's geography

I'm pretty sure that the Cordillera Real(Royal Mountains) is the range where Severian ventured, which would fit very well with a clockwise rotation of South America. He would then have reach them buy traveling up the Parana and perhaps its major tributary the Pilcomayo, and then traveling northeast. This also fits in with the fact that Typhon's mountain faced westward, and that Severian could look out of his right eye and see the coastal plain and see to the northeast. I think that Typhon's mountain was actually Illimani(http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illimani), the highest mountain in that chain and known as the 'father mountain' of Bolivia. This tectonic shift would also place Lake Titcaca to the northwest of Typhon's mountain, with the war front north and northeast. Fanciful speculations, but the pieces fit together fairly well.

2007-12-16 Pedro Pereira replying to Ashley Crill #

Disputes Ashley Crill's proposal that continental drift rotated South America so that the mountains lie on the wrong side of the continent in Folia's story. Argues the rotation is unsupported by present tectonics and plate positions, would take far longer than the available time, and would erase all recognizable continental features.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Continental drift rotated South America 90 degrees

From: [email]: [email]: Sat, 15 Dec 2007 14:02:48 -0700Subject: Re: (urth) Urth Digest, Vol 40, Issue 15

The only problem is that there is no indication that the continent is shifting 90 degrees.

Not only that, but I don't see WHY it should do that according to present tectonics and sipostion of plaques. And that would also take a lot more time than 100.000-1 million years.

Also, such a major rotation would imply that basically the WHOLE of the Earth crust (read continents and oceans) would also be completely changed and in that sense there would be no point in talking about S.A., N.A. etc. Basically, there would be no recognizable features at all (I mean, at the same level a fictitious inhabitant of Pangea I would find anything recognizable in Pangea II).
