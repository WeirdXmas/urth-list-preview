2008-10-10 Marc Aramini #

Argues that Wolfe's Book of the New Sun uses Christological symbols like water, the cross and healing in deliberately double-edged ways, citing Severian's escape from the cataract at Thrax in Sword of the Lictor. Grounds this in the wilderness temptation with Typhon, the planting of terminus est, Vodalus's false sun coin, and the natural explanation of the pelerines' floating tent, which undercut the traditional symbols.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · close-textual-reading · theories: Wolfe's Christological symbols are deliberately double-edged

The son of witz is most interested in the religious resonance of New Sun - let me direct you to the opening of Sword of the Lictor when Sev escapes the cataract at Thrax. Again and again water is coupled with death and healing, from the very first chapter of the book. In the cataract Severian wants to heal the sickly, and in his escape he falls several times bearing his large deadly cross, inundated with the flow of water. Later we get the temptation in the wilderness with Typhon and the planting of terminus est in the ground to make a true cross when sev tries to save little sev and fails.

However, these symbols and christological events in wolfe are intentionally double edged, like terminus est. The sun coin of vodalus is false, and the avern symbolizes agia with her deadly trickery as well as the new sun.

Of all the allegorists out there, Wolfe is the trickiest because he can&#39;t resist undercutting the traditional symbols with levels of resonance - see the chapter at the end of shadow which talks of the symbolism of the floating tent of the pelerines which is ultimately a natural phenomenon.

2008-10-10 John Watkins replying to Son of Witz #

Explains that under Church rules a person who has not been confirmed and does not intend to be is not counted as a Catholic, and that baptism cannot be undone. Responds to Son of Witz's remark that not being Catholic leaves him blind to the baptismal symbols in The Book of the New Sun, and corrects the terms of his claim.
informs · theology-and-religion

As for "how that works," you don't count as a Catholic because you haven't been confirmed and have no intent to be confirmed. You can't be "unbaptised" in the Church's eyes or anything like that.
