Horn as antihero; Horn's body

Whorl list · 3 messages · 2000-04-05 to 2000-04-06

2000whorlThe Book of the Long SunMichael StraightAdam StephanidesMark Millman
The thread: Readers debated whether Horn, in The Book of the New Sun-era Urth cycle's Blue-world episodes, knows he is inhabiting Silk's body and whether his betrayal in the pit can be forgiven. Adam Stephanides argued Horn's remarks about Silk being far away show he is unaware of the swap, so Silk would be unfindable without Horn mentioning it. Mark Millman countered that the question cannot be settled until Horn returns to the Whorl, noting a two-headed Pas-body would be noticed and that Horn's resemblance to Silk explains his being made Rajan. Michael Straight proposed that the two-headed figure comes from a Pas fragment or from Silk's consciousness reassembling a splintered Pas program in Mainframe, offered as speculation pending Horn's return. The thread stayed on competing readings of the text without reaching agreement.
  • Adam Stephanides: Horn's betrayal in the pit is hard to forgive, and his remarks about Silk show he does not know about the body swap
  • mark millman: Horn's ignorance of his body cannot be settled until he returns to the Whorl; his resemblance to Silk explains why he was made Rajan
  • Michael Straight: the two-headed figure comes from a Pas fragment in Silk's body or Silk's mind reassembling the splintered Pas program in Mainframe

Themes: Doubles, twins and clones, Hidden identities and parentage, Robots, androids and artificial life

2000-04-05 Adam Stephanides #

Argues that Horn's betrayal of his family in the pit is hard to forgive, and that Horn's own words about Silk being far away on Blue show he cannot know he is inhabiting Silk's body. Responds to alga by restating the case that Horn's remark about Silk being remote from them implies he does not realize the body swap, so the swap would have left Silk unfindable without Horn mentioning it.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Horn does not know he is inhabiting Silk's body

I agree that of the sins I listed, Horn's betrayal of his family in the pit is the easiest to forgive; and I may have been led by my rhetoric to speak more harshly of it than it deserves. But it was a real shock to me when I read it, especially since Horn's offer to Krait was spontaneous. I think that there are very few authors aside from Wolfe who would risk alienating their audience by allowing a
sympathetically-intended protagonist to do what Horn does, no matter what the provocation. (I'm still undecided whether or not Horn is indeed sympathetically intended.)

My point was that Horn apparently thinks that Silk may have been on Blue all the time he (Horn) was going to _Whorl_ and back. And it's hard to see how he could think this if he knew he was in Silk's body.

As I said above, if Horn thinks Silk is in another body, and doesn't want to be found, then there is no need for him to cite distance as a possible reason why Silk might be on Blue and yet not found. A Silk in another body and hiding his identity would be virtually impossible to find, even if he were living next door to Horn.

And Silk's possibly being in another body wouldn't explain why Horn doesn't mention being in Silk's body.

--Adam

2000-04-06 Mark Millman #

Argues that Horn's ignorance of whether he occupies Silk's former body, or a near-identical one, cannot yet be settled before Horn returns to the Whorl. Responds to alga's point about hiding in another body by noting a two-headed Pas-body would be widely noticed, and suggests Horn's resemblance to Silk explains why the Gaonese made him Rajan.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Horn may be in Silk's former body; Horn knows he resembles Silk

Well, if Silk were in a two-headed Pas-body, he'd certainly be a topic of conversation and very unlikely to pass anywhere with- out notice. We probably won't have much evidence on this ei- ther way until Horn makes it back to the _Whorl_.

This argument, of course, doesn't address why Horn doesn't say that he is (or thinks he is) in Silk's body. I think that he is aware that he looks like Silk--I believe he thinks that's why the Gaonese made him Rajan--but we can't yet say whether he's in the body formerly belonging to Silk, or in another one that's very like or identical to it.

Nacre

2000-04-06 Michael Straight #

Proposes that Silk's mind is not housed in a two-headed Pas body but that the two-headed figure arises either from a fragment of Pas in Silk's body or from Silk's consciousness reassembling the splintered Pas program in Mainframe. Offers these alternatives as speculation about what the Horn situation will reveal, answering mark millman's quoted claim that evidence awaits Horn's return to the Whorl.
proposes · puzzle-solving · theories: Silk's consciousness reassembles the splintered Pas program; A fragment of Pas survives in Silk's body

And I don't think we'll see Silk's consciousness in a two-headed Pas body, I think we'll either see a fragment of Pas in Silk's body (the two minds making two heads) or Silk's consciousness being used to paste together the splinters of the "dead" Pas program in Mainframe (again, two heads).

-Rostrum
