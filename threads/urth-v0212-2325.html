Heinlein in FLF

Urth list · 1 messages · 2002-12-02 to 2002-12-02

2002urthMichael Andre-Driussi

Themes: Genre: science fiction, fantasy, both, Time loops and causality

2002-12-02 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Argues that Free Live Free shows Heinlein influences, including the kitchen's door-into-summer image and a libertarian reading that the text may undercut as a conscious critique. Grounds this in time-loop parallels with a Heinlein story and frontier-liberty settings, and raises Chesterton's The Man Who Was Thursday as a fitting comparison.
proposes · genre-and-craft · myth-and-structure · theories: Free Live Free critiques libertarian reading of Heinlein

Which is funny, because I did detect odd flashes of Heinlein in FLF, mainly in the kitchen's "door into summer" image, but also the sort of Libertarian reading that Adam Stephanides was talking about (a reading that if not undermined by the text itself would produce a very Heinlein feel -- since I think it is undermined, I wonder if it is a conscious critique of Heinlein or not). As well as the whole "--By His Bootstraps" time-travel loop, of course!

If the four do end up in a "simpler past," where they enjoy the benefits of frontier liberty, then that would also be, imho, a thing to remind me of Heinlein. The frontier spirit of THE MOON IS A HARSH MISTRESS and the frontiers of FARNHAM'S FREEHOLD.

OTOH, Dave Langford's review of FLF mentions Chesterton's THE MAN WHO WAS THURSDAY, and this seems very appropriate.

=mantis=
