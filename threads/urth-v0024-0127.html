1999-04-01 Doug Eigsti #

Recommends the three comic adaptations of The Shadow of the Torturer, praising their artwork and Maitz covers for capturing the book's feel. Notes that Gene Wolfe did the adaptation, that the text is abridged but essential scenes remain, and that the series stops partway through the novel.
supports

Cliff Drane,

I can recommend the three SHADOW comics. The artwork is the best at representing the feel of the book; better than any of the book covers I have seen. And the Maitz covers are my favorite, as covers. These comics do more than grab the eye of the bookstore browser trying to judge by the cover. They have an odd quality that I found appropriate to Severian's tale. If I am not mistaken, Gene Wolfe did the adaptation. Of course the text is abridged. But the essentials to the story are still there. You will be disappointed however, disappointed that there are only three issues and they only take you part way into SHADOW. I would have liked to see the completed series. Well worth twice the price just to see some of the scenes illustrated.

=Talon=

1999-04-01 Michael Straight #

Proposes that a reader compare the Shadow of the Torturer comics with the novel to see what Wolfe chose to keep and omit, if he wrote the adaptation. Responds to Doug Eigsti's praise of the comics' artwork and covers by asking for an analysis of the adaptation's essentials.
proposes · genre-and-craft

If the comics are written by Wolfe, it seems that it would be extremely interesting to find out what he considered "the essentials to the story."

I'd love to see someone who has the comics post an analysis of what they include and what they leave out (assuming, that is, that Wolfe did the adaptation).

-Rostrum
