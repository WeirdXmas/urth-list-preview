Themes: Names and their meanings

1998-07-28 mary whalen #

Lists character names from The Book of the Long Sun and The Book of the Short Sun alongside their real-world meanings, such as Dreoilin as Irish for wren and Dace as a North American freshwater fish. Asks whether Dace's fish name connects him to Kypris and whether Desmid, a spy in Potto's service, is a woman.
questions · source-and-allusion · puzzle-solving

This is Sean Whalen (prion).

Dreoilin. Irish for wren. Iolar's favorite daughter. In irish it is spelled with an accute accent mark over the second i.

Dace. From Latin darsus. A small freshwater fish of North America of the family Cyprinidae, such as the fallfish or golden shiner. The captain of the fishing boat in Lake Limna that Scylla-as-Chenille takes over. The other man with the cyprinid fish name made a sacrifice to Kypris, is Dace connected to her somehow, even though he tells Scylla that he worships her?

Dahlia. One of many species of genus Dahlia of the Compositae family. Also the Dahlia violet. An old student of Marble's.

Desmid. Green algae that are in the order Zygnematales, most in the family Desmidiaceae. Cannot reproduce asexually. One of Potto's spies/spy-catchers who worked for Spider. Is Desmid a woman?
