Women read Wolfe too

Urth list · 11 messages · 2008-05-15 to 2008-05-20

2008urthAllan AndersonP KonopkaHelen AlgminAndy RobertsonCharles ReedHmpf MacSlowDave Tallmanaaron
The thread: P Konopka opened the thread by asking whether other women read Wolfe, saying she could find no one else who had read most of his work. Allan Anderson and Helen Algmin replied with encouragement and personal reading histories, Helen welcoming her and comparing Brust's Vlad to Latro. The discussion then drifted into recommendations of women-created comics: Hmpf MacSlow pointed to Carla Speed McNeil's Finder and Donna Barr's comics as strange works paralleling Wolfe, and Charles Reed endorsed Finder. The thread ended in comics chat with no argument about Wolfe's fiction.
  • P Konopka: longtime female Wolfe reader seeking other women readers
  • Allan Anderson: family readers, mother found Book of the New Sun too creepy, Wolfe hard to get in audio
  • Helen Algmin: welcomes her, compares Brust's Vlad to Latro
  • Hmpf MacSlow: recommends Carla Speed McNeil and Donna Barr comics as parallels to Wolfe
  • Charles Reed: praises Finder and its favourite stories

Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Genre: science fiction, fantasy, both, Beasts and the animal in us

2008-05-15 P Konopka #

Introduces the poster as a longtime female reader of Wolfe and science fiction and fantasy, a mother of three, and asks whether any other women have posted to the list. Says she subscribed because she could find no one else, male or female, who had read most of Wolfe's work.
questions

I've never posted before but felt compelled to do so today. I'm a 40 year old mother of three living in Michigan and I've read science fiction and fantasy from about the age of 10. I have never read any books that my neighbors or coworkers (i.e. other women) have ever heard of, and vice versa. Not?a lot of female sci-fi / fantasy fans out here in suburbia.? Have any other women posted?
Patty

2008-05-15 Allan Anderson replying to P Konopka #

Replies to a newcomer's post about being a woman reader of Wolfe by describing family members who read science fiction, including a mother who found The Book of the New Sun too creepy. Offers encouragement that the reader is not alone and notes that Wolfe is hard to find in audio format.
chats

My sister and stepmom read a lot of SF. I got my (non-step) mom to read the first New Sun book, but I think she found it too creepy and didn't follow up with it. I'll have to suggest the other stuff to the rest of my family. My wife has read a bunch of SF, too, but since she has poor eyesight she usually sticks to audio books. Sadly, I haven't seen any Wolfe in that format.

My point is, you're not alone!

2008-05-15 Helen Algmin replying to P Konopka #

Introduces herself as a 27-year-old woman from the Chicago area who first encountered Wolfe at an event with Neil Gaiman, and names her favorite Wolfe books and other authors she enjoys. Replies to P Konopka's earlier post, which asked whether any other women read Wolfe, by welcoming her and mentioning Steve Brust's Vlad in the Jhereg series as resembling Latro.
chats

Thanks for posting, Ms. Konopka! I admit I was feeling a little out of place.

I am 27 years old. I'm female. I live in the Chicago area. I go to the library once a week and keep a notebook with me at all times for writing down good book suggestions.

I never heard of Gene Wolfe until I went to see Neil Gaiman about three years ago. Mr. Wolfe was the special guest. If anyone has seen him speak, you will know why I was so interested. I ran right out to get a one of his books. I picked up "Pandora, by Holly Hollander" of all things!

My favorite Wolfe books are the Soldier series. And I consider trying to understand Peace to be a labor of love (I'm still mulling over your comments, Mr. Tallman!)

I am loving Steve Brust lately. Something about his main character Vlad in the "Jhereg" series reminds me a little little bit of Latro.

2008-05-16 Hmpf MacSlow replying to P Konopka #

Says the poster is a woman who has read The Book of the New Sun once and plans to reread it, then recommends comics by Carla Speed McNeil (Finder) and Donna Barr (Desert Peach, Afterdead) as deep, strange works paralleling Wolfe. Offers links to reviews and websites, noting the comics' oddness and idiosyncrasy.
informs

Yes. Me, among others. Although I only post very rarely, and never, so far, anything particularly insightful. I've only read TBotNS once, in 2003, and feel it needs several readings before I can start talking about it in an intelligent manner; haven't had time to re-read it with the attention it deserves since then, alas. Will definitely do so after I've finished university, which will be sometime in 2009.

Sort of OT, but... if anyone around here is also interested in comics, I'm writing my M.A. thesis about some comics who I feel share some of the depth and strangeness of Wolfe's creation, although they're very different on a philosophical/ideological level. And one of them actually uses the word 'Ascian' for a nomadic people it describes!

So, since we've just seem to have passed through another round of recommending authors and books, let me recommend some seriously deep, seriously weird comics:

Carla Speed McNeil: Finder

Website/samples: http://www.lightspeedpress.com/
A few reviews, because I'm too busy right now to give my own: http://www.paulgravett.com/articles/032_finder/032_finder.htm http://archive.salon.com/books/review/2005/08/05/mcneil/index1.html Possibly my very favourite review (you have to scroll down a bit, to the part titled 'Finding Carla'):
http://www.vogelein.com/JanerBlog/2005/10/

Okay, that was actually only the moderately weird comic. Now for the seriously weird:

Donna Barr: everything she's done, i.e. The Desert Peach, Stinz, Hader and the Colonel, Bosom Enemies, Afterdead

I haven't found any really comprehensive review yet, so I have to do this myself:

Donna Barr's been producing comics for decades now, and they're all just *barely* this side of totally insane - and fascinating, in a way that's often disconcerting. In many cases I still don't entirely know what I actually think about them. Which is a definite parallel with Gene Wolfe's work, for me! Donna Barr's world is filled with fictitious gay brothers of famous Wehrmacht generals, people who unexpectedly turn into horses, centaurs who fight in some close analogue of the First World War, rabbits who want to turn human and befriend harpies... not to mention that her latest work, the webcomic Afterdead, unites all these unusual characters in a world that is even more bizarre, namely some sort of far-future/life-after-death version of the Third Reich.

All of which sounds as if it's carefully designed to offend just about everyone. And really, it's incredibly hard to recommend these comics without going 'no, no, no, it's *not* like you think!' all the time. Especially if you haven't figured out what you think yourself, yet.

They're... *challenging* comics, on a number of levels, which come from a deeply personal, deeply idiosyncratic place. And, really: they're not like you think. I swear. ;-)

Donna Barr's website (which includes links to free downloads of her - possibly - least weird comic, The Desert Peach, in the 'store' section): http://www.donnabarr.com/

Hmpf (really a woman, despite the gender-neutral nick *g*)
