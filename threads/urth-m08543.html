The thread: Readers asked which way the Whorl turns and whether Silk's first look from a lander in Lake holds useful hints. Jordon Flato proposed that the barren pitted rock plain is an asteroid, citing three quoted passages, while Jeff Wilson argued it is a remnant of the original body, massive enough to look flat, ruling out the Cole process. Stuart Hamm mostly chatted and added no analysis. The thread stalled without any settled conclusion, drifting into side remarks about the tractor and the landscape.

  • Jordon Flato: the pitted naked plain is an asteroid, supported by quoted passages
  • Jeff Wilson: the rock plain is original body remnant, ruling out the Cole process, citing Pilgrims' Way cliffs
  • Stuart Hamm: likes the space-age tractor and wants one, offers no analysis

Themes: The dying sun and the cosmos, Power, rulers and politics

2009-01-26 Jordon Flato replying to Craig Brewer #

Proposes that the barren plain of rock described in Wolfe's text, pitted like a pox survivor's cheeks, is an asteroid. Grounds the claim in three quoted passages describing the naked, pitted, moss-less rock of the plain.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: The barren plain is an asteroid

"looked across a benighted plain of naked rock."

"The somber plain was pitted like the cheeks of a child who had survived the pox, and far more parren tha tne sheer clifss of the Pilgrims' Way; no tree, no flower, no least wed or dot of moss sprouted from its rock."

"The plain of rock had blanched unwatfched, and was streaked with sable...."

So, we have a plain of benighted rock which is pitted like a pox survivor. Sounds like an asteroid to me.
