Themes: Women, sex and gender

2012-05-30 Lee Berman #

Agrees with Fred Pohl's blog view that Gene Wolfe has a dark side, arguing that this darkness is too persistent and pervasive across his work to be a casual exploration. Responds to Mo Holkar's post linking Pohl's essay on Wolfe as unknowable, drawing only on the body of Wolfe's writing as evidence.
supports · author-and-biography

Thanks for that Mo. I'll have to agree with Fred. Given a limited data set, that being the body of Wolfe's work, I too detect a real dark (which is not to say evil) side to his nature. It seems too persistent and pervasive to reflect a casual lark exploration. Who knows what it means in his personal life but it surely makes him a better, more interesting writer.

2012-05-30 Jeff Wilson replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that Amanda Palmer's work shares Wolfe's dark themes of robots, objectification and the exploitation and abuse of women and children, so critics who read misogyny into Wolfe would struggle to apply that reading to hers. Responds to Lee Berman's quoted remark about a dark side in Wolfe by citing Wolfe's fondness for Palmer's "Coin-Operated Boy" and her "Map of Tasmania" as a comparison.
disputes · theories: Palmer's dark work undercuts the misogyny reading of Wolfe

Did anyone check out Amanda Palmer after Wolfe's fondness for "Coin-Operated Boy" was revealed? Numerous, similarly dark themes in their work, not just robots and the objectification of humans but particularly the exploitation and abuse of women and children. The folks who read misogyny into Wolfean imagery are going to have a hard time selling it from Palmer's.

Beware "Map of Tasmania"; I have never been able to to look at the later, moustashed pictures of Wolfe in the same way since.
