2003-07-19 Lisa Schaffer-Doggett #

Asks how the Green Man repays Severian for freeing him, since his promised future aid never appears in The Book of the New Sun. Proposes a third explanation: the Green Man already helped Severian earlier, through his mastery of time corridors, and asks when in the text this could have happened.
questions · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: The Green Man already aided Severian in his past

All of this talk about Silk (the man who doesn't eat) put me back on the track of a question that's been nagging me. How does the Green Man repay Severian for setting him free? The rescue from the Ascians doesn't count (the Green Man's own words) and he claims that he will aid him again. Then, nothing. No more Green Man. In my mind there are three explanations for this. Wolfe forgot about the Green Man's promise, or decided not to bother with it (not a viable option for me), Severian decided to omit any more mention of the Green Man for his own reasons (too close to the first, basically a cop-out), or the Green Man, being a master of the corridors of time has already aided Severian in his past (though it would be the Green Man's personal future). "When" in the text could this have happened, and what action could it be?

Don

2003-07-21 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Proposes that the Green Man rescued Severian at least once during his unrecorded year as an Ascian slave, when he lacked the Claw. Notes the text says nothing of that period and that Severian had no magical powers then, joking that he also believes Severian married Agia.
proposes · puzzle-solving · theories: The Green Man saved Severian during his Ascian slavery; Severian the Cruel married Agia

Wasn't someone mentioning fairly recently that we don't know what happened during Severian's Year as an Ascian Slave?

One of the few things we know about that period of Severian's Reign is that he did not have the Claw (and thus no magical powers to help out when things got rough).

I guess I'm the only one who figures that the Green Man saved Severian's bacon at least once during that unmentionable year.

(Then again, I'm the same lunatic who thinks that Severian the Cruel married Agia . . . )

=mantis=

2003-07-21 Stone Ox #

Argues that the Green Man's promised restitution to Severian must happen before Severian frees him, creating a time loop, and proposes that a green face glimpsed in the antechamber is the Green Man seeking a chance to free him. Grounds this in Severian escaping the antechamber on his own by using the hidden door's word of power, which rules that moment out as the repayment itself.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: Green Man's restitution occurs before Severian frees him; green face in antechamber is the Green Man

Don says:

This question has been bugging me for some time as well. The Green Man promises to set Severian free when he is
imprisoned, so we only need to look at times when he is
imprisoned (although there are a lot of those). I am also convinced that the Green Man's restitution happens before Severian frees him, thus creating yet another time loop. When Severian is imprisoned in the antechamber, one of the other prisoners there sees a green face, which I am convinced is the Green Man looking for opportunities to set Severian free. I think this shows that Wolfe hasn't just dropped this thread. However, this can't be the time that the Green Man redeems his promise, since Severian gets out of the
antechamber by himself (remembering Thecla's use of the
hidden door's word of power).

Any takers on this one?

--
Stone Ox (Peter S.)
