Twist ending?

Whorl list · 1 messages · 2001-03-28 to 2001-03-28

2001whorlThe Book of the Long SunMichael Straight

Themes: Ghosts, dreams and visions, Hidden identities and parentage

2001-03-28 Michael Straight #

Argues that the ending twist of Return to the Whorl is obvious in hindsight, because throughout the book characters treat the possessed body's original persona as the real person, as Horn and others treat Silk as Silk. Grounds this in parallels with Oreb, Marble and Pig, and in the closing line about Horn being dead without knowing it.
proposes · close-textual-reading · genre-and-craft · theories: Silk is the surviving persona inside Horn's body; Horn is dead without knowing it

Several people have mentioned the "surprise" twist at the end of RTTW, some even disparaging it as Hollywoodesque. But why is this a surprise? In every other case of a spirit inhabiting someone else's body, all the characters regard the persona original to that body as the "real" person and the other spirit as a (usually transient) intruder. Horn wouldn't start calling Oreb "Scylla" and decide that Oreb was no longer present, or think that Marble was really Maytera Rose now, or say that because of Silk Inside[TM], Pig no longer exists.

All through RTTW, people are treating Silk exactly the same way, refusing to stop seeing him as Silk just because he may have a little bit of Horn in there. So why should we be surprised that Silk turns out to be Silk? (Yes I know why, yes I was surprised, I'm just marvelling at how obvious it all is and how cleverly Wolfe waved it right in front of my face the whole time.)

Horn is dead, but he doesn't know it (even though he does). Gee, Wolfe's never done *that* before. Doh!

-Rostrum
