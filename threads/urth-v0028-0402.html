1999-11-06 Nicholas Gevers #

Argues that The Urth of the New Sun is Wolfe's best single book and completes and transcends The Book of the New Sun, even calling it possibly the best science fiction novel ever written. Offers its rich eclecticism and poetic language as grounds, comparing its intensity to Joyce, and questions whether the criteria being used are wrong. Responds to the question of whether Urth is a necessary coda.
proposes · genre-and-craft · theories: The Urth of the New Sun completes and transcends The Book of the New Sun

Re the necessity of URTH as a coda to TBOTNS: I
personally see THE URTH OF THE NEW SUN as Wolfe's best
single book, both for its rich eclecticism of focus
and its superb flights of poetic language. To me, it
completes and transcends THE BOOK OF THE NEW SUN; in
fact, I've at times thought of it as the best SF novel
ever written. This may seem extravagant praise, but
what other work of SF has come so close to the
intensity of writers like Joyce?
Maybe I'm using the wrong criteria...

1999-11-06 Alice Turner #

Argues that The Urth of the New Sun is radiant and entirely necessary to the series, and wonders why it draws hostility. Agrees with Nicholas Gevers, citing Tzad, Juturna, Apheta and the starship-sail flight descriptions, and repeats the legend that Dave Hartwell requested the book as a wrap-up.
supports · genre-and-craft

I agree here. I have never understood the hostility toward this book. It seems radiant to me, a truly beautiful, immortal book and entirely necessary to the series. Tzad is wonderful, Juturna is wonderful, the connection with Apheta may be strange and even distasteful to some, but there is magic in it. The descriptions of the leaps among the sails of the starship are unrivaled, except by perhaps some of Cordwainer Smith. The legend is that it was written at the express order of Dave Hartwell, who said that the series was too truncated and confusing without a wrap-up. If that is true (and I have my doubts), hooray for Hartwell.

-alga

1999-11-07 Kevin J. Maroney #

Reports David Hartwell's account of how The Urth of the New Sun came to be written: at a dinner, Hartwell and John Douglas suggested showing Severian's Autarch ceremony, and Wolfe proposed a separate novel. Grounds the claim in a private conversation the poster had with Hartwell months earlier, offering firsthand sourcing to a prior post's legend.
informs · author-and-biography

I asked David Hartwell about this a few months ago, and meant to report here. Here's his version:

David, John Douglas (then Hartwell's assistant editor at Timescape) and Wolfe were dining together to discuss _Citadel of the Autarch_, which Wolfe had just delivered to them. Both David and John agreed that the book was a triumph, but that they felt that it really should show, on camera, the ceremony in which Severian became Autarch. Wolfe, in Hartwell's terms, immediately started adding up words like a calculator, and then declared that he'd be delighted to show it, but that it would take an additional 50,000-100,000 words to do justice.

All agreed that adding this much material to _Citadel_ would be problematic, and on the spot they agreed that it should be an additional novel.

So _Urth_ was written at David (and John's) suggestion.
