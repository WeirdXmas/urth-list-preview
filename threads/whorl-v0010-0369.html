Dr. Crane's plot

Whorl list · 4 messages · 2000-06-21 to 2000-06-24

2000whorlThe Book of the Long SunAlice TurnerAlex David GroceJames JordanMichael Straight
The thread: Posters asked whether Silk's prophecies and dreams in the Long Sun books, and the Silk graffiti, were spontaneous or deliberately engineered. Akt argued that Crane's gang ran the graffiti campaign and that the prophecy and dream motifs are standard fiction practice. Michael Straight argued that Pas's fake religion means the fulfilled prophecies need another explanation, and listed the Outsider, superembryo psionic talent, and the sacred windows. Alex David Groce proposed that the Outsider sends Silk signs, contrasting this with Muriel Spark's scientific or metafictional foreseeing, and Jim Jordan extended this with Oreb as guide and baptism as the quickening. The thread moved toward the Outsider as the source without any recorded concession from the other side.
  • akt: Silk graffiti run by Crane's gang, prophecy and dream motifs are standard fiction practice
  • Michael Straight: Long Sun's fake religion means fulfilled prophecies need another explanation such as the Outsider, superembryo talent, or sacred windows
  • Alex David Groce: Wolfe intends the prophecies as signs from the Outsider, not a scientific explanation
  • Jim Jordan: Silk's talents quickened by baptism with the Outsider and guided by Oreb

Themes: Gods, angels and the Increate, Gnosticism, heresy and other theologies, Ghosts, dreams and visions

2000-06-21 Michael Straight #

Argues that in the Long Sun books, unlike most Wolfe works where prophetic dreams are a wink to readers, the fake religion of Pas means the fulfilled prophecies need another explanation. Answers the quoted question about prophecy and dreams by listing possible causes such as the Outsider, superembryo psionic talent, and the sacred windows.
answers · theology-and-religion · genre-and-craft · theories: Prophecies need explanation because Pas religion is fake; Outsider or superembryo talent explains the prophecies; Sacred windows may cause the prophecies to come true

But in most books this is kind of a wink at the reader; we're not supposed to ask why the madman's ravings should forshadow events in the book. In the Long Sun, however, one of the important points is that the religion of Pas, including divination via sacrifice is fake. We're supposed to ask questions about what exactly is going on with the religious stuff, so we need some other explanation for why the prophesies are coming true, e.g. the Outsider, some sort of psionic talent of the superembryos, some sort of influence from the sacred windows, etc.

-Rostrum

2000-06-21 Alex David Groce #

Proposes that Wolfe intends readers to see Silk's prophecies in Long Sun as signs sent by the Outsider rather than a scientific explanation of his talents. Agrees with Rostrum that Pas's religion is fake, and contrasts ordinary fiction's scientific or metafictional explanations of foreseeing, such as Muriel Spark's, with this one.
proposes · theology-and-religion · genre-and-craft · theories: Silk receives signs from the Outsider; Silk's talents might explain the prophecies

Thanks. That's what I was trying to say. Lots of fiction does this, but seldom are the people in the fiction TRYING to forsee the future and managing to do so--when they do, unless the work is genre fantasy, we look for a scientific explanation or a metafictional conceit (Muriel Spark pulls the latter off quite a lot). My hypothesis is that Wolfe wants it to be possible to explain this "scientifically" by assuming Silk's mysterious talents go this far (Silk seems to be the only one actually doing this in Long Sun) or some such, but really to lean us towards the assumption that Silk is being given signs by the Outsider, who clearly doesn't mean "you will be given no help" in quite the way you or I would.

2000-06-24 James Jordan #

Proposes that Silk's mysterious talents in The Book of the Long Sun are quickened by his baptismal encounter with the Outsider and guided by Oreb, a cryptic helper. Builds on Alex David Groce's remark that the Outsider gives Silk signs, citing the Ruler's words to Westwind about preferring to act behind the scenes.
proposes · theology-and-religion · source-and-allusion · theories: Silk's talents are guided by the Outsider; Oreb is a cryptic guide for Silk

My hypothesis is

I suggest the Ruler's words to Westwind in "Westwind," to the effect that He prefers not to interfere openly, but behind the scenes. I suggest that Silk's "mysterious talents" are quickened by his baptismal encounter with the Outsider, and with the arrival of Oreb (both the Holy Spirit and also from Germanic lore) as a kind of cryptic guide. Thus, it's not just Silk's talents, but those talents as guided by the Outsider.
