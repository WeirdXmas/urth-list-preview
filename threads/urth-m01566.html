Honor

Urth list · 6 messages · 2005-11-30 to 2005-12-01

2005urthCliff JudgeJames WynnChris PRoy C. LackeyDavid Duffy
The thread: Readers debated whether Able in The Wizard Knight is an honorable hero or a bully, and whether his defeat by Org was fair. Roy C. Lackey argued Able is a bully whose trial by combat is built into the book, and that Gaynor's innocence did not matter to the outcome. Transentient asked how Org could show loyalty and honor despite being evil, and whether he had made a deal before the fight. James Wynn read Org's win as a parallel to the myth of Antaios, grounded in the fight itself. Chris and transentient agreed that Able loses a fair fight and was not throwing it. David Duffy read Able as a knight acting by the code of an armed warrior elite, comparing him to Bushido and Perez-Reverte. The thread ended on the fight's fairness and Able's conduct, with no clear resolution.
  • Roy C. Lackey: Able is a bully who uses might for petty and heroic ends; the trial by combat is central to the book
  • transentient: Org shows loyalty and honor to Able despite being evil; Able loses a fair fight and the foreign army then joins him
  • James Wynn: Org's win parallels Antaios and Herakles, so it cannot be explained by a lack of soul
  • David Duffy: Able acts exactly as a knight should in a model of armed warrior elites who bully and defend honour violently
  • Chris: Able loses a fair one-on-one fight to the foreign champion, with no sign he threw it

Themes: Justice, torture and mercy, Power, rulers and politics, Myth and folklore sources

2005-11-30 Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that Able in The Wizard Knight is a bully who uses superior might for petty and heroic ends, and that the trial by combat is incorporated into the book. Responds to Roy Lackey's earlier post by citing Able's rationalizations, his magic sword and armor, and the victory he called 'ordained', and by disputing that Gaynor's innocence mattered to the combat.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Able is a jerk who uses might to bully

My point, above, was to establish that "Trial by Combat" was, indeed, incorporated into TWK. See below.

I don't know whether or not Able believed in the doctrine of might makes right. He is a jerk because he repeatedly used his superior might to push weaker people around in pursuance of his own ends, both petty and . . . 'heroic'. I have cited various examples of the petty -- and I haven't exhausted the list. Did he believe it was wrong to bully people? Apparently not. How did he rationalize his behavior, even to himself -- if he thought about it at all? By what "right" did he push people around? By his strong right hand.

Able had no need or use for his opponent's weapons and armor when he made his stands. He already had a magic sword, gilded armor and a one-horned steed from Skai.

"Ordained" was Able's suggestion. In context, I don't see how it can be interpreted as other than predestination.

Uh, no. First of all, Able didn't believe he could be beaten by anyone, and he was right; there was no doubt about the outcome. He was a Hero, not just a knight. Second, Gaynor's "innocence" wasn't really at issue. The precipitating spat between her and Morcaine was a sham, a plan concocted by Earl Marshal Escan, with the complicity of both women (who each had divergent agenda but were in agreement on this one issue), to get Able an audience with Arnthor so he could deliver his Aelf message.

Gaynor was still a virgin, btw, and Morcaine would get her jollies no matter who won the combat.

-Roy

2005-11-30 Cliff Judge replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Asks what readers think of Org's loyalty and honor toward Able, despite Org being intrinsically evil, and whether Org and his companions made a deal before the fight. Cites Org putting his neck under Able's boot, serving Able in the dungeon, and the Knight of the Lions' choice to yield in the pass, responding to Roy C. Lackey's earlier post on Able's defeat.
questions · close-textual-reading · theories: Org and companions made a deal beforehand

So what do you think about Org, and their relationship? Org beat Able soundly, but then Uri, Baki, and Gylf jumped in. Rather than running away, which might have been fine, Org put his neck under Able's boot. Perhaps this was because they had made a deal before wrestling. Despite the fact that Org was intrinsically evil and irredeemable was reinforced throughout the story, he demonstrated a definite loyalty to Able, and even extended that loyalty to whoever Able told him to. To the point of being really hungry at times. Also, even in the dungeon, when Org was apparently very big, and there was no little indication that Uri, Baki, or Gylf would jump in if he wanted to, he continued to serve Able. What do you think of Org's example of loyalty and honor?

Also, Able was defeated other times. Once in the pass, there was a similar turnaround where the Knight who had defeated him (the Knight of the Lions, was it?) made the decision to become the defeated once Able's friends - this time, the dead former weilders of Eterne - showed themselves.

2005-12-01 James Wynn replying to transentient #

Proposes that Org's victory over Able in hand-to-hand combat parallels the myth of Antaios, who could not be beaten while his feet touched the ground, so Herakles lifted and threw him. Grounds the argument in the fight itself and the observation that Able had beaten the giants, so Org's win cannot be explained by a lack of soul.
proposes · myth-and-structure · source-and-allusion · theories: Org's win mirrors Herakles defeating Antaios

That's an excellent point. Org *did* beat Able in hand-to-hand combat. I'm trying to imagine *why*. Able beat the giants, so it isn't because Org had no soul. Org beat Able by picking him up and throwing him. It reminds me of Antaios who could not be beaten in wrestling as long as his feet touched the ground. So Herakles picked him up and threw him.

J

2005-12-01 David Duffy replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that Able in The Wizard Knight acts exactly as a knight should in a model of openly armed warrior elites who bully lower orders and defend honour violently. Grounds this in comparisons to Bushido, machismo in Perez-Reverte's Captain Alatriste, and Heinlein's quip on the code duello, and links Wolfe's view of power to his recent stories.
disputes · author-and-biography · genre-and-craft · theories: Able acts as a knight should

I think he acts *exactly* as a knight "should" act in all these examples. That is, men who *openly* carry weapons push around the lower orders, and defend their honour to the death over the slightest infraction. Peasants bearing arms would be immediately executed in many cultures. That the captain doesn't believe Able a knight is sufficient grounds (in this model) to immediately and literally beat him into submission -- and to kill him if he fails to submit. Force majeure is the lone knight's only source of authority. Furthermore, they may have affectionate relations with particular servants/household members, but they must never forget the gap ("familiarity breeds contempt").

The non-Christian knightly code we see is reminiscent of Bushido (although there is no textual evidence of a direct link), and, having just finished reading Perez-Reverte's "Captain Alatriste", machismo (he comments only the Spanish would duel about not getting a good view at the theatre). Some people approve of Heinlein's quip about politeness when the code duello is in operation.

Wolfe has a rather jaundiced view of human nature in general and of the power relations in past and present societies. He always has power coming from the barrel of a gun, which is relevant to the tone of his recent short stories eg disarming of the US populace appears in a couple in the last collection.

David Duffy.

2005-12-01 Chris P replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Disputes the claim that Able was a hero who could not be beaten, arguing that in The Wizard Knight he loses a fair one-on-one fight to the foreign army's champion. Cites the fight itself and notes no sign Able threw it, unlike his earlier fight with the Leopard Knight, responding to Roy C. Lackey's reading.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Able is beaten fairly in The Wizard Knight

Hold up just a second here. I've seen this claim, and claims like it, multiple times - "Able was a god and couldn't be beaten by anyone", etc. It's not true. Able is beaten fair and square in WIZARD, one on one, by the champion of the foreign army. There is no indication that he was throwing that fight, as he did with the Leopard Knight earlier.
