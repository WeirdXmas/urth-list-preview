2001-05-08 Roy C. Lackey #

Asks why the protagonist of "Copperhead" is called the "new President" when the story's timeline puts him about fifteen months into office, and points out that the dialogue about the next election is internally inconsistent. Lists dated facts from the story (campaign, inauguration, Spring retreat, August UFO crash, autumn Changer, Spring 2002 ending) to show the contradictions.
questions · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading

Anyone else notice the temporal problems within the story? GW is on record as stating that he doesn't like to repeat himself wrt important story information. Yet, the protagonist is repeatedly referred to as the "new President" (hereafter NP). Why? He can't be the "new" President. Such an adjective could or would normally only be used for the first few months in office, yet, on the evening that the story action takes place, he has been in office for about 15 months. If we use the timeframe of the most recent election as a referent, the story yields the following facts:

A) The NP's campaign lasted most or all of the year 2000, culminating in his successful election in November. ("I had been working hard. Not only after my inauguration, but for more than a year before it.")

B) He was inaugurated January, 2001.

C) He and his wife went to their country retreat in the Spring of 2001 for R&R, but returned to Washington after "eighteen hours".

D) August, 2001, a UFO crashed.

E) Autumn, 2001, ("Fall came. Football season.") the NP receives the Changer and the naked woman appears.

F) A murder and suicide follow.

G) It is Spring, 2002. ("She rose again and went to the window, staring out into the spring night.")

The NP had this exchange with George, earlier that same evening:

"I'm sure you're right, Mr. President."

"I know I am. Fortunately we've got three years until the next election."

"Longer than that, Mr. President."

"Nearly three years until the next campaign." Although no one was watching, the new President made himself smile. Smiles showed in your voice. "That's what matters."

Now, the next election will be in the Autumn of 2004, only about 2 1/2 years in the future, and campaigning begins long before the election, so the next presidential campaign will begin, at most, two years from the day the story takes place, and definitely not "longer" than the three years mentioned above. What's going on here?

-Roy

2001-05-08 Jeff Wilson #

Suggests that the temporal discrepancy Roy Lackey noticed in the story's dialogue about the next election implies an extension of the president's term. Proposes that the Changer could be keeping the president in power without reelection; the poster admits not having read the story and relies only on the quoted exchange.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: The Changer extends the president's term without reelection

I haven't read the story, but it seems clear to me that an extension of the president's current term is being implied. The Changer could be suggesting that he can keep the president in power without need of reelection.
