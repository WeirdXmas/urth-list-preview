2009-03-31 Adam Thornton #

Shares that a loved one's medical treatment has left them unable to form long-term memories, calling the experience fascinating but hard to live with. Connects this personal situation to Latro's memory loss in the Soldier books, saying he looks forward to rereading them once life normalizes; no argument about the text is made.
informs

So, someone important in my life is undergoing a medical treatment that, among its side effects, includes the inability to convert short- term memories to long-term.

(This, by the way, may go some way towards explaining, if not excusing, my current truculence, which Brunians correctly noted; my fuse is very short right now.)

It is *fascinating*. It is also hard as hell to deal with day-to-day (in some ways harder than the person who can't remember anything), but once this phase of my life is over and normality has reasserted itself, I really look forward to re-reading the _Soldier_ books.

Adam

2009-04-01 Fred Kiesche replying to Adam Thornton #

Shares a personal account of a father whose short- and long-term memories are blurred, with reality mixed into fantasy, and jokes about his refusal to be corrected. Replies to Adam Thornton's post about a loved one's memory-impairing treatment, and his plan to reread the Latro books afterward.
chats

My father has gotten to the point where short-term and long-term memories are completely intermixed. ?
Reality is intermixed with fantasy. You never know what he is going to say. And forget trying to correct him; he gets very angry when you point out, for example, that he isn't the CEO of A.T. & T.

F.P. Kiesche III
