Borski's Lime Theory and all

Urth list · 1 messages · 2002-09-03 to 2002-09-03

2002urthThe Book of the Long SunJames Wynn

Themes: Hidden identities and parentage

2002-09-03 James Wynn #

Reports Crush's rejection of Borski's Lime Theory, including the joke that Lime might be short for Limestone, since lime is calcium oxide and limestone is calcium carbonate. Crush also disputes the idea that Newt is Hyacinth's father, citing the text's shift from regular clerk to head clerk when Silk visits, which would require an implausible promotion past several ranks at the Fisc.
disputes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Borski's Lime Theory; Lime is short for Limestone; Newt is Hyacinth's father

Bonus gratuity: although I don't belief this is true, in order to make your theory work, you might try arguing that "Lime" is short for "Limestone."

Crush answers:
Are you kidding?? Lime isn't short for anything!! It's calcium oxide. Limestone is calcium carbonate. This theory fits so well with my own (regarding Hyacinth), so in line with the way I read Wolfe's clues, and so unlikely to be coincidental that I'm inclined to contact Wolfe and try to convince him to take his answer back.

Alas, unless and until he does there's no way to get the Lime Theory off the ground. Our situation is similar to that of an orthodox Christian if God were to say, "Trinity? I'm sorry...what was that again?"

As for Newt as her father. The text names him as a *regular* clerk when Hyacinth is a child and a *head* clerk (working under "officers") when Silk goes to see him. You are suggesting that Silk has promoted someone whom he knows only thru his wife as "a horse's arse" past several ranks at the Fisc to control the purse of the most important governmental body in Viron. I can't see it.

-- Crush
