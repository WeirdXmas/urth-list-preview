Themes: Power, rulers and politics

2009-09-08 James Wynn #

Reports a remark Gene Wolfe made at Dragon*Con about his political views, in which he distinguishes democracy from the American republic and condemns the Spartan ephors and their treatment of the Helots. The poster notes that Wolfe tied this to Herodotus and to his own story Soldier of Arete, and that he discussed the relative freedom of Spartan women.

I just remembered this.

Somehow a questioner addressed Wolfe's politics.

"The form of government I favor is Democracy, which means "Rule by the People".
America does not have a democracy. It has a Republic. That means that every so often we elect people to rule over us. And once we have, you can believe they *know* it.
We are endanger, I think, of becoming eventually ruled by the Supreme Court which would be the worst of all governments. That is essentially the sort of government the Spartans had. They elected Ephors to rule over them. "Ephor" means judges. The Spartans made the Nazis look like boy scouts."

<Then he described the yearly declaration of war against the Helots declared by the ephors. And described the plot against the Helots that helped in the war against Persia. (See Herodutos' "History" and Wolfe's "Soldier of Arete".) He also went into the fact that Spartan women were freer than those of any other Greeks and explained why.>
