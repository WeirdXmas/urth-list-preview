"Another ancient greek of the same name"

Urth list · 3 messages · 1999-06-23 to 1999-06-24

1999urthThe Book of the New SunRoy C. LackeyPeter StephensonDave Lebling
The thread: Readers debated whether Jonas's line 'On Urth' in the Citadel is a homophone slip, with Severian mishearing the planet's name as the home world. David Lebling proposed that Jonas only realizes he is on Earth when he says so, having learned the name from glyphs on mirror-teleporters, and Roy Lackey independently offered the same homophone reading. Peter Stephenson agreed with Lackey, calling it the most plausible explanation and noting that Severian's errors make the narrator fallible. The thread ended in apparent agreement among the three posters, with no dissent recorded.
  • David_Lebling: Jonas learns the planet's name from mirror-teleporter glyphs and only realizes he is on Earth when he says it; Urth/Earth homophones confuse Seve
  • Roy C. Lackey: 'On Urth' is a homophone confusion, Severian mishears the planet's name as the home world, implying glyph reading on mirror-teleporters
  • Peter Stephenson: agrees with Lackey that it is the most plausible explanation; Severian's mistakes show the narrator is fallible despite Thackeray's omniscient

Themes: Unreliable narration, Names and their meanings

1999-06-23 Dave Lebling #

Proposes that Jonas only realizes he is on Urth, which is Earth, at the moment he says so, having learned the place from glyphs read off mirror-teleporters. Responds to Roy C. Lackey's quoted passage by imagining Jonas's speech software with separate slots for 'the planet I'm on' and 'the home world', so the homophones Urth and Earth confuse Severian.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Jonas realizes he is on Earth at that moment; Jonas learns location from glyphs on mirror-teleporters; Speech software confuses Urth and Earth homophones

I've always taken this to mean that only at that moment does Jonas realize that he's on Earth, rather than some very odd colony world. His knowledge is obviously based on reading the odd glyphs (there must be mirror-teleporters all over the galaxy).

Perhaps this is too simplistic a reading, but I have a mental picture of the software that does his speech having a slot for "the planet I'm on = 'Urth'", "the home world = 'Earth'"; but of course, the two are homophones, so Severian is a bit confused when Jonas says "On <the home world>."

Dave Lebling
(aka vizcacha)

1999-06-24 Peter Stephenson #

Agrees with Roy Lackey's reading that Jonas's odd phrase 'On Earth' confuses Urth and Earth, calling it the most plausible explanation for the loose end. Notes this is a mistake Severian often makes, so the narrator is fallible even though Thackeray insisted the narrator is omniscient.
supports · close-textual-reading · theories: Jonas's 'On Earth' confuses Urth with Earth

This was going through my mind, too, and it seems to tie up the loose end in the most plausible. It's the sort of mistake Severian makes a lot. Nice way of illustrating that even if (as Thackeray kept insisting) the narrator is omniscient, he still doesn't know everything :-).
