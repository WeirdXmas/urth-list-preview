Themes: Christ figures and messiahs, Genre: science fiction, fantasy, both, Ghosts, dreams and visions

1997-08-14 Peter Cash #

Suggests that Cutthroat in the story "Tracking Song" may be a Christ-figure who descends into the fallen world to suffer and die into new life. Grounds this in the sleigh's impossible journey, arguing that it can only be a death-bed hallucination if read as science fiction but a divine or angelic vehicle if read as allegory.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · genre-and-craft · theories: Cutthroat is a Christ-figure in Tracking Song; The sleigh is a divine or angelic vehicle

Alice Turner <[email]> semaphored with her cilia:

You have a PROBLEM with chems? And that's SERGEANT Rock to you, lady!

Yes! I think you're definitely going in the right direction here; this helps me. Could it be (dare I bring this up? Is this perhaps what you were thinking? ) that Cutthroat is a christ-figure? He's been cast down into the fallen world, to share the suffering of its inhabitants--and ultimately to die into a new life. Too pat, probably.

If one reads this strictly as a science fiction story, then the sleigh is a death-bed hallucination. It can't be where it is--it can't have circumnavigated (do sleighs navigate?) the world in the time available. Thus, it has to be an illusion. But if this is a religious, allegorical story, then the sleigh is something more: it's a divine or angelic vehicle, come to carry Cutthroat home.

I have to resist the constant temptation to read Wolfe's stories as only what they superficially appear to be: science fiction tales.

Sgt. Rock

1997-08-15 James Jordan #

Clarifies that the poster had already offered Christ-figure suggestions in an earlier post, arguing that for Wolfe all Christ figures are really Christian figures living in imitation of Christ. Responds to a quoted suggestion that Cutthroat is a Christ-figure by pointing back to the poster's own prior contribution and offering to resend it.
disputes · theology-and-religion · theories: Wolfe's Christ figures are really Christian figures

Just for the record, I did post a some Christ-figure suggestions in one of my posts. Remember, that for Wolfe, Christians are living in imitation of Christ, so all of Wolfe's "Christ figures" are really "Christian figures."
