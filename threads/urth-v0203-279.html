Irritating Loonies and Planet of the Grapes

Urth list · 5 messages · 2002-03-04 to 2002-03-10

2002urthThe Book of the New SunJeff WilsonDan'l Danehy-OakesAndy Robertson
The thread: Dan'l Danehy-Oakes and Jeff Wilson debated whether the Urth characters' loss of technology reflects ignorance or lack of resources, and whether the red sun seen in the Bluvian sky can be explained by a spacewarp. Danehy-Oakes argued that resource shortage explains the lost technology, that a spacewarp would make Wolfe incompetent, and that Urth characters lack technical vocabulary because technology is familiar to them. Wilson answered that the doubled sun is a deliberate storytelling device, backed by other doublings in the books. Danehy-Oakes then questioned whether hierodules built the spacewarp and asked for concrete evidence. The thread ended without resolution, with the two sides still disagreeing.
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: lost technology stems from lack of resources, and a spacewarp explanation would make Wolfe incompetent
  • Jeff Wilson: the doubled sun is a deliberate storytelling device, since the book's science is loose throughout
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: Urth characters lack technical vocabulary because technology is familiar, and hierodules are doubtful builders of the spacewarp

Themes: Time loops and causality, The dying sun and the cosmos, Books, libraries and writing

2002-03-04 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that the Urth people's loss of technology stems from lack of resources rather than ignorance, citing Severian's knowledge of the Citadel's Towers and the House Absolute's technical level. Rejects the claim that a spacetime warp could explain the red sun visible in the Bluvian sky, saying such a device would make Wolfe an incompetent author. Responds to Jeff Wilson's remark that Rudesind's society could not cover the moon with forest.

2002-03-05 Jeff Wilson #

Argues that the Urth of the New Sun's doubled sun, seen through a spacewarp, is a deliberate storytelling device rather than a scientific error, since the book's science is loose throughout. Grounds this by comparing it to other doublings in the books (Burgundofara, Severian and Apu Punchau, the Tzadkiels, the Gardens) and to the magic mirrors, flyers and white fountain, answering a critic who finds the hierodule's spacewarp technically flawed.
disputes · close-textual-reading · genre-and-craft · theories: Doubled sun is spacewarp, not a scientific error

They may not be completely ignorant, but it is plain that they parrot a lot of the lost teaching without really understanding it; they have some general idea, but the details are long lost. They know a kind of lightning makes some machines go, but not how to construct a circuit. They know that the Urth rotates, but not that it revolves around the sun. They know that some statues are actually some sort of exalted taxidermy, but nothing else about it. It's no stretch to presume that irrigation is a similar summarization of terraforming.

not necessary later, it might be earlier

The curvature of space bends the path of light just as it does the path of material objects, so the spacewarp =can= explain the Sun being visible as a distant star from Urth when looking back along the path that the Whorl took. This same effect shows up in URTH with Yesod and the ship, and to a lesser degree with the magic mirrors. This doubling of the Sun is no more a mark of incompetence than the doubling of Burgundofara, or Severian and Apu Punchau, or the various Tzadkiels are, or the bilocation of the various Gardens.

If there are a few scientific details separating the hierodule's spacewarp from what you would expect from a proper closed timelike path in spacetime, I can only point to similar technical shortcomings in the description of the function of the magic mirrors, the lifting principle of the flyers, and the White Fountain's white hole nature, among others. GW doesn't lets science get in the way of telling a story.
