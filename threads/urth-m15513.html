The thread: Lee quoted a post tying Wolfe's Whorl to the Nephilim of Genesis 6, and the thread debated whether that reading holds. Jerry Friedman argued the Bible does not clearly establish the Nephilim account, citing Wolfe's Castle of the Otter essay and Jubilees. Andrew Mason disputed that the inhumi fathered Nephilim, saying inhumi need not be late arrivals and that devils may be mythic figures or people, and that inhumi are too different to breed with humans. James Wynn then proposed that the inhumi or Neighbors influenced Typhon's launch of the Whorl through dream travel to an earlier time, answering Mason's doubts with time travel and mythic parallels. The thread ends with the interpretations still apart, and it is mainly a dispute over Book of the New Sun canon.
  • Jerry Friedman: the Bible does not clearly imply the Nephilim account, and Nephilim meaning is unsettled
  • Andrew Mason: inhumi were not necessarily late arrivals and do not father Nephilim; devils may be mythic or people
  • James Wynn: inhumi or Neighbors influenced Typhon's launch of the Whorl by dream travel to an earlier time

Themes: Myth and folklore sources, Time loops and causality, Christ figures and messiahs

Argues that the Bible does not clearly imply the Nephilim account in Lee's quoted post, since Genesis 6:1-4 has no agreed meaning for Nephilim, their size, or their moral status. Cites Wolfe's reading of the Nephilim in Castle of the Otter, the Cain-to-Beowulf-to-Crichton-to-Neandertal link, and Jubilees' claim they survived the flood, ending with a joke about who can live underwater.

2010-11-27 Andrew Mason #

Argues that the inhumi were not necessarily late arrivals on the Whorl and that devils may be mythic figures or people like Mucor, rejecting the idea that inhumi fathered Nephilim. Grounds this in a recollected interview, the Short Sun explanation of inhumi origins, Wolfe's essay 'Castle of the Otter' on the Nephilim, and the claim that inhumi are reptiles too different to breed with humans.
disputes · theology-and-religion · source-and-allusion · theories: Inhumi were late arrivals on the Whorl; Devils are figures of myth; Nephilim were children of human women and non-rational hominids; Inhumi cannot breed with humans

I had thought Wolfe had said in an interview that the inhumi were late arrivals on the Whorl. Having checked it, I think that strictly speaking he only said that Quetzal was, which I suppose allows other inhumi to have been around earlier. (But does not _Short Sun_ contain an explanation of how they got on to the Whorl?)

There may, I suppose, have been inhumi on Urth which did not get on to the Whorl. The fact they aren't apparent in _New Sun_ doesn't disprove this; there are no doubt many things on Urth that Severian isn't aware of,

But I agree with Gerry Quinn that what we are told of devils doesn't really fit inhumi very closely. I would think one of two things more likely. Perhaps devils are just figures of myth (as far as the compliers knew, at least), mentioned in some of the historic texts used to make up the Chrasmologic writings. Or perhaps they are people like Mucor. If she was geneticlly enhanced to produce her abilities, it seems likely that she was not the first person ever to have them - there would heve been such people on Urth, though not on the Whorl until the special embryos started appearing on the market. The Bible implies that

Wolfe, however, reads the Nephilim differently; he explains this in 'Castle of the Otter'. He thinks they were the children of human women with non-rational hominid fathers (who, being non-rational, were incapable of sin and so could be called 'sons of God'). If anything in the foreground story of _New Sun_ relates to this, I would think it's the man-beast hybrids.

I find it most improbable that inhumi can breed with humans. Perhaps some aliens can (there's a passage in _Urth_ which illuminates this, though somewhat obscurely). But inhumi are reptiles; the difference seems too great.

2010-11-29 James Wynn replying to Andrew Mason #

Proposes that the inhumi or Neighbors influenced Typhon's decision to launch the Whorl by dream travel to a time before the launch. Answers Andrew Mason's doubt about inhumi arrival by citing the Rajan's time travel, the Midas-Dionysus parallels, the daemon definition, and the idea that a demon imitating a god becomes one.
proposes · myth-and-structure · theology-and-religion · theories: Inhumi influenced Typhon to launch the Whorl; Neighbors reached Urth by dream travel before launch; Inhumi are Horn's son and Silk's sister

It would be very fitting indeed for inhumi or Neighbors to have had a hand in Typhon's decision to launch the Whorl.
http://urth.org/whorlmap/midas.htm
(There are a lot of arguable associations between Typhon to Midas and the inhumi to Dionysus in the Long/Short cycle. )

Since the Rajan can travel through Time --and since the Rajan can choose to go back in Time and insert himself into the grandmother's story because he recognized himself in it-- it is quite possible for the Neighbors to reach Urth through dream travel at a Time before the Whorl's launch and influence Typhon to launch it.

I say, this is all POSSIBLE -- not stated in the story-- although it could be seen as implied thematically.

A daemon, note the spelling, is merely a disembodied spirit. Inhumi contain the spirits of others, living and dead. Mucor, at times, meets this criteria. Also, an important theme is in the book is that if a demon imitates a god, he becomes, in some sense, a god. Quetzal becomes a holy man, sacrificing his life for his flock. Horn becomes Silk. Inhumi become, in a metaphysical sense, Horn's son and Silk's sister.
