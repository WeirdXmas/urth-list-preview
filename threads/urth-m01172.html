The thread: A newcomer asked why Michael wants Able rather than a stronger Overcyn, who stabbed Gilling in The Wizard Knight, and whether Able's falls after giving blood echo Christ's journey. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes answered with an Arthurian reading of Able, built on the signature "Art Ormsby" and a claimed etymology, and offered a view on the stabbing. James Wynn disputed the framing, arguing Able was not the same Able Michael first sought and so already outranked Valfather, and cited the text's denial that Baki stabbed Gilling plus a Catholic tradition about Christ's falls. The thread ended with the three positions standing side by side and no resolution.

  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: Able is King Arthur in another form, resting on the Ormsby signature and etymology
  • James Wynn: Able already outranked Valfather, and the text rules out Baki as the stabber

Asks why Michael wants Able rather than a wiser, stronger Overcyn as reinforcement, asks whether Baki or Garvaon stabbed Gilling in The Wizard Knight, and asks whether Able's two falls after giving blood to Disiri echo Christ's pre-crucifixion journey. Responds to no one in particular, as a newcomer's first post.
questions · close-textual-reading · allegory-and-typology · theories: Able's falls echo Christ's pre-crucifixion journey

Hello all,

I've recently joined this organization and have been
reading through the older often excellent posts. I've
been a Wolfe reader for the last year or so, since
picking up BotNS and have just finished the Wizard
Knight. My impression - Wolfe can do no wrong.

To be succint, my questions:

1) Since the good guys are outnumbered and possibly
outgunned, I recognize the need for reserves from
lower dimensions. But why does Michael want Able?
Wouldn't he be better satisfied with a presumably
wiser and strong Overcyn?

2) I came out with the impression that Baki did indeed
stab Gilling the first time (like Uri said) and that
Garvaon finished the job the second time. Am I wrong?

3) I'm not of a Christian background (Hindu rather)
and I was wondering if I was correct in assuming that
Able falling down twice after giving blood to Disiri
refers to the pre-crucifiction trip Christ made.

Thank for your time.

2005-06-09 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes replying to thapaguru #

Argues that Able is King Arthur in another form, citing the closing signature "Art Ormsby" and claiming Ormsby means "of the worm," making him a version of Arthur Pendragon. Responds to thapaguru's question about why Michael wants Able, and also gives a view on who stabbed Gilling, while noting Christian imagery in Wolfe's work.
proposes · puzzle-solving · source-and-allusion · theories: Able is King Arthur in another form; Art Ormsby means of the worm Pendragon

Hi, and welcome to the mailinglist.

Able seems to be King Arthur in some form. There are
clues to this all throughout, but the clincher is the last two words of the story - he signs the lengthy letter (that is the novel) by his Earth name, "Art Ormsby." "Ormsby"
can be translated "of the worm." Thus, his name is a
form of "Arthur Pendragon."

I'm sure that Baki did stab Gilling; I'm not as sure about Garvaon.

That would certainly be one possible interpretation. Wolfe definitely uses a lot of Christian imagery in his novels, most blatantly (in my opinion) in the eucharistic scenes in the Book of the Short Sun.

--Dan'l

2005-06-09 James Wynn replying to thapaguru #

Argues that Able was not the same Able Michael sought at first, so the knight who finished the story already outranked Valfather as an Overcyn. Responds to thapaguru's doubts about who stabbed Gilling by citing the text's denial that Baki did it, Able's reluctance to name the assailant, and a Catholic tradition about Christ's falls.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theology-and-religion · theories: Able became greater Overcyn than Valfather; Garvaon stabbed Gilling the second time

Welcome, Thapaguru!

Well, Able is an Overcyn, right? Are you saying "why not Valfather?" Well, I gathered that Michael reckons things 4th dimensionally differently from people of the other levels. After his first meeting with Able, he tells him that he is going to search for "that great and famous knight, Sir Able of the High Heart". The meaning is: The Able at that point in the book was not the Able Michael sought.

Able himself seems to see things in this way. He recognizes that he became a knight by wanting to be one, taking the title, bearing the responsibilities. Only later did he receive the accolade from Disiri and do knightly deeds.

I'd say that the fact that Michael sought Able out meant that at the end of the story he was already a greater Overcyn than Valfather.

IIRC The text explicitly denies that Baki stabbed Gilling. But then again, frequently I also accept Wolfe's textual denials as confirmations, so I'm not gainsaying it. I am definite that Garvaon did the deed the second time (I presume the first) since Able is unwilling name the assailant to protect the honor of his name. I don't think he would worry about that for Baki.

Hmmm I didn't even think of this. According to Catholic tradition, Christ fell THREE times on the Via Dolorosa. Maybe you missed one? (I don't have access to my books)
