Father Inire

Urth list · 8 messages · 2011-12-03 to 2011-12-04

2011urthThe Book of the New SunLee BermanAntónio Pedro MarquesGerry QuinnDavid StockhoffJeff Wilson
The thread: Readers debated whether Rudesind is tall or short and possibly Father Inire in disguise, and whether the waitress Jolenta is a former witch and possibly Severa. Jeff Wilson argued from the ladder and stool proportions that Rudesind is sitting and tall, while Lee Berman argued he is short and may be Inire. On Jolenta, Lee Berman argued she was a witch, while Gerry Quinn, David Stockhoff, and entonio argued from the word carnifex and Palaemon's remark about the Nessus chapter that she is not a witch, and Stockhoff noted the witch argument underlies both identifications. Stockhoff also offered a Celtic head-cult reading of the Pelerines. The thread ended without agreement, with the witch question left open.
  • Lee Berman: Rudesind is short and may be Father Inire in disguise; Jolenta may be Severa
  • Jeff Wilson: Rudesind is sitting, not standing, and tall, given the ladder and stool evidence
  • David Stockhoff: Jolenta may not be a witch; the Pelerines and Katherine ritual reflect Celtic head-cult beliefs
  • Gerry Quinn: Jolenta is not a former witch, since she calls Severian carnifex
  • entonio: Jolenta still sees Severian as a torturer, so the carnifex usage does not settle she is not a witch

Themes: Unreliable narration, Hidden identities and parentage, Masks, disguise and shapeshifting

2011-12-03 David Stockhoff replying to Lee Berman #

Proposes that the Katherine ritual parallels the three serious events of life, birth, sex and death, mapped onto the witches, beast handlers and Seekers, and links the Pelerines and decapitation to Celtic head-cult beliefs. Grounds this in Celtic burial customs, the Pelerines' namesakes, and Jolenta's line that one of them is a carnifex, then asks about Jolenta's coloring before Talos transformed her.
proposes · theology-and-religion · source-and-allusion · theories: Katherine ritual parallels the three serious events of life; Jolenta was a carnifex before Talos transformed her

Symmetry and the centrality of procreation does suggest that there may be some related aspect to the Katherine ritual. But birth vs death seems more likely, doesn't it?

Birth = Mag and the witches, sex/marriage = man's animal nature and the beast handlers (I don't think their patron saint is mentioned, rather Mithraism is suggested), death = Katherine and the Seekers. These are the three Serious Events of human life. It seems like all Urth's core historic religious folklore is preserved in the Citadel.

Also, in the Celtic belief system (I hesitate to call it religion), the head was the seat of the spirit. In some places, heads were apparently detached and kept for longer than the body, which was buried quickly; heads were buried weeks later, sometime with the body, sometimes higher than the body. So decapitation is a strong symbol of the separation of body and spirit/life and death and thus possibly salvation from materiality.

Beheading also seems to be a link with the Pelerines, since some but not all of their namesakes were beheaded, although that may only apply to those who leave the order, such as Catherine and Cyriaca (Mannea was beheaded, however). I don't recall whether they take vows of celibacy (like the virgin martrys) but it would make sense; they are a female counterpart to the Seekers, as are the witches.

I doubt witches leave the Tower more easily than the Torturers. But that is a solid point: anyone who recognizes a carnifex so far from the Citadel might have had some history near the Citadel, and we don't know what she used to be a month earlier. The soldiers thought Severian was in carnifex costume, as did Agia, but Jolenta said "One of you is a carnifex."

Any idea what her coloring was before Talos transformed her?

2011-12-03 David Stockhoff replying to Gerry Quinn #

Argues that the waitress Jolenta may not be a witch, since a witch recognising a torturer would likely have said so openly and the matter never came up later. Responds to Gerry Quinn's question about whether Jolenta left a witch's guild, weighing the lack of any cryptic or odd exchange between her and Severian.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Jolenta is Severa

Good eye.

But a witch who recognizes a torturer outside the Citadel might not say so; if Jolenta is some kind of escaped (or even just a failed) witch, she may not want to reveal too much knowledge to strangers that she would then have to explain. Severian would certainly have noticed had she said "torturer" (or Seeker) vs "carnifex."

You'd think it would have come up later, however. Even tangentially or cryptically. I don't recall any odd conversations between them. Maybe she is just poor and comes from an older part of the city where the Citadel is not just a legend.

Argues that the case for Jolenta being Severa depends on Severa having been a witch, so arguing from that premise already assumes Merryn is the prime candidate for Severa. disputes · puzzle-solving · theories: Jolenta is Severa; Merryn is Severa; Severa was a witch

It occurs to me that if we can argue Jolenta is Severa *because she was a witch*, we are already accepting that Merryn is (the prime candidate for) Severa.

Why? Because both arguments rely on the assumption that Severa is a witch.

2011-12-03 Jeff Wilson replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that Rudesind in the Father Inire episode is sitting, not standing, since Severian writes that he was sitting and the revision concerns only the seat, a stepladder smaller than a high stool. Reasons from ladder and stool proportions that a shorter-than-usual ladder means he is cleaning low canvas and that a tall man would be given such work to avoid stooping.
disputes · close-textual-reading

That's correct, but Sev affirmitively writes that he was sitting. the revision is in what he is sitting on, but not that he is sitting at all. Stepladders are mostly higher than high stools, so in order to maintain similar appearnces, the person on the stepladder needs to be taller than average in similar proportion.

If Rudesind is sitting on a stepladder smaller than a high stool, that indicates to me that he is trying to clean an area of canvas lower down than usual, and is doing so to avoid stooping. this also is sometihng that a taller person than average would be given to do since they have all the farther to stoop.
