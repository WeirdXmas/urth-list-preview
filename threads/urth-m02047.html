Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued the name means shadowless and links the Ascians to Erebus and Tartaros, favoring a South American Commonwealth and noting Nessus stands where Byzantium once was. The thread ended with the interview answer settling the geography question.

2006-01-30 Jeffrey Lefstin #

Asks whether Ascian is meant to evoke Asian, arguing that the Ascians seem to come from North America while the Commonwealth sits in South America. Grounds the question in the 'waist of the world' geography, the Lexicon Urthus discussion of explicit Asian references, and a recollection that Wolfe claimed a classical origin for the word.
questions · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Ascians are a North American people

On whether "Ascian" is supposed to invoke "Asian":

While no doubt Wolfe's ideas of a totalitarian state were influenced by Maoist China and North Korea, I have always wondered what to make of the fact that the Ascians seem to come from NORTH AMERICA.

The Commonwealth, as I am sure has been discussed before, seems unquestionably placed in South America. The Ascians come from the continent to the north beyond the equator ("the waist of the world.). Barring some serious continental drift, that puts them right here in the good old U.S. of A!

Besides, there are explicit Asian references in several parts of the book (see discussion in Lexicon Urthus) so implying one seems superfluous. Didn't Wolfe claim a classical origin for the word Ascian?

2006-01-30 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes replying to James Wynn #

Argues that the Ascians' name, meaning shadowless, ties to Erebus and the darkness of Tartaros, and adds that Commonwealthers mistakenly thought they came from equatorial lands. Extends the discussion by favoring a South American location for the Commonwealth while noting Alga's case that Nessus sits where Byzantium once stood.
supports · source-and-allusion · theories: Ascian name means shadowless; Commonwealth lies in South America; Nessus stands on former Byzantium

What James said: plus, the idea that the Commonwealthers thought that they came from equatorial lands, where one would cast "no shadow" at noontime, when in fact they came from northaway beyond the Equator.

Whether the Commonwealth is actually in South America has been the subject of semi-heated discussion. While I favor the preponderance of evidence that suggests it is, Alga has made a very plausible case that -- presumably after considerable continental drift (which has since stopped as the centre of the Urth solidified) -- Nessus stands on the former site of Byzantium.

2006-01-31 mournings glory replying to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Quotes Wolfe's interview remarks that the Ascians were meant as a Turkish equivalent and that the setting is really South America, with Byzantium as the historical parallel. Relays the interview to answer Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's and Alga's debate about where the Commonwealth and Nessus sit on Urth.
informs · author-and-biography · theories: Ascians are North Americans invading South America

"Insofar as I was trying to create any parallels with an actual historical period here on Earth--and obviously I wasn't aiming at developing an exact analogy--I was thinking of Byzantium. Incidentally, I also got into troiuble with some reviewers over my presentation of the Ascians, who were my equivalent of the Turks. If you read the book carefully, it's clear that the action takes place in South America and that the invading Ascians are actually North Americans."
