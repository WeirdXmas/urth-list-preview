2001-06-28 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Shares the poster's mixed reaction to the Gormenghast TV adaptation, finding it entertaining despite having abandoned the Titus Groan book twelve years earlier. Replies to bee and alga, suggesting that commercial interruptions, not the material, may have shaped his reaction, as with a Martin Short Alice in Wonderland; this is social talk, not about Wolfe.
chats

And alga, she found the show Ghastly, unwatchable.

Ha-ha! I found the book unreadable twelve years ago, abandoning it soon after the library fire; yet I found the tv show (first half, so far) quite entertaining, at times I laughed out loud.

I was hesitant to watch it at all, since I didn't like the book, but I also remembered how I didn't like the most-recent, live-action ALICE IN WONDERLAND (with Martin Short as Mad Hatter) when it first ran on tv, yet I liked it when I saw the thing on video . . . which made me suspect that the commercial interruptions were what was really bothering me the most.

Different people, different tastes--as usual.

=mantis=

2001-06-28 Alex David Groce #

Encourages a reader who abandoned Peake's Gormenghast to try again, recalling that interest rose after the library fire and praising the fight scene in Titus Groan. Off-topic from Wolfe: it is a casual recommendation about another novelist's books, with the poster saying they took longer to read than usual.
chats

Hmmm... You might want to give it another try--it's been five years now, but I recall my interest rising considerably at some point after the fire. _Titus Groan_ has the considerable virtue of containing the best "now it's personal" fight scene in all of fantasy literature. I liked both _Titus Groan_ and _Gormenghast_ and still took considerably longer to get through them that I do most books of that length--I had to mix them with other reading to avoid overdosing on whatever it is about Peake...
