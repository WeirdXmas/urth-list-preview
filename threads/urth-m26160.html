VanderMeer's Southern Reach trilogy

Urth list · 5 messages · 2014-09-07 to 2014-09-08

2014urthAllan AndersonPiotr SzczęsnyAntonin ScriabinFred Kiesche
The thread: Allan Anderson asked the Urth list whether anyone had discussed VanderMeer's Southern Reach trilogy, whether its three novels came out the same year, and whether they form one novel in parts. Fred Kiesche answered that all three appeared in 2014, called the first book an interesting homage to Poe or van Vogt, and reported a planned SF Signal podcast group read that was delayed. Piotr Szczęsny chatted that he liked the first book, had preordered the others, and praised other VanderMeer works. The thread stayed off-topic for Wolfe and ended with no argument or disagreement, only social exchange.
  • Allan Anderson: asks whether the Southern Reach trilogy is one novel split into parts

  • Piotr Szczęsny: liked the first Southern Reach book and has preordered the rest

Themes: Ghosts, dreams and visions

2014-09-07 Allan Anderson #

Asks whether the list has discussed Jeff VanderMeer's Southern Reach trilogy, whether its three novels appeared the same year, and whether they form one large novel split into shorter parts. Mentions enjoying City of Saints and Madmen and having read nothing else by VanderMeer. The post is off-topic for Wolfe, a social query about another author's work.
questions

Hey, did I miss folks talking about Jeff VanderMeer's latest? Did all three novels really come out this year? Does that mean it's really one great honking novel chopped up into "SF" sized pieces (hmm, that sounds familiar.)

I really enjoyed _City of Saints and Madmen_, but haven't read anything more by him. I'd love to hear what people think.

2014-09-08 Fred Kiesche replying to Allan Anderson #

Reports that all three VanderMeer Southern Reach novels appeared in 2014 and says the poster has read the first, finding it interesting as a homage to Poe or van Vogt with ambiguous reality. Replies to Allan Anderson's question about whether the trilogy is one novel split into parts, and notes the poster plans to read the other two over a long weekend.
answers

All three were out this year. I've read the first and found it quite interesting, a homage to Poe or even van Vogt (how much going on is real or a dream?). I'm saving the other two up for a long weekend (they are relatively short, so I can see reading the other two in three days or so).

2014-09-08 Fred Kiesche replying to Piotr Szczęsny #

Announces that the poster, a podcast participant, suggested the first Southern Reach novel by VanderMeer for a group read on the SF Signal podcast, and that the planned discussion was delayed. Responds to Piotr Szczęsny's praise of the trilogy and his plan to read the next volumes soon, with the news of a possible October episode.
informs

I am part of the (now Hugo Award winning) SF Signal Podcast at SF Signal Dot Com and on the last episode I particiapted in I was asked to suggest a book for a "group read" and suggested the first of the Southern Reach trilogy by VanderMeer.

(Alas, the plan was then sideswiped by real life when our host had family issues hit him but maybe in October?)

So keep an eye out there, at some point we'll all get together and talk about the book for an episode.

2014-09-08 Piotr Szczęsny replying to Fred Kiesche #

Off-topic social reply saying the poster liked the first Southern Reach book and has preordered the other two, plus praise for VanderMeer's City, Dradin in Love and Shriek. chats

I've also read the first one and liked it very much (instantly preordered the other two). Authority and Acceptance are waiting for me back home so I think I , just like Fred, read them soon. This is kind of a Vandermeer year for me, City was something different but most of the "stories" were enjoyable (Dradin in Love is still my favourite) but Shriek was great, loved the double narration.
