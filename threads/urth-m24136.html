2013-08-19 Brian Doherty #

Remarks that the poster was unaware of any significant critical backlash against The Book of the New Sun, which he thought was a classic from the start, and says he prefers The Book of the Short Sun from memory. He adds that he loved the first series while reading it without knowing key facts revealed later in commentary.
chats

I did not realize there had been any significant critical backlash against NEW SUN---always thought it was considered a classic right out of the gate by "considered opinion" and retained that status. (At this point I say I prefer SHORT SUN of the Suns, but that is based on memory more than judgement. And I was one of those who managed to read and love NEW SUN while remaining completely ignorant of key facts about what I'd read, having them revealed only via commentary later. Didn't effect my love or admiration.)

Brian Doherty

2013-08-19 Antonin Scriabin replying to Brian Doherty #

Agrees with Brian Doherty that the author first read The Book of the New Sun casually, without knowing its standing in literary science fiction, and was nonetheless blown away by it. Adds that he missed much and did not realize the books formed part of a larger cycle, so the post is a personal reading-experience reflection rather than a textual argument.
supports

I'm with you on this. I first read New Sun on a lark, without knowing its place and importance in the literary science fiction world. I was blown away, and enjoyed it immensely even while missing a lot of important stuff and not knowing it was part of a larger cycle.
