2002urthThe Book of the New SunNicholas GeversKevin J. MaroneyMichael Straight
The thread: Thread asked whether The Fifth Head of Cerberus belongs in the Briah Cycle and whether its paired-world structure (Urth and Lune, Blue and Green) carries real meaning. Nicholas Gevers argued for both the Briah grouping and a symbolic reading of Lune as a contrast to Urth's decline, and defended the grouping against Kevin J. Maroney's objection that the novella shares no plot, character or setting with the Sun books. Michael Straight disputed the literal Urth=Blue reading, preferring a recurring theme, and said his own early reading experience did not support Maroney's claim that liking Fifth Head predicts liking other Wolfe. The thread ended in impasse, with Gevers and Maroney still disagreeing about the Briah label.
  • Nicholas Gevers: Fifth Head is the best introduction and belongs in the Briah cycle via paired worlds and Borski's correspondences
  • Michael Straight: Paired worlds are a recurring theme, not a literal Urth=Blue equation; Lune is less prominent than Verthandi or Skuld
  • Kevin J. Maroney: Good introduction but misfiled under Briah since it shares no plot, character or setting with the Sun books
  • Nicholas Gevers (later): Lune is symbolically important as a flourishing contrast to Urth, and Wolfe sees no difficulty with the Briah structure

Themes: The dying sun and the cosmos, Deep time and ruins, Books, libraries and writing

2002-04-11 Nicholas Gevers #

Argues that The Fifth Head of Cerberus is the best one-volume introduction to Wolfe's science fiction and belongs in the Briah Cycle despite no overt link to Severian. Grounds this in the paired double-world systems across the Urth and Whorl series and in Robert Borski's noted correspondences such as starcrossers.
proposes · myth-and-structure · source-and-allusion · theories: The Fifth Head of Cerberus belongs in the Briah Cycle; Urth/Lune, St Croix/St Anne, Blue/Green double worlds correspond

Thanks to Marc, Dan'l, and Rostrum for their responses
to my article. GW himself likes it (but then he would)
and Rosemary Wolfe apparently plans to distribute
copies of it at the World Horror Convention (starting
shortly.) It's also at WHC, of course, that the
Wolfe/Gaiman chapbook, WALKING TOUR OF THE SHAMBLES,
should first be available.

The matter of 5HC as entry point into Wolfe: yes, it's
a difficult and dispiriting book; but where else can
one find Wolfe at full power within a single novel?
PEACE, yes, but that's equally challenging. 5HC is
perforce the best one-volume introduction to Wolfe's
SF oeuvre.

And 5HC's place in the Briah Cycle: apart from certain
correspondences Robert Borski has noted (like
"starcrossers"), there's nothing overt to tie it to
the universe of Severian. BUT: in the context of the
whole Urth/Whorl series, there is too much resonance
with 5HC to be ignored. Three double world
systems--Urth/Lune, St Croix/St Anne, and
Blue/Green--are in close thematic correspondence, and
disregarding the 5HC element here is fatal to any
understanding of Wolfe's overall thinking and creative
evolution. 5HC is integral to the argument of Briah;
nothing overt disqualifies it from the Briah Cycle; it
belongs there.

--Nick Gevers.

2002-04-11 Michael Straight #

Disputes the literal Urth=Blue double-planet theory, preferring to read the paired worlds as a recurring theme. Responds to Nicholas Gevers's quoted remarks on The Fifth Head of Cerberus by noting that Lune is hardly more prominent than Verthandi or Skuld in the New Sun books, unlike the other double-world pairs.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Urth equals Blue as literal correspondence; Double planets are a recurring theme

I agree. Wasn't Wolfe already being hailed as a genius for 5HC before people had even read any of the New Sun books?

I mostly agree with this. One of the main reasons I dislike the Urth=Blue theory is that I prefer to think of the double planets more as a reoccuring theme than a literal correspondence.

But I also note that lumping Urth/Lune in with the others is a bit out of place. While in the other two pairs, both planets are important settings for the story, in the New Sun books, Lune is hardly more prominent than Verthandi or Skuld.

-Rostrum

2002-04-11 Kevin J. Maroney #

Agrees with Nick that The Fifth Head of Cerberus works as a one-volume introduction to Wolfe, then argues it should not be filed under the Briah cycle because it shares no plot, character or setting with the Sun books. Notes that thematic resonance alone is not enough, comparing it to Seven American Nights, and says the label misleads new readers.
disputes · genre-and-craft · theories: Fifth Head is not part of the Briah cycle

I agree with this, and have been using it as such for the last 15 years. It is neither Wolfe's best nor worst, nor his "most typical" (is there such a thing for Wolfe?), but if someone reads and enjoys _Fifth Head_, that person is likely to enjoy other Wolfe, and if not, they are unlikely to enjoy other Wolfe much more. When _Fifth Head_ was out of print, I made it a point to buy Ace copies whenever I saw them, to pass on to people who hadn't jumped into Wolfe yet.

Numbering _Fifth Head_ as part of "the Briah cycle", as you did in your article, is misleading and, frankly, wrong. There is no plot, character, or setting connection between _Fifth Head_ and the various _Sun_ novels, and calling it such sets up expectations in a new reader which I feel will not be met. Does it resonate with the other novels? Yes, but so does "Seven American Nights"; that doesn't make "Seven American Nights" part of "the Briah cycle".

--

2002-04-12 Nicholas Gevers #

Rejects identifying the Urth/Lune pair with Blue and Green, and argues Lune may matter more symbolically than readers realise. Proposes that all three two-world systems model predatory symbiosis, so Lune's flourishing life contrasts with Urth sinking toward Ragnarok-like lifelessness; responds to Michael Straight's comment.
disputes · myth-and-structure · theories: Lune is not the same world as Blue and Green; Three two-world systems model predatory symbiosis; Lune's life contrasts with dying Urth

Michael Straight: yes, the Urth/Lune pairing is less
important than the other two, and I certainly don't
believe they are the same worlds as Blue and
Green--too much textual gerrymandering and wishful
thinking go into that conclusion. But Lune may have a
greater symbolic/thematic importance than we
realise--if all three two-world systems are
mirror-images or models of predatory symbiosis, it may
be hugely significant that Lune flourishes with life
while Urth sinks towards the lifelessness of Ragnarok.

--Nick Gevers.

2002-04-12 Nicholas Gevers #

Concedes that calling The Fifth Head of Cerberus part of a Briah cycle is experimental, but defends the grouping as a way to better understand Wolfe's baroque SF work. Responds to Kevin J. Maroney's objection that Fifth Head has no plot, character or setting links to the Sun books, noting that Wolfe himself sees no difficulty with the Briah structure.
supports · theories: The Fifth Head of Cerberus belongs to the Briah cycle

I concede that listing 5HC as part of the Briah Cycle
is an experimental proposition, but if it leads to a
fuller understanding of Wolfe's baroque/SF oeuvre--and
it should, for reasons evident in my article--it has
some chance of becoming axiomatic. Wolfe himself sees
no difficulties with the Briah structure as I present
it...

--Nick Gevers.

2002-04-12 Michael Straight #

Recounts the poster's experience reading Wolfe, saying The Fifth Head of Cerberus disappointed him at first but improved on rereading, though he finds its St. Anne and aboriginal material less interesting than other Wolfe works. Responds to Kevin J. Maroney's claim that enjoyment of Fifth Head predicts enjoyment of other Wolfe, reporting that his own early reading path contradicts it.
disputes

My introduction to Wolfe was the New Sun books, followed by the Soldier books, all of which I loved. Then I read 5HC and didn't like it very much. If that had been my introduction to Wolfe, I doubt I would have gone out of my way to read more of his stuff.

I re-read 5HC recently, after having read a whole bunch more Wolfe, and enjoyed it much more. I like Wolfe's writing in 5HC and he does some very interesting things with the story, but I don't find St. Anne/St. Croix and the abos themselves all that interesting compared with Urth, the Whorl, Silk, Severian, Latro, the inhumi, etc.

-Rostrum
