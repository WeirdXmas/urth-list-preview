2010-08-03 Lee Berman #

Proposes that Severian's life in The Book of the New Sun parallels that of St Ignatius of Loyola, citing his hospital stay after a soldier's leg injury, his troubled spiritual journey, and his miracles. Grounds the claim in the quoted account of Ignatius's visions of a serpent-shaped form and suggests Ignatius may have partly inspired Wolfe.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · source-and-allusion · theories: Ignatius of Loyola partly inspired Severian

I think that is interesting. Some other parallels between Serverian and St. Ignatius..

- he was in the hospital due to a leg injury incurred serving as a soldier

-he had a spiritual journey filled with strife and trouble.

- he called up an earthquake and also a storm at sea

- he healed the sick including a woman's withered arm.

Ignatius could have been a partial inspiration for Wolfe.

2010-08-06 Jane Delawney replying to Lee Berman #

Extends Lee Berman's parallel between Severian and St Ignatius by arguing that the Torturers' Guild mirrors the Order Ignatius founded, in its black clothing, celibacy, absolute obedience, and recruitment of boys. Cites the metal bar set at about a man's groin height and the saying about giving a child before seven, answering Lee Berman's post.
supports · source-and-allusion · theology-and-religion · theories: Torturers' Guild modeled on Ignatius's Order

So could his Order - 'black' (OK fuligin) clothing, prohibition of marriage, primacy of absolute obedience ('Torturers Obey') and their method of selecting recruits: other than boy babies born within their Tower, the Seekers of Truth and Penitence will take and educate male children who come to them (presumably with their condemned parent/s) who are able to stand beneath a certain metal bar which projects at about 3 ft. ('the height of a man's groin' - or similar, I don't have the book in front of me right now) from a bulkhead.

'Give me a child before he is seven years old and he is mine for life'.

jd
