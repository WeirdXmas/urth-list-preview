The thread: A thread that began with a lurker's question about a book bound in human skin in The Claw of the Conciliator. Duncan Truter proposed that the book Severian picks up with Thecla is The Book of the New Sun itself, which would explain Severian's sense of already being dead. Matthew Keeley linked Severian's recoil from a book to a skin-bound volume he recalled, suggesting the Conciliatory powers let him recognise it subconsciously in Ultan's library. Paul B disputed Duncan's identification, arguing the book was written in the Conciliator's time by a fellow prisoner and lost before Severian became an apprentice, and preferred the catacomb explanation for the scenery. The thread ends without resolution, with the readings left in disagreement.
  • Duncan Truter: the book Severian picks up with Thecla is The Book of the New Sun, explaining his sense of being dead
  • Matthew Keeley: Severian's Conciliatory powers let him subconsciously recognise a skin-bound book in Ultan's library
  • Paul B: the book was written in the Conciliator's time by a fellow prisoner and lost before Severian's apprenticeship; the catacomb explains the scenery

Themes: Stories within stories, Books, libraries and writing, Ghosts, dreams and visions

2008-09-30 Paul B replying to Duncan Truter #

Disputes Duncan Truter's claim that the book Severian finds is The Book of the New Sun he himself wrote, arguing instead that it was written in the Conciliator's time by a fellow prisoner and was lost before Severian became an apprentice. Responds to Duncan's reading of Severian feeling already dead by citing the book's known history and preferring the catacomb explanation for the chapter's scenery.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: The other BotNS was written by Severian's fellow prisoner

No, that's not factually accurate. The other BotNS was likely written in the Conciliator's time by Severian's fellow prisoner, and has avowedly been lost by the time when Severian is an apprentice in the Citadel.

I like the catacomb explanation just fine personally, and I never had much reason to want anything more from that chapter than the scenery it presented.

Paul
