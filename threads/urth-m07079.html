In defense of Pandora, by Holly Hollander

Themes: Myth and folklore sources, Unreliable narration

2008-10-10 Matthew King #

Defends Pandora by Holly Hollander against a reader who says he will never reread it, arguing it is worth rereading for its many of Wolfe's familiar traits. Lists mythological themes, a lamed narrator with an explainer's voice, layered first-person provenance, and frustrating narrative gaps as the reasons, comparing the voice to Silk and other narrators.
supports · author-and-biography · genre-and-craft

In defense of Pandora, it is worth re-reading, at the very least because it has so many of the notorious Lupine tropes.

- mythological themes; subversion thereof
- a lamed man, with that explainer-of-things voice Wolfe so often uses (cf Silk, Ziggurat-dad, post-Valhalla Able).
- 1st person narrative, with layered provenance. "By Holly Hollander", but this nice writer from Chicago helped her out. - exasperating lacunae. "I'll tell you about this thing later." Also just plain skipping bits, especially after something exciting happens.
