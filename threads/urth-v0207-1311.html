Silk's appetite

Urth list · 6 messages · 2002-07-07 to 2002-07-09

2002urthThe Book of the Long SunSteve StricklandMatthew MalthousePeter WestlakeAshley CrillRoy C. LackeyDavid Duffy
The thread: Steve Strickland proposed that Silk's light appetite hints at his true power, perhaps Silk absorbing traits of the inhumi he mastered, and that Horn survives in Silk's memories. Roy C. Lackey countered that Silk's abstemiousness is partly affectation, since his childhood eating habits are unknown and he eats normally at Inclito's house in In Green's Jungles. Spectacled Bear offered a material explanation instead: Silk was a poor augur who rarely had enough food, as the tomatoes in Long Sun show. Ashley Crill then read the appetite as a clue that Horn already inhabits Silk's body, seen only in hindsight after Return to the Whorl. The thread ends without consensus, with a mystery reading, a realist reading, and a possession reading standing side by side.
  • Steve Strickland: Silk's small appetite reveals his power, with Silk absorbing inhumi traits and Horn surviving in his memories
  • Roy C. Lackey: Silk's abstemiousness is partly affectation, shown by normal eating at Inclito's house
  • Spectacled Bear: Silk ate little because he was a poor augur, and food was a rare treat
  • Ashley Crill: Silk's appetite is a clue that Horn already inhabits his body, visible in hindsight

Themes: Doubles, twins and clones, Masks, disguise and shapeshifting, Hidden identities and parentage

2002-07-08 Steve Strickland #

Proposes that Silk's lack of appetite may reveal the true nature of his power, perhaps by Silk taking on aspects of the inhumi he has mastered. Grounds this in Silk's kindness to the inhumi, which protects him against them and exposes their secret, and argues Horn survives because his memories passed into Silk.
proposes · puzzle-solving · theories: Silk is an inhumi; Silk's goodness protects him from inhumi; Silk takes on inhumi nature; Horn's memories survive in Silk

This explanation has never really satisfied me. The question of why Silk doesn't eat much is probably the one that puzzles me the most.

Certainly, it is intended to suggest that Silk may be an inhumi, though there are also suggestions that he is not. One of these days I'm going to have time to re-read ShortSun and get to the bottom of it, though it's discouraging to hear of people who've read it twice and still don't know the answer.

I can't help but wonder: maybe the clues that Silk is an inhumi are all too clear and readers such as we simply refuse to accept them out of preference.

Another possibility that I wish I had time to explore: maybe Silk's lack of an appetite is a clue as to the true nature of Silk's "power". We know that Silk is profoundly good, at least in certain respects. Part of that goodness is reflected in kindness to the inhumi and it seems to be a kindness of such an absolute quality that it serves as protection against the inhumi, which seems to be related to their secret, which Silk exposes. It seems to me that just as the inhumi take on the nature of the humans upon whom they feed, perhaps Silk takes on some of the aspects of the inhumi, who he has mastered. I know that's just an idea in its inception, and it's been awhile since I've read Short Sun, but it's an idea I toyed with at the time of reading and I can't help but think there's something there.

On another subject: I think it's wrong to view Horn as gone, just because Silk is alive. Horn's memories, at least, remain in Silk, where they were transported across worlds while Horn lay dying on Green.

Steve Strickland

2002-07-08 Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that Silk's abstemiousness is partly an affectation rather than a trait of his augur training, since he was a fresh novice and his childhood eating habits are unknown. Supports this by citing the scene at Inclito's house in In Green's Jungles, where Silk eats normally when his mind is engaged elsewhere.
supports · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Silk's abstemiousness is partly an affectation

From: David Duffy <[email]>

Silk was a kid fresh out of the seminary. He was a novice as an augur, hadn't had time to inculcate the poor-as-a-church-mouse stereotypical appetite of same. His eating habits must date from childhood, about which we are told very little.

Silk's abstemiousness is, to some degree, an affectation. When his mind is otherwise engaged he can--and does--eat normally, as at Inclito's house. (IGJ, 71)

-Roy

2002-07-08 Peter Westlake #

Argues that Silk eats little because he was a poor augur living on scant food, offering the tomatoes in Long Sun as evidence that food was a rare treat for him. Responds to Steve Strickland's puzzlement over why Silk eats so little, supplying a material explanation rather than a mystery one.
answers · close-textual-reading · theories: Silk eats little because augurs were poor

As an augur he was extremely poor, and had very little food to live on. Look at what a treat the tomatoes are in Long Sun, for instance.

Spectacled Bear.
