Derivation of talus

Whorl list · 3 messages · 1998-05-19 to 1998-05-26

1998whorlThe Book of the Long SunThe Book of the New SunDave LeblingBrownes3Peter Westlake
The thread: Brownes3 opened the thread by proposing that the name "talus" in Nightside the Long Sun comes from the rubble slope a bulldozer blade leaves, citing the butter-yellow colour matching Caterpillar and Webster's definition. Peter Westlake answered with a web link to the Greek myth of Talos, the bronze guardian of Crete, offering no further argument. David Lebling later argued the name fits Wolfe's naming scheme, pairing the rock-debris sense with the mythical bronze automaton, which Wolfe has written about as a robot. The thread stayed at the level of competing proposals, with the bulldozer reading and the Talos reading left side by side rather than resolved.
  • Brownes3: talus names the bulldozer, from the rubble slope its blade leaves
  • Peter Westlake: the name derives from the Greek myth of Talos, bronze guardian of Crete
  • David_Lebling: rock-debris sense plus the mythical Talos fits Wolfe's naming, taluses as jury-rigged copies

1998-05-19 Brownes3 #

Proposes a derivation for the word "talus" in Wolfe's Nightside the Long Sun: the taluses in the painting of Pas creating the Whorl are bulldozers, and talus is named after the rubble slope a bulldozer blade leaves. Grounds this on the butter-yellow colour matching Caterpillar's brand colour and on Webster's definition of talus as a slope of accumulated rock debris.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Talus in Nightside named for bulldozer-made rubble slope

I don't recall anyone posting a possible derivation of the term of talus on this, list,although I remember at one point several posts saying "talus" was still a mystery.

Here's my try:

I'm re-reading Nightside and came to the passage that describes the painting of Pas creating the Whorl. The message mentions two taluses of a particularly lovely shade of butter yellow. Obviously, they are bulldozers, but a certain kind -- yellow is the brand color of Caterpillar Tractors. So I went looking for associations with caterpillars or butterliesa and such.

I needn't have been so devious. According to Webster's, a talus is:

"a slope formed by an accumulation of rock debris."

So a talus is named after the thing it makes: the slope the bulldozer blade leaves in the rubbled after it pulls back.

What do y'all think?

Rich Browne

1998-05-26 Dave Lebling #

Proposes that the name "talus" for the bulldozer-guard robots in The Book of the New Sun fits Wolfe's naming scheme, since talus means rock debris or a slope of it. Grounds this in the resonance with the mythical bronze automaton Talos of Crete, which Wolfe has written about as if it were a robot, and in the idea that the taluses are jury-rigged copies, like debris beside the real soldiers.
proposes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Talus names the robots as debris

The nice thing about "talus" as a term for the bulldozer-guard-robots is that the word also fits nicely within Wolfe's naming scheme, in that talus is rock debris, or a pile of such debris making up a slope.

So, we have the resonance with the Talos of mythical Crete (which Wolfe has written about in terms that suggest he "believes" it to have been a robot), and the nice fit with the Whorl's naming scheme, and the definitional parallel that the taluses of the Whorl are, compared to the soldiers, "debris," in that they are somewhat inferior jury-rigged copies of the real thing.

-- viz
