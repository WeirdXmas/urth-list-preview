2001-06-11 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that the editors of the Long Sun text do not match their stated editorial principles, because they leave obvious errors uncorrected and mark them with sic instead. Responds to Adam S.'s question about whether the unlikely accuracy of the editors' statement reflects copying mistakes or deliberate choice, citing their missed errors as evidence.
disputes · close-textual-reading

To my:

Adam S. asked,

Neither; I refer to (1) the fact that they simply do not correct some errors that they themselves seem to think are obvious, instead marking them with "sic" and so on; and (2) their having missed or ignored some errors which seem obvious to us reading the text "later."

2001-06-11 Adam Stephanides #

Questions whether readers can reach satisfactory answers about the editors of Nightside the Long Sun, doubting we can know more than what the editors left uncorrected. Replies to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes by asking whether his doubt about the editorial statement means inadvertent copying errors or deliberate substantial changes, and says the latter lacks grounds.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: the editors added deliberate distortion to Narr's narrative

Theoretically, yes. In practice, I don't see how we can reach satisfactory answers. Of course, we can know what they don't consider to be obvious errors--what they've left stand (though with the caveat in your following paragraph). And we can guess that, had they found Narr's narrative too error-ridden, they wouldn't have bothered to publish it. But given how little we know about the editors, I don't see how we can go much beyond this.

When you say that it is unlikely that their statement of editorial principles is strictly accurate, are you referring to the probability that they introduced inadvertent errors while copying the text? Or are you saying that it is almost certain they deliberately made more substantial changes than they admit? If the latter, I don't see the grounds for it.

As to interest, I admit that, for those who love these sorts of wheels-within-wheels, the idea that the editors have added an extra layer of distortion to Narr's narrative must be irresistible. Personally, I can do without it.

--Adam
