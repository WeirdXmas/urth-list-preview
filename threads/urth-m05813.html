What's So Great About Ushas?

Urth list · 9 messages · 2008-07-14 to 2008-07-14

2008urthThe Book of the New SunJeff WilsonthalassocratJohn WatkinsPaul B
The thread: The thread asked whether the Hierogrammates in The Book of the New Sun serve the Increate, and whether they can be judged evil. Roy Lackey's claim that they work for the Increate was disputed by thalassocrat and Paul B, who found little textual evidence and saw a self-serving agenda. John Watkins argued they serve the Increate's will, then shifted to a habituation reading in which evil posing as good gradually becomes good, citing Kypris and Quetzal. Paul B repeatedly rejected this, holding that an evil act producing good stays evil and that a universal divine plan would erase morality. Jeff Wilson offered a separate view of them as false gods built by the Hierodules out of hubris. The exchange ended in impasse, with Watkins and Paul B not converging.
  • thalassocrat: Hierogrammates are not Increate's agents, lack holiness, and pursue a self-serving agenda
  • Paul B: Little evidence ties them to the Increate; evil acts stay evil even when good results follow
  • John Watkins: They serve the Increate's will and evil posing as good gradually takes on goodness
  • Jeff Wilson: They are false gods made by the Hierodules out of hubris, not evil beings

Themes: Gods, angels and the Increate, Gnosticism, heresy and other theologies, Masks, disguise and shapeshifting

2008-07-14 John Watkins replying to Paul B #

Argues that the Hierogrammates, though evil and posing as angels, must still serve the Increate's will because the Outsider says a demon mimicking a god cannot help becoming godlike. Grounds this in the Outsider's remark to Silk and the Hierogrammates' angelic pretense, responding to Paul B.'s point that evidence tying them to the Increate is scarce.
proposes · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: Hierogrammates unknowingly do the Increate's will

Perhaps we're looking for the evidence in the wrong place. The strongest evidence that the Hierogrammates are doing the will of the Increate is that the Outsider tells Silk that "When a demon mimics a god, it can not help in some ways becoming like a god."

The Hierogrammates certainly pose as angelic beings. So even if all the points made about the Hierogrammates being evil are correct, and even if their agenda is indistinguishable from that of Maitre and Mr. Million, they *can not help *in some way doing the Increate's will. This could mean that Severian's brining the New Sun is not ultimately a Very Bad Thing after all.

Of course, I have no idea how this would work, or what the Increate's purpose would be, unless it really is bringing the Green Man future.

2008-07-14 Paul B replying to John Watkins #

Disputes John Watkins's suggestion that the Hierogrammates serve the Increate's will even while acting as evil, arguing that if everyone is part of God's plan then morality cannot be judged. Grounds the objection in the premise that the view erases moral distinctions, and demands proof for any claim that only some beings do God's will.
disputes · theology-and-religion · theories: Hierogrammates serve the Increate's will despite evil

If I recall correctly, someone else recently tried this as an explanation. The problem with it is that if everyone is part of God's Plan, then the concept of "morality" as we know it doesn't exist and no actions can be judged - a preposterous conclusion.
However, if you would like to make the case that only some, and not others, are doing God's will, then you'll have to prove why that is so.

2008-07-14 John Watkins replying to Paul B #

Proposes that the Hierogrammates' divine role comes from habituation rather than a cosmic plan: pretending to speak for the divine gradually shapes actions, as with Patera Quetzal and Kypris. Grounds this in Severian's remark that time turns lies into truths and in the Increate's annexing of the faithful, while insisting their actions remain judgeable. Responds to Paul B.'s objection that a universal divine plan would abolish morality.
proposes · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: Hierogrammates' divine role results from habituation, not a cosmic plan; Quetzal and Kypris were shaped by pretending to serve the divine

I don't think it means anything like the idea that everything is part of God's plan and thus morality is meaningless. I think it means something much more mundane--that, as Severian himself notes "time turns our lies into truths." In an even less poetic phrasing, habituation works--we become the thing we discipline ourself to feign being because that's how character is shaped.

When the lie in question is that we speak for the divine, habituation and the Increate together act to annex our actions. For Patera Quetzal, a bloodsucking alien pretending to be a kindly old priest, this meant that he called off the human sacrifices and generally operated as a strong and faithful leader for his flock. For Kypris, and arguably even for Pas, it means that the sham religion of the Whorl is still operating to some degree to further the Outsider's ends.

I'm suggesting that a similar explanation could be at work behind the plans of the Hierogrammates, although in a more opaque manner. I am not suggesting that there is no basis for judging the actions of the Hierogrammates--their part in a divine plan could be necessary but completely unwitting.

2008-07-14 John Watkins replying to Paul B #

Argues that Wolfe's point goes beyond evil producing good results, proposing that evil masquerading as good gradually takes on the goodness it imitates, as with Kypris and Quetzal in the Long Sun books and Pas and Silk in the Short Sun. Grounds this in the juxtaposed lines 'time turns our lies into truths' and 'When a demon mimics a god,' and suggests the Hierogrammates posing as angels may carry similar significance in The Book of the New Sun.
disputes · myth-and-structure · close-textual-reading · theories: Evil that mimics good becomes good in Wolfe

I think Wolfe's point is deeper than merely "evil actions can produce good results." There's a significant difference from the fate of, for example, Gollum in the Lord of the Rings, who does evil things which lead to good results, and the fate of Kypris or Quetzal, whose evil is, to some degree, masquerading as good. In the Urth cycle, evil that masquerades as good winds up becoming good to some degree--not just accidently having a good result, but changing internally to reflect the good that it imitates. This change isn't absolute--Quetzal still picks Green (although possibly that was a ploy to get them to Blue) and still drinks blood to survive. But it is significant.

At least, that's the sense I get from the juxtaposition of "time turns our lies into truths" and "When a demon mimics a god, it can not help in some ways becoming like a god." I would be surprised, with the significance this theme has in the Long Sun, with Quetzal and Kypris, and in the Short Sun, with the merger of the demonic Pas with the personality of the saintly Silk, if this same theme has no bearing on the New Sun.

This does not mean that I think the Hierogrammates are properly understood as archangels. Despite the name, Tzadkiel seems more sinister than that, and the scheme--genocide to further evolution--seems very morally problematic. But, again, I'd be surprised if the presumably evil Hierogrammates posing as presumably good angelic beings has no significance other than messing with our and Severian's assumptions. There's got to be something more going on than evil aliens manipulating Severian to bring about devastation--I just don't know what it is.

Gollum in the Lord of the Rings, who does evil things which lead to good
results, and the fate of Kypris or Quetzal, whose evil is, to some degree,
masquerading as good.  In the Urth cycle, evil that masquerades as good
winds up becoming good to some degree--not just accidently having a good
result, but changing internally to reflect the good that it imitates.  This
change isn't absolute--Quetzal still picks Green (although possibly that was
a ploy to get them to Blue) and still drinks blood to survive.  But it is
significant.

At least, that's the sense I get from the juxtaposition of "time turns our
lies into truths" and "When a demon mimics a god, it can not help in some
ways becoming like a god."  I would be surprised, with the significance this
theme has in the Long Sun, with Quetzal and Kypris, and in the Short Sun,
with the merger of the demonic Pas with the personality of the saintly Silk,
if this same theme has no bearing on the New Sun.

This does not mean that I think the Hierogrammates are properly understood
as archangels.  Despite the name, Tzadkiel seems more sinister than that,
and the scheme--genocide to further evolution--seems very morally
problematic.  But, again, I'd be surprised if the presumably evil
Hierogrammates posing as presumably good angelic beings has no significance
other than messing with our and Severian's assumptions.  There's got to be
something more going on than evil aliens manipulating Severian to bring
about devastation--I just don't know what it is.

2008-07-14 Jeff Wilson replying to John Watkins #

Argues that the Hierogrammates are false gods built by the Hierodules out of hubris, posing as God and falling short, with Tzadkiel aiming lower by taking the name of an angel. Responds to John Watkins's reading of the Hierogrammates as presumably evil beings posing as archangels, offering a view of them as evil creations rather than evil beings.
disputes · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: Hierogrammates are living graven images of false gods; Hierogrammates are evil creations not evil beings; Tzadkiel takes the name of an angel

I'm thinking it is intended that the Hieros built the Hierogrammates out of hubris in attempt to make a perfect, godlike creature. They are thus living graven images and as false gods they pose at being God but fall short. Tzadkiel personally aims a bit lower by taking the name of angel, but is still sorely flawed. The 'Grammates are the BOTNS equivalent of DOORS' Goddess or the deities in the SOLDIER books; beings of real but inferior spiritual power. They can play the roles of God or one of the heavenly host, but eventually limits and conflicts of interest will break down the facade.

In this light, the Hiergrammates may then be more properly considered evil creations than evil beings; they may not have been given the choice to not pursue the Hieros' agenda, or to consider if their mandate may not come from the Increate.
