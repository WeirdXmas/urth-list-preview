TAD - small, scattered thoughts

Urth list · 3 messages · 2003-02-07 to 2003-02-07

2003urthThe Book of the New SunAndrew BollenAlice Turner
The thread: Andrew Bollen argued that The App of Death draws on the dying-and-resurrected god pattern, likening Green's foggy half-life to Tammuz in the underworld with Lara as Inanna, and compared the wasteland imagery to Eliot. Alice K. Turner offered an alternative reading of There Are Doors as an allegory of the Nixon White House merged with Kafka's Castle, mapping Klamm to Kissinger and North to Ollie North. She later clarified that Ollie was meant as a general symbol of American political thuggery rather than a literal Watergate link, while admitting the merged conceit was unsuccessful. The thread ended with the two readings left side by side, with no agreement reached.
  • Andrew Bollen: The Tammuz and Inanna death-and-resurrection myth structures the book, with Lara as Inanna
  • Alice K. Turner: There Are Doors is a Nixon White House allegory merged with Kafka's Castle
  • Alice K. Turner: Ollie is a general figure of American political thuggery, not a literal Watergate link

Themes: Death and resurrection, Myth and folklore sources, Power, rulers and politics

2003-02-07 Andrew Bollen #

Argues that TAD draws on the dying and resurrected god myth, specifically the period between death and resurrection, with Green's foggy half-life likened to Tammuz in the underworld and Lara as Inanna drawing him out. Grounds this in the Tammuz, Attis, Osiris and Adonis parallels and compares the book's wasteland imagery to Eliot's Prufrock and The Wasteland.
proposes · myth-and-structure · source-and-allusion · theories: Green's half-life mirrors Tammuz in the underworld; Lara as Inanna drawing Green out

So I just finished reading TAD. There's the obvious hints at = Tammuz/Attis/Osiris/Adonis (Green & all the other Billies) and = Inanna/Cybele/Isis/Astarte (Lara). But as always, Wolfe's uses = mythological themes & fragments for his own story, putting them together = in different ways, rather than indulge in some boring reductionist = retelling of the original.=20

I read it as using the dying/resurrected god theme, but specifically the = period between death & resurrection - metaphorically, Green's foggy = half-life, like Tammuz in the underworld, with Lara/Innanna drawing him = out. A bit like Eliot - Prufrock and partciularly The Wasteland - with = similar linkages between the blighting of the world with the death of = Tammuz etc & (Eliot & Wolfe's perception of) the wasteland of modern = existence.=20

2003-02-07 Alice Turner #

Proposes reading There Are Doors as an allegory of the Nixon White House, merged with Kafka's Castle, with Klamm standing for Kissinger and North for Ollie North and the Watergate crew. Responds to Andrew Bollen's post on Tammuz and Inanna myth patterns, offering this alternative while admitting she finds the merged conceit unsuccessful but will not argue it.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · source-and-allusion · theories: TAD allegorizes the Nixon White House; Klamm is Kissinger; North is Ollie North

Another useful way to look at it is as a take on the Nixon White House, = set in a decade that is probably the defining era of serious feminism = (history makes strange bedfellows). The conceit is to merge this with = Kafka's -Castle-. Whether this conceit is successful or not will depend = on you, the reader. Me, I think not, but will not argue. Anyway, it's = pretty clear that Kafka's Klamm is meant to be Kissinger here, North is = an amalgam of Ollie North and the rest of the Watergate crew. Etc.

-alga
