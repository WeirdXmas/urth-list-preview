TWK: Thou Art god

Urth list · 10 messages · 2005-11-27 to 2005-11-28

2005urththalassocratCliff JudgeAndy RobertsonJames WynnChris PRoy C. LackeyDavid DiGiacomo
The thread: Posters debated whether Able, the protagonist of The Wizard Knight who visited Skai and gained powers, is an Overcyn. Roy C. Lackey argued he is one, citing his address to the queen and his withheld origin, and that Art's godhood raised cosmological problems. David DiGiacomo disputed this, saying the queen's remark and Able's silence prove nothing and that he remains a human with Skai powers. Thalassocrat and Chris cited the opening of the book and the snow scene as evidence that Able reveals or nearly reveals Overcyn status, while transentient offered an unverified recollection of the word. Andy Robertson added that Able is one of the Einherjar, making him an Overcyn by a different route. The thread ended without resolution, with the textual evidence left contested.
  • Roy C. Lackey: Able is an Overcyn, shown by his words to the queen and his withheld origin
  • David DiGiacomo: Overcyn status requires birth into Skai, so Able is a human with Skai powers
  • thalassocrat: the opening of the book has Able telling Toug he is an Overcyn
  • Chris: Able's snow-scene admission hides the Overcyn status, not the return
  • Andy Robertson: Able is one of the Einherjar, hence an Overcyn
  • James Wynn: Art's pass defense was a humiliating punishment, not a real duty

Themes: Gods, angels and the Increate, Hidden identities and parentage, The dying sun and the cosmos

2005-11-27 Roy C. Lackey #

Asks how much Art's godhood in The Wizard Knight affects his battles, since he fought as a god after returning from Skai and what natural limits that gave his powers. Raises the cosmological point that a Mythgarthr god bowing to mortals and dallying with Disiri breaks the proper order of homage and holiness.
questions · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: Art's godhood shapes his prowess as warrior

When Art made his vow to defend that pass, he was an overgrown kid. What complicates matters is the fact that when he actually undertook the defenses he was twenty years older, had the aid of a magic steed, and was a *god*. In fact, in all of his battles in the second book, after he came back from Skai, he was a god. That hardly seems fair. What had a god to fear in his contests with mere men? Death? He had already died and gone to Skai. What were the natural parameters of his powers as a god? Exclusive of the clearly supernatural powers he used when he broke his vow at the end of the story, how much stronger was he as a god than a mere man, and how much did that influence his prowess as a warrior?

When Overcyn Able bent his knee to Queen Idnn or King Arnthor, a Mythgarthr god was bowing to his supposed worshipers, a blasphemy of sorts, a reversal of the natural order of proper homage in the cosmology of TWK. I thought that was what got the Aelf in trouble.

And when Art the god stooped to dally with Disiri, he was twice removed from her, in terms of holiness. He was farther above Disiri near the end of the story than Michael or Parka was above him.

-Roy

2005-11-27 Roy C. Lackey replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that Able in The Wizard Knight is an Overcyn, citing his address to the queen and the king's reply, and that his omission of this fact was a half truth. Builds the case from Able's own words to the queen (W, 386) and from the later passage where he lectures the king on cosmology while withholding his origin.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Able is an Overcyn

"Oh, you Overcyns! Get up, please. I didn't mean to keep you kneeling in the snow. Rise."
I did, and her soft hand found mine. "I feel you're my friend. That you truly are. I've forty knights, and not one real friend among them. Did the Valfather send you?"
"Yes, Your Majesty. Or at least, he let me come."
She stared. "You're serious."
"Entirely, Your Majesty. When I talk to others, I try to conceal it. But I won't lie to you or the king. I won't even tell half truths, something I do much too often."

Shortly afterwards (W, 397), Able lectured the king about the cosmology of the seven worlds, pointedly neglecting to mention where he had spent the last twenty years and the fact that he was an Overcyn, which fact, had he revealed it, would have lent weight to his long-undelivered message. That smells like a half truth to me. But, had he revealed his status as an Overcyn, that would have cheapened his victory in the Trial by Arms, thereby calling into question the innocence of the queen. At least, that's the only reason I can think of not to have told him the whole truth. In any event, his word to the queen was soon broken.

-Roy

2005-11-27 James Wynn replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that Art's promise to defend the mountain pass was a punishment rather than a real military duty, since he instead rescued the King's sister from a dragon and died in the attempt. Responds to Roy Lackey's complaint that Art was a god during his later battles by reframing the pass defense as humiliating for a hero who had already done greater deeds.
disputes · close-textual-reading · genre-and-craft · theories: Defending the pass was a punishment for Art

I feel some embarrassment at piling on against Roy for the crime stirring the most interesting discussion on TWK in a while, however, here I go anyway:

Instead of defending a pass as he was tasked, he rescued the King's sister from a dragon: dying in the endeavor. Holding a remote pass was only of incidental value to the realm. It was primarily a punishment. By the time he fulfills his promise, defending a pass in the name of a mere King of Mythgarthr humiliating whereas before he probably considered it somewhat glorious.

2005-11-28 Andy Robertson replying to David DiGiacomo #

Argues that Abel in The Wizard Knight is one of the Einherjar, the chosen slain gathered in Valhalla, and so an Overcyn rather than a god. Disputes David DiGiacomo's view that Abel is merely human with Skai powers, citing the long-established Odinic theology of the Einherjar as the basis for his status.
disputes · source-and-allusion · theology-and-religion · theories: Abel is one of the Einherjar; Abel is an Overcyn, not a god; Abel's status follows Odinic theology

Abel is (specifically) one of the Einherjar, the chosen slain, who are gathered in Valhalla (or the Lady's hall Forsetti) ready for the last fight at Ragnarok.

This status and this state are part of long-established Odinic theology: it is not something Wolfe made up. Abel acts in exactly the way I would, er, expect such a being to act.

It doesn't make him a god, but it does make him an Overcyn.

hartshorn, the rather nerdy Odinist
