1997-01-31 Dave Lebling #

Argues that the Triviguantis of Long Sun are more Arab or Persian than Indian, citing Islamic survivals in their practices. Grounds this on their red triangle symbol, their monotheism centred on Sphigx, and the Arabic-sounding names and language fragments, responding to a post suggesting Calde Tussah is Hindu-Indian from Trivigaunte.
disputes · source-and-allusion · theories: Triviguantis are Arab or Persian rather than Indian

From: [email]

I thought the Triviguantis were more Arab or Persian than Indian. They at least show Islamic survivals, wherever they are from. For example, their symbol is a red triangle, "because Sphigx won't let them use images." They are monotheists (more or less, worshipping only or mostly Sphigx). Their names and the little bits of their language that we hear are more like Arabic than Hindi, as well.

1997-02-01 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Proposes that Trivigaunte in Calde of the Long Sun has a Hindu core with Turkish and Persian satellite cultures, likening the title calde to Hindi rani. Grounds this in the words khanum and Nizam and the silk road, and speculates a matriarchal Islamic-style faith, Allat rather than Allah, while admitting the evidence is slight.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theology-and-religion · theories: Trivigaunte has a Hindu core with Turkish and Persian satellites; Trivigaunte's religion is matriarchal, Allat rather than Allah; Trivigaunte resembles a Mogul-type Indus Valley empire

Reply: Item #6681520 from [email]@INET01#

Re: Hindi stratum to Trivigaunte society. "Calde'" is to Spanish as "Rani" is to Hindi (although the ranks are as different as mayor and queen). True, "khanum" is Turko-Persian, and "Nizam" is Urdu/Turkish/Arabic, but still.

Not much, I'll admit. But it's there. With a Hindu core area and satellite areas (different cities contributing the "Turkish," "Persian," elements that were not indiginous to original Trivigaunte) brought in through the military campaigns the text suggests they've gone through, I can envision a Mogul-type/Indus Valley empire with an Islamic (monotheistic/desert) aspect that is matriarchal rather than patriarchal (Allat rather than Allah). (This is largely speculative, of course.)

(Can't resist pointing out the silk road.)
