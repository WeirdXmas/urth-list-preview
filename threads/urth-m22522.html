Father Inire

Urth list · 3 messages · 2012-01-04 to 2012-01-09

2012urthThe Book of the New SunGerry QuinnLee BermanJordon Flato
The thread: Posters asked whether Father Inire, like other higher beings in Wolfe's work, takes an animal form, with Flato and Berman both proposing that animal imagery marks beings above humanity and pointing to the hieros, Melito's eagle, the armiger's daughter's lark, and the Yesod, Briah and Abaddon levels as parallels to Olympus, Earth and Hades. Gerry Quinn disputed the idea, arguing that Wolfe uses animal comparisons inconsistently and that many are ordinary or just reflect a creature's looks, citing Rudesind, Fechin, Agia, the megatherians and the undines as counterexamples. The thread ended with the motif proposal challenged but not settled, and no clear reply to Quinn appears.
  • Jordon Flato: Father Inire may take an animal form, probably a monkey, with higher levels matching Olympus, Earth and Hades
  • Lee Berman: animal forms mark beings above humanity, extending from Tzadkiel and the Cumaean to Father Inire, with snake, bird and fish associations
  • Gerry Quinn: no consistent animal-imagery motif; many comparisons are ordinary, and Inire's monkey look is just resemblance

Themes: Gods, angels and the Increate, Beasts and the animal in us, Myth and folklore sources

2012-01-04 Jordon Flato replying to Lee Berman #

Proposes that Father Inire, like other higher beings such as Tzadkiel and the Cumaean, may take an animal form, probably a monkey. Builds on Lee Berman's quotation of David Stockhoff about raising and lowering, citing the hieros' animal masks, Melito's eagle and the armiger's daughter's lark, and suggesting the Yesod, Briah and Abaddon levels match Olympus, Earth and Hades.
proposes · theology-and-religion · myth-and-structure · theories: Father Inire takes a monkey form; Higher beings appear in animal forms throughout the books; Yesod, Briah and Abaddon parallel Olympus, Earth and Hades

In terms of animal forms in the work, the most striking for me are the masks of the hieros, which seem hideous monsters, but are creatures of urth. So here the alien presence is directly linked to being masked as an animal. Of course I think there ius more to it than that

I really think you are onto something with this. Tzadkiel is meant to depict a
higher being than humanity and he/she demonstrates a full spectrum of expression
from lower being to human to angel. (the other spectrum he/she varies on is size
but perhaps that is a separate discussion).

Yesod, Briah, Abaddon= Olympus, Earth, Hades or Asgard, Midgard, Niflheim/Hel etc.?

I think we do see this and it is associated with characters which are higher beings than
humans. Tzadkiel and The Cumaean are explicitly shown in an animalistic form. The
megatherians are, by definition, beastial. Melito's angel takes an eagle form. The
armiger's daughter has her lark form. We must wonder about "Mother Pyrexia" and what
she turned into while confined.

I think an extrapolation of this theme makes it almost necessary to wonder what monkey
form Father Inire might be taking. Why would other aliens take animal form and not him?
I find Talos' vulpine nature and some animalistic comparisons for other characters to be
invitations to speculate on their alien/angelic/pagan god nature.

IMHO, the snake and bird and fish (and Pig) associations found in Long/Short Sun for
superhuman characters continue this theme.

2012-01-09 Gerry Quinn replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that Wolfe does not build a consistent motif of describing characters through animal imagery, and that many animal comparisons are ordinary or simply reflect a non-human creature's resemblance to some animal. Points to Rudesind, Fechin, Agia, the megatherians, Father Inire's monkey-like appearance, and the undines, sea monsters, Typhon and gods lacking animal associations as evidence. Responds to David Stockhoff's proposed animal-form motif for higher beings.
disputes · close-textual-reading

From: Lee Berman

The megatherians are, by definition, beastial.

Since we don?t know who the megatherians were, that is hardly certain. While the name suggests beasts, the fact of a biography suggests otherwise.

The first two aren?t characters. That said, the humans Rudesind and Fechin are also likened to monkeys. And Agia turns gracefully like a cat. I don?t think there?s any particular motif there, though. People in life and in books are not infrequently likened to animals.

He is described as looking like a monkey (admittedly he is so described by someone who has never seen him). But can it really be considered unusual for aliens to be described in terms of animal analogies? A non-human living creature is likely to remind us of some sort of animal.

On the other hand, the undines don?t seem to be likened to any particular animal, nor are the sea monsters Abaia etc. Nor is Typhon.

Some of the gods have strong animal associations, but others don?t have anything much.

- Gerry Quinn
