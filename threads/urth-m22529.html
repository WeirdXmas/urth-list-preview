The thread: Readers debated what caused the prolonged eclipse at Apu-Punchau's sighting in The Book of the New Sun and The Urth of the New Sun, the sea-monsters and megatherians, and the bean story. Lee Berman argued the Moon, then Severian's own power, caused the events, and rejected Tzadkiel's ship on size and timing. Jeff Wilson argued for Tzadkiel's ship and its time-loop engineering, while Dan'l Danehy-Oakes and Jerry Friedman disputed the megatherian link to the bean story. Gerry Quinn pressed physics objections, including the energy needed to halt the Moon and the danger of a stationary Moon, and linked the monsters to the beans. The thread ended in impasse, with each side keeping its interpretation and no shared answer reached.
  • Lee Berman: The Moon caused the eclipse, later Severian's own power or a shape-shifted Tzadkiel; rejects Tzadkiel's ship as too small, too slow and unnecessary
  • Jeff Wilson: Tzadkiel's ship is the cause and engineers the parking and sailings to preserve time loops; mirror sails could cast the shadow
  • Gerry Quinn: Sea-monsters grew from beans thrown into Urth's seas; halting the Moon would be catastrophic and demand more energy than the Ship can supply
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: Bean story and megatherians link is unsecured; a stationary Moon would distort tides and cause earthquakes
  • Larry Miller: Bean story and the undine gate disturbance both break off unfinished in one chapter, linking them to the megatherians
  • Jerry Friedman: The megatherian identification is not secure; another reading is possible

Themes: Time loops and causality, The dying sun and the cosmos, Myth and folklore sources

2012-01-06 Lee Berman #

Argues that the miracle of Apu Punchau's prolonged night in The Urth of the New Sun was caused by the Moon rather than a ship or black hole, citing Severian's pattern of blaming celestial bodies for disasters. Rejects the Tzadkiel's-ship and B, F and O ship theories on size, timing and approach, and links the Moon idea to the eclipse in A Connecticut Yankee.
proposes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: The Moon caused Apu Punchau's prolonged night; Tzadkiel's ship causes the night; B, F and O's ship causes the night

This could be a could time to resurrect a discussion regarding Apu Punchau's miracle. I think the pertinent information we are given is:

In my first reading I took the "thoughtful reader" reference to be ironic, meaning very little thought is required to guess that the moon (or Lune, as it were) was used. This was in keeping with the trend of other Severian-produced clamities when he is troubled, like storms, earthquakes and ship blackouts, which seem associated with the Urth or other celestial bodies.

I was surprised to learn from this board that others assume it is Tzadkiel's ship or perhaps B, F and O's smaller ship, come to deliver a black hole to the center of the old sun. The popularity of this theory is notable, but I have trouble with it for a number of reasons. Tzadkiel's ship doesn't seem to want to enter the solar system. B, F and O's ship isn't big enough. And the timing for either doesn't seem to be right. The black hole could either be a naturally occurring phenomenon or a punishment delivered during Typhon's time, but why bring it during this primitive time? Also, B, F and O don't arrive until a while later, after Severian has been Apu Punchau for a while and has been killed.

You could stretch the time frame around using time travel or other explanations. But I still like the moon theory best. Partly because it seems the simplest and most direct interpretation from the text and partly because it better alludes to the eclipse in A Connecticut Yankee in King Arthur's Court.

In fact, we do have some idea of the power needed to perform a resurrection insofar as it is denumerable in units of watt-seconds or whatever.
