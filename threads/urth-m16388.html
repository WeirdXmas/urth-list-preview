The thread: Antonio Pedro Marques asked whether the four-way triumphal arch of Septimius Severus at Leptis Magna was common and linked a similar Constantine arch, without tying it to Wolfe. Lee Berman proposed the arcus quadrifons in Short Sun ties to Janus Quadrifrons, with the Whorl as a faster-cycling model of planets and universes, resting on an Arc de Triomphe comparison. Marques corrected that the arches are square with four equal faces and suggested Severus's name and Constantine's might hint toward Severian's name. Mo Holkar answered that Severian's name draws on several strands of reference, such as Severin in Venus in Furs and five saints so named in Lexicon Urthus. The thread ends with the Severian name hint left as several possible sources rather than settled.

2011-08-20 António Pedro Marques #

Asks whether the four-way triumphal arch of Septimius Severus at Leptis Magna, an arcus quadrifons, was common or rare, and mentions a similar arch attributed to Constantine. Links a photo of the Leptis Magna arch and a page on the Janus arch, without drawing any connection to Wolfe's text.
questions

Sorry if this have been brought up before.

Septimius Severus's triumphal arch in Leptis Magna (Libya) is an arcus quadrifons, i.e. a square with an arch in each side. I don't know whether such arches were normal or rare; another one is one that is tought to have been erected by Constantine, but for no known purposes.

2011-08-22 Lee Berman replying to António Pedro Marques #

Replying to Antonio Pedro Marques, the poster argues that the arcus quadrifons in Short Sun may tie to the Janus Quadrifrons idea of destruction and rebirth, suggesting the Whorl is a faster-cycling model of planets and universes. The argument rests on Marques's comparison with the Arc de Triomphe, based on the Arch of Titus, and on a Wikipedia note about Janus Quadrifrons, which the poster offers as a possible link to the cycles introduced in The Book of the New Sun.
disputes · source-and-allusion · myth-and-structure · theories: Whorl is a faster-cycling model of planets and universes

Antonio Pedro Marques:

Well, I don't know a lot about architecture but I remember the Arc de Triomphe in Paris being quadrifronic http://middlezonemusings.com/wp-content/uploads/2008/01/arc-de-triomphe.jpg And it is based on the Arch of Titus, in Rome. So, I'd guess it is a pretty standard form.

With regard to its significance in Short Sun, I think this blurb from Wikipedia about Janus Quadrifrons might tie the reference to the concept of planetary and universal destruction and rebirth which was introduced in BotNS. Perhaps the Whorl is meant to show us a faster cycling version of the planets which, in turn, are faster cycling models of the universe.

2011-08-24 António Pedro Marques replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that the quadrifrons arches of Septimius Severus and Constantine are truly square with all four faces equal, correcting the idea that the Arc de Triomphe-style form is the model. Notes the interest of linking Severus's name and the first Christian emperor to the word quadrifrons, as a possible hint toward Severian's name.
disputes · source-and-allusion

No, the severin and the possibly constantinian ones really are square, all faces being the same size.

Yes; I just found it interesting that Severus's name (I never saw an explanation for Severian's name, and for whatever reason I expected one) and possibly the first christian emperor were associated to the word 'quadrifons'.

2011-08-24 Mo Holkar / UKG replying to António Marques #

Argues that Severian's name draws on several strands of reference rather than one source, noting a possible link to Severin in Sacher-Masoch's Venus in Furs and that Lexicon Urthus lists five saints so named. Responds to António Marques, who said he never saw an explanation for Severian's name and expected one.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Severian's name draws on several sources

There has been a bit of discussion relating
Severian's name to the character Severin in
Sacher-Masoch's Venus in Furs
Wolfe being Wolfe,
there are likely several strands of reference brought together in the name.

(According to LU, there are 5 saints so named.)

best,

Mo
