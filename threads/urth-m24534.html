The thread: The thread began with Marc Aramini noting that an interview piece on Latro and Aphrodite was edited to add page references and identify Aphrodite as love goddess, and Lee Berman reported Wolfe's own distinction that Aphrodite is the sex goddess and Ares a soldier god. Andrew Mason disputed this, arguing Kypris is a love goddess and the distinction reflects people growing into assigned roles. jbarach read the Whorl goddess Kypris through the Outsider, so love serves or corrupts depending on right ordering. Lee Berman then argued the Outsider may be a lesser, more involved deity and that Wolfe shifts from chess to card-game analogies. The thread drifted from interview wording toward cosmology without resolving the naming dispute.

2013-06-19 John Barach replying to David Stockhoff #

Argues that the Whorl goddess Kypris is good or destructive depending on whether her worship stands in right relation to the Outsider, so love serves or corrupts depending on that ordering. Draws a parallel to There Are Doors, where the church favours love pursued rightly but warns it can become a demonic idol.
proposes · theology-and-religion · theories: Kypris is good only in right relation to the Outsider

If Kypris is in proper relation to the Outsider, then she's just love, isn't she? If you worship Love as a goddess while not serving the Outsider, then she's a destructive and terrible goddess. If you serve love in right relation to the Outsider, then she's a good force. Isn't that some of what's going on?

Incidentally, you have similar themes in There Are Doors. Love's a great thing, and that's why Mama (the church) is all in favor of pursuing it ... the right way. But love is also a deadly and dangerous thing and it can become an destructive and demonic idol.

John

2013-06-20 Lee Berman replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that the Outsider in The Book of the New Sun may be a lesser, more involved deity than the Increate, so deviation from his plan is possible, and that Wolfe shifts from a chess analogy to a card-game analogy in the Long and Short Sun books. Grounds this in the chess-piece metaphor, the Silk-Outsider connection, and the gods of the Whorl being encoded in cards that are reshuffled across the later books.
proposes · theology-and-religion · myth-and-structure · theories: Outsider is lesser deity than the Increate; Long Sun gods are reshuffled cards; Wolfe shifts from chess to card analogy

Within the lupine universes, is it possible to not serve the Outsider?

In my view of BotNS, we have humans who are "pawns" who can only move one step forward at a time, in contrast with a "queen" like Tzadkiel who can presumably move multiple steps in any direction on the chessboard. Yet above Tzadkiel is the Increate, who is so simultaneous on every square and within every chess piece that it becomes impossible to do anything (even breaking chess rules) which is not a part of the Increate and his plan.

I'm not sure whether the Outsider is meant to represent the Increate or perhaps an order of divinity somewhere between Tzadkiel and the Increate. If the Outsider is the Increate I think it would be impossible not to serve him and his plan.

But if the Outsider is meant as something a bit lower in divinity and closer and more involved with humanity (as I suspect, due to the direct Silk-Outsider connection; such a connection doesn't seem possible with the Increate), then deviation is possible.

In my view, Wolfe moves from a BotNS game analogy of chess to cards in Long/Short Sun. The gods of the Whorl are physically encoded in cards and these cards get shuffled into new combinations and new card "games" on Blue and even on the later Whorl with its godlings and Silk (silent and silver) as a new mainframe god. In this reshuffling, the Outsider goes from being a minor god to a somewhat all-encompassing god as the 7 book Long/Short series progresses.

This might be an analogy for what Wolfe sees happening with spiritual progression on earth. Perhaps it is a reshuffling of divine cards which allows deities such as Dionysus and Pan and even Jahweh to move from minor, localized gods to major, global gods (and back to minor in the case of Dionysus and Pan).
