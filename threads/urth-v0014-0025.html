Answers Dan Parmenter's question by arguing that the paunchy suited figures in Mauss's reality are villain archetypes, the Queen, Hook and Wolf, who appear that way because that is how villains look in that world. The post is a collage of quoted fragments, including Sturgeon's "window" idea about language and feral children, and a remark on tool use among Aboriginal peoples.

Right. Sturgeon was fascinated by this stuff -- he called it a "window" in some stories published in the late '70s, by analogy with a "launch window" or "window of opportunity." Language in particular seems to be "windowed" in humans; the occasional feral child never really learns language if brought back to human society beyond a certain age ("three" sticks in my mind but that's probably too old).
