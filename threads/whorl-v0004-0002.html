Silk vis a vis God

Whorl list · 3 messages · 1997-04-06 to 1997-04-07

1997whorlThe Book of the Long SunAlice TurnerJames JordanDoug Eigsti
The thread: Readers debated who the Outsider's multiple voices in Silk's Lake passage represent. Alice Turner argued they identify the Outsider as Severian and offered Tanrec, Tenrec or Porpentine as the Vironese alias. Jim Jordan read the voices as God and the Church, the Mother and Bride of Christ, drawing on Catholic theology and suggesting any Severian parallel comes from a shared source. Doug Eigsti disputed both, arguing from Long Sun passages that the Outsider is the supreme Creator who shaped humanity, so Severian can at most be a Conciliator. The thread ended in disagreement with no resolution across the three posts.
  • Alice Turner: the Outsider's voices identify it as Severian
  • Jim Jordan: the voices stand for God and the Church, Mother and Bride
  • Doug Eigsti: the Outsider is the supreme Creator, Severian at most a Conciliator

Themes: Christ figures and messiahs, Catholic faith and sacraments, Gods, angels and the Increate

1997-04-06 Alice Turner #

Argues that the Outsider's multiple voices in the Lake passage about Silk's enlightenment identify the Outsider as Severian. Responds to Dr. Gouvea's puzzle about a Vironese alias by suggesting Tanrec, Tenrec or Porpentine, after dismissing Porcupine and Hedgehog as obvious choices.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Severian is the Outsider; Gouvea's Vironese alias is Tanrec or Tenrec

With regard to the identity of the Outsider, let me remind you of Lake, 285, where Silk is telling Crane about his enlightenment:

"Voices...One spoke into each ear most of the time. One was very masculine--not falsely deep, but solid, as if a mountain of stone were speaking. The other was feminine, a sort of gentle cooing; yet both voices were his...I believe, too, that the Outsider has a great many more voices as well. I could hear them in back of me at times, although indistinctly. It was as if a crowd were waiting behind me while its leaders whispered in my ears; but as if the crowd was actually all one person, somehow: the Outsider...."

If that isn't Severian, I'll eat my mouse.

Dr. Gouvea, assuming you've already rejected Porcupine and Hedgehog, the obvious choices for your Vironese alias, what about Tanrec or Tenrec (alternate spellings)? It's a spiny native of Madagascar. Or you could try Porpentine--I think that's from Othello, and most melodious.

Hush yo' mouf, mantis.

-alga-

1997-04-07 James Jordan #

Proposes that the Outsider's voices in Silk's account stand for God and the Church, the Mother of believers and Bride of Christ, drawing on Catholic theology. Grounds this in similar maternal figures in There Are Doors and Westwind, and says any Severian parallel likely comes from a shared source rather than the Outsider being Severian.
proposes · theology-and-religion · allegory-and-typology · theories: The Outsider is God and the Church

Hope you like plastic! :-) Or is this a pet mouse....?

I'd say it is God and the Church, the Mother of believers and Bride of Christ. Not only does this work with theology, specificially Roman Catholic theology, but it is also in Wolfe, as in the Italian Restaurant run by "Mamma and her sons" in *There Are Doors,* and as in the woman who runs the church-hostel in "Westwind."

If the same thing is going on with Severian -- and I'm not sure it is (though it is an interesting symbolic suggestion) -- then it is because they have a common source, not because the Outsider is Severian.

For my money, anyway. (In real life, a protestant theologian by trade.)

Signed: Big Rat (Nutria) -- and by the way, a Nutria might look like a mouse, but I'm not YOUR mouse, so don't try slaughtering and roasting me!

1997-04-07 Doug Eigsti #

Argues that the Outsider is better supported as the supreme Creator who shaped humanity from mud, so Severian cannot be the Increate though he may be a Conciliator, and that Silk's struggle leads to the same conclusion. Grounds this in quoted passages from Long Sun (IV, p284.9 and p341) about the Outsider shaping forebears and creating Pas, and in the triune-God reading that accommodates a difficult passage with male and female voices.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theology-and-religion · theories: The Outsider is the supreme Creator; Severian is a Conciliator not the Increate; Silk concludes Severian is the One True God

Alga,

I'll have to chime in on the side of Nutria. The Outsider is better supported in the text as being the supreme being, The Creator:

(IV, p284.9) "...who shaped our forebears from the mud of the Short Sun Whorl."

(IV,p341.-5) "created Pas"

No one would like to see Severian make a cameo in LONG SUN more than I. I cannot, however, place Severian in the role of the Increate. Concilliator, yes. It is easier to accommodate the one difficult text you quoted, with the male and female voices, by incorporating God's triune nature, as Nutria has, than to assume that Severian is the One True God. For this is the conclusion Silk comes to. His struggle with his beliefs is one of the key themes in the book. Severian just does not fit. I would love to see further evidence to the contrary. If it's there I missed it completely.

=Talon=
