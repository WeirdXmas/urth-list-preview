Themes: Doubles, twins and clones, Hidden identities and parentage

1998-04-02 Ron Crown #

Proposes that the unnamed narrator of The Fifth Head of Cerberus is really Jean, a Wolfe-like Gene, and that his family name is Wolfe, reasoning from sibling-naming patterns, the house name Maison du chien, and the numbered clones. Grounds the case in the clone-sister Jeanine, the Severian-Severa naming pattern, and the story Procreation in Endangered Species.
proposes · puzzle-solving · source-and-allusion · theories: Narrator of Fifth Head is Jean Wolfe; Clones are numbered Jean-5 and Jean-6

Further clues on narrator's identity: The name of his clone-sister is Jeanine; by the naming convention for siblings we see elsewhere in Wolfe (e.g. Severian-Severa), this should make the narrator "Jean." Technically, this becomes "John" in English but the French pronunciation of "Jean" is similar to English "Gene." (Note also the story "Procreation" in Endangered Species where two narrators, "Gene" and his sister, appear allowing Wolfe to pun on the story's theme of creation).

For the last name "Wolfe," we have the name of the house where the narrator lives, "Maison du chien;" "chien" means "dog" normally but can also mean "wolf" (I checked).

The thought occurs to me, if the narrator's name is Jean, then what is his brother's name, who is presumably also a clone. I think that it's reasonable to suppose the clones are numbered; indeed, the title should alert us to that. Then, the narrator is Jean-5? and his brother is Jean 6? (I can't remember if the brother is younger or older).

Ron Crown

Proposes that the brother in The Fifth Head of Cerberus is a control in the experiment rather than a clone, and asks who Number 1 is in the numbered series. Responds to an earlier post that suggested the clones are numbered as Jean-5 and Jean-6, and introduces the poster as a first-time contributor.
