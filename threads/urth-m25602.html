The thread: A reading-list thread that drifted from Wolfe into general books. Antonin Scriabin and Daniel Otto Jack Petersen, with Mark Lewin, traded recommendations, most notably R. A. Lafferty's Apocalypses, which Scriabin and Petersen praised as excellent while Dan'l Danehy-Oakes found later Lafferty weaker. Scriabin and Petersen also debated whether Wolfe's afterword to a Lafferty book was chosen or assigned, and whether the filming-crucifixions passage could be excerpted. The thread ended with no agreement, just separate recommendations and a brief echo from David Duffy.
  • Antonin Scriabin: Lafferty's Apocalypses is a favourite and Wolfe's afterword excerpting would fail because of the Quiche payoff
  • Daniel Otto Jack Petersen: Apocalypses deserves more regard, and Wolfe may have been assigned the afterword
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: Apocalypses was their first Lafferty and later books have worked less well
  • Piotr Szczęsny: Empire Star reads like Wolfe, with an unreliable narrator and a San Severina echoing Severian
  • Fred Kiesche: Delany may have influenced Wolfe as much as Wolfe influenced Delany
  • Marc Aramini: The Fifth Head of Cerberus has shadow children and a real death, and Port Mimizon is the inutile hand

Themes: Stories within stories, Books, libraries and writing, Genre: science fiction, fantasy, both

2014-03-12 Craig Brewer replying to Antonin Scriabin #

Recommends Jeff VanderMeer's Annihilation as a fresh continuation of the weird tradition and lists other recent reading, including Lafferty, Edith Södergran, Chambers and Michael Cisco. Offers no argument about Wolfe; the post is a social reading-list reply to a thread about what people are reading.
chats

Just finished the new Lafferty collection as well. Good to revisit old masters. :)

I can't recommend Jeff Vandermeer's _Annihilation_ highly enough. It's part of a new "trilogy" (the others will be out by September), but each book is going to be quite different. It's the smartest, most entertaining, and most effective continuation of the "weird" tradition I've read in years. He learned everything you're supposed to learn from Bierce, Blackwood, Machen, Lovecraft, C.A. Smith, and the others, and turned it into something fresh.

Poetry: Edith Sodergran, a Finnish poet (translated, don't worry), and I think Wolfe fans would appreciate her sensibilities.

I also read Chambers' _The King in Yellow_ based on all the _True Detective_ buzz. Fun, but not mind blowing.

And my new discovery is Michael Cisco. I don't know how to describe what he writes, so I'll just leave it at that.
