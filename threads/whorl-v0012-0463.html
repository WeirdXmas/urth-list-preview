2001-03-16 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that the Mother in The Book of the Long Sun can disguise part of herself as a human woman but cannot change her vast submerged mass, and admits the other questions are unknowable from the text. Responds to a numbered list of questions about the Mother, Greater Scylla and Cilinia's death by drawing on Horn's iceberg comparison and suggesting Wolfe is playing with names.
answers · close-textual-reading

If I recall correctly, she makes some part of herself look like a human woman for Horn (and earlier for Seawrack). So she has some kind of capacity for disguise, yes, but no ability to change her mass -- as I recall Horn makes a comparison to an iceberg or a mountain-island, that he sees the woman-shaped bit above the water but the vast bulk of the Mother is below.

The short answer though is a limited yes.

In absence of actual knowledge I would guess that she has a similar capacity.

2001-03-16 Robert Borski #

Asks a series of questions about whether the Mother and Greater Scylla of Urth share a shape-changing alien family trait, citing Seawrack's and Horn's remarks about the Mother changing appearance. Also asks how Cilinia died after being scanned into the Whorl, and whether the similarity between the names Cilinia and Scylla means anything.
questions · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: The Mother and Greater Scylla are the same alien family

Some more questions for those of you arguing that the Mother and Greater Scylla of Urth are from the same, galaxy-conquering, bad-ass family of aliens who all speak the same lingua monstre:

1) Are we agreed that the Mother can change shape? Seawrack tells Horn that "She used to look like a woman for me and hold me in her arms, and I used to think the woman was the real her and make her bring the woman back. She looked like a woman for you too. Remember?" Horn also believes the Mother can change shape, and at one point even compares her with the inhumi: "The inhumi can change their faces, for instance, much more than we can by smiling or sucking in her cheeks. But I believe that a better companion might be with the Mother, who--" At which point he's interrupted by Seawrack and never finishes--another of Wolfe's many aposiopeses.

2) By logical extension of the above-stated premise, does this mean that Greater Scylla can also change her shape?

3) How has Cilinia of Urth died? As she tells Horn, "I died young. It can't have been long after I was scanned for the Whorl." Unlike the process that creates Mr. Million, however, we know the scanning process doesn't kill, and apparently someone cared enough about her to bury her in the family mausoleum--it almost had to have been Pas and Echidna, otherwise how would Cilinia--aboard the Whorl as a computer program--even know she had died? But what has killed the eldest daughter of Pas?

4) Are we to make anything of the similarity in names "Cilinia" and "Scylla"? Or is this just more happy coincidence?

Robert Borski
