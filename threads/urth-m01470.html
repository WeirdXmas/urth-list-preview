Previous universe, was Re: Increate on trial

Urth list · 4 messages · 2005-03-31 to 2005-04-01

2005urthThe Book of the New SunEphrem Christopher WalbornRoy C. LackeyDavid Kirby
The thread: Participants debated whether Severian's adventures in the Book of the New Sun take place in a previous universe or in the far future of our own manvantara. Roy C. Lackey argued the text leaves the question open, relying on expanding and collapsing universes, Yesod's timeless vantage, and Severian's admitted ignorance of the previous cycle's humanity. David Kirby proposed parallel universes existing simultaneously within the cycle and questioned what manvantara means. Lackey conceded the cosmology but doubted the future-manvantara reading, and Ephrem Christopher Walborn supplied the OED definition of manvantara as a Hindu cosmological period, which ended the dispute over the word's meaning without resolving the central question.
  • Roy C. Lackey: the text does not settle that the Hierogrammates and Hieros come from a previous universe, and Severian's adventures cannot fall in a future manv
  • David Kirby: Severian's adventures could be in the far future of our own manvantara, with parallel universes existing simultaneously
  • Ephrem Christopher Walborn: supplied the OED definition of manvantara as one of fourteen Hindu cosmological periods making up a kalpa

Themes: The dying sun and the cosmos, Deep time and ruins, Myth and folklore sources

2005-03-31 Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that the Book of the New Sun text does not settle whether the Hierogrammates and Hieros come from a previous universe, citing Severian's statements in The Urth of the New Sun and The Citadel of the Autarch. Grounds the case in the cycle of expanding and collapsing universes, Yesod's timeless vantage, and Severian's admission that he was not told what became of the previous cycle's humanity. Responds to David Kirby's quoted objection that the text trumps Wolfe's post-book statements.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theology-and-religion · theories: Hieros came from a previous universe

It's not that clear cut. In URTH Sev says that "in a previous manvantara" the Hieros shaped the Hierogrammates. (137) But in the chapter "The Key to the Universe" in CITADEL (too much to quote here; the last few pages), he says that the Hierogrammates exist in Yesod, outside of Time and the cycle of expanding and collapsing universes. He also says, in effect, that with each succeeding cycle an evolution takes place so that "all things advance by some minute step". That logically implies -- no, compels -- the advancement to follow whatever went before.

But then, Sev also says: "I was not told what became of the Humanity of that cycle." and "From that vantage point [Yesod] they [the Hierogrammates] look both forward and back, and in so looking they have discovered us. Perhaps we are no more than a race like that who shaped them. Perhaps it was we who shaped them - or our sons - or our fathers. Malrubius said he did not know, and I believe he told the truth. However it may be, they shape us now as they themselves were shaped; it is at once their repayment and their revenge." (242) This implies that the Hieros could have come from a past, present, or future universe -- or so I read it. Ditto for the Urth cycle.

-Roy

2005-03-31 David Kirby replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that Severian's adventures could be set in the far future of our own manvantara rather than in a previous universe, since the cycle may contain parallel universes existing simultaneously. Responds to Roy C. Lackey by citing Urth's and Citadel's statements about the Hierogrammates and Yesod, and by questioning what the word manvantara means.
disputes · source-and-allusion · theology-and-religion · theories: Severian's adventures occur in the future of our manvantara; parallel universes exist simultaneously within the cycle

Of course not! This is Wolfe we're dealing with.

I'll check out CITADEL. Your explanation clearly establishes the expanding and collapsing universes, but I don't see where it shows that Sev's adventures couldn't be taking place in the future of *our* "manvantara," as "G.W."'s note states in the portion of my original post I deleted above. (Damn. "Manvantara" defeated my RANDOM HOUSE UNABRIDGED DICTIONARY, 2d edition, my NEW LEXICON WEBSTER'S DICTIONARY, and dictionary.com. M. Andre-Driussi, in LEXICON URTHUS, failed me as well. So what's the word *really* mean?)

I remember that latter quote, and I've always read it and similar statements to refer to a "look both forward and back" within an existing universe. (Your ". . . that cycle" quote pretty clearly kills that idea, assuming that Sev understands what he is told, that what he's told is correct, and that "G.W." translated Sev correctly. Sev isn't exactly well versed in 1980s or 2000s cosmology. [2000th? First time I've used that formation!])

This is going to get a bit too metaphysical for me, but . . . .

There clearly are, within the Cycle, parallel universes and jumpings between parallel universes. I read these parallel universes as existing simultaneously (or as existing as close to simultaneously as makes no difference, because an epoch might as well be a second when we're dealing with the existence of a universe). Think DC Comics' (pre-1985) Earth-1, Earth-2, and Earth-X, -Y, and -Z, if you're familiar with the concept. I don't see a requirement that the parallel universes exist successively outside a given "metaverse" or "Bang-Gnab cycle," to bring up a term James Wynn and I used in messages recently posted under another heading.

The time in which our universe has existed and will exist is huge! Our sun is (what?) a 2nd or 4th generation sun. How many generations of "sun" exist before the (current) universe dies? Even without time travel (but that it exists is certainly clear by URTH), why assume the existence of "pre-Bang" universes?

I suspect that replies to this message will depend upon beliefs in an "ultra-universe" god. Perhaps we'd best not get into that.

Thanks!

-- David

2005-04-01 Roy C. Lackey replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Concedes that the earlier explanation of expanding and collapsing universes is right, but questions the logic of universes evolving by "some minute step" and doubts that Severian's adventures can fall in a future manvantara. Compares the problem to a misplaced object in a drawer and jokingly predicts that Lexicon Urthus will have the word manvantara on a second look.
disputes · close-textual-reading

You're right. I realized that after I posted. I guess I have a problem with the basic concept of things existing before they are even created; but that's just me, I suppose. Still, I can't get past that "all things advance by some minute step" part in the evolution of universes. It ain't logical.

Have you ever looked for something -- say in a drawer -- where you know it's supposed to be, but it just isn't there? So you look for it everywhere else, to no avail. Then you look in that drawer again and -- presto! -- there it is. No? Well, I bet if you look in LU again it will be there this time. That, or I'm no magician. <g>

-Roy

2005-04-01 Ephrem Christopher Walborn replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Supplies the Oxford English Dictionary definition of "manvantara" as one of fourteen Hindu cosmological periods, each presided over by a Manu, that together make up a kalpa. Responds to Roy C. Lackey's post, which quoted the OED after Walborn said standard dictionaries and Lexicon Urthus failed to define the word.
informs · source-and-allusion

"Roy C. Lackey" <[email]> wrote on Fri, 1 Apr 2005 02:10:31 -0600:

From OED, 2nd edition:

manvantara [Skr., f. Manu + antara period. ] In Hindu cosmology: One of the 14 periods, each presided over by a special `Manu' or cosmic deity, which make up a kalpa.

* 1830 Lyell Princ. Geol. I. ii. 6 There has been a long succession of manwantaras or periods.

* 1834 Nat. Philos. III. Astron. App. 117 (Usef. Knowl. Soc.) The Kalpa was subdivided into 14 Manwantaras, each of 308448000 years with the addition of 1728000 years to make up the Kalpa.
