The Death of Doctor Island Movie

Urth list · 26 messages · 2006-10-15 to 2006-10-18

2006urthCliff JudgeMo Holkar / UKGDaniel D JonesDavid DuffyNathan SpearsJeff WilsonJesper SvedbergKurt Schwindpeter heynemanDavid DiGiacomo
The thread: Jesper Svedberg announced a short film adaptation of The Death of Doctor Island and asked whether others knew of it, prompting a debate over whether Wolfe's shifty narration can survive filming. Peter Heyneman and others argued the short stories translate better than The Wizard Knight, with Nathan Spears and Transentient proposing other works. David DiGiacomo called the story disturbing and offensive, and Kurt Schwind pushed back on that claim. The thread then turned to misogyny in Wolfe's fiction, with Mo Holkar, Jeff Wilson, and Daniel D Jones arguing over whether the charge holds and whether period attitudes excuse it. It ended without agreement, drifting into a comparison with Mark Twain's racism.
  • Jesper Svedberg: announced the short film and hoped for a triptych of adaptations
  • Kurt Schwind: Wolfe's shifty narration is essential and Hollywood would strip it out
  • peter heyneman: the short stories film better than The Wizard Knight
  • David DiGiacomo: the story is accessible yet disturbing and offensive, hard to film for mass audiences
  • Daniel D Jones: misogyny serves the story's force and reflects 1973 attitudes
  • Mo Holkar / UKG: misogyny is hard to avoid in Wolfe and he tolerates it for the writing

Themes: Women, sex and gender, Stories within stories, Books, libraries and writing

2006-10-16 peter heyneman replying to Kurt Schwind #

Argues that Wolfe's short stories from that era, such as The Death of Doctor Island, translate better to film than The Wizard Knight, whose shifting narration and withheld detail would become a dry sword-and-sorcery tale on screen. Responds to Kurt Schwind's question whether the stories were more accessible than The Wizard Knight, agreeing that the Wizard Knight suits teen readers.
answers · genre-and-craft

In terms of translation to pure visuals, definitely. WK, like most of his books, has so much shifty narrator business and perspective-skewing mysterious detail-dropping that turning it into a movie would be almost impossible to do right--it'd lose much of its quality and become another dry Sword and Sorcery tale. His short stories, especially from that era, rely less on the voice and more on the image.

In terms of appeal to 15 year old WOW players, I agree Wizard/Knight is perfect.

--p

2006-10-16 Nathan Spears replying to Jesper Svedberg #

Argues that There Are Doors has the least machination and hidden meaning of the Wolfe works the poster has read, making it the easiest of his books to translate to film. Responds to Jesper Svedberg's claim that Wolfe's shifty narration resists filming by suggesting a Soderbergh-directed adaptation with a Gattaca-like path to acceptance.
proposes · genre-and-craft · theories: There Are Doors is the most easily filmed Wolfe work

There Are Doors, an understated, observed piece by Steven Soderbergh. Has a Gattaca-like path to acceptance. I think TaD has the least amount of machination and hidden meaning/plot of the Wolfe canon (that I've read) and would thus be the most easily translated to film.
