1999-02-04 Kieran Cleary #

Recommends Paul J. McAuley's Child of the River as a book with stylistic and thematic similarities to Wolfe, possibly a homage. Criticises it for explaining too much where Wolfe is oblique, and says its style resembles The Book of the New Sun while its plot owes more to The Book of the Long Sun.
proposes · genre-and-craft · theories: McAuley's Confluence series is a homage to Wolfe

Anybody read Paul J. McAuley's Child of The River (first book of the Confluence series)? The stylistic and
thematic similarities are so obviously close to Wolfe, it must be some sort of homage. One major flaw though: McAuley tells us too much. Where Wolfe is coy and oblique, McAuley rushes in with the solution and the effect in a lupine setting is slightly jarring. The book is similar in style to tBotNS while the plot owes more to tBotLS. Worth a read.

Kieran C.

1999-02-05 William H. Ansley #

Jokingly responds to a post comparing Paul J. McAuley's Child of the River to Wolfe, saying a Wolfe-like book without Wolfe's coyness would be a relief rather than a frustration. Builds on the quoted complaint that McAuley explains too much where Wolfe stays oblique, with no real analysis of the books.
