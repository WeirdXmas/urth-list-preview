1999-12-14 Ron Hale-Evans #

Jokingly claims authorship of a Barthes essay as a joke, then argues that critics who declare the author dead still expect copyright, money and fame for their own work. Replies to corncrake's quoted question about who really wrote a text, using the joke as a jab at Continental theory.
chats

He didn't -- I did. The edition I'm bringing out has my byline on it as proof. :-)

Seriously, for all that the Continental critics claim the author is dead, you don't see them refusing money and fame for their work. It is copyrighted, and if someone plagiarised one of these critics as I just proposed to do, the critic would undoubtedly sue him; conclusions about the integrity of the Continental critics are left as an exercise for The Reader.

Ron H-E

1999-12-14 Peter Stephenson #

Argues that a Wolfe FAQ cannot replace repeated discussion, since the same questions recur in the archive with different answers embedded in new threads. Responds to a quoted definition of FAQs by questioning whether a FAQ can settle the mysteries of The Book of the New Sun, then jokingly asks whether Barthes actually wrote his text.
disputes

Hmmm. I don't think this is entirely appropriate with Wolfe. If you look back in the archive I think you'll find we have had the same question over and over, and each time with different answers embedded in a completely different discussion. I certainly wouldn't want to think the questions shouldn't be asked again, nor that `read the FAQ' would ever be an adequate response to anything more than the simplest question. And from the other discussions recently, that doesn't even seem to include `who is the author'.

(A propos of which, I can't resist the following, but please feel free to stop reading now.)

But how do we know that Barthes `wrote' it? And what do we mean by `write' anyway? etc.

corncrake
