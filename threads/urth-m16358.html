2011-08-11 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes replying to Lane Haygood #

Argues that Michael Moorcock's output splits into two writers: quick swords-and-sorcery books like Corum and Hawkmoon, and witty satirical works like Gloriana and the Pyat quartet, with Elric and Jerry Cornelius in between. Qualifies Lane Haygood's praise of all the Eternal Champion books by excepting the ERB pastiche written for money, and lists examples of each tier.
disputes · theories: Moorcock's output comes from two writers

Well, *almost* every Moorcock -- there was his ERB ripoff written purely for money.

I can't go quite that far.

It helps to understand that there appear to be two writers living in Michael Moorcock's head. One writes quick-and-dirty swords'n'sorcery like the majority of the EC books. These are not bad books by any means, but they don't go an inch beneath the surface. These would include -- just off the top of my head -- the Corum books, the Hawkmoon books, and the books simply labelled "eternal champion."

Then there's the Moorcock who writes elegant "comedies" like GLORIANA, the Pyat quartet, and "the Dancers at the End of Time." These are remarkably good books, full of satire and wit. And there are quite a few of them -- I want to mention BEHOLD THE MAN, and THE WARHOUND AND THE WORLD'S PAIN, and the Oswald Bastable books (which are steampunk), and BREAKFAST IN THE RUINS, and and and...

But, somewhere in the middle of these, are Elric and Jerry Cornelius ... oh my. Elric is a witty swords'n'sorcery series, almost a parody of s&s, but never failing to take its matter seriously in context. And Jerry Cornelius... well, some days I think Cornelius is Moorcock's crowning achievement; other days I think it's a vast hoax. Either way, I wouldn't be without the ten or so books that constitute the greater Cornelius Chronicles. Amazing stuff.

To make things more confusing, Elric interacts with characters from both the "lower" tier and the "upper" tier (appearing now with Corum and Hawkmoon, and then in the End of Time...), and both of them (as well as a few characters in the "upper" tier) are aspects of that damned Eternal Champion.

2011-08-11 Lane Haygood replying to Son of Witz #

Recommends Michael Moorcock's Elric books and the wider Eternal Champion series as subversive sword-and-sorcery, contrasting them with Terry Goodkind's work. Responds to Son of Witz, who asked whether Elric is fun or trash, by describing Elric as an anti-Conan and noting the genre they helped define.
supports

The Elric books (ANY Moorcock, for that matter) is in a different time zone than Goodkind's trash.

Moorcock was a very subversive counterculture figure in the 60s and 70s and his books reflect it. Elric was written as the Anti-Conan, a sickly intellectual who concerns himself too much with ethics and turns to violence and demonology only as a last resort. But they are adventure tales in the grand S&S fashion. Still, they defined the dark heroic fantasy genre.

I recommend all of the Eternal Champion books, actually.
