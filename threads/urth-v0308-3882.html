2003-08-13 James Wynn #

Argues against the one-ship theory by proposing the Whorl was a one-way ship that is breaking down, and that Severian's ship could have been the Whorl retrofitted over time. Responds to Ash's claims about chems, Jonas, Sideros and names, citing Silk's mention of the shiprock and Kypis's familiarity with robots.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: The Whorl was a one-way ship; Severian's ship was first the Whorl, retrofitted; Jonas is not a mineral or chem; Sideros is a talos not a chem

Ash quoted:
"There is only one ship, the captain says. All the ships we hail between the galaxies or the suns are this ship."

Crush responds:
Nice attempt. The bit about "only one ship" is a good arguement, but IMO there is at least an equally compelling arguement against it: The Whorl is breaking down.

Silk mentions the breaking shiprock that was supposed to last until the end of the Whorl. Well the Whorl was in orbit around Blue and Green for some 30 years before the colonists started to leave, and since the shiprock is deteriorating, and since the Whorl stayed in orbit for another 30? years after the colonists began to depart, it suggests that the Whorl was intended for a one way trip.

Also, the Whorl was built by Typhon. Could Severian's ship have been?

Your theory does, however, make me wonder about what the Whorl technicians thought what was to ultimately to become of them.

Ash says:
...it contains Chems very similar in construction and role to those found in the Whorl. Jonas himself seems to be a chem, and is a sailor as well. Jonas does not seem to be his original name, but the name of the sailor whos body he made use of, much as he took on the name Miles at a later point.

Crush responds:
Well, Jonas is a different kind of chem than Marble. All the chems are very heavy (Sand is "five-hundred weight" and Marble is too heavy for Silk to lift) but when Severian picked up Jonas he was surprised to discover how light he was. While the story reiterates two or three times how light Hyacinth is and I think there's something to *that*, it is clear that Jonas is a different kind of chem from the standard chems of the Whorl. Mind, I believe Jonas' light weight does work into the Long Sun plot.

Nevertheless, IIRC it was Miles who was the human body that Jonas took on -- a whole-body-prosthesis for a robot, just as the Ayuntamiento took chems as whole-body prosthesises. Jonas was all artificial, and Jonas is not a mineral (I don't think).

Ash argues:

2003-08-13 Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that the Whorl seen from Blue cannot have been carried inside Tzadkiel's ship, and that Jonas is a chem rather than a suit like Sidero. Supports the point by citing the Whorl's visibility to the naked eye from Blue in Exodus and Crane's remark about chem weight in Lake of the Long Sun, answering Ashley Crill's claim that it is the same ship Severian sailed to Yesod.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: The Whorl is not contained within Tzadkiel's ship; Jonas is a chem not a suit

Nah. Silk and others from the airship saw the _Whorl_ and the Short Sun of the Blue-Green system from outside the ship when they visited Mainframe in EXODUS. The colonists saw the ship as well, from the landers. The _Whorl_ was visible to the naked eye in orbit from Blue, therefore could not have been contained within another vessel. Tzadkiel's ship, at a comparable distance, would have blotted out the stars in a big chunk of night sky.

found in the Whorl. Jonas himself seems to be a chem, and is a sailor as well.<<

Sidero and his kind were glorified spacesuits with an attitude. They were hollow, meant to be worn by men, so they were relatively light of weight. Sev put on Sidero. Jonas was also lightweight (as Sev noted when he picked him up in the Antechamber), but was not large enough for a man to climb inside. Chems are an entirely different kind of machine. They have an access plate in their torsos, but are not hollow.
