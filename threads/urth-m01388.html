The thread: Kieran Mullen opened an off-topic discussion of Watchmen, arguing that Ozymandias is a villain and that the comic is about the dehumanizing effect of power, then offered Dan Simmons' Hyperion reading of Abraham's binding as a test of God. Iorwerth Thomas backed that reading as at least as good as others and possibly consistent with Kierkegaard, though he doubted it clarifies Severian in Wolfe's books. maru disputed Simmons' answer to Kierkegaard's problem of Abraham in Fear and Trembling, suggesting that if Yahweh was a false god, Abraham was a deluded killer of his son. The thread drifted far from Wolfe and ended without resolution.
  • Kieran Mullen: Ozymandias is a villain, Watchmen is about power's dehumanizing effect, and Abraham's binding was a test of God per Simmons
  • Iorwerth Thomas: The test-of-God reading is at least as good as others and may match Kierkegaard, but it doesn't clarify Severian
  • maru: Simmons' answer to Kierkegaard is flawed; if Yahweh was false, Abraham was a deluded killer

Themes: Suffering and sacrifice, Gods, angels and the Increate, Power, rulers and politics

2005-03-22 Kieran Mullen #

Argues that Ozymandias in Watchmen is plainly a villain rather than a hero and that the comic is about the dehumanizing effect of power, and cites Rorschach mailing the journal to newspapers as the failure of his scheme. Offers a comparison to Dan Simmons' Hyperion, where Abraham's binding is read as a test of God, after a Kierkegaard-based question on Abraham from James Wynn's post. This is an off-topic thread on comics and philosophy, not Wolfe.
chats

My favorite answer to this was the one provided by Dan Simmons in the Hyperion novels. One of his characters claims that actually Abraham had to go through with this because he was testing God. That is, any God who would actually make you go through with such a demand was not worth worshiping. He had to carry it out to the end to see what would happen.

I never thought Ozymandias was a hero in The Watchmen. It seems odd to even contemplate! He's so clearly the proud, self-impressed villain. He's eeeeeevil. That's the point of the pirate comic - the protagonist acts so single-mindedly that he realizes in the end he's become as bad as the pirates he hated.

Hmmm.... I wouldn't call it shameless. It would be very odd if this argument were not made. We see it made all the time today, justifying all sorts of things.

Of course, we know that MM's action ultimately is a failure - Rorshach mailed out the journal to the newspapers. I think Watchmen is about the dehumanizing effect of power.
