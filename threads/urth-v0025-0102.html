Inire as Big Sky Daddy

Urth list · 1 messages · 1999-04-18 to 1999-04-18

1999urthThe Book of the New SunRobert Borski

Themes: Gnosticism, heresy and other theologies, Masks, disguise and shapeshifting

1999-04-18 Robert Borski #

Argues that Inire, the offstage alien figure, symbolically resembles a supreme sky-god, and that his name may be Abraxas, the Gnostic supreme being. Grounds this in Basilides' view of Abraxas and in the fact that Inire is never met directly, known only through Thecla, Odilio and Hildegrin's accounts.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theology-and-religion · theories: Inire's name is Abraxas; Inire stands as a Gnostic supreme being

mantis having written:

<So is Inire "Yaweh," the big sky-daddy himself? No, of course not.>

Literally, he may not be, but symbolically I sure think he comes close, especially, if, as I've suggested, his name really is Abraxas (being an alien Inire should have a mythic name, like all the other cacogens).

At least one ancient writer, Basilides of Eygpt, an early 2nd-Century Gnostic teacher, viewed Abraxas as the supreme being, and in regards to the people of Urth Inire stands similarly, being both paternalistic steward and advocate. In other words, his cajones are major.

The irony of this? As with the oft-mentioned Fechin, we never actually meet Inire. He's always offstage or discussed in anecdotal fashion by others who've met him (Thecla, Odilio, Hildegrin).

Then again, given the importance of masks in New Sun...

Robert Borski
