Ouen and Dorcas

Urth list · 9 messages · 2010-12-04 to 2010-12-06

2010urthThe Book of the New SunJane DelawneyRyan DunnAntónio Pedro MarquesSon of WitzJeff WilsonRoy C. LackeyAndrew Mason
The thread: Readers asked whether Ouen's and the boatman's ages fit the timeline of Dorcas's death and resurrection, with Ryan Dunn noting that decades would have passed. Roy Lackey argued the search lasted about forty years, and Andrew Mason argued Ouen already knows Dorcas is his mother. The central dispute became whether Dorcas died in childbirth bearing a second child, which Jane Delawney proposed and Roy Lackey rejected as only the father's report. Jeff Wilson and Son of Witz argued over whether Dorcas's slender body and apparent age rule out a postpartum death, and Jeff Wilson later offered amnesia and Severian's limited experience as explanations. António Pedro Marques suggested resurrection could restore her in a different physical form, and the thread ended without agreement.
  • Ryan Dunn: the ages of Ouen and the boatman do not fit a timeline of decades, so Dorcas may have been older or the boatman younger
  • Roy C. Lackey: the search lasted about forty years, and the childbirth death is only the father's report with no evidence of a second child
  • Andrew Mason: Ouen already knows Dorcas is his mother, so Lee Berman's assumption about their relationship is wrong
  • Jane Delawney: Dorcas probably died in childbirth bearing a second child, and that child's fate is unaccounted for
  • Jeff Wilson: her slender figure contradicts a recent childbirth, and later amnesia and Severian's limited experience explain it
  • Son of Witz: a slender build does not rule out childbirth, and Dorcas was probably around 18 to 23

Themes: Memory and forgetting, Fathers, mothers and children, Ghosts, dreams and visions

2010-12-04 Ryan Dunn replying to Andrew Mason #

Questions whether the ages of Ouen and the old boatman fit the timeline of Dorcas's death and resurrection, since a boatman in his seventies seeking a wife resurrected as a teenager implies decades have passed. Asks whether Ouen's age, which he recalls as twenties or thirties, has been misread or left undescribed, and suggests the boatman may have been younger when they married.
questions · chronology-and-plot-logic

Can I point out one other time issue?

If the old boatman (in his 70's?) is looking for his wife, Dorcas, who is mid-to-late-teens when resurrected. That would suggest at least fifty years have passed since she died, thereabouts.

Wouldn't Ouen, her son, be in his fifties then? I seem to recall he is more in his twenties or thirties.

Just asking whether I may have missed a description or two. If that cannot be reconciled, then we have to assume the boatman was indeed likely in his thirties or forties when he hooked it up with 'Cas.

...ryan

2010-12-06 Roy C. Lackey replying to Jane Delawney #

Argues that Ouen's account of Dorcas dying in childbirth is his father's report, not evidence of a second child, since the text says nothing about a second child. Responds to Jane Delawney's point about Ouen's forty years of searching by citing Citadel where Ouen says his father told him she died in childbirth, and noting Ouen likely could not remember her.
disputes · close-textual-reading

It was Ouen who said that she died in childbirth, which is what his father had told him (CITADEL, XXXVII). But the text is otherwise silent on the subject of a second child. Ouen knew what his mother looked like from the picture of her he had, but he was probably too young when she died to have actually remembered her.

-Roy

2010-12-06 Jeff Wilson replying to Jane Delawney #

Argues that Dorcas's slender physical description when found at the lake contradicts the idea that she died in childbirth, since she could not have recovered her figure after giving birth. Responds to Jane Delawney's call for other ideas about Ouen and Dorcas, using the note about her parity and the lack of visible stretch marks as evidence.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Dorcas did not die in childbirth

Dorcas' physical description upon being found at the lake does not seem to agree with her having died in the act of childbirth, and the speculation on her parity prompted by the note also suggests she has no readily visible signs like stretch marks to settle the matter. She's otherwise rather slender, so I'm skeptical she could have lived long enough to recover her non-pregnant physique and still die from childbed fever or other birth complications.

2010-12-06 Jane Delawney replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that Dorcas probably died in childbirth while bearing a second child, Ouen's sibling, and asks what became of that child. Bases this on Dorcas's remembered shopping and childcare and on the boatman's account of a few years of marriage, and notes Ouen's age of about forty-two when Severian sends him back to Oldgate.
questions · chronology-and-plot-logic · puzzle-solving · theories: Dorcas died in childbirth bearing a second child

I concur. Also, it is deducible from the text (Dorcas remembers going to buy clothes for her child, remembers looking after a child and so forth) that if Dorcas died in childbirth, as is usually assumed, it was in bearing her *second* child, ie. Ouen's sibling.

What I can't remember right now is whether there is much textual evidence that Dorcas did in fact die in childbirth. All the old boatman tells us is that they'd been married four or five years, then 'Cas died. It's entirely possible she died of other natural causes when Ouen was a toddler.

But if she did die in childbirth, Ouen was two or three years old at the time, which would indeed make him forty-two/three when Sev sends him back to find his mother in Oldgate. But there's no information about what might have happened to any theoretical sibling. Are we to understand that the second child died with his/her mother? Or is there anyone around who might be identified as Severian's paternal uncle or aunt? I think Borski had a theory on this, but without my books to hand I can't remember what this theory was alas, and it's far too late right now for me to come up with any fresh suggestion.

be interested to know if anyone else has any ideas on this though.

cheers

jd

2010-12-06 Son of Witz replying to Jeff Wilson #

Argues that Dorcas's slender build does not rule out her having died after childbirth, since Severian may not know what stretch marks are and some women recover their figure quickly or were underweight in pregnancy. Responds to Jane Delawney's objection on Dorcas's physique and parity, and suggests Dorcas was older than many readers assume, around 18 to 23.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Dorcas died in childbirth

Hmm. I doubt Severian would know what stretch marks were. I've also known women who bounce right back from childbirth to being slender, even without effort. Crazy, I know, but I've seen it. She might also have been malnourished and underweight in the pregancy. I'm surprised everyone sees her so young. I've always thought 18-23 range suited her.

2010-12-06 Jeff Wilson replying to Son of Witz #

Argues that Dorcas's post-birth figure and apparent age can be explained by Severian's limited exposure to pregnant women and by her amnesia, which restores a childlike innocence. Responds to Son of Witz's point about stretch marks and to Jeff Wilson's own remark on her age, drawing on a family anecdote about how fast a woman's body returns after childbirth.
disputes · close-textual-reading

The business about opening pregnant women (presumably post mortem) to take the children would give him some plausible exposure to the appearance of pregnancy if not post-partum presentation.

How fast is "right back?" My mother's oldest sister was a slender woman who gained only the birthweight + placenta of her 6lb second child. She took 3-4 weeks to redistribute which is very fast as that goes, but very long in terms of childbirth complications.

Same here, although her amnesia has restored a child-like innocence making her seem younger.
