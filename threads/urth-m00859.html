Witch spirit and Tales of Silk and Horn

Urth list · 9 messages · 2005-01-06 to 2005-01-08

2005urthThe Book of the Long SunharlekinStone OxAdam StephanidesMarc AraminiChris PJames WynnDan'l Danehy-Oakes
The thread: The thread asked who inhabits Silk's body in Tales of Silk and Horn, and whether Silver Silk was downloaded into Pig, Horn, or the Narrator. Aramini1 proposed a chain of transfers ending in a copy of a copy of Silk, and StoneOx17 asked whether the Outsider has direct authority over souls. Chris argued the thread used soul, spirit and identity loosely, making a three-entity reading hard to sustain. Adam Stephanides rejected the scenario on timing grounds, citing the bandage and Pig's later speech, and Dan'l Danehy-Oakes and James Wynn argued the Narrator-download claim lacks textual support, with Wynn noting Scylla stayed trapped in Oreb. The thread ended with the proposal rejected rather than resolved, and James Wynn also raised a side question on eye-to-eye divine transfer in the Long Sun books.
  • aramini1: Horn's spirit enters Silk and Silver Silk is downloaded into Pig, leaving a copy of a copy of Silk
  • StoneOx17: asks whether souls obey mechanical rules or the Outsider's direct authority
  • Chris: a three-entity reading of Silver Silk is harder to sustain than a two-entity one
  • Adam Stephanides: the Pig-to-Horn transfer fails on timing since the bandage stays on and Silk speaks through Pig later
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: the Narrator-download claim is speculative with no textual evidence
  • James Wynn: Silver Silk stayed in Oreb and wanted out to rejoin Kypris, so no download into the Narrator

Themes: Doubles, twins and clones, Hidden identities and parentage, Ghosts, dreams and visions

2005-01-06 Marc Aramini #

Points out that Org in The Wizard Knight is a chameleon-like mutant whom Able does not call a lizardman, and notes the witch's ghost speaking through wind and rain as Apheta does in Urth. Then proposes a scheme for who inhabits Silk's body: Horn's spirit enters Silk, Silver Silk is downloaded into Pig, and the body ends as a copy of a copy of Silk.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Silver Silk is a download of Silk into Pig; Silk's body holds Horn's spirit after Horn's death; Final Silk is a copy of a copy

I am still about fifty posts short of catching up, so perhaps some of this was covered, but I wanted to respond to a few things i read before I forgot.

A)Wizard Knight: I find it interesting that Org is clearly some kind of mutant chameleon, with scales, a reptillian head, and the ability to blend into the background - but Able doesn't seem to categorize him as a "Lizard"man.

Also, the witches ghost is interesting to me in that she speaks to Able through the wind and the rain instead of with a real voice (in the hut where he meets Mani), just as Apheta does in Urth. Her spirit seems to be hanging out throughout The Wizard, too.

B)
I was reading a few posts called Tales of Silk and Horn and I wanted to say one small thing about that. I think the simplest scheme to describe who inhabits Silk's body at any given point in the narrative is that Horn's body dies on Green, his spirit is sent hurtling into Silk, whose spirit has been so wounded (or has vacated his body) that it is no longer extent in the body.

Then, the quest to find Silk actually ENDS when Horn gives his (ok, Silk's) eye to Pig, allowing Silver Silk, who is a download of Silk from the mainframe into Pig, to re-enter the body of Silk by escaping Pig's eye , creating Silk+Horn. Then, Horn begins writing. He says his goodbyes and leaves the body of Silk, and then Silver Silk, who was never the real Silk, takes up the tale of Horn and his death at the beginning of In Green's Jungles, but denies that he is Silk until he is forced into it, so that in the text, Silk admits that he is in fact Silk at about the same place that Silver Silk is freed from Pig and placed in Silk's body.

So it is a copy of a copy of Silk that we are left with, like the copy of a copy of Severian at the end of Urth.

Hmm.

Marc

2005-01-06 Chris P replying to aramini1 #

Argues that the earlier Silk and Horn thread used soul, spirit and identity as shifting shorthand, so a three-entity reading of Silver Silk is harder to sustain than a two-entity one. Responds to aramini1's proposal by distinguishing a simple view, where a download is only memories and inclinations, from a complex view, where Silver Silk is as much Silk as the original.
disputes · theology-and-religion · puzzle-solving · theories: Silver Silk was never Silk; Horn's spirit moved into Silk's body

It's an interesting angle, but the point of those posts was an issue of what constitutes the soul/spirit/identity. Without diving too deeply into it, the terms "soul" and "spirit", etc, were being used as shorthand in a way that evolved through the thread, so they can't be substituted freely. So your hypothesis is if anything even more problematic in this sense, because now you're dealing with three entities instead of two.

Saying that Silver Silk was "never Silk" commits you to what I was referring to as the "simple view". But, on the simple view, the download/upload process is merely memories and inclinations, not "real" spirit, or identity. And that's all Silver Silk would be, from that perspective: not Silk, not *anyone* in the sense of being an actual person - there would be no spirit associated with Silver Silk.

If you take the complex view, in contrast, you can't say that Silver Silk "was never Silk" because he was as much "Silk" as the original at the time of the download, and has as much claim to the title as the organic version.

So this plotline would just about double the possible branches on the tree of interpretation. And it would double (or more) again if you take into account the possibility that Pas (or someone else) might have downloaded partially into Silk before or after his upload (or before AND after, for that matter).

2005-01-06 James Wynn replying to aramini1 #

Questions whether the gods in the Long Sun books can transfer between people eye-to-eye rather than only through a Sacred Window. Cites the scene where Kypris-In-Chenille says Maytera Mint had a partial download because she looked away, arguing that if eye-to-eye transfer worked Chenille could simply have looked Mint in the eye.
questions · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Gods transfer only through Sacred Windows

Is there any unambiguous example where the gods transfer eye-to-eye rather than exclusively eye-to-Sacred Window?

I can think of one instance that suggests this is impossible. Kypris-In-Chenille tells Silk that Maytera Mint had a partial download of Kypris (because she forced herself to look away). She tells Silk that they need to get Mint to a Sacred Window before she has some sort of breakdown. But if eye-to-eye transfers were possible, Chenille only needed to get alone with Mint and look her in the eye. Thus, it seems, eye to eye transfers are not possible.

~ Crush

2005-01-06 Adam Stephanides replying to aramini1 #

Argues that the proposed Pig-Silk-Horn scenario fails on timing: Silver Silk cannot move from Pig into Horn at the glass scenes in Pig's hospital room because the bandage is still on and Silk still speaks through Pig later. Grounds the case in page references to the glass scenes and Pig's last conversation with Horn, and notes the transfer would have to occur in an unwritten gap.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Silver Silk downloads from Pig into Horn; Silk and Horn merge into Silk+Horn

I won't rehearse my disagreement with the "PigSilkHorn" scenario again, but I did want to mention something that I just noticed (if this has been noted already, I apologize). When precisely is "Silver Silk" downloaded into "Horn"? Twice, in the scene in Pig's hospital room, it is stated that Horn looks into a glass (371-2), and I've seen it suggested that it is at one of these moments. But this won't work, for two reasons. First, both these times are before the bandage has been removed from Pig's eye, so Silk cannot have left Pig yet. Second, Silk is still present in Pig during Pig's last conversation with Horn (374). If Silk's soul does go from Pig to Horn, it must be offstage, in the gap on the page between the last reported words spoken by "Pig" and Hari Mau speaking to Horn in the lander.

But neither of these passages were written by Silkhorn; the juxtaposition of the two is the editors' work.

--Adam

2005-01-07 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes replying to aramini1 #

Argues that the claim that Silver Silk is downloaded into the Narrator remains speculative, since several explanations exist for how the Narrator realizes he is Silk. Notes there is no direct or indirect textual evidence that the Silver Silk persona is reloaded, and urges supporters to find support before elaborating details.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Silver Silk is downloaded into the Narrator

I should like to point out that the downloading of "Silver Silk" into the Narrator remains speculative. There are several explanations (not all mutually exculsive) of how the Narrator comes to realize that he is Silk and what, precisely, it means. Furthermroe, there is, so far as I know, no direct or usefully indirect textual evidence to suggest that the SSilk persona is "reloaded" into the Narrator at _any_ point.

For those who hold that SilverSilk is downloaded into the Narrator,I suggest it would be worth spending a bit of effort finding support for the theory before elaborating its details more.

--Dan'l
