Trivigaunte

Urth list · 1 messages · 2003-06-25 to 2003-06-25

2003urthNicholas Gevers

2003-06-25 Nicholas Gevers #

Proposes that Trivigaunte, the city in The Book of the Long Sun, is named for a Moon Goddess, tracing its origin to the Italian form Trivigante and the Renaissance morality-play figure. Grounds this on an online Occultopedia entry and the claim that a tyrannical character in Eastern robes was assumed female, which suits Wolfe's militaristic, Arabic-speaking city-state of viragos.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Trivigaunte is named after a Moon Goddess; Trivigaunte derives from Italian Trivigante and Termagant

So Trivigante is a name for the Moon Goddess, eh? The
mystery of Trivigaunte in LONG SUN is solved at last.
A typically witty appropriation by Wolfe: in the
online Occultopedia, it's stated that Trivigante is
the original Italian form of Termagant; in the
Renaissance Morality Plays, Trivigante was an
overbearing, tyrannical character in flowing Eastern
robes, which apparel led to the assumption Trivigante
was female. So when Wolfe invented an Arabic-speaking
city state dominated by militaristic viragos,
Trivigaunte was a natural name to assign it...

--Nick Gevers.
