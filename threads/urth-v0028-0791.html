2000-02-10 Jeremy Crampton #

Argues that Wolfe's Soldier books are his most impressive work, showing the social construction of religious belief, with gods that depend on human belief. Cites the human sacrifice scene where Mother Ge says she is still young to others, and Wolfe's Greek setting where gods are met after Marathon, and compares it to Dick, Ballard, Priest and Pratchett.
proposes · theology-and-religion · theories: Gods disappear when people stop believing in them

How about the Soldier series?

Personally they're the most impressive to me (boring personal opinion but there ya go). After Vol I of New Sun, natch.

Okay I'll give one reason: Wolfe shows the social construction of belief (in the fine tradition of most skiffy, eg PKD, Ballard, Priest etc. The latter's The Affirmation is a classic example). Particularly religious belief of course. One of the central scenes in the Soldier books is the human sacrifice one, where Mother Ge talks about how for other people she is yet young, and that how when we stop believing in them the gods disappear, ie they are human-dependent.** There's also all GW's talk of how he is showing society as the Greeks really saw it, ie meeting gods on the way back from Marathon (Fennel Fields). Where are these gods now? Long time passing...

**didn't Terry Pratchett also do this? What was it; Small Gods?

best,
