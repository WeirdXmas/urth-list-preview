blonds of the Commonwealth

Urth list · 6 messages · 2002-07-15 to 2002-07-16

2002urthThe Book of the New SunAlice TurnerMichael Andre-DriussiAndy RobertsonMichael Straight
The thread: Michael Andre-Driussi argued that the fair-skinned Europeanoid people of South America are recent arrivals not yet reshaped to the continent's pattern, citing Severian's remark about generations of transformation. Andy Robertson and Alice K. Turner disputed this, Robertson on the in-world logic that foot travel and rare immigration prevent mass racial mixing, and Turner by pointing to a New York subway and calling the books satire of modern America. Turner then proposed that Typhon/Pas deliberately segregated the cities of the Whorl and Blue to preserve customs and bloodlines, which Michael Straight questioned for lack of an explicit passage, linking it to Silk's remarks on cities and calling it Typhon's Babel. The thread ended without resolution, with the segregation idea left as a contested reading.
  • Michael Andre-Driussi: blonds are recent arrivals not yet transformed to the continent's pattern
  • Alice K. Turner: mixed appearances are plausible; books satirize modern America; Typhon/Pas segregated the cities deliberately
  • Andy Robertson: foot travel and rare immigration make pan-human mixing implausible in-world
  • Michael Straight: asks for explicit textual support; sees the arrangement as Typhon's Babel

Themes: Colonization and the native, Power, rulers and politics, Evolution and the future of humanity

2002-07-15 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Argues that the fair-skinned Europeanoid people of South America are recent arrivals who have not yet been reshaped to the continent's pattern, comparing them to present-day blond Argentines. Answers Hartshorn's question by citing Severian's remark that such transformation takes a set number of generations, and dating the arrival to a few thousand years before Severian's time.
answers · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: European-looking South Americans are recent arrivals not yet altered by the continent's pattern

Right, the implication being that the Europeanoids have not been living on that continent long enough to be genetically altered into looking like the red-brown people of Apu-Punchau. If you can find the quote, iirc Severian gives the number of generations that the transformation requires.

So, like the blond Argentines today, the Europeanoids have come from elsewhere, in their case arriving maybe just a few thousand years before Severian's time.

2002-07-16 Andy Robertson #

Argues that the people of the Commonwealth in The Book of the New Sun are not a mixed pan-human population, because travel is on foot and immigration is rare in Severian's era. Grounds this in the in-world logic of technological exhaustion, noting that mass mixing of populations is historically recent and tied to fast travel.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic

Not at all. Massive mixing of populations is historcally a very new thing, the consequence of massive, fast, travel, and that has stopped in Severian's time for reasons of technological exhaustion. The peopel of the Commonwealthe are not a pan-human mixture: travel is by foot, and immigrants are very rare. You can argue about the relevance of this to the real future, but not Wolfe's internal logic.

hartshorn

2002-07-16 Alice Turner #

Proposes that Typhon/Pas deliberately built the segregated cities and enclaves of the Whorl and Blue to preserve Urth's customs and bloodlines, setting them against each other to keep the population down. Offers this as a novelist's explanation for the separation, drawing a comparison to Yahweh in the Old Testament, and extends the poster's own earlier post.
proposes · myth-and-structure · theories: Typhon/Pas deliberately segregated cities to preserve bloodlines

A thought to add to my own last post: there is a logical explanation, or at least a novelist's explanation, for the culturally, racially and sexually segregated cities (or enclaves) of the Whorl and Blue. It's that old joker Typhon/Pas at it again. He wants his "ark" to preserve as many or Urth's customs and bloodlines as possible, so he sets up these artificially segregated cities and preserves "ethnic purity" (or the cultural equivalent) by setting them all at each other's throats. (Helps keep the population down too--a lot like Yahweh, our Typhon.)

-alga

2002-07-16 Michael Straight #

Asks whether a passage in the Long Sun books explicitly states Alice K. Turner's idea that Typhon/Pas deliberately segregated the cities of the Whorl and Blue. Suggests Silk's talk with Sand about cities keeping armies against each other, and calls the arrangement Typhon's version of Babel.
questions · close-textual-reading · theories: Typhon deliberately segregated the cities as an ark

Isn't there a passage in the Long Sun books that all but says this explicitly? Maybe when Silk is talking to Sand about all the cities having armies and being set against each other so they won't gang up and storm Mainframe?

But yeah, it's Typhon's version of Babel.

-Rostrum
