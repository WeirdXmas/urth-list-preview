1997-08-05 Ranjit Bhatnagar #

Lists Milton's Il Penseroso, Enoch Arden, and a Hopkins letter praising Enoch's tropical island as island poetry worth considering. Quotes the opening lines of Il Penseroso and Hopkins's remark to A. W. M. Baillie, reports failed AltaVista and HotBot searches for a haiku, and links to online texts.
informs · source-and-allusion

Milton, Il Penseroso, which begins

Hence vain deluding joyes,
The brood of folly without father bred,
How little you bested,
Or fill the fixed mind with all your toyes;

Enoch Arden? "a passage much quoted already and which will be no doubt often quoted, the description of Enoch's
tropical island." -- Gerard Manley Hopkins writing to
A. W. M. Baillie.
