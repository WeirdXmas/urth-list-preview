Wolfe apologetics

Urth list · 3 messages · 1999-11-03 to 1999-11-05

1999urthThe Book of the New SunOuroboros The WormDamien BroderickJim Henley
The thread: Ouroboros argued that Wolfe's New Sun miracles have rational explanations, including quantum tunneling moving the Sun and Severian becoming the New Sun whose lovemaking brings the fountain, and defended the alzabo's memory transfer as improbable but not irrational. Damien Broderick playfully accepted the fountain-like cosmic entity idea, comparing it to Tipler's Omega Point while joking that he had used it first in his own 1982 novel. Jim Henley disputed the claim that a single cell cannot hold memories, citing Wolfe's mouthful of cells and the once-serious memory RNA speculation, and defended the alzabo passage as plausible for its time. The thread ended without resolution, with the rationalizing reading left unsettled against the playful and scientific objections.
  • Ouroboros: New Sun miracles have rational explanations; Severian becomes the New Sun via quantum tunneling and lovemaking
  • Damien Broderick: playfully accepts the fountain cosmic entity idea but notes its dubious physics
  • Jim Henley: memory in cells is plausible; alzabo passage was credible for its time

Themes: Robots, androids and artificial life, Memory and forgetting, The dying sun and the cosmos

1999-11-03 Ouroboros The Worm #

Argues that Wolfe's New Sun miracles have rational explanations, proposing that quantum tunneling moves the Sun and that Severian is the New Sun who becomes the fountain, so his lovemaking with Apheta brings it. Defends the alzabo's memory transfer as scientifically improbable but not irrational, citing Severian's bitten hand and the brain-eating assumption.
proposes · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: Quantum tunneling explains the New Sun's appearance; Severian is the New Sun and fountain; Severian's lovemaking with Apheta brings the New Sun; Alzabo absorbs memories by eating the brain

<<<<[Quantum tunneling being the theory behind Father
Inire's mirrors and how Severian brings the New
Sun]still leaves too much unexplained. How can QT
explain the New Sun being attached to Severian, or
being
"birthed" by an act of intercourse?>>>>>

One of the things that brings me back to the New Sun
books again and again is the conviction (based on my
experience with the story til now) that Wolfe has a
rational explanation for even the most miraculous
happenings. If Severian *did* pull a butterfly out of
a rainbow, you can bet Wolfe would have a rational
explanation for it.

What I meant by QT being how Severian brings the New
Sun is that that is how the Sun suddenly appears in
the center of Urth's orbit out of wherever.

BTW: I don't think I mentioned before that QT could
(theoretically) be used for time travel as surely as
for interstellar travel and that's how Severian does
it.

As for how the Sun is attached to Severian, I think I
recall someone telling Severian (I can't place the
quote) that the Concilliator does not just *bring* the
New Sun, he *is* the New Sun. Here's a possible
rationalization for Severian's connection to the Sun
and and how Severian and Apheta making love could
bring it:

Severian IS the fountain. Relative to the story, he
hasn't become it yet, but he will. At the time
Severian is getting it on, other things are happening
in the Universe that tell Severian (now the New Sun)
what the earlier Severian is doing and that now is the
time to become the New Sun and go to Urth. Therefore
Severian having sex "miraculously" brings the New Sun
the same way that the Concilliator "miraculously"
prophesies the future of Typhon and the future
Autarchs.

Nice neat rationalization. Now all I have to do is
*prove* it.

<<<<the memories contained in a single cell certainly
doesn't have a scientific basis>>>>>

I don't think this is how Wolfe said the alzabo got
the memories. I don't think he explained it in detail
at all, but I never imagined that if the alzabo had
bitten of Severian's hand he would have gotten all his
memories. I assumed it would have to eat the brain.
Now one can argue that such a beast or adaptation is
scientifically improbable, but the explanation is not
irrational.

Ok. There. Everything tidily explained. ;-)

- Ouroboros

1999-11-05 Jim Henley #

Corrects a claim that a single cell cannot hold memories, noting Wolfe's text says a mouthful of cells and that the 'memory RNA' idea was once a serious scientific speculation. Defends Wolfe's alzabo passage in The Book of the New Sun as plausible for its time, replying to Ouroboros's quoted objection.
disputes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading

Not a single cell. You needed "a mouthful," which is quite a lot of cells. And there was a time the "memory RNA" concept was taken quite seriously. I gather from discussion here that it has since been rejected, but at the time BOTNS was being written it was a plausible speculation. So it was gobbledygook on Wolfe's part only if some of those Urth-style ripples in the ocean of time happened to wash his way before they hit everyone else.

Best,

Jim
