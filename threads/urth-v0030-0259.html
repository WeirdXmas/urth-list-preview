Argues that the song in the story is magical, so its effects break down from a pre-Contact narrator's viewpoint rather than following science-fiction causal logic. Responds to Rostrum's objection that humans arriving almost instantly after Shadowchild ends the song seems odd, suggesting any shielding of outsiders would be psychic, so they merely believe St. Anne is not yet colonized.
disputes · genre-and-craft · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Song's magic makes shielding psychic not physical

I think we're in the wrong worldview here. The song is magical, so its effects break down immediately from the point of view of a pre-Contact narrator, no matter how acausal that appears to science fiction critics ;)

I would have thought that any shielding effects on foreigners would be psychic in nature...their own perception would be that they just hadn't got around to colonizing St Anne yet.
