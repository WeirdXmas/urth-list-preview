An Evil Guest science errors

Urth list · 6 messages · 2010-01-04 to 2010-01-05

2010urthThe Book of the New SunJosh GellerGwern BranwenJames WynnJeff WilsonDave Tallman
The thread: Readers debated whether An Evil Guest's claim that unaging humans would still die within 200 to 300 years, and its assumptions about suicide and violence in an unaging culture, hold up scientifically. Gwern Branwen argued the lifespan figure is numerically wrong given low young-adult mortality, working through survival arithmetic that Jeff Wilson confirmed with a 0.9997 annual survival factor and spreadsheet steps. James Wynn defended Chase's empirical assumptions and said suicide rates in such a culture cannot be known, while brunians argued such speculation proves nothing. Gwern also argued immortality removes any ceiling on losses, and Dave Tallman closed by offering Wolfe's 'The Doctor of Death Island' as a fictional counterpoint on meaning and love. The thread ended in disagreement over the speculative premises rather than with agreement.
  • Gwern Branwen: the novel's 200 to 300 year lifespan claim is numerically wrong given low young-adult mortality
  • James Wynn: Chase's empirical assumptions about non-aging cultures are defensible, and their suicide rates cannot be known
  • Jeff Wilson: Gwern's decay arithmetic checks out with a 0.9997 annual survival factor
  • brunians: speculation about suicide and violence in unaging cultures proves nothing because assumptions can produce any result
  • Dave Tallman: unlimited life becomes meaningless and cheap, and Wolfe's story shows this better than the science does

Themes: Christ figures and messiahs, Power, rulers and politics, Love and loss

2010-01-04 James Wynn replying to Gwern Branwen #

Suggests that the character Chase may be working from empirical data on other non-aging cultures, and that such cultures might be more violent and risk-prone. Responds to Gwern Branwen's objection that the novel's lifespan estimate is statistically sloppy, by noting that suicide rates in a culture without aging cannot be known.
disputes · genre-and-craft

Perhaps Chase is using different variables based on empirical data in other non-aging cultures. Perhaps un-aging cultures tend toward more violence and risk. And for self-slaughter, we're talking suicide. We simply cannot know the rates of suicide in cultures where life goes on and on.

2010-01-04 Gwern Branwen #

Argues that An Evil Guest's claim that immortal humans would still die within 200 to 300 years is numerically wrong, given low young-adult mortality rates. Works through the survival arithmetic for a cohort at 0.03% and 0.13% annual death rates, and shows a Haskell GHCi calculation of the decay over 100 years.
disputes · close-textual-reading

I forgot to include this in my tone email, but another thing that bothered me coming from an engineer is the apparent sloppiness with his scientific assertions.

Reis claims that even if humans didn't age, we'd still only get 2 or 300 years because of accidents and murders and self-slaughter. This is a classically conservative argument against being interested in curing aging - you're going to die anyway, and soon - but as far as I can tell, his numbers are just wrong.

I have seen statistics to the effect that a 12 year old who stopped aging could expect a median lifespan of >1000 years before disease or something else finally got her. But the mortality rates for young people in general are low enough that 2 or 300 years is ridiculously too little, and we can see this very easily,

http://gravityandlevity.wordpress.com/2009/07/08/your-body-wasnt-built-to-last-a-lesson-from-human-mortality-rates/ mentions that 1/3000 or 0.03% of 25 year-olds will die age 25. (Seems reasonable to me.)

If we assume a cohort of 10x people, and each year 0.03% of them die, then 1000 years from now, 7.4x will still be alive. 300 years from now, 9.15x will still be alive, and 200 years will see 9.42x. It's a low death rate!

Even a little basic figuring would have show 2-300 years to be absurd. And we can't make the excuse that Wolfe was estimating from the mid-40s ages of the protagonists, since the mortality rate cited for 42 year-olds is 1/750 or 0.13%. Plug in the 1/750 to the formula and ask for 300 years from now, and 6.7x are still alive. Heck, 2.6x are still alive 1000 years from now. 7/10ths or 3/10ths odds are not grounds for despair and dismissal.

I'm calculating this in a Haskell REPL, GHCi, using this expression for 100 years: 'take 100 $ iterate (\x -> x - (x * 0.0003)) 10'

You can see that each step is a year, and each step subtracts 0.03% of the previous year's population-total:

2010-01-04 Jeff Wilson replying to Gwern Branwen #

Checks Gwern Branwen's population-decay arithmetic from An Evil Guest by pointing out that the same result comes from a survival factor of .9997 per year, raised to the hundredth power. Gives spreadsheet and calculator steps (Excel's power formula, CALC.EXE scientific mode) so readers without Haskell can verify the figure.
supports · puzzle-solving

Those more modestly equiped with Excel or Windows' built-in CALC.EXE can check this by treating it as a survival chance of .9997 per year, and raise that value to (for example) 100 years with

=0.9997^100 (for Excel)

or

click View | click Scientific
.9997 [x^y] 100 [=] (for CALC.EXE)

Argues that speculation about suicide and violence in a culture where people do not age proves nothing, since any result can be reached by choosing assumptions. 2010-01-04 Gwern Branwen replying to James Wynn #

Argues that in an unaging society the risk-taking and suicide rates would differ from ours, since immortality removes any ceiling on losses, and that Reis's claims about inevitable death and suicide are unjustified. Responds to James Wynn's quoted remarks by reasoning through both endogenous and exogenous causes of suicide, and by citing overcomingbias posts on smoking studies as an aside.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic

Which would be? Woldercon?

The intuitive arguments are for exactly the opposite: if your lifespan is indefinitely long, you jeopardize indefinitely many years with violence or risk, whereas with aging, no matter the risk you take you can lose no more than 120 years of life. A ceiling on losses encourages risk-taking. (Consider recent financial bail-outs...)

I am reminded of some old quips:

Q- Why does the devil keep his deals?
A- Because as an immortal he has an infinite time horizon of other deals he jeopardizes if he betrays any given deal. Therefore the opportunity cost of any betrayal is too high.

Q- What does that make politicians then?
A- Lower in ethical reliability than the devil.
-Oldman

If the suicides are caused solely by exogenous factors, then Reis - and certainly Wolfe - cannot know this without aging having been cured in a normal human society, which it has not, so the statement is unjustified. If suicides are caused solely by internal reasons, then Reis's statement about deaths being inevitable within 2-300 years is false since people will live as long as they want to.
Any mix of endogenous or exogenous factors simply makes his statement a greater or lesser mixture of false and unjustified.

http://www.overcomingbias.com/2009/12/animal-smoking-studies.html and http://www.overcomingbias.com/2009/12/smoking-followup.html arguing that the actual studies suggest that smoking is only harmful to people with bad/vulnerable lungs (for whom it is harmful enough to skew all the other statistics which we know and fear).

Quite right. If I had thought harder, I would've realized that was the easier way to do it - but I'm a Haskeller and the iterate pattern is what my mind jumped to.
(It does have the slight advantage of showing all the intermediate results, but that's very slight.)
