Themes: Women, sex and gender

1997-06-09 Michael Richman #

Argues that Silk loved Hyacinth as a woman rather than as the embodiment of a principle or goddess, and that Silk would not stop loving her over Horn's view that she was unworthy. Grounds the claim in a comparison to the scene in Shadow where Paelamon gives Severian Terminus Est, and in Silk's defense of the Trigivantes.
supports · close-textual-reading · theories: Silk loved Hyacinth as a woman not an ideal

I think Silk loved Hyacinth as a woman, and not as the embodiment of a principle or a goddess. It is Horn's opinion that Hyacinth was unworthy of Silk's love, and even if Silk himself believed that, I doubt he would consider that a valid reason to stop loving her. I am reminded of the scene in Shadow where Master Paelamon give's Severian Terminus Est. Silk would understand that Hyacinth believed that he didn't deserve her love, and regard that as an equally valid, perhaps more valid, point of view. Recall his defense of the Trigivantes.
