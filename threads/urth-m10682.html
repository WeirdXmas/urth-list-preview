The Two Coins...

Urth list · 18 messages · 2010-08-13 to 2010-08-14

2010urthThe Book of the New SunRyan DunnAdam ThorntonGerry QuinnDavid StockhoffJames WynnJeff WilsonRoy C. Lackey
The thread: The thread asked whether the two chrisos coins Severian handles in The Shadow of the Torturer and The Citadel of the Autarch are the same coin, and whose face they bear. Ryan Dunn argued the coins match and may bear Severian's own face, while Roy C. Lackey insisted the face is the Autarch's and that Hildegrin dropped the false coin at the play. Jeff Wilson argued Severian cannot hold one coin at two times, and David Stockhoff doubted the coins were really false. Dunn conceded the time-overlap reading and accepted the coin-face explanation, but the coin-identity question was left unresolved. A side debate over whether Dorcas is Severian's grandmother, with Gerry Quinn citing canon from Ouen's locket, was pressed against Dunn's looser standard of evidence.
  • Ryan Dunn: the two coins match and may be the same coin, possibly bearing Severian's own future face
  • Roy C. Lackey: the face is the sitting Autarch's and the bad coins match the genuine ones; Hildegrin likely dropped the false coin
  • Jeff Wilson: Severian cannot hold the same coin twice, since the coins are recovered a decade apart
  • David Stockhoff: Hildegrin or someone paid in Vodalus' coin fled, and the coins may not really be false
  • Gerry Quinn: Dorcas as Severian's grandmother is canon, grounded in Severian's deliberate detective work and the locket identification
  • Ryan Dunn (on Dorcas): the locket established Dorcas as Ouen's mother, not Severian's grandmother, and similar reasoning should apply to the coins

Themes: Memory and forgetting, Time loops and causality, Doubles, twins and clones

2010-08-13 Roy C. Lackey replying to Ryan Dunn #

Argues that the androgynous face on the coin Severian gets from Vodalus in The Shadow of the Torturer is the sitting Autarch's, not Tzdakiel's. Grounds this on the chapter's description of the face as androgynous, matching how the Old Autarch is often described, and on the precedent that rulers' faces appeared on coins, including Severian's and Valeria's.
answers · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Androgynous face on coin is the sitting Autarch

Old Autarch's face on the front, and not Tzdakiel's. Anyone?<

There is more than one passage. In the third chapter of SHADOW, when he finally looks at the coin he got from Vodalus, he sees what he at first thought was a woman's face on the obverse. And in the last line of the chapter he says the face is androgynous, which is how the Old Autarch is often described. Put that together with the fact that Sev's face was on the coins when he was autarch, and Valeria's face was on the coins after she became the ruler, and one must conclude that the androgynous face on the Vodalus coin was that of the sitting autarch. At least so this one concludes. And I won't argue it; I'm just answering the question since, IIRC, this is the second time you have asked it.

-Roy

2010-08-13 Ryan Dunn #

Argues that the greased coin Talos gives Severian near the end of The Citadel of the Autarch matches the false coin Vodalus gave him in The Shadow of the Torturer, and asks who dropped the false coin at the play and whether it is the same coin. Grounds this in matching quoted phrases about the coins' smooth, greased feel and identical brassy shine and die, and suggests Hethor as the coin's carrier.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Talos's coin is the same false coin Vodalus gave Severian; Hethor dropped the false coin at the play

I just caught this, wondered what you guys thought of this.

Near the end of Citadel, Dr. Talos is granted an audience with Severian. He gives him a coin, one he never got to give him before they went to the gate. It was a false coin just like the one Vodalus gave Severian in Shadow, in the necropolis.

However, not only that, but it has some other similar characteristics...

VODALUS'S COIN:
"He laid something in my palm: a small coin so smooth it seemed greased."

TALOS'S COIN:
"I flipped the coin and caught it. It felt as though it had been lightly greased."

BOTH COINS:
"I was looking at the two coins. They had the same brassy shine and appeared to have been struck in the same die."

This brings up two questions to me...

1. Who dropped the false coin at the play that night?
2. Is this possibly the same coin?

Hethor springs to mind. He was the one who interrupted them the morning after the show, at least, and he was a visitor in the crowd at Severian's first beheading at least once before.

...ryan

P.S. I wondered where the passage was that led people to assume it was the Old Autarch's face on the front, and not Tzdakiel's. Anyone?

2010-08-13 Ryan Dunn replying to James Wynn #

Argues that the coin Vodalus gives Severian bears an Autarch's face, possibly one of Severian's own future faces, and that Severian's hidden self-recognition explains the puzzle. Grounds this in Severian's description of the coin as greased-smooth, the chapter title "Autarch's Face", and his unsettled reaction to the ship on its back, which Ryan links to the Atrium of Time scene.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Vodalus's coin shows a future Autarch's face; Severian is the Autarch pictured on the coin

I think it is from the smoothness of the coin, here is how Severian describes Vodalus's coin upon receipt:

"He laid something in my palm: a small coin so smooth it seemed greased."

The only thing I'm sure of is that Severian often goes to great lengths to conceal what he looks like when he sees himself. The flaw in my positing the face being Severians is that he is not androgynous, and Vodalus would have known him probably. I didn't get the sense that Vodalus was in on the divine plan for Severian to become Autarch.

I was trying to reconcile two things at once, 1) The title of the chapter, it's definitely an "Autarch's Face". Which rules out Tzadkiel. But which Autarch? I wondered whether Severian might not be rendered asexual as well, meaning he might not recognize himself at once, but surely he would later. He mentions something along those lines for sure:

"It seemed beyond explanation - so much so that at the time I did not even trouble to speculate about it, so sure was I that any speculation would be fruitless."

He's reacting to seeing the ship on the back, but something stirs inside him, in a manner similar to the feeling he had in the Atrium of Time, as though someone were watching him, which I took to mean the future Severian who revisits that same courtyard at the end of Citadel.

...ryan

Argues that Severian cannot be holding the same coin twice, since Vodalus' coin is recovered shortly after his return to the Citadel while Dr Talos gives him the other coin a decade later. Relies on Severian's keen memory, which would let him compare the coins mentally, and rejects the idea that the coin is one object at two times.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: The two coins are the same coin at different times

If they were the same coin within a span of each other, wouldn't they implode?

I don't think Sev has both coins at once. He recovers Vodalus' coin shortly after his return to the Citadel, but Dr Talos gives him the other coin a decade later, and it's a bit odd that he would have the other immediately. His keen memory would let him compare them mentally as easily as physically, as he has noted many times in almost as direct language.
