1998-10-27 Greg Neyman #

Asks how the Whorl gets its energy and how it propelled itself, given it sat in the blue-green system for years, and why chems with fusion packs still rely on fish oil for taluses and floaters. Grounds these questions in the Whorl as an interstellar ramjet idea and in the General Mint's azoth passing through a soldier's fusion core in Calde.
questions · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: the Whorl is an interstellar ramjet

Thanks for answering Peter. Anybody have any ideas on Silk tracing circles on his cheek?

Anyhoop, where did the Whorl get it's energy from? At first, I thought the Whorl was an interstellar ramjet, collecting hydrogen with and electro magnetic funnel, which if fused in its long sun, and expelling the helium for propulsion. But ramjets have to be moving to work, and a) something would have had to accelerate the whorl in the first place, and b) it turns out that Whorl sat in the blue/green system for several years, which would have made any ramjet inoperable.
Which leads to the next question: how did the whorl propel itself?

Another question ... if chems had personal fusion packs to power themselves (when General mint charges in Calde, she swings her azoth, and the book describes how it goes through a soldier's fusion core), why did things like taluses and floaters still rely on fish oil, presumably for internal-combustion engines?

1998-10-28 Peter Westlake #

Suggests that Silk's circles on his cheek may come from his father's razor, which the books mention, and notes that chems and other machines in the Whorl are made on board a ship with lower technology than the launching society. Responds to Greg Neyman's questions about Silk's cheek marks and why taluses and floaters still use fish oil despite chems' fusion packs.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Silk's cheek circles come from his father's razor

Someone suggested that it was to do with his father's razor, which is mentioned from time to time; he might have a scar there.

They're manufactured on board, where the technology is a lot lower than that of the society that launched the Whorl.

Spectacled Bear.

1998-10-28 Matthew Malthouse #

Argues that the Whorl's talusses and floaters run on fish oil because the people of the Whorl built them with only their own technology, while chems carried power sources from before launch. Answers Greg Neyman's question about why chems have fusion packs yet taluses rely on combustion, noting that the foundry shows the limits of local know-how and that chems' child-making raises an open question about powering parts.
answers · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: Whorl's taluses were built with local technology only

The chems were created before launch and on board before the human cargo awoke. Presumably their power source was a product of the culture that built the Whorl.

There _may_ have been some taluses with similar power that we did not see. The one's that we did see were presumably built by the people of the Whorl - and we get to see the foundry - and thus limited to the technologies they posess.

There is a gap however: chems can make children if they have the parts, how those would be powered is open to question. Maybe it is not the lack of knowledge but of working parts that limit the taluses to combustion power.

Matthew
