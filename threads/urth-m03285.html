Wolfe movies from short stories

Urth list · 7 messages · 2007-04-12 to 2007-04-15

2007urthFred KiescheRex LycanthrosaurusStuart HammMichael Straight
The thread: Posters discussed whether Wolfe's short fiction would adapt better to film than his long multi-volume works, and whether a Blade Runner comparison for "The Horars of War" was fair or a dig. Michael Straight argued that stories and novellas suit film, proposed Blade Runner-style or twist-style pitches, and joked about Hollywood tampering with a Ming the Mercilless story. Recursive Loop questioned the chronology of the Horars comparison and defended the idea that the comparison could be positive. Rex Lycanthrosaurus corrected a story attribution to Howard Waldrop and proposed a murder-mystery adaptation of The Fifth Head of Cerberus. The thread ended as a loose drift into fan proposals, with Stuart Hamm only briefly replying and mentioning an unrelated music project.
  • Recursive Loop: questions whether Horars predates Blade Runner and argues a film comparison could be positive
  • Michael Straight: short stories and novellas make better films than long series, with grim content a commercial risk
  • Rex Lycanthrosaurus: a murder-mystery film of the first third of The Fifth Head of Cerberus, simplified backstory
  • Stuart Hamm: a music CD with Henry Kaiser based on Wolfe's stories, brief reply to the movie thread

Themes: Genre: science fiction, fantasy, both, Robots, androids and artificial life

2007-04-12 Michael Straight #

Proposes that Wolfe's short stories and novellas, not his long multi-volume works, would make better movies, and lists five stories as candidates. Notes that grim content may hurt US release, suggests rewriting the ending of And When They Appear, and asks for the title of the story about three Disney robots at the end of the world.
proposes

The bulk of Wolfe's writing is either short-stories/novellas or multi-volume stories. Even Wizard Knight is way too long to do any justice with a movie. So if you really want a Wolfe movie, you should be looking to his shorter stuff.

A lot (most?) of his short stuff is probably way too grim to be greenlighted as a movie, at least in the USA, but if you leave that aspect aside, here's a few stories I think you could get a good movie out of:

When I Was Ming the Merciless
War Beneath The Tree
The Last Thrilling Wonder Story (would be panned as a rip-off of Stranger than Fiction)
The HORARS of War (Blade Runner rip-off)
And When They Appear (complete re-write of the ending would be mandatory, we'd just have to hope the screenwriter could come up with something good).

Oh, and what's the name of the story about the three Disney robots at the end of the world?

Any other ideas?

Rostrum

2007-04-12 Michael Straight replying to Recursive Loop #

Argues that a Wolfe story could work as a film if pitched as Blade Runner-style replicant ambiguity or Sixth Sense-style twist, and suggests Ming the Mercilless could make a creepy movie told through interview and experiment cuts. Jokingly predicts Hollywood would add an action third act with rescues and a heroic psychologist defeating Ming, mocking commercial adaptation.
proposes

Yeah, I was talking about how it would be received. The thing is, it's not duplicating the feel of Blade Runner (in fact it might look more like Predator or one of the last two Rambo movies) but the central high concept: is the protagonist a replicant or not?

Then there could also be the Sixth Sense ripoff: The Other Dead Man. Actually, that could be an awesome movie. Pitch it as 2001 meets the Sixth Sense and you could probably get some serious $$$.

I'd also think you could get a really good movie out of Ming the Mercilless, if you got a sufficiently creepy actor for Ming and framed it as cutting back and forth between the interview with Ming and the action in the experiment. Hollywood would probably require adding a third act where some of Ming's minons get free and rescue him and then the hero psychologist (who was opposed to the experiment from the beginning) defeats and kills him.

Rostrum

2007-04-12 Rex Lycanthrosaurus replying to Michael Straight #

Corrects Michael Straight by naming Howard Waldrop's "Heirs of the Perisphere" as the story about the Disney robots, then proposes a film adaptation of the first third of The Fifth Head of Cerberus as a murder mystery. The proposal sketches suspects from Cave Canem and suggests moving the abo-Saint Anne connection and shapechangers to Saint Croix to simplify the backstory.
proposes · genre-and-craft

On Thu, 12 Apr 2007 09:57:34 -0600, "Michael Straight"

Not by Wolfe, but Howard Waldrop: "Heirs of the Perisphere."

As for movies, I think the first third of FIFTH HEAD would make a good film, especially if directed by someone stylish like Christopher Nolan. It would start with the murder of Maitre by persons unknown, and then a prefect from Saint Croix could undertake to solve the case by looking at various of Cave Canem's denizens, each of whom might have a motive for killing Maitre: his two sons, his crippled scientist-sister, his robot progenitor, a disgruntled employee (prostitute) of or visitor (john) to the House at 666 Saltimbanque or even a visiting anthropologist from Earth. I'd also eliminate for ease of backstory the abo-Saint Anne connection and make the shapechangers native to Saint Croix. Only at the end would we learn of the cloning connection and its ramifications.

2007-04-15 Stuart Hamm replying to Rex Lycanthrosaurus #

Reports that Stuart Hamm met musician Henry Kaiser at the Bohemian Grove and that the two plan a CD based on Wolfe's stories, themes and characters. Cites an attempt to offer music to a French production of The Death of Dr Island, which already had its score, and otherwise only briefly replies to Rex Lycanthrosaurus's movie thread.
informs

My comments on GW movies and Music...
I was strolling around the Bohemian Grove when someone noticed a copy of "Attending Daedalus" poking out of my gig bag (sorry for mis-attributing this book to Borski in an earlier post...got kids?..sleep much???...) Turns out it was Henry Kaiser who has done sound for some W Herzog films (Grizzly Man..)and some fine albums, and who is I'm sure the only Bohemian to explore different interpretations of GW's writing... Turns out there is a French production of "The Death of Dr Island" and so we tried to track the producers down to see if they needed music (Much of my work has been Bassed (pun intended) on Sf writers PK Dick, William Gibson and JG Ballard.) Alas, the music was already done but Henry and I have have plans to do a CD based on GW stories, themes and characters... Cheers
Stuart Hamm
