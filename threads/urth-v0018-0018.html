Asks for the archive volume covering earlier discussion of Peace and raises a theory that Weer is dead from the start of the novel. Proposes that Weer's parents died and his mother Olivia told him they were on vacation, citing the absence of the parents and the vacation stretching from one year to over two.

1998-08-24 William H. Ansley #

Argues that Weer in Peace is certainly dead and largely unaware of his state, coining the term YADP for such protagonists. Grounds this in internal evidence and Wolfe's own statement, and cites Weer's past-tense references to his parents' return, though the passage is not located yet; responds to an earlier post suggesting the parents died.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Weer is dead and unaware of it; Weer's parents returned, so they are not dead

It is as certain as anything can be in a Wolfe book that Weer is dead, a YADP (Yet Another Dead Protanganist - another useful term I offer to Wolfe scholars), both from internal evidence and because Wolfe has said so. It also seems pretty certain to me that he doesn't know he is dead or at least is almost completely unaware of his state; although he may be close to this realization at the very end of the book.

It is creepy but I am sure that are not dead since Weer refers more than once to his parents' return in the past tense. Because of the confusion of time in the book, this doesn't *prove* anything, of course. But I find the passage where Weer says words to the effect that his parents had finally returned but would forever remain strangers very convincing. Actually, I *can't* find it; I just skimmed the book for it but missed it. Maybe I'll reread _Peace_ again to search it out. It is my favorite book by Wolfe and one of my favorite books, period.

William Ansley
