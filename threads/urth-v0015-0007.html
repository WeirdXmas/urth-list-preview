Themes: Unreliable narration

1998-06-24 adam louis stephanides #

Asks which scene in Wolfe's work Wolfe means when he complains that careless readers missed a character lying about an event later described differently. Quotes the passage from Castle of Days, an essay first published in 1990, to frame the question for the list.
questions · close-textual-reading

In _Castle of Days_, in an essay orginally published in 1990, Wolfe complains regarding careless readers: "I have had them tell me (for example) that they were completely baffled when a scene they had read was discribed [sic] differently, later in the story, by one of the characters who took part in it; because I had not told them, 'This man's lying,' it had never occurred to them that he might be." (340) Does anybody have an idea what he's referring to?

--Adam

1998-06-24 Michael Straight #

Notes that lying narrators occur in the Soldier series and possibly Free Live Free, though without specific examples, and says protagonists more often misguess events, as Silk and Latro do. Responds to Adam Stephanides' quotation from Castle of Days about readers missing a character's lie, and so engages the unreliable narration question.
informs · close-textual-reading

I know this happens in the Soldier series in several places, and I think I remember that kind of thing happening in FREE LIVE FREE too, but I can't remember any specific examples.

Of course much more frequent in Wolfe is for the protagonist to guess what's going on and guess wrong. Silk does that constantly, and Latro too.

-Rostrum
