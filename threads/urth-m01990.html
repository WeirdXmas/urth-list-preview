The Piteous Gate (was: Book of the new sun (1-4) questions)

Urth list · 5 messages · 2006-02-21 to 2006-02-21

2006urthThe Book of the New Sunmournings gloryCliff JudgeChris PTony EllisRob Davies
The thread: Thread asked why soldiers at the Piteous Gate in The Book of the New Sun drive travellers off the road when the Commonwealth's roads are reportedly banned to civilians, and whether alternate routes make that ban pointless. Tony Ellis argued the ban is the explanation, citing Master Palaemon's warning about uhlans who kill people found on the roads. Rob Davies questioned whether a road closure makes sense for a gate that acts as a barrier, while mournings glory raised the same legal and practical puzzle. Transentient proposed an entrenched order that the uhlans enforce poorly, and Chris argued soldiers profit more from sporadic enforcement and plunder than from total closure. The thread ends with several competing explanations and no settled answer.
  • Tony Ellis: the road ban is illegal civilian travel, so soldiers drive people off and this explains the riot
  • Rob Davies: if the road is closed the gate makes little sense as a barrier
  • mournings glory: alternate routes may make the prohibition pointless, given the autarch's aim to paralyze social order
  • Transentient: the ban is an entrenched but poorly enforced order, with overstretched troops or officers who wait and loot
  • Chris: soldiers profit more by tolerating travel and enforcing occasionally, which yields a large haul and keeps the road busy

Themes: Power, rulers and politics, Justice, torture and mercy, Colonization and the native

2006-02-21 Cliff Judge replying to mournings glory #

Proposes that the ban on travel by road persists as an entrenched order that the uhlans enforce poorly, so petitioners keep risking the highway to the Piteous Gate in The Book of the New Sun. Responds to mournings glory's question about how travelers get past the Commonwealth's roads by suggesting overstretched troops or officers who wait and loot, and compares this layered order to Nessus and a Babylon 5 anecdote about a guarded flower.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Road ban persists as an entrenched, poorly enforced order; Uhlan officers wait to loot travelers on the highway

But it certainly doesn't make sense that there is a society of people descended from petitioners, living out their whole lives in a waiting room at the House Absolute, living off of danishes and coffee!

So, perhaps it was an order issued some time ago, and over time it has simply become an entrenched dynamic in the Commonwealth. The uhlans clearly don't enforce it in an efficient or organized manner, or people would never try to use the highways or gates.

Maybe the uhlans - are they the regular military? - are stretched so thin with the constant war that they can't spare the troops to enforce the rule efficiently; units are rotated back to Nessus so infrequently that the desperate people get to risking the roads again, and any uhlans who encounter them enjoy some fine sport, then loot the bodies, and that's how things are. Or perhaps the officers purposely wait until there are a lot of people hazarding the roads, and then hit them and split up the booty.

Either way, it is a situation a lot like Nessus, and the BOTNS itself: new orders built upon the old without the old being resolved or understood. A theme is laid down and it takes on a new life and a new meaning.

Like that one episode of Bablyon 5 where Londo was explaining the danger that a crazed emporer posed to the Centarui Republic: he related how many centuries ago a princess had discovered a single flower sprouting from the palace grounds in the middle of winter, and had ordered the palace guard to place someone next to it to keep it from being stepped on. The flower was long since gone but the palace guard still placed a soldier in that same spot to the present day.

2006-02-21 Tony Ellis #

Argues that the soldiers at the Piteous Gate are driving people off the road because travel on the Commonwealth's roads is illegal, so the riot is explained before Severian sets out. Supports this by quoting Master Palaemon's warning in The Shadow of the Torturer, chapter XIII, about uhlans who kill anyone found on the roads.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Piteous Gate riot is driving people off illegal roads

The explanation for this has cropped up a few times in the list, actually, but since it's my explanation I'm happy to repeat it. :-)

The soldiers are driving people off the road, because it's illegal to use the roads of commonwealth.

We're given all the facts we need before Severian even sets off on his journey. Master Palaemon tells us: "...there is a redoubt, so I've heard, every fifty leagues... [the roads] are patrolled by uhlans under orders to kill anyone found on them, and since they have permission to loot the bodies of those they slay, they are not much inclined to ask excuses." Shadow, XIII

2006-02-21 mournings glory replying to Tony Ellis #

Questions how travelers in The Book of the New Sun can legally reach their destinations when roads beyond the Piteous Gate are banned, asking whether alternate routes make the prohibition pointless. Grounds the question in Severian's description of the crowded highway at the Gate and Master Palaemon's warning about uhlans patrolling the roads, and in the autarch's stated aim of paralyzing social order.
questions · chronology-and-plot-logic

Here's something I don't understand, however.

If it's illegal to travel via the roads, how exactly are all these people planning to get to where they're going? On the day in question Severian claims the highway leading to the Piteous Gate is "*crowded* with carts and wagons and traffic of all kinds." Surely, most of these people are aware that traffic is prohibited on the Commonwealth's roads. So what will they do once they've passed beyond the Gate? Jump into the Gyoll and start swimming? (River traffic does seem to be tolerated.) Melt into the woods and follow crude dirt paths--with carts and wagons and beasts of burden? Hide in the bushes whenever they sense a uhlan nears?

Moreover, if such a wide variety of traffic is allowed to exit and travel beyond Nessus by whatever means, doesn't this simply circumvent the law that made road travel illegal in the first place? Wouldn't, in fact, if the law were strictly followed, most people be turned away at the Gate? Apparently not, since enough people are aware they can reach their destinations via alternate means -- which in effect makes the prohibition useless anyway.

"We have closed the roads to paralyze the social order," the old autarch tells Severian. Methinks he needs to look again.

msg

2006-02-21 Chris P replying to Rob Davies #

Argues that the soldiers at the Piteous Gate in The Book of the New Sun profit more by tolerating road travel and only occasionally enforcing the military-use rule. Grounds this in economic reasoning: plundering everyone would deter use, while sporadic enforcement yields a large haul and keeps the road busy, responding to Tony's explanation of the gate.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Uhlans enforce the road rule only occasionally for profit

The road is officially devoted to military use, the transportation of troops and supplies, so that is what the gate is for.

I find all arguments about what happened at the Piteous Gate to be a bit tenuous, but here are a couple things which make a certain amount of common sense to me at any rate.

Even if the uhlans are authorized to plunder anyone on the road, it would probably not be profitable for them to do so (because then nobody would ever use the road). The profitable way to approach it is to leave people alone for the most part and not enforce the rule - this also happens to be good for the economy - which would encourage people to go ahead and use the road, even if they are aware that there is some small risk involved. Then, every once in a while, you come down and enforce it; and when you do so, you will end up with a huge haul rather than a couple stray travellers here and there.
