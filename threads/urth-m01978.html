2006-02-04 Jordan Dennis #

Proposes that the name Ascia and the Ascians derive from Ascolais, a place in Jack Vance's Tales of the Dying Earth, as a homage Wolfe made to his inspiration. Grounds this in Wolfe's interview admitting a debt to Vance and a passage on page 32 where a character asks to be transported to Ascolais.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Ascia and Ascians derive from Ascolais

I would like to throw in another perspective on the origins of the Ascians, if I may.

In his interview with James Jordan, Gene Wolfe said that he owed a big debt to Jack Vance. Let me quote that passage of the interview --

JJ:* Was Jack Vance's style an influence on the style of the Severian novels?

*GW:* Oh, I'm sure. I'm sure. A lot of that was my deciding to rewrite *The Dying Earth* from my own standpoint.

*JJ:* Wonderful book.

*GW:* Yes it is wonderful. And of course when you read wonderful books sometimes you think, "Gee, I would like to do that"; and you go off and do it, trying to make it different enough that you are not really ripping off the author, but rather writing something in the same vein using some of the same ideas. I have never concealed a debt to Jack Vance and a debt to Clark Ashton Smith as far as that goes. I think Vance is very much in the debt to Clark Ashton Smith.
The entirety of the interview can be found at
http://mysite.verizon.net/~vze2tmhh/wolfejbj.html<http://mysite.verizon.net/%7Evze2tmhh/wolfejbj.html>.

I recently picked up the Orb trade paperback, Tales of the Dying Earth, but only started to read it this morning, with the context of this discussion fresh in my mind, and something immediately jumped out at me. On page 32, one of the characters asked a magician to transport her to Ascolais. What if Ascia and the Ascians were a permutation of Ascolais that Gene Wolfe meant as a sort of homage to one of his inspirations? That would be a good reason to explain why he was suprised when people began associating the Ascians with Asians.

Anyway, just a thought.

2006-02-04 James Wynn replying to Jordan Dennis #

Doubts that Ascia and the Ascians derive from Ascolais, saying the only link is the name, and instead suggests Ascolais may relate to the Greek word for licentiousness. Argues that Severian is an homage to Cugel from Vance's Dying Earth, citing the subtle humor of the Cugel stories as evidence.
disputes · source-and-allusion · theories: Ascolais derives from akolasia meaning licentiousness; Severian is an homage to Cugel

eh.....I don't know. It seems a stretch since I can't think of any other connection beyond the name. But I'm no one to talk since I've always suspected Ascolais is connected to the term "akolasia" meaning "licentiousness".

But wait until you get to the story of Cugel. The writing is soo subtly funny. And Severian is surely an homage to the shirtless, sword wielding Cugel.

J
