Argues that Den Weer in Peace knows he is dead but refuses to admit it, and that his time under the tree is a purgatorial punishment guided by angelic doctors toward peace. Grounds this in the physical maladies, eating and chopping, the Mantis remark about revenge for Bobby Black, and Catholic purgatory theory, and responds to Roy's question about how Weer knew the tree was planted by Eleanor.
proposes · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: Den Weer knows he is dead but denies it; Weer's time under the tree is purgatorial punishment; Doctors act as guiding angels toward peace; Memory mansion is the house Weer built

Or, more simply, (3) he starts out knowing that he is dead and promptly "forgets" -- the normalization process. As you say,

Right. To put it otherways: it isn't that Weer doesn't know he's dead; it's that he won't _permit_ himself to know (or to admit that he knows) that he's dead. He's floating around in Egypt, diggez-moi?

This answers most of your questions, actually. Why the physical maladies? Why eat? Why haul axe? Na, na, na, na, stayin' alive, or at least maintaining the appearances.

With one point will I take exception:

Yes, he knew it but I think he was wrong. My current (for the last few months, anyway) grand unification theory (call it a gut feeling?) for PEACE involves the Doctors and possibly others acting as guiding angels leading Den Weer toward the elusive "peace."

I can see at least two useful possible-meanings for this.

1. Weer died in winter. He was mighty sick then.
2. Weer was conscious in the grave and trapped by the tree, as many have suggested, and felt mightly low indeed down there.

In fact, #2 fits nicely with the general Purgatorial theory; Weer's time under the tree is the "temporal punishment" of which some Catholic theories of Purgatory speak. (Punishment for what? Well, Mantis speaks of "a weird kind of revenge for killing Bobby Black"; certainly this could be one thing. He is trapped in the tree, as in a net, underground.)

When his "time" is up [and keep in mind that no specific amount of time is or need be involved; the ghost's time, in Eternity, is private and not related, at least in any simple or obvious way, to the physical time of Cassionsville], the tree falls and Weer is "freed" to move on towards, uh, peace. He still has to complete the process of repentance, which is, at some level, what the guiding angels are leading him through.

That's my theory and I'm sticking by it.

Until something better comes along, anyway.

Mantis articulates similar ideas, and I like the 2001 comparison.

A few specifics:

I think the simplest answer (though not one I'm prepared to argue strongly for) is that Weer built _a_ house, and that the "memory mansion" bears some strong resemblance to the house he built, for reasons which might be obvious.

Deep suspicion: This _is_ the house that Weer built -- but in the sense that Marley says, to Scrooge, "These are the chains I forged in life."

--Dan'l
