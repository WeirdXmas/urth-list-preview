1999-10-29 Damien Broderick #

Argues that Vonnegut's late novel TIMEQUAKE reads as desperate, exhausted hackwork, not the work of the writer the poster loved early on. Responds to Patrick O'Leary's defence of TIMEQUAKE by recounting the poster's own drowsy reading and asking whether earlier novels SIRENS or CRADLE could have caused the same effect.
disputes

Wrong charge, admittedly. But it's desperate stuff, Patrick, I reckon, reeking of exhaustion. I feel sorry for the man, whose early and middle work I loved deeply, and still do. This shocking thing happened to me: I read TIMEQUAKE in a borrowed copy. A year later I found it on the library shelf, flipped through, my heart going pitter-pat with anticipation at this new late work I hadn't yet read, lugged it home. Took me a couple of chapter to wake up. Could that have happened with SIRENS or CRADLE? Not even to my weary old brain, I think. Burn-out.

Damien Broderick
