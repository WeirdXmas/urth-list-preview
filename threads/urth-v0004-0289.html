1997-09-02 Kevin McGuire #

Reports that the poster checked Gordon Day's Western Abenaki Dictionary for the animal names suggested earlier and found no matches. Cites the closest candidates, wigkannid and wigkikan, and judges that they would be hard to make fit the names.
informs · source-and-allusion

Well, no dice. I checked Vol. 1 of the Western Abenaki Dictionary, by Dr. Gordon Day, and given your caveat about the spellings looked as widely as I could, but nothing seemed to correspond - some of the combinations don't even seem to be sounds that the Western Abenaki used with any frequency. The closest match was wigkannid "the one who likes to travel" and perhaps wigkikan "liking to plant, sow" - but I think you would be hard pressed to make these work.

Cheers,

Argues that Wolfe's use of the word "cutthroat" for Severian is a deliberate, if glancing, allusion. Answers Ron's request for a reference by pointing to Urth (5), pages 314-315 of the 1987 hardcover, which appears well after TS.
answers · source-and-allusion · theories: "cutthroat" is a deliberate allusion in Urth

Ron, check Urth (5) p 314-315 (hardcover, 1987, well after TS---impossible not to believe it's a deliberate, if glancing, reference: the word is not all that common).
