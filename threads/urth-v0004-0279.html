1997-08-28 Alice Turner #

Lists Algonkian-group tribes and their rough locations, then reports web research on Potawatomi, Mohegan and the word Sioux while looking for a name for the Algonkian animal-name clue. Gives no reading of Wolfe's text; it shares sleuthing findings, including a Mohegan wolf-tribe connection to Natty Bumppo and a claim that Sioux means cutthroat in French.
informs · source-and-allusion

Here, whether you want it or not, is a list of the 13 Algonkian tribes, and approximately where they hang out:

ALGONKIAN GROUP

Abenaki--Vermont
Arapaho--Great Plains (WY)
Blackfoot--Montana, Alberta
Cheyenne--Great Plains
Cree--West Coast
Fox--Minnesota
Gros Ventre (White Clay People)--Montana
Maliseet--Maine, Newfoundland
Mi'kmaq--Nova Scotia
Montagnais--Quebec, Labrador
Naskapi--Quebec
Odawa--Minnesota, Ontario
Ojibwa--Michigan

So Ojibwa (aka Chippewa--bet you didn't know that) looks good, eh? Unfortunately, I found an Ojibwa site with animal names, and it didn't work. More sleuthing: aha, the Potawatami seem to be smack dab on site, but though I couldn't find a language site for them, it's supposed to be pretty close to the Ottawa (Odawa), Ojibwa and Algonkin, and allied to the Illinois and Miami (because of the nature of web engines, it's virtually impossible to track anything down on the latter two).

But, serendipity produced its usual prizes: I discovered that the Mohegan (Mohican) is the Wolf tribe (hello Natty Bumppo!) and---are you sitting down, mantis?---Sioux is French for....the Cutthroat tribe.

-alga-

1997-08-29 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Argues that the Naked One, teacher of the animal creatures in "The Tale of the Boy Called Frog" in The Book of the New Sun, is linked to the Native American Squanto, and speculates whether his throat-cut appearance identifies the group that killed him. Builds on a prior post's point about the Sioux word for cutthroat, tying the name to the Squanto allusion and the Frog story's ploughing of fish.
proposes · source-and-allusion · puzzle-solving · theories: The Naked One is based on Squanto; The Cutthroat Tribe killed the Naked One

Reply: Item #4346774 from [email]@INET03#

alga,

Sioux as French for "cutthroat" (er, "cultellarii"?) now =that= is very interesting, since it ties in =exactly= with the element of "The Tale of the Boy Called Frog" of TBOTNS, wherein the teacher of all these animal Kiplingoids is called, not the expected Baloo the Bear (hmmm), but the Naked One, "who was also called the Savage, or Squanto" (III, ch. 19). This character is the one who taught Frog to plough the fish, leading Frog to plough his twin brother Fish; this name belongs to a Native American Indian of the Pawtuxet tribe who is said to have taught the pilgrims how to plough the fish.

Interesting that the teacher character in both stories (TS and TBCF) is linked to a Native American Indian name.

OTOH, since he looks like his throat has been cut, does this mean that we have at long last determined the group that cut him? (One presumes that the "Cutthroat Tribe" got their name for doing unto others rather than self-inflicting, no?)

=mantis=
