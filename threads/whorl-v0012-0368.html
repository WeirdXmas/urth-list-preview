Themes: Catholic faith and sacraments

2001-02-26 Jacob Corbin #

Argues that a secular reader can fully appreciate Wolfe's religious content, praising his genre deconstruction, ethical compassion and prose while admitting occasional clumsy metaphors. Responds to a poster who wondered what non-Christians see in Wolfe, offering his own reasons rather than citing specific scenes in detail beyond a eucharist-related quip.
answers · genre-and-craft

Well, I'm wholly secular, but why should that make me any less receptive to Wolfe's writing? As SF&F, his writing does a wonderful job of deconstructing and brilliantly rethinking dozens of genre tropes. The moral and ethical messages are delivered with almost overwhelmingly large amounts of compassion, empathy, and intelligence. And the prose itself is mad and beautiful and sexy and deliciously treacherous. For all of that, I can grin through the occasional kludgy metaphor ("Look, everyone! It's THE EUCHARIST! WOO!").

Jacob G Corbin
