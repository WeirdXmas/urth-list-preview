About the secret of the inhumi, again (RTTW spoilers)

Whorl list · 4 messages · 2001-04-19 to 2001-04-27

2001whorlThe Book of the Short SunKevin J. MaroneyAdam Stephanides
The thread: Kevin J. Maroney asked whether the detail that inhumi children, not the predators, gain intelligence from blood had appeared earlier, and whether it should make the secret usable as a weapon against the inhumi. Adam Stephanides said he did not recall it and offered a narrow suggestion about inhumi spawned from human blood, admitting it was of little practical use. Stephanides then proposed that cutting off inhumi returns from Blue to Green might shrink their intelligent population, but conceded this was as impractical as stopping them reaching Blue. A later post argued that drained corpses in the sewer and the Secret Krait reveals show the inhumi need huge amounts of human blood, so cutting Blue's supply would force them to consume slaves faster. The thread ends with Stephanides's own ideas and no reply from others, so it stalls without agreement.
  • Kevin J. Maroney: the blood-intelligence detail should be key to a weapon against the inhumi, and the reader may be missing why it is not usable
  • Adam Stephanides: the detail may matter only for inhumi spawned from human blood, which is of little practical use
  • Adam Stephanides: cutting inhumi returns from Blue to Green could shrink their intelligent population, but this is impractical
  • Adam Stephanides: drained corpses show huge blood demand, so cutting Blue's supply would increase slave consumption

Themes: Beasts and the animal in us, Power, rulers and politics, Suffering and sacrifice

2001-04-19 Kevin J. Maroney #

Asks whether a detail about the inhumi revealed by Jahlee in Return to the Whorl, that the children of the inhumi gain intelligence from blood, was absent from earlier discussions of the secret. Argues that if so, this detail should be key to understanding how humans could fight the inhumi, and asks what the reader is missing about why it makes the secret a weapon too big to wield.
questions · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic

In an earlier post, I pointed out this (in somewhat different words):

In _Return to the Whorl_, Jahlee tells Nettle a secret about the inhumi, which, because of the structure of the story, we are led to believe is *the* secret which has been sketched out in the first two volumes. The only detail new to her revelation is that it is the children of the inhumi who gain intelligence from the blood rather than the predators themselves.

A. Am I correct that this detail was not present in any of the earlier open discussions of the secret?

B. If I am correct, it seems to me that this detail is supposed to be a major revelation about the nature of the secret, and key to understanding how the humans on Blue and Green could fight the inhumi. But I keep failing to see how this detail transforms what we knew into "a weapon too big to wield".

What am I missing?

Any responses welcome.

2001-04-20 Adam Stephanides #

Answers Kevin J. Maroney's question about whether the detail that inhumi children, not the predators, gain intelligence from blood appeared earlier in the Long Sun books, saying he does not recall it. Offers a suggestion that only inhumi spawned from drinking human blood might matter, but admits it is of little practical use.
answers · close-textual-reading · theories: Inhumi children gain intelligence from human blood

I believe so. I don't recall it, in any case (though iirc a couple of passages can be seen in retrospect to hint at it); nor do I recall any of our discussions of the secret mentioning it.

Whatever you're missing, I am too. The only thing I can think of is that perhaps it would be enough to keep inhumi about to spawn from drinking human blood, rather than all inhumi; but I don't see how this knowledge could be used in practice.

I'm afraid mine wasn't too helpful.

--Adam

2001-04-22 Adam Stephanides #

Proposes that if the inhumi must return from Blue to Green to spawn intelligent offspring, cutting off that return could shrink their intelligent population. Reasons that stopping inhumi from returning to Green seems as impractical as stopping them from reaching Blue, so the idea offers no real weapon.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Inhumi females must go to Blue before spawning on Green

Since my first response, one potentiality has occurred to me. Assuming that there are not enough humans on Green to maintain the population of intelligent inhumi, the Secret implies that to have intelligent offspring, most female inhumi must first go to Blue, and then return to Green to spawn. So if it were somehow possible to prevent inhumi returning to Green, the population of intelligent inhumi would be set on a declining course. But I don't see how this would be possible, any more than stopping the inhumi from coming to Blue.

--Adam

2001-04-27 Adam Stephanides #

Proposes that the drained human corpses in the sewer, read together with the Secret Krait reveals, show the inhumi need vast amounts of human blood, so cutting Blue's supply would force them to consume slaves faster. Answers Spectacled Bear's query about the Neighbour in the sewer, citing the corpses and the inhumi's temporary cushion on Green.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Inhumi need huge amounts of human blood; Cutting Blue's blood supply would force faster consumption of slaves

I actually think I know this now (it just came to me). There were thousands of human corpses in the sewer, all drained of blood. In combination with the Secret, this indicates the tremendous amount of human blood the inhumi need. If the supply on Blue is cut off, they will have to consume their slaves at a greater rate than they can reproduce or be replaced.

The fact that the inhumi do have a temporary "cushion" on Green indicates that it's not necessary to provide 100% protection on Blue, just to reduce the supply greatly. So my earlier concerns about the impossibility of protecting everyone are less applicable, though even providing 90% protection would still be a major task.

--Adam
