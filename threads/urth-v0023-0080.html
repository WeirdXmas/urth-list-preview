Themes: Christ figures and messiahs

1999-02-18 Kieran Mullen #

Argues that Little Tib's demon-casting and the Oz-style imagery in the story echo Christ's temptation in the desert, and proposes that Typhon's temptations of Severian replay Christ's three temptations. Grounds the claim on the parallel between food, casting from a height, and the cities offered in exchange for submission, and responds to a quoted passage from William H. Ansley's Eyeflash of Oz serial.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · theories: Typhon's temptations of Severian replay Christ's temptations in the desert; Little Tib casts demons out like Christ

[Deletia]

This is Christic imagery, too. From the temptation in the desert, where the Devil quotes scripture about angels bearing up the messiah if he should fall. IIRC, Tib casts demons out of the computer system, no?

I slapped my head when I finally saw that Typhon's temptations of Severian are just a replay of the temptations of Christ. (Food, casting from a height, all the cities stretched out before you if you submit to me. Doh!)

Kieran Mullen

1999-02-18 William H. Ansley #

Announces the poster's intention to set aside the Christic imagery in Wolfe's story "The Eyeflash Miracles" and to discuss the Oz imagery instead, combining the two. Replies to Kieran Mullen, confirming his recollection that Tib casts demons out of the computer system in the Gaderine scene and inviting others to comment on the Christic imagery.
informs · allegory-and-typology

Quite so. And you do recall correctly; I will be getting to the Gaderine Computer scene eventually.

There is a *lot* of Christic imagery in "The Eyeflash Miracles," as I note in my first posting. I am deliberately ignoring this, except occasionally when it ties in with the Oz imagery. Wolfe manages to combine the two in clever and complex ways, as you might expect. This doesn't mean I don't welcome your comments; I do. And I would be glad to hear from anyone else who wants to comment on Christic imagery in "The Eyeflash Miracles."

William Ansley
