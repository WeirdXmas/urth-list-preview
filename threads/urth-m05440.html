significance of apu punchau

2008urthThe Book of the New SunDavid Stockhoff

Themes: Myth and folklore sources

2008-12-29 David Stockhoff #

Argues that Severian fits a dying-and-returning vegetation king pattern, citing his lame leg, the Green Man, repeated sacrifices and deaths, and his Lancelot role linked to trees and water crossings. Responds to Marc Aramini's Frazer-based reading by agreeing that Frazer's theories of cyclic kingship are an influence while treating Vodalus as a Robin Hood figure.
supports · myth-and-structure · allegory-and-typology · theories: Severian is a Lancelot figure tied to trees and water; Severian fits a dying vegetation king pattern; Vodalus is a Robin Hood figure

That's an interesting comment. Severian's lame leg points to this as well, and of course there is the Green Man. Also, Severian has undergone his share of repeated sacrifices or at least deaths. Though I think of Vodalus more as Robin Hood. He is cut down, though, unlike Robin.

I have called Severian a Lancelot, and I believe that associates him with trees and water crossings (and lakes) enough to justify including Frazer's theories here as an influence.
