The thread: Roy C. Lackey asked where the account of Urth's end under the New Sun originates, tracing the chain of tellers from Severian through Dr. Talos's play to find an original author, and citing Severian's admission in the cell scene that he knows little and relies on Talos. Tony Ellis replied, and in two posts that repeat the same argument, proposed that the story has no true originating author but forms a closed loop with Severian as its original author, whose own narrative becomes the lost Book of the New Sun that Talos's play draws on rather than Canog's text. The cards show no response from others and no resolution, so the thread ends with a single unanswered theory.

1999-03-29 Roy C. Lackey #

Asks where the account of Urth's fate under the New Sun comes from, tracing the chain of storytellers from Severian through Dr. Talos's play to find the original author. Points to the cell scene in chapter XXXVII of The Urth of the New Sun, where Severian admits he knows little and draws on Talos's play.
questions · source-and-allusion

When, in chapter XXXVII of _Urth_, Herena, Declan, the mate, and the cook visit Sev in his cell and ask him to relate to them how old Urth will fare when the New Sun comes, he knows scarcely more than they. He therefore draws upon his knowledge of Dr. Talos's play for the information. Dr. Talos's play, many centuries later, is based upon the myths and legends extant from the time of the Conciliator, that is, from Sev himself as he relates it in his cell. Never mind Canog, who also wrote the story down; he was just a scribe.
My question is this: Whence the story? What is its genesis? Who was the original author in this chain of story tellers?

Roy

1999-03-29 Tony Ellis #

Argues that the story has no single originating author but forms a closed loop, with Severian himself as the original author who witnesses Urth's end and writes his narrative. Grounds this in the implication that Severian casts his account into time, and in the idea that his narrative could be the lost Book of the New Sun Dr Talos's play is based on, not Canog's text.
proposes · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Severian is the original author of his own narrative; Severian's narrative is the lost Book of the New Sun

Well, like the note the protagonist gives to himself in "The Technicolour Time Machine", or the poems of William Ashbless in "The Anubis Gates", the joke here may be that no one quite originates the story, that it's a closed loop.

But the "original" author is Severian himself. He's the one who actually witnesses the end of Urth and lives to write about it. Sev witnesses Urth's last day, Sev writes about it, Sev presumably casts his narrative into the seas of Time. (I say "presumably" because I can't remember if he ever actually says he is going to do this or not - but surely the implication is there.) If you can accept that Severian's narrative constitutes, either wholly or in part, the "lost" Book of the New Sun on which Dr Talos says his play is based, rather than Canog's text, then no paradox exists.
