2014-10-12 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Asks whether readers have considered Latro's role as a brigand, pointing to the Latin word latro, which Wiktionary glosses as mercenary, highwayman and chessman. Notes that earlier discussion has focused on Latro as a mercenary and somewhat less on him as a pawn, and invites comment on the brigand sense.
questions · source-and-allusion

Wiktionary lists three meanings for the Latin word "latro": 1. mercenary
2. highwayman; brigand
3. chessman; pawn

I've seen some discussion of Latro's role as a mercenary, somewhat less of his role as a pawn. But has anyone anything to say about his role as a brigand?

2014-10-12 Marc Aramini replying to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that Latro's name fits the soldier sense of the Latin word, with brigand being a pejorative view of a free-company man of arms, and that he need not match every dictionary meaning. Responds to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's Wiktionary-based question about Latro as brigand by pointing to Latro's association with Pleistorus and Ares.
answers · source-and-allusion

Anything manly or combative is under Latro's provenance, since he is closely associated with Pleistorus and Ares. In the texts I think much was made of "Latro" meaning simply something close to soldier ... brigand might be simply something of a pejorative view of a free company man of arms.

[ He doesn't have to fit every definition of the word, after all - might be like trying to take "He lies in bed" as indicating someone who prevaricates while supine.]
