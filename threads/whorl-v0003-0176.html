The Pas Perplex: Two-Headed Tyrants in Generation Starship Fiction

Whorl list · 1 messages · 1997-02-24 to 1997-02-24

1997whorlThe Book of the Long SunRanjit Bhatnagar

Themes: Gods, angels and the Increate

1997-02-24 Ranjit Bhatnagar #

Riffs on generation-starship fiction, citing Heinlein's Orphans of the Sky and Benford's stories, and argues that the Whorl may be unique in keeping its cargo deliberately ignorant. Then, relaying a viscacha and mantis exchange, it floats the Lemuroid conspirators as the likely cause of Viron's godlessness in the Plan of Pas.
proposes · source-and-allusion · puzzle-solving · theories: The Lemuroid conspirators removed the gods from Viron

[sorry about the title; I just couldn't resist!]

Dominus reminds me that a major character in that other
classic of Generation Starship Fiction, Heinlein's _Orphans of the Sky,_ was a two-headed mutant who led a small group of colonists, previously unaware that they lived on a
spaceship at all, off the ship to the destination planet.

Have any of you seen the teevee scifi series "Starlost?" Pretty dreary, but I was quite fond of the MONITORS,
exquisitely polite and sometimes helpful entities who
appeared on screens when summoned (by the
colonists... unaware that they lived on a spaceship at
all...)

Someone asked on the net if there's ever been a story about a generation starship where the colonists DIDN'T forget
their history and mission, and managed to settle the target planet without a hitch. Now that I think of it, Gregory Benford has written a few stories in which the colonists didn't forget, but I don't think they ever made it to their destination in any of them. The Whorl could be unique in having a cargo deliberately kept in the dark. That they're referred to as cargo at all...

Anyway...

viscacha:
[Kypris] and Tartaros worked fulfill the Plan of Pas
by getting a lander launched from Viron. Why Viron?
Because it's a godless (in both senses) city -- no
theophanies for 30 years.

That's great! Finally an answer for "Why Viron?"

But if Quetzal wasn't primarily responsible for this
shortage of gods in Viron, um, who was?

mantis:
maybe it had to do with the switch to card currency?

But isn't there evidence that cards are used outside of
Viron too?

The Lemuroid conspirators?

Yeah, they seem to have the technical knowledge and
resources; though I've suggested that they might have
motives similar to the gods on a smaller scale, they still might not like having the gods hanging around and meddling.

-rabbit
