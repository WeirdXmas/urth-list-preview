The thread: Posters debated whether a dream passage in Silk's narration, in which Mamelta, Hyacinth, Mother, Kypris and a swallowing fish appear together, is deliberately crafted clue material or merely atmospheric. Lee Berman and Gerry Quinn both held that Wolfe does not assemble dream images at random, with Berman stressing the author's signals over Silk's own thoughts and Quinn asking how dream readings square with Mamelta's actual acts and words. Marc Aramini offered a reading of the fish as Christianity swallowing the goddess of love and the true Outsider, and asked whether Mamelta is Kypris and whether Mucor is killing the Monarch's family. The thread ends in three overlapping interpretations with no resolution.
  • Lee Berman: dream passage is deliberately crafted, so its linked figures carry meaning; Silk is not a real person whose thoughts outweigh the author's signals
  • Marc Aramini: the swallowing fish symbolizes Christianity absorbing the goddess of love, the real Outsider; asks if Mamelta is Kypris and Mucor kills the Monarc
  • Gerry Quinn: Wolfe does not throw dream images together randomly, so the fish and figures are clues; asks how this reconciles with Mamelta's acts and words

Themes: Ghosts, dreams and visions, Unreliable narration, Catholic faith and sacraments

2010-12-21 Lee Berman #

Argues that a dream passage in Silk's narration, where several women run together, is deliberately crafted by Wolfe rather than random dream imagery, so the connections between Mamelta, Hyacinth, Mother and Kypris carry meaning for readers. Responds to Gerry's claim that the sleep imagery is merely atmospheric, stressing that Silk is not a real person whose thoughts matter more than the author's signals.
disputes · close-textual-reading · author-and-biography · theories: Mamelta, Hyacinth, Mother and Kypris are deliberately linked

Easily only if there was a real person sleeping. There is not. There is an author creating this scene with carefully chosen words. It is actually quite difficult for me to interpret this as Gene Wolfe randomly throwing faces and people together with no meaning to it other than conveying a dream atmosphere.

Again, we are not dealing with a real person named Silk. Therefore whatever Silk may be "thinking" is secondary in importance to "tips to the Reader". Gene Wolfe's main purpose in writing is to communicate to his readers not to create independent, imaginary characters without meaning to the reader.

Or it may be that Wolfe repeatedly connecting Mamelta and Hyacinth and Mother and Kypris in various ways is once again trying to tell the reader something. Perhaps it is something more than simply "some women = all women". I'm willing to take a hint.

2010-12-21 Gerry Quinn replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that Wolfe does not throw dream images together at random, so Silk's dream about the fish that swallowed Mamelta, Lemur and certain women should be read as meaningful clues. Responds to Lee Berman by asking whether his own reading takes account of Mamelta's acts and words, which Wolfe also wrote, and how it reconciles them with the dream interpretations.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Silk's dream contains meaningful clues about the fish

From: "Lee Berman" <[email]>

Most of his dream is about the fish that swallowed Mamelta. It also includes Lemur, and certain women. The women seem to be ones Silk associates with sex to some degree. It's not randomly thrown together, but nor is it necessarily a cipher by which one can decode the meaning of the entire text. If it does mean something, maybe it suggests we should think more about that fish!

But he is communicating by way of a novel, asnd to do this he must create characters with some element of versimilitude. You cannot simply ignore the events of the novel in favour of what might be one of a million interpretations of apparent allusions.

By all means! But what about the part of my post that you didn't reply to, concerning: the acts and words of Mamelta herself? Gene Wolfe wrote those too, you know! Are you willing to take a hint from that part? If so, how do you reconcile it with your interpretations of the parts above?

I *don't* think Wolfe throws things together randomly. So I try to find an interpretation that fits the different clues.

- Gerry Quinn
