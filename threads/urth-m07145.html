The thread: Ryan Bonneville, having just finished An Evil Guest, asked the list for a reading order for H.P. Lovecraft's fiction as background to Wolfe. Matthew Keeley, Adam Thornton and Fred Kiesche each offered lists of well-known tales, with Keeley noting Randolph Carter may be referenced in the Wolfe novel. Jeff Wilson argued that The Whisperer in Darkness shares Wolfe's unreliable narrators and nested epistolary structure, and added a pick to Thornton's list. Kiesche also joked about reading habits and repeated Thornton's stories without an ordering of his own. The thread drifted into simple list-swapping and ended without any real debate over Wolfe.
  • Ryan Bonneville: asked for a reading order for Lovecraft as background to An Evil Guest
  • Matthew Keeley: recommended Call of Cthulhu, Whisperer in Darkness and the Randolph Carter stories
  • Adam Thornton: offered about eight well-regarded tales, inviting others to add theirs
  • Jeff Wilson: Whisperer in Darkness resembles Wolfe in its lying narrators and nested letters
  • Fred Kiesche: listed six Lovecraft stories and noted Library of America and free online sources

Themes: Unreliable narration, Stories within stories, The dying sun and the cosmos

The most obviously relevant stories are probably "The Call of Cthulhu", "The Whisperer in Darkness", and "Nyarlathotep". You might want to look at some of the Randolph Carter stories - "The Statement of Randolph Carter" and "The Dream Quest of Unknown Kadadath". (I haven't read "Dream Quest" myself, but people tell me it's somewhat important to the Lovecraft canon.. and it features Randolph Carter, who may or may not be referenced in AEG)

If you want to go a bit further, I'm a great fan of "The Rats in the Walls", "The Outsider", and "The Nameless City" (the last of which scared me half to death when I was nine or ten).

http://www.dagonbytes.com/thelibrary/lovecraft/index.html

-Matt

2008-10-14 Adam Thornton replying to Ryan Bonneville #

Offers a personal reading list of Lovecraft stories for someone who has just finished An Evil Guest and wants background on H.P. Lovecraft's work. Lists about eight well-regarded tales, including The Call of Cthulhu, At the Mountains of Madness and The Color Out of Space, while noting the picks are off the top of his head and invite others to add theirs.
answers

In no particular order, off the top of my head, *I* think the really good stuff is:

_The Call of Cthulhu_
_The Dream-Quest of Unknown Kadath_
_At the Mountains of Madness_
"Pickman's Model"
"The Rats In The Walls"
"The Color Out Of Space"
"The Dreams In The Witch-House"
"The Thing On The Doorstep"
"The Dunwich Horror"

No doubt other people will add their picks, and I will say "Oh! Yeah! Wish I'd remembered *that* one!"

Adam

2008-10-15 Jeff Wilson replying to Adam Thornton #

Argues that Lovecraft's novella The Whisperer in Darkness resembles Wolfe's fiction, pointing to its unreliable or lying narrators, cryptic nested epistolary stories, and alien conspiracy plot. Responds to Adam Thornton's quoted list of favourite Lovecraft stories by adding a pick, with no close quotation of the text given.
supports

_The Whisperer In Darkness_ stands out as somewhat Wolfean, with unreliable or downright mendacious narrators, cryptic nested epistolary stories, and alien conspiracies. Jolly good ambiguous fun!

2008-10-15 Fred Kiesche replying to Ryan Bonneville #

Recommends a set of H.P. Lovecraft stories to a reader who finished An Evil Guest and wants background reading, listing The Call of Cthulhu, At the Mountains of Madness, The Whisperer in Darkness, The Color Out of Space, The Shadow Out of Time and The Music of Eric Zahn. Responds to Ryan Bonneville's request for a reading list, noting the stories are available in a Library of America volume and free online sources.
answers

Some of my personal favorites are listed below. You can get them in the single-volume set from Library of America and multiple trade and mass market paperback collections. They are also all available online from Project Gutenberg or Manybooks.net and other sources.

The Call of Cthulhu
At the Mountains of Madness
The Whisperer in Darkness
The Color Out of Space
The Shadow Out of Time
The Music of Eric Zahn

...and many others, but those ought to get you started!
