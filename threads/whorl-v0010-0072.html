Pajaroku

1999whorlAlice Turner

1999-10-11 Alice Turner #

Remarks that the town name Pajarocu and its idea of an invisible phantom city evoke Borges and Calvino, and suggests the townspeople look more Mayan or Incan than Wolfe's description implies. Grounds this in her own sense of Mayan physical build and the way Horn's view of the features differs from hers.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Pajarocu's people modeled on Mayan or Incan peoples

Could there possibly be a more Borgesian sounding town name than Pajarocu? I :don't think so. And the concept of it, a phantom town, an invisible :city--the perfect mix of Borges and Calvino. The way the people look seems :somewhat Mayan to me, but with differences. I would not characterize the :Mayan people as "extremely thin," in fact they are rather on the sturdy, :compact side. Also, in my opinion, Mayan features suit men much better than :they do women, but Horn sees it differently. Maybe they are more Incan than :Mayan--I have never been to Peru so can't judge first-hand.

:-alga
