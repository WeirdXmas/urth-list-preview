2008urthMarc AraminiRoy C. LackeyJordon Flato
The thread: Participants debated whether the miraculous elements in Wolfe's New Sun books, especially the Outsider's enlightenment, should be read as ambiguous or as a secular, preprogrammed message from Typhon and Mamelta. Marc Aramini argued the ambiguity should stand and that such a message could still be divine, praising Wright's Shadows of the New Sun as Wolfe speaking rather than an imposed thesis. Jordon Flato asked for textual evidence that the reading is commonly accepted, argued the Outsider seems genuine, and rejected the preprogrammed reading outright, agreeing with Roy Lackey's doubt. The thread ended in disagreement with no resolution recorded.
  • Marc Aramini: Miraculous elements should stay ambiguous; the Outsider's enlightenment could be a preprogrammed message yet still divine
  • Jordon Flato: The Outsider seems genuine; the preprogrammed-by-Typhon-and-Mamelta reading is far off the mark and needs textual evidence

Themes: Gods, angels and the Increate, Free will and choice

2008-11-25 Marc Aramini #

Argues that the miraculous in Wolfe's New Sun books should stay ambiguous, and that the Outsider's enlightenment may be a preprogrammed message from Typhon and Mamelta while still being divine. Responds to Chris's view of Wright's Shadows of the New Sun, praising it as Wolfe speaking rather than an author forcing a thesis onto the text.
supports · theology-and-religion · theories: Outsider's enlightenment was preprogrammed by Typhon and Mamelta

I agree with Chris in his impressions of Wright - I feel like the ambiguity around the miraculous in Wolfe is necessary and important. The enlightenment of the outsider DOES wind up being a preprogrammed message from Typhon and his mistress Mamelta (like Marlon Brando in Superman), though Crane&#39;s interpretaion sounds lame at first. Yet it can still be divine enlightenment even if it has a rational cause.

Shadows of the New Sun is a fabulous Wright creation because it is actually Wolfe speaking and writing rather than an author with a thesis he must force the text to support, (as we all tend to in graduate lit courses when push comes to shove.)

Asks whether it is a commonly accepted reading that the Outsider's enlightenment in The Book of the Long Sun is a preprogrammed message from Typhon and Mamelta, and asks for textual evidence. Argues the Outsider seems genuine and endorses the idea that even the villains serve the Increate.
