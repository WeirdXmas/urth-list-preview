Clute's Non-Revelation

Whorl list · 5 messages · 2000-08-16 to 2000-08-21

2000whorlThe Book of the Long SunJohn CluteAlice TurnerPatrick O'LearyJames Jordan
The thread: Posters debated why Horn eats in the Book of the Long Sun, when Fava does not. Akt argued Horn's sturdier, stockier new body requires him to restrain his appetites consciously. Jim Jordan read Horn as absorbing Silk's fasting and learning the inhumi's secret of self-giving, a Christlike sacrifice, while doubting the Gattaca-like hypothesis. John Clute conceded that the fasting detail had slipped his mind and agreed Oreb may be a Holy Spirit figure, clarifying that his earlier In Green's Jungles reading was a first-response sketch. Later posts turned to welcoming a newcomer and list scope, and the thread drifted into social teasing without settling the question.
  • akt: Horn restrains his appetite because his new body is sturdier and stockier than Silk's
  • Jim Jordan: Horn absorbs Silk's fasting and learns the inhumi's secret of self-giving as Christlike sacrifice
  • John Clute: fasting detail was forgotten and not certain; Oreb may be a Holy Spirit figure

Themes: Christ figures and messiahs, Beasts and the animal in us, Suffering and sacrifice

2000-08-16 Alice Turner #

Argues that Horn's eating habits in the Book of the Long Sun are explained by his new body being sturdier and stockier than Silk's, so he must consciously restrain appetites to avoid becoming overweight. Offers this as a reply to others who were puzzled by Horn's eating on first reading, noting that unlike Fava he does eat.
proposes · close-textual-reading

Thanks for the vote of confidence, several of you. I have a thought about Horn's eating habits, which certainly puzzled me too on first reading. It's awfully Wolfean. The fact is that the Horn body was sturdier, stockier and needed more food than the Silk body. Horn's habits and appetites remain as they were, but he must consciously curb them in his new body, or he would soon be overweight. But he DOES eat, unlike Fava, sweet little bean.

-alga

2000-08-19 James Jordan #

Argues that Horn, now living in Silk's body, is absorbing Silk's fasting habits and learning the secret of the inhumi through self-giving, reading the eucharistic blood-giving idea as a Christlike sacrifice. Responds to Alga's point about Horn's appetite and to the suggestion that giving blood is the secret, backing it while doubting the Gattaca-like hypothesis and citing Horn's failed attempt to save Fava with his warmth.
supports · theology-and-religion · allegory-and-typology · theories: Horn learns the inhumi secret through self-giving; Silk's body and blood as eucharistic sacrifice; Giving blood is the secret of the inhumi

It's always interesting to agree with Alga! I'll repeat what I wrote before, in case it did not get through: Being on a kind of continual fast is what Silk does. Now that Horn is in Silk's body (as most of us assume), he's got some of Silk's habits. "Eucharistically" (and Gattaca-like), he's living off the body and blood of another, the quasi-messianic Silk. It's affecting him.
And since Wolfe has put human-inhumi relations at the center of the trilogy's concerns (by repeatedly calling attention to the mystery), it makes much more sense to see Horn as a character learning about that mystery, and learning to implement it. Whatever it may be. He needs to be a human being for this to work.
Of course, Wolfe can fool us all! But I'm very unconvinced of this hypothesis.
Now, a bit more on the secret. I suggested being willing to die for others, and specifically being willing to die for the inhumi, as the secret. It has now been suggested that voluntarily giving them one's blood might be the secret. "Eucharistically," that boils down to the same kind of thing. And it makes good sense to me as a theory, for now anyway. Finally, I note that Horn tried to save Fava by giving her his warmth. But that was not enough. Yet, it is a step along the say. Self-giving as the key.

Patera Nutria

2000-08-21 John Clute #

Clarifies that the author's earlier reading of In Green's Jungles was a general first-response sketch, not a revealed secret, and agrees Oreb may be a Holy Spirit figure. Responds to Patera Nutria's point about Silk fasting, saying the fasting detail had slipped his mind and conceding the claim is not certain.
supports · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: Oreb is a giant paraclete, the Holy Spirit

In-Reply-To: <[email]>
Patera Nutria,
Have just joined WHORL, and don't know etiquette within this august Theatre, but thought I'd tie this to yours on Silk to indicate my intuition that you are probably (though, as you say) not absolutely certainly right. That fasting is what Silk does escaped my memory, certainly, when I was laying down a first _reading_ response to _In Green's Jungles_ .
But to make it absolutely clear what _I_ was doing: no revelations were being hoicked into view, certainly not at this stage: I was laying down for readers far more general than WHORL's List some sense of the pattern of the book: it was, implicitly, an invocation to read out in the open.
Oreb as Holy Spirit. Very fine. Especially if the bird, in human terms, could be defined as a giant paraclete.

Best,
John C
