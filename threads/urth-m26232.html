The thread: The thread began as Marc Aramini's argument that Spring Wind in the Short Sun is a Green Man figure linked to Mars, Hyacinthus and blood-born flowers, which Lee largely supported and extended. It then shifted to the Long Sun, where Lee proposed that the Hieros and Hierarchs are of human origin via rebreeding, a claim Jeffery Wilson contested by arguing the Hierarchs belong to the Hierogrammates' creature line. Wilson and Lee went back and forth on whether the tongueless Foil, Blood and Oreb are Hierarchs and on the Yesodis hierarchy. The thread ended with Lee arguing Wolfe keeps the angelic Hierarchs deliberately ambiguous, with no clear resolution of the origin question.
  • Marc Aramini: Spring Wind is a Green Man and Mars figure tied to the Hyacinthus myth and blood-born flowers
  • Lee: Green Man ties to Pan, Dionysus and wine; the Hierarchs are ultimately of human origin
  • Jeffery Wilson: Hierarchs are creatures of the Hierogrammates, not lost Hiero humans
  • Lee: Wolfe keeps the Hierarchs ambiguous, like seraphim described beyond human limits

Themes: Myth and folklore sources, Gods, angels and the Increate, Evolution and the future of humanity

2014-09-15 Marc Aramini replying to Lee #

Argues that Spring Wind is more a Green Man figure than Typhon, and that the Hyacinthus myth of flowers springing from blood is vital to the Short Sun. Replies to Lee's comment on Spring Wind's half-plant nature, proposing a cycle of fruit, wine, blood and flowers that Severian's blood on the thorn completes.
proposes · myth-and-structure · theology-and-religion · theories: Spring Wind is more Green Man than Typhon; Green Man feeds on the New Sun's light; Severian's blood on the thorn completes a Eucharistic cycle

Yes ... it seems that Spring Wind is much more Green Man than Typhon ... and I have noted the Green Man feeds off the light of the New Sun in something I am writing up now.

The story of Hyacinthus, where flowers spring up from blood, is vital to the Short Sun universe ... somehow this Spring Wind story is its opposite, I think. A cyclic kind of communion is implied. fruit to wine to blood, returning to flowers. Severian's blood on that thorn also somehow completes the Eucharistic cycle.

2014-09-15 Lee Berman #

Asks Marc Aramini to add the Green Man to his plant and animal theme, tying it to Pan and Dionysus worship and to wine reverence behind Christianity. Recalls Aramini's links between the Vironese naming customs, parasitic vines and Neighbors, and the Transfiguration, citing Severian's leaf that grows legs in Shadow of the Torturer.
supports · source-and-allusion · theology-and-religion · theories: Green Man belongs in the plant-animal theme; Judeo-pagan wine reverence links to Christianity; Plant-animal theme linked to Transfiguration

IIRC you connect this theme to the Vironese custom of plant naming of women and animal

naming of men. Also to the connection between botanic parasitic vine/multi-limbed trees

and the zoological parasitic inhumi/multi-limbed Neighbors.

I'm pretty sure you've mentioned noticing that early in Shadow of the Torturer, Severian

describes anticipating a certain light (New Sun) which "engendered life in whatever

objects it fell upon, so that a leaf plucked from a bush grew slender legs and waving feelers..."

I think you've connected all this to the original Transfiguration which involved Jesus emitting

mystic beams of light signifying his (Conciliatory) status as bridge between heaven and earth.

The Transfiguration continuing to be symbolized by a conversion of the grape and grain plant

products to the animal products of blood and flesh.

But I am hoping you include also the Green Man in the constellation of elements to this theme.

Not only because of his combined nature of plant and animal essence, and his ostensible origin

from the New Sun light.

But also because of The Green Man as a pagan deity connected to Great God Pan and Dionysus

worship for which there are undertones and hints throughout the entire Sun Series and which

probably represents the connection between Judeo-pagan wine reverence and its importance to

Christianity.

http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Man

2014-09-16 Marc Aramini replying to Marc Aramini #

Proposes that Spring Wind is not Early Summer's husband's son but was born of the red flower without a thorn, so the Green Man may be partly alien. Grounds this in the Green Man's warning to Severian that a son of his would be an enemy, which Sev takes as generic prophecy.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Spring Wind born of the red flower; Green Man is partially alien

In the story, Early Summer lies to her husband and tells him that Spring Wind is his son, when in fact he is born of the red flower without a thorn. Perhaps the Green Man is partially alien too, and only believes himself a true descendent of man.

The Green Man warns Severian that if he begets a son, it will be his enemy. Sev takes this as a generic prophecy.

humaniform Hierarchs the lost "Hiero" race residing in a place beyond the

I don't know and I suspect we, the human audience, are not really supposed

to know nor fully understand the working of such superior beings. A dog

may have a general sense of "family" but the concept of "great aunt" or

"second cousin-twice removed" is probably beyond its comprehension. So

also, the interrelationships of the Hiero groups may be incomprehensible for

humans.

ultimately human origin for them is implied. If God made humanity (and angels)

in His own image, might not the reverse also be true?
