The thread: Nigel Price asked for details about Gene Wolfe's new story in the children's fantasy anthology Dark Alchemy, edited by Jack Dann and Gardner Dozois, after a Times review and a dealer listing gave few contents. Rex Lycanthrosaurus answered that Dark Alchemy is the UK retitling of the US anthology Wizards and described Wolfe's 'Magical Animals' as a story about a boy who may be Merlin and his bond with Viviane, citing Locus reviews in which only Gevers praised it. Fernando Q. Gouvêa confirmed the reissue relationship, called the Wolfe piece another Arthurian Merlin retelling, and described the book's young adult orientation and coming-of-age and kingship patterns. Price repeated his request for more information, and the thread stayed a short factual exchange with no real dispute.

2007-07-04 Rex Lycanthrosaurus replying to Nigel Price #

Reports that Dark Alchemy is the UK retitling of the US anthology Wizards and describes Gene Wolfe's story 'Magical Animals' as concerning a young boy who may be Merlin and his lifelong bond with Viviane. Answers Nigel Price's question about the anthology and story, citing the March and May Locus reviews, of which only Gevers mentions Wolfe's story favorably.
answers

"Dark Alchemy" is the retitled UK version of the US published "Wizards."

The Wolfe story, if memory serves, is "Magical Animals" and is about a young boy who may be Merlin and his relationship throughout time with Viviane. (The book has gone back to the library, so I can't look it up.)

There are reviews in the March (Gevers) and May (Horton) issues of Locus, but only the former mentions the Wolfe story. (He liked it.)

Don't know why the antho was retitled for sale in the UK -- one would think Pottermaniacs would be more inclined to pick up a volume labeled "Wizards" than "Dark Alchemy."

2007-07-04 Fernando Q. Gouvea replying to Nigel Price #

Informs the list that the British edition of the anthology Dark Alchemy appears to be a reissue of a US book called Wizards, and that its Wolfe story is another Arthurian retelling focused on Merlin. Responds to Nigel Price's quoted review of the anthology by describing the book's young adult orientation and its recurring coming-of-age and kingship story patterns.
informs

This seems to be the British edition of a book called "Wizards" in the US. The Wolfe story is another take on the Arthurian legends, focusing on Merlin this time.

The book seems to be aimed at the "young adult" market. It contains a lot of "mother was right after all" and "so then I became the king" stories.

Fernando
