A Wooden Leg Named Smith

2002urthThe Book of the New SunDan'l Danehy-Oakes

Themes: Robots, androids and artificial life

2002-11-18 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that the Claw would likely fail or take root if a wooden leg were wounded, and that Jonas and Jolenta differ from prosthetic cases because they are wholly artificial persons the Claw refuses to heal. Responds to Roy's question about whether the Claw's time-reaching power would work on a man with a wooden leg, reasoning from the Jonas and Jolenta episodes in The Book of the New Sun.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Claw refuses to heal wholly artificial persons

Roy quoting me:

Per se I do not think it would represent a problem (though I suspect that if it was the wooden leg that was woefully wounded the Claw would either fail or make it take root).=20

But in the cases of Jonas and Jolenta we don't have "the presence=20 of prosthetics" -- Jonas actually came into existence as, and=20 Jolenta has become, in a different way, an _entirely_ artificial=20 person, and I suspect that the Claw fails/refuses to work with=20 such.

--Blattid
