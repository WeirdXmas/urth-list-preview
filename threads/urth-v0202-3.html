2002-02-07 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that Wolfe, as a committed Christian, sees hope for humanity in a God-and-Man hybrid and in real transformation, reading Hyacinth's ever-blooming flower as a figure of that hope. Supports the earlier post's reading by adding the Catholic view that the hybrid's body is ingested and glorified, and by citing the carol "Lo! How a Rose E'er Blooming" on roses as figures for Christ.
supports · allegory-and-typology · theology-and-religion · theories: Hyacinth represents the hybrid Christ figure; Transformation through ingestion of the hybrid's body; Salvation comes only after physical death

Ahem.

Not to be always harping on the obvious... but...

Wolfe, as a committed Christian, certainly believes that the only real hope for the human race lies in a hybrid (of God and Man) and in a very real transformation of humanity - both the individual human person and the whole human race. The Catholic Church would, I believe, add that this transformation (into an eternal and "glorified" form - thus "ever- blooming" indeed) can come about only through the 'ingestion' of the body of the hybrid, which changes us to be "more conformed" to the "image" of the hybrid. And, as with Hyacinth, the true salvation or transformation comes (can come) only after the death of this physical self.
