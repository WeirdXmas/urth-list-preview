Wolfe in Fringe

The thread: Jordon Flato reported that an episode of the TV show Fringe name-checks Gene Wolfe, with a character asking for Lake of the Long Sun and a bookshop clerk claiming to worship Wolfe, noting the scene's link to guided evolution. Matthew Keeley followed with a clip and pointed out that an earlier season's bookstore features a first edition of Jonathan Carroll's The Land of Laughs, praising the show's taste in science fiction. Marc Aramini replied with a light joke and a remark that Carroll wrote the same book many times, offering a casual comment rather than a substantive reading. The thread stayed a brief exchange of sightings with no real debate.

2012-03-31 Jordon Flato #

Reports that an episode of the TV show Fringe name-checks Gene Wolfe, with a character asking for Lake of the Long Sun and a bookshop clerk claiming to worship Wolfe. Describes it as the most direct popular-culture mention of Wolfe the poster has seen, noting the scene's link to guided evolution.
informs

I don't know if anyone else here watches the television show Fringe (which as far as sci-fi tv goes is pretty good), but last episode they gave a great name check to Wolfe.

The two leads, Olivia and Peter walk into a bookshop to get info from the owner, and to break the ice, Peter says "I'm looking to pick up Lake of the Long Sun by Gene Wolfe for my friend here. Are you familiar with Wolfe?" Book store clerk: "Know hiim? I worship him. Of course I have it. I have the whole Solar Cycle in first addition. Signed or unsigned?" And then the scene continues on from there. Interesting to note that the information they were tracking down in another work was about guided evolution (?!?!?)

That is the most direct name check I've ever seen of Wolfe in popular culture. It gave me a little nerd woody.

2012-04-08 Marc Aramini replying to Matthew Keeley #

Jokingly remarks that a TV clip is amusing for a viewer who enjoys Wolfe, and argues that Jonathan Carroll wrote the same book many times with a recurring pattern. Responds to Matthew Keeley's post reporting a Fringe bookstore scene featuring Carroll's The Land of Laughs, offering a casual comment rather than a substantive reading.
chats

I don't watch TV anymore except for violence (boxing and whatnot) but that clip kind of cracked me up.? It's like they are saying, if you read and enjoy Gene, cuneiform is no problem.

Yes, Carroll is very good ... but he wrote the same book many times over - his books have a definite pattern that they all tend to stick to. ?
