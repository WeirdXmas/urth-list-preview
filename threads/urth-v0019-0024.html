Forlesen's end

Urth list · 1 messages · 1998-09-27 to 1998-09-27

1998urthMichael Andre-Driussi

1998-09-27 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Argues that the Yes/No sequence closing the story "Forlesen" is the answer to the final question, matching the nine archetypes (Doctor, Priest, Philosopher and so on) with Yes or No or Maybe. Responds to Robert Borski's binary observation by pointing out that Forlesen receives all nine answers because he did not specify which he wanted, and asks how a binary reading handles "Maybe."
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Forlesen's Yes/No sequence answers the nine archetype questions

Robert Borski,

It was pointed out elsewhere/somewhen that the last bit of "Forlesen," the Yes No part, is actually the answer (from the nine archetypes) to the final question.

Doctor --No
Priest --Yes
Philosopher --No
Theologian --Yes
Actor --Yes
Warlock --No
National Hero --Yes
Aged Loremaster--Yes
Novelist --Maybe

Forlesen gets all nine answers because he didn't specify which one he wanted.

This does not alter your binary observation, which is an interesting one, but how does binary (0,1) deal with "maybe" (2?)?

=mantis=
