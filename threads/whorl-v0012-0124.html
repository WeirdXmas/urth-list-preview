Themes: Memory and forgetting

2001-02-05 Mark Millman #

Proposes that a line in Return to the Whorl where Horn says "Nettle" reflects a mixing of Horn's and Silk's memories, with "It" meaning the azoth and the name meant being Hyacinth. Supports this by noting that the earlier name substitutions in On Blue's Waters have reversed, so the error fits a pattern.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Horn and Silk's memories mix in Return to the Whorl; Horn means Hyacinth when he says Nettle

For the most part, I agree with David DiGiacomo's
answers. For the question above, though, I'd suggest
that it's another example of Horn's and Silk's mem-
ories mixing. "It" is the azoth, if I recall correctly (I don't have the book here to check); he says "Nettle"
but means, I think, Hyacinth. Note, if you will, that
this error is the opposite of the ones he was making
at the beginning of OBW; at that time, Hyacinth's
name often appeared when he meant Nettle, but by
this point, the substitutions have entirely reversed
themselves.

Nacre
