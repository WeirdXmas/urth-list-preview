Inhumechanics

The thread: Posters debated the physical nature and abilities of the Inhumi in The Book of the Long Sun, starting with Dan Parmenter's speculation about a radial, many-limbed body and how far their shapeshifting goes. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued that Inhumi inherit intelligence from the parents they fed on, and sketched their life cycle, while Kevin J. Maroney and Michael Straight argued over whether they are projective telepaths that make prey see what they wish or a weaker force that merely discourages close looking. Straight conceded the text does not settle the question, so the thread ended without agreement.
  • Dan Parmenter: Inhumi have a radial, umbrella-like body and shapeshifting may be extensive, with Lamarckian inheritance possible
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: Inhumi inherit intelligence from the parents they fed on, tested against Fava's feeding on children
  • Kevin J. Maroney: Inhumi are projective telepaths who make prey see them as they wish, possibly targeting one species at a time
  • Michael Straight: Inhumi use a weak psychic effect that discourages close looking and overlooks disguise flaws, not outright alteration of perception

Themes: Masks, disguise and shapeshifting, Beasts and the animal in us, Robots, androids and artificial life

2001-03-28 Dan Parmenter #

Speculates on the physical nature of the Inhumi in The Book of the Long Sun, imagining a radial, umbrella-like body with many limbs and wondering how far their shapeshifting goes. Responds to Blattid's questions about their limbs and genetic download, and mentions Borski's telepathy idea, Lamarckian inheritance, and Wolfe's own statements on Lysenkoism.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Inhumi have a radial, umbrella-like body

Oddly enough, this hit me this weekend too. I had come to think of the Inhumi in their natural state as a kind of shapeless bat. I suppose that the question again emerges: how much actual shape-shifting can they do? And what exactly do they have to work with? We hear of limbs being stretched, but how many "limbs" do they have? I'm imagining a "radial" design, a kind of soft umbrella shape which might give them as many possible "limbs" as they need, though the shaping of hands, even poorly-functioning ones seems dicey as does any kind of walking, though it seems that the Inhumi can do both, if badly. We hear on numerous occasions of reshaping facial features and the wearing of powders and wigs, but I never assumed that they were just flying silly-putty with a head. There's also the seeming "telepathic" component. Wasn't it Borski who thought that this may well be their primary mode of "shape-shifting".

Lamarck games I hope. Wolfe has never advocated Lysenkoism and has touched on the differences in several interviews and articles. But yes, Lamarckism is clearly one of Wolfe's recurring themes.

Shellac

2001-03-28 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Proposes that Inhumi inherit their intelligence from the parents they fed on rather than from their own prey, and tests this against Fava's feeding on children. Responds to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's remarks on Inhumi shape and telepathy, correcting the attribution from Krait to Lamarck and sketching the Inhumi life cycle from eggs to hatchlings.
proposes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion · theories: Inhumi inherit intelligence from parents' prey

Black Jacques Shellac a écrit:

Whereas I had been thinking of them as looking like, well, leeches.

H'mmm. Leeches are kinda-sorta radial. At least their mouths are.

Actually, I think it was Krait -- He says something to Horn about people seeing what they expect to see and the inhumi exploiting that, which struck me as one of those "I mean more than I'm saying" things.

Uh, yeah. That. Oops. That's right. I meant Lamarck. It
was a typo, honest. Lamarck.

The point of my question being, of course, that the inhumi's kids seem to inherit the characteristics their mummsies (and poppsies?) acquire from the various sentients they suck on.

Okay, inhumi life cycle basics. They lay eggs in water. Baby inhumi come to a certain amount of development, apparently even including some degree of consciousness, in the eggses, precioussss. They hatch in some kind of aquatically correct shape -- little crocogaters? Awww, cuuuuute.

H'mmm. Wolfe describes them as reptilian; I think they're more amphibious. Anywho, eventually they go on land and
start looking for something sentient to snack on.

Now, how about this proposal: An inhumu (or inhuma) does not get his(her) smarts from who he(she) feeds on, but on who his(her) mother (parents?) fed on.

No; that won't work -- viz. Fava having to feast on children now and then to counteract the effects of feeding on Granny. Or is that just physical appearance? I'm so confused...

2001-03-28 Kevin J. Maroney #

Claims credit for proposing that the inhumi are projective telepaths, and adds that they make prey see them as they wish as well as physically deforming. Responds to Shellac's quote about telepathic shape-shifting by citing textual hints and speculating they can target only one species at a time, which would explain why animals detect them as humans.
supports · close-textual-reading · theories: Inhumi are projective telepaths; Inhumi make prey see them as they want; Inhumi target one species at a time

I actually think I was the original advocate of the "inhumi as projective telepaths" theory, but I could be wrong on both counts.

It's clear that the inhumi do physically deform to resemble the things they want to resemble, but there are also some textual hints that they make their prey see them as they want to see them. (I wonder if they can only do this to one target species at a time. This would explain why animals can detect them when they're posing as humans.)

2001-03-28 Michael Straight #

Proposes that the inhumi use a weak psychic ability that discourages observers from looking closely and overlooks flaws in their disguise, rather than altering what people see outright. Responds to Kevin J. Maroney's suggestion that the inhumi make prey perceive them as they wish, admitting the text does not spell this out.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Inhumi use weak psi to discourage close looking

My impression was a weak psi-ability that doesn't influence what people see so much as encourage them not to look too closely and to ignore the flaws in the physical disguise, but that it could be overcome by someone determined to pay attention and be wary. But I don't think it's really spelled out. They might be influencing perceptions as well.

-Rostrum
