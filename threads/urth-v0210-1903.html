PEACE: Weer's Room

2002-10-22 Stone Ox #

Proposes that the room where Weer wakes in Peace, with its long walled-in porch shape and a stone fireplace at one end, is actually his coffin. Supports the claim by pointing to the interval between his death and the falling elm, when he seems to have lived mainly in that room, and closes with Emily Dickinson's lines on the dead in their alabaster chambers.
proposes · close-textual-reading · allegory-and-typology · theories: Weer's room is his coffin

When the falling of the elm awakens Weer, he finds himself in a room with a fieldstone fireplace. He seems to have been intermittently awake between his death and the fall of the tree, but to have lived mainly in this room. Where is this room?

Consider its shape. It's described as a long walled-in
porch, and it has a stone (fireplace) at one end. It's
Weer's coffin.

-- Stone Ox

2002-10-22 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Proposes that the rooms Weer visits in Peace are really coffins in a necropolis, so the memory mansion is built on a cemetery, and raises whether Weer is a ghost haunting a real mansion. Grounds this in the coffin reading of the first room, the Necronomicon-inspired idea of a memory mansion inside the skull, and the first room matching the house Weer moved to from his apartment.
proposes · close-textual-reading · allegory-and-typology · theories: Weer's room is his coffin; memory mansion is built over a cemetery; Weer is a ghost haunting Weerland; first room is the house Weer moved to

Very nice! Have we ever seen that before? I don't think so. Gotten close, with "the memory mansion is all inside the hollow skull" stuff, via the Necronomicon passage.

It leads to another interesting possibility: what if the physically real foundation of the memory mansion is, somehow, the other coffins at the cemetary where Weer is presumably buried (if he is not buried on the grounds of the physical memory mansion, ala Elvis at Graceland)? Thus, some (all?) of the rooms he visits are really just the coffins of others? And it really is a confused tour of a necropolis.

(Because we have often wrestled with the interface between Weer's AD 2274 environment and the physical world, if such an interface actually exists. If the mansion really exists, call it Weerland, then he is a ghost haunting that place, thinking that he is somehow the last man on Earth; if the mansion does not exist, then what is he conflating with the idea of the mansion?)

About that first room: he seems to think it represents the real mansion that he moved to from his tiny apartment, a place he lived in from around age 50 to his death at 60-something. This would be the place that had the garbage disposal in the kitchen sink, for example.

=mantis=
