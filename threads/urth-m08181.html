2009urthThe Book of the New SunAdam ThorntonCraig BrewerGwern BranwenSon of WitzStuart HammKettil Rejving
The thread: A brief thread opened by Craig Brewer, who passed along a Strange Horizons year-in-review calling An Evil Guest possibly Wolfe's worst book since Operation Ares. Stuart Hamm admitted he also struggled to care about the book, while Gwern Branwen asked why critics panned it when Urth readers seem to like it, admitting he had not read it. Brewer also discussed audiobook relistening to The Book of the New Sun and the narrator's mispronunciations, and Son of Witz praised the audiobook narration while disputing the pronunciation of Patera. The thread ended without agreement, drifting from the critical reception of the novel into audiobook details.
  • Craig Brewer: passes on the harsh review of An Evil Guest as news and relistened to the audiobooks, noting mispronunciations
  • Stuart Hamm: also found An Evil Guest hard to care about
  • Gwern Branwen: asks why critics disliked a book readers seem to like, without having read it
  • Son of Witz: enjoys the audiobook narration and argues Patera should be pronounced Pah-tera by analogy with Maytera

Themes: Books, libraries and writing

2009-01-07 Craig Brewer replying to Kettil Rejving #

Passes along a Strange Horizons year-in-review by Paul Kincaid that names An Evil Guest as among the worst books of 2008, calling it perhaps Wolfe's worst since Operation Ares. Quotes the review's passage directly and links to it, so the mention is offered as news rather than argued.
informs

Thanks for the link.

And just a tiny note: _Strange Horizons_ did their year in review. Unfortunately, this was the single Wolfe mention:

"As to the worst of the year: both The Night Sessions by Ken MacLeod and Matter by Iain M. Banks were disappointing in comparison to their recent novels, but still readable. Which is more than can be said for An Evil Guest by Gene Wolfe, which is perhaps the worst thing he has written since his first novel, Operation Ares." -- Paul Kincaid

2009-01-07 Craig Brewer replying to Son of Witz #

Reports on relistening to The Book of the New Sun through audio versions, noting details that stood out and the narrator's mispronunciations of unusual vocabulary and odd characterization of Severian. Replies to Son of Witz's post about a new Nebula Awards interview with Wolfe, and expresses a wish to hear Wolfe read the whole series.
chats

Sounds indeed. My latest read-thru of New Sun was a "listen-through" from the audio versions that have been posted here before. Quite a different experience with many things standing out that didn't when I've read them before. (Also some unintentionally hilarious moments when the narrator tries to pronounce some of the unusual vocab, and even does it differently within the same paragraph...and then there's some questionable characterization at times...he makes Severian sound like a nasally git...but still quite enjoyable.) It would be wonderful to hear Wolfe read the whole thing.

2009-01-07 Son of Witz replying to Kettil Rejving #

Reports enjoying the audiobook recordings of The Book of the New Sun and Nightside the Long Sun, praising the narrator's Severian and noting how listening surfaces different details than reading. Questions the narrator's pronunciation of Patera, arguing it should sound like Pah-tera rather than Pay-tera by analogy with Maytera.
disputes · close-textual-reading

I agree, I'd love to hear Wolfe read it.
Avers does a good job though. I really like his characterization of Severian, actually, just the right mix of foolishness and sophistication. I do wonder about many of his pronunciations, which change from book to book as well. just started listening to the Nightside audiobook. I think it's Avers, but I skipped the attribution. Right off the bat I was wondering about his pronunciation. Pa(y)tera, not Pa(h)tera. I think the phenome must be Pah, not Pay, by simple contrast to Maytera.

As I may have mentioned, it's great to listen. I notice different stuff than when I'm reading.

~witz
