The Secret: Shame?

Whorl list · 1 messages · 2001-04-03 to 2001-04-03

2001whorlThe Book of the Short SunMichael Straight

Themes: Robots, androids and artificial life

2001-04-03 Michael Straight #

Proposes that Horn's talk of the secret returning the inhumi to vermin is metaphorical, arguing their insecurity about being human is what makes the secret so threatening. Grounds this in Krait's superior attitude toward Horn, read as a front for insecurity, and Krait's torment at being almost but not quite human.
proposes · close-textual-reading · genre-and-craft · theories: the secret threatens inhumi identity more than their power

Could it be that when Horn talks of the secret returning the inhumi to the vermin they once were that he is speaking metaphorically?

Humans currently view the inhumi as intelligent, dangerous enemies and predators, but what if they knew that they are "really" just reptiles who derive their intellegence from human blood. They would perhaps see them as vermin that merely mimick intellegence.

All of the inhumi that hang around Horn seem very insecure and unsettled about their identity--they want to be human, they want other humans to see them as human. Perhaps the reason they are so desperate that Horn not reveal their secret is that it would be intolerable that humans know what they really are. Perhaps it would be so demoralizing that they would be less effective predators, but maybe the real threat is not so much tipping the balance in the humans vs. inhumi struggle as shattering their fragile identities.

Krait affects a superior attitude toward Horn, talking of him as cattle, but if he feels as Jahlee, Fava, and Juganu did, then that is surely a front for his insecurity. How different might their relationship have been if Horn had known from the start that Krait would be an unthinking beast if not for Sinew's blood and that Krait was continually tormented by being almost but not quite human.

-Rostrum
