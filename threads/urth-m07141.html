"My father's mounted guard"

The thread: The thread began with Duncan Truter proposing that Severian in The Urth of the New Sun contains many selves, including Thecla and the eunuch autarch, and asked whether another presence lies within the narrator. Roy C. Lackey supported this with a quoted line about the old Autarch muttering 'Find another,' and John Watkins and Jeff Wilson asked when the old Autarch asserts himself over the narrator. Lackey then disputed the idea that the Autarch fathered Severian and Thecla, arguing Ouen was Severian's father and the mounted guard belonged to Thecla's father. b sharp later disputed the facts Truter relied on and argued that Severian's family is deliberately obscured, so parentage stays open. The thread ended without agreement, with the parentage question left unresolved.
  • Duncan Truter: Severian contains Thecla, the eunuch autarch and predecessors as mingled selves
  • Roy C. Lackey: the old Autarch lives in Severian's mind, but Ouen was Severian's father and the mounted guard was Thecla's father's
  • John Watkins: Severian's miracles may fade after the Claw and he writes as threefold throughout; asks when the Autarch asserts himself
  • Jeff Wilson: asks whether the Old Autarch overrides the narrator as Thecla seems to, without offering an answer
  • b sharp: Truter's facts about Thecla and the Castellan are uncertain; Severian's family is deliberately obscured, so parentage remains doubtful

Themes: Unreliable narration, Doubles, twins and clones, Memory and forgetting

2008-10-15 Roy C. Lackey replying to Jeff Wilson #

Disputes the fanciful theory that the Old Autarch fathered Severian and Thecla, arguing that Ouen was Severian's father and that the mounted guard in the quoted line belongs to Thecla's father. Grounds this on timing and status: Thecla's father died before Severian met her, and a servant's father is unlikely to have commanded a mounted guard.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Old Autarch fathered Severian and Thecla; Ouen was Severian's father; Thecla's father commanded the mounted guard

I don't think so, and the quote suggests the same.

My real point was to address the original thrust of this thread, which has been the subject of much debate and spawned some rather fanciful theories.

Thecla's father had a villa up north on the frontier. The Old Autarch fathered neither Severian nor Thecla. Ouen was Severian's father. Thecla's father died before Severian even met her. Waiter Ouen certainly never commanded a mounted guard. The Old Autarch had been a servant in the House Absolute. It is very unlikely that a lowly servant's father commanded a mounted guard; still less likely to have done so when Thecla was a little girl. So the father in the sentence in question must refer to Thecla's father.

-Roy

2008-10-21 b sharp replying to Duncan Truter #

Disputes Duncan Truter's claimed facts about Thecla and the Castellan, arguing the exultant rules and the ball-giving father are uncertain and that a dream sequence may be involved. Argues Severian's family is deliberately obscured by red herrings, so any proposed parentage, including Dorcas and Ouen, stays open to doubt and denial.
disputes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian's family membership is deliberately obscured by red herrings

I dunno, Roy. "Fact" is a pretty strong word for use describing an ambiguous work of fiction. But anyway, I can't remember reading that both parents were required to be exultant for exultant status. Nor do I remember any statement of class status for the Castellan but I do seem to remember reading that military officer positions might be filled by armigers or exultants. I think there is more evidence that Catherine is not an exultant, due to her height, but not sure it matters. The bit about having a ball every year until he died was part of a dream sequence I think, one which also included a father figure with the head of a brown eyed bull. Given that dreamy milieu I'm not sure we can call it a "fact" that her father is dead. And then there is that "villa"...

Anyway, as noted in my last post, I'm currently of the thought that Severian's family membership has been deliberately obscured through the use of pink herrings; hints at possibilities with no intention of there being one "true" answer which can be divined. So any suggestion of who Severian's family might be (including Dorcas and Ouen) is open for doubt, debate and denial. Any suggestion one could make is easy enough to knock down. Still seems worth pointing out these hints anyway.

In an interview somewhere, Gene Wolfe was asked how long he hoped his work would be read after his death. He was surprised at the question but he was able to answer "100 years" or "300 years" I forget which. Either way, that's pretty ambitious I think, and says to me that we'll get no deathbed revelations solving any of these mysteries. Ah well. Some like eternal mystery. Not me so much, though.
