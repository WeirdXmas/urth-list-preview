2002-02-14 Jason Ingram #

Notes that "The Abyss" can refer to a barrier between the lower and higher Sefiroth around a hidden sefira called Daleth, suggesting this might relate to Yourcenar's novel about a Paracelsus-like figure. Grounds the point in Kabbalah, observing that scholars like Gershom Scholem dismiss the Western magical version of the Abyss that the novel may draw on.
informs · source-and-allusion · theories: The Abyss refers to Kabbalistic barrier around Daleth

Alga,

Those titles sound interesting. I've been researching related topics (which are more pertinent to John Crowley, but still relevant to Wolfe), and fictional accounts should help to sustain interest.

One more tangent:

The Abyss also can refer to a barrier between lower and higher Sefiroth, around a 'secret sefira' called Daleth. Serious Kabbalist scholars such as Gershom Scholem tend to denigrate the vision of the Kabbalah developed within Western magical traditions, the source of that use of "the abyss." However, it might relate to Yourcenar's approach.

Sepia

2002-02-14 Alice Turner #

Mentions borrowing books on Kabbalah and Frances Yates and an upcoming anthology on Crowley, linking to an essay by James Hynes. Responds to a prior poster's remark about Scholem and the Kabbalistic abyss, noting Scholem's standing with Harold Bloom; the post is social and tangential to Wolfe.
chats

From Jason Ingram:

You're right on the money. I borrowed these books from the library (together with Frances Yates's -Theater of the World-) because of the year of researching Crowley I have done with Michael (mantis). But I had read "in" (as Pierce would say) Gershom Scholem's -The Kabbalah-; it is a favorite of Harold Bloom, who is to a degree my guru. Our anthology of essays on Crowley (not just by us but by many others--Bloom introduces it) will come out in April, and had we known you were a devote, we might have pressed you into service to write something. I was turned onto them by a very interesting Crowley essay (which is in our book) by James Hynes, which you can read at http://bostonreview.mit.edu/BR25.6/hynes.html.

-alga
