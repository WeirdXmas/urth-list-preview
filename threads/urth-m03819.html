The Morality of the Hierogrammates

Urth list · 13 messages · 2007-07-23 to 2007-07-25

2007urthThe Book of the New SunJeff WilsonJosh GellerPaul BBruce Hayles
The thread: Paul B. proposed that the flood accompanying the New Sun was an engineered event by the Hierogrammates to reboot humanity rather than a natural consequence of the sun's renewal, doubting that melting ice and water could produce such a flood. Bruce Hayles answered that the text gives a stated physical cause, the New Sun's gravity, as Baldanders explains in Urth of the New Sun. Brunians offered a white hole passing through the solar system as the cause within the setting, and Paul B. argued the flood is a deliberate puzzle Wolfe means readers to solve. Jeff Wilson suggested a small white-hole mass plus released water could suffice, and later speculated on Baldanders' tidal assumptions, while Paul B. increasingly argued the flood is a miracle not tied to the White Fountain. The thread ended without agreement, with the physics debate drifting into speculation about the beasts' energy sources
  • Paul B: flood was an engineered Hierogrammate social reboot, and a deliberate puzzle, later a miracle unconnected to the White Fountain
  • Bruce Hayles: flood has a stated physical cause, the New Sun's gravity, per Baldanders
  • brunians: a white hole passing through the solar system causes the flood and tectonic change; physics accuracy does not matter
  • Jeff Wilson: white fountain could be small with unusual gravity, plus released water; speculated Baldanders' tidal assumptions and beasts' renewable energy

Themes: Gods, angels and the Increate, The dying sun and the cosmos, Deep time and ruins

2007-07-23 Paul B #

Proposes that the flood accompanying the new sun in The Book of the New Sun was not required by the sun's renewal but was a separate engineered event by the Hierogrammates for social reboot of humanity. Grounds this in doubts that melting ice and Urth's water could produce so rapid and vast a flood, and in the aliens' repeated warnings to Severian about the flood.
proposes · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Hierogrammates engineered the flood separately from the new sun; Flood was social engineering to reboot humanity

I recently read the archive discussion about the theodicean aspect of BotNS, and the morality and necessity of the flood that accompanies the new sun. One thing struck me, and if it's old hat, then I've not been thorough enough in reading the archives. That thing is the precipitous pace of the flood, which apparently takes Urth in a matter of days and surprises those at the House Absolute by the time that Severian shows up. A flood of Biblical proportions, if you will.

Now, one thing that has been jokingly asked of that one, can be seriously asked of this: where does the water come from? Though Wolfe seems to be fond of supernatural means having scientific explanations, this one is puzzling if one only ties it to the new sun.

Firstly, if there is enough water to begin with in the form of ice, how does it melt so quickly? A warmer sun would seemingly also have to cook everything alive on the surface to provide enough energy to melt an amount of ice greater than Earth's in a matter of days.
Secondly, assuming Urth has a similar amount of water, solid and liquid, as Earth does, it doesn't seem even possible that there's enough to flood the land to the extent Severian describes.

While saying "the powerful aliens did it" can be seen as something of a cop-out for explaining Urth's mysteries, in this case I can't come up with an endogenous, natural, explanation.
Thus, my hypothesis is that the flood accompanying the new sun wasn't necessary as an attribute of the sun's renewal, at least not on that epic a scale. However, it may have been necessary for the social engineering the Hierogrammates were attempting, the reboot humanity out of its torpor.

That sort of global upheaval is best presented with another, the coming of the new sun, and this may in fact be why they repeatedly inform Severian that there will be a flood, just so he doesn't get surprised and start thinking later. (This would also answer why they'd choose to save the billions of Ragnarok Urth, but not the billions of flooded Urth - things are going as planned on Ushas)

This isn't to say the aliens are malicious, I've read plenty of hypotheses that give reasons why they'd want a flood, possibly for humanity's long-term benefit as well as their own, that I agree with enough to not even repeat. The thing that might be potentially new in what I'm saying is they set up the flood as a totally separate matter from the new sun, but just passed the two off as being codependent.

If this isn't new, let me know if it's covered somewhere so as to save bother. My search didn't turn it up, at any rate.

Paul

2007-07-23 Paul B replying to brunians #

Argues that the white hole's gravitational flood on Urth in The Book of the New Sun is a deliberate puzzle Wolfe means readers to solve rather than dismiss as pseudoscience. Grounds the claim in Wolfe's recurring theme of natural explanations for the supernatural, citing The Devil in a Forest and the Pelerine cathedral, and responds to brunians' remark that the physics does not matter.
disputes · puzzle-solving · genre-and-craft · theories: The white hole flood is a deliberate puzzle to be explained

As for who cares, perhaps Wolfe does.
A theme of his is natural explanations for the supernatural, such as the magic of Devil in the Forest, or the mystery of the Pelerine cathedral in BotNS.
I contend that the feasibility of the flood is a deliberate puzzle. The event that is the culmination of the whole byzantine process is not something to be hand-waved away by scifi pseudoscience in a book this serious. I think it's purposely something that cannot be explained by physics in an endogenous system, but should be explained nonetheless.

P

2007-07-24 Jeff Wilson replying to brunians #

Argues that the White Fountain need not be a star-sized gravity well, since as a white hole it could be far smaller and have unusual gravity, and that the flood could come from a small mass plus released water. Responds to brunians's description of the white hole by using tidal reasoning and noting that dams and reservoirs could supply extra water.
proposes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: White Fountain is a small white hole; flood water from dams and reservoirs

If the White Fountain is indeed a white hole, intended to undo the effect of the "black beans", it need not be on the order of a star in gravity well, only on the order of the beans' total. Also, being a white hole, it may have an inverted or otherwise unusual distribution of gravity.

As for the flood, a mass as small as the moon can cause considerable tides on the earth, so that the White Fountain can be 1% of earth''s mass, maybe smaller, depending on how close it comes.

"Additional" water can come from wrecked dams and reservoirs, from the wreckage of Abaia's tidal power devices, and such. Even today, enough water is kept from the ocean by man-made structures in the northern hemisphere to have a measureable effect on the earth's rotation; in a few chilliads, who knows how much could be waiting to burst loose?

2007-07-24 Paul B replying to Jeff Wilson #

Argues that the flood Baldanders predicts for Urth, which would submerge mountainous Thrax, cannot be explained physically if Urth is Earth, and that the flood is a miracle not tied to the white fountain. Works through Baldanders' account in UotNS chapter LXII and compares it with scientific readings of Biblical flood literature, adding that Abaia's tidal devices are unclear.
disputes · theology-and-religion · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Flood is a miracle unconnected to the white fountain

But the thing is that according to Baldanders everything will get submerged, for example mountainous Thrax will too (the two methods of escape being ship [maybe] and airship - from UotNS, ch LXII). If one makes the assumption that Earth and Urth are the same, there simply doesn't seem to be enough water on Earth to drown the Andes mountains.

If you assume Earth is Urth, then you can actually examine "scientific" Biblical flood literature (of which there's a surprisingly large amount), and there it's worth noting that no one has been able to suggest a scientific explanation for the flood, though not for lack of trying.

If Urth isn't Earth, then I suppose it's not a meaningful question to ask anymore.

Does Abaia really have tidal power devices? I have no idea what that guy is capable of.

For the record, I'm perfectly fine with calling the white fountain a miracle - it, its creation, and movement, are not physically explainable What I'm trying to get at is that the flood is also a "miracle", and the two aren't tied together by anything necessary.

2007-07-25 Jeff Wilson replying to Paul B #

Offers speculative explanations for why Baldanders would expect the New Sun's tides to submerge mountainous Thrax, suggesting he may assume worst-case tidal remaking of land or may be ignorant of geophysics. Proposes that the great beasts like Abaia and Erebus draw on renewable resources, so Abaia opposes the flood and Erebus may use geothermal power, grounded in the poster's old roleplaying discussions with Mantiss.
proposes · close-textual-reading · myth-and-structure · theories: Baldanders assumes worst-case tidal remaking of land; Abaia opposes the flood to protect tidal power; Erebus uses geothermal power from his volcano

Baldanders may be taking the pessmistic, worst-case scenario that the Fountain's tides will be strong enough to remake the land as well as the water. Or, Baldanders may be simply ignorant of the geophysical exactitudes, being primarily interested in researching more showy lost sciences.

They are notional, springing from discussions Mantiss and I had years ago when he was writing a NEW SUN roleplaying game supplement. Presumably each of the great beasts have renewable resources native to their abode that figuratively if not literally feed off of the earth, that would be threatened by the coming of the New Sun. You'd think Abaia would welcome the flood from the New Sun, but there must be some danger to his/its interests for him/it to oppose it. Ergo, tidal power. (though being hung up to rot on the dry land of a newborn continent must be an unpleasant end as well._

Likewise, Erebus presumably has geothermal stations around his eponymous volcano on the Antarctic ice.
