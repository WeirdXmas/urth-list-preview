The Key to the Universe

Urth list · 25 messages · 2010-08-13 to 2010-08-16

2010urthThe Book of the New SunTony EllisRyan DunnLee BermanAntónio Pedro MarquesDavid StockhoffJames WynnJeff WilsonDan'l Danehy-Oakes
The thread: The thread asked whether Yesod in The Urth of the New Sun is hyperspace, a real universe, or a fake built by the Yesodi, with readers quoting Apheta on time and stars. Lee Berman argued Yesod is a real universe with planets and suns, while Jeff Wilson argued those bodies are constructs and that a designer or singularity is needed in cosmology. António Pedro Marques disputed the fake-constructs view and offered hyperspace or ship-screen escapes, and Tony Ellis argued from Apheta's words that Yesod is simply an empty space. The discussion drifted into Intelligent Design and theism, with Ryan Dunn and Marques trading personal beliefs, and ended without consensus, with Ellis's reading of Apheta's order of building the main textual argument left open.
  • Lee Berman: Yesod is a real universe with planets and suns, built by angelic servants of the Increate
  • Jeff Wilson: Yesod's planets and stars are fake constructs, and intelligence cannot arise without a prior cause
  • António Pedro Marques: Yesod holds matter and energy so it is not hyperspace, though it could be ships or screens
  • Tony Ellis: Yesod's time runs differently and Apheta's order of building rules out a Big Bang, so it is an empty space
  • James Wynn: Intelligent Design concerns engineering explanations and a designer's aims are irrelevant to biology
  • Ryan Dunn: Yesod reads as a planet, and he prefers panentheism to pantheism

Themes: The dying sun and the cosmos, Gods, angels and the Increate, Robots, androids and artificial life

2010-08-13 Jeff Wilson replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that the planets and stars in Yesod are fakes, constructs simulating the real ones of Briah for the Yesodis' convenience, and that the Yesodi sun cannot be massive. Grounds this in Apheta's remark about the absence of tides on the machine-planet, and suggests a luminous satellite circling a planet too large to rotate conventionally, responding to Lee Berman's argument that Yesod cannot be hyperspace.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theology-and-religion · theories: Yesodi planets and stars are constructs simulating Briah's; Yesodi sun is not a massive star; Yesod's machine-planet is a luminous satellite arrangement

The problem is that my idea is that any planets or stars in Yesod are fakes, only constructs that simulate the appearance and useful features of the authentic ones in Briah, for the convenience of the Yesodis.

For one thing, Apheta remarks that they have no tides on the machine-planet. She attributes this to their lack of a moon, but it implies that their sun also fails to make tides, preventing it from being a massive star. It may even be that it's a luminous satellite circling the machine-planet, which is probably too large to rotate conventionally.

In that case, why bother with mindless forces at all?

I don't have a problem with the universe ultimately being of intelligent design, but I feel that people saying the intelligence *had* to intervene over time instead of making the universe self-building is presumptious. I also find it more in character for a divinity who is supposed to have a personal relationship with every intelligent being to devote Their interventions to the people rather than the pile of rocks they live on.

2010-08-13 Jeff Wilson replying to James Wynn #

Argues that intelligence cannot simply appear in the universe without a prior cause, so an intelligent designer is a question-begging assumption, and that a nested inflationary cosmology could let Wolfe use a similar model in The Book of the New Sun. Grounds this in the Big Bang evidence, the steady-state model, Andrei Linde's inflationary schemes, and Kabbalah, noting Wolfe fudges physics as he does FTL and giant robots. Responds to James Wynn's remark about Intelligent Design and Dawkins.
proposes · theology-and-religion · genre-and-craft · theories: Wolfe's cosmology resembles nested inflationary universes

That's begging the question, in the original sense. Somehow intelligence had arise for the first time in the universe, unless you have a steady state universe where intelligence has always existed. There is very strong support for the Big Bang, so the steady state model is not convincing locally.

There are some nested -inflationary schemes like Andrei Linde's (and also the Qabbala) where both can be true, as tiny bits of universe undergo local expansion into their own universe-scaled envelope, joined by a tiny bottleneck to the original, but the inflated scale and the bottleneck prevent meaningful access after the moment of sub-creation.

This still allows Wolfe to use a similar model in the BOTNS, since he has license to fudge the bottleneck into a black hole/white hole-sized opening, just like he fudges FTL and antigravity, giants and giant robots.

2010-08-14 James Wynn replying to Jeff Wilson #

Argues that if a planet's life was intelligently designed and never produced new life naturally, studying that life would reveal nothing about natural abiogenesis or the conditions it needs. Builds the case as a hypothetical thought experiment, granting the design premise and applying it to Earth's descent from a single origin, in reply to Jeff Wilson's point about physical laws.
proposes · genre-and-craft

Well, lets assume that life on a planet was intelligently designed...that it was an artifice. And lets assume that after that moment and for 3 billion years following, although the life changed superficially over time, no new life developed there on its own--that all the life found there was a descendant of the originally ID life (this part is actually applicable to our planet). It would be reasonable to presume that studying the ID life would tell us nothing about life (intelligent or otherwise) that occurred by purely natural means. In fact, the planet would tell us nothing about the environment necessary for life to occur naturally.
