mystery of the guild

Urth list · 5 messages · 2009-02-11 to 2009-02-12

2009urthThe Book of the New SunDaniel D JonesDavid StockhoffJohn WatkinsDavid Duffy
The thread: Posters debated whether the Torturers' Guild could be a fragment of a monastic or Catholic-style religious order. David Stockhoff doubted that the vast Roman Catholic Church could shrink into the tiny Guild, but suggested a monastic fragment, comparing the Keep to a Gothic cathedral and the Autarch to the Pope. He also argued that the Hieros-guild's detachment comes from their masks, gently pushing back on Tim O'Donnell's reading of them as emotional. Daniel D Jones countered that any monastic order could later turn harsh, citing the Inquisition as proof that benevolent religious bodies can act cruelly. The thread ends without resolution, with the parties stating opposing views on whether the Guild's origins are benevolent.
  • David Stockhoff: Guild may be a monastic fragment, Autarch parallels the Pope, Hieros-guild seem detached because of masks
  • Daniel D Jones: Torturers could descend from any monastic order, since benevolent religious bodies can turn harsh as the Inquisition shows
  • Tim O'Donnell: Hieros-guild read as emotional, cited Famulimus's joyful greeting to Severian

Themes: Catholic faith and sacraments, Gods, angels and the Increate, Justice, torture and mercy

2009-02-11 David Stockhoff #

Doubts that the vast Roman Catholic Church could become the tiny Guild, but suggests the Guild may be a fragment of a monastic order. Responds to Tim O'Donnell's archived theories by comparing the Keep to a Gothic cathedral and proposing that the Autarch parallels the Pope, holding spiritual and temporal authority.
disputes · theology-and-religion · allegory-and-typology · theories: The Guild is a fragment of the Catholic Church; The Autarch parallels the Pope; Christ and Pope as one spiritual and temporal authority

I am skeptical that something as vast as the RCC could ever "become" something as tiny and obscure as the Guild, but there are certainly correspondences and connections. The Guild could be a fragment of the Church, perhaps descended from some actual monastic order. But which one? They all seem pretty benevolent to me.

One correspondence is that the Keep, as I imagine it, resembles both a Gothic cathedral and a 1950's pulp-fiction finned rocket ship. Both aspire and thrust up to the heavens. Both are nostalgic and hopeful at the same time.

If Wolfe does think of Christ as an applier of pain, then the irony of Severian's connection may not be as central as I thought.

I wonder if any Catholic writer might be prone to speculation on what it might be like if Christ and Pope were the same person, holding spiritual and temporal authority. That is, if the Autarch parallels the Pope, then ... you see where I'm going here.
