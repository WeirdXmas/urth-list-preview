The thread: The thread asks who the two figures are who together form the Old Sun in Wolfe's Long and New Sun books, and whether Typhon, Baldanders, Abaia, Pas or Silk fit. Early posters propose Typhon with Baldanders or Abaia, then argue whether the same-person link is literal or thematic, with Don Palumbo reading Typhon and Baldanders through Campbell's Ogre-Tyrant. James Wynn and James Jordan dispute whether a memory or body transfer like Thecla's preserves identity, while Don Doggett cites textual soul transfers. Andrew Bollen ends by arguing that chems can have souls. The discussion drifts from the riddle into soul and identity, with no settled answer on the Old Sun.
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: the Old Sun is a doubled pair, possibly Typhon and Pas, though the same-person link is weaker than elsewhere
  • James Wynn: Typhon and perhaps Silk form the Old Sun, and memory transfer copies the person without preserving the soul
  • Don Palumbo: Typhon and Baldanders are the same thematically as the Campbellian Ogre-Tyrant
  • James Jordan: transferring consciousness to another body does not preserve the self, a kind of sleight-of-hand like reincarnation
  • Don Doggett: soul transfer happens in the books, citing the Alzabo's meal and Severian eating Appian's forebrain
  • Andrew Bollen: souls as divine Grace take root wherever they can, so chems can have souls

Themes: Doubles, twins and clones, Gods, angels and the Increate, Memory and forgetting

Argues that Typhon, the megalomaniac ruler of the Long Sun books, would never willingly submit to destruction and replacement by a copy the way Mr. Million's Original did, unlike a simulation. Supports this by contrasting Typhon with Mr. Million and Aunt Jeanine, and by asking whether readers would accept surgery where memories pass to a copy, as Thecla did with Severian.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Typhon would not accept being replaced by a simulation; simulated consciousness differs from human consciousness

Civet asked:

It was in response to Josh telling him that the current discussion (a year ago) was whether Hyacinth was a male chem. Since I suggested that theory, I remember this well. Although I no longer hold that theory, I found Wolfe's answer enigmatic - I still do. Josh does not.

As for Typhon's willingness to submit to the same procedure as Mr. Million's Original (Mr. M.O.) there are some big differences between Typhon and Mr M.O.
Mr. Million was considered by Aunt Jeanine AND Mr. Million to be *only* a simulation. She did not at first even consider him to be a candidate for one of "the five". Only after consideration did she say "maybe he was counting the simulation". Mr. M.O. was a mad scientist. Typhon was an meglo-maniac.

Typhon was perfectly fine with a simulation of himself ruling where he could not be physically present. He even had a sense of humor about it. But the idea that he would willingly have himself destroyed so an "equivalent" could take his place seems inconceivable to me.

2003-09-07 Chris P #

Argues that Baldanders is a poor symbol for the Old Sun and that Typhon is not its embodiment either, since suns are angelic like Tzadkiel and Severian. Responds to Blattid's claim that Typhon and Baldanders are the same person, and asks the list whether Wolfe had a specific reason for thinking it good he never read the list.
disputes · theology-and-religion · allegory-and-typology · theories: Typhon is the Old Sun; Baldanders is a failed future potential

Baldanders was a potential future that didn't pan out, quite different from Typhon. The Typhon identification aside, I think this doesn't make him a very good symbol for the Old Sun.

You could say that Typhon is the Old Sun, but ultimately I don't think this is the case either; stars/suns are angelic, like Tzadkiel and Severian. If you're looking for a character that embodies the Old Sun then you need to look for a Vishnu to Severian's Shiva, and I am not so sure Typhon qualifies. But I may be making absolutely no sense at all here.

A side note since it doesn't seem worth a whole second post: Wolfe's comment to Josh Geller does make a lot of sense, but it could make sense in a few different ways. Do you have any more specific idea why Wolfe thought it was probably a good thing that he didn't read this list? As I said, it makes good sense regardless, but I am curious as to whether there was a more specific context.

-- Civet

2003-09-07 Don Palumbo #

Argues that Typhon and Baldanders are the same person thematically, as both embody the Ogre-Tyrant of the status quo that the hero must overthrow. Grounds this in Campbell's monomyth from The Hero with a Thousand Faces, presenting The Book of the New Sun and Urth of the New Sun as Wolfe's treatment of that pattern, and responds to James Wynn's question about whether Typhon and Baldanders are the same.
disputes · myth-and-structure · theories: Typhon and Baldanders are thematically the same person

Just dipping in for a moment, but Typhon and Baldanders are the same person thematically,<br>
as Crush suggests, in that they are both the two clearest representatives of the Ogre-Tyrant of the status quo that it is the task of the hero as warrior to overthrow.  This comes from Campbell's THE HERO WITH A THOUSAND FACES, of course, and The Book of the New Sun overall (and echoed in <i>Urth of the New Sun</i>) is clearly Wolfe's treatment of Campbell's monomyth, the adventure of the hero, just as the Book of the Log Sun in itself is his treatment of the generational star ship story.<br>

There's nothing of what makes me ME left. The difference between reincarnation and personal annihiliation is merely a sleight-of-hand.
