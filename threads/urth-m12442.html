2010-12-27 Lee Berman #

Proposes that Severian, as Apu Punchau, may have brought the black hole or Dark Star that eats Urth's sun, and that the eclipse marking his time could mark its arrival, bookending his White Fountain. Grounds this in the fuligin imagery tied to Severian, the Dark Star nickname for the Conciliator, and the pairing of white fountain with black hole, while disputing the common view that it arrived at the end of Typhon's rule.
proposes · close-textual-reading · myth-and-structure · theories: Severian brought the black hole as Apu Punchau; The eclipse marked the arrival of the Dark Star

Hm. I already mentioned the ruins beneath Blue's Ocean. And I have long thought the "Accepted Lupine Gospel" was on the wrong track with regard to the eclipse ("Tzadkiel's Ship caused it"). What is the direction of your thoughts, Marc?

Lately I have been considering the nearly endlessly repeated fuligin imagery we get associated with Severian and remembering that one of the nicknames for the Conciliator/New Sun which confuses Severian is: Dark Star.

We hear so much about Severian's association with the White Fountain in UotNS we may be a bit distracted by the fact that white fountain is the paired opposite of black hole. Considered in this (er..)light, it seems almost ridiculous for anyone else but Severian to have brought the black hole to eat at Urth's sun.

When did he bring it? I think the concensus is that it was brought at the end of Typhon's rule. It may be that is what Wolfe intended to convey. But I wonder if there is another possibility.

As Apu Punchau, Severian can be said to have brought civilization to humanity, with the introduction of modern, durable architecture and effective warfare strategies. Could this somehow be marked by the eclipse and perhaps the arrival of the black hole/Dark Star?

It nicely bookends with the coming of the White Fountain, also brought by Severian, marking the end of civilization on Urth. The Ushas that we see is awfully low tech, (but quite spiritual).

Just some not-very-organized thoughts to throw out there.

2010-12-27 James Wynn replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that the Green-is-Urth theory in Marc's planetary-problems thread is undercut by setting details that clash with Urth, such as Green's physical conditions, and that its gains do not offset its unexplained puzzles. Responds to Lee Berman's question about Marc's direction, citing Marc's shift from Blue to Green and the eclipse theory, and uses the Rajan-on-a-hillbilly-farm analogy to contrast this with other interpretive cases.
disputes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Green is Urth; Blue is Ushas; Blue or Green is Severian's planet

This is an aspect of Marc's evolved theory that people sometimes miss. There is a lot about Blue that might recommend it to a long ago flooded world. That's the way Marc originally saw it. But after Wolfe's comment to him he began focusing on elements of the text that seem to connect Urth and Green.

The fact that Marc's theory has dramatically changed direction doesn't undercut it in my mind. A broad theory has lots of moving parts, and it is not uncommon for someone to correctly note that a surreptitious crime has been committed, but then finger the wrong suspects. Happens all the time. Some avoid that mistake by only noting the evidence of the crime's /commission/ such as Ryan Dunn did with Inire & Fetchin & Co's simian traits. But ultimately--to have any hope of making an arrest-- you have to start grilling suspects and you run the risk of people asking questions OF YOU that you can't answer--an investigator prefers to be the one asking the questions. The people who say "nothing's there" feel free to offer the flimsiest excuses for some really bizarre passages (or to just ignore them) while expecting the Theorist to explain the most arcane, vaguely implied technicalities about his explanation.

I'm not saying Roy has done that with Marc's Theory. Although, arguably, it hasn't been necessary. You see, Marc's theory has some real posers--I mean, //regarding setting//, not exposition, //not what people say//. Deciding that Green=Urth (or, to a lesser extent, Blue=Ushas) is no less strange to my mind than if the Rajan had come to Urth and found young Severian growing turnips on a farm speaking like a hillbilly. And this weird discrepancy is --at this time, Roy and others not withstanding-- the greatest refutation of the Green Urth theory. (I mean "refutation" as "an argument against" not as "proof that is untrue".)

I'm troubled by some statements and events that imply Urth is closer than anyone imagines. I'm troubled much more by the claim that Blue or Green is Severian's planet because the conspiracy is sooo big that I wonder that it could be kept hidden. Some people probably think the same thing when I say Horn was possessed by a Neighbor or Hyacinth is Fava and the Rajan is Hy's father. But I consider physical changes between Urth and Green truly daunting and categorically different. In each of those other cases, I argue, one only needs to re-interpret the scenes and dialog as they are presented. Green Urth means explaining massive events of which we were hitherto unaware.

I don't like it, but I think tha's where we are. If I sound like a devout believer who has lost his faith, I'm not. I'm someone who was always skeptical of this theory but _did_ expect it to yield some fruit. But I got to the end and discovered, unhappily, I had the same questions I started with. It explains why the dream travelers ended up on Urth. But it raises up massive really scary puzzles that are left unanswered.

2010-12-27 Thomas Bitterman replying to James Wynn #

Proposes two theories for why the dream travelers end up on Urth: Urth and Green are thematically close in the author's mental map, and the books sit near each other on shelves. Responds to James Wynn by using Krait's travel into another story as a model for Silk reaching a temporally nearby book.
proposes · genre-and-craft · theories: Urth and Green thematically close, so travel lands nearby; Bookshelf proximity explains why travelers reach Urth; Krait's story-hopping models Silk's travel to other books

Two theories on why they ended up on Urth during astral/dream travel: - Urth and Green are thematically similar. The characters are in a dreamlike state so distance is cognitive distance, not physical distance. Most times when they travel they end up physically close because places that are physically close tend to be closely associated mentally. This trip they are going to someplace close as measured by the author's (and maybe reader's) mental map.
- They are close on bookshelves. If Krait can travel into another character's story, why can't this character travel to a different book? Krait travels to a temporally close story, Silk/Whomever travels to a somewhat more temporally-distant one. As a bonus, given the way books are generally shelved, Long/Short sun and Urth are generally physically close together.
