Clute's Revelation

Whorl list · 3 messages · 2000-08-16 to 2000-08-16

2000whorlRobert BorskiKevin J. MaroneyFernando Q. Gouvea
The thread: Readers disputed John Clute's review of Wolfe's Long Sun and Silk, which argued that Horn is really an inhumu and that Silk's death was a suicide. Borski and Gouvea both rejected the Horn claim, citing Horn's handwriting, riding, weapon use, eating, and Oreb's fondness for him, and Gouvea said Clute would need a misleading narrator. Maroney was unconvinced by the suicide reading and found the Catholic teaching on suicide and burial still felt wrong. The thread ends with these rebuttals and no reply from Clute in the posts.
  • Robert Borski: Horn is not an inhumu; the Oreb angle, handwriting, and Neighbors' behavior don't support it, and the only precedent is the Fifth Head of Cerberu
  • Fernando Q. Gouvea: Horn writes, rides, eats, uses weapons, and is liked by Oreb, which inhumi don't do, so Clute would need an unreliable narrator
  • Kevin J. Maroney: Unconvinced that Silk's death was a suicide, though Clute may have noticed things others missed

Themes: Beasts and the animal in us, Unreliable narration, Catholic faith and sacraments

2000-08-16 Robert Borski #

Disputes John Clute's claim that Horn is really an inhumu, arguing that the Oreb angle, Horn's handwriting, the Neighbors' behavior, and the unclear timing of any takeover make the idea unconvincing. Responds to Clute's review by citing the Fifth Head of Cerberus Trenchard substitution as the only identifiable precedent and by questioning who would subsume Horn's personality.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Horn is an inhumu, hornjacked by another

David Lomax has already mentioned the Oreb angle, and it seems hard to believe that Incanto-the-inhumu could fool the wise bird whereas Quetzal, the Bean and Jahlee couldn't.

Yet another contraindication is Horn's handwriting. The inhumi have at best terrible writing and Horn/Incanto has handwritten two books, at least the first of which (and no doubt the second and third) has been read by Horn's wife and children with no editorial comment about Horn's rather execrable penmanship. Ditto for Inclito's mother, who receives handwritten instructions on how she can remedy her recent ill turn in health ("Er, what are these words, Incanto? 'Necklace of garlic'?"). And isn't it likely that if Horn were able to manipulate his physical appearance, he'd at least try to appear fully sighted or without the wound in his side? (Not sure how shapeshifting works if you have scars or wounds.) Or that the Neighbors would aid and abet a former vampiric nemesis?

And lastly while there is a precedent in Wolfean fiction--Victor Trenchard killing and taking the place of John Marsch in FIFTH HEAD--at least it is identifiable when it happens, something I'm not sure Clute's hornjacking is. (Who exactly is subsuming Horn's personality? Krait? Some other inhumu? And when/where does it occur?)

In other words list me among the unconvinced (as I am about Silk's suicide, since Catholics that die by their own hand are forbidden not only burial in Catholic cemetaries, but Heaven), although I do admit I'm willing to consider further evidence.

Robert Borski

2000-08-16 Fernando Q. Gouvea #

Argues against John Clute's suggestion that Horn in The Book of the Long Sun may be an inhumu or aquastor, asking why Wolfe would repeat effects from The Fifth Head of Cerberus and The Book of the New Sun. Grounds the rebuttal in evidence that Horn writes, rides, uses weapons, eats, and is liked by Oreb, which inhumi do not do, and notes Clute would need a misleading narrator.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Horn is an inhumu; Horn is an aquastor; Horn's narrator is misleading

Nah... Even Clute hedges his bets, by saying maybe he's an inhumu (spells it wrong, as "inhuma"... or does he mean that?), maybe he's an aquastor, maybe something else. My main reaction is why should Wolfe repeat his effects? He's already written "Fifth Head" and The Book of the New Sun.

I agree completely with alga that having Horn become a better human being seems to be central to this novel. One might even imagine an Inferno/Purgatorio/Paradiso triptych in Severian/Silk/Horn.

Finally, the evidence is all against it. Horn is shown writing, riding horses, using weapons (even the azoth, at one point), all sorts of stuff that inhumi don't do. Oreb likes him. He eats (not much, but he does). To sustain Clute's idea one would have to assume not only an unreliable narrator, but a positively misleading one.

Fernando
