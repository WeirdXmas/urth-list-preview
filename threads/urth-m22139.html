The thread: Nathan C. Tresch asked whether the Whorl in The Book of the Long Sun is the Universal Transport of the Solar Cycle and pointed to an outside Stack Exchange question. Gerry Quinn and António Pedro Marques disputed this, citing the Whorl's asteroid shape, lack of sails, differing interiors and limited lifespan. Tresch repeatedly argued the Whorl is an early or converted stage of the Ship, and Quinn partly agreed, linking the story Silhouette's ship to it. Sergei Soloviev rejected the identification on textual and symbolic grounds and later called the Silhouette ship an early sketch of later Wolfe ships. Gerry Quinn countered that the Ship is not the only interstellar craft, and Lee Berman judged the link possible but probably unintended, leaving the thread without consensus.
  • Nathan C. Tresch: the Whorl is an early, converted or repaired stage of the Ship, and Silhouette's ship is part of the same vehicle's development
  • Gerry Quinn: the Whorl differs too much from the Ship, though Silhouette's silhouette parallels the Long Sun material
  • Sergei Soloviev: the Whorl is not the Universal Transport; Silhouette's ship is an early sketch of later Wolfe ships
  • António Pedro Marques: the Whorl is earthly technology while the Ship is not, going by asteroid shape, no sails and interiors
  • Lee Berman: a precursor link is possible in principle but Wolfe probably did not intend it, given few shared features and no clues in the text

Themes: Robots, androids and artificial life, The dying sun and the cosmos, Stories within stories

2012-04-15 Nathan C. Tresch replying to Sergei SOLOVIEV #

Argues that the spaceship in The Book of the New Sun and the Whorl are one vehicle that transformed over time, from a lowly Earth vessel into a self-sacrificing higher being, and suggests the ship in Silhouette may be a stage of that development. Responds to Sergei Soloviev's objection that the ship is angelic and unlike a sailed craft, citing other references to The Ship and to people meeting earlier versions of themselves aboard.
proposes · close-textual-reading · myth-and-structure · theories: The Whorl transforms into The Ship over time; Silhouette's ship is a stage of the Whorl's development

Noted.

I understand that at the time Severian was aboard the ship it had evolved to what it was. That doesn't imply that it couldn't have once come from humble beginnings, as a lowly earth vessel, as Severian himself was a lowly torturer.

There are a great many other references to The Ship being one vehicle, I really didn't think that this was something I'd have to document. People were even meeting earlier versions of themselves as they came aboard what they thought was another ship from their own memory, if I recall correctly. If this is really something I need to support I'll paste more quotes.

Yeah, I think that the ship in Silhouette could also be a stage of development of the Whorl->The Ship.

2012-04-15 Gerry Quinn replying to Sergei SOLOVIEV #

Agrees with Sergei SOLOVIEV that the Whorl evolves from the ship in "Silhouette" and adds further parallels between the two works. Cites their shared human construction, mission-creep, Sleepers who feel they boarded yesterday, and the Captain's enhanced height as a foreshadowing of exultants.
supports · close-textual-reading · theories: The Whorl evolves from the ship in Silhouette

From: Sergei SOLOVIEV

I would rather place the Whorl as an evolution of the ship in Silhouette. Both are human-constructed, and both have been subject to mission-creep (although in Silhouette the crew have only been aboard about twenty years ship time). In both cases there are Sleepers who, when woken, feel they have come on board yesterday.

There are other echoes of future work: the enhanced height of the Captain is reminiscent of exultants, for example.

- Gerry Quinn

2012-04-15 Sergei SOLOVIEV replying to Nathan C. Tresch #

Argues that the Whorl is not the Universal Transport, since Tsadkiel's sailing ship is a messenger of a higher world and Typhon's ship embodies a human ruler's Luciferian pride. Answers Nathan C. Tresch's question by citing the ship's description, the sailors' legend, doubts about Purn as a source sent by Severian's enemy, and the Antichrist opposition it would erase.
disputes · close-textual-reading · allegory-and-typology · theories: The Whorl is not the Universal Transport; Tsadkiel's ship is a sailors' legend; Typhon's ship opposes Tsadkiel's messenger ship; Tsadkiel's ship develops the ship in Silhouette

IMHO, the answer is "no" -

my reasons

- it seems that there is on this list a (culturally determined) drive to identify almost everything with almost everything
else, I do not share it;

- the description - a beautiful spaceship with sails - in case of Severian's ship - is very different, the captain is Tsadkiel, a king of angelic creature, the ship is a sort of messenger of a higher world;

- it may be unique, but the story seems to be a sort of persistent legend among the sailors, it is presented as such;

- by the way, why should we believe Purn? As far as I remember, she was sent by one of the monsters who are
enemies of Severian and tried to kill him;

- last, but not least, the attempt to identify the Whorl with Tsadkiel's ship will destroy one of important dramatic
oppositions in the Solar cycle, where Typhon (Pas) who built it, is a kind of Luciferian or Antichrist figure - it
will destroy the opposition between the ship created by a human ruler who wants to be god, and messengers
of higher universes.

One extra remark - to me, from the point of view of literary evolution of Gene Wolfe, the
Tsadkiel ship seems a possible development of the idea of the ship in "Silhouette".

Regards

Sergei Soloviev

Argues that the Whorl could in principle be a precursor to Tzadkiel's Ship but that Wolfe probably did not intend it as one, since the two ships share little structure or function. Grounds this in the absence of clues in the text, especially at the end of Return to the Whorl, responding to Gerry Quinn's list of interstellar craft in the books.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: The Whorl is a precursor to Tzadkiel's Ship

I think there are two different questions being answered here. If the question is whether the Whorl could be a precursor to Tzadkiel's Ship I suppose the answer is yes.

If the question is whether Wolfe intended The Whorl to be a precursor to Tzadkiel's Ship I think the answer is probably not. As has been noted, there is not much structural or funtional similarity between these ships. Moreover,if Wolfe had intended to show a progression I would think he would have put some sort of clues in the story to that effect, especially at the end of RTTW. I don't see any.
