1999-02-24 Kevin J. Maroney #

Proposes that the word "Alzabo" is a Romanized form of the Arabic word for hyena, and that Wolfe introduced the creature as a replacement for the hyena because both laugh. Grounds this in the premise that Urth's animals are alien replacements for extinct Earth species, illustrated by the destriers, which are alien horse-like beings.
answers · source-and-allusion · theories: Alzabo is a replacement for the hyena

It's a Romanized version of the Arabic word for "hyena", if I'm not mistaken. The Alzabo was introduced as a replacement for the hyena because, like the hyena, it laughs.

(Remember that most/all of the animal species of Urth except humans were driven extinct at some point in the past, and the animals present in the Book are alien replacements which approximate the original species. Destriers aren't horses, they're alien horse-like beings. This is an inversion of the standard sf cliche where an alien world is populated by exotic creatures which are actually just cows and bunnies with different names; here the Earth is populated by alien species which are *like* cows and bunnies.)

1999-02-24 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Explains that the word "alzabo" is a creature from medieval European bestiaries, linked to legends of the hyena, and that it is not an alchemical term. Argues that Wolfe did not invent it or most of the odd words in the New Sun books, citing White's Book of Beasts and Borges's Book of Imaginary Beings as sources.
informs · source-and-allusion

Y,

Hello and welcome.

"Alzabo" is a fabulous creature from medieval bestiaries of Europe, based in part (it is thought) upon legends surrounding the hyena (a non-European creature). See: BOOK OF BEASTS translated by White, and/or THE BOOK OF IMAGINARY BEINGS by Borges.

"Alzabo" is not itself an alchemical term, to the best of my knowledge. And no, Gene Wolfe did not make it up--nor did he invent 99% of the odd words used in TBOTNS (you can trust me: I wrote a book on the subject).

=mantis=
