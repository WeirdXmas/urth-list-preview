2001-04-27 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that readers are not too sophisticated to be offended by religious bigotry, whether it favors or disfavors their own beliefs, and cites This Present Darkness and the Pullmans. Responds to William Ansley's claim that list members are too sophisticated to be offended, mocking his praise of the armored bears and noting that cosmopolitanism is a flaw in a writer.
disputes

Au contraire: I am in fact too sophisticated _not_ to be offended by religious bigotry, whether it favors or disfavors mine own belief-system. I find, e.g., books like "This Present Darkness" quite as intolerable as, I suspect, I should find the Pullmans.

On the other hand, you also wrote (in another message):

And I have to admit, I'm curious about any British writer cosmpolitan enough to write about the typically American concern with our right to keep and arm bears. Even the
best British writers tend to be a bit provincial. (Of course, this is true of American writers, too. And French writers. And... Come to think of it, excessive cosmopolitanism is almost always a flaw in a writer...)

--Dan'l
