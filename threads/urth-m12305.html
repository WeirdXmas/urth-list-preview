Proposes that the big fish in Scylla's lake which eats Mamelta is possessed by Scylla, a Whorl god, and is not a Christ symbol. Grounds this on the text's statement that Whorl gods possessed animals and on Mamelta's possible link to Kypris, replying to Lee Berman's earlier reading.

2010-12-21 Lee Berman #

Argues that the fish that eats Mamelta in the Long Sun books is a literal creature possessed by Scylla, not a symbol, since the Whorl gods were said to possess animals. Supports this by pointing to Scylla's aquatic nature and the parallel with Scylla and Echidna hunting Daddy's Mistress, and responds to Marc Aramini's symbolic reading.
disputes · close-textual-reading · myth-and-structure · theories: Scylla's possessed fish eats Mamelta literally; Mamelta is associated with Kypris

Perhaps I am being too simplistic and naive but the answer that lies between the superficial text and deeper symbolism seems too easy a puzzle, especially given your theory, Marc.

We are told that the Whorl gods attempted to preserve themselves by possessing animals. The big nasty fish is in Scylla's lake (and doesn't at all seem like any simple Christ pun/symbol). Scylla is, after all, essentially an aquatic monster.

If Mamelta is in any way associated with Kypris, it seems no mystery at all why a monstrous fish possessed by Scylla would want to chase her down and eat her. It is just the same as Scylla and Echidna in mainframe hunting down Daddy's Mistress, but in the real world...erm... whorl.
