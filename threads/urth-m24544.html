The thread: Participants debated the setting of The Devil in a Forest. Marc Aramini argued the book is anachronistic and set in Bohemia, linking Good King Wenceslas and St. Agnes's fountain to real saints and suggesting the blinded abbe echoes Agnes of Rome. Sergei Soloviev countered that the setting is medieval England, pointing to the name Wat and its echo of Wat Tyler's revolt, and conceded a re-read was needed. Mo Holkar argued for a non-specific mixed Europe, noting the English and Czech names and the French 'abbe', and said the carol was written by a nineteenth-century Englishman, so Wolfe was riffing on St Agnes's Fountain. The thread ended in disagreement with no shared resolution.

actually Wenceslaus I of Bohemia (who is NOT Good King Wenceslaus).? The Bohemian St. Agnes lived 300 years after the man who was inquiring about the peasant living in the shadow of her fountain.

Yes, Wat is a version of the common medieval English name (Germanic origin) Walter.

(I have always felt the setting was a kind of non-specific mixed Europe, though, as it mingles elements from all over the place. In addition to these English and Czech references, the term 'abbe' is afaik specifically French, for example.)

It should perhaps be noted that although the carol Good King Wenceslas has a Bohemian setting, it was written by a C19 Englishman for English consumption. It's easy to imagine Wolfe being tickled by the anachronism of its reference to St Agnes's Fountain (either ignorance or poetic licence), and deciding to riff on the earlier St Agnes instead -- good spot, Marc!
