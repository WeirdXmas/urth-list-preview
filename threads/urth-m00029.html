More Mieville

Urth list · 6 messages · 2004-12-02 to 2004-12-03

2004urthAlice TurnerBrian LindenmuthChris PDan'l Danehy-Oakes
The thread: Readers debated China Mieville's novels, starting from Alice K. Turner's claim that Mieville is a hack and her praise of The Scar over Perdido Street Station. Alice retracted the hack label but still found the ending of The Scar weak, while Dan'l Danehy-Oakes and Brian Lindenmuth argued Perdido Street Station's grotesque shock wears thin and its prose is messy and overlong. Alice conceded that readers who started with different books had different reactions, and Chris pushed back on the messiness charge by arguing Mieville's originality lies in his synthesis of borrowed ideas. The thread ended without agreement, with the exchange mostly drifting into general critical taste about a non-Wolfe author.
  • Alice K. Turner: Mieville is sincere, The Scar beats Perdido Street Station but falls apart at the end
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: The Scar is weaker because Perdido's grotesque shock is gone for returning readers
  • Brian Lindenmuth: Mieville is messy and unpolished, Perdido Street Station is a crowded kitchen-sink novel
  • Chris: Mieville's creativity lies in his synthesis of borrowed ideas, not in quantity of ideas

Themes: Genre: science fiction, fantasy, both, Books, libraries and writing

2004-12-02 Alice Turner #

Retracts calling Mieville a hack, arguing he sincerely believes in his work but loves pulp horror mostly drawn from movies, and says The Scar beats Perdido Street Station yet falls apart at the end. Replies to Alice K. Turner's recommendations, citing Perdido Street Station's repetition and clunky prose as poor editing, and calling Iron Council hopeless after three chapters.
disputes · source-and-allusion · genre-and-craft · theories: Mieville's love of pulp horror comes from movies

Yes, The Scar is a lot better than PSS (though it completely falls apart at the end), and IC is hopeless (I think, though I've only ventured into it for three chapters). I plead guilty to using the term "hack," but I'll retract that. He's not exactly a hack, since that implies churning the stuff out mindlessly. Mieville, I think really believes in what he is doing, but unfortunately (from my point of view) what he loves is pulp horror, mainly taken from movies. You can tell that from the excerpted quotes that intrigued some of you. He has also been poorly edited, especially in PSS, which is nothing if not repetitive, with terribly clunky prose.

-alga

2004-12-03 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes replying to Alice K. Turner #

Argues that China Mieville's The Scar is a weaker follow-up to Perdido Street Station because its grotesque style wears thin, so the first book's shock is missing. Reflects that readers who know the prior discussion will lack that shock whichever Mieville book they start with. Responds to Alice K. Turner's comparison of The Scar, Iron Council and Mieville's pulp horror roots; no Wolfe content.
chats

A -

I think you're probably right; if I had read _The Scar_ first I might well have liked it better than _PSS_. Alas, the sheer grotesquerie which is CM's primary stock in trade is really only good for about one book, so I found _The Scar_ something of a slog, as it didn't have the "Wow!" factor of the first encounter with CM.

Of course, for anyone who's read all this discussion, there will probably be almost no "Wow!" factor no matter _which_ book they begin with, because an expectation will have been built which won't be lived up to...

2004-12-03 Brian Lindenmuth replying to Alice K. Turner #

Gives a personal reading of China Mieville's work, saying he is not a hack but a messy, unpolished writer, using Perdido Street Station as the example of a crowded, overlong "kitchen sink" novel. Responds to Alice K. Turner's recommendation of The Scar and his warning against Iron Council, noting he has not yet finished The Scar and has not started Iron Council.
disputes

I am behind on my Mieville. I have read PSS, started The Scar but not finished it yet (Edward Whittemore has been distracting me) and I bought IC but its not even on my radar screen yet in terms of reading it. I done think he is a hack, but her is certainly a messy and unpolished writer. Maybe he would benefit from better editing. PSS was good though, if messy, a bit slow at times and too long. It was what I like to call a kitchen sink novel. He was bursting with images and ideas and wanted to get them all out without a certain amount of control or cohesiveness. Again, not the best writer in the world, but definitely a good imagination and worth watching.

2004-12-03 Chris P replying to Brian Lindenmuth #

Argues that China Mieville's creativity lies in his particular synthesis of borrowed ideas rather than in the sheer number of ideas and images he draws on. Responds to Brian Lindenmuth's remarks that Mieville is messy and unpolished, noting Mieville himself admits to borrowing widely, so the post is a general critical exchange about another writer rather than Wolfe.
disputes

I don't deny that Mieville is creative, but I don't think the wealth of ideas and images you're talking about is a sign of it... Mieville, by his own admission, borrows a lot of ideas from all over the place. The particular synthesis is unique to him and I would agree is rather creative.
