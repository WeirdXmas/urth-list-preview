2001-02-08 Peter Westlake #

Argues that the editors of Return to the Whorl keep the protagonist's identity ambiguous because it makes for a better dramatised narrative than a dry history would. Responds to Adam's objection about Hide and Hoof's narration and the later naming of Silk, granting a little literary licence provided it causes no real confusion.

Well, they *are* writing a book, and you must admit it reads better this way. It's a dramatised account, not a dry history, so I think they did it for the same reason as Wolfe did. Considering the probably influence of the Book of the Long Sun on their literary style, I'm grateful that the Book of the Short Sun is as clear and explicit as it is!

You said it yourself: "it's clear". We can allow them a little literary licence, as long as it doesn't cause actual confusion.

2001-02-10 Adam Stephanides #

Argues that the editors of the Return to the Whorl compilation would not have written with the literary games of narration that Wolfe uses, since they are non-literary people compiling a nonfiction account. Grounds this in the editors' humble, impoverished backgrounds and the factual purpose of their work, and asks why they chose their narrative method.
disputes · author-and-biography · genre-and-craft

In the first place, three of the editors are from an impoverished colony, and at least two are from a family that's not well off; the fourth is a servant. Under these conditions it's unlikely that any of them would have acquired much literary sophistication. And I don't recall any indication that any of them had a literary bent either. So it seems unlikely to me that they would have been interested in the sort of games with narration that Wolfe plays. In the second place, they're not writing a novel, they're compiling a non-fictional account of their father (or father-in-law) and of the man he admired more than anybody else. Even if they had an interest in narrative mystification, I doubt they would indulge it here.

I'm willing to "allow them" all the license they want. I just want to know why they did it.

(Incidentally, I had posted a second reply to Dan Schmidt on the authorship of the third-person segments a couple days ago, but without my realizing it my computer had reset the date to 1956, so it may have gotten lost on some systems.)

--Adam
