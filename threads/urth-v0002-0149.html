Themes: Christ figures and messiahs

1997-07-10 Alice Turner #

Argues that the unseen baby in Rosemary's Baby, like the child in Wolfe's story "Hues," could be either a demon or the Antichrist, and that Wolfe's story is blurrily apocalyptic rather than specifically religious. Responds to Ron's point that the term has been blurred, noting that "Hues" is not an overtly religious story like "The Detective of Dreams" and that the two readers agree on the general picture.
supports · allegory-and-typology · theories: The child in "Hues" could be demon or Antichrist

Ron, in Rosemary's Baby (which you should try to rent--it's a classic of its kind), the baby, which of course we don't actually see being born, could be either a demon or the antichrist, just as in "Hues." (note Rosemary vs. Marylin, same authorial coyness) I think you're quite right that the term has been blurred, and since this is not one of Wolfe's overtly religious stories (like "The Detective of Dreams") you're also right in that it's blurrily apocalyptic rather than specifically so. We do agree on the general picture.

-alga-
