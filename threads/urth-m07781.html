"God and His Man"; Master/Maser

Urth list · 1 messages · 2009-04-27 to 2009-04-27

2009urthJonathan Goodwin

2009-04-27 Jonathan Goodwin #

Reports a textual inconsistency in "God and His Man": the word maser is sometimes changed to master, as the author's afterword in The Best of Gene Wolfe describes. Notes that a Tor paperback of Endangered Species dropped the inconsistencies, asks why they were not fixed at the first reprint, and mentions an earlier list discussion of whether the change was deliberate.
questions · close-textual-reading · author-and-biography

In the afterword to this story in _The Best of Gene Wolfe_, the author writes that when the story was first published, "the word _maser_ was changed by the proofreader (I was told, at least, that it was by the proofreader) to _master_. Not all the time, only sometimes."

In my mass market paperback edition of _Endangered Species_ (Tor, 1989), the inconsistencies remain, though they seem to have been removed with this reprint. Why weren't they fixed when the story was reprinted the first time?

I've never seen the Isaac Asimov's Science Fiction Magazine edition.

I looked the archives of this list, and it seems that there was some discussion of the master/maser being a deliberate change. It's not too hard to invent a rationale for that.
