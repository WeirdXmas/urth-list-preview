Themes: The dying sun and the cosmos

1999-12-02 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Argues that Wolfe's use of the white hole theory in The Shadow of the Torturer and The Urth of the New Sun reflects science that was already outdated when he wrote, and that he does the same with discredited saints like Catherine. Responds to Mark Millman's point, offering a correction to his own phrasing and reading the non-Einsteinian return route in Silhouette as a sign of crew factionalism rather than valid science.
disputes · source-and-allusion · genre-and-craft · theories: Wolfe uses discredited science and saints deliberately

Right, I understand that (cf. "Wolfe's impishness" in "BaD sCienCe" post), so I should have written "science of its day" as "science of the day before its day." But the clunky phrase had not gotten past my internal censor. (BTW, I'm not so sure about the example you gave from "Silhouette"--the non-Einsteinian return travel route bit--since I took it as being not necessarily "science valid within the framework of the tale" but an indication of how wigged out and factionalized the starship crew had become. Old superstitions arise in troubled times, old concepts find new political purpose.)

We should also note the same Wolfe trick in using discredited saints, too. White Holes (197n model) from Big Science; Catherine from Big Religion (since she was not "on her way out"--she was OUT).

=mantis=
