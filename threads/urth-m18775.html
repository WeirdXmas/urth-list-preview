Mostly Spoiler Free Thoughts on Home Fires

2011urthJeff WilsonDan'l Danehy-OakesSon of WitzDave Tallman
The thread: Readers debated how much science fiction Home Fires contains. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes called it emotionally satisfying but loosely shaped and argued its background social speculation is substantial, while Jeff Wilson described it as a boy-meets-girl story with pirates, spies and a whodunnit and only token SF, backing this with the Forever War premise needing little justification and Skip's Columbo-style detective work. Dave Tallman joked about the absence of wolves and whether the dog stood for God. The thread ended without resolution, with the two main posters still differing on how much the speculative material matters.
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: emotionally satisfying but shapeless, with substantial background social speculation
  • Jeff Wilson: essentially a boy-meets-girl story with token SF, needing little justification beyond the Forever War premise
  • Dave Tallman: playful remark that the novel has no wolves and perhaps a dog standing for God

Themes: Genre: science fiction, fantasy, both, Power, rulers and politics, War and soldiers

2011-03-02 Jeff Wilson replying to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that Home Fires needs little science-fictional justification beyond its Forever War premise, since its plot events could mostly occur through ordinary devices. Supports this by pointing to a mistaken-identity plot that would need many devices and to Skip's detective work, which is Columbo-style, focused on why crimes happened and how to stop them rather than uncovering deep mysteries.
supports · genre-and-craft

hmm, not spoilery, so I'll just say that aside from the inherent Forever War premise in the promotion, I can't think of anything that has to be SFnally justified for things to progress the way they did, except maybe for one of the mistaken identities, which could be done with the usual devices though it would take a rather unwieldy pile of them.

Oh, and rather a lot of the detective work is Columbo-style. Skip becomes privy to a lot of the stuff that would be a deep dark mystery in other Wolfe books, but he (and the reader) have to really work on *why* such a thing was done, and how to catch or just stop the antagonists.
