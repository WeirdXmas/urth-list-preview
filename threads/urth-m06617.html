hirsute sev

Urth list · 10 messages · 2008-11-01 to 2008-11-14

2008urthThe Book of the New SunJohn WatkinsAdam ThorntonCraig BrewerPedro PereiraMarc AraminiJames WynnSon of WitzJeff Wilson
The thread: Marc Aramini opened the thread arguing that Severian's pale skin and sun sensitivity, as described in the text, conflict with the idea that humans in the book are heavily hairy, and he admitted the reading might be projection. Pedro Pereira and James Wynn disputed whether the hairy cave people reflect evolutionary regression or deliberate artificial modification, and Pereira later acknowledged a correction that the cave people are artificially modified humans. Son of Witz mostly agreed Severian is pale and imagined him athletic rather than hairy. Weeks later Craig Brewer asked why Cyriaca describes brown fingers while Thecla calls his skin pale, and John Watkins answered that the tan came from travel and the pallor from years in the Citadel. The thread ended with that tentative explanation rather than a formal resolution.
  • Marc Aramini: Severian's pale, sun-sensitive skin argues against heavy body hair in the book's humans
  • James Wynn: hairy cave people are artificially modified humans, not evolutionary throwbacks
  • Pedro Pereira: hairy cave people are a Science Fantasy device with no scientific basis; later accepted they are modified humans
  • Son of Witz: Severian is pale per the text and is better imagined as athletic than hairy
  • Craig Brewer: asks whether skin colour is significant given brown fingers versus pale skin
  • John Watkins: tan comes from travel and pallor from years in the Citadel

Themes: Beasts and the animal in us, Robots, androids and artificial life, Evolution and the future of humanity

2008-11-01 Marc Aramini #

Argues that Severian's described pallor, with dark hair on his head and sun-sensitive skin, conflicts with the idea that he has heavy body hair. Grounds this in Thecla's warning about sunburn and the veins on his hands, while admitting the reading may be projection.
disputes · close-textual-reading

The description of Sev is one of paleness punctuated by dark head hair - Thecla says he must stay out of the sun or his pale skin will burn terribly. This does not seem to jive in my mind with someone who has excessive body hair that would take away this paleness and cover him from the sun - and it is the veins on the back of his hands that make him a man, not his hairy chest.

But I have no chest hair and it is certainly possible I am projecting a narcissistic image upon him that has no basis in the text.

2008-11-01 Pedro Pereira replying to Marc Aramini #

Disputes Marc Aramini's suggestion that humans in The Book of the New Sun are hairy, arguing the hairy cave people are a Science Fantasy device without scientific basis. Points to the text: Severian is not hairy, and nothing in the book supports hairier humanity, since Thecla describes his pale skin.
disputes · close-textual-reading · genre-and-craft

If I rememeber correctly there's those hairy humans in the caves that Severian meets and scares with the light of The Claw who are supposedly humans who "regressed" to earlier evolutionary stages. I don't think there's any scientific basis for this at all, it's another example of Wolfe's Science Fantasy take on things. As for Severian, no, he's not hairy at all and there's no reason to believe humans in general are hairier in TBotNS, not only on a scientific basis, but also no textual evidence whatsoever.

2008-11-13 Craig Brewer replying to Marc Aramini #

Asks whether Severian's skin colour is significant, noting that Cyriaca's story in Sword describes his brown fingers while Thecla calls his skin pale. Raises whether ruddy hands from being uncovered, relative paleness, or ethnicity explain it, and questions Thecla's worry about sunburn under a dim sun.
questions · close-textual-reading

Just a small point on Severian's coloring, but I was just looking back over Cyriaca's story in _Sword_, and, at one point, Severian mentions looking down at his "brown fingers." She also of course mentions the dark hairs on his hands, but the brown fingers seems interestingly different from Thecla's comment about his pale skin.

So...pale with ruddy hands because his hands aren't covered by a mask/cloak, as is the rest of his body? Is "brown" here just a loose rendering of "vaguely beige" skin color? Or is this a contradiction? Or is Thecla's "pale" a relative pale rather than actually white or devoid of color? Why, in the end, would it matter, and what would Severian's skin color signify? An illustrator is of course naturally interested, but is Sev's skin color supposed to indicate something about his ethnicity, which would of course be filtered through years of Urth history. I'm simply trying to figure out if Sev's coloring is actually significant, or if I should stop worrying about it.

I also thought I should mention that Thecla's worrying about a sunburn under a pale, dying sun seems odd. Yes, ultraviolet need not be accompanied by a "bright" light, but a deliberate mention of worrying about the harmful effects of a dim sun seems to stand out.
