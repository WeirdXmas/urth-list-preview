City on Green

Urth list · 21 messages · 2010-12-31 to 2011-01-02

20102011urthThe Book of the New SunJordon FlatoLee BermanDavid StockhoffMarc AraminiSon of WitzJeff WilsonRoy C. Lackey
The thread: Participants debated whether the ruins on Green were built by the Neighbors or by future people of Urth, with Marc Aramini and Jordon Flato arguing over whether a leaf-turning-into-a-bug passage is literal evidence for a new sun. Marc Aramini proposed a vegetative schema linking City on Green to Empires of Foliage and Flower, while Son of Witz and Jeff Wilson engaged his points without settling them. A separate strand over Nessus's position and centrality was pursued by Jeff Wilson and Lee Berman, who disagreed about whether closed roads imply once-open ones. Lee Berman then argued Neighbors and Inhumi could have built Nessus during an interstellar gap, against Roy Lackey's denial, and drew Hercules parallels that David Stockhoff engaged. The thread ended in fragmentation and speculation, with no consensus reached.
  • Marc Aramini: Green's ruins and vegetation reflect future Urth people and a New Sun-driven vegetative schema
  • Jordon Flato: the leaf-to-bug line is metaphor, and the daydream passage cannot serve as evidence for the new sun
  • Jeff Wilson: daydream passages still count as devices; Nessus shifts location, so closed roads prove little
  • Lee Berman: Nessus was built by Neighbors or Inhumi during an interstellar era and paralleled Hercules' labors
  • Roy C. Lackey: the Neighbors came from Blue and could not have built the City on Green or Nessus
  • David Stockhoff: Severian modeled on Hercules gives episodic structure; Urbis and Pestis point to Rome, not Nessus

Themes: Myth and folklore sources, The dying sun and the cosmos, Evolution and the future of humanity

2010-12-31 Lee Berman #

Proposes a synthesis: the City on Green ruins might be like Nessus on Urth, possibly built partly by Neighbors during an interstellar era, linking Fomalhaut, the red-star hints and Blue's Neighbors. Responds to Roy Lackey's claim that the Green ruins were built only by the Neighbors, and it grounds the idea in Marc's Nessus evidence, James's paradox, and Borski's multi-limbed-Neighbors clue.
proposes · source-and-allusion · myth-and-structure · theories: Nessus on Urth built partly by Neighbors; Fomalhaut is the origin of Neighbors; Typhon's home planet is Blue

"Asinine" and "unimaginative asshole" are terrible things to call someone. Roy, you've received high praise for your contributions to the lupine world many times in the past and present and I wish to join in that.

But is it possible for a bit of imagination to help? Marc finds evidence that the ruins on Green are like Nessus. Roy says this is impossible because the ruins on Green were built by Neighbors. Must one be wrong and the other right?

James finds paradox. For me, understanding paradox is like understanding an optical illusion. Flip your perspective and recognize the truth in both views. What if Nessus, on Urth was built (at least partially) by Neighbors?

Do we know when Nessus was first built? Is it impossible that it was built during a time period when Urth was part of an interstellar empire with back and forth commerce and travel for both humans and aliens?

Fomalhaut is a pretty close star. Could this be the source of the name "Neighbors"?

Gerry has noted that Fomalhaut is a red star in BotNS. But so is Sol. Both are young stars now; neither is red now. Perhaps Wolfe was hinting at a similar stellar history. Perhaps there is the suggestion that any Fomalhaut planets may have gone through periods of black holes in the sun and the subsequent white fountain recovery + Flood. The Fish references only bolster the idea that aliens on Urth may have come from a flooded planet.

Robert Borski has suggested that the multi-limbs (and heads?) of Neighbors is an auctorial clue that Blue is the home planet of Typhon. Father Inire and The Cumaean are supposed to be aliens. What planet do they come from? How could Typhon, Inire and the Cumaean (none of whom have saint names) come from the same place? They are so different. It might be possible if their home star had two planets and two species.

Is it possible the Neighbor-Inhumi conflict/interaction was played out in Urth's history as well as on Blue and Green?

I'll leave it at that for now, just saying that I prefer such a synthesis of everyone's ideas to each insisting they are correct and all others wrong. Gerry likes to insist that some ideas are bad but I have respect for all the people (Roy, Borski, Marc, etc.) who have contributed their well-thought out and researched ideas here.

2010-12-31 Jordon Flato replying to Marc Aramini #

Argues that the New Sun's light animates vegetation, so life on Green hybridizes and the Neighbors are future people of Urth, and sets out numbered rebuttals to Marc Aramini's points. Grounds this in a quoted passage from The Shadow of the Torturer about a leaf growing legs, and in Silk's shifting identity in The Return to the Whorl.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Neighbors are the people of Urth; Green life hybridized by the New Sun; Silk's jump caused by Rigoglio

That line about the leaf turning into a bug is a symbol, a day dream...not something that actually happened. How can that be used as evidence?

Let me get all your rebuttals in order, because I feel like we just argue in circles and circles.

1) The vegetation and life are too different. - I think vegetative evolution and hybridization is going on. This quote from shadow of the torturer, chapter 2:

"Two thoughts (that were nearly dreams) obsessed me and made them infinitely precious. The first was that at some not-distant time, time itself would stop .... the colored days that had so long been drawn forth like a chain of conjuror?s scarves come to an end, the sullen sun wink out at last. The second was that there existed somewhere a miraculous light - which I sometimes conceived of as a candle, sometimes as a flambeau - that engendered life in whatever objects it fell upon, so that a leaf plucked from a bush grew slender legs and waving feelers, and a rough brown brush opened black eyes and scurried up a tree."
(Shadow and Claw 18)

As early as Chapter 2 of New Sun, the coming of the New Sun is animating vegetation.
All life left on green is a result of that hybridization.

2) Time travel is not involved in Silk's astral travel - he shows up in the story in the past at the beginning of iGJ

3) The neighbors made the city on Green - I think the people of Urth are the neighbors, or will become them.

4) Rigoglio is responsible for them winding up on Urth. The text at least shows that Silk is thinking of Green at a time before it was corrupted and his son Sinew when they make the jump. This muddles the jump.

5) What is blue, and how do you see the light from space? paradoxes explain everything, baby. But seriously, this is what we need to work on.

Saying the neighbors built green or that Silk says they are different places are not very useful as absolute refutation because he does not even understand who he is. In RttW I can quote Silk as saying "I am Horn.", but the end, where "Silk nodded" works against that assertion.

I honestly believe Gene sat down and said, who, what, when, where, and how are going to be very hard to distinguish in this book, and a central mystery answers all of these questions. Even Horn's fate is answered by paying attention to the vegetation.

Argues that a daydream passage in City on Green should still be weighed as a device, since Wolfe presents ideas to readers in a less than literal way. disputes · genre-and-craft

It's a method of presenting an idea to the reader that is meant to be taken less than completely literally, but should still be considered otherwise why bother to introduce it at all? It is a similar device to the "true" lies in various characters' dialogue: they suggest to the reader something that needs to taken into consideration, though since the lies tend to be less symbolic and less figurative since they are intended by the speaking characters to be believed as if literally true, of course, which limits the author. A wholly internal conceit on the other hand can be as fanciful as necessary.

I gave it a preliminary shot. I think I had comparisons for five or six of the twelve labors. Some were awfully metaphorical: Hercules captures the deer with golden antlers= Severian's relationship with Dorcas (name means "gazelle"). Hercules, like Severian, carries the weight of the world on his shoulders when he subs for Atlas. etc.

I think "one for one" may be too much to ask...it would be like Typhon having 100 heads like his mythological namesake. Too direct and obvious a borrowing for Wolfe. Still, Nessus, a twin, disguised parentage, mysterious strengths and indominability are pretty good clues. I think there are others, not yet discovered.

Someone recently mentioned The Town That Forgot Fauna. I think there is much there which reflects on the founding of Nessus. It is on a river. It has grand walls and towers. There are black beans mentioned. We never learn the "grand name" of this city but its nicknames "Urbis" and "Pestis" are highly evocative of the name "Nessus" to me.

Unexplored, I think, is the name of the owner of the original piece of land, Fauna. This Roman goddess is significant to me in that she is the daughter of the god Faunus. As I have noted elsewhere, an epithet for Faunus is Innus, derived from the copulatory verb, "inire" and a name associated with monkeys. BotNS does have a male and female character who are both very old.

Reports that a web search for the nicknames "Urbis" and "Pestis" turned up little beyond Rome and the plague, and suggests Nessus might be seen as a nexus like postimperial Rome. Offers a link to the Stanford digital map of the Forma Urbis Romae marble city plan for puzzle-minded readers, responding to Lee Berman's comparison of the city in The Town That Forgot Fauna to Nessus.
informs · source-and-allusion · theories: Nessus modeled on Rome as a nexus

I googled "Urbis" and "Pestis" to see what loose associations might come up. There were none of any great interest: pestis (plague) is the species name of the Black Death, and Urbis tends to bring up Rome, but you would expect that. Rome is the Eternal City, which Nessus resembles in its lifespan, and of course both cities are notorious for other reasons. Thinking of Nessus as a Nexus in the sense that postimperial Rome was a nexus might be productive.
