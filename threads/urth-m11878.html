2010urthThe Book of the New SunJeff WilsonDavid StockhoffLee BermanSon of Witz
The thread: Readers debated whether the House Absolute in Wolfe's Urth books works like Versailles as a seat of power, and how its underground links to Nessus and other sites might function. David Stockhoff argued the House is a parody of Versailles whose ornament aims at the aristocracy, and that its power lies in hiddenness rather than display. Lee Berman held it is mainly a palace with functional ornament, and proposed a transporter device to explain rapid travel, pointing to the portal in Tzadkiel's ship. Jeff Wilson suggested many underground conveyances, including Inire's time tunnels, and cited the White House tunnels as a parallel. The thread ends without resolution, with Berman's questions about whether walking the tunnels is practical left open.
  • David Stockhoff: House Absolute is a parody of Versailles whose ornament mocks the aristocracy, and its power lies in hiddenness
  • Lee Berman: House Absolute is mainly a palace like Versailles, and a transporter device links its locations
  • Jeff Wilson: many underground conveyances, including time tunnels, could connect the Citadel and House Absolute
  • Gerry Quinn: compared the House to Versailles

Themes: Buildings, cities and labyrinths, Power, rulers and politics, Hidden identities and parentage

2010-12-15 David Stockhoff replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that the House Absolute, though underground and invisible, is a parody of Versailles whose ornament is aimed at the aristocrats rather than the House itself. Responds to Gerry Quinn's and Lee Berman's comparisons by pointing out that the House cannot serve as a regime ornament because no one can see it, while conceding Versailles was also largely unseen.
disputes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: House Absolute is a parody of Versailles

There's a contradiction here. Since no one can see the House Absolute, it cannot serve as an ornament to the regime. It is useless in that regard as well, though nobles could visit. Versailles was certainly an ornament. The House is partly a parody of Versailles because it is underground.?However, Versailles as a whole is so huge, with its great vistas of lawn and fountain, that no one could see it who was not invited or who worked there. So it was in a way invisible too. At any rate, I have to say again: it is the aristocrats who are shown as useless. Not the House itself.

2010-12-15 Jeff Wilson replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that the Citadel and House Absolute could be joined by many underground conveyances, from boats to one of Inire's time tunnels hidden in the Botanic Gardens. Responds to Lee Berman's transporter idea by citing the White House's tunnels as a real-world parallel for an underground route.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Inire's time tunnels link the Citadel and House Absolute; Underground conveyances connect the Citadel and House Absolute

The White House has numerous tunnels giving underground access to the property, and so it would be unusual for there to be no access between the age-old seat of government and the "Old Port". As for dainty prostitutes and decrepit curators making the trip, there could be any number of conveyances from ancient golf carts, Typhonian boats, a literal underground railroad, all the way up to one of Inire's time tunnels. Which there might well be, perhaps disguised as part of the Botanic Gardens.

2010-12-15 David Stockhoff replying to Son of Witz #

Argues that the House Absolute projects power differently from Versailles, since Versailles uses landscaping built on fortress principles to command the view while the House hides the Autarch behind an absence. Replies to Son of Witz's claim that exultants, armigers and entertainers visit the House grounds, and concedes only that hiddenness could be a form of power.
disputes · genre-and-craft

I'm trying to convey a very basic point.?
Versailles is an unparalleled example of the use of landscaping---yes, LANDSCAPING---to project power. Landscaping performed according to principles derived from fortress design, and vice versa. Landscaping that takes into account sweeping fields of fire so that all approaches are covered and conversely the Palace looms in all directions. The House has landscaping, and perhaps if hiddenness is power then it projects power by hiding the House if everyone knows it's there. But otherwise not. It's just a pleasure park where drunk people drift in paddleboats with their lovers. The Autarch himself is never seen as such: no parades, no speeches, nothing. He is an absence; so is the House except to those allowed within.

Argues that House Absolute is mainly a palace like Versailles, defending its ornaments as functional for the aristocracy, and that its links to Nessus are special places rather than a literal extension.
