Whom Does Wolfe Read?

The thread: Readers asked which authors Gene Wolfe has read or praised, prompted by a John Barach question about Sharon Baker and Journey to Membliar. Richard Payton and James Wynn compiled lists of praised writers, citing Wolfe's interviews, blurbs and references in his fiction, including Borges, Le Guin, Lewis, Chesterton and Graves. Another poster added a Jennifer Stevenson blurb as an erotic contrast to Wolfe's work. Chris cautioned that Wolfe's blurb for a weak Frankowski novel shows author blurbs cannot be trusted, and the thread ended there without a real resolution.
  • John Barach: asks which Wolfe-blurbed books are worth reading and what else he has praised
  • pthwndxrclzp: reports Wolfe blurbed Jennifer Stevenson's Trash Sex Magic, an erotic contrast
  • Richard Payton: lists authors Wolfe praised, citing a 1986 McCaffery interview
  • James Wynn: lists writers and books Wolfe seems to have read, grounded in blurbs and textual references
  • Chris: blurbs are unreliable since Wolfe praised a poor Frankowski pulp novel

Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Myth and folklore sources

2005-04-15 John Barach #

Asks which novels by Sharon Baker, a writer Wolfe blurbed, readers have read and whether they are good, and what other authors and books Wolfe has praised or blurbed. Prompted by finding Journey to Membliar in a used bookstore after reading Castle of Days, where Wolfe's reading tastes showed (Le Guin, Kress, Budrys).
questions · author-and-biography

Urthers --

When I read _Castle of Days_ for the first time earlier this year, I learned a bit about Wolfe's reading. For instance, it's evident that he likes Ursula LeGuin, Nancy Kress, and Algis Budrys.

Today, in a used book store, I came across Sharon Baker's _Journey to Membliar_, which had a blurb from Wolfe on the front, praising Baker highly. I've never heard of her. _Journey_ is apparently her second novel, following _Quarrelling, They Met the Dragon_ (http://www.amazon.com/exec/obidos/tg/detail/-/0380892014: the Amazon blurb is written by her son, apparently) and followed in turn by a sequel _Burning Tears of Sassurum_. Anyone read these novels (besides Wolfe)? Any good?

What other authors has Wolfe praised or indicated that he enjoys? What other books has Wolfe written blurbs for?

Curious....

John

2005-04-15 Richard Payton replying to Andy Robertson #

Lists the authors Gene Wolfe has praised, naming Borges and, from a 1986 McCaffery interview, a range of science fiction writers plus Nabokov and Lovecraft. Responds to Andy Robertson's question about which authors Wolfe has praised, quoting Wolfe's own admiring remarks as the evidence.
informs

Borges, certainly. Also, from a 1986 interview with Larry McCaffery:

LM: Who are some of the contemporary writers you most admire?

GW: Among SF writers I'd include Algis Budrys, Joanna Russ, Ursula Le Guin, Damon Knight, Kate Wilhelm, Michael Bishop, Brian Aldiss, Nancy Kress, Michael Moorcock. Among non-SF writers I most enjoy are Nabokov (Pale Fire is a truly amazing book) and Borges. Of course . . . Lovecraft (who is usually regarded as a SF writer, but to me he's the real successor -- if that's possible -- to Poe's line of horror.)

2005-04-15 James Wynn replying to John Barach #

Lists the authors and books Gene Wolfe seems to have read or praised, including Avram Davidson, Kate Wilhelm, Le Guin, Sherlock Holmes, Dickens, Kipling, Lewis, Chesterton, Proust and Robert Graves. Grounds each claim in blurbs, interviews, references in The Fifth Head of Cerberus and Castle of Days, and Wolfe's Greek myth borrowings from Graves.
informs · source-and-allusion

I going to presume we're talking fiction here. He wrote a gushing blurb for a book of Avram Davidson's short stories. He's referred to Kate Wilhelm in several interviews and chats and Number 5 finds one of her books in "The Fifth Head", so that's gotta be one. He *seems* to have been great friends with Le Guin at one time but his affection for her work *seems* like it may have declined with that friendship.

He's certainly not just a SF fan. He is a big fan of the Sherlock Holmes mysteries...probably the whole Mystery genre including Poe. He likes Dickens and Thackery (but you would know that from "Castle of Days"). He read a whole lot of Kipling as a kid. He seems to have read most of C.S. Lewis and G.K. Chesterton. I'm told there are a lot Proust references in "The New Sun" but I don't know enough about Proust to verify that.

He clearly likes Robert Graves and openly praises "I, Claudius". His references to Greek Mythology are very often tapped directly from Graves' "The Greek Myths". And he likes World Mythology (not just the Greek stuff) -- look for a lot of Persian and Hittite mythology in his next Latro book if it is ever finished.

~ Crush

2005-04-16 Chris P replying to James Wynn #

Argues that Wolfe's blurb praising Frankowski's writing, which appeared on a poor pulp novel, shows that readers should not trust author blurbs. Responds to James Wynn's list of writers Wolfe seems to admire, including Avram Davidson, Kate Wilhelm and Le Guin, by adding a caution about endorsements.
disputes

He also dished out a big juicy quote on the virtues of the writing of Frankowski, which ended up attached as a blurb to one of the worst excuses for a pulp novel I have ever seen. Lesson learned: do not trust blurbs, even from Wolfe.
