Wolfe knows he's pushing it.

Urth list · 1 messages · 2002-04-11 to 2002-04-11

2002urthThe Book of the Long SunMichael Straight

2002-04-11 Michael Straight #

Speculates that Wolfe is genuinely unsure whether his next novel will be published because his books may be too obscure, and that he is writing mainly for himself. Grounds this in his apparent knowledge of SF readers' tastes and in examples of his difficult choices, such as Oreb, Wizer's speech, Pig's accent, and the sparse treatment of Auk and Chenille.
proposes · author-and-biography

I was thinking about Wolfe's apparent modesty with regard to whether or not his next novel will be published. Wolfe is a SF fan, he knows what's popular, and he knows that his books are off-putting to many people. I think he may be genuinely not sure whether he's pushing things too far, being too obscure. In some ways he's writing for himself, trying to make a book he thinks is fun and beautiful, and is pleasantly suprised that there are still some other people who like that sort of thing.

But perhaps he still thinks: they liked Oreb, but will Tick be way too much? They managed to decipher Wizer's Yodaspeak, but will they put up with Pig's accent? Can I really get away with saying this little about Auk and Chenille, or is Hartwell going to finally fling a manuscript back at me in frustration?

-Rostrum
