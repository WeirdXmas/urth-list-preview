Another Avern for you

Urth list · 9 messages · 2002-07-29 to 2002-08-01

2002urthThe Book of the New SunBratt IanMatthew MalthouseAdam StephanidesJames WynnDan'l Danehy-OakesJames Jordan
The thread: Readers debated why the Ascians are called shadowless and what their name, Avern, signifies. James Jordan read the Ascians as a tyranny against story and argued the name points to the equatorial sun, while James Wynn first proposed a darkness etymology and later supported the equator derivation as deliberate. Bratt Ian suggested the lack of shadow means lack of soul and individuality, and Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued the name is folk etymology that the text never explains. Adam Stephanides concluded it is a true etymology built on a false belief, which Wynn agreed Wolfe knowingly exploited. The thread ended with a rough consensus on the equator reading rather than a resolved interpretation of the shadow itself.
  • James Jordan: Ascian name reflects equatorial sun, a true versus false sun, and a general tyranny against story
  • James Wynn: Name derives from darkness at first, later deliberate equator derivation Wolfe knowingly exploited
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: Ascians are north of the equator; name is folk etymology and the answer may lie in The Shadow of the Torturer
  • Adam Stephanides: True etymology built on a false belief, citing Ash and Ava's statement that Ascians are not human
  • Bratt Ian: Shadowless means lacking individuality and soul, as vampires lack reflections
  • Crush: Darkness reading of the Ascian name (referenced, not posting in this thread)

Themes: Names and their meanings, Beasts and the animal in us, Myth and folklore sources

2002-07-29 James Jordan #

Argues that the Ascians' Marxist-like timelessness is less a specific allusion to Mao or Orwell than a general tyranny against story, and that Wolfe's point is that narrative breaks through anyway. Grounds this in Plato's hostility to myth, the name Ascian meaning shadowless, and the shadows in A Solar Labyrinth, responding to a post about Mao and Orwell.
disputes · myth-and-structure · source-and-allusion · theories: Ascians are shadowless, a symbol of timeless philosophy; Story and shadow are inescapable in Wolfe's world

Though the discussion is fun, I do think that's all it is. Any writer knows Orwell and Mao and thought control; Wolfe surely knows Plato's hostility to story and myth. Attempts by extreme tyrants to cut people off from their pasts, and from story, narrative, and myth, are pretty ... well ... timeless! (Typhon did not go so far.) Trying to go much beyond this and say, "Wolfe is building on Mao, not Orwell" etc. strikes me as overinterpretation -- though we can do it here for fun, planting averns -- unless I'm missing something significant.
Wolfe's "point" is that story and narrative are inescapable aspects of human life in the real world, and that they will break through somehow even in the most mathematically "timeless" and philosophical cultures that tyrants can ever attempt. Attempts to freeze history and eliminate story are fruitless. Homer and the Bible win! Plato and Pythagoras (seventeen) lose!
I'm more inclined to see this as a "Christian narrative view of reality" defeating a "Philosophical-gnostic timeless mathematical attempt to control reality."
But for Wolfe's "sources," well, it's all of the above, lupinated. Yet, another thought also: The Marxist motif in connection with the Ascians is obvious, and as I say I don't think we need to try and pinpoint it to Mao or anyone else. But though "Ascian" sounds like "Asian," in addition to that allusion it actually means "shadowless." Shadowless, directly under the sun, seems to me a good symbol for a timeless philosophical worldview. The shadows, however, are inescapable, as is narrative and story. Stories are shadows cast by persons, with the sun behind them, so to speak. "Loyal's" story isn't much, but it's still a story. Even with the sun directly overhead, you have a little shadow. (Plato does not like shadows; he wants us out of the cave.) Wolfe takes up shadows in "A Solar Labyrinth" (which came first?), and I wonder how much light (or shadow) that tale casts on the Ascians and Loyal. The shadows are cast by various objects, and block approach to the true center. A nice image, if intended by Wolfe (?), of how wrong narratives and culture myths mislead people into wrong patterns of life and thought. Yet some of the shifting shadow-narratives-myths move people gradually to the center.
Anyway, that's an avern from me, to add to the thousands!

Nutria

2002-07-30 Bratt Ian #

Proposes that the Ascians are called shadowless because they lack individuality, reasoning that having a shadow means having a soul and so being human, as vampires lack reflections. Replies to Crush's darkness reading of the Ascian name, the roses and the nenuphars, saying he finds the darkness theory reasonable but offers his own alternative.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Ascians are shadowless because they lack individuality and soul

Interesting idea. I've always thought they were "shadowless" because of their lack of individuality. If we take the route that to have a shadow is to have a soul (Vampires have no reflection) and to have a soul is to be human, then the Ascians have lost what makes them human. In this case, I've always considered that to be their individuality. Although I find the darkness theory reasonable and even somewhat similar.

-Ian

2002-07-30 James Wynn #

Proposes that the Ascians take their name from darkness, linking them to Erebus, the Hesiodic darkness of Tartarus, and reading the Ascians as shadowless because they live in continuous dark. Grounds this in Agilus's line 'Another avern for you' and in symbols tied to Severian as the New Sun, such as roses for life and water lilies for death.
proposes · source-and-allusion · myth-and-structure · theories: Ascian name ironically implies darkness

"Another avern for you", said Agilus

Hmmm. I thought they were "shadowless" because they were in continuous dark? Although, no doubt they interpreted it the other way. But the Ascians are under the control Erebus (Erebus according to Hesiod is the "darkness of Tartaros")

Severian is the New Sun, the roses (which are sun symbols) represent life, nenuphars whose roots extend to the darkness of the water represent death.

Surely darkness is ironically the intended implication of the name "Ascian".

2002-07-31 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Disputes the claim that Ascians come from the equator under the sun, arguing the text places them north of the equator and that their name is a folk etymology. Quotes Patera Nutria and notes the text never says why Ascians are shadowless, hinting that The Shadow of the Torturer's title holds the answer.
disputes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion · theories: Ascians come from north of the equator, not under the sun; Ascian is a folk etymology in the Commonwealth; The Shadow of the Torturer's title explains the shadowlessness

As regards the Ascians, Patera Nutria inscribed:

Actually, the text makes clear that they are from _north_ of the equator (or "waist of the Urth"), and that this is a false belief, a folk-etymology common among the people of the
Commonwealth. The real reason they are "shadowless" is not given ... but note the title of the first volume of tBotNS.

--Blattid

Interesting point. But doesn't the text make is clear that they are from the equator, and directly under the sun, and that is the basic meaning of Ascian?

Absolutely. I would see this as true versus false sun. But I'm certainly open to correction.

2002-07-31 James Wynn #

Recalls Wolfe's explanation in "Castle of the Otter" that a shadow implies the sun standing behind the thing that casts it, and that the torturer's shadow signals death by blotting out the sun. Adds a playful wish that the Ascians had come from the equator, which would have fit Lupine's name-based associations.
supports · author-and-biography · close-textual-reading

IIRC In "Castle of the Otter" Wolfe says that a shadow implies the sun because the sun *stands behind* (as in, supports, affirms, etc) the one that casts a shadow. Also, that the "shadow of the torturer" represents death when he performs an execution because he blots out the sun.

It would have been cool if the Ascians *were* from the equator. It would have been very Lupine if they had been. I'm sure Wolfe would kick himself that he didn't think of it at the time.

-- Crush

2002-07-31 Adam Stephanides #

Argues that the name 'Avern' and the shadowless Ascians rest on a true etymology built on a false belief, citing Ash's speech in The Citadel of the Autarch about a man who sold his shadow. Supports this by pointing to Ava's line that Ascians are not human, correcting Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's dismissal of the term as mere folk-etymology.
disputes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion · theories: Ascian name derives from shadowless belief

Well, it's not a "folk-etymology" in the sense of being an incorrect etymology; it's a true etymology based upon a false belief (cf. Ash's remarks in TCotA, chap. 16, pp. 121-2 of Timescape ed.).

Ash goes on to say "In a fable made in the earliest morning of our race, a man sold his shadow and found himself driven out everywhere he went. No one would believe that he was human." Earlier, Ava told Severian that "Ascians are not human." (TCotA, chap. 10, p. 72).

--Adam
