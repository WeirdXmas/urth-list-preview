The Book of the New Sun

Urth list · 7 messages · 2006-11-13 to 2006-11-15

2006urthThe Book of the New SunMatthew DeLucaJeff WilsonDan RabinDon Doggett
The thread: Participants discussed the Latro books, focusing on whether the amnesiac narrator Latro can foreshadow events as Severian does. Dan Rabin argued that Latro cannot foreshadow directly but Wolfe can plant it through the gods, oracles and seers Latro reports, and proposed that the placement of name-lists and translator's notes is a deliberate inversion of the New Sun volumes. Jeff Wilson countered that Latro can foreshadow unreliably because he rereads his scroll each day before writing. Matthew DeLuca praised Soldier of Sidon's plainer style and later asked whether the wax woman Sabra's reanimation has a mythic parallel or echoes Severian's necromancy. The thread stayed on a few points between two posters and did not reach a resolution.
  • Dan Rabin: Latro cannot foreshadow like Severian, but Wolfe plants foreshadowing through gods and seers; front and back matter placement is a deliberate inversi
  • Jeff Wilson: Latro can foreshadow unreliably because he rereads his earlier scroll entries daily
  • Matthew DeLuca: Soldier of Sidon may be Wolfe's best Latro book for its plainer prose; Sabra's reanimation may echo Severian's necromancy
  • don doggett: Enjoying the rereading and narrative power of the work rather than detailed theorizing

Themes: Unreliable narration, Memory and forgetting, Myth and folklore sources

2006-11-13 Dan Rabin replying to Matthew DeLuca #

Announces the poster has finished the first reading of the Latro books and is ready to discuss them once others finish, then raises two points: the placement of name-lists at the end of the Latro volumes versus the front of the Long and Short Sun books, and the placement of translator's notes at the start of Latro volumes versus the end of New Sun volumes. Argues these are deliberate inversions, with the foreword notes solving the problem that amnesiac Latro cannot foreshadow within the narrative.
proposes · close-textual-reading · genre-and-craft · theories: Translator's forewords plant foreshadowing that Latro cannot

I'm done with my first reading, and I'm ready to join a discussion once enough other people are done so that we can be free with the spoilers.

This is going to be fun. We've got the charisma of ancient Egypt to lap up, somewhat like the appeal of dinosaurs to children (our knowledge of both relies on impressive reconstructions of a hidden past). Then there's the Darkest Africa thing to contend with.

Non-spoiler stuff: all three Latro books have the name-lists at the end, whereas the Long and the Short have them at the front, where some entries are slight spoilers. I've always assumed that Wolfe chose the positioning of this matter, but it's conceivably a choice made by the editor or even the book designer. Does anyone think this is important enough to ask Wolfe about?

Also, the "translator's" notes in the New Sun books, whose narrator never forgets, are at the ends of the volumes, whereas those of the Soldier books, whose narrator cannot remember, are at the beginning. This fits the general pattern of inversions between the two series, but also solves a technical problem: the construction of Latro's situation makes foreshadowing within the body of the novels immpossible, so the "translator" can plant a bit in the forewords.

-- Dan Rabin

2006-11-14 Jeff Wilson replying to Dan Rabin #

Argues that Latro can foreshadow in Soldier of the Mist, though unreliably, because he revisits themes hinted at earlier when he re-reads his previous entries before writing new ones. Builds on Dan Rabin's quoted point and Jeff Wilson's earlier claim that Latro cannot foreshadow as Severian does, reasoning that his daily re-reading of the scroll must be assumed for him to continue it at all.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Latro foreshadows through re-reading his earlier entries

Now that I think about it, Latro can do this, though not reliably. Certainly in _Mist_, he revisits themes and topics hinted at earlier, and this is explained by him re-reading his earlier entries immediately prior to inscribing new ones. In fact, he pretty much has to be assumed to re-read at least "read this every day" for him to know to continue the scroll at all.
