The thread: Participants in the Memorare hardcover thread mostly drifted from Wolfe into comparisons with Larry Niven. Matthew Groves asked whether Memorare resembles Niven-style hard SF, and Jeff Wilson granted the surface resemblance while arguing Wolfe only seems to write hard science fiction and skips things like the fossil record. Michael Straight agreed the science reads like a classic Niven-era SF story, and Recursive Loop mostly offered social chatter about reading Niven. Matthew Groves and Jeff Wilson then argued over whether Niven's Protector breeders clash with the fossil record, with no clear resolution.

2007-12-08 Jeff Wilson replying to Matthew Groves #

Argues that Larry Niven's novel Protector explains primate ancestry through induced mutation but never connects primates to the rest of earthly life, calling that link a metafictional joke rather than plot. Responds to Matthew King's question about why homo habilis shares ancestry with other Terran species, a point about Niven's science rather than Wolfe.
answers · genre-and-craft

The shared ancestry among primates is explained by induced mutation, but no explanation is made to connect primates with the rest of earthly life.

To be sure, that part is metafictional one-upmanship rather than an integral part of the plot. Psi powers were very popular among genre fiction at the time, and it's hard to top the power to control the author/editor/publisher.
