Inhumi in the Whorl

Urth list · 23 messages · 2010-08-16 to 2010-08-20

The thread: Lee Berman opened by asking whether the inhumi came from the Whorl, then pushed a long run of theories linking Wolfe's monsters to Greek myth: Scylla, Echidna, Typhon, and the Mother of The Book of the Long Sun as a megatherian or sea monster. James Wynn argued Scylla is a software entity named after a megatherian and that Typhon links to Bill Ries in An Evil Guest, while Dan'l Danehy-Oakes supplied Borges as the source of Baldanders' name. David Stockhoff disputed whether megatherians must be non-human, and Berman ended by conceding the Baldanders point and calling the megatherian status an open question. The thread drifted from the inhumi into speculation with little textual evidence and no settled conclusion.
  • Lee Berman: inhumi may be imported from the Whorl; Mother and Scylla/Echidna/Typhon are mythic megatherians
  • James Wynn: Scylla is a software entity named after a megatherian; Typhon is linked to Bill Ries
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: Baldanders is named after the Borges monster, not the German shapeshifter
  • David Stockhoff: megatherians need not be non-human, so Typhon could be human
  • Jeff Wilson: suggested Blue could be Scylla's home world
  • adam: questioned the setting and offered Greek monster links without evidence

Themes: Myth and folklore sources, Beasts and the animal in us, Names and their meanings

2010-08-16 Lee Berman #

Asks whether the inhumi, shapeshifting vampires, could have come to Urth from the Whorl, looking for evidence in the Book of the New Sun's stories of shapeshifters and blood bats. Points to Dorcas's bat remark and the unseen manatees, which may euphemize undines, and suggests imported creatures could be the source of these legends.
questions · source-and-allusion · theories: Blood bats euphemize some other creature; Inhumi arrived on Urth from the Whorl

If we are wondering if the Whorl came with Inhumi, it seems we should be looking for evidence of shapeshifting vampires on Urth. Shapeshifters are mentioned in BotNS. albeit in stories. What is the source material of those stories?

Dorcas thinks Severian looks like a black bat who has been sucking blood all night after he eats a pomegranate. We are told often of blood bats and we see the wounds they supposedly make. But do we ever actually see one?

We are also told of manatees and we never see those either, though we do see the undine(s) that manatees are fairly clearly a euphemism for. What might blood bats be a euphemism for?

Moreover, Urth used to be the hub of an empire of 10,000+ planets. It hardly seems impossible that one of the millions of spaceships flying around managed to land on Green and its cargo eventually made it to Urth. What are the sources of the legends...?

2010-08-18 Lee Berman replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that the Mother in The Book of the Long Sun is analogous to sea monsters like Erebus and Abaia, and that the pirate ship is a naviscaput she can take the form of. Builds the case from Horn's shooting of the woman on the boat, which removes an arm of a piece of the Mother shaped as bait, Seawrack, and from Horn's reasoning about the pirates' cove.
proposes · close-textual-reading · myth-and-structure · theories: The Mother is analogous to Erebus and Abaia; The pirate ship is a naviscaput; Seawrack is a piece of the Mother shaped as bait; The barnacle thing was old bait for Neighbors

My view is that The Mother is analagous to the sea-borne monsters like Erebus and Abaia that we hear about on Urth. As such she is capable of taking on a form of a Naviscaput. Later The Mother appears to Horn as a great underwater mass with a human form above the water. What would that be, a Homo sapiens-caput? Anthro-caput?

Anyway, I think naviscaput is what the pirate ship is. Horn wonder about the homebase of the pirate ship, concluding it must be a cove since there are no towns anywhere close enough. When Horn shoots the woman on that boat, he is shooting off the arm of a pinched off piece of the Mother which has been formed into a human shape as attractive bait- Seawrack.

She falls into the water...I think she leaps onto Horn's boat but is scared by Babbie, and jumps off, leaving some blood on the deck for Babbie to lap up. Underwater, The Mother feels that it is more expedient to just sew up the amputated arm and hope Seawrack still works as damaged bait rather than going through the time and trouble of pinching off and re-forming a whole new siren and risk Horn escaping.

As mentioned, I consider it possible that the barnacle thing was an old piece of bait, pinched off and designed for Neighbors. Understandable that Seawrack wants it out of there.

2010-08-18 James Wynn replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that the Scylla named in Wolfe's Short Sun books is a software entity who took her name from a megatherian, and that the undersea creature called Mother on Blue may be linked to that megatherian. Supports the idea with the Oreb meeting, the Urth megatherian, and the naming of Scylla after the monster.
proposes · puzzle-solving · source-and-allusion · theories: Mother is the megatherian Scylla; Scylla named herself after a megatherian; Echidna hides among the colonists

But, as you know, determining which clue is the right one is the trick.

Scylla on the Whorl is software entity and she came with the Whorl. When we do meet her in the Short Sun books, she is in Oreb and her original form as a young girl. We learned that she consciously named herself *after* the megatherian for whom she was an agent. Echidna might well be hiding out in one or more of the colonists, but how could she become Mother?

The megatherian Scylla is a solid being. A gigantic underwater dweller. Megatherian, Mother, Vironese Scylla. Which one of these is not like the others?

Look, in the five New Sun books, we never meet a megatherian that I know of. In the Short Sun, the main character travels all the way to Urth to meet one. It's not one of the big "male" megatherians either (Erebus or Abaia). It's the one named after a Greek female monster. And then we have huge undersea creature on Blue named Mother. I'm just sayin'. It's "fishy".

Argues that Typhon is a megatherian monster-god, grouping him with the giant mythologically named beings on Blue and Urth, and that the Whorl's gods are uploaded minds of Typhon and his family, with Echidna and Scylla possibly The Mother. Responds to James Wynn's claim that the megatherians are never met, citing Typhon's name, telepathy, and his resurrection of Piaton as evidence.
proposes · theology-and-religion · source-and-allusion · theories: Typhon is a megatherian monster-god; The Whorl gods are uploaded minds of Typhon's family; Echidna or Scylla is The Mother; Megatherians originated on Blue and spread to Urth

James, I'm sensing a misunderstanding of my general framework for BotNS, which Roy derisively calls the "Grand Unified Theory". I have one which works pretty well for me but I understand it doesn't always jibe with the theories of others causing misunderstandings. I'll post my G.U.T. sometime soon if it seems worth it but for now I'll try to just answer your questions, hoping it is understood there is a supporting framework beneath it, perhaps unseen.

I disagree. By naming convention alone I think we do meet one. The acknowledged megatherians generally have the names of giant mythological monsters (Arioch, the fallen angel, is an exception but that's a different part of the G.U.T). And yes, we seem never meet them as we meet other giagantic creatures like Tzadkiel and The Mother. (I do think we do meet a very small one named Juturna and another even smaller named Idas)

But we have another character who is unequivocally named for a mythological monster. And this one we do meet. Typhon of course. By his name alone we can group him with the others. His human form should not fool us. That was a common trick of the ancient gods. (We actually do see The Mother and Tzadkiel do a certain pinching technique to create a human form)

When we add the fact that Typhon has superhuman powers of telepathy and clairvoyance (just like the mandragora in the bottle) and his claim that he "was not born" in the sense that Severian understands the term I think his status as megatherian is solidified. His poor sense of identity with his own body, and willingness to be grafted onto Piaton's adds more.

One might wonder about his resurrection. Severian can only resurrect human beings. It is my general thinking that megatherian monster/gods have a component of immortality. It is only Piaton that Severian resurrects.

Yes, it is my understanding that The Whorl was built by Typhon (Pas) on Urth and the gods of The Whorl are the uploaded minds/personalities of Typhon and his family. Kinda like Mr. Million. As Typhon was fleshly real, so were Echidna, Scylla, Tartaros, etc. Unlike Mr. Million I think the uploading needn't have killed the donor. Thus, copies of Scylla, both biological and electronic, can exist simultaneously on Urth and the Whorl and Blue.

Agreed on the connection. But Echidna is also female and was "*The Mother* of all Monsters". Moreover the Whorl humans have shifted worship toward Echidna now that they are on Blue. I don't know if the evidence for Scylla or Echidna as The Mother is stronger, I'm only suggesting both are there for me.

I think if the megatherians on Blue and Urth are related, the more original source is Blue (and perhaps other planets). From that source they were spread to Urth during its First Empire (the black beans). After a while, Typhon realized Urth was no longer hospitable (black hole) and perhaps that he was contending with powers too great for him and decided to send a ship of the delightful Urthly human worshipers and slaves he treasured back to the home planet(s) before he escaped himself.
