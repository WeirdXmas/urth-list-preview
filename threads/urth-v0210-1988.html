Themes: Memory and forgetting, Time loops and causality

2002-10-29 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Argues that Weer's doctor visits in Peace are split across different years, with Dr. Van Ness likely dead or retired by 1973 and Weer seeing a new doctor, and that Weer's warning to Dr. Black may have reached Bobby as a threat. Builds this on the dated sections, the 1963 island reference, the 'Could you come back this afternoon' line, and the reading of Weer's 'thin' versus 'fat' description.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · puzzle-solving · theories: Weer's doctor visits splice multiple years; Dr. Van Ness dead or retired by 1973; Weer's warning to Dr. Black reaches Bobby as threat

Another bit, not directly doctor face-to-face, but about the timeframe: "[the island], at about the time I imagined myself visiting Dr. Van Ness, to harbor a hermit called Crazy Pete" seems to peg the "I asked for help with my future stroke" episode to circa 1963.

I have a creeping sense that Dr. Van Ness did not see Weer after Weer's first, non-fatal stroke -- that Dr. V was himself dead or retired by that year of 1973 -- and Weer's stroke doctor is some new guy he doesn't know and can't remember his name (and Weer's memory is getting shakey enough at that point that he isn't tracking who has died, or maybe he never knew that Dr. V died or retired). For Weer's second stroke he wants a trusted doctor to talk to, hence his attempts to talk to Dr. V.

I also wonder at this detail: one of the last things Dr. V says is "Could you come back this afternoon," but the whole trippy doctor's office thread started very specifically at 1:30 pm (first few pages of the book). It was already afternoon when they started. If the doctor was in the same timeframe, he would probably say "later this afternoon" or "this evening," but as it stands it seems as though Dr. V's interview with Weer has happened in the morning hours.

Since I'm not advocating time travel, I cannot help but wonder if Weer's attempt to warn Dr. Black, which was received as if he was Cassandra, didn't in fact get back to Bobby, who received it as some kind of weird threat . . . which prompted the fight on the stairs. (Standard time travel trap.)

(And the doctor's office is in Blaine's old bank building, right? Hmmm, I wonder if there's an angle there.)

2002-10-30 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Proposes that the Peace doctor thread is out of sequence, so Weer's walk to the afternoon appointment falls in 1963 and his breakdown occurred between Charlie's visit and Charlie's letter. Builds the case on the slippery timeline, Weer's shifting between the memory mansion and the mundane timeline, and the buzzer call to Miss Birkhead answered by Miss Hadow.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: Doctor thread given out of sequence in Peace; Weer's breakdown happens in 1963 between Charlie's visit and letter

Oops, correction!

I =am= advocating time travel! That was a slip of the keypad.

My textual reference here is the fact that when Bobby arrives at Weer's party he is already presented in a belligerent mode, and we never get any backstory as to why this is the case.

More on this one:

I toy with the notion that we have been given the doctor thread out of sequence: that Weer walking in at 1:30 is Weer going to his afternoon appointment. (Which would make it 1963 when he is walking in.) And then the other 1963 material we see is from earlier that same day.

If this mental breakdown was for outside observers a single episode that happened for a few hours one morning in 1963, then it is not too difficult to imagine a sheepish Weer going to the afternoon appointment now normal again (his "possession" is past), and everybody can chalk it up to overwork.

In any event, according to the slippery text, Weer went from the doctor's office to the factory and there started reading the letter from Charlie Turner. Again, this points to a specific time circa 1963, and it seems to rule out the Doris letter as being a mundane cause of the mental breakdown (since it comes after the episode at the doctor's office). So the breakdown would seem to have happened between the time of Charlie's visit and the arrival of Charlie's letter.

The time-slippage continues, however; and where Weer once thought he was in the memory mansion casting his mind back, now he seems enmeshed in the mundane timeline but with thoughts that it is all an illusion. He rings the buzzer for Miss Birkhead in 1963 (the day of his brief mental breakdown) and 1974's Miss Hadow answers (the day of his death).
