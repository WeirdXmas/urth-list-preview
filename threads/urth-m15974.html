Themes: Memory and forgetting

2010-09-27 Andrew Mason #

Argues that Severian's 'perhaps' in his musing refers to how far back his retrievable memories reach, not to when the practice of inherited memory began. Notes that considering memories older than Ymar's suggests Severian has inside knowledge of the practice's history, since otherwise Ymar would naturally be assumed first. Agrees with Jane Delawney's doubt about the uncertainty of the memory reach.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian has inside knowledge of the memory tradition's history

I take it the 'perhaps' means Severian is unsure how far back the earliest memory he can retrieve goes - on the uncertainty of that I totally agree with you - not that he's unsure when the process of transmission started. That he should even consider the possibility of memories going back before Ymar suggests he has some inside knowledge about the history of the practice; if it weren't for that the natural thing to think would be that Ymar was the first.
