The thread: Fred Kiesche asked whether Samuel R. Delany admired Gene Wolfe, seeing parallels in pacing and feel between The Book of the New Sun and the Neveryon stories. Jerry Friedman and Gerry Quinn argued for shared interests and influences rather than direct influence, noting Delany's earlier publication record. Nessun Saprà offered a textual link, a 'melody machete' reference in Wolfe's knife-throwing story. Marc Aramini and David Stockhoff argued that Delany's concern is power, language and social construction rather than religion. The thread then drifted into Delany's political views on man-boy love, where Dan'l Danehy-Oakes and aaron disagreed, and it ended without consensus on the Wolfe question.
  • Fred Kiesche: sees Wolfe-like pacing and feel in Neveryon, suspects a shared influence such as Borges
  • Jerry Friedman: Delany and Wolfe are near opposites, though they share Proust, Nabokov and the pulps
  • Marc Aramini: Neveryon lacks Wolfe's religious obsession and centres on postmodern power relations
  • Gerry Quinn: shared interests are likelier than influence, since Delany published first
  • Nessun Saprà: Wolfe's 'A Few Points About Knife Throwing' cites Delany's melody machete, a direct connection
  • David Stockhoff: Delany treats words, language and symbols as power and dismisses religion as another power source

Themes: Power, rulers and politics, Books, libraries and writing
