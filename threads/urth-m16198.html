The thread: Roy Lackey shared a YouTube compilation whose final segment came from the film Death of Doctor Island, and the thread turned into a question of whether the movie was ever released. Stuart Hamm, who said he helped fund filming, replied from personal experience, while James Wynn and Matthew Keeley traded jokes about its release prospects. Jeff Wilson argued the film stalled in postproduction because of costly CGI, and he and Gerry Quinn speculated about motives and whether aging actors could be replaced with body scans. Son Of Witz and Wilson then debated whether greenscreen filmmaking and rising post-production costs would doom more films. The thread drifted from the film itself into general speculation about the economics of filmmaking, with no settled answer on the movie's status.
  • Jeff Wilson: postproduction stalled because CGI effects were too expensive, and the effects are the film's main purpose
  • Stuart Hamm: helped fund filming and never received promised stills, so he speaks from firsthand experience
  • James Wynn: jokingly suggests the unreleased film might be sold to cable or held back
  • Son Of Witz: virtual reshoots from body scans only work if planned from the start, and greenscreen without post budget signals trouble
  • Matthew Keeley: nudity and low resolution would keep it off TV
  • Roy C. Lackey: passes along the compilation link and cannot view video on dial-up

Themes: Books, libraries and writing

2011-04-29 Son of Witz replying to Jeff Wilson #

Argues that Gerry Quinn's idea of filling in later scenes of the film Death of Doctor Island with virtual reshoots from body scans only works if the film was planned that way from the start. Points to greenscreen shooting without a post-production budget and predicts that more films will run into trouble with post costs.
disputes

For Gerry's take to work, it would have had to been imagined like that from the beginning. With footage shot like "yeah, we'll comp that background in later" or, "react to this non-existent thing on set" there is only so much you can do without a post production budget.

Actually, I've long wondered how many movies will flounder at this stage the more they make movies on greenscreen sets. I've always figured people would start getting in waaaaaaay over their heads in regards to what they will actually be able to afford in post production.

~witz
