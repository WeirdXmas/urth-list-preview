2010-07-20 John Watkins replying to Matthew Keeley #

Argues that Ymar's near-saintly name fits a Scholastic view of virtue, in which earthly justice is only a participation in higher, eternal virtues. Responds to Matthew Keeley's Buddha-like reading by recasting Ymar's goodness through Scholastic theology of degrees of justice, without citing any passage from the books.
proposes · theology-and-religion · theories: Ymar's name reflects Scholastic participation in eternal virtues

In Scholastic thought, the virtues as we know them, including justice, are not real things in themselves, but human participation in eternal virtues (the names of those escape me.)

Humans in Heaven will attain these eternal virtues, but will find that even they are only the lesser shadows of divine virtues.

So ever someone who is "just" on Earth is not "just" in the same sense as someone who is "just" in Heaven, and even that person would not be "just" in the sense that God is just.
