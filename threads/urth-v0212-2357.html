Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Stories within stories

2002-12-04 Marc Aramini #

Argues that the Book of Canog exists before Typhon makes the Whorl, so Typhon could have read Severian's whole story through it, and that Talos later copied it into the Book of the New Sun for his play. Responds to Blattid's claim about how Pas and others heard of Severian's drowning, and grounds the claim in what Severian may have told Canog and Ymir while imprisoned.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · puzzle-solving · theories: Typhon heard Severian's story through the Book of Canog; Talos copied Severian's story into the Book of the New Sun

In response to Blattid's assertion as to where Pas and everybody may have heard of Sev's drowning:
Well, well well - here we have it: the book of Canog is available to Typhon, maybe even before he makes the whorl - and since it is, that means perhaps the ENTIRE story of Severian is available to the whorl- we don't known exactly what he said to Canog or Ymir while incarcerated, but it is his story, which was later re-copied into the Book of the New Sun that Talos copies for his play. Typhon heard it from Severian himself, through Canog, far before Sev was ever born. Not that that will convince you or anything, I know it won't. Shouting in previous posts was just for emphasis - I have no means of placing italics in this mailing program. Sorry.
Marc Aramini
