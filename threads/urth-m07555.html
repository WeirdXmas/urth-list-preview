Hut/Ultan's Library/Catherine

Urth list · 22 messages · 2009-04-05 to 2009-04-08

2009urthThe Book of the New SunJohn WatkinsJosh GellerDavid StockhoffJames WynnStanisław BocianSon of WitzJeff Wilsonb sharp
The thread: Participants debated whether Ultan's library and the dead woman tied to Severian's mother Catherine, Ultan's cannibalism, and why Severian feels dead there. B sharp argued Ultan's corpse-meal links to Vodalus and Catherine, while Stanislaus B. and Jeff Wilson disputed the reading and the proposed corpse-eating. Jeff Wilson and James Wynn pushed the barrow-wight and clone theories, which Wynn and John Watkins questioned and which drifted into Ouen, khaibit clones and the Spring Wind beans. Little was settled; the thread ended in impasse and wandered off into sexuality and the tribade remarks.
  • b sharp: the library links by portal to the House Absolute, and the corpse may be Catherine tortured for adultery
  • Stanislaus B.: Vodalus chose the corpse as an easy victim so eating her would bind his followers
  • Jeff Wilson: Ultan resembles a barrow wight or Norse dwarf, and Severian feels dead from the tomblike cellar
  • James Wynn: Severian's sense of death has a literal cause in a clone extension of Dogget's theory
  • David Stockhoff: the entombed library makes Severian feel dead, though the reading is a stretch
  • David Stockhoff: the tribade talk reflects libertine desire to experience heterosexual sex from a woman's view

Themes: Ghosts, dreams and visions, Robots, androids and artificial life, Doubles, twins and clones

2009-04-05 b sharp #

Proposes that Ultan's library connects by magical portal to Rudesind's art gallery and the House Absolute, and that the dark-haired woman seen on the Path of Air may be the same woman as the Necropolis corpse and Severian's mother Catherine. Grounds this in the Hut thread's description of the Library's size, Tzadkiel's picture-door transport, and Severian's remark about Father Inire's model.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Ultan's library links to Rudesind's gallery by portal; The dark-haired woman, the corpse and Catherine are one person

Well a somewhat fruitful thread, for me at least.

Regarding Duko Rigoglio's rant, I too agree that it might describe both the large natural size of Nessus and the Library and the magical stuff. I just find the hints of magical stuff more interesting: "A city larger than the whorl on which it stands", "countless towers" "tunnels without end" etc.

I liken Ultan's library to Rudesind's art gallery so I suspect a magical portal between them and the House Absolute. I don't think we are meant to think Rudesind walked the same distance as Severian for their second meeting in Father Inire's wing of the House Absolute. I don't think the mechanism is like the mirrors but more like a picture on the wall you step through. Tzadkiel uses something like this to transport Severian from her Captain's Room to his stateroom. Severian says of it, "We have such things in the House Absolute. I see whence Father Inire took his model, though ours are not so well contrived."

I've always wondered about why Severian sees the face of the dark haired, pale gown wearing corpse from the Necropolis grave in Ultan's face. Ultan really likes the idea of eating the dead and that's what Vodalus and his followers did with that corpse. Something weird is going on there. She must have been someone important for vodalus to go through so much trouble to get that particular corpse.

On the day the House Absolute is drowned, Severian sees a woman being taken on the Path of Air. She has dark hair and wears a pale gown like the corpse. She also has olive skin and an oval face, (like his mother Catherine,the Pellerine? Same woman, all three?) Anyway, my only guess had been that the Path Of Air led to the top of Valeria's tower allowing the dark haired woman to be brought back to the time of Severian's youth.

Jeff Wilson's reminder of the 49 floors of the Library allows me to consider that the Path Of Air leads to an upper floor of the Library. This could allow for the connection between Ultan and the mysterious dark haired woman.

-bsharp

2009-04-05 b sharp replying to b sharp #

Argues that the corpse Ultan eats in The Book of the New Sun is not random but a loyal follower of Vodalus, and proposes that she is Catherine, tortured to death for adultery. Builds the case from the pale gown and resemblance linking the corpse, the Path of Air woman and Catherine, the strangling by Allowin's necklace, and the Cyriaca parallel, while responding to Stan's suggestion that Ultan is a secret corpse-eater.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: The Necropolis corpse is Catherine; Catherine was tortured to death for adultery; Vodalus chose loyal followers as alzabo feasts; Ultan has secretly eaten corpses before

Interesting ideas. I had never noticed anyone assume the fact of Ultan as a Vodararian so casually. Ultan is asked by
Severian about the "corpse-eaters" and he replies:

"It is unwise to know too much about these practices, though when I think of sharing the mind of a historian like Loman, or Hermas..."

Severian then sees on his face an "agony of desire".

Ultan continues, "But from what I once read, you are correct, though I do not now recall that the book you hold treats of it.

I always took all that to mean Ultan had never indulged in corpse eating but would like to. I guess Stan feels Ultan is lying and is actually quite familiar and that is the source of Severian seeing the corpse's face in Ultan. I would agree that a nice possibility.

I disagree that the corpse is a random pick. First it is too much trouble for Vodalus to get it when there would seem to be no shortage of freshly dead random bodies all over the Commonwealth. I think the alzabo is very dear and they don't feast often; perhaps only once every couple years. Moreover Thecla's body seems to have been kept on ice to preserve it until the feast. As I understand it, she was picked and preserved with such care because she was loyal to Vodalus and he hoped to imbue this loyalty to his feasters. I must assume the Necropolis corpse was likewise a person loyal to Vodalus (and I think the poorest people were buried further down, closest to the river).

Anyway, when I mention that the corpse must be an important person I mean important to the story, more than in social status. That she is Catherine isn't very easy to argue but here are the puzzle pieces I put together:

There is the physical resemblance and the pale gown to tie the three women (corpse, Path of Air woman and Catherine) together as one. I suspect the cause of death of the corpse is choking by Allowin's necklace as that torture device is mentioned more than it needs to be and she has a livid face. Before the Thecla eating feast, Vodalus' followers express hope she isn't another lesbian. I think this suggests the Necropolis corpse was a lesbian.

For me, this may be the reason Catherine didn't stay with the Pelerines and perhaps didn't stay true to some high placed marriage (I suspect Ouen was a refuge not a love interest). I think she was tortured to death for adultery and the St. Katherine maid was drawn from a version of her from the past each year. Part of this thinking comes from Cyriaca's situation. She is ordered to be tortured to death, for adultery. The method: strangulation (Severian didn't have an Allowin's necklace in Thrax). I guess there are family connections all through this scenario. (Catherine being sister to Cyriaca, Severian being cousing to Agia and Agilus etc.)

Just my attempt to tie all these random tidbits together into some comprehensible back story because I have always had the feeling Wolfe doesn't really use a lot of random tidbits in his stories.

-bsharp

2009-04-05 Stanisław Bocian replying to b sharp #

Argues against b sharp's claim that Ultan's link to the dead woman's corpse is weird, holding that Vodalus chose her as an easy victim so eating her would bind his followers. Grounds this in quoted livid-woman passage, noting Ultan is a Vodalarian who ate part of the corpse and she was buried among the poor and guarded by volunteers.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Vodalus chose the corpse as an easy victim; Eating the corpse bound Vodalus's followers like a gang crime; Ultan's cannibalism is not unusual among Vodalarians

Hello!

...
...

"Again I seemed to hear bronze, and quite suddenly I felt that he and I were dead, and that the darkness surrounding us was grave soil pressing in about our eyes, grave soil through which the bell called us to worship at whatever shrines may exist below ground. The livid woman I had seen dragged from her grave rose before me so vividly that I seemed to see her face in the almost luminous whiteness of the figure who spoke. "

Why weird? Ultan is a Vodalarian, and has eaten part of the corpse. He is hardly a great exception.

The woman was buried among the poor, and guarded by volunteers. It seems she was chosen by Vodalus as a low-hanging fruit. He needed to have some body to eat, in order to cement the faith of his subordinates. This is a fairly common idea in gangs and cults - the crime committed together serves to bind a new member to the organization.

Responds to James Wynn's question about why Severian and Ultan both feel dead, offering the setting and mythic comparison as the grounds for the pairing.
answers · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Ultan resembles a barrow wight or Norse dwarf

A few months ago we went over this - they meet in unlit underground tunnels, essentially catacombs for books instead of bodies. Ultan is also very much like the barrow wights or dwarves of the Norse epics, essentially a barely animated corpse guarding grave goods and other treasures.

Proposes that if Severian is a clone, Ouen must be one too, and that Dorcas is Ouen's birth mother and possibly Sev's grandmother through a marriage to Sev's birth mother. Supports this by citing Wolfe's other mismatched family titles, such as Mucor as Marble's granddaughter and Number Five calling Maitre his father, and by noting the many Number Fives in The Fifth Head of Cerberus.
proposes · puzzle-solving · theology-and-religion · theories: Ouen is a clone of Severian; Dorcas is Ouen's birth mother and Sev's grandmother; Severian's patrilineal heritage are clones of himself

I would presume that it would have to be a given in that case that Ouen is a clone too (given his resemblance to Sev), and that Dorcas is Ouen's birth mother and Ouen is likely the husband of Sev's birth mother. This seems the most obvious route to Dorcas becoming Sev's "grandmother".

Remember that Mucor was the "granddaughter" of Marble and that the Raja said Jahlee "could be" the sister of Hide (or maybe Hoof--I can't remember right now) and that Number Five called Maitre his father and Jeanine his "aunt". All this is fair warning that that familial titles in a Wolfe story should be read with suspicion or at least laxity. There are mythical/theological reasons IMO that lead Wolfe to choose to make familial titles to be true/untrue in his stories.

Why would an important clone like Ouen be a commoner in Nessus? There were dozens (or hundreds) of Number Fives running around in The Fifth Head of Cerberus. If Ouen was married to Sev's birth mother, I guess it implies a more deliberate plot, however. Further, once you move down this corridor, the possibility cannot be ignored that all of Sev's patrilineal heritage are clone's of himself, so that amnesiac Dorcas found it very easy to take Sev as her lover. But all this, is not part of Don's theory. It is just a potential logical extension of it.

J.
