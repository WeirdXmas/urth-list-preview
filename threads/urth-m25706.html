The thread: Thread on Jeff VanderMeer's Southern Reach volume Authority, which is off-topic for the Wolfe list. Antonin Scriabin opened by asking whether others had read it and described its slow, eerie pacing compared with Annihilation. Fred Kiesche and Piotr Szczęsny mostly reported their own reading status, with Kiesche still waiting to read it and Szczęsny having put it on hold. Craig Brewer offered the only substantive view, arguing the middle volume mainly sets up the third book and praising its weirdness and the character Whitby, while holding final judgment until Acceptance. The thread stayed a light exchange of reading notes with no real debate and no resolution.

  • Fred Kiesche: has not read it yet and will get to it after other queued books

Themes: Ghosts, dreams and visions

2014-05-20 Antonin Scriabin #

Asks whether others have started Authority, the second Southern Reach volume by Vandermeer, and reports being halfway through it. Describes its slower, eerie pacing against Annihilation, and the poster's curiosity about where the strangeness goes; the post is off-topic and does not concern Wolfe.
chats

Has anyone started the second volume of the Southern Reach trilogy yet? I'm about halfway through. It is much slower than the first volume ( *Annihilation)*, but that is clearly intentional. There is a sort of slow, rolling eeriness to it that plays off the faster-paced first volume really well. Vandermeer knows how to feed little tidbits to keep it interesting, even when it meanders. I like *Authority* quite a bit, and am really curious to see where the hell all this strangeness is going.

2014-05-21 Craig Brewer replying to Piotr Szczęsny #

Argues that Authority is an enjoyable read whose main purpose is to set up the third volume, praising the weirdness and the character Whitby. Compares it favorably to Annihilation's handling of the weird and expects Acceptance to conclude the trilogy, noting the judgment of the middle book must wait. Replies to Piotr Szczęsny's post, which is not quoted beyond the list footer, and concerns Jeff VanderMeer's Southern Reach trilogy rather than Wolfe.
chats

Authority is a fun ride, but much of what it shows seems to be setting up for the third. There's plenty of fun weirdness along the way, but I thought Annihilation played up the "Weird" brilliantly. I expect that Acceptance will cap it off well. Odd to say, but I feel like I won't know what I think about the second one until I read the third.

I thought Whitby was an amazingly drawn...character? Person suffering and trying to maintain himself? But his appearances were always my favorite.
