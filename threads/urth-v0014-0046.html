1998-06-18 Paul C Duggan #

Argues that Severian the writer is himself a torturer, so his comparison of torturers to authors with readers explains why he manipulates readers' minds, and that he becomes more forthcoming after passing the test on Yesod. Builds on a quoted claim that Severian likens torturer-client ties to author-reader ties, while the poster doubts the author-reader comparison generally.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian the writer is a torturer toward readers

Whether Wolfe wants to torture us or not, Severian the writer IS a torturer and if he thinks that that is the kind of relationship an author has with his reader it's not suprising that he messes with our minds in such a fashion.

And after his passing the test on Yesod, he becomes much more forthcoming.

hmmm..

paul

1998-06-19 William H. Ansley #

Argues that the torment readers feel in Wolfe's fiction is not unique to Severian, since most of Wolfe's work is torturous in the same way and sometimes more so. Responds to a quoted post claiming Severian as writer torments his readers, noting that Wolfe's nonfiction is by contrast fairly straightforward.
disputes · author-and-biography · theories: Wolfe's fiction torments readers throughout, not only via Severian

You can't just blame it on Severian. Most, if not all, of Wolfe's fiction is torturous in the same way and, in a few cases, possibly to a greater degree.

His nonfiction tends to be pretty straightforward, though.

William Ansley
