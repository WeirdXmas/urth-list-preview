Fifth Head and Baldanders

Urth list · 1 messages · 1998-04-05 to 1998-04-05

1998urthThe Book of the New SunDan Rabin

Themes: Gods, angels and the Increate, Doubles, twins and clones

1998-04-05 Dan Rabin #

Argues that Baldanders and the family in The Fifth Head of Cerberus share thematic traits: laboratories, the quest for knowledge, experiments on their own bodies or clones, and frustration at fruitless labor. Adds that Baldanders has cut himself off from the Increate and that the final child could be the fifth head if the original and Mr. Million are counted as one, accepting Robert Borski's head-counting scheme.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · close-textual-reading · theories: Final child is the fifth head; Original and Mr. Million are one head; Baldanders cut himself off from the Increate

Baldanders and the family portrayed in _Fifth Head_ seem to have a lot in common, thematically. They maintain laboratories, seek knowledge, and hope to lift themselves above the common lot thereby. Both experiment on their own bodies (or clones) in pursuit of the quest. Both are frustrated that their labors bear no fruit.

I can't find the citation right now, but I recall a comment that Baldanders has cut himself off from the line that leads back to the Increate. He claims himself as his greatest, and only great work. Self-created, not self-ruler (autarch).

About the number of heads, I accept Robert Borski's persuasive scheme, but if we count the original and Mr. Million as the same head, then the child at the end could be the fifth.

-- Dan Rabin
