Earth to Urth Pt 3: Addenda

The thread: Nigel Price proposed three readings: that "Our Neighbour by David Copperfield" is a Dickensian pastiche that may sketch The Book of the New Sun, that "Free Live Free" allegorizes grace through its lodging motif, and that the Oz imagery in "The Eye Flash Miracles" is an imperfect vocabulary through which Little Tib perceives the divine. Peter Stephenson engaged the grace reading, arguing that the characters' aimless blundering makes salvation seem iffy, then backed away from it as going too far. Peter Westlake and Stephenson mostly traded Carry On casting jokes for The Book of the New Sun characters, with Westlake adding lines for Jonas and Hethor. The thread ended in levity rather than resolution, and the grace debate was dropped.

  • Peter Stephenson: the grace reading makes salvation seem iffy, though he retreated from it as too far
  • Peter Westlake: playful Carry On casting for The Book of the New Sun, with added lines for Jonas and Hethor

1999-05-06 Nigel Price #

Argues that Wolfe's "Our Neighbour by David Copperfield" is a Dickensian pastiche that may sketch The Book of the New Sun, and that "Free Live Free" allegorizes grace through its lodging motif. Also proposes that the Wizard of Oz imagery in "The Eye Flash Miracles" is an imperfect symbolic vocabulary through which Little Tib perceives the divine, citing Ansley's posts and recalled essays by Gevers and Groce.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · source-and-allusion · theories: Free Live Free allegorizes grace; Wizard of Oz images are Little Tib's imperfect symbols of the divine

#Addendum 1

Meant to mention, in connection with Wolfe's use of Dickens and his imitation of Dickens' realism, the interesting story "Our Neighbour by David Copperfield", which is included in "Endangered Species".

First published in 1978, the story is an example of Wolfe explicitly practising a Dickensian pastiche. I wonder whether we should regard this short piece as a sketch for certain aspects of the larger canvas on which Wolfe was beginning to paint in the late Seventies, namely The Book of the New Sun?

#Addendum 2

Also meant to mention the thematic connection between the free board and lodging provided for the young couple in "Kevin Malone" with the titular free board and lodging provided in "Free Live Free". Any one else agree that the latter is also, in some perverse and deeply fascinating Lupine way, an allegory of Grace?

#Addendum 3

Who was it who wrote that brilliant essay on the list recently in which they explained Wolfe's use of masks, and how, as a Catholic, Wolfe would regard God as able to use all sorts of imperfect images and agents as symbols of his power and love? It was very good, and I must lok it up again...

(After a brief search: my poor memory - more Latro than Severian - tends to conflate and distort list messages and essays over time, but I think that I was recalling excellent postings from Nicholas Gevers and Alex Groce. Thank you, gentleman - and everyone else who joined in - a fascinating discussion. Well, I liked it, anyway!)

My suspicion is that that is what is probably happening in "The Eye Flash Miracles", as so well detailed in William H. Ansley's recent posts. The references to "The Wizard of Oz" seem to act as an imperfect symbollic vocabulary through which Little Tib perceives something of the divine and the "spiritual" battle taking place around him. Presumably the images come from "The Wizard of Oz" books either because Little Tib had read the books or had them read to him, so that the images were latent in his memory, or because the images partake of some sort of archetypal symbolic system universally accessible to children.

It's late, what am I talking about?

Nigel Price
Minety, Wiltshire

1999-05-07 Peter Stephenson #

Riffs on Nigel Price's suggestion that Free Live Free allegorizes grace, arguing that the characters blunder about aimlessly before receiving a second chance, which makes salvation seem an iffy notion. Jokingly casts British comic actors from the Carry On films as the figures, then backs away from the grace reading as going too far.
chats · allegory-and-typology · theories: Free Live Free allegorizes grace

Actually, maybe that's the only way to understand it... the characters blunder around senselessly for most of the novel so that even Free himself gets infuriated, and at the end are given a second chance anyway. But it's a pretty iffy notion of salvation... a second chance to the same things all over again, or maybe not. It's as if grace should lead one into doing works... isn't that almost what you were saying before?

No, I'm sorry, that's going too far. That leaves Joan Sims for Dorcas and Kenneth Williams and Hattie Jacques to play Dr Talos and Jolenta ("Ohhhh, maaytron!")

corncrake
