The thread: Posters debated whether the unnamed family in The Fifth Head of Cerberus is surnamed Wolfe, a theory built on the house name la maison du chien, the aunt Jeanette, and a homophone of Gene. John Barach and JWillard argued for it, with JWillard reporting that Wolfe confirmed it in person and citing a Clute anecdote. Maru Dubshinki pushed back, arguing the Clute quote only says the narrator 'could' be Wolfe and that the library-book and Jeanette clues conflict with the theory. Chris favored an English surname over a French one, and Jeff Wilson proposed Maitre's books as Wolfe's notes and Dr. Veil as an assumed name, answering the objections by noting Wolfe had published no books when he wrote the original story. The thread ended without resolution.
  • John Barach: family surname is Wolfe, via la maison du chien, Jean/Gene homophone, and Jeanette
  • JWillard: Wolfe confirmed in person the family surname is Wolfe, citing Clute's Strokes
  • Maru Dubshinki: Clute quote only says narrator 'could' be Wolfe; library and Jeanette clues weigh against it
  • Chris: surname beginning with W is more likely English than French, from a later settler wave
  • Jeff Wilson: Maitre's books are Wolfe's notes; Dr. Veil is an assumed name; Wolfe had no books when writing the story
  • Los Arboles Del Frio: noted chien means dog and loup means wolf

Themes: Unreliable narration, Names and their meanings, Masks, disguise and shapeshifting

2005-08-23 Maru Dubshinki #

Argues that the theory of the unnamed family in The Fifth Head of Cerberus being surnamed Wolfe clashes with the three-headed dog imagery, since Cerberus is a dog and the family's house is called the house of the dog. Notes the only clue is the children calling the house the house of the dog, and asks whether the archives resolved this difficulty.
questions · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: The unnamed family in The Fifth Head of Cerberus is surnamed Wolfe

As usual, I've been reading through the archives again; I've noticed that one of the more
popular theories for Fifth Head of Cerberus is that the unnamed family is surnamed "Wolfe".
I can see the attraction of this, in an novel concerned with identity, and with the only obvious
clear clue to the surname the statement "the kids called our house "la maison du chien"" (
to quote from memory) in the first novella. But this has a problem with solving as Wolfe.
Cerberus is a dog; the three-headed beast is a dog. "Wolfe"=wolfe !=dog != Cerberus/three-headed
dog in the front of the mansion.
The archives don't tell me how this difficulty was resolved, if it was, or even if it ever came up.

~Maru

2005-08-23 Chris P replying to Los Arboles Del Frio #

Argues that the Fifth Head of Cerberus family's surname, which begins with W and relates to dog, is more likely English than French since the family comes from a later English-speaking settler wave. Responds to Los Arboles Del Frio's remark that chien means dog and loup means wolf, noting the name would be English if evocative of dogs and admitting it brings Wolfe to mind.
disputes · source-and-allusion · theories: Fifth Head family surname is English, not French

If I recall correctly we know that the last name begins with a "W", and has some sort of association with "dog". While the original settlers in the system were French-speaking, this particular family is from a later wave, from someplace English-speaking. So if it were a word evocative of dogs, you would expect it to be in English word.

Personally I don't see much importance in this little tidbit, but when I read it "Wolfe" was indeed the first name that came to mind.

2005-08-23 John Barach replying to Maru Dubshinki #

Argues that the unnamed family in The Fifth Head of Cerberus is surnamed Wolfe, building on the house name la maison du chien, the library shelving near Virginia Woolf, and the aunt Jeanette to suggest the father is Jean, a homophone of Gene. Cites Wolfe's essay on dogs as tame wolves and credits Robert Borski with the theory, then asks whether Borski's website is back up.
supports · puzzle-solving · source-and-allusion · theories: The family surname in 5HC is Wolfe; Number 5's father is Jean, homophone of Gene

The name of the house is not the only obvious clear clue as to the family surname. We're also told that Number 5's father has written lots of books and that Number 5 looks for them when he visits the library. He clearly expects the books to be in the fiction section, since that's where he's looking, and the last name they're under is in the W section near Virginia Woolf (if I recall correctly).

So we're looking for a last name that is related to the French word "chien" (dog) and begins with a W. There's only one I can think of and that's "wolf." Doesn't Wolfe have an essay somewhere (perhaps in Castle of Days) where he talks about how dogs are just tame wolves?

Furthermore, we're also told that Number 5's aunt is named Jeanette. Given that she's the sister of Number 5's father and that both of them are therefore likely to be clones of the grandfather, the theory is that Number 5's father is probably named "Jean," the French version of John. But pronounced in an English way, "Jean" is homophonic with "Gene."

That, at least, is the theory set forth (again: if I recall correctly) by Robert Borski. Speaking of which, is his Fifth Head website up again yet?

2005-08-23 JWillard replying to John Barach #

Reports that Wolfe has confirmed in person that the unnamed family in The Fifth Head of Cerberus is surnamed Wolfe, citing a passage from John Clute's Strokes about a student's question. Adds the poster's recollection that French chien can mean wolf as well as dog, linking the house name la maison du chien to the surname.
informs · puzzle-solving · source-and-allusion · theories: The narrator of The Fifth Head of Cerberus is named Wolfe

I believe this is one of the few things that Wolfe has actually confirmed in person...but I don't remember where it was right off. Maybe in Strokes, by John Clute...Yeah, here's the quote:

"A member of the class then asked a question about The Fifth Head of Cerberus. Certain clues about the narrator's name seemed to lead in one particular direction, he said. Given these clues, could the narrator's name really be Gene Wolfe? Gene glanced at the student in mild surprise. "Why of course," he said, as though he were the last person in the world to have a secret."

I thought I read somewhere that chien's predominant meaning was dog, but could also be wolf. But I don't remember for sure...

2005-08-25 Jeff Wilson replying to Maru Dubshinki #

Proposes that Maitre's books are Wolfe's private notes on vivisection and world takeover, and that Dr. Veil, meaning 'that which hides', is an assumed name, perhaps through a marriage to a Mr. Veil. Responds to Maru Dubshinki's doubts about the Number 5 book problem and Jeanette's identity by noting that Wolfe had published no books when he wrote the original 5HC story.
proposes · puzzle-solving · author-and-biography · theories: Maitre's books are Wolfe's private notes on vivisection; Veil is an assumed name; Jeanette married a Mr. Veil to aid the takeover plot

At the time Wolfe wrote the original story, he had not yet published any books to my knowledge. Maitre's books are probably his private volumes of notes on vivisection and theories of how best to take over the world.

Veil (that which hides) could easily be an assumed name. She may even have married a Mr. Veil in an attempt to further the planetary takeover plot.
