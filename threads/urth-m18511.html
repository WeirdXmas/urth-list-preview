2011-01-25 Dave Tallman replying to Mr Thalassocrat #

Proposes that Rick in Home Fires was uploaded with a scan of Master Sergeant Mort Pununto, the spy Chelle shot, so his drive to kill her is revenge. Grounds the theory in the detailed account of Pununto's execution, where Chelle alone aimed to hit him, and argues that story has no other purpose.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Rick was uploaded with Mort Pununto's scan; Rick kills Chelle out of revenge

to kill Chelle?

My theory is revenge. Rick may have been uploaded with a scan of Master Sergeant Mort Pununto, the spy Chelle killed. He was the best noncom she ever had. A good officer (promoted from the ranks) and an talented spy would be valuable skills for a cyborg to possess, whether he spied for the EU or the Os. When Mort was caught as a spy Chelle was the only member of the firing squad to aim to hit him, so she was directly responsible for his death. I see little other reason for Wolfe to have included this otherwise completely extraneous story in detail.

2011-01-26 thalassocrat replying to Son of Witz #

Argues that Home Fires is best read as a character novel rather than a plot novel, with decent character work but a recycled-feeling world and plot furniture. Compares it unfavourably to The Sorcerer's House and to the first half of An Evil Guest, answering Son of Witz's question about whether he enjoyed the book at an entertainment level.
answers · genre-and-craft

I did, with caveats. I read it mainly as a "character" novel, rather than a "plot" novel. That's fine for me, but while some of the character work is pretty good, Wolfe really isn't top-notch as a character writer, IMO (Having been on a heavy Trollope diet for a while, I'm spoiled in this regard). The world and plot "furniture" all has a slightly recycled feeling; if you didn't know better, it'd be easy to guess that it was one of those dystopian future novels from the 1970's.

Overall, I'd say not as enjoyable as Sorcerer's House, but more enjoyable than the first half of AEG (ie, before the beautiful writing of the second half kicks in and the deli sandwiches disappear).

2011-01-28 Matthew Keeley replying to Dave Tallman #

Argues that Wolfe's detail of Chelle's firing squad gives insight into her mental state, since readers learn of her shooting Mort only at the book's end. Playfully raises doubt about Mort's existence by noting that the dead spy's name is 'Mort', in reply to Dave Tallman's theory that Rick was uploaded with Mort's scan.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Rick was uploaded with Mort Pununto's scan

I think one reason Wolfe includes this detail is to give us a little insight into Chelle's mental state; when first we hear of Mort, we just hear that Chelle shot him. We don't learn that she was in a firing squad until the last page of the book.

Of course, if we want to introduce crazy conspiracy theories (we know they'll come out eventually), we could cast some doubt on Mort's existence. Isn't it strange, after all, that the dead spy is named "Mort?"

-Matt
