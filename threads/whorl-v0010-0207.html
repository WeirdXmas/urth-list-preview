2000whorlThe Book of the Long SunKevin J. MaroneyJames Jordan
The thread: The thread asked what secret Horn or Jahlee holds over the inhumi in The Book of the Long Sun, a question Wolfe's readers have long puzzled over. Jim Jordan proposed that the inhumi will adopt humanity's loving conduct because they imitate people, tying this to Quetzal's ambiguity. Kevin J. Maroney offered the In Green's Jungles cover's many-legged Neighbor as a hint for Jim's extra-legs idea, while conceding it proves nothing. Jordan later retracted his earlier guess, arguing the threat must be something immediately actionable rather than a general moral rule like the Golden Rule. The thread ended with Jordan's own revised reasoning, with no consensus reached.
  • Jim Jordan: inhumi will adopt human loving conduct because they imitate people
  • Kevin J. Maroney: cover image of a many-legged Neighbor hints at the extra-legs idea, but proves nothing
  • Jim Jordan: retracts his earlier suggestion; the secret must be doable on the spot, not a general moral rule

Themes: Beasts and the animal in us, Robots, androids and artificial life, Hidden identities and parentage

2000-01-15 James Jordan #

Proposes that the secret behind the inhumi in The Book of the Long Sun is that they will adopt humanity's loving and charitable conduct, since they imitate people. Grounds this in the inhumi being imitators of humanity, and explains Quetzal's ambiguity as the result of having learned from Patera Pike.
proposes · puzzle-solving · theology-and-religion · theories: Inhumi will follow humanity's loving and charitable example; Quetzal is ambiguous because he learned from Patera Pike

I still say that the secret (probably) is that when humans learn to live with each other in love and charity (not enslaving one another, for instance), the inhumi will follow suit, being imitators of humanity. That's why Quetzal is ambiguous, because he learned from Patera Pike.

2000-01-19 James Jordan #

Argues that the secret Horn holds over the inhumi cannot be a general moral rule such as the Golden Rule, since that would require universal cooperation and could be undone when one person defects. Responds to a prior post by Jim Jordan by reasoning that the threat must be something more immediate that the inhumi could be made to act on individually.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Horn's secret is not the Golden Rule; Horn's secret is something implementable without universal cooperation

It seems to me that your proposal has the same problem as the "golden rule" notion that I've been forced to reject. Everybody has to do it. It's just a kind of negative side of the golden rule. Yet what Horn-dude (whoever he is) threatens seems to be something more immediate. Also, both the golden rule proposal and your proposal mean that as soon as one person falls out of the system, he can be used by the inhumi again, and the "defeat" is undone.
I think that both the "golden rule" and the "universal protection" ideas are present and important in the book, but the "secret" would seem to be something that can be implemented without such univeral cooperation.

Nutria
