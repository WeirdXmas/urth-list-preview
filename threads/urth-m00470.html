Has anyone heard of C. S. Cooney?

Urth list · 9 messages · 2005-08-10 to 2005-08-13

2005urthJason WillardJWillardMaru DubshinkiDon Doggett
The thread: Jason Willard and Maru Dubshinki asked whether C. S. Cooney, the poet credited in the Starwater Strains dedication for the title poem "Lady Rosemary," was real, after searches of Google, Wikipedia, ABE Books and the Library of Congress turned up nothing and a Seamus Cooney who wrote no poems. Maru also questioned whether the dedication's pronoun pointed to a pseudonymous group of writers. A reply relayed through David Hartwell and Wolfe's agent identified C. S. Cooney as Claire Cooney, a neighbor, fan and family friend who is a singer and poet. Maru voiced disbelief, while don doggett welcomed the answer and urged list members to seek her poetry. The thread ended in good humor, with Willard joking about sending her a poem and promising to buy a volume if one appeared.
  • Jason Willard: asked whether the poet was invented or a pseudonym, then accepted the agent's account with humor
  • Maru Dubshinki: doubted the poet was real and read the pronoun as a group pseudonym, then expressed disbelief at the answer
  • don doggett: welcomed the identification as a neighbor, fan and poet and urged readers to seek her poems

Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Masks, disguise and shapeshifting, Hidden identities and parentage

2005-08-10 Jason Willard #

Asks whether Gene Wolfe has invented a poet, C. S. Cooney, credited in the dedication of Starwater Strains as the source of the title poem "Lady Rosemary." Notes failed searches on Google, Wikipedia and ABE Books and wonders whether Wolfe used a pseudonym.
questions · source-and-allusion · theories: C. S. Cooney is a Wolfe pseudonym

I just got my copy of Starwater Strains and the dedication says the title is from a poem by C. S. Cooney called "Lady Rosemary." Rosemary I get, of course, but is Mr. Wolfe in the habit of making up poets? I can't find this poet mentioned in Google or Wikipedia or ABE Books. (As fabulous as they are, admittedly limited sources.) Am I missing something obvious? Has Wolfe used this as a pseudonym?

2005-08-10 JWillard replying to Maru Dubshinki #

Asks whether anyone knows of a poet named C. S. Cooney, whose poem "Lady Rosemary" supplies a book's title and a dedication. Lays out three possibilities: an obscure poet, a printing error, or a prank, after checking a Seamus Cooney who edited Bukowski letters but wrote no poems.
questions · source-and-allusion

It reads:

"The title of this book is taken from "Lady Rosemary," a poem by C. S. Cooney; and this book is dedicated to them."

That's it. Three possibilities (as far as I can see): Cooney is an obscure poet (and Wolfe does seem to have some obscure things in his library); something's misprinted; or someone's doing some leg-pulling. Thanks for the feedback!

2005-08-10 Maru Dubshinki replying to Jason Willard #

Suggests that the poem cited in Starwater Strains may not come from a real poet named C. S. Cooney, noting that a Seamus Cooney edits poetry and letters but has written no poems. Asks whether Wolfe included the poem in the book or only mentioned it, and briefly checks whether the name might be a code or anagram, finding none of note.
questions · puzzle-solving · source-and-allusion

There is a man of letters by the name of "Seamus Cooney". Cooney is not a common last name at all, and that fellow has at least one of the right initials. But he doesn't appear to have authored any poems, rather just edited a lot of poems and letters by such luminaries as Charles Bukowski; there is an Irish principal who likes poetry, but that seems unlikely; and it doesn't appear to be a code of some sort- at least, the most significant anagram is "Once Cosy". Perhaps you could copy the poem, if Wolfe included it (or did he simply mention it in passing?).

~Maru

2005-08-11 Maru Dubshinki replying to JWillard #

Questions whether the poet C. S. Cooney, credited in the dedication of Starwater Strains, is real, after failed searches of the Library of Congress. Argues that the dedication's pronoun "them" fits the author better as a pseudonym for a group of writers than as a reference to the title or to Rosemary.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: C. S. Cooney is a pseudonym for a group of writers

Well, I gave the Library of Congress a shot; no joy.
But something rings wrong about that text. "this book is dedicated to *them*"? Is that pronoun correct? There are three possible referents for that pronoun: "Starwater Strains" (unacceptable, since that is a very strange thing to anthromorphize, and I'm not even sure what starwater strains could be, anyways), Rosemary (well, Wolfe *could* be a herbal fanatic. It's possible, but improbable), and finally, C. S. Cooney, in which case the name could well be a pseudonym for a collective/duo/misc. group of writers, somewhat like Bourbaki is for mathematicians.

~Maru
