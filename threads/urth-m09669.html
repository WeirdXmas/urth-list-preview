The thread: The thread, which has no subject line, began as a sprawling political argument on the Urth list about property rights, with no reference to Wolfe's fiction. Brunians defended property rights as a matter of faith, attacked Rousseau's treatment of his children and defended Jefferson's conduct as necessity, and endorsed enforcing Native treaty rights. Jeff Wilson and Adam Thornton pressed back, calling Jefferson a moral criminal for keeping slaves, questioning the double standard applied to Rousseau, and asking about peoples driven from their land. Stuart Hamm argued file sharing is theft while Wilson disagreed. The exchange never touched Wolfe's books and ended in unresolved disagreement, with Wilson's last post disputing the Rousseau claims on historical grounds.

  • Jeff Wilson: Jefferson was a moral criminal for keeping slaves; calls the double standard on Rousseau out; file sharing is not theft though possibly wrong
  • Adam Thornton: asks whether intellectual property equals real property and whether downloading equals taking a television

2009-05-20 Josh Geller replying to Jeff Wilson #

Argues that Rousseau and Grover Cleveland were not examples of bad moral character, citing Rousseau's abandonment of his children to an orphanage and Cleveland's acknowledged illegitimate child. Responds to Jeff Wilson's jibe dismissing Buddha, Franklin and Cleveland by offering historical detail about each man's family circumstances.
disputes

Rousseau sent his infant children to an orphanage and did not check up on them. The death rate in 18th century French orphanages approached 100%.

Left his children in the care of his royally wealthy wife and parents.

Acknowledged and supported one legitimate child, and raised two others with his partner of many years (they never married).

This is an interesting one. Grover, famously, acknowledged having an illegitimate child during the presidential race. What actually happened was this: he and his law partner, Oscar Folsom, were hanging out with the same lady, who was a widow. She got pregnant. Oscar was married: Grover wasn't, so Grover 'did the right thing'. She named the child Oscar Folsom Cleveland. Many people have speculated that the child was actually Oscar's.

Sorry, I don't think any of these are examples bolster your argument.

disputes · author-and-biography · source-and-allusion
