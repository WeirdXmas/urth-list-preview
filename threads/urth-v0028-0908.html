Three Fingers

Urth list · 1 messages · 2000-04-17 to 2000-04-17

2000urthMichael Straight

2000-04-17 Michael Straight #

Argues that Disney's guarding of characters borrowed from older lore in 'Three Fingers' is undercut by Captain Hook's own borrowed origins in Blackbeard and Captain Kidd. Asks why the hero is not a dealer in pirate lore, and suggests the hallucinatory ending was an editor's addition to avoid Disney's lawyers.
proposes · source-and-allusion · author-and-biography · theories: Hallucinatory ending was added by an editor

I initially thought Wolfe had a cool idea here, pointing out the irony of Disney vigorously protecting the rights to characters that they've swiped from better sources and humanity's common story-telling tradition.

But then I'm led to ask that if Captain Hook is just a watered-down version of Blackbeard and Captain Kidd and Barbarosa, then shouldn't the hero of this story be someone who is a dealer in Blackbeard stories and Captain Kidd paraphernalia? Or conversely, if Mickey Mouse and Snow White are sufficiently distinguished from their literary ancestors to be worth stealing, it's a little less ironic when Disney objects to the theft.

I enjoy the ending more if I read it as Wolfe wanting us to imagine that the "it's all just an insane hallucination" bit was tacked on by the editor to avoid trouble from Disney's lawyers.

-Rostrum
