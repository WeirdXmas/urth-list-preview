2000-03-15 Nicholas Gevers #

Proposes that Typhon's reign on Urth was limited, since he cannot escape the rebels closing in, and that Urth's impoverishment by building the Whorl explains material and thematic details. Responds to Matthew and Thomas on the author's earlier Two Typhons note, and cites the Cargo's Urth origin as shown in In Green's Jungles.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Typhon's interstellar travel capability was limited; Urth was impoverished by building the Whorl; The Cargo are from Urth

Thanks to Matthew and Thomas for their responses to my
Two Typhons note. Let me just say that I was trying to
reconcile the physical details of Typhon's reign on
Urth (in URTH) with the nature of the Whorl. In URTH,
Typhon doesn't seem terribly much more powerful than
the succeeding Autarchs (who after all also carved
mountains in their own images). He can't escape when
the rebels close in, so his interstellar and even
interplanetary travel capability appears rather
limited. And Urth being impoverished by the needs of
the Whorl's construction would explain a lot, both
materially and thematically. And the Cargo are
definitely from Urth (see the forthcoming IN GREEN'S
JUNGLES).

2000-03-15 Daniel Fusch #

Proposes that Typhon was caught off guard by the revolt and was left stranded on his divan on the mountain, possibly abandoned by his own troops, rather than lacking travel capability. Responds to an earlier post's objection that Typhon cannot escape the rebels, drawing on the indications in The Book of the New Sun.
proposes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Typhon was surprised by the revolt and abandoned

It may be that the revolt took him by surprise. From the indications in The Book of the New Sun, he was left abandoned in his divan on the mountain, perhaps deserted there by his own troops.

Just a thought....
