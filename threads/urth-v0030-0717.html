2001-10-17 Adam Stephanides #

Informs the list of a little-discussed 2000 Wolfe story, "Pocketsful of Diamonds," about two foster children who visit a mysterious carnival and leave for the wider world. Compares it to "And When They Appear," notes its more hopeful ending, and describes the anthology Strange Attraction built around a supernatural ferris wheel sculpture.
informs

On the subject of new, or at least newish, Wolfe stories, there's a story by Wolfe entitled "Pocketsful of Diamonds" which I don't recall seeing discussed here; it was published in 2000, but I only saw it recently. The story is a fantasy story involving two children, living at a foster home, who visit a mysterious carnival. It's a bit like "And When They Appear": at the carnival the children meet figures who offer them knowledge and at the end they leave their quasi-parental home and go off into the wider world. The ending is more hopeful than was "Appears," though. It's a good story, I think, though a cryptic one.

The story appears in an anthology entitled STRANGE ATTRACTION, edited by Edward E. Kramer (Shadowlands Press, ISBN 1-930595-00-x; there's also a limited edition). The anthology is a rather peculiar theme anthology: it's based around a sculpture of some sort of supernatural carnival ride, a ferris wheel iirc (I don't recall the details well), and each author was requested to incorporate it into his or her story. Wolfe wisely downplays it.

Incidentally, does anyone else think it's odd that a writer of Wolfe's stature seems so ready to contribute to this sort of project? There were also the SANDMAN and CROW anthologies, and the Liavek stories he wrote.

--Adam

2001-10-18 Nicholas Gevers #

Praises the style of the short story "Pocketsful of Diamonds" as richer and more mythic than Wolfe's recent folksy voice, singling out its diction. Compares it unfavourably with "The Walking Sticks," judging that story's quasi-folksy narration as annoying, and says Wolfe should write more in this mode.
supports · genre-and-craft

I mentioned "Pocketsful of Diamonds" onlist last year,
very briefly. A good story--I tend to be guided in
assessing these short Wolfe tales by the strength of
their style; "The Walking Sticks" is written in that
annoying quasi-folksy voice that Wolfe too often
affects these days, whereas "Pocketsful" shows off his
rich, mythic diction rather well. We need more of that
from him...

--Nick Gevers.
