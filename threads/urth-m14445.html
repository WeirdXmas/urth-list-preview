The thread: Ranjit Bhatnagar, as list moderator, announced renewed rules keeping discussion to Wolfe's fiction and asked posters to keep original subject lines as threads drift. Gwern Branwen supported the rules and argued subject lines should change only when the topic really changes, since renamed threads escape muting and filters. Jeff Wilson disputed the subject-line request as unfair and inconsistent with Bhatnagar's earlier advice, and blamed digest-mode subscribers for broken threading. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes explained that digest headers, not topic shifts, caused the apparent subject changes. Jonathan Laidlow ended with a request to trim quoted material, and the thread drifted into list etiquette without resolution.
  • Ranjit Bhatnagar: keep original subject lines and merge replies into digests
  • Gwern Branwen: change subjects only on real topic change so readers can filter threads
  • Jeff Wilson: unfair to demand fixed subjects; digest subscribers cause the mess
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: subject changes come from digest formatting, not topic drift
  • Jonathan Laidlow: trim quoted material in replies

2010-06-07 Ranjit Bhatnagar #

Announces a renewed set of list rules: keep discussion directly tied to Wolfe's fiction, avoid general religion and atheism, and aim for about five messages per person per day. Issued by the list moderator as a list-administration notice, with no reading of Wolfe's books or response to another poster.
informs

The urth list is getting way too busy these days, so I'm gonna lay down the law again. Please send any comments or complaints directly to me <[email]> rather than to the list.

1. Please stick to the core mission of the list, which is discussion that *directly* relates to Gene Wolfe's fiction (with occasional diversions for notable events in the sci-fi and literary worlds). Religion in general and atheism in general are not suitable topics.

2. I'm setting an informal goal of no more than approximately five messages per person per day. It's ok to go over five now and then, but don't feel like you need to use all five every day, either. I'm not going to bother counting individual's messages, but I'm hoping that we'll get slower-paced and more focused discussions. If this doesn't slow the list down enough, I'll make it a technical restriction, which will be much more annoying for everyone. We'll see how it goes.

- Ranjit

2010-06-07 Ranjit Bhatnagar replying to Gwern Branwen #

Argues that posters should keep the original thread subject when replies drift, and should merge several replies into one digest-style message. Responds to Gwern Branwen's complaint that changed subjects bypass muting and filters, presenting the list's filing-system practice as officially encouraged.
proposes

This is also officially encouraged behavior. Think of it as a bureaucratic filing system - even if the topic of discussion drifts, keeping the subject the same helps keep the thread of thought together.

Also, if you have a lot of related messages to reply to, I encourage you to combine all your replies into one message, digest-style.

- ranjit

2010-06-08 Gwern Branwen replying to Jeff Wilson #

Argues that subject lines should change only when the topic genuinely changes, so readers who opt out of threads can find them, and rejects the charge of inconsistency. Replies to Jeff Wilson's quoted remark by saying he asked him offlist to review June's threads and cites a thread switching from 'travelling north' to a digest header as a subject edit with no topic change.
disputes

I see. Will you be extending your little anarchist free speech crusade to the *entirety* of netiquette? Or just the parts that people bring up?

I ask for subject changes to match genuine topic changes precisely so other people do not have to change what they write, to allow me to opt out. If they don't, then I'm forced to either unsubscribe entirely, or do my best to destroy each offending thread. I don't want to do either.

There's nothing inconsistent here, except in your head.

I asked you offlist to look at the list of threads for just June and tell me whether you thought each and every one was a genuine subject edit in response to a topic change. You didn't. So I'll provide an example from http://lists.urth.net/pipermail/urth-urth.net/2010-June/thread.html

What topic change, pray tell, is signaled by switching from 'travelling north' to 'Urth Digest, Vol 70, Issue 15'?

2010-06-08 Jeff Wilson replying to Gwern Branwen #

Argues that digest-mode subscribers who leave subject lines unedited are responsible for the thread mess, and that the list software could hold them accountable without penalizing everyone. Responds to Gwern Branwen's accusation of anarchist free-speech hypocrisy, denying an offlist message was ever sent and blaming digest mode for breaking threading.
disputes

Yes, the pro-moderation anarchist, that's me.

I didn't see any offlist messages from you, sorry.

That's a consequence of the list owner allowing digest mode to the people who habitually overlook the part of each digest that says something substantially similar to "When replying, please edit your Subject line so it is more specific than 'Re: Contents of GurpsNet-L digest...'" The list software is more than capable of being used to hold those people accountable for their oversights without penalizing everyone else.

I mean, digest mode has it uses, but aside from the subject mangling it also fubars the message threading that would make life much easier for you by letting you blacklist a conversation and never see replies in it ever again, regardless of what their subject lines say. *That* is not just a crime against notional nettiquette but violates internet technology standards as well as creating technical problems for people with strong presentation preferences like yourself.
