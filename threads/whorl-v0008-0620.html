1998-05-09 Brownes3 #

Asks why Maytera Mint seems to regard Urus as wholly evil, when she sees some good in everyone else, including Eland's devotion to the tunnel gods. Grounds the question in Remora's remark in the tunnel scene about the "false gods" gathered and sacrificed, and notes the point seems unaddressed on the list.
questions · close-textual-reading

First, I would like to thus _custodes_ responsible for maintaining these two sites. Having just finished reading Exodus and being confused :) , I am grateful to all for the many elucidations.

Second, a comment. In the scene in the tunnel where they are scrambling after Eland's bufes, Remora says, early on "These victims,eh?" "For, hem! Pas. So they are gathering up one set of "false gods" in order to sacrifice them to another "false god." I chuckled over that one and missed another bit that puzzles me. I haven't seen this directly addressed elsewhere?

I understand Urus has conspired in the assassination attempt, but does Maytera Mint consider him human pornography -- no redeeming social value? Everyone else she sees a little good -- Eland loves the false tunnel gods.

Why is Urus pure evil?

Rich Browne

1998-05-14 Peter Westlake #

Suggests that Urus may simply be evil, or that Maytera Mint distrusts him as a member of a hostile group rather than for any single act. Responds to Brownes3's question about why Urus is presented as pure evil in The Book of the Long Sun, asking whether that reading is too simplistic.
answers · close-textual-reading

Perhaps he just is? It could be that Mint knows he's plotting something, but it could also be that he is a member of a hostile group who she doesn't trust simply because he isn't a nice person. Or is that too simplistic?

-- the ever-simplistic,
Spectacled Bear.

Peter Westlake, Harlequin Ltd, Barrington Hall, Barrington, Cambs CB2 5RG.
