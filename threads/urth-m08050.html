Themes: Christ figures and messiahs

2009-02-10 O'Donnell, Tim (BOSI) #

Argues that Severian is a quasi-Christ, agreeing with earlier posters, and contrasts him with Jesus as a carpenter and fisher of men. Quotes a line from The Claw of the Conciliator where Severian is called Life and could take up another trade, noting Severian is a torturer who maneuvers into leadership rather than fishing for men.
supports · allegory-and-typology · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian is a quasi-Christ

I am fully on-board in the thoughts that Severian is a quasi-Christ (as has been said here a number of times and a number of different ways that he represents some of the aspects of Christ).

I posted this on the Gene Wolfe Book Club yesterday:

The following occurs in Claw:

"To me you're Life, and you're a
young man named Severian, and if you wanted to put on different clothes and become a carpenter or a fisherman, no one could stop you."

To me this indicates that Severian is like Christ in some ways but Wolfe is here drawing a difference between Jesus (carpenter by trade and fisher of men) and Severian (torturer by trade and doesn't so much fish men as wangle his way into position as head of the fish factory).

2009-02-10 O'Donnell, Tim (BOSI) replying to O'Donnell, Tim (BOSI) #

Proposes that Severian is less a Christ figure than a Saul-like figure, comparing his persecuting past as torturer with Paul's persecution of Jesus's followers and his gradual moral awakening, notably disbanding the guild at the end. Offers this tentatively, admitting limited knowledge of the Bible and Catholic teaching; the post is also cluttered with a bank disclaimer.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · theories: Severian is a Saul figure, not Christ

For those also posting over on the Gene Wolfe Book Club I have just posted something similar there but in brief,

I wonder if Severian is not so much Christ as Saul (Paul of Tarsus)?

Paul violently persecuted the followers of Jesus after all (could you say tortured them?) and then discovered Jesus/God and underwent a conversion.

In Severian's case there is no dramatic conversion but a gradual awakening to a new moral code (as exemplified by his disbanding his own guild at the end of the series).

Having said that, I could never claim to be well versed in the Bible or Catholic teachings so I could be way off on this...
