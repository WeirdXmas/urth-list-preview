Time Structures on Urth

Urth list · 11 messages · 2010-08-11 to 2010-08-11

2010urthThe Book of the New SunAllan AndersonRyan DunnLee BermanJames WynnJeff WilsonDan'l Danehy-Oakes
The thread: Lee Berman opened by proposing that Wolfe's Last House, Valeria's tower and the Jungle Hut form a family of time structures, with lower levels in the past and Earth lying in the future of Urth, citing Severian's failure to find Valeria's tower and Valeria's antiquated dress. Jeff Wilson disputed that the jungle hut is special, arguing any time travel lies in the Botanic Gardens as a whole and pointing to Robert seeing Severian in real time and an anachronistic airplane. Ryan Dunn and Ryan Dunn's later post extended the idea with mirrors that alter time in the Botanic Gardens, and James Wynn argued Baxter's window exit leads into a spirit world, which Dunn corrected to a sneaking-out rather than a jump. The thread ended with mutual considerations and Berman promising to look further, with no settled conclusion.
  • Lee Berman: Jungle Hut, Last House and Valeria's tower form a family of time structures with Urth's lower levels in the past
  • Jeff Wilson: Time travel lies in the Botanic Gardens as a whole, not the jungle hut specifically
  • Ryan Dunn: Botanic Gardens mirrors alter time speed, and windows act as portals to what the occupant sees
  • James Wynn: Baxter's window exit leads into a spirit world of werewolves and psychics
  • Allan Anderson: The Wizard Knight's Tower of Glass is another example of the time-structure theme

Themes: Time loops and causality, The dying sun and the cosmos, Deep time and ruins

2010-08-11 Lee Berman #

Proposes that Wolfe's Last House, Valeria's tower and the Jungle Hut form a family of time structures, with lower levels in the past and flimsier structures spanning greater time, and that Earth lies in the future of Urth as Yesod does. Grounds this in Severian's failure to find Valeria's tower after retracing his steps, Valeria's antiquated dress, and the Jungle Hut's ascent to Robert, Marie and Isangoma, then closes by asking whether a higher Christ-figure would be more advanced than Jesus.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theology-and-religion · theories: Valeria's tower is a time structure with lower levels in the past; Jungle Hut shows Earth as Urth's future; Earth lies in the Yesod universe; Flimsier structures span greater time

I posted this theory a few years in the past but, at Dan'l's request I'm trotting it out again now. It seems like an appropriate time. We get a very nice tour of the Last House and how it works. I think there are other, similar, structures to be found in BotNS.

At the end of CotA, Severian is flying around the Citadel but unable to find Valeria's tower. He is only able to find it by retracing his steps through the catacombs to the Atrium of Time. This seems a parallel to when Severian tries to take a shortcut to Last House but can't find it until he follows the correct path.

So I assume that the top of Valeria's tower, like the top of Last House, exists in Severian's future which is why he cannot find either. Valeria is said to be old fashioned and antiquely dressed. She lives in the lower levels of her tower. I deduce that her tower (and perhaps other towers around the Atrium of Time) is a time structure with lower levels in the past. From Severian's perspective, the Last House does not have any lower levels.

the features of a labyrinthine entrance path to a vertical structure which transports people to a different time. I think the clearest example is the Jungle Hut. I think Robert, Marie and Isangoma are meant to be shown as on Earth and not Urth. Since Severian and Agia have to ascend to get there I think it shows that Earth, in some way, is the future of Urth.

An interesting irony can be concluded regarding the time scale which is traversed. I have reason to think that Valeria's tower spans decades (explanation in another thread if requested), we know Last House spans millenia and it would seem the Jungle Hut traverses cosmologic time. So, the flimsier the structure the greater the span of time which is traversed.

I think this theory supports the idea that Earth is in the Yesod universe because it demonstrates that Earth, like Yesod, is in the future of Urth. For so many years Urth was seen as the distant future of Earth.

I thought the theory of Yesod being a Kabbalic balcony or second story to Briah and Urth was born back in the time when Earth and Urth were considered to be the same planet. But, from the recent posts, it appears that even when Urth is recognized as being a previous version of Earth, the view of Yesod as separate from the Briatic sequence of destructuion and creation remains for those who prefer it. That's cool.

I still prefer viewing Yesod as the next higher universe in sequence with Briah and Earth as the next higher in sequence with Urth. It allows the Hierogrammates (or their cognate race) to evolve just as humanity can evolve and escape Yesod to a higher universe still; one step closer to the Increate. Tzadkiel seems to lament that he remains infinitely far away.

I started a thread a while back called "Wolfe as Heretic". In one interview he observes that, with the episode in the Temple, Jesus knew what it was to be a torturer. I think we can all recognize that Jesus was more spiritually advanced than Severian. Is it heresy to suggest that the Christ-figure in the universe which is above Yesod would be more spiritually advanced than Jesus?

Technically, that is inductive rather than deductive reasoning. Deductive reasoning would require at least part of thee tower to be visible midway between the past and the future, if that were the case.

2010-08-11 Ryan Dunn replying to Jeff Wilson #

Argues that the Botanic Gardens in The Citadel of the Autarch work through mirrors that alter time, slowing it in some rooms and speeding it in others, and that the building relates to the Sorcerer's House. Responds to Jeff Wilson's point about the jungle hut and the Last House by drawing on the mirror-caused time shifts and the disproportionate interior sizes, and compares it to Danielewski's House of Leaves.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · genre-and-craft · theories: Mirrors in the Botanic Gardens cause time changes between rooms; Botanic Gardens relate to the Sorcerer's House

Furthermore, the entire Botanic seems to work off the mirrors causing the time changes. Time is slower in some rooms, and faster in others, but the mirrors are always present, bending things in ways Severian can't understand.

Botanic Gardens seem to relate to Sorcerer's House though, given the disproportionate sizes from inside and out.

Anyone read Danielewski's "House of Leaves"? It does a similar thing, though there is a definite psychological parallel to the house's behavior, and the narrator's.

...ryan
