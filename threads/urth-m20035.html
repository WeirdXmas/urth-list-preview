Themes: Christ figures and messiahs, Myth and folklore sources

2011-11-17 Lee Berman #

Argues that Wolfe deliberately shifts his mythic allusions from Greco-Roman myth on Urth to Egyptian myth for the Whorl, supporting James Wynn's Osiris point. Grounds this on the shared names Typhon and Sphinx, which appear in both Greek and Egyptian myth and both occur in the story.
supports · myth-and-structure · source-and-allusion · theories: Wolfe shifts allusion from Greco-Roman to Egyptian myth

I concur. Moreover there is some evidence that Wolfe is intentionally shifting his allusion from Greco-Roman myth allusion of Urth to the sea neigbor/rival Egyptian mythology for the Whorl. The two(as far as I know) Greek and Egyptian mythological characters which share a name are Typhon and Sphinx. And Wolfe has included both in this story.

2011-11-17 Larry Miller replying to David Stockhoff #

Supports the earlier replies by Stockhoff and Berman, arguing that readers of the New Sun books should apply only one or two interpretive layers at a time. Describes his last reading of The Book of the New Sun, in which he looked only for references to Theseus and Christ.

I agree with both of you. I think its best when reading the sun books to aply only one or two layers. On my last reading of New Sun I simply tried looking for references to Theseus and Christ for example.
