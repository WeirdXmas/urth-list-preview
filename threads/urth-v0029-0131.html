Writing as therapy

Themes: Ghosts, dreams and visions

2000-08-04 Adam Stephanides #

Observes that three novels from 1987 to 1989 feature protagonists who are seriously depressed, citing Severian in The Urth of the New Sun, Latro in Soldier of Arete, and the protagonist of There Are Doors. Links the depression to the books' tone and wonders what inner demons Wolfe was facing, while cautioning against biographical readings of fiction.
proposes · author-and-biography

[This originally appeared on the whorl list, but I'm replying here because my reply deals solely with non-Whorl novels]

It's striking that the three novels published between 1987 and 1989 all have protagonists who are seriously depressed during all or part of the book. Severian in UotNS and Latro in SOLDIER OF ARETE both go through periods of severe depression; Severian is near-suicidal iirc, although this isn't too surprising since he's just caused the death of billions of people. The protagonist of THERE ARE DOORS is clinically depressed, which imo explains much of the book's strange claustrophobic tone.

Making biographical deductions from fiction is not to be encouraged, but I do wonder what "demons" Wolfe was wrestling with at that time.

--Adam
