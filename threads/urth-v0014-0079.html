Themes: Hidden identities and parentage

1998-06-22 Alice Turner #

Argues that eye colour in Wolfe's VRT is a deliberate clue, noting that Marsch's eyes are remarked on at each of his appearances in The Fifth Head of Cerberus and that the diarist and the boy both note eyes in VRT. Counts this as evidence against a rival theory, responding to Rostrum's joking remark about genetic engineering of eye colour.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Marsch's eyes are a clue to his identity

Well, yes, I'm sure we will...but we're analyzing a Wolfe book where he plays by rules of his own. And a particular point in made re "Marsch's" eyes--they are mentioned as a curiosity on each of his appearances in 5HC. And, in VRT, the diarist (Marsch, for argument) makes a point of the boy's eyes, and the boy, remembering, makes a point of his mother's eyes. I'd say this qualifies as a bona fide Wolfe clue, and a hindrance to the Sarge's theory.

-alga-

1998-06-23 adam louis stephanides #

Argues that the mocking titles spoken by Marsch's interrogator in The Fifth Head of Cerberus are fake academic credentials, and offers historical guesses about why a Polish count would signify imposture. Responds to Sgt. Rock's question about the quoted lines and disputes Tony Ellis's claim that the titles map one-to-one onto real academic degrees.
disputes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Marsch's academic credentials are fraudulent; Polish count titles signify imposture

It certainly sounds like a quote, but I can't identify it. As for what it means, in a general sense the interrogator is clearly implying that "Marsch's" academic credentials are fraudulent. One possibility that occurs to me for "A Polish Count" is that in Poland before it lost its independence at the end of the 18th century, a high percentage of the population was noble and many nobles were impoverished,
and so being a Polish count would have meant very little in terms of status. Another possibility is that in Western Europe, where this doggerel presumably originated, it would have been difficult to check on the bona fides of someone claiming to be a Polish count, so many impostors might have claimed this title and it might have come to symbolize such imposture. But this is just speculation. (My feeling is that contra Tony Ellis, the "titles" of the poem don't correspond one-to-one to genuine academic titles.)

--Adam
