2003-08-16 Jeff Veyera #

Proposes that Tzadkiel crosses between universes by a principle like Father Inire's mirrors, not a solar sail, while the Whorl travels within a single universe. Responds to teflon's claim that the Whorl never left Briah, citing Severian's comparison of the sails to the mirrors and linking the idea to the apports and the Briah-Yesod split.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Tzadkiel travels between universes by hyperspace; The Whorl travels within one universe; Briah and Yesod are different universes

I don't believe Tzadkiel travels via a solar sail, but through a = principle similar to Father Inire's mirrors (which, not incidentally, = Severian compares the "sails" to during his walk outside the ship). = This is also consistent with the theory offered as to where the apports = come from, and of Briah and Yesod as different universes.

In short, is it not reasonable to view Tzadkiel as a hyperspace ship = traveling between universes, and Whorl as a spaceship traveling within = one universe? =20

Teflon

2003-08-18 Matthew Malthouse #

Proposes that the apports on the ship are an unintended side effect of the sails being mirrors, which reflect light as solar sails do. Responds to Jeff Veyera's suggestion that Tzadkiel travels by a mirror-like device similar to Father Inire's mirrors rather than a solar sail, and argues the sail and apport explanations fit together.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Apports are side effect of mirror sails

The oposite. The apports are brought as an unintended and often unfortunate side effect of the sails being mirrors - which they are to most effectivly reflect light: solar sails.

Matthew
