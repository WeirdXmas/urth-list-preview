The thread: A single poster, David Stockhoff, asked for strong defenses of The Land Across and then argued several readings himself. He supports identifying the man in black as a Slavic Wolf-Shepherd, after first suspecting Vlad the Impaler, and cites the haunted lake house and talk of demons and fairies. He proposes that Grafton is aligned with beings opposed to the Fee and cherubs, perhaps werewolves, based on bells on p. 50. In a later post he argues the Archbishop's fall is ambiguous and cannot show whether he is an imprint doll, citing blood, speed, and silence. No other posters replied, so the thread ends without agreement or rebuttal.

  • David Stockhoff: Grafton is aligned with anti-Fee beings, perhaps werewolves, and resembles Latro
  • David Stockhoff: the Archbishop's fall is ambiguous and does not prove he is an imprint doll

2015-01-15 David Stockhoff replying to David Stockhoff #

Argues that the Archbishop's fall in The Land Across is ambiguous and cannot settle whether he is an imprint doll, since readers lack a clear sense of how the dolls or the Undead Dragon behave. Responds to David Stockhoff's list of theories by citing three textual details: the tiny dot of blood, the Archbishop's unexpected speed, and his silence during the fall.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: The Archbishop was an imprint doll when he fell; The Archbishop was the Undead Dragon

Greg

The imprint dolls are a pain, like playing with a deck full of wild cards. I guess we need to look for anything out of the ordinary, just as in a Wolf novel with androids.

(1) When the archbishop dies, there is a small bit of blood: "a tiny dot of red."

(2) The archbishop moves "a lot faster than I would have thought a man his age could."

(3) "People in movies scream all the way down," but "He did not make a single sound until he hit."

Very ambiguous. Unfortunately, I don't think we have a good idea of how the dolls behave, nor how the Undead Dragon would behave. The guy seemed pretty much like an old archbishop to me.
