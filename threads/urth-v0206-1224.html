2002-06-12 Michael Straight #

Argues that the Pancreator and Outsider cannot be identified with the Holy Trinity, rejecting the idea that Typhon inadvertently absorbed real Christian references into the Whorl's garbled faiths. Responds to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes using Noah's flood and baptism language to show that Severian's flood cannot be distinguished from the Deluge, and suggests the books are an icon rather than literal salvation history.
disputes · theology-and-religion · theories: Pancreator and Outsider are the Holy Trinity; Typhon inadvertently incorporated references to the real God; Severian's flood is an icon not salvation history

I assumed that these were deliberate parodies of Islam, Judaism, Christianity, etc. by Typhon et. al. But since Typhon thought it was all bunk, he inadvertently incorporated bits that connect to the real God - references to Jesus, the Outsider, etc.

I don't think this works. The New Testament writers use baptism language to describe Noah's flood (and vice versa), but that doesn't mean the Deluge wasn't the thing God promised not to do again. And I don't know how you could define "not destroyed, but renewed" in a way that includes Severian's flood but excludes Noah's.

I think the only out is to either assume this is an earlier universe or that we're not supposed to try to literally fit Severian's adventures into Christian Salvation History -- they are an icon of God's work in our world, rather than literal speculation about how our story might continue.

-Rostrum

2002-06-17 Michael Straight #

Argues that the religions in the Whorl are a deliberate mishmash of borrowed elements, possibly invented by Typhon as a joke, citing mock Christian priests, a pantheon, animal sacrifice, and a supposed Islam with women in charge. Responds to Jerry Friedman's point that the Vironese faith resembles Roman religion more than Judaism, accepting the parody reading while broadening it to the whole Whorl.
supports · theology-and-religion · theories: Typhon deliberately parodied real religions in the Whorl

Yeah, what I meant is that it's all a mishmash that might have seemed funny to Typhon: "Let's have Christianesque priests worshiping a pantheon of Gods and doing animal sacrifices. Oh and computer, throw together some holy texts from a bunch of random religions. And then lets have version of Islam with the women in charge!"

There's probably also a city in the Whorl filled with "Buddhists" who hire people to meditate for them and spend their time partying.

-Rostrum
