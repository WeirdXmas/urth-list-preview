Typhon's folly?

Urth list · 8 messages · 2003-09-08 to 2003-09-09

2003urthThe Book of the New SunMatthew MalthouseAndy RobertsonPeter WestlakeJames WynnDon DoggettRoy C. Lackey
The thread: Roy C. Lackey proposed that Typhon drew energy from the sun to hollow out and build the Whorl, so his fallback project caused the sun's decline and his downfall. Andy Robertson disputed the energy budget by comparing the Sun's size with the Whorl's, then argued the Whorl was a solar-level starcrosser whose flaws show Typhon never reached galactic-level technology. Spectacled Bear suggested a small black hole as a practical excavation tool, while Don Doggett and James Wynn said Typhon's solar tampering could have caused the damage, with Wynn invoking Set and the sun-devouring serpents. Matthew Malthouse accepted the black-hole-through-tampering idea as scientifically nonsensical but narratively traditional, while rejecting it for Wolfe's Urth. The thread ended in a light exchange rather than agreement.
  • Roy C. Lackey: Typhon tapped the sun to build the Whorl, causing the sun's decline and his downfall
  • Andy Robertson: Sun is far larger than Whorl so little energy was needed; Whorl is a solar-level starcrosser showing Typhon never reached galactic technology
  • Spectacled Bear: a small black hole would be a practical tool for hollowing out an asteroid
  • Don Doggett: Typhon's solar misuse caused the black hole, which the hieros placed to punish his hubris
  • James Wynn: means used to draw off solar energy need not match Typhon's known technology; cites Sumerians, Greeks and Set
  • matthew.malthouse: tampering with the sun via a failed drawing-off device could cause the damage in SF tradition, but not in Wolfe's Urth

Themes: The dying sun and the cosmos, Power, rulers and politics, Myth and folklore sources

2003-09-08 Roy C. Lackey #

Proposes that Typhon tapped the sun for the energy needed to hollow out and build the Whorl, so that his fallback project caused the sun's decline and his own downfall. Grounds this in the enormous scale of the Whorl, its provisioning and Mainframe, and the Long Sun and Short Sun sequence of crop failure, revolt and Typhon's death alone.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: Typhon tapped the sun to build the Whorl; Typhon's Whorl caused the sun's decline

Last month mantis suggested that the reason Typhon built the _Whorl_ was as a hedge against the possible failure of his plans for a new empire. Maybe so. Over the years there have been two main schools of thought on what--make that "who"-- caused the black hole in Urth's sun. Some have held that it was caused by, or at the behest of, the Increate, as punishment for the past sins of the Old Empire. Others hold that Typhon was somehow the cause. I have argued in the past for the first theory; now I want to explore the other possibility; not the mechanics of it, but how it may relate to the LS and SS books.

We don't know just how big the _Whorl_ was, but it was huge. Its long axis had to have been hundreds of miles long. It would have taken tremendous amounts of energy just to hollow out a rock that big, to say nothing of the resources consumed by the landscaping of the interior, the construction of cities, Mainframe, the Long Sun and all the other provisioning. I think it has been suggested before, by others, that Typhon somehow tapped the sun for energy in furtherance of his plans for empire, and that it was his tampering with the sun that caused its decline. I am suggesting that he tapped the sun for the energy needed to build the _Whorl_.

If mantis is right about Typhon building the _Whorl_ as a hedge against the failure of his plans for empire, then the irony is that his fall-back position led ultimately to that failure. With the decline of the sun came crop failures, a revolt of the masses, his desertion by his most trusted lieutenants and, eventually, his death, alone, behind that curtain on the mountain that bore his likeness.

-Roy

2003-09-08 Andy Robertson #

Argues that the Sun is far larger than the Whorl, so almost none of its energy would have been needed to reshape or vaporise the Whorl. Responds to the quoted claim that the Whorl was hundreds of miles long by comparing its scale with the Sun's diameter of about a million miles.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Whorl was reshaped and vaporised by the Sun's energy

The Sun is rather bigger, of the order of a million miles across, 100 times wider than the Earth. No perceptable fraction of its energy could have been needed up to reshape the Whorl or indeed to vaporise it.

2003-09-08 Andy Robertson #

Argues that the Whorl is a starcrosser built with solar-level technology, unlike the Ship, and that Typhon's failure to reach galactic-level being is shown by the flaws and malfunctions of his creation. Grounds this in Wolfe's distinction between local, solar and galactic technology and his understanding that FTL implies time travel, responding to an earlier post about hollowing out an asteroid with a small black hole.
proposes · myth-and-structure · genre-and-craft · theories: Whorl is a solar-technology starcrosser; Typhon failed to attain galactic-level being

Nontheless, I really think we are missing something here.

Wolfe talks at one point of "local" "solar" and "galactic" levels of technology, right?

I think the fundamental significance of the Whorl is that it is a starcrosser built be "solar" technology. It does not have the almost existential structure and function of the Ship. It is complex and full of wheels, not simple enough for a star-sailor to handle. It is bounded within the world of relativity, where events must follow one sequence (for Wolfe clearly understands that FTL = time machine and therefore that the Ship can travel back and forth in time).

In Wolfe, technology always has a symbolic function. The "galactic" levels of tech actually merge into the machineries of the Increate. The failure of Typhon to attain this level of being is signalled by the flaws and malfunctions of his creation.

hartshorn

2003-09-09 James Wynn #

Argues that Typhon's tampering with the sun could have caused the damage, since the means used to draw off energy need not match the technology he is known to have had. Defends this against Roy's objection by citing the Sumerians, Greeks and Typhon's ignorance of how his own technology worked, and by linking the idea to Typhon's identification with Set and the serpents that devoured the sun.
disputes · myth-and-structure · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Typhon's tampering caused the sun's damage

Matthew says:

Well, the concept fits thematically with Typhon's name and identification with Set and all the other serpents that devoured the Sun during an eclipse. So naturally the concept is attractive to me.

Once one presumes the technology to implant a black hole into a star, all bets are off for plausibility. It doesn't have to mean sending a beam of "energy" to where you want it ~ it could be some other method as inconceivable to us as power lines would be to someone in the 17th century.

Propulsion/Mirror technology and Energy technology are not necessarily the same thing. There is no need to believe that because Typhon did not have access to one, that he did not have access to the other. Typhon himself didn't seem to understand how this technology worked precisely himself.

The ancient Sumerians invented algebra, but never figured out papyrus or advanced metallurgy. The Greeks made startling advances in astronomy (discovering heliocentricy, the Earth's spherical shape, and methods for measuring the distance to the moon and the circumference of the Earth) but never developed a scientific method.

It's possibly due to my naiveté in practical science, but it never struck me as awkward.

Crush

2003-09-09 Matthew Malthouse #

Argues that Typhon's tampering with the sun's energy, through a string-like drawing-off device whose shutdown failure collapsed into a singularity, could be the cause of the Whorl's damage, though scientifically nonsensical. Grounds this in an SF narrative tradition and the analogy to nuclear power plants built without shutdown plans, while rejecting it for Wolfe's Urth because Typhon lacks the required technological sophistication.
disputes · genre-and-craft · theories: Typhon's energy-drawing device caused the Whorl's damage

Roy suggests that Typhon's tampering might have done the damage - the means emplyoyed to draw off energy rather than the amount of energay actually utilised.

In the venerable tradition of SF wand waving say a "string" taking energy from the sun to your asteroid working facility. Job done have shutdown failure that collapses the string into a singularity. Scientifically it's nonsense yet plausible with a vigorous enough wand. In human terms it's entirely consistant with the type of mind that built nuclear power stations leaving shutdown and cleaning solutions to be developed when they might be required.

As a narrative I like the concept. Within Wolfe's Urth I'd find it akward because it implies a technological sophistication that I don't believe Typhon had.

Matthew
