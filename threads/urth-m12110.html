Themes: Justice, torture and mercy, War and soldiers

2010-12-17 Lee Berman #

Argues that Earth is gradually becoming more humane, and that Urth's cruelty under Typhon's empire and the Hierogrammates reflects a missing progressive goodness rather than inherent human corruption. Replies to Andrew Mason's claim that such cruelty is inherent corruption, citing Dresden, Hiroshima, and recent conduct in Iraq and Afghanistan as evidence of moral progress.
disputes · theories: Urth lacks the progressive goodness found on Earth

In my view, the world is gradually, with frequent backsliding, gradually improving and becoming more humane. The atrocities of WWII are now recognized for what they were, even by the nations which perpetrated them (sadly the USA lags behind in acknowledgement of the atrocity of the purposeful killing of over 1 million civilians in Dresden, Tokyo etc. and of course Hiroshima).

Since then we have progressed to the point that killing a few thousand civilian Iraqis and Afghans is somewhat frowned upon as are simulated drownings and stacking naked Muslim men into pyramids. Generally, I see progress.

If Urth was Earth in the future it would show complete backsliding, back past WWII to Medieval times and ethics. Even the skimpy history/future of Urth we are given with Typhon's empire and the creation of the Hierogrammates depicts a universe of cruelty.

I find it a far more positive and uplifiting view that Urth is missing some essence of progressive goodness that we on Earth possess.

2010-12-17 Jeff Wilson replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that Urth's world has squandered the light of Christ, so that humanity is poorer for it, while still noting that good people exist and feel the urge to help. Responds to Lee Berman's quoted claim that the world is gradually becoming more humane, offering a religious counterview rather than engaging the war examples directly. The post is opinion on the moral state of the world rather than a reading of a specific text.
disputes

That's my opinion; Urth is a place where humanity (for whatever reason) has squandered the light of Christ and is so much the poorer for it. There are still good people in the world and essentially everyone feels the urge to help one another at some point, but it is so much easier to ignore some atavistic impulse than the Word of God, and the immediate temporal reward is so much more tempting to really, grindingly poor people that they stay in their rut for ages upon ages.
