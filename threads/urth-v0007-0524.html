Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Beasts and the animal in us

1998-02-06 John Bishop #

Argues that the rats in the Urth books write by leaving footprints and waste, which can be read as signs like "I was here", and suggests semiotics to distinguish a footprint from a drawing of one. Responds to the earlier idea of rats' crude writing by treating their traces as a kind of inscription.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Rats' footprints and waste are a form of writing

With respect to rats writing, I read that as saying
that rats fouled the books with their waste and left
footprints, etc. These tracks and evidences are a
kind of writing in that one can "read" them, gleaning
details of the rat's life.

In this sense, a footprint means "I (a rat) was here".

Check out semiotics for careful definitions of the
difference between a footprint and a drawing of a
footprint and so on.

-John
