Themes: Humour in Wolfe, Myth and folklore sources

2000-09-04 Nicholas Gevers #

Reports reading Wolfe's new story "Pocketsful of Diamonds" in Ed Kramer's anthology Strange Attraction and proposes that it indirectly retells the Eden myth. Grounds the reading in the talking toffee apple and talking candy floss as forms taken by the Fruit of the Knowledge of Good and Evil, and notes the fairground humour.
proposes · myth-and-structure · allegory-and-typology · theories: Pocketsful of Diamonds retells the Eden myth

I've just read Wolfe's new story "Pocketsful of
Diamonds" in Ed Kramer's anthology STRANGE ATTRACTION.
It's a wonderful piece of writing, very atmospheric,
very cunning; it seems to be an indirect retelling of
the Eden myth, with the Fruit of the Tree of Knowledge
of Good and Evil taking the form of a toffee apple
which can talk. And also candy floss that can talk.
Weird fairground humour abounds.
