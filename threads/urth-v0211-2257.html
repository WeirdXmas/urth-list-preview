Blushas

Urth list · 4 messages · 2002-11-26 to 2002-12-10

2002urthThe Book of the New SunJeff VeyeraDan'l Danehy-OakesAndy RobertsonDon Doggett
The thread: The thread asked how Blue and Ushas could end up in Severian's time without relativity, since the Duko projection seems limited to places a group member has visited. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes and Jeff Veyera pressed supporters of the Blue-is-Ushas theory for an answer, and Veyera doubted Blattid's suggestion that Scylla, Typhon's daughter, possessed Oreb. Don Doggett accepted the challenge, arguing the narrator carries mingled traces of Severian, Silk and Horn and that the inhumi could fly to Urth, then proposed Wolfe wrote an ambiguous text allowing both readings. Andy Robertson, in December, reframed the question, arguing Blue and Green are becoming Ushas and Lune in a transformed Urth, grounded in the Short Sun ending and a possible fourth book. The thread ended without resolution, with the challengers unsatisfied and the replies drifting toward reinterpretation.
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: Blue-is-Ushas theory cannot explain Duko projection landing in Severian's time without relativity
  • Jeff Veyera: Scylla's possession of Oreb does not explain the characters' arrival in Severian's time
  • Don Doggett: narrator carries mingled Severian, Silk and Horn traces, inhumi could fly to Urth, and Wolfe's text is deliberately ambiguous
  • Andy Robertson: Blue and Green are becoming Ushas and Lune in a redeemed Urth, completed perhaps in a fourth book

Themes: Time loops and causality, Doubles, twins and clones, The dying sun and the cosmos

2002-11-26 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Challenges supporters of the theory that Blue is Urth/Ushas to explain why astral projection with the Duko lands the narrator in Severian's time rather than Typhon's. Argues the Duko's origin in Typhon's era breaks the rule that projection only reaches places a group member has visited, and offers extra credit for an answer without relativity.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · puzzle-solving · theories: Blue is Urth/Ushas; Green is Lune

Okay, let's take this one step at a time.

Suppose that Blue is Urth/Ushas and Green is Lune.

Okay, let's suppose that. That means we have to allow astral time travel.

Then we have the question, "Why, when astrally projecting with the Duko, did the Narrator wind up in the age of Severian?"

Well, that seems a little difficult to me. To diffuse one=20 answer -- the presence of the Duko will _not_ resolve the problem, because the Duko comes from the time of Typhon. Thus, if we extend the "you can only project to somewhere someone in your group has been to before" idea to time, you wind up having them project to the time of Typhon -- which, evidently, they don't.=20

I challenge the supporters of the "Blue =3D Ushas" theory to come up with a good and reasonable explanation of why they wind up in Severian's time.

I offer extra credit if it does not include any lame=20
invocations of relativity. ("Oh, see, well, 1000 years have=20 passed for the cargo of the Starcrosser, so they project to 1000 years after when the Duko left...")

--Blattid

2002-11-26 Don Doggett #

Accepts a challenge to explain how Blue and Ushas reach Severian's time, arguing the narrator carries mingled Severian, Silk and Horn traces and that the inhumi could reach Urth by flying. Then proposes that Wolfe wrote an ambiguous dual text that allows both the Blushas reading and its rival to stand.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · author-and-biography · theories: Blue equals Ushas; Narrator is partly Severian; Wolfe wrote an ambiguous dual text

" I challenge the supporters of the "Blue = Ushas" theory to come up with a good and reasonable explanation of why they wind up in Severian's time.

I offer extra credit if it does not include any lame
invocations of relativity. ("Oh, see, well, 1000 years have passed for the cargo of the Starcrosser, so they project to 1000 years after when the Duko left...")

--Blattid"

I'll take that bet, mister. If the person in the pit who revives Horn is Severian as a Vanished Person then there was a comingling of the two. Whether you accept that it was Severian or not, at least grant me that there was a mingling of "whatevers". Therefore the narrator is also part Severian in addition to being part Silk and part Horn, and could thus travel to Severian's time. The only invocation of relativity I will offer is that Silk is either Severian's half brother or his genetic father, but pretend I didn't say that as it has no bearing on this discussion. As for the inhumi travelling from Lune to Urth, they simply would have flown, like they do from Green to Blue. And there are plenty of instances where the inhumi could be on Urth simply through the connotations of Urth being Earth. Vampires, goatsuckers, etc. In one sense that's what they are - demonic bloodsucking shapeshifters. I'd argue it's only natural for them to be on Urth.

Blattid, your subject heading leads me to something that has been preying on my mind for the last several days. This Blushas argument has been going on and off for quite some time and no one is really willing to give ground, nor should they. But what if what Wolfe has done is write a dual text, one that is ambiguous enough that you can read it either way and still not lose its basic meaning. I don't think that is beyond him at all. I'm not proposing some insipid "We're both right!" solution. I'm just saying that no one here is stupid or terribly careless and yet no one can seem to deliver the goods for either argument.

Don

2002-12-10 Andy Robertson #

Argues that Blue and Green are not yet Ushas and Lune but are becoming them, a new Urth whose physics and history were retroactively shaped by Wolfe's themes of redemption and fallen creation. Grounds this in the ending of The Book of the Short Sun and the idea that a possible fourth book could complete the transformation.
proposes · theology-and-religion · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Blue and Green are becoming Ushas and Lune; Lupiverse physics shaped by redemption themes

The fact is that, while I don't believe that Blue/Green are Ushas/Lune, it does ring true on a deep level.

I have been puzzling over this.

All I can suggest is that Blue/Green are a new "Urth" but are not "Ushas", if that makes sense.

Wolfe has to write about certain themes of redemption and of man's relation to God in a fallen universe.

These themes drive, not merely the plot, but the *Physics* of the Lupiverse: and they also drve its (retroactively corrected) history

I suspect that when he started the BOTLS Wolfe did not know where he was going, did not realise the Whorl was in orbit round a sun, and did not know about Blue and Green.

At the end of BOTLS, Blue and Green existed, but most definitely were not Ushas and Lune

At the end of BOTSS, I think Blue and Green have moved some way towards becoming Ushas and Lune.

They are not there yet, no. But if Wolfe ever pens a fourth book in thebtrilogy, the doubters may get some suprises.

(and of course, if Blue and Green become Ushas/Lune at the end of this fourth book, they will **always** have been Ushas and Lune)

hartshorn
