Wounds and food

Whorl list · 1 messages · 1997-03-12 to 1997-03-12

1997whorlThe Book of the New SunCharles Dye

1997-03-12 Charles Dye #

Quotes Jim Jordan and Ranjit Bhatnagar, who suggest Robert Graves's Claudius novels explain Severian's wounds and that the grave robbers in Shadow may be a name pun, and who list the food scenes in The Book of the New Sun. Bhatnagar notes the plain peasant fare, the larks passed by Vodalus and the mate, and jokes that Wolfe is not anorectic.
informs · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Graves's Claudius novels explain Severian's leg and facial wounds

This is from Jim Jordan:

If you haven't already, grab copies of three of Robert Graves's magni opi: "I, Claudius," "Claudius the God," and "King Jesus." These will explain a great deal about Severian and company, including the prevalence of leg and facial wounds. They're also damn good books in their own right. I nurse a deep, dark, ugly suspicion that the grave robbers in Chapter I of "Shadow" are a veiled homage / name pun.

And this from Ranjit Bhatnagar:

Hum .... From memory, as I don't have the books here: bread and mutton, leeks and lentils ("That's the difference; torturers get mutton.") Severian's stolen meal near Orithyia: bread and cheese, jerky and an onion (brings tears to my eyes); larks stuffed with almonds and candied figs (described in Dr. Talos's play;) and doesn't Vodalus pass the larks? Severian often drinks mate, probably an homage to Borges. Most of the food in the "Book" is plain peasant fare, but Wolfe describes it with the same depth of emotion you'd get from the Frugal Gourmet. I've never met Mr. Wolfe myself, but I strongly suspect that the man is Not Anorectic!
