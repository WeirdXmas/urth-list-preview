Blue as Ushas - long + More LONG!

Urth list · 4 messages · 2002-11-17 to 2002-11-18

2002urthThe Book of the Short SunThe Book of the New SunJeff VeyeraRoy C. LackeyRyan Thistlethwaite
The thread: Participants debated whether the Neighbors in Wolfe's Long Sun books were morally superior or fallen, and whether they ever left Urth for the stars. Jeff Veyera argued the Neighbors were fallen, since the inhumi nearly exterminated them and Silk's remark implies inhumi reflect the people they feed on. Roy C. Lackey countered that Silkhorn's passage in In Green's Jungles says the Neighbors moved to a new home circling another short sun, answering Ryan Thistlethwaite's claim that no text supports a stellar migration. Thistlethwaite clarified he never assumed spaceships and conceded the point was minor, a personal impression rather than a textual argument. Thistlethwaite also proposed the Neighbors and Inhumi are related, and that Severian's pit visit explains confusion, though the thread did not develop these ideas further.
  • Jeff Veyera: Neighbors were morally fallen, not superior, since inhumi nearly drove them to extinction
  • Roy C. Lackey: Silkhorn's In Green's Jungles passage directly shows the Neighbors moved to a star circling another short sun
  • Ryan Thistlethwaite: Neighbors and Inhumi are related, and he never claimed spaceships; the stellar point is a minor personal impression

Themes: Gnosticism, heresy and other theologies, The dying sun and the cosmos, Evolution and the future of humanity

2002-11-17 Jeff Veyera #

Argues that the Neighbors in the Long Sun books were morally fallen rather than superior, since the inhumi nearly drove them to extinction, contrary to Silk's and Horn's positive portrayal. Grounds this in Silk's own remark that inhumi merely reflect the people they feed on, and suggests Silk surrounds himself with inhumi to redeem them by example.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Neighbors were morally fallen, not superior; Silk surrounds himself with inhumi to redeem them

To your point, Ryan, Silk lamented at one point that the inhumi were merely a reflection of those on whom they feed, not evil in and of themselves, and that were humans better people, the inhumi would be no threat. The Neighbors apparently were similarly fallen, in that the inhumi drove them to near extinction; impossible if Silk's interpretation was correct and the Neighbors were morally superior to humans. The Neighbors get a very positive treatment from Silk and Horn throughout the book---the truth likely wasn't so wonderful.

This might also explain Silk's willingness to surround himself with inhumi---might he not have been hoping to redeem them through his own example?

2002-11-17 Roy C. Lackey #

Quotes Silkhorn's passage from In Green's Jungles saying the neighbours have gone to a new home circling another short sun and may revisit this one. Answers Ryan Thistlethwaite's claim that no text suggests the neighbours went to the stars by citing that passage as direct evidence.
answers · close-textual-reading · theories: The neighbours went to another short sun

Silkhorn speaking:
"As for me, I have walked with them, and sat with them around their fire. Thus I know that they exist. They have gone elsewhere--found a new home circling another short sun. But they have our permission to revisit this one whenever they choose." (IGJ, 72)

-Roy
