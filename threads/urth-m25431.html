First Polish edition of Peace

Urth list · 5 messages · 2014-01-17 to 2014-01-18

2014urthThe Book of the New SunGwern BranwenGerry QuinnAntónio Pedro MarquesPiotr SzczęsnyDan'l Danehy-Oakes
The thread: Piotr Szczęsny announced the first Polish edition of Peace and shared its cover, mildly disappointed with the art and inviting non-Tor editions from other countries. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes praised the cover as a riff on the older arrow-in-hill design and ranked it second-best behind a Gahan Wilson skull, while Gerry Quinn dismissed it as weak because its newspaper imagery has nothing to do with the novel. António Pedro Marques asked whether "Pokój" is the best Polish title and digressed into how Portuguese SF translations are published and how translators struggle with names, doubting Wolfe would translate well. The thread ended without resolution, with the cover dispute left as a matter of taste and the translation question unanswered.
  • Piotr Szczęsny: announces the Polish edition and mildly dislikes its cover
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: likes the cover as homage to the arrow-in-hill design, ranks it second-best
  • Gerry Quinn: cover is weak, newspapers have nothing to do with the novel
  • António Pedro Marques: questions whether Pokój is the right title and doubts Wolfe translates well

2014-01-17 Piotr Szczęsny #

Announces the first Polish edition of Peace, read by the poster in English, and shares a cover link from the series "Feast of Imagination" that includes Vandermeer, Shepard and harrison. Expresses mild disappointment with the cover art and invites readers from other countries to share non-Tor editions.
informs

Hello,
I live in Poland, and two days ago was a lucky day for Wolfe fans here- frist Polish edition of Peace. I Read it in english, but I'm glad that it finally arrived in my native language, for sake of fellow Wolfe readers here who are not so proficient in english :) Now I can gift this to my friends.

Here is a link to a cover:
http://www.mag.com.pl/pliki/ksiazki/Pok%C3%B3j/Uczta_wyobraz__ni_____Poko__j_M.jpg The vertical writing at the bottom is a name of the series, it translates roughly as "Feast of Imagination" and contains some very interesting books (Jeff Vandermeer, Lucius Shepard and mj harrison to name a few).

As for my opinion... I Think the cover is pretty decent but, to be honest I'm a little disappointed, some of this series have mindblowing cover arts.

I hope you enjoy this little "local" curiosity, maybe some of you are from other countries and can share cover arts other than TOR editions ?

Greetings from Poland,

Peter.

2014-01-18 António Pedro Marques replying to Piotr Szczęsny #

Asks whether the Polish word "Pokój" is the best translation of the title Peace and shares a link to Portuguese SF pocketbook listings. Digresses on how Portuguese translations of SF have been published and how translators struggle with names, doubting Wolfe would translate well.
questions

(...grumble about never having gone far enough with my polish...)

Is 'Pok?j' the best translation for 'Peace' here?

Aside from the mainstream classics, you hardly find anything other than pocketbook translations of SF in portuguese, such as http://www.europa-america.pt/advanced_search_result.php?keywords=Gene+Wolfe The results may very. I've quite enjoyed some translations of other authors (we actually have most volumes of an SF collection that started off in the 50s and lasted until recently, running into the 500s), and that's how I met Simak, Cornbluth, Poul Anderson and many others (back in the day it wasn't so easy to get the originals even if you could read them - though I first met Tolkien and Zelazny in English, thank God). Generally speaking, SF could translate well into Portuguese, I think, if it weren't for the fact that translators can do very little with names. Translated Tolkien is painful to read and I guess the same would hold for Wolfe - I never even looked at it, even though I think I may have one of those pocket volumes.

We did have a couple of nice collections, but mostly fantasy. One had a book of short stories by S?awomir Mro?ek. Also, Lovecraft has been reasonably well done.
