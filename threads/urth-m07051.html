Best of Gene Wolfe and another thing

Urth list · 4 messages · 2008-10-08 to 2008-10-09

2008urthNigel PricethalassocratJosh GellerDon Doggett
The thread: Don Doggett announced an advance copy of The Best of Gene Wolfe with its 31 stories and suggested Charles Montgomery's The Shark God as a possible real-world analog for the Takanga Group in An Evil Guest, without evidence Wolfe read it. Brunians disputed the Melanesia reading, arguing the name sounds like Tonga and the people are Polynesian, and floated Nan Madol as a model for Lovecraft's R'lyeh. Nigel Price asked Doggett to share the story list, since advance copies go out for publicity and his readers would be the book's market. Mr Thalassocrat sided with brunians on Tonga, adding that a monarchy suggests a Commonwealth country, though he said it matters little. The thread ended with the Polynesian identification largely accepted and the story list request left unresolved in the posts shown.
  • don doggett: Takanga may be modeled on Melanesia, with The Shark God as a possible analog
  • brunians: Takanga sounds like Tonga and its people are Polynesian, not Melanesian
  • Nigel Price: asks for the story list since advance copies are for publicity
  • Mr Thalassocrat: agrees Takanga resembles Tonga and suggests a New Zealand High Commission

Themes: Myth and folklore sources, Colonization and the native, Names and their meanings

2008-10-08 Don Doggett #

Announces an advance copy of The Best of Gene Wolfe with thirty-one stories and offers to list them, then recommends Charles Montgomery's travel memoir The Shark God as a likely real-world analog for the Takanga Group in An Evil Guest. Mentions no evidence that Wolfe read the book, only that the poster does not know.
informs · source-and-allusion · theories: The Takanga Group is modelled on Melanesia

Sometimes, probably most times, God, or fate, or whomever or whatever, throws an unworthy dog(gett) a bone. I was perusing a box of ARCs at work today and what did I pull up from the churning depths of the Wordage Sea but an advance copy of The Best of Gene Wolfe. There are thirty-one stories total, and I'm willing to list them if there is interest and if none of the members with more editorial savy than I have find it objectionable.

On An Evil Guest related note, I've just finished The Shark God, by Charles Montgomery, about his experiences in Melanesia, which is the obvious analog for the islands of the Takanga Group. I have no idea if Wolfe has read this book but it is a fascinating travel memoir, and at the end, sublimely moving. I can't recommend it enough.

Don

2008-10-09 Josh Geller replying to don doggett #

Disputes Don Doggett's claim that the islands of the Takanga Group in An Evil Guest are modeled on Melanesia, arguing instead that the name sounds like Tonga and that the people are Polynesian. Also offers a side note that Nan Madol may have been Lovecraft's model for lost R'lyeh, supported by a Wikipedia link and a description of Polynesian versus Melanesian appearance.
disputes · source-and-allusion · theories: Takanga Group is modeled on Tonga; Takanga islands are Polynesian rather than Melanesian; Nan Madol was Lovecraft's model for R'lyeh

Beg to differ, Takanga sounded a lot more like Tonga than it does like anyplace else. http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonga

In any case it is obviously a Polynesian and not a Melanesian country: Polynesians are the brown giants, Melanesians are smaller and look like Africans, more or less.

On an aside, Nan Madol (http://www.pohnpeiheaven.com/nanmadol.htm) seems to have been Lovecraft's model for lost R'lyeh.

.

2008-10-09 Nigel Price replying to don doggett #

Asks Don Doggett to list the stories in the advance copy of a Gene Wolfe collection, arguing that advance copies are sent out for review and publicity so sharing the contents is fair. Responds to Don Doggett's earlier post about The Best of Gene Wolfe, noting the list's readers would form a prime market for the book.
questions

Don Doggett wrote about "The Best of Gene Wolfe"...

I don't know what the terms are under which advance copies of books are distributed, but the prime concern of most publishers is generating interest and publicity for their books. Advance copies are geberally sent out so that they can be reviewed, so in that spirit I can't see there being any objection to your giving us a list of the stories contained in this volume. I'm certainly interested, and I'm sure others are too. Indeed, collectively, I would have thought that we constituted a prime market for this book!

So...

Which stories have they chosen to include, Don?

Nigel

2008-10-09 thalassocrat replying to brunians #

Agrees with brunians that the island nation Takanga resembles Tonga, noting it is Polynesian and a monarchy with large people. Adds that its king suggests a Commonwealth country, so a New Zealand High Commission rather than a Consulate would fit, though it does not matter much. Responds to Don's point about Takanga's Polynesian character.
supports · source-and-allusion · theories: Takanga is modeled on Tonga

Yep, I agree - Tonga is the closest real place; Polynesian, it's a monarchy & the people are big!

Wherever, it's almost certainly a Commonwealth country, judging by King Woossisname, so it should be "New Zealand High Commission", not "Consulate". Not that it matters.
