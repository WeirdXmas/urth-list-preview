Themes: Myth and folklore sources

2007-06-19 Matthew Groves #

Lists the many sources that readers have suggested for Tracking Song, including Genesis, the Icarus legend, Great Raven legends, Homer, Virgil and Jack London, and asks for help understanding how they connect. Responds to a question about the moose-man's fate by admitting confusion and speculating that the collection's title hints at multiple sources and that Norse myth may be involved.
questions · source-and-allusion · myth-and-structure · theories: Tracking Song draws on many mythic sources; The winged man at the end is a raven

Roy asks:

I now understand this story less than ever. As you observe, it seems to be the primary feature of Tracking Song that it is woven of many disparate strands. This is true of Wolfe in general, but especially, it seems to me, the stories in _The Island of Doctor Death and Other Stories and Other Stories_, which leads me to speculate that this collection's title is a wink acknowledging this multiplicity of sources. So far, in relation to Tracking Song, these have been identified, or at least suggested:

Genesis
the Icarus legend
the Great Raven legends of Western North American Indians The Iliad of Homer (The Odyssey must be there too)
The Aeneid of Virgil Jack London, _The Call of the Wild_ and _White Fang_

I know next to nothing about most of these. And if the winged man at the end is a raven, then I suspect Norse myth must be involved too, another subject about which I know nothing. Rather than groping in darkness (where Wolfe's protagonists so often find themselves) I am hoping someone will show me the light of day (perhaps even Great Raven himself).

Matt G.
