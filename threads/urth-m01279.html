Who's in Oreb

Urth list · 15 messages · 2005-06-24 to 2005-06-27

2005urthThe Book of the Long SunDan'l Danehy-OakesJames WynnChris PRoy C. Lackey
The thread: The thread asked whether the bird Oreb in Wolfe's Long Sun books was possessed by a god after Silk's resurrection of it, or was merely playing dead. James Wynn argued for possession by Pas, citing Silk's butcher's experience, black birds as Pas's signatories, and foreshadowing, while Dan'l Danehy-Oakes countered that possession was documented only once under extreme conditions. The debate drifted into whether Pas intended to rule Blue in person, with Roy C. Lackey reading Return to the Whorl against Wynn's reading of the Rajan and Cilinia passages. It then shifted to whether Scylla or the Rajan knows Pas best, and to the reliability and identity of the narrator. No side conceded the core question, and the thread ended in impasse.
  • James Wynn: Oreb was possessed by Pas, Silk's resurrection a miracle, and Pas intended to rule Blue in person
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: possession documented once under extreme conditions, so Oreb may have been playing dead; the Rajan speaks with unwarranted confidence
  • Roy C. Lackey: the text does not say Pas meant to rule Blue in person; Silkhorn never denied it only records Scylla's conditional belief
  • Chris: the Rajan absorbed some of Pas's memories, so he knows Severian; a child Scylla would not understand her father well

Themes: Ghosts, dreams and visions, Death and resurrection, Unreliable narration

2005-06-24 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes replying to James Wynn #

Disputes James Wynn's claim that a being must have possessed Oreb after its resurrection, arguing that possession by a Mainframe god is documented only once and under extreme conditions. Points to Pas's shattered state and Kypris's lack of fear at the time, and asks whether Oreb was simply playing dead.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Oreb was playing dead rather than possessed

That's rather a large assumption. There is only one known-for-sure example of a god of Mainframe possessing an animal (Scylla-in- Oreb, forsooth and fershoor), and that was in extreme circumstances. Pas at this time was _already_ shattered and scattered, and, while Kypris was not, nothing in her early encounter with Silk suggests that she was in particular fear at that time.

Is there any reason to suppose that Oreb was possessed, rather than, say, playing dead?

--Dan'l

2005-06-24 James Wynn replying to James Wynn #

Argues that the bird Oreb in The Urth of the New Sun was probably possessed by Pas, Kypris or another god rather than merely playing dead, and that Silk's death-and-resurrection scene is a miracle. Grounds the case on Silk's expertise as a butcher, black birds being signatories of Pas, and foreshadowing of Pas's own death and return.
proposes · puzzle-solving · allegory-and-typology · theories: Oreb was possessed by a god of Mainframe; Silk's death-and-resurrection of Oreb foreshadows Pas

Well, "playing dead" is no small clue. Silk is a trained and practiced "butcher". He deals with dead animals all the time. He had every reason to want Oreb NOT to be dead. It was a terrible
disheartening event for the bird to die mid-sacrifice. If Silk was convinced Oreb was dead, I think his assessment is trustworthy. His death and resurrection can hardly be termed anything but a miracle.

Maybe I'm wrong about who was in there. I can't imagine who else it might be because even though Kypris does tell Silk she's on the run, I agree she does not seem to be as desperate as the the renegade gods seem to have been when they fled into animals. Also, black birds are signatories of Pas. Also, in a literary sense, the bird's death and resurrection foreshadow Pas's same.

But maybe I could be wrong. After all, we don't have any proof to the degree we get regarding Scylla in RTTW. Or maybe even the Outsider did it. But that was a significant event that was never
explained in the story. And I think my explanation is the most probable.

~ Crush

Corrects James Wynn's account of Return to the Whorl, arguing that the Rajan's passage does not say Pas intended to rule Blue in person. Quotes Silkhorn's report of Scylla's belief that Pas would go to Blue himself, and Silkhorn's own doubts that he would come.
disputes · close-textual-reading

I'm not sure what the point of that last statement was, but that's not quite what is said. I'm not going to quote it all; readers can read it for themselves, pages 361-362. In short, Scylla is reported by Silkhorn to have believed that "Pas would not have peopled Blue unless he had some way to go there himself to rule them." Silkhorn thought she was wrong. Eventually, Silkhorn says, "Pas has not yet come, perhaps. Or perhaps he has, and Oreb simply failed to find the place that Pas found or created." And, later, "Then I believe Pas knows that as the years pass we will come to realize how much we need him, and bring him." [ . . .] "The same impulse [that sent Horn to find Silk] will be applied to Pas in another generation, surely."

-Roy

2005-06-25 Roy C. Lackey replying to James Wynn #

Argues that Silkhorn never denied that Pas intended to rule Blue in person, since the quoted line only records Scylla's conditional belief about colonization. Grounds this in the text: Silkhorn disputes Scylla's premise that Pas must be present, notes Blue and Green were already colonized, and says nothing about how Pas ruled the colonies.
disputes · close-textual-reading

Hardly. Silkhorn did not deny that "Pas intended to rule Blue in person", as you claimed.

That statement says no such thing, exactly or otherwise. The statement purports to express a conditional belief of Scylla's. It says nothing about what Silkhorn may have thought about Pas' intentions, one way or another, to rule Blue. What Silkhorn disagreed with, it seems to me, is the condition Scylla believed would have kept Blue (and Green) from being colonized at all. That condition was that Pas had to be there, somehow. Silkhorn knew, for a fact, that both Blue and Green _had_ been colonized. He also had no reason (and therefor neither do we readers) to believe that Pas was present on either planet, in any form. Thus Scylla was wrong, in his opinion.

There you show where your bias lies. You _want_ your theories to be true. Silkhorn's disagreement with Scylla's assessment says nothing about Pas' proclivities for ruling the colonies via clones or bodies grown from enhanced embryos. Implications are one thing, but they have to take a back seat to what was actually written.

I didn't call you a liar. All I said was "that's not quite what is said". I stand by that.

-Roy
