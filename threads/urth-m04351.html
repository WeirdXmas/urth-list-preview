2007-11-28 Lane Haygood replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Proposes possible sources for three names in The Book of the New Sun: Laurentia as the feminine of Laurentium, linked to Lawrence and Laura; Cadroe as a Scottish saint; and Sabas as a saintly name. Grounds these in etymology and saints' names, while admitting Sabas cannot be tied to Sprague de Camp.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Laurentia derives from Laurentium and Lawrence; Cadroe refers to St. Cadroe

Here's some initial thoughts:

Laurentia is the feminine version of Laurentium, so if Laurentium = Lawrence (Larry) then Laurentia should = Laura or something similar.

Cadroe probably refers to St. Cadroe, a Scottish saint educated in Ireland. Stone might refer to a rock or an archaic measure of weight.

Sabas is also a saintly name; I can't tie to to "Sprague de Camp," though. I know the L stands for Lyon or something like that, though.

Lane

2007-11-29 Rex Lycanthrosaurus replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Proposes that the name Cadroe of the Seventeen Stones in The Book of the New Sun hides a joke: 'cod roe' is a Scottish delicacy, and the letters of 'Seventeen Stones' can be rearranged to spell Stevenson, pointing to Robert Louis Stevenson. The post offers these wordplay links as a playful guess at the puzzle of the 'bloody three' names, answering Roy C. Lackey's invitation to crack them.
proposes · puzzle-solving · theories: Cadroe of the Seventeen Stones references Robert Louis Stevenson

Harpers Magazine runs brain-stretching puzzles similar to this every issue and I've never been particularly good at solving them, but I'll gladly take a crack at Cadroe of the Seventeen Stones.

As anyone who has ever been to Scotland knows, *cod* roe is one of its national delicacies (especially the smoked variety). And since Seventeen Stones can be rearranged to spell Stevenson, could this be a reference to Scottish writer Robert Louis Stevenson?

2007-11-29 Rex Lycanthrosaurus replying to brunians #

Replies to brunians that he knows of no discussion of Stevensonian influence on the New Sun names, and points to Stevenson links in Wolfe's work. Cites the story "The Walking Stick" as drawing directly on Jekyll and Hyde, suggesting a Thecla/Severian parallel, and sees Treasure Island residue in Pirate Freedom.
answers · source-and-allusion · theories: The Walking Stick draws on Jekyll and Hyde; Thecla/Severian parallels Jekyll and Hyde

Nothing that I'm aware of, though I haven't read all of the lycocentric litt?rature, but at least one short story ("The Walking Stick") draws directly on "Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde" (a parallel perhaps of the Thecla/Severian relationship?), while a residue of "Treasure Island" might be intrinsic to _Pirate Freedom._ (Alas, I've never been able to find any connection between Stevenson's "Imp in the Bottle" and the mandragora.)
