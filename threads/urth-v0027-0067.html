Themes: Robots, androids and artificial life, Gods, angels and the Increate

1999-06-08 David Duffy #

Argues that Wolfe's artificial creatures, such as the hollow men and the Long Sun beings, are made in the image of God and can be saved, so they share common ground with human experience. Responds to corncrake's point that Wolfe's aliens are only slightly altered humans, and contrasts this with Philip K. Dick, where humans risk becoming automata.
supports · theology-and-religion · theories: Wolfe's artificial beings are made in God's image

That is definitely the case for all his artificial creatures, such as the hollow men, (and more noticeably in the Long Sun) -- they are all made in the image of G*d, and all can be saved. So, they have a commonality with human experience. In contradistinction to PK Dick, where humans are vulnerable to becoming automata.

David Duffy.
