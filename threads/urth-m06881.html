2008-11-21 Marc Aramini #

Argues that Silk is the better man by modern moral standards, though Severian shows a striking capacity to spare enemies, such as Agia and others, where Silk kills Jahlee in wrath. Grounds the comparison in Silk's sexual and behavioral conduct and in the contrast of these specific scenes, while admitting the comparison is uncomfortable.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Silk is the better man than Severian

I believe Craig mentioned we were meant to contrast Sev and Silk in a meaningful fashion and I agree, yet at times it is disturbing to do so. Silk is the better man by modern standards - sexually and behaviorally he is certainly more in tune with our modern definitions of morality. However, Severian is able to spare his enemies in an astonishing way (Agia and others escape death) while Silk kicks Jahlee to death in wrath as one of his last recorded actions (certainly NOT Horn by then). This is problematic to some degree but I would certainly maintain Silk is still the better man.

2008-11-21 John Watkins replying to Marc Aramini #

Argues that Silk's killing of Jahlee in the Long Sun books is driven by his own guilt over what he has already done to Horn's family. Responds to Marc Aramini's contrast of Severian's mercy with Silk's wrath, offering a psychological reading that still condemns the act.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Silk's killing of Jahlee expresses his guilt

I think Silk's guilt was playing out in that scene. He'd already done enough to Horn's family, he couldn't let Jahlee do more.

Doesn't make it ok, of course.
