Top Posting Makes The Theoanthropos Cry

Urth list · 18 messages · 2006-08-27 to 2006-09-01

2006urthCliff JudgeJosh GellerIorwerth ThomasJWillardChris PJeff WilsonDan'l Danehy-Oakesb sharpMaru Dubshinki
The thread: Jeff Wilson asked list members to stop top-posting and reply below quoted text, arguing it follows natural reading order. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes and Chris defended top-posting, with b sharp arguing it spares readers from rereading quoted material, while maru dubshinki praised interleaving as a collaborative book. The thread then drifted into a side debate over whether "author" as a verb is acceptable, with Dan'l opposing it, JWillard asking for his reasoning, and Transentient and brunians responding with jokes and claims that English changes through use. The thread never touched Wolfe's fiction and ended without agreement, simply trailing off.
  • Jeff Wilson: asks members to stop top-posting and reply below quotes for readability
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: defends top-posting and coinscription, opposes using nouns like "author" as verbs
  • b sharp: top-posting is better for general replies, since bottom-quoting forces rereading
  • Chris: top-posting suits a low-volume list because interleaving prunes and mangles replies
  • maru dubshinki: interleaving is appealing as a collaborative book built page by page
  • Transentient: English is what its users make of it, regardless of education

2006-08-27 Jeff Wilson #

Asks list members to stop top-posting, placing replies above quoted text, because it makes posts hard to follow. Argues that replying below the quotes follows the normal top-to-bottom reading flow and links an external guide on quoting etiquette; it names no one.
proposes

People (I won't embarass Bob, Iorwerth, Dan'l, or Matthew by mentioning their names),

Please lay off the top-posting; it makes your posts very hard to follow. Even if your mail program puts your cursor at the top of the quoted material to which you are replying, there is nothing to stop you from moving to the bottom before beginning to type in your received wisdom, and following the conventional flow of reading down columns of English eases the reader's eye.

You can read more about it here:
http://www.greenend.org.uk/rjk/2000/06/14/quoting.html

Thanks,
Jeff

2006-08-27 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes replying to Jeff Wilson #

Explains the poster's personal habits for top-posting versus interleaved replies on the mailing list, coining the term coinscription for writing inside others' texts. Responds to Jeff Wilson's complaint about top-posting, with no reference to any Wolfe book or passage.
chats

Jeff,

I top-post, as a general rule, only when I don't think there's a need for interleaving (an ugly word; I coined the word "coinscription" to describe the way we write inside each others' texts in email/mailing lists, and will take this oppty to plug it). As a general rule, I try to write such notes so that no context is required and cut the original out
completely -- as here; but sometimes I forget to cut it.

And sometimes, considering that, due to the vagiaries
of mailing programs and such, some may see the reply
before the original, I'll deliberately leave the original below to provide that context. But it's usually just a mistake (forgetting or laziness), since I believe that the coinscriptive style is causing many people to forget how to write a
coherent reply without coinscribing.

I'll try to be better about it.

2006-08-28 b sharp replying to Jeff Wilson #

Argues that top posting is preferable in many cases, especially for general replies rather than point-by-point ones. 2006-08-28 Chris P replying to Jeff Wilson #

Argues that top-posting is acceptable on a low-volume list, since interleaving forces pruning that can mangle a reply and unpruned quoting buries short answers. Responds to Jeff Wilson's request that members stop top-posting and move their replies below the quoted text.
disputes

I generally don't find top-posting hard to follow (yes, I'm doing this on purpose), especially on a relatively low-volume list where the post that's being responded to is fairly recognizable. I save interleaving for posts that have individual points that need to be addressed separately. To interleave on a post like this, for example, I would have to sit there and selectively prune out the text that wasn't directly relevant to my response; this can sometimes lead to a pretty confusing, mangled post. On the other hand if you don't prune you end up with two pages of quoted text with a single-sentence response at the bottom that can be easily mistaken for a sig - posts like that usually go in the trash bin without being read.

2006-09-01 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes replying to Transentient #

Argues that using "author" as a verb weakens English and that "write" is always the better choice, defending a love of the language rather than grammar pedantry. Responds to Transentient's remark that English changes through use, where the poster declines to give a detailed argument because the list is for discussing Gene Wolfe's works.
disputes

Yes, but they don't speak English very well there. My problem is not a generic love of grammar for its own sake but a deep love of the English language.

This is undeniably true. Nor am I a complete grammar crank who shudders at a split infinitive or a well-used dangling modifier. But I think changes that weaken the language ought to be resisted, and "author" as a verb weakens the language. I'm not going to give a detailed explanation of why I think so -- this is still a list for discussing the works of Gene Wolfe, last I looked, and not a grammarian's alcove -- but I'll say that I can't imagine a case in which the shorter, more direct "write" isn't better. "Never use two syllables when one will do,"

Also: the Anglo-saxon word is almost always more powerful in English than the Latinate. (Which is why I try not to refer to Wolfe, or any other writer, as an "author.")
