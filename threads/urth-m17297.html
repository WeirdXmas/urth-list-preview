2011-02-19 Adam Thornton #

Proposes an extended analogy between The Book of the New Sun and The Wizard of Oz, mapping Severian to Dorothy, Jonas to the Tin Woodsman, and Agia and Agilus to the Wicked Witches. Floats Baldanders as the Cowardly Lion and the Autarch as the Wizard, then asks if a book artifact matches the slippers.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · source-and-allusion · theories: The Book of the New Sun as an extended analogy to The Wizard of Oz

So, and I want to preface this by stating that I am NOT on mushrooms, we've often been able to find promising lines of inquiry into Wolfe's work by looking at what books HE likes and then finding their echoes in the things we're studying.

Recently we've been (or at least I've been) thinking a lot about Borges in the context of Ultan, the books of _BotNS_, etc.

But this morning it occurred to me that there might be an elephant in the room we're missing.

Is there any way to stretch a consistent analogy to _BotNS_ from....

wait for it....

_The Wizard of Oz_ ?

We have Severian as Dorothy, obviously, and Triskele as a present-only-in-spirit Toto. Jonas is certainly the Tin Woodsman. Baldanders might be the Cowardly Lion and Talos the Scarecrow (that doesn't seem to work, quite). The Wizard: the Autarch or Inire? I can't decide. Don't forget that "Oz" stands for "Oscar Zoroaster." The Agia/Agilus twins as the WIcked WItches, East and West? Or is Vodalus the W.W.W. ? Hmmm. This is getting less convincing, isn't it? Time to change the subject:

The Silver (Ruby if you're playing along on the Technicolor version) Slippers? The ones whose power, it turns out, was inside Dorothy the whole time? Does this sound like any well-known artifact in the books?

Yeah, this probably doesn't work.

Adam

2011-02-19 David Stockhoff replying to Adam Thornton #

Responds to Adam Thornton's suggestion that Borges's works might illuminate Wolfe's books by arguing that such borrowed echoes need not match exactly. Offers as an example that Jonas in the Long Sun books is a Tin Man figure or descended from one, and includes a stray advertising link.
supports · source-and-allusion · theories: Jonas is a Tin Man or descendant of one

I don't think it's crazy. You don't need to find 100% correspondence. There is no doubt, for example, but that Jonas is "a" Tin Man or a descendant of him.
