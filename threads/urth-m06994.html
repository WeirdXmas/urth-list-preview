I'm reading through Wright's collection of interviews, and I stumbled on this:

"What do [I] dislike most about [my] own writing...? That so often it doesn't work -- that I can say X, flatly and in so many words, and later have editors, reviewers, and readers say 'But you never told me X.' I point out that I did, at the top of page twenty-two, or whatever, and they say, 'Well, it doesn't come through.' Obviously it doesn't, and I don't know how to make it." (78, interview with Elliott Swanson)

I don't get the sense that Wolfe is being coy here, but honestly worrying that at times he doesn't communicate what he wants to. And what strikes me about this little bit is that it suggests that so much of Wolfe's "puzzle" style of writing may not be intentional. How much of it is just how he communicates? The downside is that, as we all know and have seen on the list, it can send people looking for puzzles where there aren't any, which leads to some very creative readings of what's going on in a Wolfe story. But it also suggests that the image I think many of us have of Wolfe as a puppet master over the details of his own stories may not always be the case.

I mean, as someone recently joked, he's mortal, so of course some things just don't work like he wants. But this comment was so close to reflecting on what people admire about his style that I thought it at least worth thinking about.

2008-11-28 Marc Aramini replying to David Stockhoff #

Argues that Wolfe's ambiguous statements invite overreading, and proposes that Kypris, Typhon's mistress, is the mother of the special embryo planted on the whorl to complete Pas's plan. Grounds this in a quotation from Epiphany of the Long Sun, where the Outsider says Kypris is Hyacinth and Mother, and in the name Pelagia's pelagic and Agia roots, while rejecting Borski's Ida-from-Baldanders derivation.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Kypris is the mother of the special embryo; Hyacinth was possessed by Kypris; Pelagia combines pelagic and Agia

I agree with Roy - Wolfe's subject matter leads us to posit all the possible things that can explain the unexplainable gaps, and often we latch onto the wrong explanations.? ?
For example, Borski deriving the name Ida from Baldanders with his cryptonymic scheme seems a step too far for me, but the name Pelagia really does seem to combine pelagic with Agia, making it difficult to entirely dismiss these name relationships. ?
Wolfe's statements are also intentionally ambiguous. For example, the end of a quote I just pulled up and no one commented on, from p 220 of Epiphany of the Long Sun. ?
"Become the goddess of love in a century or less, said the Outsider standing not behind Silk as he had in the ball court, but before him - stanidng on the still water of the pool, tall and wise and kind, with a face that nearly came into focus.? I would claim her in that case, long before the end.? As I have so many others.? As I am claiming Kypris even now because love always proceeds from me, real love, true love.? First Romance. ?
The Outsider was the dancing man on a toy, and the water the polished toy-top on which he danced with Kypris, who was Hyacinth, and Mother, too." ?
These are the kinds of statements Wolfe could be talking about, because they are very easy to either over analyze or ignore completely.? Hyacinth was possessed by Kypris, and this could mean either his mother is true love or that Kypris really is the mother of the special embryo pre-programmed to carry out the plan of Pas with the ability to influence others and even their stories, who knows how to fight with a sword without training. I prefer to read it as an affirmation that Kypris, the mistress of Typhon, is the mother of the special embryo planted on the whorl to bring about the completion of Pas' plan. ?
I can point to the text and say, it says Kypris is his mother right there on the page ... but it isn't that easy in Wolfe, and there is way too much wiggle room because of possessions, ambiguities, time warping, identities shifting, and other things that make him fun to read but it is hard to definitively prove ... that's what he is saying, right there on the page. ?
