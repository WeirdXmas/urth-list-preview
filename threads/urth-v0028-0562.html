Black Holes Again

Urth list · 1 messages · 1999-12-01 to 1999-12-01

1999urthGreene, Carlton

Themes: The dying sun and the cosmos

1999-12-01 Greene, Carlton #

Argues that a white fountain's far end would behave like a black hole, since any mass forced through a tight passage would heat up and emit X-rays, so the difference between a black hole and a wormhole-like bridge is largely semantic. Responds to Rostrum's claim that the white fountain would be a distinct phenomenon, grounding the point in how matter ripped apart by gravity releases high-frequency radiation.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: White fountain's far end is effectively a black hole

<<Well sure, but my point is that, just as the dying sun initially looks like it's dying of old age but is actually being prematurely sabotaged, this opposite end of a white fountain would suck everything up very much like a black hole, but instead of the energy being emitted in this universe as X-rays, it is actually a wormhole spewing energy into another universe. It might be confused with black holes, but would be a different astronmical phenomenon (as would be appararent to any astronomer looking for X-rays).>>

Black holes emit x-rays (and Hawking radiation, and other stuff) because the process of sending large quanitities of matter "down the drain" is not a tidy one. Suns, etc. get ripped apart as they are pulled inside, releasing lots of energy -- in fact, black holes release x-rays (high frequency) rather than other E-M emissions precisely because of the amount of energy involved.

I am not an astrophycisist, but it seems to me that this would happen any time you stuff a lot of celestial bodies and accompanying gas and dust through a smaller pipeline. Thus, regardless of what you call it -- blackhole, E-R bridge, whatever, you are going to get x-rays and other traits normally associated with black holes. So the ultimate difference in what you call the "black chasm" may be largely semantic.

Carlton
