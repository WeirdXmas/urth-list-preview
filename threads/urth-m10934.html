The thread: Members debated whether the Urth list's rising volume (forty-two threads and 193 messages in about three days) warranted slowing down, after Mark Millman cited the moderator's five-messages-a-day request. Jeff Wilson and James Wynn argued readers should filter rather than posters restrain themselves, while Dave Lebling and Fred Kiesche lamented that short quoted replies had replaced longer essays. Lee Berman and Ryan Dunn disputed the complaint, with Dunn's local statistics showing the most prolific posters exceed the limit. The exchange ended in impasse, with Millman withdrawing and Wilson and Millman trading barbs; António Marques then pressed for discussion that refines theories rather than repeating them.
  • Mark Millman: volume is excessive and the moderator's five-post limit should be honored
  • Jeff Wilson: readers should choose what to follow, and posters need not send fewer messages
  • Dave Lebling: essays and new ideas get lost under quick quoted replies, and the list has changed
  • Fred Kiesche: quality has fallen with short replies, and he prefers longer posts and thicker threads
  • Lee Berman: the surge is temporary and yearly averages stay under five posts per day
  • Ryan Dunn: local statistics show prolific posters exceed the limit, and censure of debate is unfortunate

2010-08-16 Dave Lebling replying to James Wynn #

Laments that list volume has grown so high that short original essays and thoughtful new ideas get lost under quick quoted replies, recalling older posts. Responds to James Wynn's remarks about slowing down and reading mail, arguing the list's character has changed since the Long Sun and Short Sun era.
disputes

I do many things to help control the list and yet I find the volume nearly overwhelming.

I hate to do the "when I was a lad" thing, but in the old days on the list (before Long Sun, before Short Sun, etc.) most of the posts were in the nature of short essays rather than the quick
few-lines-added-and-many-quoted that we are seeing lately. People would read them and comment thoughtfully with original content. No doubt my memories are showing the sunsets more beautiful and the leaves more green, but interesting new ideas (like the connection between Greek myth and Silk's life) are lost in the shuffle at the current volume of messages per day.

And get off my lawn!

-- Dave Lebling, aka vizcacha

2010-08-16 Lee Berman replying to Mark Millman #

Argues that the current burst of list activity is temporary, caused by a minor irritant that provoked contentious threads, and that posting volume will fall back to a few posts per day. Responds to Mark Millman's lament about shorter, quoted-heavy posts by citing a yearly average of under five posts per day, so the complaint is overstated.
disputes

While I am sympathetic to Mark Millman's sentiment, I am, in contrast to James Wynn, not contrite or chastened. In my opinion this current flurry of activity is temporary and the result of some minor irritant which has infected the board and prodded and provoked members into multiple contentious discussions.

Soon the topics that have intense interest will decline in number and total postings will die down to the handful of posts per day that recent times have seen. Moreover I think if we take a yearly average of posts per day for all members it will compute to rather less than 5 per day even for the most prolific.

This sentiment I tend to agree with.

2010-08-16 Ryan Dunn replying to Lee Berman #

Complains that censure of list activity and discouragement of debate is unfortunate, apologizing for his own part in the recent surge but objecting to being asked to apologize to members who dislike full mailboxes. Responds to Lee Berman's remarks on the flurry of contentious threads and the expected decline in posting, with no Wolfe content.
disputes

The censure of activity coupled with the censoring of debate is a bit unfortunate.

I am partially to blame for the recent resurgence of activity, and for that I apologize. But the mere fact that I should apologize to the prudish few -- those who don't like their mailbox clogged and haven't learned how to set up Rules and Filters to quarantine the Urth messages, and so forth -- is in itself a let down.

I personally really, REALLY like the discourse on this mailing list, but alas it is not MY mailing list, so if the powers that be don't want to encourage spirited, renewed debate, and still others long for a sunnier yesteryear when every post was a monumental essay on all things holy and Wolfean, then so be it.

Five posts a day it is, sirs and madams.

...ryan

2010-08-19 António Pedro Marques replying to Mark Millman #

Argues that list discussions should distinguish between theories that beg a question, where pointing out an alternate answer is useful, and theories that supply an answer, where merely suggesting another possibility is idle. Responds to Mark Millman's complaint about hypotheses not being refined, agreeing with him and warning that discussion for its own sake wastes effort.
disputes

I think this nails it in the head. Imo some of the participants here have little regard for the subject matter of the list.

I'd especially like it if people here learned to distinguish two quite different situations:

- Theory A BEGS question X, and someone points out that the answer to X is likely to invalidate A.
Fine. Useful. May shoot down A or make it better if an alternate explanation is found.

- Theory B PROVIDES answer Z to question Y, and someone says that the answer could be W instead.
Wrong. Useless. Doesn't shoot down B because it doesn't invalidate Z and B doesn't depend on Y anyway. Serves no purpose at all than idle chatter because of course there could always be a ton of answers to Y, that's not what's under discussion.

With such a complex prime matter, discussions can go on forever and never advance an inch. I personally LOATHE discussion for the sake of discussion, specially because it almost always means people don't take the trouble of understanding what it's all about, only caring to have a seemingly clever word to say. But hey, that's me.
