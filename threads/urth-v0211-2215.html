2002-11-22 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Presents a list of identity questions across Wolfe's fiction, from The Fifth Head of Cerberus and the Book of the New Sun to the Long Sun, Short Sun and Latro books. Builds on Crush's remark that identity runs through Wolfe's work, and separates such questions from puzzles about whether two figures are the same person.

Crush writes ...

While I do not feel competent to interpret it, it seems to me that a constant theme of identity (and especially the ambiguous nature=20 of identity) runs through most, if not all, of Gene Wolfe's major work -- and certainly through the "Lupiverse" or "Briah Cycle," including the "Sun" books and, possibly, THE FIFTH HEAD OF CERBERUS; also in the Latro novels.

One might go so far as to accuse Wolfe of "deconstructing" identity=20 through these books. 2002-11-22 James Wynn #

Argues that the Greek gods in the Soldier books have questionable identities, noting that Demeter and Kore seem opposed yet their orders to Latro need not conflict. Cites Latro's comparison of the gods to servants of a great king and to one actor changing masks in a Greek play, and Eurycles/Drakaina in Soldier of the Mist as both necromancer and serpent spirit.
proposes · puzzle-solving · source-and-allusion · theories: Latro's gods are one actor in different masks; Eurycles is both necromancer and serpent spirit

Well, for the Soldier books the entire Greek pantheon is of questionable identity. Demeter/Earth Goddess and Kore/Artemis are ostensibly at odds yet their orders to Latro are not necessarily at odds. Furthermore Latro says in Mist that the gods are like servants receiving supplicants of a great king. But (in Arete I believe) he says iirc that in talking to the gods he has the sense of watching a Greek play in which a single actor plays different characters merely by putting a different mask before his face.

Also, (in Mist) Eurycles/Drakaina's identity is in question seeming to be simultaneously the necromancer and the serpent spirit that possesses him.

--Crush
