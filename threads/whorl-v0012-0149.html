The thread: Participants debated the mechanics and family relations in Return of the Long Sun-era Urth plotting. Michael Andre-Driussi argued that Horn's spirit remained in the Horn/Silk/Silk combine until the quest ended and that Severian is a dark son of the royal family, citing Scylla's body in his favourite mausoleum. David DiGiacomo questioned whether consciousness could pass directly from Silk's eye into Horn's body, calling the eye transfer a weak point. mark millman disputed the royal-son claim, arguing Severian is a grandson or remote descendant of Typhon and offering an allegorical reading of Typhon's family. Jeffrey Meyers answered DiGiacomo by treating the eye-uploading as symbolic, citing Matthew 6:22. The thread ended without resolution, with positions left standing.
  • Michael Andre-Driussi: Horn's spirit stayed in the Horn/Silk/Silk combine; Severian is a dark son of the royal family
  • David DiGiacomo: Silk's consciousness moving through Pig's eye is implausible; suggests a path via glass
  • mark millman: Severian is a grandson or remote descendant of Typhon; family reading is allegorical
  • Jeffrey Meyers: gods' uploading through eyes is symbolic, not mechanical (Matthew 6:22)

Themes: Doubles, twins and clones, Fathers, mothers and children, Gods, angels and the Increate

2001-02-08 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Argues that Horn's spirit stayed in the Horn/Silk/Silk combine until the quest ended, and that Severian is a dark son of the royal family, since Scylla's body lies in his favourite mausoleum. Responds to Dan and Adam's exchange on the editors' ambiguity and Silk's eye given to Pig, and speculates about why Nettle and Seawrack leave Blue.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Horn remained in combine until quest ended; Severian is a dark son of the royal family; Nettle left Blue because she learned the secret

Dan wrote, and Adam responded:

Allow me to interject: I don't think that Horn was completely evacuated from the Horn/Silk/Silk combine (that is Horn spirit/Silk body/mainframe Silk-via-Pig's body spirit) until after the big quest was completed.

Just as Scylla's quest took them on a "tangential" side trip to Urth, so that she could "come home" and be reunited with her original body in her original homeland. Same thing for Horn. Or so it seems to me.

After Horn is laid to rest, the combine is Silk/Silk, or Silk2. Still not cured of that awful melancholy, suggesting that main frame isn't an easy cure-all, but perhaps he is a bit better?

As for editors creating mystery, I think the lack of their fingerprints in IGJ (footnotes, comments) was something larger in that it suggested that they (editors and family) had not received the book.

Part of the difficulty surrounding Horn giving Silk's eye to Pig, it seems to me, is the fact that at precisely this moment, when Pig first looks at his benefactor after the operation, the transfer happens, and Horn/Silk becomes Horn/Silk/Silk.

One of the first tricky questions for me was, "Why would Nettle leave Blue? Why not be a grandmother?" For this I think the biggest answer is probably that she was told the damning secret, and as a result she would be hunted on Blue. Beyond that, well, the colony has gone to hell, time to move on!

What about Seawrack? Here's the weird case where she was Horn's girlfriend, and while the Horn/Silk/Silk combine occassionally confused Seawrack with Hyacinth, still, I don't think that Silk2 is likely to have an affair with Seawrack, just as Horn wouldn't do so with Hyacinth. So why is Seawrack going? She is such a wild, untamed thing, and the Whorl seems like such a domestic scene . . . unless she will live in the Lake as the replacement for Scylla?

Silk2: widower; Nettle: widow; Seawrack: siren; Marble: mother; Oreb: free bird?

But back to Urth--that Scylla's body was in Severian's favorite mausoleum (remember the bronze that has his own features) causes all sorts of big ripples . . . is Severian, at root, a member of the same family? He cannot be Typhon, since Typhon is blond and entombed on Mount Typhon. So if he is a member of the royal family, he must be a son. One of the two sons. A dark son. Perhaps the dark son who had a mildly helpful role in TBOTLS.

2001-02-08 David DiGiacomo #

Questions how Silk's consciousness could have moved into Horn's body through Pig's eye, arguing a direct bio-to-bio transfer seems implausible and proposing instead a path via glass. Responds to Michael Andre-Driussi's claim that Horn's gift of Silk's eye to Pig produced a Horn/Silk/Silk merger, calling the eye transfer a weak point in the Long Sun series.
questions · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: Silk's mind moved into Horn via glass

Is there any reason to think that a god can do a direct bio-bio transfer? If not, the path must have been Pig->glass, glass->Horn/Silk, and it happened shortly after Pig saw Horn/Silk.

It seems odd that Silk in Mainframe hadn't noticed his body long before this, and entered it to see what was going on.

Can anyone think of an explanation for gods uploading themselves via the eyes? Normal eyes are not output devices, unless some sort of twitch code is used, and another part of the body would serve as well for that. Perhaps Pas bioengineered something, but there is no evidence of biotechnology that advanced. It seems like a weak point in the series.

I don't think the second reason would be enough to make her go on a trip with Seawrack.

The Mother told her to go.

2001-02-08 Mark Millman #

Argues that Severian is a grandson or remote descendant of Typhon, reasoning from who could have fathered or borne him given Typhon's family and the Ouen and Dorcas relation. Responds to Michael Andre-Driussi's suggestion that Severian is a dark son of the royal family, questioning how Scylla's burial in his mausoleum fits, and offers an allegorical reading of Typhon's extended family as an alternative.
disputes · puzzle-solving · allegory-and-typology · theories: Severian is a grandson of Typhon; Typhon's family is an extended allegorical family; Cilinia is Severian's daughter

Surely, mantis, Severian is a grandson of Typhon, at nearest, if not a remote descendant. Unless you're prepared to throw out the conclusion that Ouen is Severian's father and Dorcas is Ouen's mother, we know Severian's father is not a member of Typhon's family. That leaves his mother, and unless Typhon & co. were (1) walking the corridors of Time, which, for all their other activities, we haven't known them to do, (2) allowing Echidna to go gallivanting about getting herself impregnated by men other than Typhon (a bad way to ensure the continuity of a royal line, to be sure), or (3) playing body-games -much- more interesting than the one that Typhon tried with Piaton, I don't see how Severian could be a member of Typhon's immediate family.

I'd much prefer the more allegorical explanation that, as the exultant families came to Urth with Typhon, they're all part of his extended "family", latterly including Severian through his mother.

Of course, that still leaves open the question that you're trying to answer, to wit: Why would Scylla insist on being buried in Severian's mausoleum? Perhaps Echidna's original was un- faithful to Typhon, or was already pregnant when she married him, and Cilinia is Severian's daughter.

Nacre

Suggests that the gods' uploading through the eyes in Return to the Whorl is a symbolic device rather than a mechanical one. Answers David DiGiacomo's question about how gods upload via eyes by citing Matthew 6:22, "The eye is the lamp of the body."
answers · source-and-allusion · theology-and-religion · theories: Eye-uploading is a symbolic rather than mechanical device
