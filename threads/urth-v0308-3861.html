What becomes of THE WONDERS OF URTH AND SKY

Urth list · 10 messages · 2003-08-07 to 2003-08-08

2003urthThe Book of the New SunJeff VeyeraMooncalfWilliam H. AnsleyAndrew BollenAshley CrillChris PDon PalumboMichael Andre-Driussi
The thread: Readers asked what happens to the Book of the Wonders of Urth and Sky, which Severian carries, and whether he still has it on returning to the Citadel. Michael Andre-Driussi and Don Palumbo both pointed to Citadel chapter 34, where Severian intends to keep the book, and Palumbo confirmed it. Chris and Mooncalf debated whether a photographic memory makes rereading unnecessary, with Mooncalf arguing Severian's memory is only ordinary recall. Ashley Crill noted Severian admits he has never read the whole book. Andrew Bollen then raised a separate question about where the book falls among the books fetched for Thecla. The thread ended with the textual answer settled but the memory question left open.
  • Michael Andre-Driussi: Severian still has the book on return to the Citadel, per chapter 34
  • Don Palumbo: Asked the question, then confirmed the chapter 34 answer
  • Chris: Keeping and rereading a known book is a pleasure, not a need
  • Mooncalf: Severian's memory is modest, not photographic, which explains carrying read books
  • Ashley Crill: Severian says he never read the whole book, so one reading should suffice
  • Andrew Bollen: Asked where the book fits in the list of books fetched for Thecla

Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Memory and forgetting, Unreliable narration

2003-08-07 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Answers Don Palumbo's question about what becomes of the Wonders of Urth and Sky book, suggesting Severian still has it when he returns to the Citadel. Cites Citadel chapter 34, where Severian intends to keep the book and hopes carrying it will inspire reading, while wondering if it is lost near Lake Diuturna.
answers · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Severian still has the Wonders book on return to the Citadel

BOOK OF THE WONDERS OF URTH AND SKY in the Book of the New Sun? Does Severian still have it by the time he returns to Nessus as Autarch, or does he lose it sometime after he reads the "Tale of a Boy Called Frog" to little Severian, perhaps around Lake Diuturna, where both <italic>Terminus Est</italic> and the Claw are destroyed?

Citadel, chapter 34: Severian still has it when he returns to the Citadel; he intends to keep it, and hopes that the sight of him carrying it will inspire more reading.

2003-08-07 Chris P #

Argues that Severian's attitude toward books mirrors Wolfe's own, so keeping a book he already knows by heart is natural and rereading its pages is a pleasure rather than a practical need. Responds to Teflon93's claim that a photographic memory makes a second reading of The Book of the Wonders of Urth and Sky unnecessary, offering personal experience as support.
disputes · close-textual-reading · author-and-biography · theories: Severian's attitude to books mirrors Wolfe's

I believe that Severian says enough to indicate that he feels much the way Wolfe does on the subject of books, and it's a feeling I share. The idea of keeping a special book that you already know by heart doesn't seem strange to me. Opening the physical book and re-reading the pages, no matter how well you remember them, is a pleasure rather than a practical concern.

Civet

2003-08-08 Mooncalf #

Argues that Severian's claim to never forget is only a modest claim that he retains general memories, not a perfect photographic recall, which would explain his lapses and why he carries books he has already read. Builds on Ashley Crill's point about Severian keeping a book he apparently finished, and supports the reading with a lunch-memory example and an aside about the list.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Severian's never-forgets claim means only general recall, not perfect memory

Ashley Crill pointed out (regarding why Sev needs to retain a book after he [apparently] read it):
book. Perhaps he never finishes the thing during his journey.

Good point. Also Severian mentions that while he never forgets anything he doesn't always have instant recall. Therefore it might be faster (and easier) for him to go back to the original source rather than trying to search through his memory in order to bring up a particular passage.

It's all a crock though in my opinion. At least in regards to him having a perfect memory. Perhaps he is trying to deceive us or just himself. Who knows. But of course no one can truly claim with any certainty to having such a memory. Not unless somehow everything in one's life has been recorded and can therefore be played back in order to determine the accuracy of that claim. Otherwise how would you know? I mean even if you could remember verbatim what you read in some book and can verify it by going back to that book and checking...still, that doesn't mean that your other memories will also be 100% accurate (i.e., you might have a better memory for what you read as opposed to what you heard or what you saw...or you might even unconsciously replace certain true memories, for whatever reason, with false ones).

Then again does he ever say he has a perfect memory or just that he never forgets?* If only the latter then maybe he means it not so much in that he has a photographic type of memory but rather that he can simply recall, say, what he had for lunch five years, three months, and two days ago (roast beef and peas) on a very uneventful day. Perhaps not how many peas there were or how many bites it took to finish the roast beef, but rather just that he didn't forget about having had that meal and what it generally consisted of. To most of us, i'd wager, we'd consider that amazing and that therefore he was well justified, at least in some sense, in claiming he never forgets. Especially since most of us can't recall what we had during an ordinary lunch on an ordinary day a mere 20 days ago (or even whether we had lunch that day or not, if we're prone to skipping them once in a while). Memories like that are soon "lost in the mist" for us but maybe they're still there for Severian, though maybe not down to the very last detail. That would explain his lapses at times and also why he might feel the need to carry books around with him that he's already read.
