Sleeping Baldanders

Urth list · 19 messages · 2008-12-29 to 2008-12-30

2008urthThe Book of the New SunMatt TeelTim WaltersJosh GellerBruce HaylesPaul BCraig BrewerAdam ThorntonDavid StockhoffSon of WitzMatthew GrovesDave TallmanDavid Duffy
The thread: Matt Teel opened by asking what the sleep-talk exchange between Severian and Baldanders in The Book of the New Sun Chapter XV accomplishes beyond recapping Severian's expulsion and journey. Dave Tallman argued Baldanders only pretends to sleep-talk, and Bruce Hayles and Paul B. attributed his shifts between beast, scientist and child to hormones or rage, with Paul B. invoking the Hulk. Son of Witz and Bruce Hayles debated the 'strike off' line, with Paul B. offering the idiom 'set out on one's own' against the headsman reading. Tim Walters's Silverlock recommendation drew Melville and Moby-Dick comparisons from Matthew Groves and David Stockhoff, and the thread drifted into Silverlock, ending without consensus on the sleeping scene.
  • Matt Teel: the sleeping exchange must do more than recap, and he proposed combining hormone and pretence readings
  • Dave Tallman: Baldanders is pretending to be dim, playing the giant rather than the genius
  • Bruce Hayles: Baldanders' shifts come from fluctuating hormones, not deliberate acts
  • Paul B.: Severian often misreads events, and Baldanders' shifts are rage-driven like the Hulk
  • Son of Witz: the 'strike off' line needs a question mark and Severian's headsman reading is misdirection
  • Matthew Groves: the sleeping scene echoes Moby Dick and the 'strike off' remark is picaresque humour

Themes: Doubles, twins and clones, Humour in Wolfe, Books, libraries and writing

2008-12-29 Matt Teel #

Asks what is really happening in the short sleep-talk exchange in Chapter XV of The Book of the New Sun, where Baldanders utters four fragments and Severian answers as if half awake. Argues the scene must accomplish more than recapping Severian's expulsion from the guild and journey toward Thrax, and invites readers' ideas on its purpose.
questions · close-textual-reading

I'm wondering about the short section in Chapter XV ("Baldanders"), in which Baldanders seems to be talking in his sleep.? Severian is half awake and responding to what he thinks Baldanders is saying.? Any thoughts on what's really going on there? ?
Baldanders says four things: (1) Never."? (2) "Baldanders."? (3) "You--strike off."? (4) "Tomorrow, then." ?
The scene is obviously meant to accomplish something, but I can't figure out what.? Severian's answers simply recap things that have already been established, but I can't believe Wolfe just wanted to make sure we knew that Severian had been tossed out of the guild and is headed toward Thrax, or whatever.? So, any ideas? ?
Matt Teel

2008-12-29 Bruce Hayles replying to Dave Tallman #

Argues that Baldanders' shifts between beast, scientist and childlike behavior are caused by fluctuating hormones rather than a deliberate act. Grounds this in three episodes: his charge at the crowd that the cacogens burn down, his scholarly manner at his castle, and his childish fight with Severian there. Responds to Matt Teel's claim that Baldanders only pretends to be a dim giant.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Baldanders' behavior caused by fluctuating hormones

I'd assumed that the changes in Baldanders behavior were not an act but something caused by wildly fluctuating levels of hormones. Otherwise I cannot explain how Baldanders goes from enranged beast (when he charges the crowd and the cacogens have to burn him down) to scientist (at his castle) to some sort of crazed child (when he fights Severian at his castle).

2008-12-29 Paul B replying to Bruce Hayles #

Proposes that Severian is wrong in his interpretations of events more often than not, and explains Baldanders' inconsistent behavior as a kind of rage-driven shift, comparing it to the Incredible Hulk. Offers the examples of Baldanders charging the audience and fighting Severian, noting that he is otherwise analytical. Responds to Bruce Hayles and Craig Brewer's agreement about Severian's understanding.
proposes · close-textual-reading · genre-and-craft · theories: Severian is wrong more often than not; Baldanders' rages explained as Hulk-like transformation

I haven't kept track or anything, but I'd be willing to entertain the notion that Severian is wrong more than half of the time in his interpretations of events.
The issue of Baldanders' inconsistent behavior could also be explained (as I explained it to myself) by "incredible hulk" syndrome. When he gets upset (as when he charged the audience or fought Severian) he flies into a rage, but is analytical otherwise.

Paul
