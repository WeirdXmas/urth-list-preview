Themes: Christ figures and messiahs

2010-12-16 Lee Berman #

Disputes the idea that the Urth phase and successive universes culminate in Christ figures, arguing that the Old Testament's genocide makes Jesus no model and that Severian is more brutal. Argues that dismissing named characters and philosophies imposes the reader's own worldview on the text, and that the critic's own leaps are overlooked.
disputes · theology-and-religion · theories: Jesus is absent from the Urth phase text; Each universe produces a better Christ figure

None taken from this former one, anyway. The Old Testament is a cesspool of genocide all in the name of the Chosen People. Joshua was as merciless and bloodthirsty as any conqueror in history. Happen to have the bad luck of being an Egyptian? Too bad, locusts and frogs in your yard, boils on your skin then your oldest son dies. I can remember being 8 years old and thinking "Damn, God is a real *******!. Pharaoah wants to let the Hebrews go but every time he tries, God hardens his heart. WTF is the guy supposed to do?"

That's the impression I get. Might have been the basis for my Wolfe-as-Heretic thread. Jesus Christ is better, less of a torturer, than Severian. But the next universe might get someone even better. Maybe Jesus v.2 will stop the wickedness of the moneychangers in the Temple with a simple word or two and a gentle hand to the shoulder instead of a whip.

Not by name. Nor do we see any significant whiff of Salvation, indicating a Jesus Christ. This point has been made too many times already by David and me.

Many think names are extremely important in the work of Gene Wolfe. By dismissing named characters and philosophies and elevating non-named vaguely addressed characters and philosophies you are imposing your own worldview on the text.

It is okay, to do this. That's what Peter Wright does with his atheistic interpretation of BotNS. But I think it is good to acknowledge one's own bias and viewpoint when interpreting from a "self" point of view.

Wise words to consider when people start discussing their intuitive theories. Good to recognize that when you accuse others of making an "unlikely leap" you are in the process of leaping yourself.
