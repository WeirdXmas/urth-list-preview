The thread: The thread asked whether the "-latry" suffix in Latro's name carries a hidden meaning tied to Wolfe's ideas about gods. David Stockhoff began with monolatry and argued that the name pairs thief or mercenary with worship, which he linked to Latro's head wound. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes explained that latria is the Catholic worship owed only to God and suggested Wolfe, a Catholic, would know that sense. Mo Holkar granted the latro/latreia wordplay but called the suffix too common to hide anything and doubted the mono- reading. Stockhoff conceded the mono idea was only the starting point and pushed the latria reading further, endorsing Marc's Ahura Mazda theory. The thread ends with the question unresolved and Holkar's skepticism unanswered.
  • David Stockhoff: Latro's name pairs thief with worship, linking him to a god who takes worship owed to God
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: latria is the Catholic term for worship owed only to God, and Wolfe would know that sense
  • Mo Holkar: the -latry suffix is too common to carry a hidden meaning, and mono- reveals nothing

Themes: Names and their meanings, Gods, angels and the Increate, Catholic faith and sacraments

2015-04-22 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes replying to David Stockhoff #

Explains that "latria" is the Catholic term for worship owed only to God, distinguishing it from "dulia" for saints and "hyperdulia" for Mary. Responds to David Stockhoff's and Mo's discussion of the "-latry" suffix in Latro by suggesting Wolfe, a Catholic, would know the theological sense of the word.
supports · theology-and-religion · source-and-allusion · theories: Wolfe intended Catholic meaning of -latry in Latro

For what it's worth, "-latry" is an important term in Catholicism, usually in the form "latria." Latria is the worship to which only God is entititled; what we offer to the saints in general is "dulia," respect or reverence, and to Mary, "hyperdulia," extreme reverence.

Wolfe just *might* know that...:)

2015-04-22 Mo Holkar / UKG replying to David Stockhoff #

Argues that the "-latry" suffix in "monolatry" is too common a word-part to carry a hidden meaning tied to Latro's name, while granting that Wolfe played on latro (thief) and latreia (worship). Responds to David Stockhoff's quoted Wiki note linking Latro's name to Wolfe's ideas about gods, questioning whether the "mono-" form specifically reveals anything.
disputes · source-and-allusion · theories: Latro's name puns on latro (thief) and latreia (worship)

"-latry" as a suffix can be found with a number
of other prefixes, perhaps most familiarly in
"idolatry". Of course Wolfe was thinking of the
play on latro in Latin meaning thief, while
latreia in Greek means worship: but it seems a
bit of a stretch of inference to see hidden
meaning in the "mono-" prefixed version specifically?

best,

Mo

2015-04-22 David Stockhoff replying to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that the Catholic terms latria and dulia suggest a further layer to the word 'thief' in Latro, linking Latro to a god who takes worship that belongs to God. Builds on Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's quoted note on the '-latry' suffix and supports Marc's theory that Latro is Ahura Mazda, while noting that hierodules may give or receive reverence.
supports · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: Latro is Ahura Mazda; Latro's thief hints at a usurping god

Huh! That suggests another dimension to "thief," since a thief takes what he is not "entitled" to. But I wouldn't know where to go from there, unless another god is involved. Thus, Marc's theory that Latro is Ahura Mazda fits, because AM would be taking God's rightful worship.

I didn't know that meaning of "-dule," which I have always seen rendered as "slave." Hierodules need not be slaves at all. In fact, if they are analogous to saints, it might be debatable whether they are meant to give reverence or receive it. Or both.
