Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Names and their meanings

2010-09-22 Brian Lovely #

Argues that the names Horn, Hide, and Smoothbone in the Long Sun books point to writing materials and tools: Horn as a papermaker whose book trumpets Silk's deeds, hide as parchment, and hoof and horn boiled into glue for ink. Offers these as a wordplay pattern noticed in the Names and the Whorl thread, with no citation beyond the names and their uses.
proposes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Horn, Hide and Smoothbone are names for writing materials

Regarding the "Names and the Whorl" thread. I'm pretty sure someone else has noted this, except, perhaps, for the Smoothbone and trumpet parts:

Horn is a papermaker who writes a book about Silk 'trumpeting' his achievements (horn as megaphone). Hide can be made into parchment (admittedly a paper substitute, but a still a support for writing), and horn, hide, and hoof can all be boiled into glue. The glue can be combined with pigment to make ink. Smoothbone sounds kind of like a pen handle.
