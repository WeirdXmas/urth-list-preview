Themes: Names and their meanings, Hidden identities and parentage

2000-11-14 Tony Ellis #

Argues that the name John V. Marsch in The Fifth Head of Cerberus is a deliberate pun, with Marsch carrying his fate in his name, and that he is replaced by a real John-of-the-Marsh. Responds to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's suggestion that VRT took Marsch's abo name, rejecting it because characters call him Doctor Marsch before he meets VRT and the surname would not survive a replacement.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Marsch is replaced by a real John-of-the-Marsh; The name Marsch is a Wolfian pun on his fate

Unfortunately, this intriguing idea is at least partially shot down by the fact that once or twice Dr Marsch is called "Doctor Marsch" by the people he talks to, long before he goes into the back of beyond with VRT. It seems unlikely that VRT would keep the surname and replace the first names.

On the other hand, the names do seem suggestive, don't they? I would suggest that what we're looking at here is a classic Wolfian pun: the joke being that Marsch carries his eventual fate with him all the time in the words of his name. Dr John V Marsch, the man who comes to St Anne to find aborigines, gets killed and replaced by a real
John-of-the-Marsh. Now there's irony for you.
