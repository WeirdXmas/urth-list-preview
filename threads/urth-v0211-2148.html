FTL Travel

Urth list · 16 messages · 2002-11-18 to 2002-11-20

2002urthThe Book of the Long SunJoe CilluffoJerry FriedmanJosh GellerAndy RobertsonJames WynnDon DoggettGareth JelleyRoy C. Lackey
The thread: The thread asked whether faster-than-light travel necessarily means travelling backward in time, relayed from Crush via James Wynn. Andy Robertson argued in relativistic terms that FTL appears as backward time travel in another frame and framed Wolfe's Ship as a doorway to a timeless divine realm, drawing on Boethius; Josh Geller disputed the Boethius and Catholic origin, noting time-as-dimension ideas are older and widespread. Jerry Friedman countered that backward time is a standard reductio, that the Lorentz factor gives imaginary rather than negative time, and that the Whorl shows no FTL. Wynn defended the simplified claim as possible rather than settled, and the thread ended in playful banter about physics credentials and an admitted unclear point rather than resolution.
  • Andy Robertson: FTL appears as backward time travel in other frames, and Wolfe's Ship is a doorway to a divine timeless realm
  • Josh Geller: time-as-dimension ideas are far older and widespread, so Boethius and Catholic origin is unproven
  • Jerry Friedman: backward time is a reductio, and the Lorentz factor yields imaginary time, not negative time
  • James Wynn: relaying Crush, FTL implies backward time, a possible rather than settled implication
  • cilluff1: perception of dimensions need not limit movement through them, questioning the vision-of-God claim

Themes: The dying sun and the cosmos, Gods, angels and the Increate, Deep time and ruins

2002-11-18 James Wynn #

Explains that James Wynn is relaying Crush's answer to Don's question about faster-than-light travel, noting that time slows near light speed and that FTL would send time backward. Quotes Crush's claim that a physics basis supports reversed time and that a generation ship is therefore unnecessary, citing Stephen Hawking secondhand.
informs

Don asks:
Please explain. Is this the usual SF take on FTL or is there a physics explanation that makes backward time travel the most likely? I'm pretty ignorant on this.

Crush responds:
As one moves faster and approaches the speed of light, time slows down, exponentially, until time stops at the speed of light. I am informed by Stephen Hawking (not personally) that it takes an infinite amount of time and an infinite amount of energy to reach the speed of light.

It is simple mathematics that as one moves faster than the speed of light, time moves in the opposite direction. No doubt, there are other mathematical models.

Hence, a FTL generation ship is unnecessary.

-- Crush

Responds to Andy Robertson's quoted argument that Wolfe conflates God outside time with relativistic physics, offering no textual evidence from the books.

These are all recent developments, Aquinas, Aristotle, all of it.

You find 'advanced' ideas about time among indigenous Africans, Americans, Australians, Polynesians and 'heathen' Europeans, as well as in ancient Middle-Eastern and Indian books.

2002-11-19 Jerry Friedman #

Argues that FTL travel implies backward time travel is a standard reductio rather than a plot device, and that Wolfe's fiction already allows time travel, so FTL poses no problem there. Responds to James Wynn and Andy Robertson on relativity, citing Severian's realization near the end of The Citadel of the Autarch and noting the Whorl shows no sign of FTL.
disputes · genre-and-craft · close-textual-reading · theories: Time travel already possible in Wolfe's universe; Whorl has no FTL technology

"...that it *would* take..."?

I wouldn't call it simple mathematics. What Andy Robertson is talking about is that if observers moving with some velocities see an object move from A to B faster than light, then all observers with other velocities see the object move from B to A faster than light. You can use this fact to show if FTL travel or even communication is possible, you can send messages to your own past. This doesn't say anything about what would be experienced by someone traveling faster than light, if such a thing is (were) possible. I think it probably isn't possible, and if it is, no one has the slightest idea what someone traveling FTL would experience.

In reality, sending messages backward in time is often cited as a refutation of FTL, a reductio ad absurdum. In Wolfe's fiction, time travel is possible already, so FTL is no problem. Severian makes this connection, by the way--it must be near the end of _Citadel_, after the secret of the universe.

...

That one I agree with (with an exception that's probably unimportant to BotLS). I see no evidence at all that the _Whorl_ is capable of FTL. I agree with Andy Robertson that Wolfe associates FTL with transcendence, and would add that the _Whorl_ seems to be entirely secular. (I don't think I'm the first person to say that on the list.)

Jerry Friedman
