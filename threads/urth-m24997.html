2013-09-13 Lee Berman #

Proposes that the name Ash and Master Ash's cryptic reference to Vine derive from Norse myth, where Ask and Embla are the first humans, while entertaining an Egyptian god Ash linked to oases, vines and the desert storm god Typhon. Grounds the Norse reading in the Ragnarok association and the translation of Embla as vine or liana, and the Egyptian one in the desert-oasis and Typhon connections.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Master Ash named after Norse Ask and Embla; Master Ash drawn from Egyptian god Ash

I've long wondered about the significance of the name of Master Ash and his cryptic reference to Vine.

I've found one likely reference in Norse mythology, in which the first man and woman were named Ask and Embla, which translate in English to ash tree and elm tree, but alternatively Embla can be vine or liana. http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ask_and_Embla

Given Ash's association with Ragnarok, Norse seems like a likely source.

But I just bumped into a tantalizing alternative. One of the most ancient Egyptian gods was named Ash, and as god of the desert oases, he was also the god of the vine. As a desert god he was also associated (sometimes sexually) with Typhon Set, the god of desert storms.

I guess I still think the Norse inspiration is mostly likely the primary one. But it is hard to dismiss a nominative clue which relates to both Typhon and The Outsider/Dionysus.

2013-09-27 Andrew Mason replying to Lee Berman #

Points out that Wolfe said in Castle of the Otter that he intended the Norse reference for the name of Master Ash, while noting that other possible references may also be present. Responds to Lee Berman's question about Ash's name and his cryptic mention of Vine by supplying Wolfe's own stated intention.
