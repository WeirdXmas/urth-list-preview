2001-05-23 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Proposes that the final words of the story, 'a mountain ram winded its horns,' allude to a shofar blown in the Temple on the High Holy Days. Grounds this in the belief that unfallen animals had abilities absent from the fallen world, so the ram might sound its own shofar unkilled. Responds to Roy's question about the ending.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theology-and-religion · theories: Final ram's horn line alludes to a shofar

I presume it was a reference to the ram's horn or _shofar_ blown in the Temple on the High Holy Days (Yom Kippur?). It is generally agreed (among those who believe in the Fall to begin with) that the unfallen animals had characteristics & abilities not present in the Fallen world -- i.e., the lion & the lamb were at peace, Eve wasn't surprised to hear a serpent talking, etc., etc. -- so perhaps in an unfallen world, the ram might blow his own _shofar_ without humans needing to kill him? I dunno...

--Dan'l
