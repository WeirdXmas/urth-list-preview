Themes: Catholic faith and sacraments, Gods, angels and the Increate

1997-04-07 Kieran Mullen #

Argues that the two voices Silk hears in The Lake of the Long Sun, one masculine like a mountain of stone and one feminine and cooing, reflect the Holy Spirit and a Trinity-like plurality rather than the Outsider's identity. Grounds this in Roman Catholic tradition of the Spirit as the feminine part of the Trinity and in Genesis's plural divine self-reference, and it answers Alice Turner's Silk-and-God reading.
disputes · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: Silk's two voices reflect the Trinity

How would you like it cooked?

"Cooing" seems more like a reference to the Holy Spirit, in RC tradition. It has sometimes been thought of as the feminine part of the Trinity. I can't see how you could get Severian to have the voice of a mountain of stone.

In Genesis, God often refers to him/herself in the plural. (This in itself has sparked much discussion). This seems to me to be Wolfe's take on this metaphor/image/idea.

Kieran Mullen
