2010-11-30 James Wynn replying to Andrew Mason #

Agrees with Andrew Mason that the inhumi are demonic in Wolfe's Short Sun books, but argues they need not be the source of the demon idea, since the writings draw on many traditions. Says the inhumi simply fit the demonic type, citing the accumulated mythology behind the C. writings, including the Odyssey, the Gospels and Islamic tradition.
supports · source-and-allusion · myth-and-structure · theories: Inhumi merely fit the demon type rather than originating it

Well, the inhumi are "demonic" but the C. writings are an accumulation of mythology, the Odyssey, the Gospels, Islamic tradition, and other writings. So, I agree that it is unnecessary to ascribe inhumi as the original source of demons. They merely fit the Type.

2010-11-30 Andrew Mason #

Disputes James Wynn's claim that inhumi resemble devils, arguing that Pike's inhumi are spirit-absorbers who are themselves possessed rather than possessors who enter people. Concedes a thematic link but doubts the chrasmologic writers had inhumi in mind, and admits he has not yet read Short Sun.
disputes · source-and-allusion · theories: Inhumi are possessed by absorbed spirits rather than possessing

OK, so there's a thematic similarity between inhumi and devils, which makes it appropriate that Pike called them that. But if I am reading you rightly, the inhumi as Pike knows them - creatures which fly in through windows and suck blood - don't possess people; rather they are themselves in a sense possessed by the spirits of others whom they have absorbed. (Bear in mind I haven't read _Short Sun_ yet, so don't know the details.) I still think it's unlikely the chrasmologic writers has inhumi in mind.
