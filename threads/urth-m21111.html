Father Inire

Urth list · 8 messages · 2011-10-12 to 2011-10-13

2011urthThe Book of the New SunRyan DunnLee BermanDavid StockhoffLarry MillerJeff Wilson
The thread: Lee Berman proposed that several small, silent, bent guides in the Long Sun and Short Sun books, including the cowled servitor and the old boatman, are Pan-like iterations of Father Inire, citing physical resemblances, Rudesind's letter and an Isangoma link. Larry Miller questioned which guides were meant and suggested the jungle hut figures from the narrator's own time instead, and later proposed Inire as a hierodule who sheds parts of himself into other bodies. Jeff Wilson disputed the Isangoma link, noting the cowled guide speaks and the Isangoma passage mentions no metal skin or Bible. Ryan Dunn argued Inire could be a time-travelling figure behind Severian's roles, then proposed Fechin is Inire and that Hethor traveled through time. David Stockhoff offered time dilation as an explanation for Inire's longevity. The thread ended with competing speculations rather than agreement.
  • Lee Berman: silent guides across the books are multiple iterations of Father Inire
  • larry miller: Inire is a hierodule shedding parts of himself, and the jungle hut figures come from Severian's own time
  • Jeff Wilson: the cowled guide speaks and the Isangoma link is unsupported by the text
  • Ryan Dunn: Inire is a time-travelling twin who aided Severian, and later Fechin is Inire
  • David Stockhoff: time dilation from mirror travel explains Inire's longevity

Themes: Doubles, twins and clones, Time loops and causality, Masks, disguise and shapeshifting

2011-10-12 Lee Berman #

Proposes that several small, old, bent, silent guides in The Book of the New Sun and The Citadel of the Autarch, including the cowled servitor, the Jungle Guide, Rudesind and the old boatman, are multiple Pan-like iterations of Father Inire. Supports this with physical resemblances, Rudesind's Inire letter, the Isangoma link and the covered faces, admitting the links are guesses.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Father Inire appears as multiple guide figures; Cowled servitor and Jungle Guide are Inire; Inire is a Pan-like being serving the Commonwealth

I think those are pretty good guesses for Inire appearances. The cowled servitor does appear in Father Inire's wing of the House Absolute. And the appearance of the Jungle Guide in Citadel connects with the Father Inire letter Rudesind carries which says he has been in the North with the Ascians.

But both are guesses. Neither of these characters is confirmed by any other characters as Father Inire though it would have been easy for that to happen. Why didn't the old Autarch tell Severian, "follow Father Inire to the thiasus and your acting troop."? Note that both these characters are silent despite the fact that they serve as guides. Both characters have their faces covered. I think something deeper and more mysterious might be going on here.

Moreover both these characters seem to fit the description of Father Inire being small, old and bent. Interestingly, this description comes from characters who are themselves small, old and bent, Rudesind and the old boatman (Rudesind also sharing the Inire characteristic of being monkey-like). Both Rudesind and the old boatman claim an association with Father Inire.

The Jungle Guide has a dark, metallic looking skin which is reminiscent of another jungle guy, Isangoma, from the Jungle Hut. Who, by chance, is also a small, old bent kind of guy. And how does Isangoma know The Bible. Is that connected to Rudesind cleaning a painting of an Apollo space mission?

And so on. There are other interesting and (for me) compelling connecting links of these guys to other characters. Though they aren't obvious, I am willing to consider that they represent a collection of multiple Pan-like iterations who are getting things done in the Commonwealth and have been doing so for all its history.

Proposes that the supposed Father Inire sightings may be a collective of beings, with Inire as a hierodule who sheds parts of himself into different forms and bodies like Tzadkiel. Suggests the apelike features of these figures link to Zak's first apelike form, which Severian describes.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Father Inire sightings are a collective of beings; Inire is a hierodule who sheds parts of himself; Zak's apelike first form connects to Inire sightings

As for a collective of beings constituting Supposed Inire sightings I might be able to get behind. Inire being certainly a Hierodule may be able to shed parts of himself into different forms and bodies (like Tzadkiel) and this may also explain thier Ape like features. Remember Severian first describes Zaks first form as apelike.

2011-10-12 Ryan Dunn replying to larry miller #

Argues that Father Inire, as a time-travelling twin, could be the figure who allowed Severian to be the Conciliator and Apu Punchau, since Inire and Severian both walk the corridors of time. Grounds this in the bent time and space of the quest, citing Famulimus and the Atrium of Time, and responds to Larry Miller's suggestion that Inire is a hierodule who sheds parts of himself into other bodies.
supports · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Inire is a time-travelling twin of Severian; Inire allowed Severian to be the Conciliator; Severian is Apu Punchau through time travel

I had also made a case for Inire's time travel twinning, which conversely allowed Severian to be Conciliator, Apu Punchau, etc. After all, both he and Severian (and Hethor, for example) walked the corridors of time, and time and space are bent throughout this quest with cases like Famulimus, Atrium of time, etc.

...ryan

2011-10-12 Jeff Wilson replying to Lee Berman #

Points to the text's passage where the cowled figure directs travelers and suggests Isangoma's Bible knowledge may come from being in a room with a woman reading scripture.
disputes · close-textual-reading

Where do you see this? In my copy, Isangoma is written to be naked, crouching, sinewy, and to sing with a child's voice, and that's it.

Do you suppose him being in a room with a woman reading scripture might have something to do with it?

2011-10-13 Ryan Dunn replying to Jeff Wilson #

Proposes that Fechin is Inire, reasoning that Inire built the botanical gardens and has a wizardly mastery of the corridors, so he may have taught Severian to use the mirrors and traveled through them himself. Suggests Hethor came through time on his travels and acquired his pets and poppets, pointing to shops that seem out of sync with time, in reply to Jeff Wilson's question about when Hethor and Inire entered the Corridors.
proposes · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Fechin is Inire; Inire taught Severian to use the mirrors; Hethor came through time to acquire his pets and poppets

I am a paranoid reader who thinks Fechin is Inire, for one.

And as the one who built the botanical gardens, inire's wizardly predisposition suggest a mastery of the corridors, and makes it rather easy for me to believe that he not only taught Severian how to use the mirrors, but also traveled through them himself.

And I had figured Hethor came through time on his travels where he acquired his pets and poppets. The shops do seem to move out of sync with time, though I could be mistaken.

2011-10-13 David Stockhoff replying to Ryan Dunn #

Proposes that time dilation from frequent travel through the mirrors could explain Inire's apparent longevity for his short-lived species. Replies to Ryan Dunn's quoted message about Jeff Wilson's idea that Inire traveled the mirrors, adding that Inire's species could just as well be Homo sapiens.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Time dilation from mirror travel explains Inire's longevity; Inire's species could be Homo sapiens

Depending on exactly how the mirrors work, time dilation from frequent travel through them could explain Inire's stated apparent longevity for his short-lived species. Which, btw, could just as well be Homo sapiens as far as that goes.
