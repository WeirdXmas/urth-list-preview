Are the Neighbors REALLY the Neighbors?

The thread: Jordon Flato proposed that the Long Sun Neighbors are dream-traveling inhumi, while Lee Berman argued they are real beings and Horn's dismissed immortal-Neighbor theory holds. António Pedro Marques called the idea untestable, and David Stockhoff insisted Neighbors still need a sun and planet; Berman guessed they travel between related time strands. Flato then said he never believed his idea, and the thread drifted into horns: Wynn and Berman linked Moses, Michelangelo, Alexander, Midas, Typhon and Azazel, while Dan'l Danehy-Oakes disputed the Moses mistranslation claims. It ended in drift, with the Neighbors question abandoned for mythic comparisons and a closing point that heroes and villains swap roles.
  • Jordon Flato: Neighbors are dream-traveling inhumi in Neighbor form, later said he did not really believe it
  • Lee Berman: Neighbors are real beings; Horn's immortal-Neighbor theory is true; horns stem from Latin mistranslation

  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: Moses horns are a mistranslation of Exodus, not a radiance symbol

2011-02-14 Jordon Flato #

Proposes that the Neighbors Horn meets in the Long Sun books are really dream-traveling inhumi wearing neighbor form, not corporeal beings. Grounds this in the analogy to inhumi appearing human in dream travel and in the barnacle-encrusted creature that tries to climb aboard Horn's boat in Blue.
proposes · myth-and-structure · theories: Neighbors are dream-traveling inhumi wearing neighbor form; Neighbors seeded the Whorl to keep Horn on blue

Thinking about all of these ideas flying around recently about the nature of dream travel and its mechanics, a crazy idea occurred to me. It's really crazy, and not at all likely to be true (although compared to some theories it might possibly be made viable with some creative readings of the text). The question occasionally comes up as to what dream traveling inhumi fed on neighbors would look like? This made me think "what if we never actually meet neighbors? What if what we are seeing as neighbors in the books are actually dream traveling inhumi who LOOK like neighbors because that is what their souls are like, much like an inhumi fed on humanity appears human in dream travel? What if they seeded the Whorl and try to get Horn to stay on blue as a means of ensuring their progeny's existence?

Preposterous. And yet, there is a subtle clue in that the possibly only fully corporeal 'neighbor' we meet is the barnacle encrusted monstrosity that tries to climb aboard the boat with Horn early in Blue (?? is that when it happens?) Maybe this a clue that there are only Zombie neighbors left, and that all we see of the neighbors from Silk-Horns perspective are these dream traveling Inhumi...

Preposterous, I know. Or is it?? Dum dum DUUUUUUM.. (cue dramatic gopher video)...

2011-02-15 Lee Berman replying to Jordon Flato #

Argues that the Neighbors Horn meets in The Book of the Long Sun are purely dream-traveling beings who seem half-present, and that Horn's dismissed theory of an immortal Neighbor is actually true. Supports this by citing the passage in the middle of Blue's Waters where Horn tries to dismiss the creature, and by reasoning about the Neighbors' disappearance and the goddess Seawrack as The Mother.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theology-and-religion · theories: Neighbors are purely dream-traveling beings; Horn's immortal Neighbor theory is true; Neighbors went to a neighboring plane at death; Seawrack is The Mother

I think I see where this is coming from. Still I lean in the direction of James' theory that dream travel is the province of Neighbors (and thus SilkHorn is part Neighbor). I think the Neighbors Horn encounters are dream travelling Neighbors, purely. They seem to be sort of there and not there, like the sort of beings who could pass through walls but also be able to shake your hand (or have sex or bleed).

Well, middle of the book (pg. 163 in my copy). And that certainly seems to be the best conclusion based on Horn's theory of that thing which he tries to dismiss but, c'mon, is Wolfe really going to waste 3 paragraphs on a false theory? Why would he?

The idea Horn has about the creature is that one Neighbor is blessed (but really cursed) with immortality. He lives his life in immortal debauchery for a few centuries but then all his fellow species-members have "dwindled". He throws himself into the sea to be succored by the goddess who favored him. This goddess is surely The Mother as Seawrack has a power of persuasion over this immensely strong creature.

So where did all his species members "Vanish" to? I agree with those who theorize they are on a "neighboring" plane of existence. So what we call "death" is something which frees the Neighbors to go to another (better?) place, except for this one immortal guy (and the immortal goddess who succors him in barnacle crusted glory).

2011-02-15 António Pedro Marques replying to Jordon Flato #

Argues that if inhumi fed on neighbours appear human, dream-travelling neighbours and dream-travelling inhumi would be indistinguishable, so the theory cannot be tested. Responds to Jordon Flato's suggestion that the neighbours seen in the books are really dream-travelling inhumi, granting it is coherent but noting that only encounters with non-dream-travelling neighbour-fed inhumi could help decide.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Neighbors are dream-travelling inhumi fed on neighbours

I see nothing preposterous about it. In fact, the idea is that neighbour-fed dream-travelling inhumi would be indistinguishable from dream-travelling neighbours. The problem is that, if we can't tell them apart, we can't tell if it is so either. It might help if we ever met neighbour-fed *not*-dream-travelling inhumi, but they can be anywhere the neighbours can.

2011-02-15 David Stockhoff replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that the Neighbors, though possibly on another plane, still need a short sun and a rocky, watery world to live on, reconciling this with Roy's account. Supports this by quoting Roy's statement to Silkhorn in In Green's Jungles that the Neighbors went elsewhere to circle another short sun.
disputes · close-textual-reading

I'm one of those theorizers, but let's try to reconcile this with what they told Silkhorn:

"As for me, I have walked with them, and sat with them around their fire. Thus I know that they exist. They have gone elsewhere -- found a new home circling another short sun. But they have our permission to revisit this one whenever they choose." (IGJ, 72)

-Roy

My assumption is that even if they are on another plane/dimension/level, they still need a short sun and a spherical lump of rock, gas, and water to live on.

2011-02-15 James Wynn replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that Wolfe often spends paragraphs on theories that are wrong yet point readers toward a puzzle, so Horn's theory about the Neighbors in the Long Sun books should not be dismissed as a waste of space. Supports the point with the example of Silk spending several pages speculating on why he called Blood "son" at their first meeting.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Wolfe's offered theories are usually wrong in key ways

Actually, it is not at all uncommon for Wolfe to spend paragraphs of a hand-wringing over theories that are wrong but alert the reader to a puzzle to be solved. An obvious example (to me) is when Silk spends 2 or 3 pages speculating on why he called Blood "son" on their first meeting. A good rule of thumb for reading Wolfe is that when a theory is offered, it is probably wrong in some key way (although in some ways correct as well).

2011-02-15 Lee Berman replying to Jordon Flato #

Proposes that the Neighbors' dream-travel happens between closely related time strands, so visited worlds keep much of the same physics while differing in some laws. Offers this as a guess in reply to David Stockhoff's claim that the Neighbors still need a sun and a planet, adding that Neighbors appear as faerie to Horn and others on Blue.
proposes · myth-and-structure · theories: Dream-travel occurs between closely related parallel time strands; Neighbors are faerie to Horn and Blue people

Yes, absolutely. My guess is that the dream-travel phenomenon is travel between two closely related time strands. So back on the "home planet", beings have to follow all the laws of the physical universe just as we do.

But when they travel to a close parallel time strand, they have travelled to another universe. Since the universe is different, some physical laws are no longer followed. But since it is a very close universe much is also the same.

Neighbors are faerie to Horn and others on Blue. Humans and inhumi are faerie to those they visit when they dream travel. I think it is something like that.

2011-02-15 James Wynn replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that Mr. Barnacles remains a Neighbor and fits the Midas pattern if Silk was a clone of Typhon, and suggests he may link to the Rajan as Severian's Apu Punchau encounter does. Responds to Lee Berman's Midas comparison and adds that Latro's curse shows divine curse and divine blessing are just ways of saying touched by a god.
supports · puzzle-solving · source-and-allusion · theories: Mr. Barnacles is a Midas-like Neighbor; Mr. Barnacles connects to the Rajan; Silk is a clone of Typhon; Latro's curse is a divine blessing

We have so little to go on regarding Ol' Barnacles. I've always wanted to connect him to the Rajan--sort of a Severian-Apu Punchau-style encounter or a Pike's Ghost encounter. Unfortunately, if that's true, we have as much hope of untwining that mystery as we did the Apu Punchau mystery without "The Urth of the New Sun", or Pike's Ghost without "The Book of the Short Sun".

On the other hand, I would say that still makes Mr. Barnacles a Neighbor.

If one presumes that Silk was a clone of Typhon, Barnacles-as-Midas works perfectly.

Incidentally, Latro's curse from the gods is also said to be a blessing. It causes him to lose his memory but also enables him to see the divine and be a mediator between the divine and the mundane--touch the divine and it becomes visible in the mundane realm. In other words, "divine curse" and "divine blessing" are explained to be just alternate terms for "touched by a god".

2011-02-15 Lee Berman replying to Jordon Flato #

Argues that the barnacled creature in Horn's passage is a genuine flesh-and-blood Neighbor, the only one Horn shows, and that Horn's account can be trusted on this point. Supports the claim by citing Horn's examples of people cursed by gifts from the gods, comparing them to Midas and the Nephilim, and suggesting the creature may be Blue's counterpart to Baldanders.
proposes · myth-and-structure · source-and-allusion · theories: The barnacled creature is a real Neighbor, Blue's version of Baldanders; Horn's account of cursed gifts is reliable

I agree with both of these assessments so I consider myself suitably chastened in regard to general Wolfean auctorial tropes. Still I think this particular passage is a somewhat unique example/exception.

Since we already know so much about Horn , I don't think these paragraphs are written primarily for expository revelation about the narrator. They really are about the crusty, barnacle creature.

With Blood and Silk and other elements of the story, we have multiple sections discussing these characters and their relationships. So we have a basis for correcting a wrong theory by a character. For this passage this is all we ever get regarding the crusty barnacle guy. So by what basis could we correct anything that Horn gets wrong?

I'm willing to accept enough of what Horn says to agree with the portion of Marc's theory that this is the only example of a fully flesh-and-blood Neighbor that we see.

Horn uses examples from The Whorl of people ostensibly blessed but really cursed by wealth or other gifts from the gods (James, interesting that you mention Blood) as the basis for his theory. To me this hearkens to similar examples from earth's own mythology, most obviously Midas (a reference I think would be of great interest to James). Perhaps this is how gifts from the gods on all whorls work (so what does that say about Severian and his divine gift?).

Wolfe makes the crusty creature so strong it swats Babbie aside like a fly. A bit speculative but this reminds me of Nephilim- biblical men of reknown, Baldanders etc. Perhaps this barnacle guy is Blue's version.

The Book of the Long Sun is plotted over the life of Aristaeus, as described by Robert Graves, as described by Herodotus, as described by Pindar (Soldier of the Mist). Aristaeus is the author of the story of the Hyperborans.
