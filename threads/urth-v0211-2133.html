horn + horn, sev + sev

Urth list · 5 messages · 2002-11-17 to 2002-11-19

2002urthThe Book of the New SunJoshua A. SolomonMatthew MalthouseJerry FriedmanMarc AraminiDon Doggett
The thread: Readers debated whether the tall figure in the pit on Green is a Vanished Person, a hybrid of Horn and the island, revived through Severian's auto-resurrection. maa32 built the case from Avern-leaf scenes, the spider reference, and the Neighbor's failure to resurrect Horn, and Don Doggett proposed eidolon-based reasoning tied to Blue being Ushas. Joshua A. Solomon accepted a synopsis of the revival but rejected its DNA point, while matthew.malthouse argued early readers lacked the clues to credit Severian or the Claw, so Agia's lie stays plausible. Jerry Friedman then objected that polyploidy is the wrong term for the proposed doubling of Bluvian animal organs, since it refers to chromosome sets. The thread ends without agreement, with the resurrection question unresolved and the terminology dispute left standing.
  • maa32: the pit figure is a Vanished Person, a Horn-island hybrid revived by Severian's auto-resurrection
  • Don Doggett: if Blue is Ushas, Severian would become a vanished person by absorbing his surroundings
  • Joshua A. Solomon: the revival synopsis is accurate, but its DNA point is wrong
  • matthew.malthouse: early readers lacked clues, so Agia's lie about the leaves remains credible
  • Jerry Friedman: polyploidy is the wrong word; dimerization would fit doubled organs better

Themes: Death and resurrection, Doubles, twins and clones, Robots, androids and artificial life

2002-11-17 Marc Aramini #

Argues that the tall figure in the pit on Green is a Vanished Person who is a hybrid of Horn and the island, with Severian's auto-resurrection enabling its revival of Horn, and links the blue glass to the Claw of the Conciliator. Builds the case on Avern-leaf scenes in The Shadow of the Torturer, the spider reference, and the Neighbor's inability to resurrect Horn in The Fifth Head of Cerberus.
proposes · puzzle-solving · myth-and-structure · theories: Tall pit figure is a Vanished Person hybrid of Horn; Neighbors are born from the donor's death; Severian's auto-resurrection revives Horn; Claw of the Conciliator is from a bush

Well, several readers believed that the tall creature in the pit may have been Severian on their first reading of OBW way back before 2000. Then, the spider reference made it clear that this was a vanished person, and not Sev (four arms and four legs). However, if we accept that Severian can become a vanished person, then this response is not absurd.

This is not the first time this has been done by Wolfe - when Severian is struck by the Avern leaf, you think that his own power somehow resurrects him - then looking back you think it's the claw, then looking back again you think it really is a part of Severian's power bestowed upon him by his association with the New Sun because the Claw is just like any other thorn, then looking back again you realize maybe the hierodules are messing with time and the thorn has Severian's DNA on it, etc. etc.

In any case, I had tried to reconcile the appearance of the neighbor Horn with Horn's resurrection, and I had thought that VP Horn was the one in the pit healing bald, human Horn - that VP Horn had been born of the union between the blood of Horn and the transgenic recombination inherent in the vegetable island of the pit.
I had thought that perhaps Horn hadn't died, but had somehow survived the transformation into a neighbor, thus splitting in two - four armed and legged Horn and normal bald Horn. I was wrong - he died. But the VP version of Severian may have brought him back to life - but not by any stretch the human Severian, who may have died a long time ago, or may still exist in two armed, two legged, gimpy form as the stiff-legged dude on Green. I posit that the mechanism through which Neighbors are born involves the death of the donor - Horn should have died to give birth to the hybrid VP Horn. Severian should have died to give birth to the hybrid VP Severian. However, both of them can cheat death through Severian's auto-resurrection. Later, the Neighbor on Green cannot resurrect Horn, they can only send him into Silk. This indicates that the ability to revive is limited to that special Vanished Person in the Pit. The blue glass all over the island does seem to resemble the blue glass that surrounds the Claw of the Conciliator. Perhaps the replicating factor of the island has attempted to replicate the miraculous restorative powers of the claw by spitting out copies, but failed. Perhaps the power of the claw - which is really the negation of time - indicates where the vanished people have gone - someWHEN else. And perhaps the claw is in the tree. Wait a minute - the claw is from a bush, eh? Mixed with Severian's blood, it does seem to have some small restorative powers. And remember that the green man has access to the corridors of time. But are there two Sevs, or one? Is there one left who has two arms and legs?
Marc Aramini

Argues that if Blue is Ushas, Severian would become a vanished person, since an eidolon absorbs food, water and air from its surroundings and so becomes real. Responds to Marc by citing the Hierodules' explanation in Apu Punchau's tomb and suggesting that environmental polyploidy would affect Severian, with the other Severians being visitors from the corridors of time.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: If Blue is Ushas, Severian becomes a vanished person; Eidolons become real by absorbing their environment; Other Severians are visitors from the corridors of time

I think it only stands to reason that Severian would become a vanished person if Blue is Ushas. Remember in Apu Punchau's tomb when the Hierodules explain to Severian that even though he was an eidolon, as he took in the food and water and air of his environment it would be incorporated into his body and make it real? Severian would most certainly evolve with his surroundings and whatever created the polyploidy in all the other creatures would affect him as well. The other Severian's are perhaps visitors from the corridors of time.

Argues that early readers of The Shadow of the Torturer lack the clues to credit Severian or the Claw with resurrective power, so Agia's lie about the leaves remains a credible explanation. Responds to Joshua A. Solomon's remark that a synopsis is off target except for the point about Sev's DNA, reasoning that later revelations about the Claw and the white fountain justify reassessing the matter.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Agia lied about the lethal leaves

Ummm... When on first reads SotT I don't think the reader can at that point have enough clues to endow either Severian or the Claw with such power.

Sev himself thinks that perhaps Agia lied about the lethal nature of the leaves and we do have enough knowledge of Agia at that point to find that explanation credible.

Later when Severian is lead to believe in the power of the Claw or later still when it becomes apparent that the power is that of the white fountain which will bring the new sun it's reasonable to reasses the matter.

2002-11-19 Jerry Friedman #

Argues that polyploidy is the wrong word for the proposed idea that Bluvian animals were made by doubling limbs and organs of Urth animals, since polyploidy refers to chromosome sets. Explains the term's meaning, notes plant-animal hybrids would not be called polyploid, and asks for a Greek-derived word such as dimerization instead.
disputes · source-and-allusion · theories: Bluvian animals produced by doubling Urth animals' limbs

Just thought I'd mention that polyploidy is a red herring. It means having a multiple (by an integer greater than 1) of the usual number of chromosomes. It has nothing to do with the number of limbs in animals. Hypothetical plant-animal hybrids might have a full set of chromosomes from each parent, I guess, but I don't think that would be called polyploidy.

I'm not refuting claims that Blue is Ushas, felwolves are descended from wolves (or wolfs), and the like (though those claims seem very unlikely to me--in contrast to the claim that something is very weird, possibly sentient, about the trees). I'm just saying that "polyploidy" isn't the word you want. For those who believe that the Bluvian animals were produced by doubling limbs, eyes, and whatever of Urth animals, can some Greek scholar come up with the word? Dimerization?

Jerry Friedman
