William Ansley answered that Adam Stephanides had already noticed the shift in voice, argued the change happened between the books, and insisted the narrator is the same Horn returned from the Whorl, so the alteration occurred near OBW's end. The thread is short and ends with Groce's view standing beside Ansley's, without any resolution.

2000-07-30 Ian Smith #

Asks why Horn's self-loathing in Book of the Short Sun's first volume, On Blue's Waters, vanishes by In Green's Jungles, given the short time gap between them. Proposes the mysterious forest encounter at the end of On Blue's Waters, where Horn is comforted in the dark, as a possible cause.
questions · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: The forest figure in On Blue's Waters caused Horn's change

Hi, I'm new to the list contribution-wise, although I've been listening (& learning) for a while now.
Finally my copy of IGJ has arrived, and I've been able to read all the recent posts, so I thought it might be worth asking a question which I don't think anyone has got around to discussing yet.

No-one seems to have queried yet the reason for the sudeen change in Horn between the first two volumes. At the end of OBW we leave Horn still wallowing to some extent in self-loathing, still questioning whether he should be writing his book. At the start of IGJ this has disapppeared, and throughout he confines himself to minor self-depreceatory remarks (very much in the manner of Silk...). As the time lapse between the end of writing the end of OBW and the start of OGJ is purportedly very short (I think there are hints that it is only around 2 days?) what as caused this transformation? Could it have something to do with the mysterious encounter in the woods before Horn meets brother and sister?

"I found him in he forest, sitting in the dark under the trees. I could not see him, it was too dark to see anything. But I knelt beside him, and lay my head upon his knee, and he comforted me."(OBW p.378)

At first I thought this was another neighbor encounter, but in IGJ Horn says he hasn't encountered them since they moved him to a new body. It could also be a particularly obtuse dream sequence, I suppose. Any ideas?

Ian

2000-08-01 Alex David Groce #

Argues that Horn's narrative voice changes between On Blue's Waters and In Green's Jungles because, no longer ruling a city as Silk, he is freed to become a holy man and a good man. Grounds this in his near death by the inhumi, his giving up his concubines, and his faithfulness to Nettle and Seawrack, closing with John 8:32.
supports · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: Horn's voice changes because he stops being Silk

I agree with this strongly. In my opinion the change stems at least partly from something (fairly) simple: Horn is no longer ruling a city "as Silk." Horn is freed to become something more like a holy man precisely because he is no longer in the false position of officially _being_ Silk. Horn's reluctance to identify himself with a man he sees as, unlike himself, good is key to his actually becoming a very good man himself. Another couple of things: (1) he's come very close to death by the inhumi--Wolfe's characters always grow quite a bit when they willingly face death and (2) I think he's much more at peace now that he's without his concubines and is being faithful to Nettle (and Seawrack!)
