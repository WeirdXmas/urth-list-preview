Themes: Beasts and the animal in us, Names and their meanings

1997-10-23 Alice Turner #

Argues that the animal identities in Wolfe's Long Sun books are settled by textual evidence: Nashwonk is a moose rather than an elk, the buffalo is ruled out by the antler fight, and Mimmunka reads as a lynx or bobcat. Responds to John's proposals by citing the chair-and-antler passage and Wolfe's quoted description of Cim as a mink, and rejects the idea that the Min are monkeys.
disputes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion · theories: Nashwonk is a moose; Cim is a mink; The Min are not monkeys

John,

Actually, I place Nashwonk as a moose rather than an elk, but a moose is a kind of elk, so either would be ok. But a buffalo can't cut it. Read the description of the fight with the chair, then mantis's clever gloss on how the points of the chair correspond to the points of the antlers. A buffalo, even the beloved sambhur, doesn't have that kind of equipment.

An African lion is what you're thinking of, and indeed he has the mane that Ketin enjoys. But there is a tawny mountain lion with a big shaggy head in the northwestern New World too, who stands in very well for Mr. and Mrs. Ketin. And the description of Mimmunka is, to my mind, clearly that of a lynx or bobcat; a leopard would be described quite differently (and would have spots!) What's more, dog was just a first guess for Cim; she is a mink, as the actual quotation from Wolfe (it's back there in the archive somewhere) and her own erratic behavior make clear.

The Min as monkeys is certainly a new idea---but not one that I take to.

-alga-

1997-10-23 John Bishop #

Asks the list to stop reposting material and quoting digest boilerplate, and then asks what the word "cim" means, since Cim Glowing says she is named after the "cim glowing on the trees" and it is not in his dictionary. Notes Wolfe usually uses obscure or archaic words rather than inventing them, and plans to check the OED.
questions · source-and-allusion

Fellow Wolfians:

Please don't post stuff twice, especially if the
second posting is HTML-source. It's ugly and takes
up space. Ditto for long quotes, especially quotes
of the boilerplate at the end of the digest.

On another matter, Cim Glowing says she is named after the "cim glowing on the trees" (approximate quote).

ideas what it is? Wolfe doesn't usually make up words, but he does use obscure or archaic ones. Tonight I'll check my wife's OED.

-John
