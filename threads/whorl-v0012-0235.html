Silk's suicide

Whorl list · 1 messages · 2001-02-14 to 2001-02-14

2001whorlThe Book of the Long SunAdam Stephanides

2001-02-14 Adam Stephanides #

Argues that Silk, grieving after his wife's death, likely attempted suicide by slashing himself and wrecking his house rather than being attacked by Pig or someone unknown. Grounds this in the text's description of oozing cuts on both arms and wrists, the Neighbor's statement that Silk's spirit is dying, and Silk's earlier suicide attempts. Responds to Robert Borski's quoted challenge to Adam Stephanides and vizcacha, and rebuts Alex David Gorce's claim that the jump is passive.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Silk committed suicide by slashing himself and wrecking his house

vizcacha may concede on the details, but I'm not about to. It seems quite plausible to me that Silk, insane with grief, wrecked his place. Nor is it impossible that he would slash himself, too, under the same impulse. Or the cuts on his face and neck may have been accidentally acquired while he was destroying things.

In further support of the suicide theory, we are told that Silk has "oozing cuts in both his arms and both his wrists." This sounds much more like a suicide attempt than like something inflicted by someone else, either randomly or deliberately. It's certainly unlikely as a result of Pig blindly flailing about, if that's what you want us to believe (it would make more sense for Pig to grab Silk, hold tight, and then stab; but that's not likely to leave the wounds Silk receives, either). And the alternative is that some unknown person or group decides, for some unknown reason, to leave Silk half-dead and trash his house, just when he has every reason to feel suicidal. (And I don't buy Alex David Gorce's argument that Silk kills himself only passively, either; jumping off an airship doesn't strike me as any more passive than slashing yourself.)

This is a possibility, but given that Silk has attempted suicide before and will again, and given the Neighbor's statement that Silk's "spirit is dying," suicide seems the likeliest bet.

--Adam
