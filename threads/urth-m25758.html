2014-11-11 Daniel Petersen replying to Marc Aramini #

Praises a video discussion that places Wolfe between modernism and postmodernism, arguing his fiction uses postmodern play to undermine postmodern fragmentation and subjectivity. Responds to Marc Aramini's video, singling out its section on Trip Trap, and notes the richness of Wolfe's work across reader, literary, philosophical and spiritual levels.
supports · genre-and-craft · theories: Wolfe uses postmodern trappings to undermine postmodern fragmentation

Great, Marc! Loved the section on Trip Trap especially. I think I've not consciously tried to understand where Wolfe fits in between Modernism and Postmodernism all this time and I think you summed it up nicely: he kind of uses the trappings of postmodernism to undermine its commitment to utter fragmentation and subjectivity. Very sly, as you'd expect from Wolfe. I think that's why his works are so rich at many levels: that of reader enjoyment/engagement, that of deep literary analysis, that of philosophy and theology, that of sheer spirituality, that of symbolism, and so on. You get all the 'play' and 'reader-response' of pomo but with a profound sense of a deeper underlying structure that really stays put, so real it can make you shiver.

Thanks. I look forward to the next.

-DOJP
