Why did Vodalus risk...?

The thread: Aaron asked why Vodalus, Hildegrin and Thea would risk robbing a necropolis grave in The Shadow of the Torturer, whose corpse it was, and whether Thea's presence points to a family member as the target. Bruce Hayles argued that Vodalus joins from character motives: he shares his men's risks, and his pride leads him to underrate the watchmen's danger, as shown by his remark to Thea and his giving up the gun. Jeff Wilson proposed that the body belongs to a loyal follower and would be used to bind people and perhaps turn spies with the alzabo drug. All three posts came on the same day, with no reply or agreement recorded, so the thread ended without resolution.

2007-07-25 aaron #

Asks why Vodalus, Hildegrin and Thea risked robbing a grave in the necropolis in The Shadow of the Torturer, and whose corpse they wanted. Raises whether Thea's presence points to a family member being the target, and rules out Thecla as a first guess.
questions · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Thea's family member was the grave robbery target

I have a question for you guys, and hopefully you can offer me some insight here. I am on my umpteenth reread of TSotT, and I only just realized what a great risk Vodalus takes when he, Hildegrin, and Thea rob the grave in the necropolis. My next thought was: Why would he take such a risk? Or, for Whom? Whose corpse did they take? Obviously, they wanted a particular person's body, or else why would they go so far out of their way and (again) take such a risk? And does Thea's presence there indicate anything? Perhaps a member of her family was their target. Why else would she tag along on such a dangerous mission? Couldn't possibly be Thecla's body, as that was my first thought. Sorry, but this is driving me up the wall! Thanks to all in advance. If I come up with a good theory on the subject, I will post it, but so far I have nothing.

Aaron Singleton

2007-07-25 Bruce Hayles replying to aaron #

Argues that Vodalus joins the grave robbery in the necropolis from character motives: he leads by sharing his men's risks, and his pride makes him underrate the watchmen's danger. Grounds this in the opening chapter of The Shadow of the Torturer, where Vodalus tells Thea that declining risks would look bad to the others, and in his giving up the gun and being saved by Severian, who is repaid with a false coin.
proposes · close-textual-reading · genre-and-craft · theories: Vodalus's character explains his risk-taking

In the opening chapter of Shadow, Thea says something to Vodalus that Severian does not catch; Vodalus says in response, "You didn't have to come, Thea. How would it look to the others if I took none of the risks?"
This reminds me of generals fighting at the front of their army. By taking the same risks as his men, he gains their respect. Vodalus is also very prideful. He does not seriously appreciate the danger of the watchmen. He disdains his earlier use of the gun, and when the watchmen are nearly upon them, gives the gun to Hildegrin, saying that Hildegrin might need it. This is despite the fact that he most desperately needs it, since he is holding off the watchmen. His pride drives him needlessly to fight them with his sword. When Severian saves him, he is repaid with a false coin.
Wives of generals would come along with them on risky campaigns, so Thea coming along doesn't seem implausible, even if it is unnecessary for the task.
I think the explanation of Vodalus taking the risk is Vodalus's character.

2007-07-25 Jeff Wilson replying to aaron #

Proposes that Vodalus risks the grave robbery in the necropolis to recover the body of a loyal follower, so it can be used to bind people and possibly convert spies to his side with the alzabo drug. Responds to aaron's question about why Vodalus would take such a risk in The Shadow of the Torturer, offering a motive based on the story's events.
proposes · puzzle-solving · theories: Vodalus robs the grave to recover a loyal follower's body; Alzabo can bind and convert spies to rebel allegiance

My thought is that the body is that of a particularly loyal, devoted follower, and he is there personally to secure it for use in further binding and perhaps even converting people to his side with the alzabo drug. A sufficiently powerful inherited personality may even turn a spy or double agent among the rebels to true allegiance.
