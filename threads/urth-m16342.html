Themes: Deep time and ruins

2011-08-07 Sergei SOLOVIEV #

Argues that Donaldson's and Moorcock's prose styles are what the poster dislikes, not their heroes, and contrasts Moorcock's erudition with Tolkien's use of history and languages to build depth. Responds to Jeff's claim that he found the heroes repulsive, clarifying his objection is to the writing rather than the characters.
disputes

Dear Jeff,

I didn' t write that the heroes (or anti-heroes) - Covenant and Elric - were repulsive to me. I wrote
that the books were (their authors writing style). I don't remember to be shocked by the "rape
scene" from Donaldson (I was not a teen), but I remember an intense dislike of how the
book was written.

I glanced at the "Elric". I have to admit that this time (after many years) my dislike
was much less, I was just bored.

I'll try to explain. There is no doubt that Moorcock is quite an erudite. But I don't like
how he uses his erudition. To me it is like a badly done theatric background - the pieces
of this and that squeezed into some rough picture.

It is told that Elrik is in some way antagonist to the heroes of Tolkien. To Conan, that
you mention, as well.

But Tolkien used his knowledge of history, folklore, languages to create depth,
what is most impressive in his "Lord of the Rings" is more than 10000 years of
history subtly influencing the present

2011-08-08 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes replying to Sergei SOLOVIEV #

Recommends Steven Erikson to readers who admire Tolkien's sense of deep history, arguing that Erikson draws on his anthropology and archaeology to build a vast past that shapes the present. Responds directly to Sergei Soloviev's praise of Tolkien's ten-thousand-year history, offering a comparable author rather than discussing Wolfe's fiction.
proposes

If you like that aspect of JRRT, you should definitely check out Steven Erikson. He is an anthropologist/archaeologist rather than a linguist, but he uses his own areas of expertise, much as Tolkien did, to create a huge sense of the abysmal past and how it continues to influence the story's present.
