The thread: Readers discussed Interlibrary Loan in a spoiler-free thread, with Dave Lebling arguing it is hard science fiction and describing a first reading as opaque but compelling. Ab de Vos framed the book as a set of riddles, listing open questions about the Erns and the ending, while Dan'l Danehy-Oakes and eb reported finishing it and found the last pages confusing. Evan Pickett asked whether the book was rushed, feeling the final chapters were missing material, and Marc Aramini replied that Wolfe submitted it shortly before his death. Factory Farmer suggested the sketchy ending was expected because more volumes were planned, and the thread ended without resolution.

  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: the last four pages were hard to follow, especially the final line
  • Evan Pickett: the last three chapters seem to lack about 100 pages of material

  • Factory Farmer: the sketchy ending was expected because later volumes were planned

Themes: Unreliable narration, Ghosts, dreams and visions, Stories within stories

2020-07-18 Dave Lebling #

Argues that Interlibrary Loan is hard science fiction rather than fantasy, going against many reviews, and describes the first reading as opaque, disjointed, and by turns weird, scary and funny. Reports that re-reading A Borrowed Man first made it clearer, and laments that Interlibrary Loan could have used another draft and will never get one.
proposes · genre-and-craft · theories: Interlibrary Loan is hard SF rather than fantasy

Has anyone read this, and if so, do you have thoughts?

I re-read *A Borrowed Man* before reading *Interlibrary Loan*. I had found *A Borrowed Man* to be very opaque on first reading, and much more reasonable on a second reading. (This is my normal pattern with Wolfe; each reading produces more clarity.)

I found the first reading of *Interlibrary Loan* to be even more opaque, and also a bit disjointed. It was by turns weird, scary, and funny. I believe it is actually hard SF rather than Fantasy, which seems to go against a lot of reviews. It could have used another draft. It is extremely sad we will never get one.

Dave Lebling

2020-07-18 Ab de Vos replying to Dave Lebling #

Argues that Interlibrary Loan is built as a set of riddles, listing open questions about how many Erns there are, whether the storyline is scrambled, what is in the green box, and what becomes of the key Ern takes from a dead Ern. Responds to Dave Lebling's request for thoughts, noting the ghosts' status and the story's link to A Borrowed Man, and calling the book fascinating but frustrating.
questions · puzzle-solving

I read it as well and everything in the book seems to be a riddle. How many Erns are there, is the story line mixed up, what is in the green box, what happens to the key Ern takes from dead Ern, the status of the ghosts, the relation of the book to Borrowed Man etc.

Fascinating but frustrating.

2020-07-18 Evan Pickett replying to eb #

Asks whether the book was definitively finished or rushed to press, saying the last three chapters feel like about 100 pages of material are missing. Responds to the spoiler-free thread about reading the book, and notes it is hard to tell whether such gaps are intentional in Wolfe.
questions

I read it and (I think) kept up with it until the last ~3 chapters. Does anyone know if the book was definitively finished, or was it finished enough that the publishers want ahead? Not casting aspersion; just curious. It felt like there was another 100 pages of material in the last couple chapters, that just wasn't there. With Wolfe, it's hard to say if that's intentional or not.

Evan

2020-07-18 Marc Aramini replying to Evan Pickett #

Reports that Wolfe finished and submitted the novel shortly before his death, and suggests the sketchy final chapters may reflect that timing rather than design. Responds to Evan Pickett's question about whether the book was definitively finished, noting the pace accelerates at the end.
answers · author-and-biography · theories: Final chapters are sketchy because Wolfe was dying

Wolfe finished it and turned it in very shortly before he died. I do feel that the sketchiness of the last few chapters might have something to do with that fact, though of course the green square is present throughout. Certainly the pace accelerates at the end.
