Themes: Justice, torture and mercy

1997-07-23 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Asks whether the torture device that writes on its victim in Wolfe's Matachin tower derives from historical practice rather than fiction, and recalls Kafka's writing machine that writes until death. Responds to John Bishop's impression by playfully linking a Holly catching a criminal to Catcher in the Rye, and reads the Matachin device as a non-lethal tattooing apparatus.
questions · source-and-allusion

John Bishop,

Ah, but since Holly "catches" a criminal . . . (is there any "rye" around when she does it?)

That it was just your impression is good enough. We'll all make a note of it and see if anything else bubbles up.

Re: the torture device that writes on the victim. Are you sure that this isn't from history rather than fiction? And didn't the Kafka device write and write and write on the victim until death ensued (or am I thinking of some other work here?)? The device in the Matachin tower seems more like a "Scarlet Letter" brand Autodoc-cum-tatooiton, that is, not lethal by itself.
