Themes: Beasts and the animal in us

1998-06-16 Tony Ellis #

Argues that the St Anne carabao episode in Caraboo-hoo is not evidence of shape-shifting but suggests the boundary between human and animal is blurred on the planet. Grounds this in Marsch's repeated shooting of local fauna, the tears of Marsch and VRT when the carabao dies, and VRT's childhood list of 'people' that includes animals and trees.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Animals of St Anne are people too; Human-animal distinction blurred on St Anne

Nor me. It's funny how any evidence for shape-shifting sort of melts away when you look at it closely, isn't it? (The one exception being the Old Wise One's mention of this ability - that's currently the only reason I think shape-shifting has anything to do with St Anne.)

As you say, Marsch's cruelty seems to be significant, and the tears too. And why does Wolfe _keep_ showing us Marsch gunning down the local fauna?

My impression is that this is all meant to suggest that the animals of St Anne might be "people" too. Not in the sense that they are all some sort of morphing alien race with a big SFx budget, but simply that on St Anne the distinction between human and animal is blurred. There are numerous references to the Annese being just "animals shaped like people", and also hints of the reverse. When VRT lists the "people" he used to see when living in the wilderness with his mother he includes animals, birds, trees and Shadow Children as if there was no distinction between them at all. He, too, cries when the carabao is killed.

That would make all those other animals Marsch so cheerfully shoots "people" too. Sort of ironic, given that Marsch wants so badly to meet the native Annese, no?

1998-06-16 adam louis stephanides #

Proposes that the shape-shifting animals of V.R.T.'s St Anne may derive from observed phenomena, Annese lore, or Veil's Hypothesis, rather than being settled fact. Responds to Tony Ellis's doubts about the morphing carabao theory by comparing the ambiguous human-like beasts to the intelligent creatures in Tracking Song whom characters kill and eat.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: St Anne shape-shifting derives from Annese lore; St Anne animals derive from Veil's Hypothesis

And this occurs in a work of fiction by V.R.T.; we don't know if it reflects something he saw or thought he saw, is derived from Annese lore, or is his own extrapolation from Veil's Hypothesis.

There was a similar phenomenon in "Tracking Song." There, too, you had characters who were intelligent, and who looked like people, but whom other characters regarded as "just animals," and quite cheerfully killed and even ate.

--Adam
