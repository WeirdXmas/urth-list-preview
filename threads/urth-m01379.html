2005-03-22 James Wynn replying to thalassocrat #

Argues that "Overcyn" is a coined word combining Old English-style "cyn" (people, kin) with "over," likely echoing Uber-as-superior versus under-as-inferior, rather than a surviving old word. Supports this with the etymology of "cyn" from Icelandic and the claim that "over" is Indo-European, and reads it as both "the people above us" and a superior-race connotation.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Overcyn coined by Wolfe from cyn and over; Overcyn echoes Ubermensch and undermensch

"cyn" is Icelandic for people, race, and yes "kin" as in people related to each other and by extrapolation "my kin" or relatives.

Over is from Indoeuropean, so AFAIK there is no old word "overcyn". Wolfe made it up. One could translate it merely as "the people above us" as well I suppose but I think the connection to "Uber=over or super (as in ubermensch) as opposed to Under = inferior, sub, below (as in undermensch) is also reasonable and likely intended.

~ Crush
