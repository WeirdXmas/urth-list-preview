2001-04-26 Dave Lebling #

Disputes William Ansley's claim that Pullman's third book is an anti-Catholic screed, arguing the attack targets authoritarianism common to many religions, including Calvinism. Points to the fictional detail that John Calvin became pope in that universe as evidence that the critique is not specifically Catholic.
disputes · close-textual-reading

(I previously sent this reply to the Whorl list by mistake, apologies to all for that.)

Really? Anti-Christian, perhaps, but specifically anti-Catholic? Am I misremembering, but in this universe, didn't John Calvin become pope? It seems to me Pullman is mostly attacking the authoritarian streak that is wide in many religions, including Calvinism and Catholicism.

And I wouldn't characterize it as even "thinly-disguised.".

Dave Lebling

2001-04-26 William H. Ansley #

Concedes that the poster's earlier claim about Pullman's anti-Catholic attack was mistaken, and explains that the concern was about the vehemence of the anti-religious message in books aimed at children rather than the message itself. Responds to David Lebling, who had pointed out that Calvin becomes pope in Pullman's universe and that the target is authoritarianism across religions.
disputes

Yup, I was wrong.

I wrote too quickly based on already-fading memories (my poor, poor brain) which turned out to be even less reliable than usual.

I felt I should warn people on this list of a facet of the books that was potentially offensive; that was unnecessary. All of the people who post messages to this list are obviously too sophisticated to be offended by any such thing.

I was bothered enough to do so not because of the anti-religious message itself, but because of the vehemence with which Pullman presents it and because these books are putatively for children, who are likely, even in this day and age, to possess much less sophistication than those who write here.

William Ansley
