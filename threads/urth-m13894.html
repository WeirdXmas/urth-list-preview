Juturna, the Missing Sister

Urth list · 27 messages · 2010-07-27 to 2010-08-01

2010urthThe Book of the New SunJohn WatkinsJerry FriedmanRyan DunnLee BermanAdam ThorntonJack SmithJosh GellerGerry QuinnDavid StockhoffJames WynnJeff WilsonDan'l Danehy-Oakes
The thread: Readers debated whether Juturna is Severian's missing sister and whether hair colour, names and appearance can prove family ties in The Book of the New Sun and related books. Lee Berman, Jeff Wilson and James Wynn argued for genealogical readings using hair colour, Mendelian inheritance and undine ancestry, while John Watkins and Dan'l Danehy-Oakes countered that colour proves nothing and Wolfe gave such details little thought. A second strand, begun by Jerry Friedman, asked whether the child in Baldanders' bed is a catamite, with Jack Smith, Gerry Quinn and brunians offering son, replacement or fuel-source readings. The thread ended in impasse, with each side still holding its theory and some posters conceding points.
  • Lee Berman: Juturna is Severian's sister, and hair colour and names point to a family curse and genealogy
  • John Watkins: hair colour cannot prove kinship since any colour can be fitted to any relation
  • Jeff Wilson: family ties explain Juturna's strategy and attraction, with dominance rules and Mendelian genetics as support
  • James Wynn: hair colour is a family signature linking Silk to Typhon, and Wolfe gave Typhon much thought
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: Typhon's appearance is a chance encounter and the Long and Short Sun books are an afterthought
  • Jerry Friedman: Severian twice doubts the boy is a catamite, so the claim should not be repeated

Themes: Fathers, mothers and children, Mirrors, pictures and art, Names and their meanings

2010-07-27 Lee Berman #

Argues that Juturna is Severian's missing sister and that the mutual sexual ardor between Severian and Juturna signals a family incest curse, which the New Sun breaks. Proposes that Jolenta may be Severa, the absent twin, with Severa's name a stage name, and suggests Severian's witch meeting in the Stone Town aimed to save Jolenta.
proposes · puzzle-solving · theology-and-religion · theories: Juturna is Severian's sister; Jolenta is Severa, Severian's twin; Severian family incest curse broken by New Sun; Severian's Stone Town meeting aimed to save Jolenta

Jeff Wilson-

Well, many major power players including Father Inire, The Old Autarch and The Cumaean (via Hildegren) explicitly state that their strategy involves playing both sides so as to benefit no matter who wins. Why not Abaia?

The fountain connection is nice but for me the strongest evidence for a family connection between Severian and Juturna is the ridiculous, apparently unfulfillable, sexual ardor they share for each other. Sounds like the incestuous Severian family curse in action. Seems even stronger than what Severian and (his cousin?) Agia feel, so maybe "sister" is a good assessment.

This family curse is shared by all the family pantheons of pagan gods. Who did they have (initially) to mate with but each other? Adam and Eve's kids faced a similar problem (as perhaps did Adam and Eve- clones via rib?).

In my interpretation, the lust between Severian and Juturna is gone at their meeting on Ushas because the family curse of incest has been broken by the coming of the New Sun as the pagan curse on Earth was broken by the Flood and the rise of Judeo-Christianity with its immaculate conception and virgin birth. But yes, surely Juturna carries some potential for demon-spawn within herself.

Still, when you say "missing" sister I have to think of Severian's absent twin, Severa. I have a difficult time tracing a Juturna lifestory which allows for that identity switch. Actually, among all the candidates for Severa, I find only one has the potential for Severa as a real name- Jolenta, which could be only her stage name.

We expect Severa to have been a witch (exiled?) and we don't know what Jolenta was doing before her months as a waitress. Wolfe hides her hair color when Severian first meets her then seems to reveal it with the "newly hatched chick" thing. Borski thinks the "gold" hair is a sign for Severian family-hood.

There is a lot of dialog to the contrary but I must wonder whether Jolenta was the real purpose for Severian's meeting with the witches in the Stone Town ...trying to save the life of one of their own, via Severian's power. Perhaps similar to Severian's "accidental" resurrection of Dorcas from the Lake of Birds.

2010-07-27 John Watkins replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that hair colour can be made to fit any relation in the Severian family tree, so colour cannot prove a genealogy. Responds to Lee Berman's hair-colour genealogy by granting it works, then citing the claim that Baldanders' overgrown child with gold hair is Severian's child, showing how each colour could be read as supporting a theory.
disputes · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Baldanders' overgrown child is Severian's biological child; Hair colour establishes Severian family relationships

That works just fine. I dunno about Cyriaca being the mother of Agia and Agilus but I have no strong feelings about it either way.

My issue is that in reverse, hair of any color can fit into the Severian family tree. And so we see Borski saying that Baldanders' overgrown child (and possible catamite), who has gold hair, is Severian's biological child. Because he has gold hair! And gold represents the sun!

But if he had dark hair, he'd look like Severian, and so also could be argued to be Severian's kid.

And if he had pale blonde hair, he'd look like Dorcas.

And with "chestnut" hair, he might be related to Agia and Agilus--maybe even be their incest-spawn--and therefore Severian.

Red hair would be proof of an Inire connection, as you note.

At some point, every detail becomes a prop for one wild theory or another, and the loop closes.

2010-07-27 Jeff Wilson replying to James Wynn #

Argues that hair colour in the Severian family follows dominance rules, with darker hair dominating lighter and red counted as a form of blonde. Responds to James Wynn's claim that hair colour is a family signature by offering general genetic reasoning, adding that skin colour is a mixture.
supports · close-textual-reading · theories: Hair colour follows dominance rules in the family

Generally darker hair dominates over lighter, though hair can appear as lighter colors at longer lengths and with increasing exposure to the elements. Red hair counts as a form of blonde in this way. In general, color also dominates darker over lighter. Skin's a mix.

Gold hair by itself just isn't enough.

Jurupari shares its name with a cichlid family member, also called Demon Fish.
