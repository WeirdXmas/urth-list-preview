2002-06-10 James Jordan #

Argues that the Urth of the New Sun is a cycle before ours, so events there predate any covenant like Noah's and God need not have governed that cycle as he governs ours. Responds to Steve S.'s point that a remnant of the church may survive on other worlds, using the cosmic-cycle framing to support the broader claim.
disputes · theology-and-religion · myth-and-structure · theories: Urth is a universe-cycle before ours; Urth events predate the Noah covenant

I think that's reasonable, but also...

.... remember that Urth is a universe-cycle before ours. These events predate the promise to Noah by about 50 billion years maybe. We don't know that such a promise had been given to any Urnoah in Urth history. Nor, returning to the first point, do we have to assume that God managed Briah precisely the same way He manages our cycle.

2002-06-14 James Jordan #

Agrees with Rostrum that Typhon may have unknowingly built Christian-sounding references into his parodies of religion, while recounting that Wolfe rejected the idea of God as torturer. Cites Wolfe's reaction to the Shadow of the Torturer reading, which the poster presents as an argument against seeing Severian as God's agent, and prefers the poster's own reading.
supports · theology-and-religion · author-and-biography · theories: Typhon unknowingly incorporated real divine references; Severian as God's shadow-agent torturing the world

Good point. Hadn't thought of it that way. Just what Typhon might well have done.

Well put.

Oddly, Wolfe reacted quite negatively to the God As Torturer idea when I presented it to him. I thought "Shadow of the Torturer" meant that Severian was God's shadow-agent, torturing the world through distresses toward perfection. But Wolfe did not like that. No, no. The whole torture business is Severian's horrible background out of which he is struggling to emerge as a more decent person. As for me, I like MY idea better! It certainly seems to fit the overall narrative better.
