Themes: Ghosts, dreams and visions, Memory and forgetting

1997-01-27 Dave Lebling #

Proposes that the dead relatives Horn sees in Mainframe are built from the viewer's own memories, with public records as scaffolding, and links this to near-death experiences as a possible guide to Silk's visions. Offered as an answer to an unnamed question about how one sees dead kin when most people are outside the Windows, Glasses and Monitors.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Mainframe visions of dead relatives are built from the viewer's memories

The question was raised (I don't recall by whom) of how one can see one's dead relatives in Mainframe if most people spend their lives (and deaths) outside the view of Windows, Glasses, and Monitors. I should think the answer is obvious. The data about the dead relatives is extracted from the _viewer_. That is, when (say) Horn talks to his relatives, it is his own memories of them that are used to create what he sees(*), perhaps with whatever "public record" scaffolding is available to Mainframe to firm them up.

Dave Lebling
([email])

ps: Most sceptics (and I am one) will tell you that this is what is happening during "near-death" experiences as well; perhaps a guide to interpreting Silk's?
