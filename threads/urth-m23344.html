The thread: Marc Aramini opened by proposing that Zelazny's 1977 story "No Award" is an in-joke aimed at Wolfe, tied to the 1973 Nebula mix-up over The Island of Doctor Death and to Wolfe's later The Death of Doctor Island. In follow-ups he recounted the episode itself, saying Wolfe was announced as winner before it emerged that no award had been given. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes reported Zelazny's own explanation that he titled the story to sit just before No Award on alphabetized ballots, though it was never nominated. Mo Holkar corrected the premise, saying Wolfe never won and that his story placed highest among the remaining contenders. The thread ends with the in-joke reading contested on factual grounds rather than resolved.
  • Marc Aramini: Zelazny's story is an in-joke about Wolfe's Nebula mix-up, echoed in Death of Doctor Island
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: Zelazny said he titled it to land before No Award on alphabetical ballots
  • Mo Holkar: Wolfe never won; Asimov erred in claiming a winner when No Award won

Proposes that Zelazny's 1977 story "No Award", about a man whose split brain hemispheres hold rival personalities, is an in-joke aimed at Wolfe. Grounds this in the 1973 Nebula mix-up over The Island of Doctor Death, and in Wolfe's later The Death of Doctor Island, which inverts that story with a severed corpus callosum.

2012-05-22 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes replying to Marc Aramini #

Reports an interview in which Roger Zelazny explained why he titled his story "No Award": since ballots list authors alphabetically, he kept landing just before the No Award option and wanted to create confusion. Grounds this in that remembered interview and notes the story was never nominated, so the scheme failed; it responds to Marc Aramini's joking remark about the short story project.
informs

Heavens, no it isn't. I've been wondering if you could get a book out of it, actually.

I read an interview with Roger Zelazny, and he said that the reason he called the story "No Award" was because he noticed that, since the ballots were alphabetical by author, he always wound up right before No Award. He wanted to have two "No Award"s in a row and cause confusion. Unfortunately for his dastardly plot ( :) ), it didn't get nominated for anything.
