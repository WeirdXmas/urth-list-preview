Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Myth and folklore sources

2010-12-16 Andrew Mason #

Argues that a phrase Silk's vision may draw from the Chrasmalogical writings more likely comes from those writings than from Silk himself, and points to a similar allusion in On Blue's Waters. Supports the claim by comparing Marble's line about the sign of the fish's belly with the Gospel of Matthew's account of Jonah, noting that the text does not explicitly attribute it to the writings.
proposes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Marble's fish's belly line quotes the Chrasmalogical writings

I think some people see this as part of Silk's vision (which may be why it worries them that the theology is not correct). I would agree that it's more likely to be from the C.W.

Another likely reference is in OBW, where Marble says 'The city searches the sky for a sign, but no sign shall it have but the sign from the fish?s belly.' (Compare Matthew 12.39-40. 'an evil and adul;terous genration asks for a sign, but none will be given it except the sign of the prophet Jonah. For Jonah was three days and three nights in the belly of a huge fish...') Again, this is not explicitly said to be from the C.W., but it reads to me more like that than a spontaneous remark by Marble
