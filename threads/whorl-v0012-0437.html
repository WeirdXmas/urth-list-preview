The thread: Readers debated Typhon's physical nature in The Shadow of the Torturer: whether he is a Neighbor and what his hidden body could be. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued Typhon had one head before transplant onto Piaton's body, rejecting the Neighbor theory and proposing an engineered in-vitro birth. Robert Borski countered that Typhon's head was grafted onto Piaton, quoting Chapter XXV, and compared him to the many-headed Classical Typhon. Alex David Groce accepted the correction on graft direction but kept open questions about the Neighbors' language and their human-looking faces. The thread ended with the disagreement unresolved, shifting to the Neighbors' appearance rather than settling Typhon's status.
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: Typhon had one head before transplant, so he is not a Neighbor; proposes engineered in-vitro birth
  • Robert Borski: Typhon is not a Neighbor since his head was grafted onto Piaton; hidden body could be anything, citing Chapter XXV
  • Alex David Groce: Accepts Typhon's body is grafted onto Piaton's head, but Neighbors' language and human-looking faces remain open questions

Themes: Robots, androids and artificial life, Beasts and the animal in us, Myth and folklore sources

2001-03-12 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that Typhon had one head before his transplant onto Piaton's body, reasoning from why the surgery was done to keep 'the face'. Rejects the 'Typhon is a Neighbor' theory and the wiggle room it needs, and proposes instead a genetically engineered in-vitro birth, responding to the earlier suggestion of many heads or limbs.
disputes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Typhon was originally a single-headed man; Typhon is a gengineered in-vitro birth; Typhon is a Neighbor

Some of us, as it happens, _have_ reread SWORD recently. My copy isn't here at work with me, but... Do you recall _why_ he did a head transplant instead of just putting his brain into Piaton's body? "The face." One. People were accustomed to obeying this one face. Reasonable conclusion: Typhon-pre-surgery had one face and thus, most likely, one head.

Supporting evidence: A race with two or more heads (in, at least, the usual meaning of this phrase) and only one personality would _not_ have the personality "stored" in a single head. Either it would be distributed among heads, or it would be somewhere else (cf. Pierson's Puppeteers). But Typhon is a single, apparently normal human, head, grafted onto Piaton's body.

Yes, but not for the "Typhon is a Neighbor" theory. (When a theory starts needing wiggle room, it's probably time to abandon it.) Much more plausible (particularly given the level of biotech that produced Silk and Mucor) is the idea that he was a gengineered in-vitro "birth."

Actually, I picture him as looking like Ricardo Montalban...

--Blattid

2001-03-12 Robert Borski #

Argues that Typhon is not a Neighbor, since Typhon's head was grafted onto Piaton's body rather than the reverse, and that Typhon's hidden body could be anything. Supports the point by quoting Typhon's line in Chapter XXV of The Shadow of the Torturer, which says he was grafted to Piaton, and by comparing him to the many-headed Classical Typhon.
disputes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion · theories: Typhon is a Neighbor

<Fine and well, but you still haven't dealt with the fact that when Severian meets Typhon in SWORD, Typhon doesn't remotely resemble a Neighbor, in any way, form, or fashion. Indeed, if he's a Neighbor, the grafting of a human's head onto his shoulder seems unlikely in the extreme.>

Then batting second Kevin Maroney added:

<Pas's second head is artificial. This is explicitly stated in _New Sun_. Typhon doesn't show any evidence of having any other attribute of the Neighbors, including a long nose, an excess of limbs, etc. I wouldn't presume to call something that obvious a "coincidence", but I think that the connection is more subtle than that.>

Gentlemen, you both need to reread SWORD or increase your dosage of gingko biloba because your memory apparatus is slightly skewed.

Observe what Typhon tells Severian near the end of Chapter XXV:

"No, I was not born as I am, or born at all, as you meant it. Nor was Piaton grafted to me. _I was grafted to him._"

So no, of course, Piaton-Typhon doesn't resemble a Neighbor in any way, form or fashion because Piaton is a human and Typhon's head is grafted to him, not Piaton's head to Typhon's body.

For all we know Typhon's body--the part we never see (although I hear the autopsy photos may soon appear on FOX)--could have any number of limbs, or even one hundred heads, like the Typhon of Classical Mythology.

Factoring in the "I was not born as I am, or born at all" leaves even more wriggle room, no?

Robert Borski

2001-03-13 Alex David Groce #

Argues that Typhon's body is grafted onto Piaton's head, not the reverse, and that this still leaves the Neighbors' language and the Mother's knowledge of it open to question. Responds to Robert's quotation from Chapter XXV by granting the correction, then defends the claim that Neighbors have human-looking faces and that Horn's need to ask Urth Scylla fits Seawrack's teaching.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Typhon is grafted onto Piaton's head; Neighbor language derives from space-travelers' tongue

OK, you (sort of) have us here. Kevin & I were thinking of Piaton's head being the graft-on, but of course it's the other way around (otherwise Typhon doesn't get a studly young body out of the deal, and the nervous system isn't in Piaton's control so Severian can kill Typhon). BUT, this doesn't really change things much. Is your claim that Neighbor's faces look human enough that Severian notices nothing odd? That "The _man_ had two heads, like an ogre..." is notable, but that one of these heads is alien is not? Severian mentions both heads when he discusses the corpse in chapter XXIV, and the distinugishing feature is hair color.

Blattid gives a pretty strong rebuttal of the idea that old Typhon had two heads (if Neighbors have two heads) and the explanation of the "born as I am" he gives seems dead-on to me.

Well, again, there is a question of what is meant when Urth Scylla tells Silkhorn how to contact the Mother, but that's a question about Scylla's knowledge of the language(*). It would be pretty strange if the Mother, who is a goddess of the Neighbors and has been on the planet before the arrival of the Whorl, I assume, did NOT know their language. From that to Seawrack's knowledge is obvious. Now, why Horn has to ask Urth Scylla, if Seawrack has taught him something of this language (which the translation in IGJ indicates) is a bit puzzling, but I'm not sure that "how to communicate" necessarily means "how to speak her language." If I give you someone's email or phone number, it could be said I'm telling you how to communicate with them, even you already share a common tongue with them.

(*) Well, she's her "sister." I take it that Abaia, Erebus, Scylla, etc. are all definitely space-travelers. So if it is a common language that's the issue here, they might have both learned Neighbor from their "Fodor's Blue/Green System for Great Old Ones" or Neighbor may be linguistically derived from the tongue of the Space Monsters.
