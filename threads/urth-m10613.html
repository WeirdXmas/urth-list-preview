The thread: Posters debated whether Urth is a Christ-world, whether the Hierogrammates were created by Urthlings, and whether Severian is a true or false Christ figure. Antonio Pedro Marques argued that each Briah has its own Jesus or that Urth's lack of a redeemer is theologically problematic, while James Wynn, Ryan Dunn and Lee Berman held that Severian is Christ-like or Christ-figure on Urth without a historical Jesus. Gerry Quinn pressed dating and anachronism points, including whether the Citadel's craft are not from the 1960s, and Jeff Wilson offered a post-Rapture reading. The thread ended without agreement, sprawling across many overlapping sub-debates.
  • António Pedro Marques: every Briah has its own equally good Jesus, and Urth's lack of a redeemer is theologically troubling
  • James Wynn: Severian is the Christ of a gnostic Briah and there was no historical Jesus, so Urth needs no other Jesus
  • Ryan Dunn: Severian is a false or inverted Christ figure, Christlike but not Christ, with Urth forgetting Jesus
  • Gerry Quinn: Urth's dating allows a local Jesus, saint names may be translation artefacts, and the ships are not Cold War craft
  • David Stockhoff: early Christianity did promise an earthly paradise, deferred only after it became the state religion
  • Lee Berman: raised the thought experiment of a Church without Christ and questioned whether understanding Wolfe is scientific or religious

Themes: Christ figures and messiahs, Catholic faith and sacraments, Gnosticism, heresy and other theologies

Proposes that the hierogrammates were created by Earthlings, so that Severian's bringing of the New Sun might renew Briah and the hierogrammates' future. proposes · allegory-and-typology · theology-and-religion · theories: Earthlings created the hierogrammates; Severian renews Briah by bringing the New Sun; Severian and Apheta have a child who is the hierogrammates' creature

I thought it was pretty clear that (as far as Severian knows and can guess), and as far as Apheta's words and Malrubius's, that the hierogrammates were NOT created by Urthlings.

As I mentioned earlier, the most "poetic" interpretation, and still plausible, is that Earthlings created the hierogrammates, meaning Earthlings were the Hierarchs. The reason this is appealing is because it suggests that with the bringing of the New Sun, Severian may have imploded and rebirthed Briah all together, and in Apheta's own words... "It may be that from your ancient Urth, reborn, that the Hieros will come. I believe it to be so."

If the anvil the hierogrammates strike against is the need for life, then we sense a dependency on Severian and his actions, with relation to the hierogrammates and their future perhaps.

Furthermore, can we guess whether Severian fathers a child with Apheta? And if so, back to poetry, might that child be the creature the hierogrammates were aiming to procreate all along? Which means, is Severian the ideal Urthling sent to Yesod to bring the New Sun, and give the hierogrammates new life in a very literal sense?

2010-08-13 James Wynn replying to David Stockhoff #

Argues that Severian is the Christ figure of Briah (n) and that there was no historical Jesus, so parallels to Catholic ritual arise from the parallel worlds of There Are Doors. Explains this by comparing the worlds to strings on a guitar tuned to one note, so that each universe stays in sync with its neighbours through approximation, not by any shared Jesus.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · theology-and-religion · theories: Severian is the Christ of Briah (n); There was no Jesus of Nazareth; Parallel Briahs stay in sync by approximation

As I have said before, I think it is a mistake to analyze these stories to see how their settings (as opposed to their themes) conform to systematic theology. However, I'll play along.

I presume that Severian is the Christ of Briah (n). There was no Jesus of Nazareth. If one asks, well how could there be all these parallels...some having to do with the RCC...if there was no Jesus? Wolfe addressed that question in There Are Doors.

In that novel, the parallel worlds*** remain sync with each other by virtue of being in approximation to each other.

(***The parallel worlds, from Green's perspective, might be inscrutable from a cycle of expanding and collapsing universe iterations since such iterations are not divided by Time--because Time also collapses along with each universe iteration)

The parallel worlds are like strings on a guitar that are strung to the same note...strike one and those next to it vibrate along with it. Therefore in Lara's world, grooms wear black at their weddings because men die after having sex. In Green's world (ours), grooms wear black too. It's just a formality for us, but we actually do it here only because they do it there. The parallel worlds have the same languages and very similar customs differing only in minor and peculiar ways (such as no ice trays in Lara's world).

Presumably, the Briahs (n -/+) remain in sync because they are compelled to by their approximation to each other. So there will be similarities between Briah (n -/+) that make no internal sense. There might well have been a Catholic Church with nearly the same rituals (and an American moon landing as well) even though there was no Jesus to influence the next 2000 years of history.

proposes · theology-and-religion · allegory-and-typology · theories: Every universe has an equally good Jesus; The Conciliator is no Jesus, a red herring; Some universes' original church disappears from sight

I'll just repeat what I said earlier: I think there's a Jesus in any universe, and I think it's as good a Jesus no matter where. The Jesuses will differ in their accidentals because the universes are different, but there will be good enough Jesuses for all. And each Jesus said something equivalent to 'Thou art Peter, and upon this rock I will build by church, and the gates of hell shall not prevail against it'.
