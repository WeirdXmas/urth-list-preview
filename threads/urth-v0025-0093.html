Sev's grandpa

Urth list · 1 messages · 1999-04-14 to 1999-04-14

1999urthThe Book of the New SunRoy C. Lackey

Themes: Hidden identities and parentage, Time loops and causality

1999-04-14 Roy C. Lackey #

Supports Jonathan Laidlow's theory that Severian is his own grandfather, citing a passage on the raft where Pega sees Severian's resemblance to the portrait of the Autarch and Severian asks whose grandson he is. Adds that the family-resemblance idea was already set up when Severian recognized Odilo as the son of the Odilo he knew, then closes with a joking comparison to the Christian Incarnation.
supports · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Severian is his own grandfather

Another point in support of my theory about Sev being his own grandfather is found when he is on the "raft" with Odilo (son of Odilo), Pega, and Thais, after the flood. It dawns on Pega that Sev looks like the image she has seen in the House Absolute of the long-gone, badly-scarred Autarch Severian, who, she thinks, looks like a cutthroat. She thinks that Sev may be descended from him. Sev says, in part:

"But wouldn't the cutthroat have to be my grandfather? Severian the Great would be eighty or more if he were alive, surely. Whom should I ask about him, Pega? My mother or my father?"

An odd thing to say, no? Wolfe wouldn't hide a clue in plain sight, would he? <g> The idea of intergenerational, familial resemblance had been prefigured in the chapter when Sev recognized Odilo as being the son of the Odilo he had known.

Well, I don't want to start a religious debate, but the most recent Christian holiday is related. <g> Jesus, Who is Lord, Son of God, engendered by the Third Member of the Trinity, Which is One.

Roy
