Themes: Christ figures and messiahs

2011-05-26 John Barach #

Argues that at the end of "Bloodsport" Valorius becomes a king rather than a queen-taker, as a chess move would suggest, citing his transformation at the opened tomb, his father's rest, the passing of a mantle, and his link to the sun that enables him to kill the moon-queen Lurn. Responds to James's chess-capture reading and proposes that the sun-moon contest points to checkers rather than chess.
disputes · allegory-and-typology · close-textual-reading · theories: Valorius becomes a king in checkers, not chess

Thanks for the response, James.

I understand that a knight can take a queen in chess. But I'm not persuaded that that's what happens at the end of "Bloodsport."

First, Valorius undergoes a transformation at the end, marked by the opening tomb, the presence of his father, now at rest, and the passing on of a mantle.

Second, Valorius is linked up with the sun at the end, and it is by the sun that he is enabled to kill the moon-queen, Lurn. This is associated with their previous conversation about sun and moon combatting. Sun and moon correspond to man and woman or, more specifically, to king and queen. So it seems to me that Valorius is now a king, which could happen only in checkers (though, I'd add, he's also clearly under Our Lord the Sun, which is an appropriate view for a Christian king).

And then there is a common expression "X was playing chess when Y was playing checkers" (or vice versa): _http://tinyurl.com/444c5pw_ (http://tinyurl.com/444c5pw) .

John

2011-05-27 James Wynn replying to JBarach #

Argues that Valorius, in the Bloodsport story, is portrayed as a Christian knight-figure rather than a king, citing his self-description of serving as a sword and healer. Responds to JBarach's claim that Valorius becomes a king by linking sun and moon to king and queen, and identifies his fasting and protecting travelers with the Knights Hospitaller and Templars.
disputes · allegory-and-typology · theology-and-religion · theories: Valorius is a Christian figure, not a king

Yeah, but the following is not the description of a king:

"Should our folk require a sword, I am the sword that springs to their hands. Asked to heal, I cure their sick -- when I can": He isn't Christ, but he is a Christian figure. If they bring food, I eat it. If they do not, I fast or find my own".

That's the Knights Templar (The Hospitalers), who protected pilgrims (lost travelers).
