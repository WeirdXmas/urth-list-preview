The Invisible Library

Urth list · 9 messages · 2001-06-11 to 2001-06-14

2001urthThe Book of the New SunMatthew MalthouseWilliam H. AnsleyAdam StephanidesRoy C. LackeyMichael Andre-Driussi
The thread: The thread began as Adam Stephanides proposed an online index of imaginary books cited in Wolfe's works, and the discussion turned to whether invented titles in Peace and other Wolfe texts count as real books within the fictional world. Roy C. Lackey catalogued the Amanda Ros bibliography in Peace and suggested Louis A. Gold's Book of the Names of the Dead might echo Lovecraft's Necronomicon, while Michael Andre-Driussi argued Amanda Ros was a real eccentric Irish author and only The Lusty Lawyer is a likely forgery. William Ansley asked whether The Book of Gold appears as an actual title or is a general figure, and Stephanides answered that Master Ultan's remark in The Shadow of the Torturer could go either way. The thread ended unresolved, with Stephanides conceding the question was open.
  • Adam Stephanides: proposes an index of fictional books in Wolfe and argues invented titles like Wonders of Urth count as books in the Wolfe-world
  • Michael Andre-Driussi: argues Amanda Ros was a real author and only The Lusty Lawyer is a forgery in Peace
  • Roy C. Lackey: catalogues the Amanda Ros bibliography and suggests Book of the Names of the Dead echoes Lovecraft's Necronomicon
  • William Ansley: questions whether The Book of Gold is an actual title or only a general figure for a formative favourite book

Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Stories within stories, Names and their meanings

2001-06-11 Adam Stephanides #

Proposes compiling a list of fictional books appearing in Wolfe's works for an online index of imaginary books, naming The Book of Gold, The Lusty Lawyer by Amanda Ros from Peace, and the contents of Bibliomen. Notes that the Invisible Library website has no Wolfe entry and offers to act as clearinghouse for contributions.
proposes

There is a website called The Invisible Library --
http://www.invisiblelibrary.com -- which lists books which don't actually exist, but whose titles appear in other books. It lists the author and title of the real books they appear in, as well as the "author" and "title" of the imaginary book. It's a fascinating site, but there is not one entry for Gene Wolfe.

Obviously, this can't go on. There's _The Book of Gold_, _The Lusty Lawyer_ by Amanda Ros from _Peace_ (and wasn't there another book by her as well?) and everything in _Bibliomen_ (my copy of which is not accessible at the moment, unfortunately); and there must be others. Should we make it a group project? I'm willing to act as clearinghouse, if people don't want to clutter up the list.

--Adam

2001-06-13 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Corrects Roy's suggestion that the Amanda Ros titles in Peace are invented, reporting that Amanda Ros was a real eccentric Irish author with books like Irene Iddesleigh and a biography, O Rare Amanda. Supports the point by citing available listings for her books and noting that only Lusty Lawyer is the likely forgery in Peace.
informs · source-and-allusion

Ach, Roy--he got you!

There really =is= an Amanda (McKitterick) Ros, eccentric Irish author (take note, PEACE ethnographers!), and she really did write IRENE IDDESLEIGH (Bookfinder.com lists a few available today, ranging from $8.50 to $20) and HELEN HUDDLESON (ranging from $75 to $106).

And O RARE AMANDA really is a biography of her, written by Jack Loudan!

Since LUSTY LAWYER by Amanda Ros is the forgery (?) in PEACE, this is the one for the invisible library: the others I assume are all real titles.

2001-06-14 Adam Stephanides #

Notes that Master Ultan in The Shadow of the Torturer speaks of The Book of Gold as the book that draws a child to become a librarian, and suggests it may be a general term rather than a specific title. Responds to William Ansley's question by pointing to the chapter VI passage and admitting the argument could go either way.
answers · close-textual-reading · theories: The Book of Gold is a generic term, not a specific book

In SHADOW OF THE TORTURER (chap. VI), Master Ultan speaks of _The Book of Gold_ as the book which, when a child discovers it, leads to his becoming a librarian. It may be, though, that this in fact doesn't refer to a specific book, though Ultan doesn't say so explicitly. I could make an argument both ways.

--Adam
