2000-04-17 Dan Parmenter #

Argues that Palaemon was the boy Malrubius mentioned and an early conciliator candidate, who faced similar trials but chose loyalty to his guild. Answers Dan Parmenter's question by citing textual parallels of Severian walking in Palaemon's steps, then asks where the image of the bare-chested masked torturer historically comes from.
answers · allegory-and-typology · source-and-allusion · theories: Palaemon was an early candidate for the conciliator; The first Severian was closer but not quite right

From: "Christopher Culver" <[email]>

Welcome back!

I've always assumed so. There seems to be good evidence from the text (sorry, no citations) that Severian walked in Palaemon's steps in more ways than one. I've often wondered if Palaemon was an early candidate for the concilliator: the hieros had figured out by a certain point that a torturer was needed. Palaemon went through many of the appropriate experiences, but in the end, loyalty to his guild won out. The "first" Severian (the one who lies in Severian's tomb) was even closer, but still not quite right.

Another question: what is the historical basis (if any) for our conception of how a torturer looks? In other words, from whence comes the image of the bare-chested masked torturer?

Lex Shellac
