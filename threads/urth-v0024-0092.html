The thread: Readers compiled southern constellation references from The Book of the New Sun and debated whether Urth's sky places the story in the far future. Mitchell A. Bailey argued the surviving constellations show the setting is near rather than millions of years ahead, while Matthew Malthouse countered that geological evidence such as Saltus mining, the cliff and the mountains requires vast ages to have passed. Peter Westlake questioned how the familiar sky and fauna square with deep time, suggesting repetition in a cycle of the universe, and asked for a chapter reference on Vodalus and the reversed magnetic field. The thread ends without resolution, with the near-future and deep-time readings still standing side by side.
  • Mitchell A. Bailey: surviving constellations show Severian's sky is near-future, not millions of years ahead
  • Matthew Malthouse: geological evidence such as mining, cliffs and mountains requires that geological ages have passed
  • Peter Westlake: familiar constellations and fauna may be repetitions in a different cycle of the universe
  • m.driussi: simply posted the constellation reference list without argument

Themes: The dying sun and the cosmos, Deep time and ruins

1999-03-31 Matthew Malthouse #

Questions how the 'sooner rather than later' reading of Urth's sky can be squared with geological evidence in The Book of the New Sun, citing Saltus mining, the cliff Severian descends to Cas's cottage, and sharp peaks rising after older carved mountains. Replies to Mitchell A. Bailey's argument that surviving constellations place the story in the near future, arguing that geological ages must have passed.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Sev's time lies in a far future of geological ages

I've seen this discussed more than once but remain unhappy with the "sooner rather than later" conclusion.

How can this be reconciled with:
1: The mining industry of Saltus
2: The cliff face Sev decends to Cas' cottage
3: The 'sharp' peaks of mountains thrust up after those older ones carved inbto the likenesses of Autarchs?

I can't avoid the conclusion that geological ages have passed since our epoch. Yet other thing otherwise (which is presumably the function of others. :))

Matthew

1999-03-31 Peter Westlake #

Asks whether the Urth books' constellations, fauna and geography, which resemble our own, can be squared with the 'sooner rather than later' view of geological time, and suggests the similarities are repetitions in a different cycle of the universe. Also asks for a chapter reference for a passage where Vodalus says the Earth's magnetic field has flipped, to support a theory about Gyoll's location.
questions · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: Similarities between epochs are repetitions in a different cycle of the universe; Earth's magnetic field flipped according to Vodalus; Gyoll lies in the north-east visible from Typhon's eye

..

I've wondered about this too. The problem is that the constellations, fauna, and (arguably) the geography are quite like those of our time. The people even drink matte still. The only answer I can think of, which is that this is a different cycle of the universe to our own and that the similarities are only repetitions and not identities, so that they don't have to occur at the same time or even at all, is hardly satisfying.

On the subject of geography: I've seen references to a passage where Vodalus says that the Earth (or its magnetic field) has flipped over, but I can't find it anywhere. Does anyone have a chapter reference? It would help bolster my theory about where Gyoll is, how events in the north-east are visible from Typhon's right eye, and why Severian keeps getting lost all the time.

SBear.
