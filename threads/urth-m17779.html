Urth is Green Redux

The thread: Lee Berman argued that Urth may be physically the same planet as Green, with Lune's disappearance or growth as the key clue, and that Lune is a natural moon rather than a domed one. Jeff Wilson disputed the idea that Wolfe can do whatever he wants with his worlds, arguing that Urth and Lune are not invented from scratch and so constrain him, citing Wolfe's own handling of times and tides. Berman answered that domes and greenhouses are post-hoc fabrications and that BotNS shows no sign of civilization on Lune. The thread ends with the exchange unresolved, with Wilson's constraint argument left unanswered.

2011-01-11 Lee Berman #

Argues that Urth may be physically the same planet as Green, and that the puzzle of Lune's disappearance or growth is the key to that hypothesis. Responds to Marc Aramini's claim that Wolfe's connections are not coincidence, and asks about the captain in The River Battle and whether the Osterlings carry List-allusions.
proposes · puzzle-solving · myth-and-structure · theories: Urth is physically the same planet as Green; Lune grew into Blue or was replaced by a twin planet

Don't worry, you don't need to sell me on this idea. I think there are neglible coincidences in Wolfe's work. The mystery to me is how a thoughtful reader could dismiss connection after connection as coincidence in a carefully constructed fiction.

Plus, I am open to the idea of a shared identify of planets. For me, nothing is more central to understanding the whole of BotNS than Eschatology and Genesis. One of the many principles we learn is that the Author would rather disguise his players to play the various roles rather than bring in new actors. If true for characters, why not planets?

In contrast to the elitist types who argue all popular SF is beneath Gene Wolfe, I find it quite in fitting with his style to make the ruins on Green to serve a Statue of Liberty/Planet of the Apes function. In my view, Gene Wolfe knows and borrows great stories from everywhere; his gift is in his unique vision, rewriting and disguising of them.

Thus I am willing to give the working hypothesis of Urth physically = Green a lot of consideration. It did seem like the best objection to this hypothesis was the question- Where is Lune? Assuming it is the same size as our Moon, it is a few sizes too small to hold an atmosphere and thus water and life. Did it grow in size to become Blue? Or did it disappear to be replaced by a twin planet? (Moon seems a few sizes too big to have become The Whorl, plus Lune is still orbiting in Severian's time).

It seems like this question would need to be answered (unless it has been already and I missed it?) This is part of the reason I asked about the capitalization in the Gene Wolfe note. "Urth is GREEN!" is slightly but significantly different than "URTH is Green!". As previously noted, I think it could relate to Lune.

The objections to the rules of astral regarding mental travel maps vs. physical travel maps are interesting. They could relate to metaphorical connections between the planets. But working with real physical sameness as a hypothesis I feel like the most important key is the explanation for Lune.

Just out of curiosity with regards to The River Battle, do you have some ideas on who "the captain" might represent? (and do the whole of the Osterlings have List-allusion or is it just the captain?)

2011-01-11 Jeff Wilson replying to Lee Berman #

Disputes Lee Berman's claim that Wolfe can do whatever he wants with his worlds, arguing that Urth and Lune are not invented from scratch and so constrain him. Points to Wolfe's own handling of the inconvenient details of times and tides, and his explanation of their absence, as evidence he respects those limits.
disputes · genre-and-craft

With his worlds, like Green and Blue and St Anne and St Croix and Yesod, absolutely. However, Urth and Lune are not his creations de novo and he is limited to what he can do with them and still entertain the reasonable person's willing suspension of disbelief. He acknowledges this by including the inconvenient details of times and tides and their absence, and explaining the latter.

2011-01-11 Lee Berman replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that Lune's forests and atmosphere imply a larger, natural Lune rather than a domed moon, so the Whorl-as-Lune theory weakens while Urth-as-Green and Lune-as-Blue gain probability. Answers Marc Aramini's question about whether the Lune of Severian's time is still the moon by dismissing domes and greenhouses as post-hoc fabrications and noting BotNS shows no sign of civilization there.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Whorl as Lune theory is less probable; Urth is Green and Lune is Blue; Lune was once a full-sized forested planet

Damn! I think this is an excellent point!

I think postulations of domes and greenhouses are just post-hoc argument fabrications against your theory Marc. In 30 years of picturing "the forests of Lune" I never once pictured a dome or greenhouse. The word "forest" implies natural growth and that's how I pictured it. I suspect the same is true of all others but we'll have to see what they claim.

I guess there could be a planetary forcefield to hold in atmosphere...but that would imply Lune has civiliation. And we get NO sense of that in BotNS IIRC. I get the sense that if anything, green Lune has gone wild, gone to seed, so to speak.

Arguments of "difficulty" regarding the increase in size of Lune also fall flat in my book. Great glass domes and lunar volume expansion are both equally easy to create in fiction, Wolfe can do what he wants with his worlds. Forest implies atmosphere and atmosphere implies bigness. Damn...maybe Lune has been planet sized all this time.

We still have the possibility that Urth is not in the same universe as Earth. Perhaps Urth's satellite was always bigger than our Moon, more like a twin planet. It could be a literary tip of the hat to 5HoC all along. (this implies the astronaut picture is not from Urth's history but, like certain stories, from Earth)

Well, that's how I see it. From my perspective, this argument of Lune's atmosphere and size makes "Whorl as Lune" become lower probability. But it makes "Urth= Green" and "Lune (full-sized and forested, previously flooded) = Blue" a greater possibility.
