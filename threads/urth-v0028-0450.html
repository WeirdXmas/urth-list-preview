1999-11-10 Charles Dye #

Argues that Severian changes over The Book of the New Sun, citing his judgments as Autarch in The Citadel of the Autarch that overturn Commonwealth sentences, against Daniel Fusch's claim that he fears judging. Also reads Foila's story as a statement about marriage and her independence, and offers a note on the mausoleum visits as possibly a different man.
disputes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Severian changes and accepts judgment as Autarch; Foila's tale sets terms for marriage

Ah, but Severian changes during the course of TBOTNS. One of his first official acts as Autarch, in Chapter XXXIV of Citadel, is judgment of the Guild's clients. In a few cases, he overturns the sentences imposed by the judges of the Commonwealth. If Severian ever had a horror of rendering judgments, I think he lost it somewhere along the way.

Mantis suggests:

Is the fight rigged? Foila's story obviously reflects her situation and tells us a great deal about her character. But I think it's noteworthy that the maid in Foila's tale does not "choose to keep herself for herself."

Shortly before Foila tells her story, the Pelerines' slave Winnoc informs us that "There's only three ways a man can be a slave.... Though for a woman it's different, what with marriage and the like." I don't think that Foila's story necessarily tells us *who* she intends to marry. Rather, she's letting her suitors (and us) know just *how* the marriage is going to work. The armiger's daughter is the brains of the partnership; she knows how to give orders by requesting a decision. Foila is a combat veteran, and she wants it understood that she is not going to be a chattel of her future husband -- whoever he turns out to be.

On the other hand, it's entirely possible that Foila has already decided who will win the contest, and intends to nudge Severian in the correct direction if she sees that he's about to get it wrong. One obvious way would be to clarify the word "best."

You could also view it as being literally accurate. The Severian who visits the tomb as Autarch is not the same man as the one who placed the coin under the stone.
