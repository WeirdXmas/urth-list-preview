PEACE: oh wabe!

Urth list · 4 messages · 2002-10-24 to 2002-10-30

2002urthDan'l Danehy-OakesWilliam H. AnsleyMichael Andre-DriussiJosh Geller
The thread: Readers debated how to pronounce 'wabe', the odd oath in Wolfe's Peace, and whether its sense ties to Carroll's Jabberwocky. Michael Andre-Driussi quoted the passage, asked about pronunciation, and linked the oath to the orange people's sundial worship as a mirror of the narrator's telephone-pole religion. Josh Geller argued for 'wob' using Carroll's own gloss from swab or soak. William Ansley reconstructed the passage from memory, offered WAYB, and admitted his earlier pronunciation had failed. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued for a single syllable rhyming with 'babe', citing the rhyme with 'outgrabe' and the poem's regular rhythm. The thread ends with the pronunciation question unsettled, with several competing readings and no consensus.
  • Michael Andre-Driussi: confirms the passage, links 'wabe' oath and sundial worship to telephone-pole religion, asks how to pronounce it
  • Josh Geller: 'wabe' should be 'wob', per Carroll's gloss from swab or soak
  • William Ansley: 'wabe' is an alien-breaking oath, pronounced WAYB, mirrors telephone-pole worship
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: single syllable rhyming with 'babe', supported by 'outgrabe' rhyme and meter

Themes: Names and their meanings, Myth and folklore sources, The dying sun and the cosmos

2002-10-24 Josh Geller #

Argues that the word 'wabe' from Carroll's Jabberwocky should be pronounced 'wob' rather than rhyming with 'baby' or 'babe', citing Carroll's own gloss. Supports this by quoting Carroll's definition (from swab or soak, the side of a hill soaked by rain) and linking a online Jabberwocky glossary; the post replies to Michael Andre-Driussi's pronunciation question in a Peace thread.
informs · source-and-allusion

According to Humpty-Dumpty.

But according to Carroll's original definition:

Wabe (derived from the verb to swab or soak). The side of a hill (from its being soaked by the rain).

So it looks like it is pronounced 'wob'. See
http://www.math.luc.edu/~vande/jabglossary.html.

.

2002-10-29 William H. Ansley #

Argues that the word 'wabe' in Peace is an oath that breaks the familiar with alien strangeness, and that the orange people's sundial worship mirrors our telephone-pole worship. Reconstructs the passage from memory, pronounces 'wabe' as WAYB, and admits his earlier pronunciation failed, while agreeing with an earlier post about a film.
supports · close-textual-reading

It should be. _Peace_ is still my favorite of Wolfe's books and I have read it many times.

Interesting. I'm going to quote the whole passage.

[T]he orange people with their weird religion demanding the worship of sundials (as our own seems to others that of telephone poles), so that in friendly locker-room conversations, when we have at last and at the threat of certain legal pressures admitted them in Pinelawn and are discussing the round now past, they out with strange oaths. What is a wabe?

I have always thought Wolfe meant that to others (although just who these others might be, I'm sure I don't know) we seem to worship telephone poles because we have them all over the place, so they must be some kind of totem object.

<Foghorn Leghorn>
Totem! Poles! Totem poles! I made a joke, son.
</Foghorn Leghorn>

"WAYB" is, of course, the canonical pronunciation. Considering that I made a special effort to phrase my explanation above in such a way as to prevent anyone reading it from arriving at "WAY-bee," the depth of my failure is all the more humiliating. <g>

I agree whole-heartedly with mantis here. This film is not playing in a lot of theaters, but it is well worth going out of your way to see it.

2002-10-30 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that the word "wabe" in Peace should be pronounced as a single syllable rhyming with "babe," not "WAY-bee." Supports this with two grounds: its rhyme with "outgrabe" in Jabberwocky and the poem's otherwise rigidly regular rhythm, which a two-syllable "wabe" would break.
supports · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion

Two points in support of the estimable Ansley's prescribed prounciation:

a) "Wabe" needs to rhyme with "outgrabe," whose origin (if it can be called that - past tense of "to outgribe") does not in any way suggest it should be pronounced "out-GRAY-bee."

b) Rhythmically, "Jabberwocky" is structured:
baDA baDA baDA baDA
baDA baDA baDA baDA
baDA baDA baDA baDA
baDA baDA baDA
The rhythm is _extremely_ regular. Ignoring "wabe," the only possible exception occurs the final line of stanza 2, "the frumious Bandersnatch;" however, as the adjective can be pronounced "FROOM-yuss," there is no real irregularity. For "wabe" to be pronounced bisyllabically, then, and especially with an unstressed second syllable, would create a lone
irregularity in a pome whose rhythm is otherwise extremely, almost obsessively, regular.

--The Pedantic Blattid
