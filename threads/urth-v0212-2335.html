Michael Andre-Driussi objected that concealing the credential ambushed a casual poster, explaining that the figures looked Newtonian and that he meant to showcase time dilation in The Book of the Long Sun (333 Whorl years equaling 1000 Urth years). Friedman then apologized for the hurt, denied predatory intent, asked whether Andre-Driussi was assuming a Newtonian viewpoint, and promised not to reuse his numbers. The thread ends on a conciliatory note with the disagreement about tone unresolved.

Jerry Friedman apologizes for hiding his physics PhD and proposes the terms "Urth time" and "Whorl time" for measuring elapsed time in the books, tying this to the relativity calculations discussed earlier. He reports using rough approximations of the thread's numbers in his Conceptual Physics class and plugs The Book of the Long Sun, while jokingly disclaiming the title of professor.
proposes

I apologize. When I thought about it, I thought that my reasons for not mentioning my Ph. D. were better than my reasons for revealing it, so I probably should have stayed "in the closet".

That works, but I still like my suggestion of "Urth time" and "Whorl time". To me, that's precisely what you're talking about--how long it's been according to timekeeping on Urth and on the _Whorl_.

By the way, we covered S. R. in my Conceptual Physics class today, and I used rough approximations to your numbers as an example, with a plug for TBotLS. Thanks!

2002-12-04 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Disputes Professor Jerry's claim that he should have stayed closeted about his physics PhD, arguing that concealing the credential ambushed a casual poster. Explains that the Whorl-time and Urth-time figures seemed Newtonian, so the reply aimed to showcase time dilation in the Long Sun books and the numbers of 333 Whorl years equaling 1000 Urth years.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic

I strongly disagree: not mentioning it upfront was what made it a back-alley ambush of a housewife by an academician. In the animal kingdom this is predatory camouflage.

I think it is clear that I was addressing a person who seemed to be exhibiting Newtonian thought. Without the "Ph.D. in Physics" context, the tagging of "Urth time" and "Whorl time" looked non-Einsteinian, and rather Newtonian: the poster's unwritten, perhaps even unexamined assumption (like blattid's non-Einsteinian initial and secondary estimates of the distance between Urth and Breen systems) might well be something as simple and clean as "Whorl years are 3 Urth years long," since after all, this will establish how 333 years can equal 1000 years. The point of my response was to gesture at the wonder of time dilation as an Einsteinian reality (the counterintuitive gulf between the universes of Newton and Einstein) and to state the fact that I did not coin the terms.

You are not welcome. I will strive to avoid similar alleys and people in the future. And you should do your own calculations to avoid the taint of pseudoscience and other academician nasties.

=mantis=

2002-12-04 Jerry Friedman #

Apologizes to Michael Andre-Driussi for the hurt caused by an earlier unannounced physics-based reply, denying any predatory intent and asking whether his objection stems from assuming a Newtonian viewpoint. Offers to explain the 'Urth time' and 'Whorl time' terms and promises not to use his numbers again.
chats

Clearly you're more upset about this than I gathered from your previous post. I'm sorry. I had no intention of hurting anyone, certainly not you, someone whose posts I'm often grateful for, and I feel bad that that was the result of what I did.

However, I'm not entirely clear on why you feel so strongly about this. Is it because if you'd known I'd studied physics, you wouldn't have assumed I was responding to you from a Newtonian point of view? I'd appreciate clarification if you're interested in providing it. In any case, from my point of view there was nothing predatory about it. I wasn't thinking, "Aha, I've trapped Mantis into making a statement that I can correct!"--or enjoying the "ambush" in any other way.

Finally, I don't know whether this is important to you, but I won't do this again in my other mailing-list and Usenet discussions.

I'm afraid I didn't even think, "What mistake does he believe I'm making?" I can see, though, that if you did want to use "Urth time" and "Whorl time" in that table, you might need to explain those terms so people wouldn't make that mistake.

Indeed, I've seen "subjective" and "objective time" before in sf, and if you were to tell me they've been used in popularizations as well, I wouldn't be surprised.

If that's how you want it, I won't use your numbers any more.

Jerry Friedman
