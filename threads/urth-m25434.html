First Polish edition of Peace

Urth list · 6 messages · 2014-01-18 to 2014-01-23

2014urthAntónio Pedro MarquesPiotr SzczęsnyMarc AraminiGerry Quinn
The thread: A list member offered a trade for the first Polish edition of Peace, which led to a discussion of the Polish title 'Pokój', meaning both peace and room. Marc Aramini argued the English title echoes the Gaelic 'people of peace' or 'people of the mound' and the sidhe, since narrator Weer is literally a person of the mound. António Pedro Marques asked whether 'pokój' means a division of a building or a comfortable roomy space, and Piotr Szczęsny explained it refers to comfortable rooms people spend time in. Gerry Quinn's jokes added little substance, and the thread ended without a real debate on the novel.
  • Marc Aramini: English title Peace evokes the Gaelic fairies as people of peace or mound, linked to the sidhe and to narrator Weer as a person of the mound
  • Piotr Szczęsny: Polish 'pokój' means peace and room, a pun Wolfe would like, and it is used only for comfortable rooms people spend time in
  • António Pedro Marques: asks whether 'pokój' means a building division or roomy comfortable space, guessing the latter but unsure
  • Gerry Quinn: jokes that no language could bear the confusion of 'pokój' meaning both peace and room

Themes: Names and their meanings, Myth and folklore sources, Books, libraries and writing
