Themes: Myth and folklore sources

1997-03-25 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Responds to Patrick's essay by linking the Nemi priest-king succession from the golden bough myth to Scylla's plans and Hackum's killing of Wee Flea in the Long Sun books. It also agrees with Patrick on how the gap between what lesser characters say and how they spin it shows their change, citing Incus and Remora.
supports · myth-and-structure · source-and-allusion · theories: Scylla's plans follow the Nemi priest succession

Reply: Item #7628847 from [email]@INET02#

Patrick,

There you go! See, it isn't so hard or time consuming--we'll get an essay out of you yet!

Good point, re: Scylla's savage plans. Invokes the whole "golden bough" thing--priest of grove at Nemi can only be replaced by the candidate who successfully gets a sacred bough (uh, avern plant?) and then kills the priest in hand-to-hand combat. Auk for Calde'! (Another reason for Hackum to push Wee Flea down a pit and to his death--like Den to Bobby Black in PEACE, perhaps?)

Good point, also, re: the relative ease of tracing the gap between utterance (text) and interpretation (self-serving "spin") in the lesser characters where reader is presented with both. I.e., since we don't have Silk's Enlightenment verbatim and in toto, we wonder about his "spin factor"; it is a delight and a relief to see the clownish Incus bloom into the would-be tyrant, just as the Richelieu-like Remora descends into a sympathetic character.

=mantis=

P.S. And thanks for the plug!
