2000-07-25 Nigel Price #

Recommends a radio reading of Nathaniel Philbrick's In the Heart of the Sea about the Essex survivors' cannibalism, then turns to Severian's passage through the gate of Nessus. Notes that details about it emerge gradually across The Claw of the Conciliator, and says the poster is rereading the book for a symposium paper.
chats

Hi Corncrake!

Did you hear the reading of "In the Heart of the Sea" on Radio 4 recently? It was their "Book of the Week" about a month ago and was absolutely excellent - really gripping. (I have a long drive to work each day - from Minety, Wiltshire to Oxford - and get to listen to a lot of radio.) The account of the Essex survivors' cannibalism was horrendous and terribly moving. The captain had promised the cabin boy's mother that he would look after her son on the voyage, but he ended up shooting him and eating him. It's an extraordinary tale.

As to what happens to Severian at and immediately after his passage through the gate of Nessus, well, you do gradually get bits and pieces of the story, a detail here and there throughout the length of TCotC. I'm rereading the whole thing very slowly as part of the research for my paper for the symposium at the end of August. Will you be there?

Not the least extraordinary thing about TBotNS is that it really does get better every time you read. Wish I understood it all, though!

Nigel

Jokingly replies to Nigel Price's wish to understand The Book of the New Sun fully, saying that full understanding would be fatal. Responds directly to Price's quoted remark about the book improving on rereading, with no textual evidence offered.
