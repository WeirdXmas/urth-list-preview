Two Ships

Urth list · 7 messages · 2003-08-13 to 2003-08-15

2003urthThe Book of the New SunThe Book of the Long SunMatthew MalthouseLisa Schaffer-DoggettJames WynnChris PAndrew BollenJames Jordan
The thread: Readers debated whether the Whorl is the same ship as Severian's Ship of Tzadkiel, and whether the New Sun and Short Sun books fit together at all. James Jordan and Lisa Schaffer-Doggett argued the Whorl is a corrupted or imitation ship, with Typhon's will shaping it, while James Wynn and Andrew Bollen pressed timeline, technology, and cargo problems. Matthew Malthouse and Chris offered reconciling readings, one qualitative and one Platonic, that treat the ship as more than a physical freighter. The thread ended without consensus, with the two-ships theory remaining contested.
  • James Jordan: Whorl is a counterfeit of the One Ship, a false church-like imitation
  • Lisa Schaffer-Doggett: Whorl is a corrupted or deliberately shaped Typhon-made world, not a reflection of Urth
  • James Wynn: Two-ships theory fixes timeline but not cargo; weighs alternatives
  • matthew.malthouse: One ship is a qualitative distinction, a single Ship crossing Briah and Yesod
  • Chris: Tzadkiel's ship is an archetypal ideal visiting every era
  • Andrew Bollen: New Sun and Short Sun do not fit; Wolfe back-filled to link them

Themes: Stories within stories, The dying sun and the cosmos, Time loops and causality

2003-08-13 James Jordan #

Argues that the Whorl-equals-One-Ship hypothesis fails, but Wolfe may have loaded Whorl with thematic counterfeits of the One Ship as a Church symbol, like Typhon's false religions. Responds to Crush's objection, then suggests Typhon's mountain sleep before a Flood may echo fringe Christian claims that the Great Pyramid survived the Flood.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · source-and-allusion · theories: Whorl as counterfeit One Ship; Typhon intends circular journey back to Urth; Typhon sleeps on mountain awaiting Flood; Wolfe drew on Great Pyramid Flood myth

I don't think your Whorl = OneShip hypothesis can stand close inspection, as Crush has pointed out. Thematically, however, there may be something Wolfe intended. The One Ship is related symbolically to the Church: seven sided, existing throughout all of time, moving between the heavens and the Briah-world. The Church-as-Ship is just WAY too common historically for us not to consider it, and Wolfe certainly positions the One Ship in such a way as to point to Church-symbolism.
But that brings up Typhon's ship as a counterfeit, of course, housing false pastiche religions that he established as a way to control the people inside of it. Given that this is indeed the case, Wolfe may have loaded Whorl with "counterfeit equivalents" to the features of the One Ship, for thematic reasons. This might be worth exploring, building on the parallels you have mooted.
There has been speculation here to the effect that Typhon intended Whorl to make a circular journey and come back to Urth/Lune at a time after the Flood so he could take over the world again. (To wit: that Blue/Green is a later form of Urth/Lune, or vice versa.) If that theory ever is fully borne out, the notion of a circular journey would be another point of comparison.
You know, just for fun: Various fringe Christian sects have said that the Great Pyramid is a key to all of human history, and that it was built before the Flood and is the one human artifact to survive the Flood. Now, consider Typhon sleeping on the highest mountain on Urth, perhaps intending to awaken and take over after the coming Flood. Assuming for the nonce that this was Typhon's intention, perhaps Wolfe had this old Pyramid myth in mind....

Nutria

Reports that Don agrees with the Whorl-as-corrupted-ship idea, sees it as a likely scenario, and suggests the Whorl might be a lesser imitation of Tzadkiel's ship. Notes Silk left for the Whorl at the end of Return to the Whorl and speculates the ship may have gone to Yesod, linking this to Silk as a redeemed Typhon.
supports · close-textual-reading · theories: Whorl is a corruption of Tzadkiel's ship; Silk is Typhon redeemed; One Ship went to Yesod

Don agrees. He finds this a most likely scenario, the Whorl as a corruption of Tzadkiel's ship. Or perhaps a lesser imitation since he is suspicious of the "holiness" of the Hiero's.

Don doesn't think this is likely. However, Silk did leave for the Whorl at the end of RttW and it went on it's merry way to who knows where. Maybe it went to Yesod. If Silk is Typhon redeemed then this makes an interesting circle. Hmmm.

2003-08-14 Matthew Malthouse #

Proposes that the "one ship" in Wolfe's Book of the New Sun is a qualitative distinction: ordinary ships of men versus a single Ship of Tzadkiel that navigates time and crosses between Briah and Yesod. Responds to the Two Ships theory in the quoted post by James Wynn, offering a reconciling reading rather than a plain rejection.
proposes · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: There is one Ship of Tzadkiel distinct from ordinary ships of men; Ship of Tzadkiel crosses between Briah and Yesod and appears many

There's another way of looking at it. A qualitative difference between "ships" and "Ship".

The ships of men which are prosaic things, with which the empire of man (from which Typhon retreated) was made and possibly one type of which form the towers of the Citadel. The Ship of Tzadkiel, the ship that can navigate the rivers of time, that can cross between Briah and Yesod, that can appear to be many because it's voyages cross and recross it's own path in both space and time almost infinitely.

Matthew

2003-08-14 Chris P #

Proposes that Tzadkiel's ship in The Book of the New Sun is an archetypal or ideal ship, a metaphysical form rather than a physical freighter, visiting Urth in every technological era because all ships are the Ship. Builds on Crush's suggestion that Severian's ship was first the Whorl, offering a Platonic and angelic reading to resolve the technology and cargo problems.
proposes · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: Tzadkiel's Ship is the archetypal ship; All ships are the Ship; Tzadkiel operates in the symbolic angelic realm

Civet throws in:
Or, perhaps Tzadkiel's Ship is the archetypal ship (or the ideal form of "shipness" if you want to go Platonic). Tzadkiel himself seems to operate in the symbolic/angelic realm. It seems to make more sense than interpreting it in a strictly physical way (and the transition from Yesod to Briah is debatably a metaphysical one in the first place).

It also, now that I think about it, has a certain logic of its own. Tzadkiel's Ship seems to visit Urth during even the most primitive periods of technology or sophistication of the natives. Why? Well, strictly speaking of it as a freighter, there's no good reason I can think of. Instead, we can say that Tzadkiel's Ship visits all these eras because *it has to, by nature* - because all ships are the Ship.

2003-08-14 Lisa Schaffer-Doggett #

Argues that the inconsistencies between the New Sun and Short Sun books may be deliberate, suggesting Typhon intends the Whorl as a recreated Urth shaped by his will rather than a reflection of Urth's trends. Supports this by pointing to recreated French and Latin from library books, and to the cost of chem armies and absence of bioengineered animals.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: Typhon intends the Whorl as a renewed Urth; The Whorl reflects Typhon's will rather than Urth

Don replies:

I think that these "inconsistencies" that you bring up may very well point to another motive for the existence of the Whorl. In some perverse way, I think Typhon is seeking to create a renewed "Urth" (meaning whatever planet he chooses to colonize. It will be Urth like, be full of Urthlings, Urth technology and Urth animals). French and Latin could easily be recreated from the books in the Library (or whatever stands in for the Library in Typhon's time). Remember, the Whorl is not Urth and doesn't have to reflect what the trends and patterns are there. It is only a reflection of Typhon's will. As far as chem armies, they'd be much more costly and alienating than human ones. The bio engineered animals aren't there because Typhon doesn't want them. Just some thoughts.

2003-08-15 Andrew Bollen #

Argues that The Book of the New Sun and the Short Sun books do not fit together, since taken alone one reads as near future and the other as deep future, and that Wolfe back-filled to link them. Lists concrete inconsistencies: Typhon's interstellar ambitions without faster-than-light travel, established human colonies, the Chras Writings' survivals of our age, French and Latin surviving, missing bioengineered animals, and the absence of chems on Urth.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: New Sun and Short Sun are inconsistent timelines; Wolfe back-filled to connect the series

The universes of New Sun and Short Sun really don't fit together very snugly. IMO, if you took them as standalones, you'd say that Short Sun is in the future, while New Sun is in the *deep* future. A few hundred years versus a geological age or two. I think that GW did a lot of back-filling to tie them together. (Not that I really mind - myths are generlly cobbled together from pieces which don't fit very well, in a logical sense.)

Obvious examples:

- Typhon in New Sun at least desires to rule an inter-stellar empire, and offers Sev the job of satrap of Earth. How does this make any sense without superluminal travel? If Typhon can travel faster than light, why build the Whorl? If he can't, is he really going to feel good about a clunky slower-than-light hollow asteroid crawling through space while the immense shp/s of the Hiero-whatsits leap/s over dimesnional boundaries? - The universe of New Sun has a bunch of human-settled planets in some kind of contact with each other, if only via port-calls by Tzadkiel's ship. Why would Typhon think it was a cool thing to send out a slow boat to found a crappy little colony when these old, established ones already exist, and he presumably intends to conquer them?
- The literary survivals from our age in the Chras Writings are *really* implausible for a deep future setting.
- At some point in Exodus Silk talks of French and Latin as extant languages (at least literary languages). Ditto.
- Why none of the bio-engineered animals from New Sun on the Whorl? No destriers, no smilodons ...
- Why no chems on Urth, apart from throwbacks like Jonah? Maybe there are some around at the time of Sev vs Typhon in UOTNS; we don't meet them, AFAIK. But surely Typhon's army would have been predominantly chems?
