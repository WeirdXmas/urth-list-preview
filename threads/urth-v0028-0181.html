1999-10-07 Michael Straight #

Questions whether details from the Long Sun books should be used to interpret the New Sun books, treating it as cheating, while expecting the Long and Short Sun series to form one story. Responds to Paul C Duggan's question about the Whorl religion parodying Typhon's state religion, and is prejudiced against rumors that Severian appears in the Short Sun books based on the differing feel of the series.
disputes · genre-and-craft · theories: Severian appears in the Short Sun books

Oooo! A tricky question. How much do details from the Long Sun "count" in interpreting the New Sun books? Somehow I feel that's cheating.

On the other hand, I don't feel that way at all about the Short Sun & Long Sun books, I guess because it feels like it's going to really be all one story.

As for rumors that Severian may be involved somehow in the Short Sun books, I am prejudiced against the idea. The Typhon bit works because it's such a minor connection. But the feel of these two series is so different, I have trouble imagining them being brought together without one seeming out of place. (I haven't read _Blue_ yet, so I can't say anything about the 'feel' of that book.)

-Rostrum

1999-10-08 Peter Westlake #

Argues that Severian's godlike magical powers by the end of The Urth of the New Sun make him hard to bring into the Short Sun books, since such heroes make everything too easy. Responds to Michael Straight's objection that the two series feel too different to join, extending his doubt with a craft-based reason.
disputes · genre-and-craft

...

There's also the problem that by the end of _Urth_ Severian has acquired god-like magical powers, and such heroes are famously difficult to deal with in fiction. Everything's too easy.

S.Bear
