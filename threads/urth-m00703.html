Themes: Slavery and service

2005-02-06 Jim Raylor #

Clarifies that he did not claim Wolfe favours slavery, arguing that Wolfe's handling of it reflects the historical Roman context. Cites the Severian dynasty's 212 AD grant of citizenship and the standing of Roman household slaves, and notes that Latro sees Io as a slave who chooses her destiny.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Wolfe's slavery reflects deeper Roman historical understanding; Io sees herself as a slave who chooses her destiny

Just to makr it clear, I never intended to suggest
Gene Wolfe was in favour of slavery, just that his
understanding of it in historical context was deeper
than the usual one-sided view.
Before the Severian dynasty gave citizenshop to all
freemen in the Empire (212 AD),a slave in a good Roman
household had more rights and social standing than
most free non-Romans.

Latro never saw Io as a slave, but that seems to be
how she sees herself, albeit a slave who actually
chooses her destiny.
