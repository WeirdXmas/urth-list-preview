AEG: Dr. Lao and Woldercan

Urth list · 5 messages · 2009-09-21 to 2009-09-22

2009urthDave TallmanRoy C. LackeyDon DoggettJames Wynn
The thread: Roy Lackey proposed that the doubles of Woldercan in the film Dr. Lao could explain Cassie's trip to Woldercan in An Evil Guest without time travel. Dave Tallman objected that a world supporting copies is implausible and that a double could not win Cassie's love, pointing instead to Tony Randall's seven roles as a hint that characters play multiple parts. James Wynn offered that a real copy would love Cassie as the original did, and that Chase tearing up Reis photographs might mean no Reis is authentic. Don Doggett argued the novel The Circus of Dr. Lao, not the film, is the likelier source, and that its townsfolk are not duplicated. The thread ended with Lackey conceding he had not read the book or seen the film in years and did not recall Woldercan, so the duplicates idea was dropped without resolution.

Themes: Doubles, twins and clones, Time loops and causality, Ghosts, dreams and visions

2009-09-21 Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that the 'doubles' of Woldercan mentioned in the film Dr. Lao, not the book, could explain Cassie's trip to Woldercan to find Reis in An Evil Guest, avoiding time travel. Grounds this on the poster's finding that the book lacks doubles and on Wolfe's use of the Oz film in Free Live Free, asking others to weigh in.
proposes · source-and-allusion · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Woldercan has doubles of townfolk from the film; Wolfe drew on the Dr. Lao movie in An Evil Guest

This was in the context of the spelling of Woldercan, so perhaps not enough attention was paid to it. A day or two later, I googled the quote and found out that the Wikipedia summary in question is of the movie, not the book. So I researched the book online for a while and concluded that the mentioned "doubles" were not in the book. I may be wrong about that; I just don't know.

Anyway, I was disappointed because, if there were some sort of analogs of people on Woldercan, that would be a simple solution to the problem James Wynn has raised about what Wolfe could have meant by his remark about Cassie going to Woldercan to find Reis. I doubted that Wolfe would have referenced the movie, rather than the book, upon which it is only loosely based. So I blew it off.

Then I was reminded that Wolfe used references from the Oz movie in FLF, so I may have been wrong to dismiss the possibility of him using the movie version of Dr. Lao as a source in AEG. If there are duplictes of humans on Woldercan, it eliminates the need for hypothetical time travel and other problematic solutions.

What say you?

-Roy

Points to the film Dr. Lao's seven parts, played by Tony Randall, as a hint that the main characters play multiple roles.

Roy C. Lackey wrote

I find that solution very unsatisfying. The movie does have a feature that might be a clue. It's called "The Seven Faces of Dr. Lao" because Tony Randall plays seven parts. This is probably another indication that the main characters play multiple roles.

2009-09-21 James Wynn replying to Dave Tallman #

Proposes that Woldercan's copying of people, if real, would produce a true copy who loves Cassie because the original did, and that Chase tearing up photographs of Reis may signal that no Reis Cassie meets is authentic. Grounds this in Dave Tallman's objection, St. Anne's native copying in The Fifth Head of Cerberus, and the Whorl chronicles of the Long and Short Sun.
proposes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion · theories: Woldercan copies are genuine replicas of originals; No Reis Cassie meets is real

If Wolfe intended this in his reference to the Woldercon of Dr. Lao, then I would presume that he intended it as a place like St. Anne, where being copied by native was a genuine possibility, maybe even a likely one, although probably not a foregone conclusion. If it were a *real* copy then it will love her because the real Reis did--if not immediately then when it learns that Reis loved her. This a lesson to be derived from The Fifth Head of Cerberus and the Whorl Chronicles (Long & Short Sun). If it is the *real* Reis, then the Cassie is relying on the quality of the copy.

Hmmm...in the first chapter of AEG, Chase makes copies of some photographs of Reis, tears up the originals. If this is intended as a metaphor then it does not bode well for the originals of any copies made on Woldercan. Perhaps *none* of the Reis's Cassie meets are real and the Reis she is going to find is not real either.

J.

2009-09-21 Don Doggett replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that Wolfe borrows from popular culture and that the novel The Circus of Dr. Lao, not its film version, is the likelier source for Dr. Lao in An Evil Guest. Responds to Roy Lackey's suggestion that duplicated townsfolk on Woldercan could explain the story without time travel, saying the townsfolk are not duplicated in the novel.
disputes · source-and-allusion · theories: Dr. Lao in An Evil Guest derives from Finney's novel rather than the film; Woldercan townsfolk are duplicated

I'd say that Wolfe tends to appropriate and redefine any bit of literature and pop culture that appeals to him, and that he is of just the right generation to have been a fan of the movie as well as the book. On the other hand, he does mention Charles Finney (the author of the The Circus of Dr. Lao) by name in AEG, so that tends to skew things towards the book. I own a few copies of it and no, the townsfolk are not duplicated in the novel. It's a wonderful book, Roy, if you haven't read it, and it has influenced many great writers, most notably Ray Bradbury. It definitely belongs in the canon of classic fantastic fiction.

Don
