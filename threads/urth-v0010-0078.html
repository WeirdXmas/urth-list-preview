"The First Severian"

Urth list · 1 messages · 1998-05-04 to 1998-05-04

1998urthThe Book of the New SunDan'l Danehy-Oakes

Themes: Christ figures and messiahs, Gods, angels and the Increate

1998-05-04 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Proposes that the narrator's 'first Severian' is a first draft of a character in a creator's world, with Severian-1 the unrevised version and Severian-2 the one shaped by divine intervention. Grounds the idea in Wolfe's Catholic view of eternity versus space-time and in the Jericho miracle analogy, linking the intervention to Tzadkiel and the White Fountain.
proposes · theology-and-religion · allegory-and-typology · theories: Severian-1 is the first draft of Severian; divine intervention reshapes Severian's history via Tzadkiel and the White Fountain

I was at first surprised at all the stuff about who "the first Severian" was. The Severian who writes tBotNS -- the one who tells us "I know that I am not the first Severian" -- has been killed and resurrected/reconstructed from a "ghost" (aqastor, whatchmajiggy) more than once, after all.

But that doesn't cover what he means; he talks about a Severian who has a parallel career but does not bring the New Sun.

So -- consider another possibility. Wolfe, as an intellectual Roman Catholic, is very aware of the distinction between the view of Eternity and that of Space/Time.

So consider (if you will) the world as the artwork of a Creator. He (and, from the RC PoV, there is really very little alternative to using the masculine singular pronoun) creates a world as a "first draft." It goes along according to its natural laws -- and the Creator is _not_ pleased with the way it comes out.

So He intervenes. From in spacetime, these look like suspensions of natural law -- we call them "miracles." (From outside spacetime, from the Eternal point of view, of course, they're perfectly natural.) But they change the flow of history. Before the Creator intervenes, for example, Joshua and his army get wiped out at Jericho; then the Creator intervenes and the walls fall.

And, from the spacetime point of view _it has always happened that way_.

Severian-1 is the Severian of the "first draft." (Actually, the _nth_ draft, since until certain events in dimmer history -- like Jericho -- have taken place, events will produce no Severian at all: but he is the first draft of Severian.) He does the things Severian-2 describes. Then the Creator -- or, perhaps, His servants like Tzadkiel -- intervene, probably from the moment of his conception (not immaculate, but special), linking his being with that of the White Fountain. That is the Severian-2 we know and, well, whatever. (He's not terribly lovable, is he...?)
