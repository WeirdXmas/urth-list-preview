Emigrating chems

Urth list · 4 messages · 2002-10-07 to 2002-10-08

2002urthThe Book of the Long SunJames WynnRoy C. Lackey
The thread: Participants debated whether chems emigrated from the Whorl because they belong in an artificial world, and whether Chenille in Exodus from the Long Sun is genetically engineered. James Wynn argued both that chems are artificial and that Chenille cannot be Tussah's natural daughter, since Tussah is an inhume, citing Tussah's makeup, Chenille's knife throw, and Remora's deductions. Roy C. Lackey disputed the engineering claim, pointing to the Exodus character list that calls Chenille Tussah's natural daughter. Wynn then questioned whether Wolfe or Horn and Nettle wrote that appended list, noting Wolfe had not previously used such lists. The thread ended without resolution, with the authorship of the character list left open.
  • James Wynn: chems belong in an artificial world and Chenille is genetically engineered, so she cannot be Tussah's natural daughter
  • Roy C. Lackey: disputes the engineering claim and treats the Exodus character list calling Chenille natural daughter as counter-evidence
  • James Wynn: the character list was likely written by Horn and Nettle, not Wolfe

Themes: Robots, androids and artificial life, Names and their meanings, Fathers, mothers and children

2002-10-07 James Wynn #

Argues that chems left the Whorl because they belong in an artificial world, with Marble leaving because she contains Betel and Rose, and that the Long Sun story reflects disdain for cloning and embryo engineering. Responds to Roy's claim that Marble's emigration was a fluke and that no other chem went to Blue or Green, citing Chenille's divinely-created humanity and a papal sermon against man making himself a product of technology.
proposes · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: Chems stayed because they belong in an artificial world; Marble left because she contains Betel and Rose; Long Sun underscores disdain for human cloning

Roy noted:
Marble ending up on Blue was a fluke; as Mucor's guardian she was just in the right place at the right time, and being the calde's friend didn't hurt. Cijfer was married to Wijzer, who was a bio, so I don't think she was a chem. Silkhorn traveled to several other cities and was even the ruler of one. There is no textual evidence to indicate that any chem other than Marble ever went to Blue or Green that I am aware of. Chems would have been impervious to inhumi attack and near invincible as a weapon against them, which is probably why Wolfe left them on the _Whorl_.

Crush interjects:
I was intrigued at the lack of interest any chems had in escaping the Whorl. I hypothesize that the reason is that as totally artificial people, they belong in a totally artificial world. Marble contained within her Betel and Rose, which is why she chose to leave the Whorl. By the same token, even though Chenille is also an "artificial" person (in the sense that she's been genetically engineered), she also contains divinely-created humanity.

The more I delve into this, the more I believe this story is underscored with a disdain for human cloning and the genetic engineering of embryos. On how Wolfe personally feels on these issues I am not qualified to speculate. But while I'm not Catholic, I do recall the Pope sermonizing against "making Man a product of his own technology."
-- Crush

2002-10-08 James Wynn #

Argues that Chenille in Exodus from the Long Sun is genetically engineered and cannot be Tussah's natural daughter, since Tussah is an inhume. Responds to Roy's question by arguing the character list was written by Horn and Nettle, and citing Tussah's makeup, Chenille's knife throw, and Remora's deductions as evidence.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Chenille is Tussah's biologically engineered heir; Tussah is an inhume; Remora is the Ophidian Python of Apollo

Roy asked:
I meant to say something about that parenthetical bit above the last time you brought it up. Where is the evidence that Chenille is "genetically engineered"? The list of characters in EXODUS says of her: "Tussah's natural daughter."

Crush responds:
Yes, so it does. Now the question is, who wrote the list of characters and places? Was it Wolfe? He's never added any such list to his books in the past. The answer is, "the list of characters and places was written by the author of the Book of the Long Sun": Horn and Nettle.

So the list of characters and places should not be viewed as decisive. What of textual evidence? I can't at this point name the page, but at one point Silk's remembers seeing a picture (or image?) of Calde Tussah and he's wearing makeup. We know men don't wear makeup in Viron because Quetzal had to justify his use of it due to a "skin condition". What reason would Tussah have for wearing makeup if he were not an inhume? If he's an inhume, then he can't have a natural human daughter.

Also, Chenille exhibits stunning visual acuity and manual dexterity when (while talking to Remora in LS2) she was able pull a knife from the wall, throw it from across the room and stick it exactly in the slot she from which she pulled it. I realize that she was possessed by Kypris at the time. But there is no reason to believe possession grants the possessee with any powers he would not have while unpossessed - any more than Mario Andretti could win the Indy 500 in an '85 Toyota Sentra. Anyway, proficiency with knives is a province of Kypris. That trick required physical powers normal humans could not perform after any amount of practice.

Remora himself knew this to be true. He told Gulo that she was the biologically engineered heir of Tussah ("son not my son" this is another hint of why Chenille's name is sexually ambivalent although I have not unraveled the puzzle yet). Remora was a "whorlly-wise" political operative; knowledgeable about, and usually involved with all occult (in the old sense) activity in Viron. His deductions should not be casually dispensed with.

Anyway, as a reader, I can step outside the narrative and realize that Remora (the eel, the underwater serpent) is thematically the underworld Ophidian Python of Apollo - the creature Apollo conquered to gain authority over prophecy. It is Remora who predicts to Silk in LS2 that the mantaeon *would* be saved - as I recall, that the sale "would not come to pass" or if it did, "the old structures would be replaced with new ones." Both these predictions were proven true, since Blood and Musk were killed and the people of the mantaeon were moved to Blue. Remora is also the one who declared to the Chapter that Silk was the new Calde.

To sum up: (1) Tussah is an inhume, (2) Chenille has physical abilities beyond those of a normal human, (3) Remora, after a SINGLE conversation with Chenille, was convinced that she was genetically engineered AND was the Tussah's heir.

-- Crush
