Deciphering the Divine

1999urthThe Book of the New SunNicholas Gevers

1999-04-30 Nicholas Gevers #

Argues that Wolfe's religious subject matter is conveyed through a vast game of subtle clues, so that the shape of God can be discerned in precise details. Disputes Jonathan Laidlow's view that the book should not be read literally, citing Robert Borski's literal detective work and debates over Jonas and Sidero as intended.
disputes · puzzle-solving · theology-and-religion · theories: Wolfe's God is hidden in textual details

Our discussion of the religious or non-religious nature of Wolfe's work has certainly become complexly and compulsively interesting. Noting Jonathan Laidlow's comments on TBOTNS as a text not to be taken too concretely or literally: I don't quite agree. To reiterate something I've said before: although Wolfe's subject matter is primarily religious and so symbolic or non-literal, he does play a vast, subtle game of clues. Wolfe's God is in the subtle, precise, insinuated details of his work; deciphering those details is crucial to a proper, complex understanding of that work. When Robert Borski engages in close, literal literary detective work, or when people debate the precise nature of Jonas' and Sidero's physical character, they are doing precisely what Wolfe intends: drawing out the myriad details out of which the shape of God can be discerned.
//--------------------------------------
Nicholas Gevers
