The thread: The thread began as an offer to make Wolfe-inspired music and tapes available to the list, then spread into logistics: whether attachments and images are allowed, whether the wiki can host music, and where to post links. Participants shared fan tracks and videos, including Turtle Island String Quartet's "Row, Brothers, Row" and a Book of the New Sun-themed Phaserprone tape, and Son of Witz proposed an album mapping the book's sections onto styles. Discussion mostly stayed social and ended in a late, tangential exchange about the crab canon as a musical form linked to Wolfean symbols, with no clear conclusion.
  • Ranjit Bhatnagar: announced a separate music thread and advised against attachments, favoring links and urth.net hosting
  • Son of Witz: proposed a Book of the New Sun album mapping sections to musical styles and pitched the Amphisbaena song idea
  • Tim Walters: offered to compile and host a collection and shared his own science-fiction-inspired pieces
  • Matthew Groves: explained the crab canon and linked it to the Ouroboros and Caduceus
  • Mo Holkar: said hosting music on the Wolfe wiki would depart from its textual commentary

Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Myth and folklore sources, Names and their meanings

2008-11-06 Ranjit Bhatnagar replying to Son of Witz #

Advises against image and mp3 attachments on the list, since they may not appear in digests or archives and burden slow connections, and recommends posting links, emailing him to host images on urth.net, or using the wiki. Responds to Son of Witz, who asked whether an illustration could be shared as an attachment.
informs

I would discourage image attachments on the urth list. They probably won't show up in the digest or in the archives, and might annoy people on slow connections or Blackberries. Best to post a link, or email me privately and I'll post your image on urth.net. The same goes for mp3s or other large files you'd like to share.

It might also be possible to host these things on the wolfe wiki, but I'll let the wiki's administrators comment on that!

- ranjit

2008-11-06 Son of Witz replying to Ranjit Bhatnagar #

Asks whether the poster should share links to unofficial MP3 transfers of the Book of the New Sun audiobooks, which he assembled into playlists for listening alongside his rereading. He explains he could not find legitimate purchases and worries about piracy and about the reader Roy Avers, noting he finds listening gives him fresh insights.
questions

speaking of hissy mp3s...
Online I found the hissy mp3s of the SUN audio books (someone transferred them from tapes) and I joined them up into M3U files that have the Audiobook 'Resume' function. I've thought about sending them back out to the internet, but I don't want to be accused of piracy. I looked all over for legitimate copies to buy, but only found resellers who had it on CD, so obviously the original parties wouldn't have gotten any proceeds anyway. So these are out of print, in an obsolete medium, and they sound pretty crappy. Nonetheless, I've enjoyed listening to them right behind my reading, so I'm getting a 3rd pass simultaneous to my second read. I find that since I know the scenes better, having just read them, I often get bigger insights when listening, because the mind is less actively engaged in the reading effort.

anyway, so what do you think? should I post em to rapidshare and share the links. I don't want Wolfe of Avers to be pissed at me.

Roy Avers' reading is pretty great, FWIW.

~witz

2008-11-07 Mo Holkar / UKG replying to Ranjit Bhatnagar #

Replies that hosting music or artwork on the Wolfe wiki would depart from its current textual commentary, though it has a sparse section linking to other-media Wolfe material elsewhere. Responds to Ranjit Bhatnagar's suggestion that Wiki administrators decide whether Wolfe-inspired music could be hosted there.
informs

Wiki policy is determined by the collective of its contributors, but hosting artwork or music would be a bit of a departure -- at the moment its content is just textual commentary. Although there is an area (currently rather sparse) for links to other-media Wolfe material held on other sites.

best wishes,

Mo

2008-11-07 Tim Walters replying to Greg Bates #

Offers to compile, master and host a collection of Wolfe-inspired music tracks, and points to the Turtle Island String Quartet's "Row, Brothers, Row" as the most prominent existing Wolfe-related piece. Says it captures the folky but exotic tone of an Urth work song, apart from a generic jazzy middle section.
proposes

I hereby volunteer to compile and master the tracks, and host them on my site if that's what we want to do.

Probably the highest-profile Wolfe-related piece of music already extant is "Row, Brothers, Row" by the Turtle Island String Quartet, from their album BY THE FIRESIDE (yes, a Christmas album; no, I don't know what it has to do with Christmas). Except for a short generic "jazzy" section in the middle it hits the folky-but-exotic tone of an Urth work song quite well.

2008-11-10 Matthew Groves replying to Son of Witz #

Explains what a crab canon is: a melody set in counterpoint with its own retrograde, so a tune plays backwards against itself, answering Son of Witz's joke that the idea sailed over his head. Suggests that a perpetual canon could illustrate the Ouroboros and a mirror canon the Caduceus, linking the musical forms to symbols in Wolfe's work.
informs

A crab canon is a palindromic contrapuntal composition in which a dux (leading voice) is placed against a comes (following voice) which is the retrograde of the dux. In other words, a melody is used in counterpoint with itself played backwards. You could similarly illustrate Ouroboros with a perpetual canon. And maybe Caduceus with a mirror canon.
