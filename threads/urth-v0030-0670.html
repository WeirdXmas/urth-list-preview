Argues that Peter F. Hamilton and Gene Wolfe aim at entirely different outcomes, so criticizing Hamilton for lacking seriousness or philosophic depth misreads him. Responds to a list thread that faults Hamilton by contrasting Hamilton's adventure fiction and Mindstar books with Wolfe's religious-philosophical concerns and subtle jokes.

I realise this group is composed of "Wolfe fans" and also "Wolfe professionals", (I count myself among the former, but not the latter) But why are you wasting your time slamming Hamilton for, I guess, lack of seriousness, lack of artistry, philosophic shallowness etc????

Anyone who has enjoyed both Hamilton and Wolfe can immediately see they are trying for two entirely different outcomes. Hamilton is an "adventure" writer. You can see that in his lively if somewhat silly "Mindstar" books. He is interested in exploring future tech, arranging a sweeping narrative, and resolving a somewhat interesting problem (the possession)

Wolfe, as we all know, is interested in religious-philosophical issues, and working out long, subtle jokes, that attract the interest of the professional unravelers such as yourself.

You seem to be blaming Hamilton for not being like Wolfe. Face it, there's only one Wolfe. I count myself among one of his greatest admirers.
