2007-05-18 Roy C. Lackey #

Proposes that CASTLEVIEW and TWK share many correspondences, and that CASTLEVIEW may have influenced the writing of TWK. Lists shared elements such as young Arthurian figures from modern America, Odin and Sleipnir, fairies, a talking cat, and a castle in the sky, and notes that the books sit on opposite sides of the boundary with Faerie.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Castleview influenced the writing of The Wizard Knight

My recent reading of CASTLEVIEW forced me to appreciate for the first time just how many points of correspondence there are between it and TWK. It's hard to imagine that the former didn't have some influence on the writing of the latter, and I wonder if one book can be used to inform the understanding of the other. Of course, I don't have the answer, but maybe it would help to point out some similarities.

In both books we have a young Arthurian figure whose name isn't revealed until the end and who hails from modern America. There is a certain disconnect from an older, absent brother. We have Odin and Sleipnir, the Wild Hunt and mention of the Green Man. We have fairies and a fairy world that can be blundered into, and a young, regal, immortal fairy woman of uncertain loyalties. We have a talking cat. A big dog. Blood sucking females. An ambulance on a highway. Ghosts. Giants. A castle in the sky. The mingling of Norse and Arthurian material.

The older book takes place mostly on the American side of the indistinct boundary with Faerie, the newer on the other side. For whatever that's all worth.

-Roy

2007-05-18 Craig Brewer replying to Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that Castleview and The Wizard Knight share a deeper connection than casual reuse of an older motif, especially in their castle descriptions and in how Wolfe handles Arthurian, faerie and Norse material. Responds to Roy C. Lackey's comparison of the two books by saying he has not settled how strong the link is, and asks whether anyone in Castleview is named Ormsby or Ben.
proposes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Castleview influenced The Wizard Knight

You beat me to my next post (which I was saving until
I finished _Wizard_ again).

I haven't yet decided how strong the connection
between the books might actually be. At the very
least, we're seeing something about how Wolfe deals
with Arthuriana/Faerie/Norse-ness in fundamental ways.
But if nothing else, the description of the castles is
more than similar and, I think, more than a casual
reworking of an older motif.

And, of course, I want to know if anyone in Castleview
was named Ormsby. Or Ben.

--Craig
