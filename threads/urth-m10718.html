Urth list · 14 messages · 2010-08-13 to 2010-08-15

2010urthThe Book of the New SunRyan DunnLee BermanAntónio Pedro MarquesDavid StockhoffJames WynnJeff WilsonDan'l Danehy-Oakes
The thread: Dan'l Danehy-Oakes proposed a two-dimensional cosmology placing Yesod above and Abbadon below successive Briahs, and argued that our universe is a later Briah where the Increate is born as the Theanthropos. Lee Berman pressed whether each higher universe has a different Christ figure, and Jeff Wilson argued the same Christ figure would recur in every Briah. Ryan Dunn supported the layered planes reading while adding divine years and human-made gods. The thread drifted into Catholic perspective, a heresy reassurance, and a song quote, and ended without resolution, with the cosmology left as an open puzzle.
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: Yesod sits above each Briah and Abbadon below in a two-dimensional schema; our universe is a later Briah where the Increate is born as the T
  • Ryan Dunn: Yesod and Abbadon form a heaven and hell of planes, each Briah cycling separately, with possible gods made by humans in an earlier Briah
  • Lee Berman: Asks whether the Christ figure differs in each higher Briah, and whether Jesus's perfection means one shared Christ
  • António Pedro Marques: Every Incarnation is equivalent across Briahs, varying only in the surrounding universe
  • Jeff Wilson: Every Briah, including those beyond n+19, carries the same Christ figure; suggests aleph notation and Surreal Numbers

Themes: The dying sun and the cosmos, Gods, angels and the Increate, Christ figures and messiahs

Proposes that Yesod need not be a predecessor of Briah; instead, Yesod exists both before and after each iteration of Briah, sitting above it while Abbaddon sits below, in a two-dimensional schema. Offers the diagram of time running left to right as the basis for fitting the Briahtic multiverse together.
proposes · myth-and-structure · theories: Yesod exists before and after each Briah iteration

Ummmm. I think that for Yesod to be "before" Briah doesn't necessarily mean that Yesod is a predecessor of Briah. Consider this diagram (time proceeds from left to right):

YYYYYYYYeeeeeeeessssssssoooooooodddddddd
...Briah(n-1) Briah(n) Briah(n+1) ....
AAAAbbbbbbbbaaaaddddddddoooonnnn

So that Yesod exists not only before but also after any given iteration of Briah, but "above" it while Abbaddon is "below." This two-dimensional schema allows for, I believe, everything we know about the Briahtic multiverse.

2010-08-13 Ryan Dunn replying to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Agrees that Yesod and Abbadon can be read as a heaven and hell of planes, with each Briah living, dying and being reborn on its own cycle without overlapping the next. Responds to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's two-dimensional diagram of Yesod and Abbadon around successive Briahs, accepting its structure while adding that Yesod may have its own divine years and that gods might be made by humans in an earlier Briah.
supports · theology-and-religion · myth-and-structure · theories: Yesod as heaven and Abbadon as hell; gods created by man in a previous Briah

As long as you agree that each universe lives dies and is reborn, then yes. At least, Briah does this, and when it does this, there is not overlap. Each Universe should have its own life cycle independent of the next. I think whether each Universe has the same rules is up for debate, in Wolfean cosmology at least, but I'd like to believe that Yesod has its own divine years, also.

I do like the idea of Yesod as a heaven, and Abbadon as a hell, in terms of planes. I also like the idea of the gods being created by man in a previous Briah.

2010-08-13 Jeff Wilson replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that every Briah universe, including those beyond n+19, would carry the same Christ figure, perhaps better received, and offers an aleph-based notation for the sequence of universes. Responds to Lee Berman's question about Briah(n+20) by endorsing Adam Thornton's suggestion of Surreal Numbers as a guide to infinite numbers and linking it to Zeno's paradox.
answers · theology-and-religion · myth-and-structure · theories: Christ figure is identical in every Briah universe

The same, but perhaps he is better received.

The mathematical term for this would be Briah(aleph). But there can still be Briah(aleph+1).

I must second Adam Thornton's recommendation of _Surreal Numbers_, it is the best "pilot hole" I've seen for drilling your way into the intricacies of infinite numbers, and covers things my previous readings and math teachers hadn't. This is not just an egghead math thing, it has real and direct applications to liberal arts, like solving Zeno's paradox.
