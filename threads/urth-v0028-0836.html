More 5HC Variant texts

Urth list · 3 messages · 2000-03-12 to 2000-03-16

2000urthDamien BroderickMichael Andre-Driussi
The thread: Participants compared the wording of a passage in The Fifth Head of Cerberus across editions, specifically the Old Wise One's explanation of the Shadow Children's singing, where the 1976 Ace paperback adds a fiery death clause missing from the Scribners hardcover. Michael Andre-Driussi reported the exact texts and offered to pass the question to a bibliographer. Damien Broderick argued the difference was a compositor's typesetting slip, a claim he grounded on the repeated 'worlds will' phrase falling about a line apart. He later conceded he had misread which citation was the earlier text, while maintaining the typo reasoning still holds. The thread ended without settling which edition came first.
  • Michael Andre-Driussi: reported exact passage wording from Ace paperback and Scribners hardcover and offered to ask a bibliographer
  • Damien Broderick: variant is a typesetting error corrected later, supported by repeated 'worlds will' phrasing
  • Talarican: recalled that the hardback added a line (mentioned, not argued in these posts)

Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Myth and folklore sources

Reports the exact wording of a passage from the Old Wise One's explanation of the Shadow Children's singing in 5HC, comparing the 1976 Ace paperback with the Scribners hardcover edition. Well remembered Talarican!

Here is the 1976 Ace version (p. 95 in both the $1.75 and $2.50 version <g>):

"That which you call nothing is what holds all things apart. When it is gone, all the worlds will be born. But now listen to me."

Here is the Scribners 1972(?) hardcover edition (p. 88):

"That which you call nothing is what holds all things apart. When it is gone, all the worlds will come together in a fiery death from which new worlds will be born. But now listen to me."

This is all I can do, since I don't have the ORBIT version. But clearly there is a difference between the Ace paperback and the Scribners hardcover!

Sounds like a case for Robert Borski!

2000-03-13 Damien Broderick #

Argues that the variant wording in The Fifth Head of Cerberus, where 'all the worlds will be born' appears in one edition and 'all the worlds will come together in a fiery death' in another, is a typesetting error corrected later rather than a deliberate change. Grounds this on the repeated 'worlds will' phrase falling about a line apart, which suggests the compositor slipped between instances.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Variant wording is a typesetting error

Looks to me like a classic typesetting error first time around, corrected later. Regard the break point: two instances of `worlds will', separated by about a line of text. No need to get cosmogonic abt it.

Damien Broderick
