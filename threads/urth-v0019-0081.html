Catherine the thief, adrift in time

Urth list · 2 messages · 1998-10-10 to 1998-10-13

1998urthThe Book of the New SunRobert BorskiMatthew Malthouse

Themes: Hidden identities and parentage, Time loops and causality

1998-10-10 Robert Borski #

Argues that Catherine, adrift in time, is a time-travel escape hidden in the Atrium of Time, and that the Claw cannot vanish but takes people as it took Severian. Proposes that Caesidius is Severian's maternal grandfather and that Catherine is his daughter, and asks raster and others who the woman arrested in the loggia of the House Absolute is.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Catherine escapes through the Atrium of Time; Caesidius is Severian's maternal grandfather; The Claw takes people rather than vanishing; The woman arrested in the loggia is the original Katharine

raster does not like the Corridors/Atrium of Time escape clause, but I'm sticking with it. Or to quote the LEXICON URTHUS entry for A of T: "A time traveling structure, like the Last House, located in the heart of the Citadel. Inhabited by at least Valeria and her old servant, it may also house Catherine." Like mother, like daughter, eh mantis (whose second larva has recently emerged: Congrats!)? Of course we could both be wrong.

raster also brings up the-claw-has-never-vanished argument. To my mind, if someone steals my wife's wedding ring and I knew who it is, the ring has not vanished. It's been stolen by a definite individual (as opposed to it simply dematerializing, or burning, since diamonds, the hardest substance known to man, are flammable). I also think the answer to the Hierodule's twice asked question (it's even repeated once in italics)--"Where did you get it?" (I.e., the Claw)--and which is later revealed to be a test that Severian has failed--is: "I didn't get the Claw, it got me." It gets Catherine in the same way, I'd argue.

Also in regards to Caesidius resembling Severian (as Eata notes), this is because he is Severian's maternal grandfather. And Catherine, his daughter, is easily adult-age when the deluge strikes. Severian's been gone several decades, after all.

Question for raster, then (or Kieran M or anyone else.): who's the woman arrested by the Praetorians in the loggia when Severian returns to the House Absolute (Urth, Ch. 41) since you seem to think my theory's full of balloon juice? I still say it's the *original* Katharine, she of canonical fame to the Torturer's guild. Who do you?

Also, I'd be very happy to entertain anyone's elses notions of who Sev's maternal grandparents might be. Of course, that would require original thinking, and since it's much easier and cheaper to shoot at other people's clay pigeons...

Robert Borski

1998-10-13 Matthew Malthouse #

Asks whether the thread's family-relationship claims about Caesidius, Catherine and Severian depend on the Lexicon Urthus, since the poster has not read it and cannot match the suggestions to the two Urth books. Responds to Robert Borski's post, which said Caesidius is Severian's maternal grandfather and Catherine his adult daughter.
questions · close-textual-reading · theories: Caesidius is Severian's maternal grandfather

Okay, rather than consign you all to the mad house mayby I should first ask how much of this discussion depends on information from the LEXICON URTHUS which I have not read?

I can see the lovely lines being drawn here for the various relationships of the characters, but I can't relate what's being suggested to TBotNS & TUotNS - what am I missing?

Matthew
