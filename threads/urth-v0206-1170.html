Themes: Humour in Wolfe

2002-06-06 E Dorian #

Praises Barry Hughart's Bridge of Birds for its wit and charm, saying the book stayed fun to read even during a village plague. Reports starting the novel and noting John C. Wright's The Golden Age as the next book in the queue, responding to a thread about Kai Lung and Wolfe's style.
chats

I started _Bridge of Birds_ last night, and I have to praise its wit and charm. Hughart did an excellent job of making the book fun to read. It probably sounds strange, but I had a smile on my face, even when a plague beset the village.

I have also checked out _The Golden Age_ by John C. Wright, which is next in my queue.

-Darryl/Dragonfly

2002-06-06 Michael Straight #

Argues that Hughart's Bridge of Birds sustains a light, often hilarious tone while depicting grim events, and treats its characters and their sufferings as real rather than as cartoons. Supports Darryl Swett's praise of the book's wit and charm, agreeing that the humour and the plague scenes sit together.
supports · genre-and-craft

Not at all strange. One of the chief virtues of BoB is Hughart's ability to maintain a light (and often hilarious) tone while telling about grim events, and yet still managing to treat the characters and their sufferings as real, not as just cartoons to be laughed at.

-Rostrum
