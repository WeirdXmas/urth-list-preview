Nettle's motive

Whorl list · 1 messages · 2001-04-11 to 2001-04-11

2001whorlMichael Straight

2001-04-11 Michael Straight #

Argues that Nettle follows Silk back to the Whorl out of a wish to be with him as a disciple, since Horn is dead by the end of Return to the Whorl and her husband is gone. Grounds this in a quoted line from On Blue's Waters where Horn says Nettle feels the same about wanting to see Silk, and offers a speculative reason for Seawrack's return tied to the undines.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Nettle follows Silk as a disciple after Horn dies; Seawrack returns because the undines want her elsewhere

Don't have the passage in front of me, but in OBW, Horn tells Wizer that he wants to see Silk again and talk with him more than anything else in the whorl, and "Nettle feels the same."

I think Horn is pretty much gone at the end of RTTW, and that Nettle knows he's dead. So with her husband dead and (as you note) her children grown, she follows her desire to be with Silk, not as a wife, but as a disciple.

Seawrack, I don't understand, but I think that Scylla knowing how to summon her is a clue. I'm guessing her leaving has more to do with the designs of the undines, the Mother wanting to send her to other whorls, than any attachment she has to Silk. Why Silk would summon her, I don't know.

-Rostrum
