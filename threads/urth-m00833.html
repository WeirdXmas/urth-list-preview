2005-01-03 David DiGiacomo #

Argues that Sir Garvaon, not Idnn, killed Gilling out of jealousy because he loved Idnn and was a better suitor than Gilling or Svon. Grounds this in Able's remark that the killer cannot be punished, which implies the killer is already dead, while Idnn still lives, and notes the puzzle is not hidden for long.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Garvaon killed Gilling out of jealousy

Jealousy. Garvaon loved Idnn, and was obviously a more worthy suitor than either the monstrous Gilling or the cowardly Svon.

It wasn't concealed for very long, as Wolfe's puzzles go.

When Able says that the killer cannot be punished, Garvaon is dead, but Idnn is still alive.

I wondered about that, since I don't remember another mention of the mothers of the Mice having difficult births.

Of course, I don't understand why the human women wouldn't take simple precautions to avoid pregnancy. Based on Wolfe's peculiarly graphic descriptions, the giants wouldn't notice or care.
