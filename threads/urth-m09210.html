Argues that Wolfe's treatment of time is a straight clue, citing the fluid time of the New Sun and myth and a story that converts slow time in Elfland to space-time travel. Responds to John Watkins, who, reading a passage about a flower leaping into being, now finds Dorcas's view that the Claw reverses time more plausible than divine power.

Dorcas thinks that the Claw works by reversing time. I've always thought that that was wrong-headed--that it just works because it's divine power--magic.

Now that I read this passage, however, I'm starting to come around to Dorcas's view. My knowledge of physics is pretty minimal, but I have this vague idea that time slows down as one speeds up and would actually go backwards if one could exceed the speed of light, which of course is impossible. But if it *weren't *impossible, it would still be *impractical *because of the tremendous amount of energy it would take...unless one had the ability to channel some kind of divine energy, flowing from a higher plane of existence, much like Severian does. Maybe that's *exactly *how the Claw works, and this thing about the flower leaping into being is a hint of that.

2009-03-20 Son of Witz #

Builds on John Watkins's remark that the Claw may reverse time, linking it to the idea that light is the Increate's shadow and that darkness is disorder. Quotes the Shadow of the Torturer passage on order, light and darkness, and calls it both hard SF and theological.
supports · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading

Oh, good stuff Watkins.
when you mention time reversing when exceeding the speed of light, that resonates with some of the other meat of this opening passage, the idea of the light being God's shadow. you're sort of sketching in hard sf terms. When I read the passage I was thinking, Wow, this is sort of a hard SF idea, AND a completely theological idea, And completely poetically surreal, and excellently paradoxical.

shadow chap 24
"The Increate maintains all things in order surely; and the theologicans say light is his shadow. Must it not be then that in darkness order grows ever less, flowers leaping from nothingness into a girl's fingers just as by light in spring they leap from mere filthiness into the air? Perhaps when night closes our eyes there is less order than we believe. Perhaps, indeed, it is this lack of order we perceive as darkness, a randomization of the waves of energy (like a sea), the fields of energy (like a farm) that appear to our deluded eyes---set by light in an order of which they themselves are incapable---to be the real world."

~witz

2009-03-20 Son of Witz replying to Son of Witz #

Points to a passage in The Shadow of the Torturer where Dorcas tells Severian that he is like her in wanting to bend time backward, and argues that she grasps much of his nature right away. Quotes the lines in which Dorcas says his face showed nobility and that high thoughts come when the world is horrible, noting she was not mocking him.
informs · close-textual-reading

Shadow Chap 24 (Orb omnibus 1 p148)
""I would tilt it as far back as I can, until at last the evil runs out altogether," I said. "It might be the good that would run out. But I am like you; I would bend time backward if I could.""

Dorcas is the second speaker.
very interesting. "I'm like you: I would bend time backward if I could"

Dorcas intuits a lot of Severian's nature immediately in this scene. more, from right before that quote above:
"In my ear, too softly, I think, even for Hildegrin to hear, Dorcas said, "Yes, you were. Your face was full of beauty, of a kind of nobility. When the world is horrible, then thoughts are high, full of grace and greatness." I looked at her, thinking she was mocking me, but she was not,"

~Witz
