The thread: Marc Aramini opened by asking what the 'green goddess' dish in An Evil Guest is and whether Deanna Diamond could be the altered Donny Dukes, noting weight loss at the end. Bruce Hayles answered that it is a salad dressing and attributed Cassie's weight loss to months on an island with little food. Fernando Q. Gouvêa listed several salad dressings and suggested Wolfe was simply having fun, and Matthew Keeley and brunians followed with jokes about red herrings and readers chasing small details. Stuart Hamm cited an interview suggesting food may have been deliberately mapped into the book. Dave Tallman later argued at length, with page references, that Donny Dukes and Deanna Diamond are the same person, so the thread ended with the food question settled lightly while the identity question was left with Tallman's textual case.

2008-10-15 Dave Tallman replying to Marc Aramini #

Argues that Donny Dukes and the assassin Deanna Diamond may be the same person, presenting textual clues from An Evil Guest: Donny's swishiness, the assassin's slight build, a thick green drink, a dancing remark, and a role in a play. Responds to Marc Aramini's suggestion by listing page references and noting Wolfe's habit of making pretense become reality.
supports · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Donny Dukes is Deanna Diamond

That's very reasonable, given the same-initial clues we have elsewhere. Also:

1) "In real life Donny Duke was small and swishy..." p. 53. The assassin is a "little bit of a thing." p. 256.
2) He drinks something green which is thick like toothpaste, appropriate for a Cthulhu cultist. p. 63,64.
3) He can "dance the paper off the wall, but he's not male" p. 136. 6) He played Seurat, who murdered Cassie's character Mildred Norcott in Act Two of "The Red Spot" p. 63. Wolfe often has pretense become reality.
