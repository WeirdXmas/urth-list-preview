Themes: Catholic faith and sacraments

2010-04-19 Gwern Branwen #

Points readers to John C. Wright's blog post linking a Borges story to The Book of the New Sun, and says Wright's reading of Borges as a parodist of non-theistic philosophy reveals more about Wright than Borges. Grounds the disagreement in Borges's own introduction to the Book of Imaginary Beings, where he says the Trinity is more fantastic than any invention, and in his remark about Omar Khayyam.
disputes · theology-and-religion · source-and-allusion · theories: Borges is a parodist of non-theistic philosophy

http://johncwright.livejournal.com/331497.html

Connects 3 Borges story to TBOTNS. I don't think the connections will be much controversial.

(I do disagree with his theory of Borges as a parodist of non-theistic philosophy; that he sees Borges that way says more about Wright than Borges. Borges can't believe in the Christian God because, as he says in the introduction to _Book of Imaginary Beings_, the Trinity is more fantastic an imagining than any he could devise.
To know theology is not to believe: "He [Omar Khayyam] is an atheist, but knows how to interpret in orthodox style the most difficult passages of the Koran; for every educated man is a theologian and faith is not a requisite.")
