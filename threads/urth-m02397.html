Ship Citadel

Urth list · 8 messages · 2006-05-06 to 2006-05-07

2006urthThe Book of the New SunDuncan TruterMacronautRex RacerthalassocratRoy C. Lackeyb sharp
The thread: The thread asked whether the Citadel of the Autarch is itself a spaceship or a set of ships converted into towers. b sharp argued it is partly built from old ships, citing a hulk at the old port and passages on a propulsive chamber. Rex Racer and thalassocrat argued the towers are separate landers, not one ship, while Roy Lackey claimed most towers are spaceships. Macronaut and Duncan Truter offered an uncited memory of a tower compared to structures not meant to fly. The thread ended in impasse: b sharp said consensus needed that citation, and Roy cited a CLAW passage describing the witches' tower as a mere building.
  • b sharp: the Citadel is partly built from old spaceships, with towers perhaps made from former masts
  • Rex Racer: the Citadel is several landers converted into towers, not one ship
  • Roy C. Lackey: most towers are individual spaceships, but the witches' tower is merely a building
  • thalassocrat: individual towers are spaceships while the Citadel itself is not
  • Macronaut: the Beast Tower was once called a Zoetic Transport, and a tower is compared to structures not meant to fly
  • Duncan Truter: recalls the uncited tower-comparison passage as support for the ship reading

Themes: Buildings, cities and labyrinths, Memory and forgetting, Robots, androids and artificial life

2006-05-06 b sharp #

Argues that the Citadel of the Autarch is built partly from old spaceships, with the towers perhaps made from former masts, citing an officer's reference to a hulk at the old port and passages describing a propulsive chamber, a portless cubbyhole, and a port meant to hold the void. Grounds the claim on textual quotations from The Shadow of the Torturer and The Urth of the New Sun, and responds to Roy's earlier reference.
proposes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion · theories: The Citadel is constructed from old spaceships

Well, interesting. Roy's reference to Urth XXXV was illuminating actually in that an officer tells Severian he is being taken, "To the old port. One of the hulks there has been fitted up to hold prisoners". So perhaps the Citadel is constructed of more than one old ship. But spaceship it is, as evidence by these other passages:

Shadow III: Description of the Matachin Tower- "(for the examination room was the propulsive chamber of the original structure)".

Shadow XI: Description of Severian's new room after elevation- "because I was the most junior of the journeymen, the least desirable in the tower, a portless cubbyhole".

Urth XXXVII: Description of the port in Severian's cell (Roche's future bedchamber)- "its broad, smooth flanges, of a shaped I had never considered, had clearly been intended to hold the void at bay"

I think there are one or two more I can't find scattered in the text. I wonder if perhaps the many towers of the citadel were made from the former giant masts. Just speculation there.

-Bsharp

Argues that the Citadel is made of several separate landers converted into towers rather than one ship with other ships attached, disagreeing with b sharp's reading of the hulks passage. Grounds this on the wording of the supporting passages, which describe individual landers at rest at a spaceport, and on the idea of a collection of towers and walls.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: The Citadel is a collection of converted landers

Yes. The supporting evidence after the bit you quite applies to individual "landers" that have been converted to towers, landers/spaceships that were at rest on a regular old space port. The Citadel is a collection of towers and walls, not a monolithic construct studded with other ships.

2006-05-06 Roy C. Lackey replying to b sharp #

Argues that most towers of the Citadel in The Citadel of the Autarch are individual spaceships, offering passages on the towers being ready to leap into the air and an old map with towers not in the Citadel as direct proof. Responds to b sharp's reading that the Matachin Tower was a spaceship by saying those passages prove only that tower, and cites Severian's dreams of old ships blasting off as further support.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Most towers of the Citadel are individual spaceships; The Citadel was once a spaceport

The above passages prove only that the Matachin Tower was a spaceship. More than once in the Urth cycle Severian dreams/imagines the old spaceships blasting off into space. Somewhere he speculates that, ancient as the Citadel may be, the tunnels beneath it antedate it. There is no doubt about it; most of the "towers" in the Citadel are individual spaceships.

But if you want more direct proof:

"The Citadel itself was visible now as a whole, and for all its vastness seemed a glittering flock fluttering upon the hill, its thousand metal towers ready to leap into the air at a word." CITADEL, chapter XXXVII

And in the next chapter he went looking for Valeria from above.

"I asked the castellan for a map. After much searching he produced one, large and old, cracked in many places. It showed the curtain wall whole, but the names of the towers were not the names I knew - or that the castellan knew, for that matter - and there were towers on that map that are not in the Citadel, and towers in the Citadel that were not on that map. " I ordered a flier then, and for half a day soared among the towers. No doubt I saw the place I sought many times, but if so I did not recognize it."

The map obviously dated to a time when the Citadel was still being used as a spaceport.

-Roy

2006-05-07 Roy C. Lackey replying to b sharp #

Cites a passage in CLAW chapter XXX where Severian recalls the witches' tower, a tall building resembling a skyscraper that lacked the solidity of his old spaceship home. Responds to b sharp's claim that parts of the citadel were spaceships, offering this text as evidence that the tower is merely a building.
informs · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian's old home was a spaceship

He must be thinking of a long paragraph in chapter XXX of CLAW, when Severian was thinking of his childhood visit to the witches' tower, where he first saw Merryn. His description of that tower shows it to be merely a tall building, after the fashion of modern skyscrapers. It lacked the solidity he was used to in his old spaceship home.

-Roy
