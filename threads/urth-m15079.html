GW for Kids

Urth list · 4 messages · 2010-03-23 to 2010-03-23

2010urthStuart HammJames WynnFernando Q. GouveaJosh Geller
The thread: Stuart Hamm asked the list how to introduce children to Wolfe's challenging fiction, describing reading 'The War Beneath the Tree' and La Befana to his kid, noting alien-world clues and a Christian message that non-Catholic readers might miss, and mentioning that his wife had a very different interpretation. James Wynn argued that the boy-and-monkey conversation in 'The Sailor Who Sailed After the Sun' would appeal to children and suggested it as an entry point to Egyptophilia, without detailed textual support. Fernando Gouvea asked about the wife's reading and replied with an anecdote about a college seminar where students found Wolfe harder than other assigned books. The thread stayed brief and ended without a settled answer to Hamm's question.
  • Stuart Hamm: Wolfe's Christmas stories carry alien clues and a Christian message that a child can be guided through, though his wife reads them differently
  • James Wynn: The monkey conversation in 'The Sailor Who Sailed After the Sun' would appeal to children and introduces Egyptophilia
  • Fernando Gouvea: Wolfe was hard for college students, judging from a residence-hall seminar, and he asks about the wife's reading

Themes: Christ figures and messiahs, Catholic faith and sacraments, Beasts and the animal in us

2010-03-23 Fernando Q. Gouvea replying to Stuart Hamm #

Asks Stuart Hamm about his wife's interpretation of a Wolfe story read to their child, and recounts running a college residence-hall reading seminar on Wolfe's stories that students found harder than the other assigned books. Responds to Hamm's account of reading Wolfe with his daughter, including the Befana story and the alien-world clues, with a personal anecdote rather than analysis.
chats

Now I'm curious about your wife's interpretation...

I didn't try Wolfe with my kids until they were teenagers. I did once try The Island of Doctor Death and Other Stories and Other Stories with a group of college students. They have here something called a "residence hall reading seminar" that's light and ungraded, and I was persuaded to run one of those. After a couple of weeks one of the students came up to me and said "You do realize that most of the books assigned in these seminars are much easier than this one, right?"

:-(

Fernando
