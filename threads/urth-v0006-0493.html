Urth list · 10 messages · 1998-02-01 to 1998-02-05

1998urthThe Book of the New SunDave LeblingJason VoegeleKieran ClearyJames JordanMichael Straight
The thread: Readers debated whether humans in Severian's far-future world have evolved beyond Homo sapiens sapiens, a question spurred by vizcacha's claim that human life is unchanged fifty million years on. Jason Voegele argued for change and sought the textual passage, and Jim Jordan and Nutria backed that view, pointing to tortured victims' endurance and engineered people. Jason then cited Shadow & Claw II, Ch. 8, where Jonas implies the man-apes and miners were altered, and Kieran Cleary gave a vague citation of the same scene. Michael Straight offered an alternative explanation, that torturers' skill rather than victims' hardiness explains endurance, while allowing both factors might hold. The thread ended without full agreement, with the evidence resting on a passage cited from memory.
  • Jason Voegele: humans of Severian's era have evolved beyond Homo sapiens sapiens, citing Shadow & Claw II, Ch. 8
  • Jim Jordan: the far-future setting is supported by the claim of evolved humans
  • Nutria: tortured people's endurance and human engineering suggest development beyond today
  • Michael Straight: victims' endurance under torture reflects torturers' guild skill, not victims' hardiness
  • Kieran Cleary: the human-change reference comes from Severian talking with Jonas after leaving the mine at Saltus

Themes: Beasts and the animal in us, Evolution and the future of humanity, Deep time and ruins

1998-02-01 Dave Lebling #

Argues that the sun's illness is probably deliberate sabotage against Typhon's empire, and that the mountains are carved in human shapes, the continents were frozen by technology, and the tetralogy's near-future setting is the work of Deep Time. Supports these points with the text's statements about the sun, the mountains, the South American geography and the tectonic evidence, and answers CRCulver's geography point.
proposes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: The dying sun was deliberately sabotaged to weaken Typhon's empire; The mountains are carved in the shape of people; Technology froze the continents in place; Severian may be a sliver of Tzadkiel

What a great flurry of new activity! And some new blood as well. Yumbo!

On the illness of the sun: It is stated in several places that the sun is being devoured by a black hole. That much is impossible to argue with. The debatable point is whether it's natural causes or murder. It seems pretty clear to me that it was done on purpose to weaken Typhon's empire. The coincidence is too unlikely otherwise. Who dunnit? The obvious SFnal idea is that it's "the rebels," whomever they might be. The unobvious idea is that it's Severian himself, or more generally the forces of good. It's pretty clear that the sort of empire Typhon was ruling is one you don't want as a neighbor, so...

On the mountains: This may just be me reading things into the text that aren't there (what! never!), but the mountains are clearly "all" carved in the shape of people. And it isn't just autarchs, either. You could look it up.

On geography, to CRCulver: The action of the tetralogy takes place in South America, and yet there is a great river that flows southwestward over a plain, and great mountains to the north and east of the plain. This isn't _our_ South America. Either lots of time has passed (and getting serious mountains in that end of SA is going to take a lot of time -- the plate tectonics are all going the wrong way), or someone has been fiddling. On the other hand, the statement that such stuff has stopped is a pretty strong argument for Deep Time. Perhaps the same technology that reshaped the continents froze them as well -- what a nuisance if you get the continents where you want them and they start drifting off again!

Tzadkiel and Severian: Recall that Tzadziel can send slivers of itself off as independent beings. Now we have the suggestion that Severian can do the same. Perhaps Severian _is_ Tzadziel, in that sense?

Deep Time: The hardest thing to believe about TBOTNS taking place 50 million years in the future is that _so little_ has changed. People are still people, exactly as they are now. The average species lasts on the order of three million years. Even with all sorts of advanced technology, would 50 million years have changed so little?

Wolfe is a master at making things simultaneously not be what they seem and giving clues to the truth. It's perfectly appropriate that it should seem like millions (or even billions) of years have passed, just as it seems that we're reading a sword & sorcery story at first. After all, one of many things the book is is an homage to _The Dying Earth_, which is pretty clearly science fantasy. One of Wolfe's goals was to make the science fantasy into science fiction. Vance's book doesn't make sense as science fiction, so Wolfe spent a great deal of effort to make sense of it.

-viz
([email])

1998-02-02 Kieran Cleary #

Proposes that the black hole devouring the Old Sun is the necessary complement to the New Sun, a White Fountain erupting from another universe, with both linked through Severian as two sides of one coin. Grounds the claim in itemised textual points: the Conciliator called Dark Sun or Dark Star, the New Sun described as the opposite of a black hole, and a black hole in the Old Sun.
proposes · close-textual-reading · myth-and-structure · theories: Black hole destroying Old Sun complements New Sun; Severian joins the two suns as one coin

How about this then:

Item: The Conciliator is sometimes referred to as "Dark Sun" (or "Dark Star" can't remember which.)
Item: The New Sun itself is a White Fountain. This is described in the text as being the opposite of a black hole, i.e. an erruption of matter from one (lower) universe to another (higher) universe.
Item: There is a black hole in the Old Sun
Item: The White Fountain that is the New Sun was not born in the same universe as Urth. (shaky here, but I thought that was mentioned in tUotNS)

So, speculation follows: the black hole that is destroying the Old Sun is merely the necessary complement to the New Sun. One has caused the other, and are both different sides of the same coin that is Severian.

1998-02-05 Michael Straight #

Suggests that the endurance of victims under torture in the Book of the New Sun reflects the skill of the torturers' guild rather than any hardiness of the victims. Responds to Jim Jordan's reading that the victims' ability to bear pain implies humanity has evolved beyond its present state, offering an alternative explanation that both factors could be true.
