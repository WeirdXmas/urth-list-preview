Babbiehorn?: Was: a sincere question mostly for roy

2011urthThe Book of the Long SunThe Book of the New SunGerry QuinnDavid StockhoffJames WynnLee Berman
The thread: Lee Berman asked why Babbie's species name (Babbiehorn) sounds unlike other Blue-world names, suggesting it reflects domestication, and asked what Mucor intends by sending Babbie with Horn. James Wynn replied that Babbiehorn is a fine Vironese name, citing the real babirousa, and that Mucor's last-moment command leaves its intent unknown. Gerry Quinn argued the overt explanation suffices, citing the island setting, lack of water for Mucor, and Horn's need for protection. Lee Berman later disputed David Stockhoff's claim about Ushas becoming a jungle planet, placing Ushas and Blue in a post-Flood, pre-Christ past grounded in a Wolfe interview. The thread drifted from the name question into the cosmology of the planets, without a clear resolution.

  • James Wynn: Babbiehorn is a plausible Vironese name and Mucor's intent is unknown
  • Gerry Quinn: the overt island-and-protection explanation for Babbie's role is sufficient
  • Lee Berman: Ushas is distinguished from Green by its oceans and sits in a post-Flood, pre-Christ past

Themes: The dying sun and the cosmos, Myth and folklore sources

2011-11-21 Lee Berman replying to Lee Berman #

Disputes David Stockhoff's claim that the New Sun will make Ushas a jungle planet, arguing instead that Ushas's oceans distinguish it from Green. Places Ushas and Blue in a post-Flood, pre-Christ past alluding to Egyptian, Hebrew, Greek and Roman material, while Green is an Eden after the Fall; grounds this in Wolfe's video interview about creating a universe without Christ for The Wizard Knight.
disputes · allegory-and-typology · author-and-biography · theories: Green is Eden after the Fall; Ushas and Blue are post-Flood pre-Christ earth; Wolfe creates worlds without Christ

I disagree. Ushas may be warm but it is not a jungle planet. Ushas differs from Green in one very important aspect: oceans. This issue was discussed recently but I think it is so crucially important that it bears repeating.

This is where Marc and my disagreement over time frame becomes important. He sees Green (and the Green Man) as part of humanity's future. I see them as the past and Green as an allusion to Eden, after the Fall, dominated by reptilian, parasitic things. Both Blue and Ushas seem to me to allude to a post Flood, pre-Christ earth. That time period has ALL the stuff Wolfe is interested in- Egyptians, Hebrews, Greeks, Romans Persians, etc.

(Well, he is interested in medieval times, Vikings and Norse myths also. As he stated in the video interview, he had to create a new universe to stage Wizard/Knight; one which lacks Christ. That seems an important key to most of Wolfe's work. He is acknowledged as a Christian writer but he doesn't want to write stories in which the presence of Christ is a factor. It is his absence that interests Wolfe.)
