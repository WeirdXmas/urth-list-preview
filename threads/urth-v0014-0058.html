Authors as torturers?

Urth list · 1 messages · 1998-06-19 to 1998-06-19

1998urthThe Book of the New Sunadam louis stephanides

Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Unreliable narration

1998-06-19 adam louis stephanides #

Corrects William Ansley's recollection by pointing to a passage in The Shadow of the Torturer, chapter 33, where Severian compares authors and readers to executioners and spectators rather than torturers and clients. Notes that Severian's stated writing principles match poorly with his actual narration, though his requirement to add a personal feature to the execution is fulfilled by the book.
disputes · close-textual-reading · genre-and-craft · theories: Severian's narration contradicts his stated writing principles

Could you be thinking of the passage in Shadow, ch. 33, in which Severian gives us his thoughts on authorship? But here he compares the relationship between authors and readers not to that between torturers and their clients but between executioners and their spectators. And he at least presents himself as wanting to please his readers, not torture them.

Incidentally, it's interesting to compare what Severian set forth here as the principles of writing he's following with his actual practice as a narrator. IMO there's a pretty poor match, except for his final self-imposed requirement that "he must add to the execution some feature however small that is entirely his own and that he will never repeat." This the book fulfills in spades.

--Adam
