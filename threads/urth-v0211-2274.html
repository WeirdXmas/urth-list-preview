2002-11-27 Marc Aramini #

Proposes two speculative theories for how time travel converges on the point in Urth's past where Silk meets young Severian: Severian is folded into Scylla or a dark swimmer near the boat and will be placed in his tomb, or Severian acts as a time vortex attracting time-travelers. Grounds these in the Vanished Person Swimmer on Horn's boat, Severian's presentiment of his future, and Rigolio's earlier visit.
proposes · chronology-and-plot-logic · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian is folded into Scylla; Severian will be placed in his tomb; Severian is a vivimancer time vortex; Malrubius and Silk form the time anchor

Here are two absurd theories that attempt to account for the time travel to that particular point in Urth's history where Silk can meet Severian as a young man.
Here is one: that Severian is actually going back with them to be placed in the tomb he will one day lie in - part of Scylla is Severian, who is ACTUALLY the dark spot floating next to the boat in both worlds (as the Vanished Person Swimmer who comes up on Horn's boat)- and perhaps he is folded into himself in the past of Urth and this explains why Severian always had some presentiment of his future from the very beginning. Of course, this only accouts for Severian's presence on Blue in the last two visits to Urth, not in the first case with Rigolio. Or perhaps when he goes back he joins up with Scylla to rest inside Oreb. But it does account for his statement that he will end up in that tomb, which we have never seen actuated.

The other is that Severian is a vortex of time - a vivimancer, who attracts anyone with the ability to cross time (like the green man or Ossipago, et al.)

Or the affinity between Malrubius and Silk could serve as that anchor. I need to re-read the texts again. I will find a link the next time through. Marc Aramini

2002-11-27 Chris P #

Disputes the second of two fanciful theories about Severian's presence in the time-travel scene, arguing that if Severian is a magnet for such events he would have to be a stronger one at that particular moment than at any other time. Responds to the earlier post's quoted theory that Severian is the dark swimmer in the boat, and notes the poster has a separate theory about Severian's presentiments.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Severian is the dark swimmer in the boat

I don't know about the first (though I have my own theory as to Severian's presentiments, and the apparent Severian/Severian-2 deal).

With the second, though, the problem is that even if Severian is a sort of magnet for such events, Severian is practically everywhere and everywhen. So not only does he have to be a vortex, but he has to be more of a vortex at that *particular* time than any other time.

Chris
