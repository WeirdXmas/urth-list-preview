2014-09-03 Lee Berman #

Argues that the Shadow Children, Inhumi, Aelf, Undines, Neighbors and hiero-types in Wolfe's fiction are best understood as faerie folk, and that they may be changelings replacing humans. Builds this on hints about Shadow Children on Earth in deep-time places like Gondwanaland, Atlantis and Mu, and on David Stockhoff's earlier faerie framing, and floats a changeling reading of The Wizard Knight.
proposes · myth-and-structure · source-and-allusion · theories: Shadow Children are faerie folk; Shadow Children replace humans as changelings; The Wizard Knight based on the changeling concept

Actually I still think you were on the right track. In "Gondwanaland",

"Atlantis" and "Mu" I find hints that some version of Shadow Children

were on Earth even before the evolution of Homo sapiens.

In a discussion here a few years ago on the same topic, David Stockhoff

invoked the concept of "Faerie" folk as a way of understanding some

underlying themes in Wolfe's work.

As with leprechauns, kelpies, brownies, fairies, elves, trolls, mermaids,

sirens, etc. from mythology, there are categories of Wolfean characters

who are not science and technology based like humans but rely more on

cleverness, trickery and "magic" in accomplishing their goals. Thus, in

Wolfe's work we have Shadow Children, Inhumi, Aelf, Undines, Neighbors and

perhaps the hiero-types.

Consider the concept of "changeling" found so commonly in our tales of faerie

folk. I think it adds focus to the idea of Shadow Children, Abos, Inhumi, Hieros

and others in Wolfe stories imitating human form and even, for Shadow Children/Abos

replacing specific human beings.

(I sometimes wonder if Wizard/Knight might ultimately be based on the "Changeling"

concept. If an earth boy finds himself in a Faerie world, might there be a

replacement version of him living on Earth?)

2014-09-03 Jeff Wilson replying to Lee #

Proposes that a spacefaring race brought imitators from St Anne to Earth in the Homeric period, where they infiltrated as fairies and elves and were assimilated, setting up later French explorers. Grounds the idea in the changeling and Shadow-children material raised by Lee and in a comparison to colonial divide-and-rule patterns such as Rwanda.
proposes · puzzle-solving · myth-and-structure · theories: Imitators from St Anne seeded Earth's fairy myths; Shadow children are imitators assimilated into humanity; St Anne spacers returned to set up French explorers

Could it be that some other spacefaring race brings imitators from St Anne to Earth during the time of the Homeric Greeks, where they infiltrate as feys and elfs then are assimilated? The other spacers could return to St Anne with humans who are imitated and assimilated, setting the stage for the French explorers to land 4 000 years later?

In reading about the middle eastern strife, I see that there is indeed a colonial pattern of using multiple races to dominate one another, even importing a elite minority when necessary; thus the Rwandan civil war after the Belgians stopped supporting the Tutsi as their exploiters of the Hutus and the the Twa pygmies.
