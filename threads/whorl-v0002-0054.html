The thread: James Stewart asked whether readers must reread the earlier Long Sun books before Exodus from the Long Sun, and whether Wolfe's praise of Lamarckism is a fit list topic. Fernando Q. Gouvea and Doug Eigsti both advised rereading, citing the long gap between volumes and forgotten details, and Eigsti found rereading Lake of the Long Sun richer than the first pass. Revjack said Exodus tied things up and was less opaque than the earlier books, and asked what Lamarckism means. Derek Bell added that rereading the early Book of the New Sun made the characters, especially the sybils and Maytera Rose, more vivid. The thread drifted from advice on reading order to appreciation of the characters, with the Lamarckism question left mostly unanswered.

1997-01-21 Doug Eigsti #

Advises a newcomer that rereading the earlier Long Sun books before Exodus from the Long Sun is worthwhile, because the two-year gap makes details easy to forget. Grounds this in his own group's decision to reread and his experience rereading Lake of the Long Sun, which he finds richer than the first pass.
answers

Our local reading group, The Fictionados, decided it would be wise to reread the first three books before trying to tackle EXODUS. The two year gap being the main reason. Wolfe is brutal to readers who forget critical details. I am now in LAKE and am finding the experience much richer and more satisfying. I was still trying to learn the rules of the Whorl on the first pass. Now I can dig deeper and delve into some of the fun mysteries Wolfe has buried. Some of the comments on this archive are driving me to read EXODUS but I know that a careful reading of the precursors will pay off when I get there. I am reading all the "spoilers" posted. Wolfe has never been about what happens next. Getting there is the fun.

=Doug Eigsti=
