The thread: Lee Berman and Jeff Wilson debated whether the boy artist Rudesind is secretly the painter Fechin, with Dorcas's husband as a possible link. Berman argued the brushes in the boy's portrait mark it as a self-portrait and pressed the identification. Wilson first proposed Fechin is the old man on the ladder and the boy portrait is a self-portrait meant to suggest Rudesind, citing the wrong name Becan and Fechin's career. Wilson then floated Fechin as Rudesind's biological father, but objected to the age implications for the two men. The thread ends without resolution, with the father theory undercut by the ages.
  • Lee Berman: Rudesind is really Fechin, and the boy portrait is a self-portrait
  • Jeff Wilson: Fechin is the old man on the ladder and the boy portrait is a self-portrait made to resemble Rudesind
  • Jeff Wilson: Fechin might be Rudesind's biological father, though the ages make this implausible

Themes: Unreliable narration, Doubles, twins and clones, Masks, disguise and shapeshifting

2011-01-16 Lee Berman #

Argues that the boy artist Rudesind is really the painter Fechin, and therefore Dorcas's husband, citing the brushes in the boy's portrait as a classic clue that the painting is a self-portrait. Responds to Jeff Wilson's remark about Fechin and Casdoe's father's supposed misremembering of the name, pressing the identification further.

Careful Jeff. YOu are going too far down my path. ;- ). Rudesind was a boy artist who meets an artist ("Fechin, Fechin, himself"). Rudesind's boy portrait includes paint brushes which is an old "mystery" prop which allows the solver to figure out the painting was a self-portrait. I.e. Rudesind is Fechin. If Fechin had painted the picture of Dorcas then yes, it would be worth a lot of money, as Ouen's words suggest. So Fechin/Rudesind is Dorcas' husband. Both Rudesind and Dorcas' husband speak in the same folksy dialect. Bent, twisted monkey stuff all around.

I'll stop there but you can probably guess where it is headed.

2011-01-16 Jeff Wilson replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that the artist Fechin is the old man on the ladder and that the boy in the portrait is a self-portrait meant to make people think he resembles Rudesind. Replying to Lee Berman, it cites Casdoe's father's wrong name Becan, his schoolmate tone toward Fechin, and Fechin's known career when Rudesind was a boy.
proposes · puzzle-solving · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Fechin is the old man on the ladder; Fechin's brushes signal a self-portrait disguised as Rudesind

Not at all. Casdoe's dad calls Severian "Becan" even though he knows it is not the right name at least some of the time, so we know he gets names wrong some times, right? In fact, he can't name the girl in the anecdote at all. But he otherwise speaks as thoguh he and "Fechin" are school chums, while Severian judges him to be Rudesind's age. Meanwhile, Fechin was a publicly known artist at the time Rudesind was a schoolboy, so he has to be significantly older than Casdoe's dad and Rudesind.

Sev can see that the boy in the portrait is the younger appearance of the old man on the ladder. If the brushes really signify a self-portrait, it's not Fechin on the ladders pretending to be Rudesind, it's a plot by Fechin to make people think he looks like Rudesind.
