Harry S Truman

2000-06-27 Adam Stephanides #

Reports the punctuation of Smoky's and Truman's names in Crowley's story, noting Smoky's period after the S while the suit's Truman appears without it. Cites the story's text in 'A Suit of Truman's' and suggests Crowley omits the S on purpose, joking about a paper on the letter S.
informs · close-textual-reading

If you're asking about the punctuation of Smoky's legal name, he does put a period after the S.

Truman's name, on the other hand, appears as "Harry Truman," no S at all ("A Suit of Truman's," I, 4, p. 59 of Bantam TPB), although the S appears in the suit's monogram. Come to think of it, it strikes me as very unusual to refer to Truman as "Harry Truman"; either "Truman" or "Harry S Truman" are far more common. It's as if Crowley is emphasizing the "S" by omission.

Okay, so who's going to do the article on "The Letter 'S' in John Crowley's _Little, Big_"?

--Adam
