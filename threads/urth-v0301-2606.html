2003-01-05 Josh Geller #

Argues that Typhon, in Severian's meeting with him in The Sword of the Lictor, understands from Severian's use of the term 'New Sun' that Severian is the New Sun, even though Typhon himself never uses the term. Rests this on the exchange where Severian names the New Sun and Typhon recognizes him as the man who disrupted his operation, disagreeing with Marc Aramini's reading of the scene.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Typhon recognizes Severian as the New Sun

No. As you said, the conversation went like this:

Typhon's pretty sharp. He figured it all out, except which head to defend, and who can really blame him for that?

My reading of this is almost the exact opposite if your reading of it.

Typhon does not know the term 'New Sun'. Why should he? The term has come into use in the time he has been dead. But as soon as Severian uses it he knows both that Severian is the New Sun and that Severian does not know that he is the New Sun.

But he certainly does recognize Severian. Why shouldn't he? This is the guy who just screwed up his whole operation, in his timeline.

.

2003-01-05 Roy C. Lackey #

Disputes Josh Geller's claim that Severian wrecked Typhon's operation, arguing that Typhon's siege, the wounded sun, the departed ships and the peasant revolt all came before Severian arrived. Grounds the point in the timeline: Severian was on the scene for under a week and Typhon died shortly after he left.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Severian did not cause Typhon's downfall

Screwed it up how? Typhon was already besieged on his mountain when Sev arrived in his timeline. Sev was there for less than a week. The sun had already been wounded, all the space-worthy ships were gone, the peasants were in revolt, and Piaton was dragging his heals about giving up his body, all before Sev came on the scene. Typhon died not too long after Sev disappeared. Typhon's problems were all of his own doing; Sev had nothing to do with any of it.

-Roy
