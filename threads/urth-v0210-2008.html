2002-10-31 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Adds a playful observation that the Japanese syllabic character usually romanized as N changes sound before B or P, so Rampo, Rambo and Tombo are spelled with it, and lists Japanese words and film titles as examples. Responds to Crush and hartshorn, who noted the spelling Ranpo for the writer Edogawa Rampo, with a joke about the spelling confusion.
informs · source-and-allusion

Responding to Crush, hartshorn quoted

That's funny, because I initially wrote it as RAMPO two or three times and then corrected it to the Ranpo that Crush gave!

The humor goes further, in that (iirc) the syllabic character itself is/says "N," except in the case where it is followed by "B-" or "P-" (so Rampo, Rambo, Tombo, Tompopo). Slippery little character! <g>

To-to-ro!
Ra-pyu-ta!
Mo-no-no-ke!
Chi-hi-ro!
