2002-05-14 Marc Aramini #

Argues that Apple Blossom's line about her chop, or seal, in Wolfe's story "Underhill" is a joke, since she first mentions saving her chopsticks before shifting to her seal. Reads the magical box as a white Chinese takeout container and says the story treats Western mystification of Chinese food as a major theme.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Underhill's magical box is a white Chinese takeout box; Apple Blossom's chop line is a deliberate joke

I'm not sure if anyone was confused by Apple Blossom's statement in "Underhill", but when she says "there is also my chop-my seal perhaps?" she is first talking about saving her chopsticks, then decides to make herself more important by talking about her seal and defers talk of saving the "the ivory sticks that feed [her]" for a sentence or two. The joke is that the magical box is a white box of chinese take out food, I guess - if there is a joke at all there. Alas, the mystification of Chinese food in the west seems a major theme of this story.
Marc Aramini

2002-05-15 Russell Wodell #

Argues that Apple Blossom's mention of her "chop" in "Underhill" refers to a personal seal or stamp rather than chopsticks, citing an OED definition of the Indian and Chinese usage. Responds to Marc Aramini's earlier reading, and adds that the story's box is a takeaway carton and that Chinese food's mystification is a theme.
disputes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Apple Blossom's chop means a personal seal

Apple Blossom's statement is _not_ about chopsticks; her usage of "chop" is quite mundane, about a personal name stamp or "seal," viz this definition from the OED.:

"In India, China. A seal or the impression of a seal; an official impress or stamp.

1614 MILWARD in Purchas Pilgr. I. 526 (Y.) The King [of Achen] sent us his Chop. 1678 Lett. from Dacca Fact. in India Office (Y.), Alledging that they came without ye Visiers Chaup to him. 1696 J. OVINGTON Voy. Suratt 251 (Y.) Upon their Chops as they call them in India, or Seals engraven, are only Characters, generally those of their Name. 1818 JAS. MILL Brit. India III. 340 Mr. Pigot is said to have seized his chop, or seal, and applied it to the paper. 1859 SIMMONDS Dict. Trade, Chhap, an official mark on weights and measures, to indicate their accuracy; an eastern Custom-house stamp or seal on goods that have been examined and have paid duty."
