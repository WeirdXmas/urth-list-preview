2010urthThe Book of the New SunJane DelawneythalassocratDavid StockhoffJeff WilsonDan'l Danehy-OakesRoy C. LackeyAndrew Mason
The thread: The thread asked whether Typhon founded the Urth-based Empire in The Book of the New Sun or was its last Monarch, with the carved mountains and succession to the first Autarch as the key evidence. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued Typhon was the last Monarch and so did not build the Empire, while Andrew Mason argued he created it as a lone figure seeking the lost stars. David Stockhoff pushed back in two posts, proposing Typhon was a minor despot who may have seized power by force, and Jeff Wilson and Jane Delawney argued Typhon was the first to carve a ruler's likeness into the mountains. Roy C. Lackey and Mr Thalassocrat reinforced the debate with textual points, and the thread ended without agreement, with several readers citing different passages.
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: Typhon was the last Monarch, not the empire's founder, based on the carved mountain chain and succession to the first Autarch
  • David Stockhoff: Typhon was a minor despot who may have seized the throne by force, with Urth his chosen capital
  • Jeff Wilson: Typhon was the first to carve a face into the Andes, and later Autarchs inherited earlier minds
  • Andrew Mason: Typhon created the Empire as a lone seeker after the stars, not a galactic ruler by inheritance
  • Jane Delawney: Typhon was the first carved ruler, and the Whorl may have been his last message
  • Roy C. Lackey: Typhon was not ruling a galactic empire, and the Whorl expedition was a gesture

Themes: Books, libraries and writing, Power, rulers and politics, Deep time and ruins

2010-09-23 David Stockhoff replying to Andrew Mason #

Argues that Typhon was a minor despot ruling a handful of star systems rather than a galactic conqueror, and that Urth became his capital by personal and political choice rather than by inheritance. Responds to Andrew Mason's point about Typhon's origin in the stars, offering that Typhon may have been human-stock from a more advanced planet while Baldanders created himself.
proposes · close-textual-reading · myth-and-structure · theories: Typhon ruled a handful of star systems; Urth was not the center of the human universe; Typhon was created from human stock

Exactly. I see Typhon as far from a galactic conqueror, that he?ruled a handful of star systems.?I see him as a minor despot who is a towering figure only because the true emperors are gone. Nor do I imagine starliners ever going back and forth between worlds, like trains. But that's just a suspicion, and it's based on Severian's world. Maybe the robot-culture empire was indeed more familiar to us than his viewpoint makes it; maybe Typhon was more like the Mule than I think.? Anyway, even if he created his empire I'd apply the same logic, because it would be natural for an Urthian to make Urth his capital.? Another assumption I admit to is that I don't see Typhon's empire as created from scratch---I see him as going out and roping together a number of planets like ancient city-states that were once united. They were colonized long ago, in other words, and at the time rivaled Urth.? I get no sense from Typhon that any world in his empire lorded it over any other. Rather, he owned, possessed, and ruled them all. If Urth were still the mother planet, she would be the metropole and would suck up all the others' resources, which would almost demand Typhon's identification with the mother world's elite and thus his location of his capital there. But again, he seems to have chosen Urth deliberately for personal and political reasons, which suggests Urth was not the center of the human universe at the time.? If so, another world may have been greater than Urth ever was, which again provides an explanation for whatever technology (if that's what it is) produced him. Possibly his planet's elite practiced genetic modification on themselves, just as Urth's did, but discovered how to produce greater mental powers, as they did not. Perhaps the mandragora came from there. So as to the "monster" question, Typhon would then have been created from human stock. Baldanders created himself. I can see both as Monsters.

2010-09-23 Jane Delawney replying to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that Mount Typhon was the first mountain carved with a ruler's likeness, with later Autarchs following the tradition, and speculates that Typhon sent out the Whorl as a last message. Grounds this in the scene where Severian, returning to the Cursed Town as Typhon's prisoner, sees the unmodified peaks and judges from the carving's state that Typhon has little time left.
proposes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: Typhon was the first Monarch carved into a mountain; Typhon sent the Whorl as a last message

True, that's made quite explicit in BOTNS

On the contrary I believe it is stated quite clearly in UOTNS that Mount Typhon is the first to be carved in this way. Severian-the-Conciliator is struck by the unmodified mountain peaks when he revisits the Cursed Town as Typhon's prisoner and he is able to tell the Chiliarch that from the observed state of the works, Typhon has little longer to live (presumably he remembers the positions of the (stopped) digging machines and the exact state of the carving from his earlier/later visit).

There's no suggestion that any of the other peaks are similarly carved at this stage, indeed I think it is stated clearly that they are not. Typhon was the first to have the massive ego I suppose! Later, some Autarchs adopted the 'tradition' and had their own likenesses carved into other mountains; at Severian's (original) time they've been carved that way for so long that the bare peaks seem strange to him.

Shame we're not told who some of the others might have been!

Typhon didn't build the Empire though; that's very clear. He was a (monstrous) product of its last days and its decadence - to the extent that having despatched various ships including the generation ship the Whorl, he found himself marooned on Urth and unable to leave.

I do wonder (as one who has not read the Long Sun / Short Sun books) whether Typhon sent out the Whorl as some kind of last hurrah, a message from the vast ego of the last Monarch of Urth to the galaxy that his predecessors once dominated.

jd

2010-09-24 Roy C. Lackey replying to Andrew Mason #

Argues that Typhon was not ruling a galactic empire and that his Whorl expedition was a gesture rather than an effort to preserve or spread humanity. Supports this with Apheta's remark to Severian that humans were already sown over tens of thousands of worlds, and with the passage where Severian sees the unshaped mountains before Typhon carved them.
disputes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion · theories: Typhon ruled only a few planets, not a galaxy-wide empire; Typhon's Whorl expedition was a one-finger salute

Yes, her story seems to link him to the library at the Citadel, and the physician who looked in on Severian there after the "earthquake" lends that some support. But Typhon had dreams of a Second Empire, as her tale specifies. The First Empire had covered the whole galaxy and maybe others. The old spaceships at the Citadel in Typhon's time were rusting relics of any earlier wave of spacefare; the Citadel is even referred to then as the "old port". Typhon evidently ruled some undetermined number of planets, some of them probably outside of Urth's solar system, but such planets as he ruled paled in comparison to the First Empire.

[snip]

I don't think anyone has claimed that Typhon ruled a galactic empire, and he didn't. But it is also true that humans were already spread all over the galaxy, at a minimum. As Apheta told Severian: "Because your race is important to us. It would be far less laborious if we could deal with it all at once, but you are sown over tens of thousands of worlds, and we cannot." (URTH, chap. XIX, p-135)

Typhon's _Whorl_ expedition was more of a gesture than anything else, a one-finger salute to the universe and a sop to his ego. It had absolutely nothing to do with preserving the human race or spreading it around the galaxy.

BTW, in another thread, it is true, as others have pointed out, that Typhon was the first to carve one of those mountains into human likeness. When Severian was taken to meet Typhon on the mountain, he stepped out of the flier and saw: "All around us ragged, faceless peaks loomed through pent clouds. We were among mountains, but mountains that had not yet put on the carven likenesses of men and women--such unshaped mountains, then, as are to be seen in the oldest pictures." (URTH, chap. XXXVIII, p- 271)

-Roy
