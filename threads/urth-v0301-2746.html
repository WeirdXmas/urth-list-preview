2003-01-21 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Argues that the month length used in his calculations about the doors in There Are Doors is loose, sometimes 28 days and sometimes 30, and that a strictly lunar Otherworld calendar would imply thirteen months, which the text does not directly show. Responds to David Duffy, who quoted him and asked whether the figuring assumed lunar months given that he had not read the book.
answers · chronology-and-plot-logic

Probably. If the question is "what number(s) am I using for a month in these figurings," I've been pretty lax -- sometimes using 28 days, often using 30 days.

No, wait: if the Otherworld months were strictly lunar, then there would be 13 months to their calendar -- we have no direct evidence of this.

2003-01-21 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that an Otherworld calendar need not have exactly thirteen lunar months, using the Hebrew calendar as a model: it adds a leap month in seven years of each nineteen-year cycle, repeating Adar rather than adding a numbered thirteenth month. Supports the point with the varying lengths of Hebrew years, from 353 to 356 days for normal years and 382 to 385 for leap years.
disputes · source-and-allusion

Not necessarily ... consider the Hebrew calendar, which has twelve lunar months and adds a thirteenth "leap" month when=20 needed -- it's needed in seven years out of the calendar's nineteen year cycle. But it isn't a "thirteenth month," as=20 such, it's a repeated month -- they have two months of Adar. Go figure. _Plus_, there are actually eight different possible lengths for the year, due to rules about what day of the week the year can start on and other things. (Normal years can have from 353 to 356 days; leap years from 382 to 385.)

--Blattid
