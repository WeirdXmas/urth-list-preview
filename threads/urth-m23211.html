2012-03-28 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that Asimov's Three Laws of Robotics were designed to make robots perfect slaves, a coded commentary on minority persons and Black people. Responds to David Stockhoff's remark that robots are more moral than humans, citing the human habit of calling a robot 'boy' as evidence for the social reading.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Asimov's Three Laws encode commentary on race and slavery

More to the point, the Three Laws of Robotics are designed to make Asimovian robots the perfect slaves, and thus a coded way of talking about the situation of minority persons in general and blacks in particular at a time when you couldn't openly talk about that stuff in popular fiction. (If you have any doubt about this, note how hostile humans refer to a robot as "boy.") Reading Asimov's early robot stories with this in mind opens a whole world of social commentary right there on the surface.
