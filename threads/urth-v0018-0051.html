Weer's House

Urth list · 3 messages · 1998-08-28 to 1998-08-28

1998urthC R CulverWilliam H. AnsleyAlex David Groce
The thread: CRCulver opened the thread by arguing that the rooms in Weer's house in Peace are not physical rooms that really exist, citing a Wolfe interview remark about recreated rooms and offering it as a tentative opinion. Alex David Groce replied with a recollection of Wolfe's essay "Peace of My Mind" in Castle of Days, in which Wolfe objects to readers treating the museum house as an eldritch Poe-like device and describes it as more ordinary than some think, though Groce was unsure whether the essay implies Weer built such a house. William H. Ansley disputed the eldritch reading, arguing via the same essay and reviewer Colin Greenland that Greenland misread the device and that the house is plausible enough to be built. The thread ends with the two sides split between supernatural and ordinary readings, with no resolution in the posts.
  • CRCulver: the rooms are not physical rooms that exist, supported by Wolfe's interview remarks
  • Alex David Groce: Wolfe's essay rejects the eldritch Poe-like reading and describes the house as ordinary
  • William H. Ansley: the house is plausible enough to be built; Greenland misread the device

Themes: Memory and forgetting, Ghosts, dreams and visions, Mirrors, pictures and art

1998-08-28 C R Culver #

Argues that the rooms in Weer's house are not physical rooms that exist, based on a quoted Wolfe interview remark about recreated rooms and supernatural or mental explanations. Grounds the claim in Wolfe's own words to James B. Jordan, offered as a tentative opinion inviting other readers' views.
proposes · author-and-biography · theories: Weer's house does not physically exist

Wolfe said in the interview with James B. Jordan:

"I wanted to do the rooms that were recreations and
rooms that [Weer] had known as a very young man and I didn't think that anybody would actually do that. That it had to be a supernatural stick or a mental quirk or something of that sort."

I think this means the house didn't exist...just my opinion. Perhaps the other listees can get more out of it.

1998-08-28 William H. Ansley #

Argues that the museum rooms in Weer's house in Peace are not eldritch or Poe-like, citing Wolfe's essay "Peace of My Mind" in Castle of Days and the reviewer Colin Greenland's remark. Says Greenland seems to misread the device, since a conventional house with memory-triggering rooms would not have drawn his complaint, and concludes that the house is plausible enough to be built.
disputes · author-and-biography · genre-and-craft

My novel _Peace_ has just been published in Britain.... Colin Greenland has reviewed it kindly in the London _Times Literary Supplement_, but what we are doing here at Aussiecon Two strikes me as proving that I am right and he is wrong. Let me quote a bit of his review: "Drawing on the energies of the past, he wanders through the house, whose rooms seem to change about him. Weer remembers designing it and 'interspersing among the functional rooms of my home certan "museum rooms" ', 'duplicating or nearly duplicating certain well-remembered rooms whose furnishings had fallen to me by inheritance'. The sheer Poe-like oddness of this contrivance is the metafictional trick."

Mr. Greenland supposes--in his review, at least--that to come accross odd corners of one's past in odd corners of one's house (which is what Alden Dennis Weer does in _Peace_) is, as H. P. Lovecraft might have said, eldritch.

-from "_Peace_ of My Mind" in _Castle of Days_

I don't think Wolfe is being fair to Mr. Greenland here. Obviously, if all Wolfe had done was have ADW find items that brought back memories in "odd corners" of a conventional, stable house then Mr. Greenland wouldn't have written what he did. However, I don't think I understand or think I don't agree what Mr. Greenland is saying about metafiction. But, since the quote is out of context, I guess I am not being fair, either!

Wolfe says he didn't think anyone would actually build a house such as Weer describes designing in _Peace_. After reading _Peace_, *I* believe that Weer would have built such a house. Wolfe is just that good!

I think the anti-house camp has expended some convincing ammunition, but you need not waste any more on me. I prefer to stay here in my bunker and preserve my illusions.

William Ansley
