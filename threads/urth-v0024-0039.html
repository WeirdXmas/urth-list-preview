1999-03-04 Roy C. Lackey #

Argues that Kevin Malone cannot be possessed by the elder Malone, because the timeline of The Pines shows the elder Malone could not have grown up on the estate whose memories Kevin has. Works through dates and ages: the estate's construction, Betty Malone's age gap, and the NASA-era present to show the elder Malone was born too late to have been raised there.
disputes · chronology-and-plot-logic · puzzle-solving · theories: Kevin Malone is possessed by the elder Malone

The above does not address when/how KM came to be possessed by the elder Malone. While he was still an innocent (so far as The Pines is concerned) orphan, who had had no prior connection to the estate? The "spirit", or whatever, of the elder Malone, operating at some remove from the estate, attaches itself to a random, orphaned boy, implants false memories of and causes to pine for, a home the child had never known? Causes the boy to grow up and amass a fortune just so he can buy the estate (how long did that take, 20 years, 30 years?)?
Or did a rich man, who need not have been an orphan, just happen to buy the estate and only then become possessed? If so, why then give him false memories, either of growing up on the estate and then leaving for the orphanage or not growing up on the estate but still going to an orphanage? KM can not have the elder Malone's memories of growing up on the estate because the estate didn't exist when the elder Malone was a boy! We are told The Pines was one of the estates designed "During the teens and twenties..." (p-38). The current KM is middle-aged (what, 50?). The "now" of the story is probably the mid-to-late 1970s, because NASA is mentioned. KM would have to have been born circa the mid-to-late 1920s, the murder/suicide circa the late 1930s, in order for him to go to the orphanage at age 12. Betty Malone is stated to have been 20 years younger than the elder Malone (p-48). Even if Betty was relatively young when she was killed - say 20 -, that would make the elder Malone 40. He would have to have been born at about the turn of the century. Ergo, it was not possible for him to have been raised at The Pines.
The dates and ages I have given are necessarily approximations, but they can't be far enough off for KM to have the elder Malone's memories of growing up on the estate. My theory is looking better all the time! <g>

Roy

1999-03-04 Tony Ellis #

Argues that the story never says how or when Kevin Malone came to be possessed by the elder Malone, so the question does not matter, and rejects Roy's theory as adding unwritten Malones and events. Grounds this in the story's ghost-story framing, noting it was commissioned for Ramsey Campbell's New Terrors, and says the theory flattens the creepiness.
disputes · close-textual-reading · genre-and-craft · theories: Kevin Malone is possessed by the elder Malone

Absolutely. We don't -know- how or when this happened. The story doesn't tell us. Like the question "how many children had Lady Macbeth?", it's not important.

Once again: since the story doesn't tell us one way or the other whether the elder Malone grew up on the estate, it can't matter. We're not supposed to know precisely what memories KM has or believes he has. All we need to know is that he thinks he's the son of Betty and the elder Malone, and that there never was a son.

Well, as SBear points out, your theory requires the writing-in of extra Malones and events that just aren't in the original story. For me that's enough to reject it out of hand. And perhaps more importantly, this is a -ghost- story - originally commissioned by Ramsey Campbell for his anthology New Terrors.... No offense, but your explanation turns a fine, insidiously creepy story into something pretty flat.<g>
