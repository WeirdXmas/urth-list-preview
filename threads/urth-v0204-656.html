A Price on my Head (was: Wash Post article)

The thread: Readers debated whether Wolfe's books share one universe, with James Jordan offering a tongue-in-cheek extension linking Urth to the Soldier books via Marc's remark about memory. Michael Straight argued against resting the claim on Occam's razor and proposed that thematic links such as double planets, shapeshifters and colonization make The Fifth Head of Cerberus a companion to the Sun books. Nicholas Gevers held that Cerberus belongs to the Briah Cycle, extending his Five Steps Towards Briah framework, and challenged opponents to name what disqualifies that assumption. Roy C. Lackey's post was truncated and contributed no content, so the thread ended without resolution.
  • James Jordan: all Wolfe works share one universe, playfully extending from Urth being Old Earth
  • Michael Straight: Cerberus is a companion to the Sun books based on thematic links, not Occam's razor
  • Nicholas Gevers: Cerberus belongs to the Briah Cycle, and the twin planet parallel requires conceding it

Themes: The dying sun and the cosmos, Stories within stories, Buildings, cities and labyrinths

2002-04-18 Michael Straight #

Questions whether the assumption that Wolfe's books share one universe should rest on Occam's razor, and argues that thematic links such as double planets, shapeshifters and colonization justify treating The Fifth Head of Cerberus as a companion to the Sun books. Responds to Nicholas Gevers, who asked what disqualifies the idea that Cerberus is set in Briah, by weighing the Long Sun Pas-Typhon connection as a test case for shared settings.
proposes · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic · theories: The Fifth Head of Cerberus is set in Briah; A Solar Labyrinth is about reading Wolfe's books

This just further supports the theseus that "A Solar Labyrinth" is about reading Wolfe's books.

I'm not sure which way Occam's razor cuts. Should one assume that an author's works are all set in the same universe unless there is evidence otherwise ("Universes should not be multiplied unnecessarily"), or does the assumption lie in the other direction ("Connections should not be assumed unnecessarily")?

Would you have assumed the Long Sun books were set in Briah if Wolfe hadn't made the Pas-Typhon connection?

I'm much more inclined to see a thematic connection (double planets, shape shifters, colonization, etc.) and I think that's sufficient reason to argue that 5HC is a companion work to the Sun books.

-Rostrum

2002-04-18 Nicholas Gevers #

Argues that The Fifth Head of Cerberus belongs to the Briah Cycle, a hypothesis extending his Five Steps Towards Briah framework, and that the twin planet parallel requires conceding it. Responds to Kevin Maroney, defends the labyrinth metaphor and the shifting significance across Long Sun and Short Sun, and challenges opponents to name what disqualifies the Briah assumption.
proposes · myth-and-structure · theories: The Fifth Head of Cerberus is part of the Briah Cycle; Twin planet parallel requires conceding the Briah hypothesis

Nigel Price: Thanks for your kind remarks about my
article. In response to your points:

1) When I referred to Wolfe as a maze builder, yes, I
certainly had “A Solar Labyrinth” (one of Wolfe’s best
stories) in mind, but the labyrinth metaphor is one
with a broad natural application to Wolfe. It simply
fits, specific story or no. Your analysis of Wolfe’s
shifting shadows of significance is elegantly
formulated. That example of the end of LONG SUN is a
good one—when I wrote my “Five Steps Towards Briah”
article back in ’98, the flight from false world to
pleroma seemed very clear, but the bases shifted for
SHORT SUN—somewhat, not invalidating but rather
recontextualising what had gone before.

2) Colons: I’m an inveterate user of colons and
semicolons; I like long, rhetorically balanced
sentences. I think the editors at the Wash Post did a
lot of repunctuating in the article, and the standards
they used were very much theirs.

3) Yes, I’m proposing that 5HC is part of the Briah
Cycle, retrospectively included of course. As I
recently said in response to Kevin Maroney, this is
experimental, something I think should be tested, and
used if found to be sound. I think that the twin
planet parallel requires that my hypothesis be
conceded. Perhaps I should articulate a challenge, to
those who don’t think 5HC is set in Briah: what
actually disqualifies my assumption? Why do you say so
emphatically that 5HC doesn’t fit?

--Nick Gevers.
