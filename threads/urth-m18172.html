Themes: Beasts and the animal in us, Names and their meanings

2011-01-17 Lee Berman #

Argues that names like Tick, Silk, Auk and Chenille group around silk-like weaves, and that some characters with monstrous names began human and transformed into monsters over time, using Scylla and Baldanders as examples. Grounds the claim in Scylla's dual human and snake-armed appearance on a dream trip to Urth and Baldanders' growth in the Long Sun books.
proposes · close-textual-reading · allegory-and-typology · theories: Tick, silk, chenille and auk names group as silk-like weaves; Some monsters began human and became monsters; Scylla is a transitional stage in Cilinia's transformation; Baldanders grew from human shape into monster

Tick weave, like tussah and chenille, can be considered a form of silk. I think the name grouping you are looking for might be found there.

I suspect Roy doesn't but actually I do. Scylla appears with snake-arms on a dream trip to Urth. She is also shown with normal human bones and with a saint name. This dualistic rendering of her suggests to me that some beings on Urth have a dual nature, human and monster.

It may be that some monsters on Urth get their monstrous names because of a monstrous origin. But there is evidence that some characters start out human but get a monster name as they become monsters.

Take Baldanders. He seems to have started with a normal human shape. By the time we meet him he has grown into a small monster and has a monster's name. But he is still basically human in shape. But, if we could see how he looks after 1000 years of living underwater I suspect he would be so big and distorted he would no longer be recognizable as human.

Likewise with Scylla. Perhaps a girl with long snake arms was a transitional stage in Cilinia's transformation to sea monster. The final stage is the gargantuan Great Scylla thing we see pinching off human-shaped clones at the end of RttW. Took about 1000 years.
