2000-08-10 Dennis G. Berdanis #

Argues that Silk was already dying in In Green's Jungles, not merely in despair, citing the Neighbor's phrase "instead of two dying men" as evidence. Challenges the suicide reading by asking what text supports it, suggesting Silk may have died of grief after Hyacinth's death.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Silk was dying of grief, not suicide

.

Also note that is says "instead of two dying men" which implies to me that Silk was already dying and was not just in deep despair contemplating suicide.

I also don't see the evidence that Silk had committed suicide. Perhaps he was just at the end of his life span. Many times when two people are joined in spirit and body for many years as he and Hyacinth (sp?) were when one dies the other doesn't live much longer. Perhaps he was just dying from sadness/grief.

What in the text leads the suicide believers to believe so?

Dennis/Endy

2000-08-10 James Jordan #

Proposes that Silk is dead at the end of In Green's Jungles, with his gifts passed on through his genetically enhanced body, as fits the book's religious structure. Responds to Adam Stephanides' point that the text implies Silk was already dying, and admits the poster misremembered that the fate of Silk's soul is never stated.
proposes · theology-and-religion · close-textual-reading · theories: Silk dies and passes his gifts to another

Yeah. I did not remember right. We aren't told what might have happened to Silk's soul. My guess is that he is dead. For Silk to die and to pass on his gifts to another in the process (i.e., his genetically enhanced body) is in keeping with the religious structure of the book. But with GW, you never know!

Nutria
