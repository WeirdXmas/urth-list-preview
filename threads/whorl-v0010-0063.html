Themes: Ghosts, dreams and visions

1999-10-06 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Asks how the events around Seawrack's missing arm fit together, including whether the huss bit her, why Horn did not wake, and whether Mucor directed the attack. Cites the blood on the deck, Horn's later licking-up theory, and Babbie's wrestling remark as the evidence that makes the episode confusing.
questions · close-textual-reading · chronology-and-plot-logic

Did I miss something about Seawrack, the semi-Venus de Milo?

That is: first there was the blood on the deck.

Then, didn't Horn decide that the huss had =not= struck whatever it was, but only licked up the blood after?

Then Seawrack shows up with one arm.

And much later, she mentions that Babbie wrestles hard (blowing up Horn's earlier thought that Babbie couldn't have been so active that night without waking him up?).

So, okay--huss nipped her arm off, just below the shoulder. She grendeled it back beneath the wine dark sea.

But why he bite? Just because she was climbing aboard? (Or is it more a case of being directed by Mucor, because she doesn't like Seawrack?) Was she trying to do some violence on that first visit? (Doesn't seem to fit the preparations of dolling her up--but maybe that was only done on the second try?) And why didn't Horn wake up?

It is just one of the confusing episodes that changes over time in the course of the novel and I don't quite have a grip on it yet, nor am I comfortable in my wallow of ambiguity.

Help me, somebody! Thanks in advance.

=mantis=
