The thread: Participants debated whether Wolfe's short story Copperhead is worthwhile, with Dan'l Danehy-Oakes and Alice Turner dismissing it as unresolved, trivial, or venting political spleen, and Alex David Groce calling it a weird throwaway whose forked-tongue dialect irritates. Michael Straight answered Groce's question about non-trivial short Wolfe pieces by listing favorites, and James Russell then turned to decoding the dialect words and linking the inhumi breeding detail to the Short Sun plot. The thread shifted from critical dismissal to close textual puzzle-solving, ending without resolution of the story's merit.

2001-05-04 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that Copperhead's opening threads go unresolved, the strange-voiced woman's behavior is unexplained, and its Genesis-like ending does not follow, and suspects the story vents spleen at Bill Clinton. Responds to Oolp's list of Wolfe short shorts by naming 'The Detective of Dreams' as his favorite and calling several listed pieces conceits or fluff.
disputes · close-textual-reading · author-and-biography · theories: Copperhead vents spleen at Bill Clinton

Oolp -- well, if you're going to go that long (I have trouble thinking of a couple of these as "short shorts") don't neglect "The Detective of Dreams," my single favorite Lupine text, _period_. OTOH, I would regard about half the ones you name as essentially conceits ("Peritonitis," "The Whip") or bits of fluff ("War Beneath," maybe "Useful Phrases").

My first reaction to "Copperhead" was roughly equivalent to my first reaction to several other Lupine shorts, notably the ever-present "Suzanne Delage": "Huh?"

I'm not ashamed to say that I Don't Get It, and (unusual for me with Wolfe) I'm not at all convinced that "it"'s there _to_ get. there's bunch of stuff at the beginning that isn't followed up at all; I have no idea why the woman with the strange voice from another dimension behaves as she does; and the ending vaguely-out-of-Genesis simply doesn't follow from the story in any way I can perceive. I suspect the whole thing of being to some extent a venting of spleen at Wm Jefferson Clinton, who doubtless has it coming, but would probably not have a clue that spleen had been vented at him.

--Dan'l

2001-05-04 Alex David Groce #

Argues that the short story Copperhead is a weird throwaway piece, not especially obnoxious apart from its irritating forked-tongue alien dialect, and that Wolfe should stick to novelette length. Responds to alga's dismissal of the story by asking whether many short Wolfe stories are anything but trivial, naming two other short pieces.
disputes · genre-and-craft

Are there more than a handful of Wolfe stories this short* that _aren't_ weird little throwaways? I didn't find this one particularly obnoxious, other than that ultra-irritating "alien woman speak with forked tongue" dialect, just par for the course.

Wolfe should stick to novelette length, at least. And avoid trying to be Lafferty.

* "Three Million Square Miles," "A Cabin on the Coast," and... there must be something else slipping my mind.

2001-05-04 Michael Straight #

Lists Wolfe's short stories the poster likes, including "A Solar Labyrinth," "Westwind," "How the Whip Came Back," "Peritonitis," "The War Beneath the Tree," and "Useful Phrases," a translation of a Borges story. Answers Alex David Groce's question about short Wolfe stories that are not throwaways, adding pieces from Peace and noting that some readers prefer "Suzanne Delage."
answers

Other good Wolfe Short Shorts:

"A Solar Labyrinth," "Westwind," "How the Whip Came Back," "Peritonitis," "The War Beneath the Tree," "Useful Phrases" (actually Wolfe's translation of a Borges short story, but still good). I also like the one that's in Peace, the tall-tale about the cat and the big ship, and I also love the little story about the sidhe at the end of Peace; some people also seem to like "Suzanne Delage" more than I do.

-Rostrum

2001-05-07 Jim Russell #

Proposes that the dialect words 'neffer' and 'grawnt' in the story Copperhead are 'never' and 'grunt', reading the line as Jane Doe refusing the President sex, and guesses that 'groose' means 'gross'. Also asks whether the inhumi's breeding method, described by Jahlee or Fava, bears on the Short Sun plot and on Horn or Silk as possible inhumi options.
proposes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion · theories: Copperhead's 'neffer' and 'grawnt' are dialect for never and grunt

It seems to me that 'neffer' and 'grawnt' are 'never' and 'grunt'. "You will never grunt over me" again is Jane Doe denying the President sex, I assumed. What 'Groose' is supposed to be I dont know. "Gross", perhaps?

Also, with regards to the Short Sun, I was wondering if anyone thought that the inhumi's method of breeding had any bearing on the secret (or even the dubious Horn or Silk as inhumi option). Jahlee or Fava, I can't remember which, claims that the inhumi make love exactly as a man or woman would do, before moving on to the more technical, amphibious process. I'm sure that this has some bearing on the plot, although I haven't really managed to work out what.

On a personal note I haven't contributed to this list before, but have been lurking for ages. Hello everybody.

Jim Russell
Exeter. UK.
