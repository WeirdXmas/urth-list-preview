2008-11-06 Stephen Case #

Half-jokingly welcomes a bumpersticker reading "Silk for Calde" and asks for a spray-painted or chalked version, then argues that the Severian-Malrubius exchange on forms of government reads as a Catholic view of ideal rule, with monarchy as the best form and Christ as the perfect monarch. Grounds this in the passage's own content and in Chesterton-style reasoning, admitting Wolfe's political leanings are unknown to him.
proposes · theology-and-religion · theories: Malrubius exchange expresses a Catholic view of monarchy

Regarding the "Silk for Calde" bumpersticker: this is great! I will immediately print it off and attach to the back of my Metropolitan . . . that is, unless anyone has a version that looks a bit more "spray-painted" (or, I should say, "chalked").

Regarding the posts on politics, the first thing that struck me when I read Marc's post was also the exchange between Severian and Malrubius on the forms of government. To me (who doesn't know much about Gene's political leanings) this seems to me more of a summation of a Christian (or at least Catholic) view of the ideal government (realized, of course, in the Church). I think Gene might say something about Christians remembering that democracy isn't actually the most "Christian" form of government. Like Malrubius gets Severian to realize, it's monarchy. Of course, to a Christian the perfect monarch would be Christ.

A simple interpretation, to be sure, but that's how this passage has always struck me: a very Chestertonian explanation of a Christian view of politics.

-Steve

2008-11-06 Paul B replying to Stephen Case #

Explains that the poster made the Silk for Calde bumpersticker in white-on-black to approximate a chalked look, and says blurring letter edges or using a different background would add little when printed on a regular printer and viewed from a distance. Responds to Stephen Case's praise and request for a spray-painted or chalked version, offering a practical design note rather than an interpretation.
informs

Yes, white-on-black is about as close as I was able to get to a "chalked" feeling, assuming chalk is white. It's possible to get it a bit better by blurring the letter edges a bit, or swapping the black background for something close to shiprock, but if you're going to print it on a regular printer and view it from 10+ feet away, there's really little point.

Paul
