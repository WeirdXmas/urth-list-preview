The thread: Readers debated how Wolfe's worlds relate: whether Urth precedes Earth or lies inside Yesod, whether Pas is still Typhon, and how Catholic the Chapter in The Book of the Long Sun is. Tim O'Donnell, Lee Berman and Jeff Wilson argued for temporal or dimensional readings, while James Wynn pressed the Catholic and demiurge parallels and John Watkins and brunians pushed back. A separate argument over whether the Ship's faster-than-light sailing is physically plausible, with Gerry Quinn objecting, drifted into physics and then into a riddle game and personal exchanges. The thread ended in impasse, with much off-topic sparring.
  • Tim O'Donnell: Urth-in-future squares with parallel worlds, and later events ripple back in time
  • Jeff Wilson: Urth does not precede Earth; the Ship exceeds light speed via a fifth dimension
  • James Wynn: The Chapter is a twisted Catholic Church, and Pas is Typhon as a Gnostic demiurge
  • John Watkins: Pas is more demiurge than antichrist, and Wolfe plays with ideas rather than in-world causes
  • brunians: Pas and Typhon have diverged into distinct persons; celibacy is biologically grounded
  • Gerry Quinn: Faster-than-light tacking breaks relativity and light gives no sideways push

Themes: Time loops and causality, The dying sun and the cosmos, Catholic faith and sacraments

2010-06-04 Lee Berman replying to Tim O'Donnell #

Proposes that the Jungle Hut in The Book of the New Sun is on Earth rather than Urth, reached by Severian through a portal, and that Father Inire is the figure found there. Builds on Tim O'Donnell's idea of Baldanders/Talos affecting Mary Shelley's dreams, citing Borski's identification of missionary models for Robert and Marie and the naked Isangoma glimpsed in the hut.
proposes · puzzle-solving · source-and-allusion · theories: Jungle Hut exists on Earth via portal; Robert and Marie based on real missionaries; Father Inire is the naked Isangoma

I like the idea of the presence of Baldanders/Talos rippling through time and space to affect the dreams of Mary Shelley on Lake Geneva (or vice versa).

Still there are other Earthly artifacts on Urth which seem to demand a more substantial connection, most notably in the Jungle Hut. I think the Jungle Hut actually exists on Earth not Urth, and that Severian has passed through a portal from one world/universe to the other to get there. I think Borski identified a historical pair of missionaries who perhaps form the basis for Robert and Marie. And I suspect the master of portals on Urth, Father Inire, is found in that hut. We get most of our glimpses of the mysterious Padre in cowled or masked form but I think in the Hut we get an all too explicit view of him as buck naked Isangoma.

Argues that the tacking method for faster-than-light travel in The Urth of the New Sun fails on two grounds: relativity forbids superluminal speeds, and light exerts no sideways pressure like wind does. disputes · genre-and-craft

I know that even 'hard' SF writers generally get a free pass when it comes to FTL travel. But the method described in 'Urth of the New Sun' has two problems with it.

First, there is the usual issue of travelling faster than light. Special relativity is well established, and while it could be replaced by a new 'aether' theory, any such theory would have the same issue with a maximum speed. We regularly push stuff to >99% of light speed and we know what happens.

Secondly, the 'tacking' method of travelling faster than the wind works for sailboats. But the analogy between wind and light radiation doesn't hold up because light does not exert pressure in directions orthogonal to its direction of travel, as moving air does.

It's not a big issue, and in fact I mentioned it because I think it's a very nice idea. But we can be pretty certain that no future technology will use it.
