Themes: Catholic faith and sacraments, Names and their meanings

2000-02-01 James Jordan #

Proposes that the name Buster Hill in the story Pepper Mill is a deliberate Christian reference, reading Hill as Calvary and Buster as broken. Ties the foot wound to Genesis 3 and suggests that writing and public relations stand for the church's Word and evangelism, though the poster admits this may be a stretch.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · source-and-allusion · theories: Buster Hill names Calvary and brokenness; Foot wound echoes Genesis 3

Heading is spoiler warning, so you've been warned.

I compleatly omitted to mention that Buster Hill as a name seems to be a pretty clear reference:
Hill = Calvary
Buster = Broken
The foot wound, which seems to have several significances in Wolfe's more complex works (i.e, both saint and Claudius, etc.), here in this simple story would seem to be directly from Genesis 3. "Writing" and "public relations" can, in the light of this symbolism, be seen as cyphers for aspects of the church (Word; evangelism). Maybe. But I think the name itself is clearly deliberate.

Nutria
