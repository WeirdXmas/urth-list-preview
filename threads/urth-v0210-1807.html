2002-10-05 James Jordan #

Argues that all people share a sinful inherited condition, but that God's grace separates those who turn to Him, such as Mother Teresa, from those who do not, such as Hitler. Responds to Roy's claim that Wolfe's beliefs, as inferred from his work, do not weigh an individual's acts in the final balance. Notes that Wolfe might allow for deathbed conversions, though this is probably not happening in Peace.

Just a note from a theologian:

"Equal in God's eyes" is tricky, but I know what you mean. No. They start out the same: conceived in iniquity and born dead in trespasses and sins, and thus with an inbuilt tendency to rebel more and more against God. But their condition inherited from Adam is (more than) matched by the grace offered by God to those who turn to Him. And that's where the whole course of one's life comes in. Hitler did not turn, and got worse and worse. Mother T. did turn, and became a saint.

To be sure. But Wolfe would also allow for deathbed conversions. Not that this is probably going on in PEACE.
