Island of Doctor Death

Urth list · 4 messages · 2000-04-18 to 2000-04-20

2000urthTony EllisOl' PappyMichael StraightAlex David Groce
The thread: Readers discussed how to interpret Wolfe's "Island of Doctor Death". Alex David Groce proposed that a literal character-in-a-book reading and a Catholic parable reading are compatible, citing Chesterton and Muriel Spark. Tony Ellis argued that Dr. Death's remark about starting the book again makes Tackman a character who reappears on each reread, so the story is about reading stories, and he disagreed with a Catholic reading. Michael Straight objected that rereading cannot make Tackman older, and suggested the line might mean the story itself grows older. The thread ends with Straight's objection standing against Ellis's reading, with no resolution recorded.
  • Alex David Groce: literal character-in-a-book reading and Catholic parable reading are compatible
  • Tony Ellis: Tackman is a character who reappears on each reread, so the story is about reading stories
  • Michael Straight: rereading cannot age Tackman; the line may mean the story grows older

Themes: Stories within stories, Books, libraries and writing, Catholic faith and sacraments

2000-04-19 Alex David Groce #

Proposes that a character-in-a-book reading and a Catholic parable reading of the story are compatible, citing Chesterton's view that Christianity suits the spirit of boys' adventure stories. Grounds this in Wolfe's familiarity with that idea, his essays in Castle of Days, and Muriel Spark's The Comforters linking creatures of God with characters in a book.
proposes · theology-and-religion · source-and-allusion · theories: Catholic parable and character-in-a-book readings are compatible

Perhaps the character in a book reading and the Catholic parable reading aren't incompatible: Chesterton, after all, quite a few times talks about Christianity as being highly in tune with the spirit of Boys' Adventure Stories (and, more generally, the idea of the novel). This is an idea Wolfe is certainly familliar with (and his essays in CASTLE OF DAYS have some of the same spirit). You could also tie in Muriel Spark's take on this in THE COMFORTERS, which certainly links being a Creature of God and being a Character in a Book.

2000-04-19 Tony Ellis #

Argues that Dr. Death's remark about starting the book again means that Tackman is a character in a book who reappears each time the story is reread, so the story is about reading stories and the title points to that. Responds to Christy's memory reading and explicitly disagrees with Nutria's Catholic reading of the same story.
disputes · close-textual-reading · genre-and-craft · theories: Tackman is a character in a book who is rewritten on rereading; The Island of Doctor Death is a story about reading stories

No no no no no. He's talking about books. Tackman doesn't realise it, but he's a character in a book. And when we start the book again, there he is, writing his name in the sand.

This is why I would tend to disagree with Nutria's (still fascinating) Catholic reading. For me this is a story about reading stories. Hence the title.

2000-04-20 Michael Straight #

Disputes Tony Ellis's reading that Dr. Death's remark about restarting the book means Tackman is a character in a book, arguing that rereading cannot make him any older. Notes that Tackman never ages past the moment of his sentence however often the book is reread, and suggests the line might instead mean the story grows older.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Dr. Death's line means the story grows older

But then Dr. Death's statement makes no sense. No matter how many times we read the story, Tackman will never be a minute older than he was at the end of Death's sentence (heh, accidental double entendre there).

I guess he could be refering to Tackman's story getting older as we read it over and over.

-Rostrum
