2000-02-10 Ol' Pappy #

Argues that the posthumous-life idea in Wolfe's new collection Strange Travelers derives from Swedenborg's system of afterlife, and that Wolfe likely drew on Borges's writing about Swedenborg. Points to Blake and M. Night Shyamalan's The Sixth Sense as later uses of the same idea, and poses whether the characters are already dead.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Posthumous life in Strange Travelers derives from Swedenborg

I haven't read *Strange Travelers* yet (it is en route from Amazon), but I can say that the jag on living on after death is Swedenborgian. No doubt at the behest of Jorge Luis Borges, we can assume that Wolfe has read some of his material (*Heaven and Hell* is the most famous and interesting) and is devoting some airplay to it. Swedenborg explains a system of thanatosis that has appealed to many (most apparent is William Blake), and most recently to M. Night Shyamalan, the writer/director of *The Sixth Sense*. Watch that movie, or read Swedenborg's book, to ask the same horrible question that Wolfe must have already asked himself: am I already dead?

-C-

2000-02-11 Alex David Groce #

Argues that the dead protagonists in Wolfe's "The Other Dead Man" and Peace reflect a broader purgatorial-fantasy tradition rather than deriving from Swedenborg. Points out that Wolfe's approach treats the afterlife with ghost-story tropes, as an alternative to Dante's place-based purgatory, and that Swedenborg matches it only partly.
disputes · source-and-allusion · theology-and-religion · theories: Wolfe's dead narrators follow a purgatorial fantasy tradition, not Swedenborg

I'm sure Wolfe's come across Borges references to Swedenborg, and perhaps read some--but really the idea of dead protagonists (from "The Other Dead Man", PEACE, etc.) is not necessarily thus derived. It's not an unheard of theme. Wolfe's general take on it seems to be in the mode of "Purgatorial Fantasy"--an alternative to Dante's view of purgatory as a place, bringing in the tropes of the ghost story instead. It does match Swedenborg well, but that's probably not the only possible source.
