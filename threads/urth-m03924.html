2007-06-07 Joe Smulkstis #

Recommends that list members take to heart Alan Moore's remark that not everything in Watchmen means much, and notes a misquoted Zappa lyric from Daniel's post. Asks whether Wolfe's readers are more religious in general than typical science fiction readers, linking this to the poster's own Catholic upbringing.

Daniel quoted Alan Moore re "Watchmen":

"After several months of this, Moore said, in another interview, that, while everything in the comic meant something, not everything meant *much*."

I love this discussion group but I think there are times when everyone should
take those words to heart.

Daniel, I noticed the (slightly wrong) quote from Zappa's "Evelyn, a Modified Dog".
I'm a little surprised that a Catholic (which I was raised as, I'm now agnostic)
would be a Zappa fan, but as a feminist and a Zappa fan I guess I shouldn't be.

I'm curious as to whether Wolfe's audience is more religious in general than your average SF reader.

Muskrat

Responds to Joe Smulkstis's quip about a misquoted Zappa lyric and his question about whether Wolfe's readers are more religious than typical SF readers. It's Dan'l. Honest.

?? How did I blow it?? At any rate, a favorite song from a favorite album.

Have been since first hearing "Billy the Mountain" in my freshman year of collitch. And I'm a RC, a Zappa fan -- a fan of "progressive" music in general -- *and* as much of a "feminist" as a man can reasonably be. (I worry about appropriating the term, blablablah.) Not to mention an existentialist, a humanist,
a Taoist, and a skeptic. I am large (or at least overweight); I contain multitudes.

Not in my experience... I think that religious SF readers may be slightly more attracted to Wolfe, but not by a huge margin; and certainly quite a few of the listmembers here (which I take as about as paradigmatic a grouping of Lupines as you'll get) are non-religious.

2007-06-07 Daniel D Jones replying to Joe Smulkstis #

Argues that interpreting Wolfe requires knowing when to stop looking for meaning, and states the poster's own views on feminism and religion while noting an admiration for Zappa. Responds to Joe Smulkstis's remark about a Catholic upbringing and a Zappa fan, offering a joking account of his own skepticism about God and Santa Claus.
chats

I've made the same point a couple of times in the past. A big part of interpreting Wolfe is knowing when to stop.

I'm neither feminist nor misogynist (I respond to and judge people based on their actions, not their sex, and vehemently uphold equal treatment under the law, but I think radical feminist cross the border into misandry, or perhaps simply misanthropy.) I'm also a huge Zappa fan.

But certainly not religious. I put the probability of the existence of God as slightly behind the Easter bunny but slightly ahead of Santa Claus. (There's just something about visiting all those houses in ONE night that really raises my skeptical hackles. I have _serious_ doubts about Ol' Saint Nick.)
