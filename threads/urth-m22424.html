Silkhorn as Blue god

Urth list · 3 messages · 2012-12-09 to 2012-12-10

2012urthThe Book of the Long SunMarc AraminiLee Berman
The thread: Lee Berman opened by proposing that the ending of On Blue's Waters merges Horn with Silk, Babbie and blind Pig, implying possession by Whorl gods through a sacred Window, and likened this to Severian's godhood at the close of The Urth of the New Sun. Marc Aramini largely agreed, adding possibly blind Tartaros and citing Horn's call to Babbie on a river island. Berman then pressed further, arguing the narrator turns into a pig and that Phaea and Tartaros are parts of Silk-Horn, while disputing Aramini's claim that the big tree is the Vanished god. The thread ends in mid-argument with the identity question unresolved.
  • Lee Berman: Horn, Silk, Babbie and blind Pig merge at the ending via possession by Whorl gods; narrator becomes a pig; Phaea and Tartaros are parts of Silk-Horn
  • Marc Aramini: Horn is conflated with Silk, Babbie and blind Pig, possibly blind Tartaros too, with possession through a Window, paralleling Severian's divinity
  • Lee Berman: the Vanished gods link to Whorl gods like Scylla and Echidna, so the big tree is not the Vanished god

Themes: Unreliable narration, Gods, angels and the Increate, Ghosts, dreams and visions

All of these things have textual explanations, though. Windcloud got to know Silk and Horn because they both lived in his house for a time, possibly the hus Babbie. Pig carried Silver Silk in him and the eye is necessary to get the fragment out (and back to Silk's body, too?), Babbie winds up carrying most of Horn in that scene, our narrator changes from 1st person Horn with dominant control of narrative to Silk in denial with fragment of Horn left at that particular point, in IGJ the depiction of Horn's time on green is now in 3rd person and the 1st person narrative is mostly present tense and preachy preachy preachy, clearly Silk. - The Rajan is a god insofar as Silver Silk is. Pretty sure that the flashing lights at the end of RTTW indicate silver silk is returning to Silk's body, which has been animated by Horn, and will be mixed until the end of OBW, the scene you reference.

2012-12-10 Lee Berman replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that the narrator of On Blue's Waters turns into a pig, citing his response to the call for Babbie and his nuzzling into someone's lap, and that Phaea and Tartaros are parts of Silk-Horn. Disputes Marc Aramini's claim that the big tree is the Vanished god, arguing the Vanished gods are linked to Whorl gods like Scylla and Echidna, as The Mother is.
disputes · close-textual-reading · allegory-and-typology · theories: Horn turns into a pig at the end of On Blue's Waters; Phaea and Tartaros are parts of Silk-Horn; Vanished gods connect to Whorl gods like Scylla

Thanks for the detailed response Marc. I always appreciate your eye for detail. The reason I think the narrator essentially turns into a pig is that he responds to the call for "Babbie", he then trots through the forest and nuzzles his head into someone's lap for comfort. Interestingly, Babbie is weirdly humanized in an earlier passage. Seawrack is arguing that Horn doesn't recognize Babbie as a person. Horn then does acknowlege that Babbie helped row the boat on occassions, even with an acknowlegement of Babbie having hands.

I have to disagree about the big tree being the Vanished god. I do agree with you that there is an intimate connection between trees and Vanished People/Neighbors. But the Vanished gods seem to be something else, from what I see.

First, The Mother is rather explicitly acknowledged to be a goddess of the Vanished People and she is not of the human nor Neighbor race. (supporting quotes below). Second, The Mother is rather explicitly connected to Scylla (and Echidna I think, perhaps in daughter-bride fashion as we saw for Abaia). Thus I would expect other Vanished gods to have a similar connection to the Whorl gods. I see both piggish Phaea and blind Tartaros seeming to be a part of SilkHorn at the end of OBW.
