Themes: Christ figures and messiahs

1997-06-21 Alice Turner #

Argues that Severian cannot be called a Christian in the strict sense because he has never heard of Jesus Christ, though he can be accepted as a Christ avatar. Responds to Ratty's claim by defining Christianity as belief in eternal salvation through Jesus Christ and rejecting the looser sense of being a good person, while refusing to rely on Wolfe's interview.
disputes · theology-and-religion · allegory-and-typology · theories: Severian is a Christ avatar

Ratty,

I define a Christian as someone who believes that he or she will attain eternal salvation through Jesus Christ. Severian has never heard of Jesus Christ, and he himself has brought salvation (through destruction) to Urth. How does that make him a Christian (please don't cite Wolfe's interview since he isn't here to defend what he actually meant, and I think we'd agree that he is tricky)? Perhaps you are using Christian in the old-fashioned sense of being a good person, or "one of us." I can't accept that, having known many good people of other faiths (or none). I can accept Severian as a Christ avatar, however.

(This really belongs in the other group, doesn't it?)

mantis,

-alga-
