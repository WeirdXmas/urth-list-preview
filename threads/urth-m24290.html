Editor in China requesting help with translation

Urth list · 5 messages · 2013-12-17 to 2013-12-18

2013urthThe Book of the New SunGwern BranwenJerry FriedmanDavid StockhoffLee Berman
The thread: An editor in China asked for help translating a passage from The Book of the New Sun, and the thread turned to the tarot-like cards and the word 'blue' in the Thecla prison scene. Lee argued that the quoted 'she' is Thecla addressing Severian, with her apology concerning beard shadow and her ambivalence about him, and preferred the tarot sense of 'suit'. Jerry Friedman agreed about Thecla's anxiety and argued the cards need not be tarot, citing ordinary playing cards in Carmen. David Stockhoff argued that archaic political words like king signal political unreality on Urth and proposed elector or patroness, while also posting an off-topic joke. Gwern Branwen listed grep hits for king and queen from five ebooks without drawing a firm conclusion, and the thread ended without a clear resolution.

  • Jerry Friedman: Thecla anxious about offending Severian; cards need not be tarot, like Carmen's playing cards

2013-12-17 Lee Berman #

Argues that the quoted "she" is Thecla speaking to Severian in prison, so her apology concerns beard shadow rather than paleness, showing her ambivalence about him as a boy. Supports the majority reading of "blue" and prefers Gwern's tarot-card sense of "suit" over the suitor sense, since it fits both the card layering and the love-affair outcome.

IIRC, the "she" is Thecla speaking to Severian while imprisoned. I can't see why she would beg for pardon for saying he wasn't yet pale enough.

Thecla struggled between dismissing Severian as a boy toy and truly caring about him. So I am with the majority in thinking the "blue" refers to beard shadow and this is an example in which she fears she had implied he was not yet a man and hopes to make up for it.

My first thought on seeing the word "suit" in the context of a love affair is the idea of seeking a hand in marriage, as in "suitor".

Perhaps it is a bit of that, but I prefer the primary meaning as interpreted by Gwern as tarot card reading as it relates both to putting a suit upon a suit and confirming a love affair outcome through fortune telling.

2013-12-18 David Stockhoff replying to Gwern Branwen #

Argues that the archaic political words in the translator's Book of the New Sun text, such as king and prince, are not Severian's usage but signal political unreality on Urth. Responds to Gwern Branwen's grep of the ebooks by suggesting the tarot card should be elector or patroness rather than king, given the Byzantine and Holy Roman touches.
disputes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion · theories: Kings and princes are translator's usage, not Severian's; Tarot card is elector or patroness, not king

Well, he knows the words, or seems to, though AFAICT none of these usages is Severian's, though ultimately all cases are the translator's usage. One is Jonas's.

But let's take them at face value. To Severian those words must be as archaic and obscure as, say, "haruspex" is to us, or perhaps only "godking" or "imperator." There are no kings or queens in Urth and may have not been for chiliads, because of Typhon, the autarchy, and Erebus/Abaia/Ascia. I interpret their use to suggest political unreality (and in a sense generic neutrality, as opposed to, say, "Chairman") vs. political reality---same as in our fairy tales but more so. Similarly, "prince" is the son of a king, not the lord of an ancient principality.

The upshot is that I don't expect the card above "patroness" to be "king." In the tarot, as Gwern points out, there are four face cards; in the regular deck only three. Either it is elector/patroness/autarch or it is something like page?/uhlan?/patroness/elector. And given the Byzantine and Holy Roman touches, I'd go with the latter (Tarot).
