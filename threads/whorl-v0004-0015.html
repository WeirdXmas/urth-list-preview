The thread: Posters debated whether Thecla is the coo-making character linked to the Conciliator and whether that coo supports a Christ-figure reading of Severian with a Blessed Virgin parallel. Alice Turner argued the coo idea conflicts with Wolfe's careful assignment of traits, while Jim Jordan held that Severian is a Christian figure rather than a true Christ figure or the Conciliator, and that the Outsider is not the pancreating Increate. Doug Eigsti asked for the textual reference to Thecla's cooing and argued the Increate is the true Creator, with the Conciliator worshipped but not divine. Derek Bell agreed Wolfe is never careless and stressed the sequences' concern with writing, then asked for an archive link. The thread ended without agreement, with the textual evidence for the coo still unsettled.
  • Alice Turner: Thecla is the coo-maker linked to the Conciliator; the BVM-as-coo idea clashes with Wolfe's careful trait assignment
  • Jim Jordan: Severian is a Christian figure, not a Christ figure or Conciliator; the Outsider is not the pancreating Increate
  • Doug Eigsti: Asks for the textual basis of Thecla's cooing; the Increate is the true Creator and the Conciliator was worshipped, not divine
  • Derek Bell: Wolfe is never careless, and the sequences are deeply concerned with writing and unreliable narration

Themes: Christ figures and messiahs, Gods, angels and the Increate, Gnosticism, heresy and other theologies

1997-04-08 Alice Turner #

Argues that Thecla is the coo-making character linked to the Conciliator, and that the BVM-as-coo idea conflicts with Wolfe's careful assignment of traits, reinforcing a Christ-figure reading of Severian. Grounds the claim on the Conciliator's quasi-worship and Thecla's family coo trait, and responds to posters who suggested the BVM parallel.
disputes · allegory-and-typology · close-textual-reading · theories: Thecla's coo marks her as a distinct character not the BVM; Severian becomes as much a god as Christ is

But I can't resist one more reminder. Who do we KNOW was worshipped on Urth from the time of Typhoon on? Why, the Conciliator, that's who, though it's not formal worship. And who do we KNOW who coos? Thecla, that's who. And not for a minute do I think that Wolfe would create a character with a trademark coo (it's a family trait, her sister coos too), and then be so careless as to assign this coo to another character in a related book--even if, as some of you suggest, that character is the BVM.

I grant that the Sevarian is not the Increate (neither is the Outsider), but by the end of the Urth series he is certainly as much of a god as JC is thought to be on this Earth--I think some of you want parallels to be too literal . Remember how the Brown Book twists things....

-alga-

1997-04-09 James Jordan #

Argues that Severian is a Christian figure rather than a true Christ figure or the Conciliator of Urth, and that the Outsider is not the pancreating Increate. Responds to Alga's claim that the coo of Thecla and the Conciliator link them, citing the Holy Spirit's dove, the Protestant missionaries in Shadow, and Jesus being God incarnate while Severian is not.
disputes · theology-and-religion · author-and-biography · theories: Severian is a Christian figure, not the Conciliator; The coo echoes the Holy Spirit's dove; The Outsider is not the pancreating Increate

Worship has degrees, esp. in Roman Catholicism. Gene is very clear that Severian is not a Christ figure, but a Christian figure. Is Severian really the one true and original Conciliator for Urth?
Also, the Outsider worship is PRE-Typhon.

The Holy Spirit, as dove, coos. (Now, which ear heard the cooing, and does Severian's bird consistently ride the same shoulder? Hmmm.) Now, you may have a point here, though again the question comes whether we are dealing with identity or analogy.
You may think I'm pressing theology onto Wolfe, but that was really what my interview with him was designed to find out. And the result is: It is really there. Two voices, one clear, the other cooing. One is the Second Person, the Word of God, who speaks; and the other is the Spirit. Both are aspects of the Uncreated Increate Pancreating Creator who is Outside the creation.

Whoa! On what basis? It seems to me that the Outsider is indeed the pancreating Increate.

Nope. Remember the protestant missionaries from the 20th century (equivalent) in "Shadow." It was not Severian they worshipped. Also, you gotta bear in mind that for Gene, Jesus is not just thought to be God, He IS God incarnate. And that is something Severian is not. Now, if Zelazny had written these books, you'd have a point....
Given what a trickster Wolfe is, I don't want to press the parallels too literally, but at the same time, you gotta avoid going too far in the other direction either. Severian is to the Jesus of Urth what I or any other Christian is to the Jesus of Earth: an imperfect follower, following in His footsteps and doing the things He did -- but not being Him. Jesus tells us to pass out cups of cold water to the needy, and Severian does nothing more than that, though on a really big scale.

I believe Nutrias, being aquatic, feed on algae....

Nutria

1997-04-10 Doug Eigsti #

Asks Alga for the textual reference to Thecla's cooing and disputes that a mortal could be the Outsider, arguing the Increate is the true Creator and the Conciliator was worshipped but not divine. Grounds this in a Shadow of the Torturer-era citation about forebears shaped from the mud and Silk's realization that the Outsider is the God of gods.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theology-and-religion · theories: The Conciliator was worshipped but not divine; The Outsider is not the Increate

Alga,

You may have something with the cooing of Thecla. This has never registered through my readings of NEW SUN. Do you have the reference so that I can check it out?
I still see an issue with the Outsider as THE Creator. My understanding is that the Increate is THE Creator, making the Outsider the Increate:

(IV, p264.9) "...who shaped our forebears from the mud of the Short Sun Whorl."

This citation, and Silk's progressive realization that the Outsider is the "God of gods", leads me to believe that no mere mortal could take on the role of the Outsider. The Conciliator may have been worshiped but was not truly divine.

I am warming up to the idea of Severian making a cameo, especially if I can locate those "coo" references, mainly because it would be a great inside joke. A joke that I somehow missed. Does that make me an outsider?

=Talon=

1997-04-10 Derek Bell #

Agrees with a poster's claim that Wolfe is never careless and argues that the Book of the New Sun and Book of the Long Sun are deeply concerned with writing, citing unreliable narrators, Talos' play, pencase references, Severian's manuscripts and the Chrasmologic Writings. Responds to Alga's point about Thecla's coo, then asks for a link to the list archive's Volume 4.
supports · close-textual-reading

I agree entirely, alga. (I've forgotten what BVM is, though.) Wolfe is rarely, if ever, careless.

There's a lot about the nature of writing in tBotNS
and tBotLS - unreliable narrators, self-referential passages (e.g. Talos' play), several references to pencases in tBotLS (possible hints from Horn), Severian mentioning his manuscripts, the titles of the two series themselves, the Chrasmologic Writings - these contain at least extracts from Marcus Aurelius' work and the Bible...

BTW, is there a link to Volume 4 of the archive? Volume 3 stops in mid-March.

Tercel
