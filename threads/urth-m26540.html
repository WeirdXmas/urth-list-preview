Urth is a colonised planet

Urth list · 5 messages · 2017-09-26 to 2017-09-27

2017urthThe Book of the New SunAntónio Pedro MarquesGerry QuinnDavid DuffyAb de Vos
The thread: Gerry Quinn argued across two posts that Urth in Severian's time is a colonised planet, ruled by alien cacogens through local aristocracy, and that colonisation does not require settlers since overlords exploit conquered territory. He grounded this in Urth's collapsed economy, wasted human effort against undersea monsters and Ascian resistance, and local rulers obeying the aliens. Ab de Vos replied that Urth is a galactic backwater visited rather than colonised, with humanity's former glory set to be renewed toward Yesod, citing a reading attributed to Peter Wright. The thread ends as a single-sided exchange with no recorded rebuttal from Quinn, so it stops at disagreement rather than resolution.
  • Gerry Quinn: Urth is a colonised planet under alien cacogens, even without settlers
  • Ab de Vos: Urth is a backwater visited by aliens, not colonised; humanity will rise toward Yesod
  • Peter Wright (via de Vos): mankind is the chosen race

Themes: Colonization and the native, Power, rulers and politics, Gods, angels and the Increate

2017-09-26 Gerry Quinn #

Proposes that Urth in Severian's time is itself a colonised planet, ruled by alien cacogens who enforce decrees through the local aristocracy, once the seat of Typhon's conquests of many star systems. Extends the Fifth Head of Cerberus colonisation reading by noting the Commonwealth's name for these visitors, cacogens, and contrasting Urth's past conquests with its present subjection.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Urth in Severian's time is a colonised planet; Cacogens are an alien ruling class ruling through local aristocracy

This just occurred to me as I was talking about _BOTNS_ in another venue.

We often talk about _FHOC_ as a discussion of colonisation, which of course it is.

But Urth in Severian's time is surely a colonised planet.? What brought it home to me was that I referred to visiting aliens, and I subsequently remembered they were called cacogens by the poeple of the Commonwealth.? But in fact they are the alien ruling class, enacting their decrees via the local aristocracy.

Urth has a glorious history, of course - Typhon conquered many star systems.? But now it has been brought to heel.

- Gerry Quinn

2017-09-27 Gerry Quinn replying to António Pedro Marques #

Argues that Urth can be called a colonised planet even without settlers, because the alien overlords exploit conquered territory for their own advantage regardless of how they control it. Grounds this in the collapsed economy on Urth, human effort spent fighting undersea monsters and the Ascian resistance, and local rulers obeying the aliens.
proposes · myth-and-structure · theories: Urth is colonised without settlers

[damn, replied to just you instead of list - you will get this twice]

You're right, in that the very meaning of the word seems to include settlers.? But another way of looking at it is to think of the alien overlords as using the conquered territory to their perceived best advantage, regardless of the details of how they control it.

There are no farms on Urth worked by human serfs.? (Or ARE there? Would we even be told?)? But the economy is collapsed, and almost all human efforts are expended battling the undersea monsters with their unacceptable ideas.? The rest are spent battling the Ascian resistance . The local rulers do what the aliens tell them, however crazy and irrelevant.

- Gerry Quinn

Offers as grounds a reading attributed to Peter Wright that mankind is the chosen race, and replies to Gerry Quinn's colonization thread.
proposes · theology-and-religion · theories: Urth is a galactic backwater visited by aliens; Humanity is the chosen race destined to rise to Yesod
