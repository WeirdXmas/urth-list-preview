The thread: David Stockhoff asked whether Lee Berman's reading of Wolfe's St. Croix address as a trickster sign means Wolfe is a mountebank, or that evil is merely performance. Lee Berman agreed nothing is truly fake in Wolfe, tying the fake coin to a balance of good and evil, and later argued that feigned evil sustained long enough becomes real, citing Lucifer and Judas as faithful agents and Snape in Harry Potter. Jerry Friedman read the 666 address as a hint about Wolfe as an egotistical cloner who could become the Beast, and raised Wolfe's views on cloning with Chris in Pirate Freedom as a possible clone. The thread stayed among these three voices and ended without a settled answer.
  • David Stockhoff: asks whether Wolfe is a mountebank, or evil is only performance
  • Lee Berman: nothing is truly fake; feigned evil can become real, citing Judas and Snape
  • Jerry Friedman: the 666 address hints Wolfe as egotistical cloner could become the Beast

2011-01-03 Lee Berman #

Agrees with David Stockhoff that nothing is truly fake in Wolfe and adds that truth and reality contain lies and myth, tying the fake coin to a Tao or Ouroboros balance of good and evil. Argues Severian's godhood from the angels' star is transitory while his enduring godhood comes from the undine, and notes a playful link between Wolfe's address and the Satanic Performer.
supports · allegory-and-typology · myth-and-structure · theories: Severian's godhood from the angels is transitory; Severian's enduring godhood comes from the undine

I think this is a very astute assessment of a symbol. It might disturb some, though.

I agree with David but I would add the complementary balance: All truth contains lies. All reality is partially based on myth and personal bias. Tao/Ouroboros symbol. A bit of evil in all good things and a bit of good in all evil things.

Thus Severian's godhood and immortality based on the gift of a star from the angels is transitory. He loses that power as soon as it serves their purpose. His more enduring godhood comes as a gift from the "demonic" undine.

2011-01-03 Jerry Friedman replying to DAVID STOCKHOFF #

Suggests that the 666 street address reads as a hint that Wolfe, as an egotistical cloner, could become the Beast, and that the author's use of his own name implies everyone shares this potential. Responds to David Stockhoff's quoted post by also raising Wolfe's views on cloning, with Chris in Pirate Freedom as a possible clone, and mocks the claim that the word saltimbanque is too low for a clone pun.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · author-and-biography · theories: Gene Wolfe's address hints he could become the Beast; Chris in Pirate Freedom was cloned

I'm sure I've read that 666 is a hint that the Gene Wolfes, having egotistically cut themselves off from humanity by cloning, could become the Beast. The author's use of his own name suggests that he and everyone have this evil potential. And yes, maybe that some acts are evil regardless of intent.

I believe this, and I'd thought it was widely believed, but maybe I generalized from one reader to all.

If I'd been alert enough when that guy asked for interview questions, my first suggestion would have been how Wolfe feels about cloning now that it is or may soon be possible, and in light of the possibility that Chris in Pirate Freedom was cloned. (My next suggestion would have been about torture.)

Jerry Friedman is sure that "saltimbanque", meaning "clown", couldn't possibly be a clown-clone pun, because that would be beneath Wolfe. Not.

2011-01-03 Lee Berman replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that feigned evil, sustained long enough, can make a figure truly evil, and proposes that Lucifer and Judas were God's and Jesus's most faithful companions for carrying out necessary roles at personal cost. Draws the parallel to Snape as Dumbledore's most trusted ally in Rowling's Harry Potter books, reinforcing a point about feigned goodness raised earlier on the list.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · theology-and-religion · theories: Feigned evil becomes true evil; Lucifer and Judas as God's most faithful companions

See my last post. (and remember I am playing a role)

No, not overthinking. Entirely and directly to the point, I think. We on this list have focused on the principle of feigning goodness long enough that it becomes the truth. What about the reverse? If someone feigns evil for long enough they become evil.

I am suggesting that Lucifer and Judas might be considered the truest and most faithful companions of God and Jesus because they faithfully carried out their necessary roles, knowing what it would do to them. What it would make them become.

*SPOILER* (for movie fans)

Just as we learn Severus Snape was Dumbledore's most faithful, trusted ally. More so even than Harry Potter. Rowling may not be a Wolfe but she has certain mythological and story-telling principles solidly in place.
