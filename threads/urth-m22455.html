Gene Wolfe to Appear on Coode Street Podcast

Urth list · 6 messages · 2012-12-18 to 2012-12-20

The thread: Fred Kiesche announced that Jonathan Strahan and Gary K. Wolfe would host Gene Wolfe on the Coode Street Podcast and invited list questions. Jerry Friedman proposed questions on Wolfe's beliefs, including human cloning, torture in the New Sun and Pirate Freedom, and whether the pagan gods were real, while Ryan Dunn passed on a neighbor's puppetry questions about Agia and Severian. Marc Aramini suggested a question on whether America's decline would dethrone English, which was off-topic, and aaron asked whether Father Inire appears under another name. The thread consisted of submissions and announcements, with no real debate or resolution.
  • Jerry Friedman: wanted questions on Wolfe's beliefs, cloning, and torture, and expected Wolfe might decline story-specific questions
  • Ryan Dunn: relayed puzzle questions suggesting Severian's life is a puppet show with unseen strings
  • Marc Aramini: proposed a general question on English as a global language, with no textual argument
  • aaron: asked whether Father Inire from Thecla's story appears in the New Sun under another name

Themes: Robots, androids and artificial life, Justice, torture and mercy, Gnosticism, heresy and other theologies

2012-12-18 Jerry Friedman replying to Fred Kiesche #

Asks Gene Wolfe questions about his beliefs for the upcoming Coode Street Podcast, covering human cloning and the clone narrators of The Fifth Head of Cerberus and Pirate Freedom. Also raises torture in The Book of the New Sun and Pirate Freedom, including Abuse at Abu Ghraib, and Wolfe's remark that the pagan gods were real. Responds to Fred Kiesche's call for questions and suggests Wolfe may decline specific story questions.
questions · author-and-biography

Is there any reason to think Wolfe would answer specific questions about the stories, such as the ones Ryan Dunn suggested?? I think he's said he won't do that any more.? Even if he won't, we might have fun discussing what questions we most want the answers to and seeing whether there's any agreement. ?
Anyway, here are questions on his beliefs (mostly).? The introductory parts about his stories can be shortened or removed. ?
In "The Fifth Head of Cerberus", the?narrator is a clone--possibly a clone of you, some generations later--and many readers have understood his appalling character as suggesting that this unnatural method of reproduction would produce people cut off from the good side of humanity, maybe even leading to the Beast of Revelation.? In Pirate Freedom, the narrator is the product of genetic engineering, possibly a clone, and though he has a good side, he does some terrible things and calls himself a "half-human monster".? Now perhaps human cloning is possible or will soon be.? What do you think of it?? What would you tell somebody who was thinking of trying to get a clone made of himself or herself or of?a dead family member? ?
In The Book of the New Sun, the narrator is a torturer who eventually decides to end torture in his Commonwealth.? In Pirate Freedom, the narrator comments that the torture that has been in the news recently (which may be the same in his world as in ours) isn't even true torture and too much of a fuss has been made over it.? What is your opinion on the harsher interrogation techniques that?American soldiers, and intelligence operatives have used in this century on prisoners at Abu Ghraib, Guantanamo Bay, etc.?? Are they torture?? Are or were they morally justified?? Would you like to see them outlawed? ?
In an interview with James Jordan, you said, "I think that the gods of paganism were real."? Do you still believe that?? What can you tell us about these beings?? Do you believe they still exist and still interact with us?? Do they appear in your fiction outside the Soldier series? ?
Jerry Friedman
?

2012-12-18 Ryan Dunn replying to Fred Kiesche #

Passes along a neighbor's questions for Gene Wolfe's podcast appearance, asking whether Agia is a robot, a retired sex toy, or Hethor's poppet, and whether Severian's life is a puppet show with unseen strings. Relays the questions without offering an argument, drawing on Severian's marionette dreams and the traveling play about the New Sun as the grounds for the puppetry idea.
proposes · close-textual-reading · theories: Severian's life is a puppet show; Agia is a robot or poppet

These (New Sun) questions comes from an old neighbor of Gene's who now resides outside NYC...

- Is Agia a robot? A retired sex toy? Hethor's poppet?

- He dreams of marionettes. He finds himself in a traveling play about the New Sun. He is overtly and covertly being watched throughout the books. Would it be fair to surmise that Severian's life is one big puppetshow, and that, while we can't see the strings, they are going up his back throughout his story?

...ryan
