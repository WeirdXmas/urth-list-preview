2000-07-09 Dave Lebling #

Proposes that Vodalus and Jolenta are linked because both are artificial constructs propped up by others and both aspire to be what they are not, then both are discarded. Answers Charles Dye's question about their connection, offering the sin of Vanity as a unifying idea, in a brief reply.
answers · allegory-and-typology · theories: Vodalus and Jolenta are both artificial constructs discarded by others; Vodalus and Jolenta share the sin of Vanity

Both are artificial constructs (in different senses) propped up by other actors. Both aspire to be something they are not. Jolenta is discarded, Vodalus is discarded (presumably to be replaced by Agia).

The sin of Vanity?

-- Dave Lebling
(aka vizcacha)

2000-07-10 James Jordan #

Argues that Vodalus cannot save himself because only the Increate can change humanity, reading the New Sun and the Flood as divine deliverance from bondage. Responds to Dave Lebling's comparison of Vodalus and Jolenta as artificial constructs who aspire to be what they are not, offering a theological reading of Vodalus's failure in The Book of the New Sun.
disputes · theology-and-religion · theories: Only the Increate can deliver humanity from bondage; Vodalus is wrong to think he can act alone

Perhaps this also: We cannot change ourselves. The Increate must do it to us. We must be acted upon by Him, which is what the New Sun and the Flood are all about. Only He can deliver humanity from "Egyptian bondage," through His agents. We are doomed unless He acts. Vodalus thinks he can act and do it, but he's wrong.

Nutria
