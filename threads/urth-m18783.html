OT: R.A. Lafferty estate sale

Urth list · 16 messages · 2011-03-07 to 2011-03-15

2011urthMatthew KeeleyAdam ThorntonCraig BrewerGwern BranwenGerry QuinnMarc AraminiDan'l Danehy-OakesDaniel Petersen
The thread: Members of the Urth list discussed a notice that R.A. Lafferty's copyrights were up for sale, with the thread quickly turning to the price and who might buy. Marc Aramini objected that auctioning an author's legacy seemed unjust, while Gerry Quinn argued an estate sale reflects the author's own choice. Gwern Branwen judged the roughly $70,000 asking price poor value using a net present value estimate, and Dan'l Danehy-Oakes doubted Gaiman or Wolfe could afford it. Discussion drifted into jokes and suggestions about Neil Gaiman buying the estate, and it ended without any engagement with Wolfe's books.
  • Marc Aramini: buying up an author's copyrights after his death seems unjust
  • Gerry Quinn: an estate sale is the author's choice, so the sale itself is not improper
  • Gwern Branwen: $70,000 is poor financial value given likely royalties; buyers must be fans or status-seekers
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: Lafferty could reach a wider audience like Philip K. Dick, and Gaiman or Wolfe could not afford the price
  • Adam Thornton: $70,000 could fund 99-cent ebooks recouped through cheap sales
  • Daniel Petersen: Gaiman, a Lafferty fan, should buy the estate and promote it

Argues that the $70,000 high bid for Lafferty's novels at an estate sale is poor financial value, since royalties would need to average about $4,000 a year. Supports this with a rough net present value calculation over the remaining copyright term and suggests buyers are fans or status-seekers; responds to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's complaint about the price.
disputes

Yeah, that is very high an asking fee for novels that never sold very much. ($70k invested over the remaining ~70 years of the Lafferty copyrights is *a lot* of money; I can't see how the Lafferty copyrights could possibly compete. Some quick figuring with the net present value approximations says that at 70k, to be a good investment, the royalties/revenue would have to average like $4000 a year every year.)

Presumably the target audience is Lafferty fans, or those seeking status & prestige (kind of like how Encyclopedia Britannica or the New York Times stay afloat).

2011-03-07 Gerry Quinn replying to Marc Aramini #

Argues that copyright in a deceased author's work is sold only because the author sold it or left it to someone who sells it, so an estate sale is the author's choice. Responds to Marc Aramini's objection that buying up an author's rights is unjust, rejecting the idea that the sale itself is improper. Off-topic to Wolfe, concerning the R.A. Lafferty estate.
disputes

From: "Marc Aramini" <[email]>

At the end of the day, if it's for sale it's because the author sold it or left it to someone who is selling it. Copyright initially belongs entirely to the author, and it's up to him to choose what to do with it.

- Gerry Quinn
