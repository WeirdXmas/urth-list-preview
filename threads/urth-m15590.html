2010-11-30 Cliff Judge #

Asks why Wolfe gave the Autarch a cameo in An Evil Guest, pointing to a news interview in which a young man wears a golden phallus around his neck. Adds an observation that Izanami, a major Japanese goddess, married Izanagi and bore the fire god as her last child, offered without a stated link to the novel.
questions · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion

It was definitely my least favorite Gene Wolfe novel and I have read all of them except for that one really old one.

One question and one observation:

Q. When Cassie was watching the news, to get across the idea that the world of this novel was kind of fallen, the reporter interviewed a young man on the street who wore a golden phallus about his neck. Why did Wolfe choose to give the Autarch a cameo appearance?

O: Izanami was one of the two most important gods in the Japanese pantheon; she married Izanagi and they had several children. The last child was the god of fire.

2010-12-02 Ryan Dunn replying to Jerry Friedman #

Claims that Cassie in An Evil Guest reminds Wolfe of his wife Rosemary, and that Wolfe sees parts of himself in Chase and Reis, based on the poster's private conversations with Wolfe. Grounds the claim in an interview where Wolfe grew emotional about Cassie taking over the story and in the poster's own talks with him, answering Jerry Friedman's puzzlement over why Wolfe is proud of the book.
informs · author-and-biography · theories: Cassie is modeled on Rosemary Wolfe; Wolfe sees himself in Chase and Reis

As someone who was fortunate enough to have spoken with Gene about AEG on more than one occasion, I can deduce with some confidence that Cassie reminds Gene of his wife Rosemary.

Some of you may have heard the interview with Gene where he chokes up when saying how Cassie took the reins of his story and became the star. Well, he expressed the exact same emotion when talking to me about it in front of my house whilst walking Bobby around the block.

I nay also be so presumptuous in saying Gene sees turns of himself in both Chase and Reis, but that would be even more speculative.

...ryan

2010-12-02 Rebecca Bushong-Taylor replying to Ryan Dunn #

Responds to Ryan Dunn's question about why Wolfe seems proud of An Evil Guest, especially Cassie and Reis, by offering what Wolfe told her. Says Cassie resembles Rosemary Wolfe and is motivated by love, unlike Chase and Reis, and that Wolfe enjoyed writing the book, while she doubts his pride in Reis.
answers · author-and-biography

Hi Ryan -

There is certainly a tart sweetness to Cassie that is very much Rosemary Wolfe. He told me once that Cassie is motivated throughout by love (as opposed to Chase and Reis who are motivated by a number of other things). He's quite fond of the character. I'm less sure about his pride/affection for Reis.

I don't know if Gene is especially proud of AEG, but I do know that he had a great deal of fun writing it.

Hope you and your family are well -

RBT
