2002-03-06 Michael Andre-Driussi #

Asks whether Severian ever draws Terminus Est from his back and proposes that, when the sword is in front of him, he unsheathes it by pulling the sheath opposite to the blade's movement in a two-hand action. Responds to Rostrum's question about how the long sword leaves a back-slung sheath, offering the mechanics as a practical reading rather than citing a specific scene.
proposes · close-textual-reading

Does he ever actually "draw it" from that position on his back? (Offhand I don't recall a case, but I could be forgetting something.) My sense of him unsheathing it when it is in front of him is that he is pulling the sheath in the opposite direction as he moves the sword: a two-hand operation, though once it is begun the sheath can be flicked off by swinging the partially sheathed sword.

2002-03-06 Michael Straight #

Asks how Severian can quickly draw Terminus Est when it is slung across his back, reasoning that a sword as long as his arm span could not be reached and pulled out. Poses the question as a light, practical change of pace from the book's larger debates, without citing a passage.
questions · close-textual-reading

As a little break from our more involved discussions, here's a down-to-earth question that probably has a straightforward answer.

How does Severian quickly unsheath his sword when it's slung behind his back? I'm imagining it being long enough that he can't simply reach back and pull it out (his arm not being long enough to get it all the way out of the sheath). And if its length is comparable to the span of Severian's arms, I'm not sure how he gets it out at all. Am I wrongly imagining Terminus Est as much longer than it really is?

-Rostrum
