1997-09-01 Alice Turner #

Reports that Alice Turner's search for the names of animals in Wolfe's text fails to match Abenaki, Algonkian, Iroquois or Sioux languages, and lists words she has tried. Responds to Kevin McGuire's offer to look up names and to Ron Crown's point about "Nadan," and revisits her view that a large bird is an angel, given Wolfe's Catholic faith.
informs · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Wolfe's invented names derive from Abenaki or Algonkian languages

Kevin McGuire,
Thanks for your offer to look up names, but I don't think the Abenakis, a Maine Algonkian tribe, will be much use. I don't think the names are Algonkian any more. Or Iroquois. Or Sioux. Sigh. Give it a whirl, though. Moose (or elk)=Nashwonk;
deer (or antelope)=Lenizee; wolf=Wiggikku; pig (boar)=Panagiku; lion=Ketin.

(Some of those spellings are no doubt off; my house is upturned and I can't find my book--but they're good enough for a dictionary look.)

Ron Crown,
Very interesting stuff, esp about "Nadan." The reason I was opposed to the bird theory was that I saw it as an angel. Given that Wolfe is a believer and all. It seemed too large to me to be a bird---but Great Raven is more than a bird. I have not yet found one word of Athabascan, BTW, but when I go back to work tomorrow (we have a T1 line with a permanent log-in) I'll try again.

-alga-
