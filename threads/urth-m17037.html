2011-02-09 David Stockhoff replying to Lee Berman #

Proposes that the Urth/Whorl universe can be read either as a ladder of purer angelic beings rising to the Increate or as a chain of predators descending to humanity as lowest prey. Offers this as a matter of viewpoint and closes by comparing the cosmos to human cruelty, with Lee Berman's question about humanity's place in the Sun Cycle as the prompt.
proposes · theology-and-religion · myth-and-structure · theories: Whorl cosmos is a ladder of predators and prey

???
One interpretation of the Urth/Whorl universe is that a ladder stretches from us to the Increate, with an "angel" of increasing purity on every rung.

Another is that a ladder stretches downward to the lowest sentient prey/host (us), with a predator (wolf?) on every rung.

It's all in your point of view. Or how you spell "pray."

Either way, the universe is a dangerous place, where sentient beings are only as [in]humane to us as we are to one another. "Man Is Wolf To Man," as the title of the autobiography of an WW2 Eastern Front survivor has it.

2011-02-09 Lee Berman #

Disputes a reading that the inhumi parasite destroys its prey, arguing instead that inhumi do not kill hosts and that cyclical succession with resurrection sustains the ecosystem. Points to the wedding-party assault, the Neighbors, and creatures like The Mother and Great Scylla as evidence that humans are not necessarily the dominant species.
disputes · close-textual-reading · myth-and-structure · theories: Inhumi do not kill their hosts; Humans are not the top species in the Sun Cycle

Yes, I think we are in agreement on Wolfe's intentions. Especially if "creation", "God's Plan" and "Word of the Increate" are all synonymous.

I don't quite get you here. Why has the parasite destroyed its prey? Inhumi don't normally kill their host. All three species seem destined to live on in one form or another. Do you mean that Neighbors are not seen in corporeal form?

Only Urth (and its ocean-bound denizens) seem threatened with extinction by a future of ice. But Severian and his handlers have taken care of that. Cyclical succession including resurrection is a perfectly viable way to maintain an ecosystem. Happens every year in some places.

Heh, sure that's how it always is- your own race is always the winner, is always the superior one (just like on St. Anne ;- ) ). Sometimes victory is actually slavery in disguise.

Given the assault on the wedding party by an army of inhumi that will only get larger, I'm not sure "humans end up in possession of Blue" is the only way to look at it. I suppose cows can be said to be in possession of the field they graze upon. But McDonalds views the situation a bit differently.

Not completely. Horn encounters them in a certain form. And I supect we can find Neighbors in other forms. Wolfe gave them limb, body and head multiplicity for a reason. I think it is symbolic. What is the source of giant creatures like The Mother and Great Scylla who can produce multiple copies of human-like forms? You can't ignore these guys and their place in the ecosystem.

What does The Mother use Seawrack for? What do Scylla, Erebus, Abaia et al. use humans on Urth for?

I guess it can be pleasant to think of one's own race as the ultimate top of the food chain or whatever, but can we be sure that is humanity's place in the Sun Cycle? Top Dog?
