2010-01-11 David Stockhoff #

Disputes John Smith's suggestion that destriers are genetically modified horses, arguing instead that they may be alien in origin or an alien-equine clone hybrid. Points to the red eyes of destriers versus the brown eyes of horses, and notes that Wolfe says the 'destriers' are not strictly horses; the post answers John Smith's citation of the Appendix to The Shadow of the Torturer.
disputes · close-textual-reading · puzzle-solving · theories: Destriers are of alien origin; Destriers are alien-equine clone hybrids

I have to disagree with that. There is no evidence whatever to suggest that destriers are in any way related to horses. I could see that if Wolfe presents them as swift horses, but he doesn't. He merely says they are used as horses where horses cannot be used.

Horses have brown eyes. Destriers have red eyes. Why would you genetically modify horses to have red eyes?

Alien origin is a much easier explanation. However, some kind of clone hybrid (an in vitro merging of alien and equine DNA) in which red eye color is a byproduct or a persistent alien feature would be a valid subset of that theory.

2010-01-11 Jerry Friedman replying to David Stockhoff #

Argues that the appendix's phrase "not strictly correct" implies destriers are close to horses, so they may be a mutation or engineered Urth animal, with their claws as stronger evidence. Responds to David Stockhoff's claim that destriers have no connection to horses by citing the Claw of the Conciliator appendix wording and the destriers' claws.
disputes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion · theories: Destriers are a mutated or engineered horse; Destriers may be genetically engineered Urth animals

The appendix to _Claw_ says "I am certain the word [horses] is not strictly correct." Usually "not strictly correct" means "but close"--otherwise there's no need for "strictly". (People may use "not strictly correct" as a euphemism when contradicting others, but no one is being contradicted
here.)

Maybe it's a mutation. Maybe you think it looks cooler, as people have said. Destriers' having claws is much
stronger evidence, in my opinion.

Or genetic engineering of Urth animals, not necessarily
extant in our world.

Jerry Friedman
