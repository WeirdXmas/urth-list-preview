McKillip. Stoppard, Shakespeare

1999-01-20 Alice Turner #

Recommends Patricia McKillip's Song for the Basilisk as a forceful revenge drama with Vancian overtones and praises Winter Rose, while pointing to a Stoppard running gag about John Webster in the film Shakespeare in Love. Off-topic from Wolfe, it responds to earlier replies about McKillip and offers no argument about Wolfe's fiction.
chats

Thanks to those who responded re McKillip. I'm happy to report that I've done a 180 on this assignment. The book I want to recommend to this butch bunch is SONG FOR THE BASILISK, due for paperback later this year (watch for ads from Ace), and well worth your attention. It's a tough, forceful revenge drama with Vancian overtones, though Vance would never be so violent. Muy macho--don't be put off by the cover. I still haven't gotten to the Riddlemaster, so don't ask, but WINTER ROSE is extraordinary, maybe too girly for you lot.

While I'm so off topic, do not miss "Shakespeare in Love," the most intelligent (and funny) movie in years. Watch for Stoppard's inside running gag re John Webster, who will grow up to be the author of "The White Devil" and "The Duchess of Malfi," still unmatched for on-stage gruesomeness.

-alga
