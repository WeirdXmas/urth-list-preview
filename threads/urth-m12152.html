2010-12-18 Lee Berman #

Argues that Ultan's library and his talk of manvantaras echo Malrubius' description of multiple universes, so the Urth books must connect to other worlds, and that Ultan is not a throwaway character. Supports this by pointing to Ultan's Gnostic symbolism, his resemblance to the woman in the coffin, Cyriaca's uncle, and a suggestion that Cyriaca is Severian's aunt.
proposes · allegory-and-typology · source-and-allusion · theories: Ultan is linked to Malrubius' manvantaras; Cyriaca is Severian's aunt; Ultan resembles the woman in the coffin

This sounds so much like description of manvantaras by Malrubius I feel one has to perform contortions to avoid the connection.

I could accept this better if I thought Wolfe wrote BotNS primarily to create a strange, alien planet so different from our own. Instead this is a planet tightly allusionally bound to our own so there must be significant connections between the two planets. It could be that is a result of Urth being future Earth and has nothing to do with alternate manvantaras. But if that were true, why would WOlfe dwell on the multiple universe theme so strongly? What purpose to make Malrubius' story and UotNS the "big reveal" if it reveals nothing about the main story?

I do. But this is because I am not willing to default to the assumption that Ultan is just a throw-away character: show up, reference Borges, then disappear forever in the story. Weird things are going on with him. Gnostic symbolism, as has recently been discussed. The woman from the coffin is seen in his face. His hunger for the knowledge of the dead, etc.

We have the example of Cyriaca's "uncle" casually sharing the secrets of the universe with her (then Cyriaca casually sharing them with Severian and us). As, noted before, I see a thematic connection between Ultan and this book-loving "uncle" and his Library story. This connects them both to a guy who seems to be almost everywhere.

[FWIW, I strongly suspect Cyriaca to be Severian's aunt, sister of Catherine; both dark-haired, failed Pelerines. Both sentenced to strangulation for adultery. Thus the resemblance of Ultan to the woman in the coffin]

2010-12-18 Jeff Wilson replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that the Yesodi plot is a concealed secret of the Commonwealth, masked by the cacogen pretence, while the citadel's ancient lore is merely forgotten history held in Ultan's library. Answers Lee Berman's link to Malrubius's manvantaras by noting Yesodis call galaxies creations, and explains that Cyriaca's uncle fits the gothic citadel setting.
disputes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Yesodi plot is a concealed Commonwealth secret; Yesodis hide master plan from Autarchs; Citadel lore is forgotten, not secret

Even bonafide Yesodis casually use "creations" to refer to galaxies when thy are speaking to be understood by Urthlings.

Because it's a time travel story, and a religious story, and deals with the beginning and end of the world as well.

So he's Cyriaca's uncle, and fits in with the general macbre, gothic mode of the citadel.

The story of the citadel library and the ancient machine companions, et al, isn't a secret of the universe, just forgotten history. Ultan can be free with that sort of general ancient lore because it's all in the library somewhere and it's his job to facilitate access to it. The Yesodi plot is for-sure current state secret of the Commonwealth that is actively concealed by the "cacogen" masquerade, and the Yesodis keep some of it from even the Autarchs until they get a certified sun-bringer, to whom they have to eventually reveal their master plan in order to secure his participation, or we'd never learn of it at all.
