teratoid symbols

The thread: The thread asked what the teratoid symbols marking Father Inire's areas in the Long and New Sun books actually depict, and whether they are radiation hazard signs. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued that teratoid means resembling a grotesque embryo or monster, so the symbols would not look like a modern radiation sign. b sharp read the prefix as meaning monster or portent and noted radiation is only one teratogen. Macronaut proposed the symbols are Chinese characters, citing the mirror cabinet and the unprotected entry of the characters, then conceded Crossley's Korean-name point while keeping the theory. Transentient offered a personal reading of the symbol as a distorted stick figure, and the thread ended without resolution.
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: teratoid means monstrous embryo, so the symbols would not resemble a radiation sign
  • b sharp: the symbols are monster or portent signs, not radiation warnings
  • Macronaut: the symbols are Chinese or possibly evolved far-future Asian script, not radiation warnings
  • Transentient: personally imagined the symbol as a wavy distorted human stick figure

Themes: Names and their meanings, Robots, androids and artificial life, The dying sun and the cosmos

2006-07-25 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes replying to b sharp #

Explains that "teratoid" means resembling a teras, a grotesquely deformed embryo or monster, and argues that teratoid symbols would look nothing like a modern radiation hazard sign. Responds to b sharp's quoted question about radiation warning signs in the House Absolute by defining the word from medical usage and an online Webster's entry.
answers · close-textual-reading

In medicine, "teratoid" means resembling a teras, or grotesquely deformed. (A teras is a deformed embryo.)

An online version of Webster's defines "teratoid" as "resembling a monster."

In short, "teratoid symbols" would not at all resemble a contemporary radiation hazard warning sign, but would be either grossly deformed or monstrous-looking symbols.

2006-07-25 b sharp #

Argues that the teratoid symbols marking Father Inire's areas in The Book of the New Sun should be read as monster or portent signs rather than radiation hazard warnings, as an unnamed reviewer had claimed. Grounds this in the Greek root of the terato prefix meaning monster or portent, noting that radiation is only one of many teratogens.
disputes · source-and-allusion · close-textual-reading · theories: Teratoid symbols mark Father Inire's areas as monster portents

In a recent criticism of my Urth before Earth ideas, one anonymous reviewer asserted that Earth must be in the past of Urth because of

"the radiation hazard warning signs in the House Absolute, the radioactive isotopes in the mine at Saltus which have decayed to stable, non-radioactive heavy metals"

Are these Lupinology world truisms or the foibles of this one person?

Regarding radiation hazard warning signs, I have seen mention of them in the past but I didn't know it was accepted dogma as explanation for the teratoid symbols which seem to mark Father Inire's areas. Worth addressing.

I think the interpretation is a mistake, though understandable. Radiation sources are often referred to as "teratogenic" but radiation is only one of a number of teratogens. The terato prefix means malformed. And its roots are from Greek for monster (or portent).

I suspect Wolfe intended that interpretation for Father Inire.

-bsharp

2006-07-27 Macronaut replying to b sharp #

Proposes that the teratoid symbols marking Father Inire's areas in The Book of the Long Sun are Chinese characters rather than radiation warnings, linking this to the Chinese-speaking past of humanity. Grounds the reading in the mirror cabinet in the presence chamber, the stated portentous and malformed senses of teratoid, and the fact that Inire, Domnina and Jonas enter the active chamber unprotected.
disputes · close-textual-reading · source-and-allusion · theories: Teratoid symbols are Chinese characters

I had a different interpretation -- that the teratoid
symbol, which as I recall appears on the mirror
cabinet of the presence chamber, was a chinese
character. Some of the more complex, squiggly Chinese
characters can look monstrous, and this interpretation
fits with the other indications that mankind spoke
Chinese during its space/time faring heyday (Kim Lee
Soong, Jonas speaking in a "garbling sing song" etc.).
Stretching a bit, it also fits with the connotation
of teratoid as portentious and malformed: portentious
because the mirrors seem to reach across time as well
as space, and malformed because they evoke a past
where mankind's domination corrupted him. I think the
teratoid as chinese character has been suggested
before.

A radiation warning seems to me to make little sense,
given we know that Inire and Domnina stand in the
chamber while it's active, and that Jonas, who is
reasonably frail despite his cyborg body, used the
chamber to travel without any special protections
against radiation.

Macronaut

2006-07-27 Macronaut replying to James Crossley #

Concedes James Crossley's point that Kim Lee Soong may be a Korean rather than Chinese name, and argues it does not clearly undercut his theory about monstrous-looking writing. Reasons that Hangul looks simpler than Chinese characters, while far-future Asian scripts may have evolved beyond both.
supports · source-and-allusion

Actually an excellent point. I don't know how it
affects my theory: on the one hand, Hangul is a lot
simpler visually than Chinese, and can't be said to
look "monstrous" the way Chinese can. OTOH, we're
talking about an asian civilization thousands of years
forward -- who knows how their writing (if it's meant
to be theirs) has evolved?

informs · close-textual-reading

I just wanted to chime in that when I first encountered these symbols, I looked up _teratoid_ in a dictionary, and since the definition I found was simple "abnormally formed" I took a very simple interpretation. I imagined it was something like a wavy, distorted stick-figure of a human, something like a "CAUTION: SPACETIME DEFORMITIES AHEAD" sign.
