Clones of the Long Sun

Urth list · 8 messages · 2003-09-06 to 2003-09-08

2003urthThe Book of the Long SunLisa Schaffer-DoggettJames WynnDon DoggettDan'l Danehy-OakesJames Jordan
The thread: Readers debated whether Silk in The Book of the Long Sun is a clone of Typhon, a son of Typhon, or a product of Kypris, and whether a Trinitarian reading fits. James Wynn argued Wolfe merges identities rather than relying on one obvious method, and later that Silk derives from Kypris, citing Pike's rabbit sacrifice. Lisa Schaffer-Doggett argued that orthodox doctrine makes Father and Son the same, so Silk is both clone and son, while also insisting Silk remains Silk. James Jordan proposed a Trinity model of Goodpas, Silk and Kypris with a parent-son relation. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes and Don Doggett then argued over whether orthodoxy makes Father and Son identical, ending in a doctrinal dispute rather than agreement.
  • James Wynn: Silk is a merged or cloned product of Kypris or Typhon, not a son; Wolfe does not use parallels merely as spiritual symbols
  • Lisa Schaffer-Doggett: Silk is Typhon's clone and son under orthodox identity of Father and Son, but Silk remains Silk
  • James Jordan: Silk is Typhon's son; Goodpas, Silk and Kypris form a Trinity with Kypris as Love
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: Father and Son are one in substance but distinct persons, not the same
  • Don Doggett: Trinity analogy applies to Pas, Typhon and Silk sharing one base identity; God is one, not pagan gods

Themes: Doubles, twins and clones, The dying sun and the cosmos, Catholic faith and sacraments

2003-09-06 James Wynn #

Argues against the claim that Silk is a clone of Typhon, saying Wolfe merges identities in many ways and is unlikely to rely on one obvious method again. Grounds this in Silk's invitation to be scanned and merged with Pas, the Calde augur's odd backing, Silk's beard, and merging motifs across the Long Sun and Fifth Head books.
disputes · puzzle-solving · allegory-and-typology · theories: Silk is a clone of Typhon

As I see it, the chief benefit of the Typhon Clone explanation originally put forward by Don is the number of narrative puzzles it answers, and that it explains many of Wolfe's literary choices.

If you never wondered why Silk was invited to by the Mainframe operators to be scanned and merged with Pas....
or if it doesn't seem odd to you that Kypris, Echidna, and Scylla would back as Calde' an obscure augur of the poorest mantaeon in a Whorl-city that has relinquished proper worship (human sacrifice...
or if it is not striking to you that Silk has a "typhonic" beard (reddish-blonde) as Grave's (an important Wolfe source) called it in "King Jesus"...
or if any significance to Silk sharing in many of the same archetypal references as Pas and Typhon fly right by you...

...well, then recognizing that Silk is a clone of Typhon would only convolute things for you. If those bits seem peculiar or significant then Silk-as-Typhon's-clone clears up a lot of things.

The danger as I see it is that there are a lot merging and confabulating of identities in the Long Sun and it is too easy to try to resolve them all with one more clone. Wolfe has shown himself too clever to over-use a single method let alone such an obvious one. First he has bios merging with chems, and then separately he has chems merging with bios. He has a boy merging with his teacher. He has Mainframe gods merging in various ways with humanity, and according to Silk, one of them merging identies with the real God. He has an alien monster merging self-interest with the best attributes of humanity to the point that he became a saintly old man sacrificing his life to guide his people to Green so they can be food so his race can further internalize humanity. In the NS we have people merging with an alien monster by being devoured and people merging with people through eating them (once again self-interest merged with altruism as the beast gave its life to protect its food/family). In tFHoC we have clones, yes, but we also have alien "abos" becoming and replacing humans (has anyone pointed out that VRT and Number 5 share dreams as though they are twins?).

I'm not sure yet why or how Auk is Silk or Severian. But I doubt it will be only another clone. It will be some new method or a clever twist on some method already used.

-- Crush

2003-09-06 Lisa Schaffer-Doggett #

Responds to James Jordan's quoted post by restating the poster's earlier point against Crush's Trinity reading of Goodpas, Silk and Kypris in Mainframe.
disputes · theology-and-religion · theories: Silk is a clone of Typhon; Silk is a son of Typhon; Pas is the unholy spirit

Yes, but (according to orthodoxy) the Father and the Son are the same. As I pointed out, by making Silk a clone of Typhon he is both Typhon (genetically) and his son ("born" through the action of the original). And Pas is the (un)holy spirit - also the same.

Don

2003-09-06 Lisa Schaffer-Doggett #

Argues that Typhon's family were placed on the Whorl as embryos, so Silk and the other gods are secondary identities of Typhon, while insisting that Silk remains Silk rather than being reduced to a clone. Responds to James Wynn's defense of the Typhon clone explanation by agreeing it is hard to ignore and adding that Wolfe's work resists simple reductionism.
supports · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Typhon placed his family on the Whorl as embryos; Silk is Typhon but remains Silk

Yes, I think it really is hard to ignore.

I agree with this but I do believe that in addition to Silk, it makes perfect sense for Typhon to have placed his family on the Whorl in embryonic form, considering that he took the time to have the nasty buggers uploaded as gods in the first place. Whether GW has taken the narrative time to have them all in tBotLS is another matter. Regardless, that would only be their secondary identity, just as Silk is not really the Typhon that Severian meets. I think that Wolfe's work defies simple reductionism: Silk may be Typhon but he is still Silk, just as we all carry many metaphors throughout our existence. Even if there were only ten actual characters in the entire cycle (something I don't believe) and every other character was a clone or copy of these ten it would only be one level of a very rich narrative. The real onion is in it's layers.

Don

2003-09-08 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Clarifies Trinitarian doctrine, arguing that the Father and the Son are One in substance but distinct persons rather than the same, as orthodox Catholic and Orthodox teaching holds. Responds to Don D.'s claim that orthodoxy makes Father and Son identical, a point raised in a thread on clones in The Book of the Long Sun.
disputes · theology-and-religion

Well, it's a little more complex than that. They are One, but not The Same. That is, they are distinct persons who share a single substance, being, and Godhead. I'm not prepared to explain all that right now, but that's the actual
orthodox Catholic - and, I'm pretty sure, the orthodox Orthodox - position.

--Dan'l

2003-09-08 Don Doggett #

Clarifies that the Trinity analogy applies to Pas, Typhon and Silk as sharing one base identity, while God the Father, Son and Holy Spirit remain one God, not pagan gods. Responds to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's point on orthodox Catholic doctrine and raises C.S. Lewis's argument about paganism preparing the way for Christianity.
proposes · theology-and-religion · theories: Pas, Typhon and Silk share one base identity

Let me rephrase and hopefully not bumble:

They are different aspects but the Son is God, not of a lesser order and there are not two Gods, or three including the Holy Spirit. It's God as the Father, God as the Son, and God as Love, but always the big G. Not Zeus, Apollo, and Eros(first one to my mind) though I guess GW is tying all gods to the Godhead (isn't that CS Lewis' argument? Paganism paving the way?). But that's the analogy I was shooting for and the only way I can see that working in the corporeal world is through Pas, Typhon and Silk having the same base identity.

Don
