1997whorlThe Book of the Long SunDavid Wells

Themes: Catholic faith and sacraments

1997-09-10 David Wells #

Replies to an atheist's praise of Wolfe's ambiguity by arguing that Wolfe's Severian and Silk narratives do present a gospel-driven worldview, so a Christian writer communicating his faith can count as evangelism. Responds to the claim that Wolfe is not trying to convert anyone, citing Wolfe's story The Detective of Dreams as the model of a stealthy gospel invasion against tyranny.
disputes · theology-and-religion · theories: Wolfe's fiction presents a stealth gospel message

One thing I, as an atheist, like about Wolfe is that he leaves room for interpretation.
To me, Crane's explanation of Silk's encounters with the Outsider are plausible.
Just as theists have told me they dread reading science fiction by authors they
know to be atheists - in case the author decides to dive into some sub-Von-Daineken
scientific explanation of the nature of God in the final chapter - I approached
BoTLS with some apprehension in case Wolfe decided it was time he did a C.S.Lewis and beat us over the head with an unambiguous Christian message.

In fact, of course, he didn't. I like this, not just for selfish reasons but because I
think it makes for more interesting fiction.

newt
