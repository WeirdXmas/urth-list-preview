The thread: The thread, under the subject Short Sun notes: Remora, mostly asked whether the Neighbors' view that humans could cope with the inhumi is borne out, and whether Horn or Silk represents the species. Andrew Mason argued the Neighbors seem superior only because Horn meets them, and Gerry Quinn offered The Fifth Head of Cerberus as a parallel. Lee Berman disputed a reading of the ending as human failure, citing hope in unity and courage, and later suggested Quetzal's tree may be a neighbor in human form. David Stockhoff argued the Neighbors would value potential over typicality, so Silk fits better. Much of the rest drifted into astronomy: Marc Aramini and David Stockhoff traded size estimates for the Whorl, Blue, and Lune, with Stockhoff questioning the lack of a mechanism for the Whorl's move and Aramini rejecting future-moon claims without textual evidence. 2013-10-06 Andrew Mason #

Argues that the Neighbors seem superior to humans in Short Sun only because Horn meets them, and that they misjudged human ability to cope with the inhumi. Responds to Lee Berman by suggesting Quetzal may be atypical because his source, perhaps Pike, is atypical, and that the Neighbors were influenced by Quetzal's example.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Neighbors' apparent superiority reflects who readers see; Quetzal atypical because Pike is atypical

The iNeighbours whom Horn/the Rajan meets certainly seem to be higher than humans, but we should remember that they thought that humans would be better able to cope with the inhumi threat than they did, so it may be that it's just a matter of who we see. (They may have been wrong about that, of course: It's likely that they were influenced by the example of Quetzal, and it's possible that he isn't a typical inhumu because his source - perhaps Pike - isn't a typical human.)

2013-10-08 Lee Berman replying to Andrew Mason #

Argues against the view that the Short Sun ends in human failure, reading the final human-inhumi war as a sign of hope in human unity and individual courage. Responds to Brian and Andrew Mason by citing Horn's typicality among humans, the broken dream-travel cooperation, the marooned humans on Green, and Silk and Horn in the sewer.
disputes · myth-and-structure · author-and-biography · theories: Short Sun war signifies human hope and triumph

I'm hearing you guys, and considering a change of perspective. There is some thematic sense to this view. But some barriers still exist for me.

1. In their experience with humans, wouldn't the Neighbors more likely to find Horn as a typical representation of the species than Silk? And while Silk was surely kind and gentle, he clearly had the capacity for violence and destruction when he felt the need.

2. The story/series doesn't end with some sort of peace or reconciliation or understanding between human and inhumi. Quite the opposite. After the bond of cooperation established during the dream travelling, that is tossed aside and the final interaction between the races is akin to an all-out war.

I guess it could thus be argued that Wolfe ended this long story/series with a failure of the human race. But while Wolfe can be pessimistic, my intuition pushes me to think that the human war with inhumi was meant to signify hope and eventual triumph, rather than acquiescence to the inevitable.

When the peaceful Neighbors went to Green, they were twisted by their contact/entwinement with inhumi into making war on their brothers back on Blue. But humans, usually so quarrelsome among each other, band together to fight against the inhumi threat on Blue.

The band of humans marooned on Green showed similar unity and pluck in the face of overwhelming odds. And SilkHorn's experience in the sewer on Green seemed to suggest that one man with a sword and a light can be the catalyst for major changes to a corrupted environment.

I wouldn't suggest Gene Wolfe is a simplistic, American patriot, but this idea of fiercely independent individuals coming together to fight against a common enemy seems like a positive value he might espouse as "uniquely human". Especially in contrast to a pluralistic, non-individualistic species such as Neighbors (no I won't mention communism, heh).

2013-10-08 David Stockhoff replying to Lee Berman #

Argues that the Neighbors would be interested in humanity's potential rather than a typical human, so Silk rather than Horn fits their purpose, and that the Secret as a weapon means violence is not excluded. Responds to Lee Berman's question about whether Horn or Silk better represents the species, using the Secret being called a weapon and a love-as-weapon idea, plus the Fay/Sidhe pattern in Wolfe.
answers · close-textual-reading · myth-and-structure · theories: Neighbors seek humanity's potential rather than typical humans; Love is the weapon of the Secret

And yet, remember the Secret is referred to as a "weapon," which means violence is not exactly ruled out. Indeed, if "love" is a weapon in this "war," I think Silk would get that.

Also, the "typical" human must surely be of little interest to the Neighbors. It's humanity's /potential /that they see.

Once again, the Fay/Sidhe template emerges. The Neighbors can no longer interact with the world the way we can. But they need just one hero to start on the path they have set for us.

2013-10-10 Marc Aramini replying to Marc Aramini #

Argues that the Eucharist scene in The Sword of the Lictor is the inverse of the Hyacinthus myth: bread and wine become flesh and blood, while blood becomes a flower that lives on. Grounds the reading in the Catholic ceremony's sense of transformation and consumption, framing both as images of continued existence through change, and answers Marc Aramini's earlier remarks on Short Sun sizes only by way of thread context.
proposes · myth-and-structure · theology-and-religion · theories: Eucharist inverts the Hyacinthus myth

And when I mention the Eucharist scene as the opposite of Hyacinthus' story, I mean that in terms of the Catholic ceremony itself: bread and wine made from grapes transform into flesh and blood. This time it's the actual ceremony's import that is resonating: vegetative substance becoming flesh and blood, involved with consumption. The story of Apollo and Hyacinthus is the opposite, blood becomes a flower and lives on. Continued existence through transformation. Though man dies something else that is his essence lives on.
