17 megatherians

Urth list · 11 messages · 2006-05-18 to 2006-06-01

2006urthThe Book of the New SunthalassocratAdam ThorntonJosh GellerJeff WilsonRoy C. Lackeyb sharpMaru Dubshinki
The thread: Participants debated what the seventeen megatherians in Wolfe's Book of the New Sun and Long Sun might signify, and whether the number and the word itself carry hidden meaning. b sharp proposed a map onto communist states, rejecting the flood and Korean-service explanations, and Roy Lackey supplied a Lexicon Urthus line linking seventeen to the Soviet politburo. Adam Thornton offered Borges's Minotaur as a source for seventeen meaning infinity, but maru dubshinki disputed the count in the Spanish original and Thornton conceded the text has fourteen. The exchange on whether Abaia and Erebus are animals split b sharp, thalassocrat and Jeff Wilson over 'megatherian' as a loose or occult term, and it ended unresolved with the number and word readings left contested.
  • b sharp: the seventeen megatherians map onto communist states, with Abaia and Erebus as USSR and China
  • Adam Thornton: seventeen signals infinity via Borges's Minotaur, then retracted after the count was checked
  • Roy C. Lackey: Lexicon Urthus links seventeen to the Soviet politburo
  • maru dubshinki: Borges's original gives fourteen, not seventeen, and 'infinite' is a translator's rendering
  • thalassocrat: 'megatherian' just means big animal, and the Group of 17 reading is a red herring
  • Jeff Wilson: Abaia and Erebus are great beasts both occultly and literally

Themes: Power, rulers and politics, Myth and folklore sources, Names and their meanings

2006-05-18 b sharp #

Proposes that the seventeen megatherians in The Book of the New Sun are meant to map onto communist states, with Abaia and Erebus standing for the USSR and China and the rest for allied nations. Grounds the idea in the Ascians' comparison to communist societies, rejects Noah's flood and Wolfe's Korean service as explanations for the number 17, and checks it against Politburo sizes.
proposes · source-and-allusion · allegory-and-typology · theories: Megatherians represent communist nations; Abaia and Erebus stand for USSR and China

There are theories on why the number 17 might be important as a significant and/or evil number to Gene Wolfe in BotNS. Because Noah's flood happened on 'February' 17? Because Gene Wolf was in the 17th company (or some other military division) in Korea? These aren't compelling to me.

Since the Ascians, the slaves of Erebus, have often been compared to communist/socialist societies (Group of 17, Gang Of Four?), the possibility of a 17 member Politburo as inspiration is appealing. I think there is circumstantial evidence to suggest Wolfe is no fan of communism. But my research suggests the typical politburo membership was 12-15 in the USSR and 7 for the equivalent in China. Why pick 17 to be important?

Now, Abaia and Erebus are clearly the primary megatherians. To me, Abaia with his vast bulk and network of agents might invoke the USSR while Erebus with billions of slaves who all look alike and quote from a book of stock phrases might invoke China. Of course these two nations were the major communist powers in the 70's. Perhaps the other megatherians represent the other established communist nations existing in some degree of collaboration or servitude to the major two at the time of BotNS's writing.

My list of those nations would be Cuba, E. Germany, Poland, Czechoslovakia, Hungary, Romania, Bulgaria, Yugoslavia, Albania, Mongolia, N. Korea, Viet Nam, Cambodia, Laos and Burma. 15. + 2.

I know this list is an easy target for the sarcohamphi but, well...the 17 had to come from somewhere and this might be it. Without the New Sun would we have had to add the People's Republic of Baldanders? ugh.

Interesting that Wolfe chose to have the Ascians originate from what is probably the current USA.

-bsharp

2006-05-18 Adam Thornton replying to b sharp #

Proposes that the number seventeen in Wolfe's work comes from its use meaning infinity by the Minotaur in Borges's story "The House of Asterion," so that the Group of Seventeen stands for everyone. Responds to b sharp's rejection of the flood-date and Korean-regiment theories by offering this literary source instead, with no further evidence cited.
proposes · source-and-allusion · theories: Seventeen means infinity via Borges's Asterion

It's the number used to mean "infinite" by the Minotaur in the Borges story "The House of Asterion."

"Group of Seventeen" thus becomes "Everyone"--quite literally The Will of The People.

Adam

2006-05-18 Roy C. Lackey replying to b sharp #

Quotes a passage from the Lexicon Urthus stating that the number seventeen alludes to the Soviet politburo, which typically had seventeen members including alternates. Responds to b sharp's question about why Wolfe would pick seventeen by offering this reference as a possible answer.
informs · source-and-allusion

Fwiw, according to M.A.-D. in LEXICON URTHUS, "The number seventeen alludes to the Soviet politburo, which typically had seventeen members (eleven to twelve full members and six to nine alternate members)." (p-118)

-Roy

2006-05-20 Jeff Wilson replying to thalassocrat #

Argues that the megatherians Abaia and Erebus count as great beasts both in the sense of occult power and literally, since they are inhuman beings beyond human knowledge. Responds to thalassocrat's objection that calling them animals is unwarranted by pointing to the occult usage of the term and the Crowley-style self-description.
disputes · close-textual-reading · theories: Megatherians are great beasts in occult and literal senses

They are "great beasts" with occult power, as Aleister Crowley styled himself on occasion. Abaia and Erebus, being inhuman Things Man Was Not Meant to Know, also count as great beasts in a more literal sense.
