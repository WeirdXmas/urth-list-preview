The jungle scene

1999-03-04 Craig Christensen #

Argues that the airplane in the jungle scene is a pulp-adventure homage rather than a reference to a specific historical event, noting its incongruity in the glassed dome and its shift in time. Grounds this in comparisons to 1950s Tarzan films, King Solomon's Mines, and two pulp-era stories the poster later read.
proposes · genre-and-craft · source-and-allusion · theories: The jungle scene's airplane is a homage to pulp adventure

I always felt that this scene is a tribute to the genre of pulp adventure, rather than a specific reference to a historical event. The plane, I thought was a device to show that the characters have been moved both in space, as in the desert garden, and now in time, too. A prop plane is jarringly incongruous in the glassed-in dome of the Gardens and for me invoked the Tarzan movies of the 50's and King Solomon's Mines.

Later, when I read The Last Thrilling Wonder Story and The Island of Doctor Death, I was reminded of the jungle scene. All three are, at least in part, an homage to a genre.

Craig
