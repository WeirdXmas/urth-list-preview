2008-06-25 b sharp #

Argues that Valeria lives in the lower levels of a time-portal tower in the past, so Catherine is more plausibly her mother, and proposes that the corpse in the pale gown is a Catherine clone. Grounds this in the old-fashioned names and clothes, Severian seeing the corpse's face in Ultan's, and the tribade remark before the Thecla alzabo feast.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Valeria lives in Severian's past; Catherine is mother to Valeria; The corpse is a Catherine clone; Catherine was a lesbian, linked to alzabo clue

Dave Tallman, earlier I missed your post in this thread which discussed Catherine and Valeria, but I'll comment now:

I think Borski came up with some brilliant observations and analyses, but also some spectacular misses. This is an example. It seems a bit loony to contort and distort the story and time and everything else to make Valeria the mother of Catherine when a much simpler answer presents itself. I think part of Borksi's problem is a blind spot created by his guess that kaibits are a red herring and don't exist at all.

Valeria lives in a tower off the Atrium of Time and it is clearly a time portal like Master Ash's Last House. Severian can't find the tower when flying around the citadel because the top of the tower exists in the future. But Valeria and her family (conspicuously absent; all we see is her maid) live in the lower levels of the tower, thus they live in Severian's past. Catherine's name is notable described as "old fashioned" and the same is said about Valeria's clothes and could be said about her name. It is far simpler and more explanatory to think that Catherine is mother to Valeria.

But we have the corpse with dark hair, livid face and a pale gown, perhaps choked to death as Severian thinks his mother might have been (side note, Severian sees the corpse's face reflected in the face of Ultan- what can THAT mean?). Perhaps the corpse is Catherine's clone who escaped to the future at the top of the tower but was brought back to be executed. (or the reverse, perhaps the clone plays St. Katharine; does it matter?) Could Valeria be a younger clone? Surely Barbea and Gracia are clones but perhaps Severian prefers the other clone connection of Catherine and Valeria as you suggest. It could mean the Thea-Thecla connection but I think it very much fits the tone of this story for Wolfe to ask us whether it is incest if you marry a clone of your mother. Less compelling if Valeria is a normal daughter and thus just a half-sister to Severian.

Another possibly related clue: Before the Thecla alzabo feast, one of Vodalus' followers mentions hoping this one isn't a tribade (lesbian) again. I can't imagine this to be a pointless throwaway line. So what could it mean? It seems to me the last alzabo meal mentioned to us was the corpse in the pale gown. I think various speculations could be made if we learn that Catherine (and/or her clone) was a lesbian.

-bsharp

2008-06-26 Dave Tallman replying to b sharp #

Proposes that the grave-robbery corpse in The Shadow of the Torturer may be connected to Severian, suggesting Ultan could be his maternal grandfather and that the body's death was staged to draw Vodalus to the graveyard. Responds to b sharp's note that Severian sees the corpse's face reflected in Ultan's, arguing the coincidence can be reduced without time travel, using the long-term prisoners of the oubliette and Severian's resemblance to an exultant.
proposes · puzzle-solving · close-textual-reading · theories: Ultan is Severian's maternal grandfather; Grave-robbery corpse staged to draw Vodalus

That's an interesting reference -- until you pointed that out I regarded the grave-robbery corpse as a red herring. There's no reason for that body to be any relation to Sev. If it's a coincidence it's too much, especially since time-travel would seem to be involved. It could be intentional on the part of those manipulating Sev's life (the Autarch has an agent in the torturer's guild, and has contact with Vodalus), but what would be the point?

But if Ultan's face in dim light strongly reminds Sev of that graveyard scene, it has to be considered. Ultan could possibly be Sev's grandfather on his mother's side. He is a tall, "a true exultant" (Shadow VI). Sev could have inherited his photographic memory.

With a little work, we can cut the coincidence down to size. Time-travel is not strictly necessary, since there were long-term prisoners on the third level of the oubliette. The death of a prisoner with interesting court connections could be used to attract Vodalus to the graveyard that night, and the early meeting of Sev with Vodalus was a critical turning point in his life. The Katharine of the elevation ceremony could be unrelated, or she be a khabit of the dead woman.
