Attending Daedalus

Urth list · 3 messages · 2006-06-12 to 2006-06-13

2006urthThe Book of the New SunChris PTristan Davenportb sharp
The thread: b sharp asked whether others had read Peter Wright's scholarly book Attending Daedalus and proposed that Wright reads The Book of the New Sun as a political power struggle over Severian, with themes of colonization and imperialism, while admitting the academic prose was hard to follow. Tristan Davenport objected that this was fashionable everything-is-political criticism, judging the book without having read it and comparing such readings to New Testament allegorizing. Chris did not take a side, pointing b sharp to earlier list threads on the book. The thread ended without agreement, with the debate left unresolved and the book's actual argument still unverified by most participants.
  • b sharp: Wright reads the New Sun as a political power struggle manipulating Severian, invites other readings
  • Tristan Davenport: the political-allegory reading is fashionable and overreaching, and was offered without reading the book
  • Chris: no interpretation offered, points to earlier list archives on Attending Daedalus

Themes: Power, rulers and politics, Colonization and the native

2006-06-12 b sharp #

Asks whether others have read Peter Wright's scholarly book Attending Daedalus, and proposes that Wright reads The Book of the New Sun as a political power struggle in which two groups manipulate Severian, addressing colonization and imperialism. Admits the academic language makes the argument hard to follow and invites other readings.
questions · myth-and-structure · theories: Severian is manipulated by two powerful groups

Has anyone read Peter Wright's Attending Daedalus? The scholarly language makes me fear I miss the point. I think he is saying that BotNS story is really about a political power struggle between two powerful and not very nice groups manipulating Severian, the overall philosphy being to address issues like colonization and imperialism and such. Any other thoughts on this?

-bsharp

2006-06-12 Tristan Davenport replying to b sharp #

Disputes b sharp's reading of Peter Wright's Attending Daedalus as a political power-struggle allegory, calling it fashionable everything-is-political criticism without having read the book. Grounds the objection only in b sharp's description and compares such readings to ones applied to the New Testament.
disputes

I haven't read Attending Daedalus, but from your description it sounds like the kind of everything-is-political criticism that's fashionable in PoMo academia these days. I believe the same kind of thing has been said about the New Testament, among other works.
