The thread: Thread debates whether the chalcedony angel and graveyard fight in the opening chapter of The Book of the New Sun foreshadow the later new sun and Apheta. Wesley Parsons proposes deliberate foreshadowing, citing the white quartz description and the parallel with Severian's lovemaking with Apheta. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes disputes this, arguing Wolfe likely devised Apheta and the star-making method only after a publisher requested a sequel, so the link is retrospective. Stilskin agrees the resemblance is not planned, calling it a common writerly phenomenon and invoking Stephen King's 'boys in the basement'. Mo Holkar offers the coined term 'foreconning' for apparent retroactive setup, as an alternative to ex post facto foreshadowing. The thread ends without agreement, with the debate reframed around terminology.
  • Wesley Parsons: the chalcedony angel and graveyard fight deliberately foreshadow the new sun
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes: the link is retrospective, since Apheta and the star method likely came after the sequel was requested
  • stilskin: the resemblance is an unplanned writerly phenomenon, as with Stephen King's unconscious work
  • Mo Holkar: proposes the term 'foreconning' for a later work seeming to retroactively set up earlier continuity

Themes: Time loops and causality, Robots, androids and artificial life, Books, libraries and writing

2006-09-07 Wesley Parsons (7065) #

Proposes that the chalcedony angel and the graveyard fight in the opening chapter of The Book of the New Sun foreshadow the later creation of the new sun. Grounds this in the stone's description as lustrous white quartz and in the parallel with Severian's lovemaking with Apheta, a lustrous silver angel, in The Urth of the New Sun.
proposes · close-textual-reading · myth-and-structure · theories: Chalcedony angel foreshadows Apheta and the new sun

Severian watches the fight between Vodalus and the volunteers over the shoulder of a "chalcedony angel." Chalcedony is typically a lustrous white quartz. Vodalus lives and two volunteers die.

Later, in Urth of the New Sun, Sev makes love with Apheta, a lustrous silver angel, and the new sun is created. The fight in the graveyard may foreshadow the creation of the new sun.

2006-09-08 stilskin replying to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes #

Argues that the apparent foreshadowing of Apheta and the star-making method in the Long Sun books is a common writerly phenomenon rather than evidence of deliberate planning. Responds to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes by drawing on the poster's own experience as a writer and on Stephen King's idea of unconscious work by 'the boys in the basement'.
disputes · author-and-biography · genre-and-craft · theories: Wolfe invented Apheta only after writing the sequel

As a writer myself, who's discussed this point with a
lot of other writers, I can tell you that this sort of
"ex post facto foreshadowing" happens quite often.
It's a result of what Steven King calls "the boys in
the basement" going about their work and producing
what the author needs not only in the current chapter
or book, but beyond what he or she has consciously
planned for the future of the project.

The wondrous feeling, "So *that's* what I was up to!"
is one of the real delights of writing fiction.

Paul
