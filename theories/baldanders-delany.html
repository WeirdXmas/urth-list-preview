Baldanders is modelled on Samuel Delany

Baldanders is a portrait or caricature of Samuel R. Delany.

6 threads · 8 posts · for 3 / against 2 · first raised 2003-01-29 by Josh Geller
Main voices: Josh Geller (4), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (2), Michael Andre-Driussi (1), James Wynn (1)

The debate

The Baldanders-Delany idea was first raised on the list by Josh Geller on 2003-01-29, who proposed that Baldanders might represent Samuel R. Delany, citing Wolfe's earlier remark that Master Ultan stands for Borges [Josh Geller, 2003]. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes replied the same day, asking what in the character points to Delany when Borges and Frankenstein are the more obvious sources [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2003].

The idea came back in July 2003. Geller raised it again as an ironic aside, asserting the identification without new evidence [Josh Geller, 2003]. Michael Andre-Driussi then asked Blattid, the list's Delany expert, to address the claim, hoping to cool a heated exchange [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2003]. Geller answered with a parable about endless, partly imagined puzzles [Josh Geller, 2003] and then with a qabalistic letter-rearrangement, offering letter fragments as proof that Baldanders and Baldy hide the name Delany [Josh Geller, 2003]. Alice K. Turner objected that Baldy has no link to hair or to Delany, and Geller half-disclaimed the puzzle as a joke about list nitpicking [Josh Geller, 2003].

Opponents were mostly unconvinced. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued that Baldanders and Dr Talos fit a common mad-scientist-and-monster archetype, so without specific evidence the Delany link looks like coincidence [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2003]. James Wynn said he had only seen pictures of Delany and did not accept the link on that basis [James Wynn, 2003]. The threads summarised for this theory show the exchange moving from source questions into arguments about Delany's sexual content and personal conduct, and ending without resolution and without Blattid visibly answering.

For

  • Geller proposed the link in the first post, extending a view that other writers are encoded in Wolfe's characters and relying on Wolfe's remark about Ultan and Borges [Josh Geller, 2003].
  • Geller asserted in July 2003 that the identification is widely accepted, without offering new textual evidence [Josh Geller, 2003].
  • Geller offered letter fragments and an anagram-style rearrangement as hidden evidence for the Delany name in Baldanders and Baldy [Josh Geller, 2003].

Against

  • Danehy-Oakes asked what in Baldanders points to Delany, noting the Borges and Frankenstein sources [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2003].
  • Danehy-Oakes argued that the mad-scientist-and-monster archetype is common, so the Delany link needs specific evidence to rise above coincidence [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2003].
  • Wynn said he had only seen pictures of Delany, so he could not see a basis for the identification [James Wynn, 2003].
  • Turner objected that Baldy has no connection to hair or to Delany, undercutting the anagram argument [Josh Geller, 2003].

Where it stands: Raised by Josh Geller in 2003 and questioned by several posters, but never adopted by the list and left unsupported by any textual evidence, with the discussion dissolving into personal sniping.
