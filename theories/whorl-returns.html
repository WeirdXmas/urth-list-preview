The Whorl returns to Urth

The Whorl returns to Urth (or Ushas), carrying humans back to repopulate it.

12 threads · 14 posts · for 7 / against 4 · first raised 1997-10-06 by John Eric Ivancich
Main voices: Jeff Wilson (3), John Eric Ivancich (1), Jeff Snader (1), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (1), Don Doggett (1), Roy C. Lackey (1)

The debate

The theory was first raised as a question on 1997-10-06 by John Eric Ivancich, who asked whether Typhon launched the Whorl to repopulate or retake Urth [John Eric Ivancich, 1997]. It was argued more fully in 2001, when Jeff Wilson proposed that the Whorl returned to Urth via Typhon's plan, citing gravity changes and the moon's shifting distance [Jeff Wilson, 2001]. In March 2002 Wilson argued for an Increate-planned space warp [Jeff Wilson, 2002], Jeff Snader proposed the Whorl as an ark to be returned to Urth [Jeff Snader, 2002], and Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued that Tzadkiel or its masters reroute it to repopulate the world [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002].

In November 2002 the idea became part of the Blue-as-Ushas debate. Don Doggett argued that Blue is Ushas and that the Whorl had returned there, defending the claim with an FTL time-stopping effect [Don Doggett, 2002]. Roy C. Lackey disputed this, holding that Typhon's Whorl was not planned for a return and citing Apheta's testimony [Roy C. Lackey, 2002]. Later posters revived the idea in various forms: Matthew Groves argued from the book's thematic returning and its corn and hyacinth imagery [Matthew Groves, 2009], and in 2011 Sergei Soloviev proposed that a detour could explain the Whorl's apparent relativistic speed [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011].

In 2011 the debate split further. Jeff Wilson mentioned a Planet of the Apes version of the return [Jeff Wilson, 2011], while Lee Berman compared the Whorl to Noah's ship and Tzadkiel's ship as an ark repopulating a flooded Blue [Lee Berman, 2011], and Larry Miller asked the list for evidence on that ark theory [Larry Miller, 2011]. Andrew Mason argued that Typhon probably did not know about the floods and that the return was not in the original plan, citing Wolfe's remark that he only realised Typhon's role while writing Long Sun [Andrew Mason, 2011]. In 2014 Marc Aramini argued that the bird-legged figure in Return to the Whorl may be Severian, placing that book later than The Urth of the New Sun [Marc Aramini, 2014]. Many other threads, such as 'Space Vampires from the Green Planet!', 'Lune & Urth' and 'Problematic element in chronology', returned to the same questions without reaching agreement.

For

  • Wilson argued the Whorl could return to Urth through Typhon's plan, citing gravity-altering technology and the moon's changing distance [Jeff Wilson, 2001]
  • Wilson proposed the return as a planned space warp by the Increate, citing similar unknowing returns in the Sun books and other works [Jeff Wilson, 2002]
  • Snader argued Typhon built the Whorl as an ark to return humanity to Urth, grounding it in the Green Man's time travel [Jeff Snader, 2002]
  • Danehy-Oakes argued the Whorl is rerouted to repopulate Urth, a more plausible motive than Typhon's concern for posterity [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002]
  • Doggett argued Blue is Ushas, relying on The Book of the Long Sun and a claimed FTL time-stopping effect [Don Doggett, 2002]

Against

  • Lackey held that Typhon's Whorl was not planned for a return, citing Apheta's testimony about the Increate's limits [Roy C. Lackey, 2002]
  • Mason argued Typhon probably did not know about the floods, so the return was not in the original plan [Andrew Mason, 2011]
  • Mason argued the people landed from the sky come from Tzadkiel's ship rather than from the Whorl's return [Andrew Mason, 2011]
  • Aramini argued the bird-legged figure with Horn may be Severian, which would place Return to the Whorl after The Urth of the New Sun [Marc Aramini, 2014]

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never reached agreement, with the ark and return readings continuing to be argued against the view that Typhon did not plan a return.
