Patera Incus is a woman

Patera Incus is a woman disguised as a man.

9 threads · 26 posts · for 12 / against 13 · first raised 2002-08-29 by James Wynn
Main voices: James Wynn (18), Gerry Quinn (3), Lee Berman (2), Roy C. Lackey (1), António Pedro Marques (1), Stuart Hamm (1)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by James Wynn in August 2002, when he said his Incus-is-a-woman theory was better defended than his Hyacinth essay [James Wynn, 2002]. It was stated as a proposal in December 2004, alongside his claims about Chenille as a clone of Tussah and Hyacinth's gender, in reply to Emily Zilch's complaint about Wolfe's gender tropes [James Wynn, 2004]. In 2009 Wynn said he was firm on Incus being a woman, though less firm on Horn as Typhon [James Wynn, 2009].

The debate flared in August 2010 around Patera Incus. Wynn argued from the letters to Fulmar, including the underlined 'a woman' and 'myself' wording, and from Auk's failed sacrifice of Hyacinth and Aristaeus parallels [James Wynn, 2010] [James Wynn, 2010] [James Wynn, 2010]. Gerry Quinn disputed the letter reading, calling Incus's 'Bee himself' phrasing discreet and saying the sex was speculation [Gerry Quinn, 2010] [Gerry Quinn, 2010]. Roy C. Lackey argued the cipher notes did not reveal a woman and pointed to a coworker clue [Roy C. Lackey, 2010]. Wynn replied by stressing the repeated word 'hymen' and the link to the name Incus [James Wynn, 2010]. Quinn then argued the male pronoun in the character list was not significant [Gerry Quinn, 2010]. Stuart Hamm insisted the text settled the matter as female [Stuart Hamm, 2010]. Lee Berman and António Pedro Marques supported Wynn's working hypothesis, while Berman also described recognising disguised posters by verbal clues [Lee Berman, 2010] [António Pedro Marques, 2010] [Lee Berman, 2010]. Wynn at one point argued Incus is best read as a man [James Wynn, 2010].

In November 2011 Marc Aramini and Wynn linked Incus to Maytera Corn through the Aristaeus myth and cross-referencing of Long Sun names [Marc Aramini, 2011] [James Wynn, 2011]. Wynn later cited Incus's remark about Femur and the Latin cognate feminis as a hint [James Wynn, 2011]. The thread summaries describe the 2010 exchange as ending in impasse, with Lackey conceding nothing could change Wynn's mind.

For

  • Wynn argues the underlined words 'a woman' and 'myself' in the letter to Fulmar show Incus is conveying a female identity [James Wynn, 2010] [James Wynn, 2010].
  • Wynn links Incus's name to virginity and reads Auk's failed sacrifice of Hyacinth alongside Greek myth parallels such as Aristaeus [James Wynn, 2010] [James Wynn, 2010].
  • Wynn argues a female augur would be unacceptable in Vironese society and under the Charter, and cites Incus's small hands as a clue [James Wynn, 2010].
  • Wynn notes that Silk chose Incus to shrive Marble and cites Maytera Corn references as textual support [James Wynn, 2010].
  • Marc Aramini identifies Incus as female and Maytera Corn by tracing Long Sun names to the Aristaeus myth and cross-referencing the text, and Wynn follows the same method [Marc Aramini, 2011] [James Wynn, 2011].

Against

  • Quinn argues the sexual identity of Incus is a matter of speculation and refuses to rule out that Incus is a man [Gerry Quinn, 2010].
  • Quinn argues the male pronoun in the character list shows Wolfe did not clearly reveal Incus as a woman, since chems come in both sexes [Gerry Quinn, 2010].
  • Quinn argues Incus's 'Bee himself' wording is a discreet, interception-proof reference rather than a sign of gender [Gerry Quinn, 2010].
  • Lackey argues the cipher notes do not reveal a woman and that the coworker clue and unfinished second note point to a different sender [Roy C. Lackey, 2010].
  • Lackey and Hamm objected to text-only readings, and Wynn himself argued in 2010 that the letters and Soldier of the Mist make Incus best read as a man [James Wynn, 2010] [James Wynn, 2010].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the thread ended in impasse, with Wynn and a few supporters holding the theory while Quinn, Lackey and others refused to accept it.
