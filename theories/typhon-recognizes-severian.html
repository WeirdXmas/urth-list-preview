Typhon knows who Severian is

Typhon recognizes Severian as the Conciliator or the New Sun when they meet.

10 threads · 14 posts · for 6 / against 9 · first raised 1998-06-22 by Michael Straight
Main voices: Gerry Quinn (4), Marc Aramini (3), Michael Straight (2), Josh Geller (2), James Wynn (2), Jeff Veyera (1)

The debate

The theory that Typhon recognizes Severian was first raised on the list by Michael Straight in June 1998, who argued that Typhon takes Severian to be playing coy and suspected a descendant or a long-lived Conciliator [Michael Straight, 1998]. The question returned in January 2003, when Jeff Veyera relayed Teflon's view that Severian's changed appearance and coma-like emergence from hibernation could explain Typhon's failure to recognize him, while supporting Marc Aramini's point that little time had passed for Typhon [Jeff Veyera, 2003]. Marc Aramini then argued that Typhon's line about the New Sun kneeling and his linkage of the New Sun with the Conciliator show recognition [Marc Aramini, 2003]. Josh Geller disputed this, holding that Typhon understands Severian's use of 'New Sun' even though Typhon never uses the term [Josh Geller, 2003], and in January 2003 Marc Aramini and Josh Geller traded further objections on the scene's wording [Marc Aramini, 2003].

In 2003 the debate continued in threads on Sword of the Lictor. Lisa Schaffer-Doggett proposed recognition in that book and asked for pros and cons [Lisa Schaffer-Doggett, 2003]. Marc Aramini argued in August 2003 that the Conciliator and the New Sun are equated so Typhon would not want Severian to bow, and Josh Geller flatly rejected claims of non-recognition without giving further grounds [Marc Aramini, 2003][Josh Geller, 2003]. The later posts from 2010 and 2011 shift to the Long Sun books, where Gerry Quinn and James Wynn disputed whether Typhon's family or Silk would be recognized by the Sleepers and gods, citing memory tinkering and the lack of recognition in the text [Gerry Quinn, 2010][Gerry Quinn, 2011][James Wynn, 2011][James Wynn, 2011]. Gerry Quinn also suggested Typhon may not have recognized Cilinia's significance when she was scanned as Scylla [Gerry Quinn, 2011][Gerry Quinn, 2011].

The thread summaries show the Typhon-recognition question was tied up with the wider debates on Typhon's nature and the family-relation theory, which often turned into meta-arguments about critics' motives and tone. The 'typhon's knowledge' thread saw Marc Aramini argue Typhon knows more than Roy Lowe allowed, while James Wynn and Jeff Veyera backed the time-passage point [Jeff Veyera, 2003]. The quotation dispute in the 'Typhon's scene' thread ended in impasse over the ethics of paraphrase rather than the scene itself.

For

  • Typhon takes Severian to be playing coy about his identity, suggesting recognition [Michael Straight, 1998]
  • Typhon's line about the New Sun kneeling and his linkage of the New Sun with the Conciliator imply he knows who Severian is [Marc Aramini, 2003]
  • Typhon's bowing parallel with the Conciliator bowing shows he recognizes Severian as the Conciliator [Marc Aramini, 2003]
  • Little time seems to have passed for Typhon, so his failure to recognize Severian is implausible [Jeff Veyera, 2003]
  • Severian's changed appearance and a coma-like emergence from hibernation could explain Typhon's non-recognition [Jeff Veyera, 2003]

Against

  • Typhon understands the New Sun name from Severian's use of it even though Typhon never says it, so he recognizes Severian as the New Sun [Josh Geller, 2003]
  • Marc's reading depends on paraphrasing Typhon's words inside quotation marks, which Matthew Malthouse found misleading [Marc Aramini, 2003]
  • Typhon's hold over people may depend on recognising his face, but that does not settle whether he recognizes Severian [Gerry Quinn, 2010]
  • The gods' memory tinkering makes recognition of Typhon far from self-evident in the Long Sun books [James Wynn, 2011][James Wynn, 2011]
  • Typhon may not have recognized the significance of the name Scylla or Cilinia's treason when the Whorl launched [Gerry Quinn, 2011][Gerry Quinn, 2011]

Where it stands: Still disputed: raised in 1998 and revived across 2003 threads, with Josh Geller and Marc Aramini in open disagreement and no agreement reached in the list discussion.
