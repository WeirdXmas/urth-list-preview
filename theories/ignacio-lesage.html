Ignacio and Lesage

Ignacio secretly ran Lesage's pirates, or betrayed Valentin, or is Lesage.

13 threads · 22 posts · for 17 / against 7 · first raised 2007-12-02 by paul witcover
Main voices: thalassocrat (9), Ashley Crill (3), Dave Tallman (3), paul witcover (2), Dave Lebling (2), Matthew Keeley (2)

The debate

The idea that Ignacio is tied to Lesage's piracy was first raised on the list by Paul Witcover in December 2007, when he proposed that the younger Chris worked near his father in Cuba with Ignacio as a mentor [paul witcover, 2007]. Thalassocrat quickly pushed further, arguing that Ignacio financed and orchestrated Lesage's rise and that Chris is his own father via Ignacio [thalassocrat, 2007] [thalassocrat, 2007]. Matthew Keeley's January 2008 question about whether Ignacio's name hints at Ignatius Loyola was an early, unanswered side point [Matthew Keeley, 2008].

The murder and betrayal strand developed in March 2008, when Stone Ox asked whether Lesage killed Valentin with Ignacio's help [Stone Ox, 2008] and Matthew Keeley asked whether Ignacio and Lesage are one person [Matthew Keeley, 2008]. Ashley Crill then argued Ignacio sold Valentin's cave and Burt's caravan to Lesage for a share of the loot [Ashley Crill, 2008], and Stone Ox proposed Ignacio fed Lesage future information [Stone Ox, 2008]. Thalassocrat echoed the control-of-the-hijacking idea, saying Ignacio was in charge with Lesage fronting [thalassocrat, 2008]. Dave Tallman later argued that Ignacio, a time-traveling later Chris, killed Valentin and his dog to manipulate Lesage [Dave Tallman, 2008].

Opposition came mainly from Henry Eissler, who disputed that Ignacio killed Valentin or betrayed Burt [Henry Eissler, 2008], and from Paul Witcover and Marc Aramini, who argued over whether the elderly Ignacio's tears show culpability or relief [paul witcover, 2007] [Marc Aramini, 2007] [Dave Lebling, 2007]. Jerry Friedman disputed the need for the time-travel device and questioned whether the older-Chris identity was necessary [Jerry Friedman, 2009]. In 2012 Andrew Bollen again proposed Ignacio as Chris at an older age, building on Chris's gold-driven conduct [Andrew Bollen, 2012]. Several threads, including the Lesage and Ignacio thread, ended with competing guesses and admitted rereading needs.

For

  • Ignacio secretly ran or financed Lesage's operation, arguing Lesage could not have commanded the large ships alone [thalassocrat, 2007]
  • Ignacio sold Valentin's cave location and the date of Burt's caravan to Lesage in exchange for a share of the loot [Ashley Crill, 2008]
  • Ignacio was in charge of the hijacking with Lesage fronting for him, a view Thalassocrat offered as a guess [thalassocrat, 2008]
  • Ignacio, as a time traveler, killed Valentin to enlist Lesage, citing the musket, the head shot and Lesage's missing Harker [Dave Tallman, 2008]
  • Ignacio may feed Lesage future information, explaining why events repeat exactly as before [Stone Ox, 2008]

Against

  • Henry Eissler argued Ignacio did not murder Valentin or betray Captain Burt, citing Father Chris's temptation and Sabina's sketches [Henry Eissler, 2008]
  • Marc Aramini held that Ignacio is coldly culpable for his past, which Paul Witcover resisted by reading the tears as relief at returning to Novia [paul witcover, 2007] [Marc Aramini, 2007]
  • Jerry Friedman argued the older-Chris identity is predictable and perhaps unnecessary, since Chris could simply return to the Cuban monastery [Jerry Friedman, 2009]
  • Dan argued that Ignacio and Novia would never admit piracy while living in Veracruz [Dan, 2008]
  • Witcover's Hoodas reading cast Hoodas, not Ignacio, as the Judas figure in the Rio Hato betrayal [paul witcover, 2007]

Where it stands: Still disputed and unresolved: the list produced many competing Ignacio–Lesage readings across 2007–2012, with no consensus and several posters admitting they needed to reread the book.
