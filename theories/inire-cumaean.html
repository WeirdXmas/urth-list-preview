Father Inire and the Cumaean are one kind

Father Inire and the Cumaean ("Father" and "Mother") are beings of the same order, fallen angels or old gods, perhaps one entity.

28 threads · 47 posts · for 31 / against 20 · first raised 1998-07-10 by Michael Andre-Driussi
Main voices: Lee Berman (23), b sharp (13), Jeff Wilson (4), Roy C. Lackey (3), Robert Borski (2), Ryan Dunn (2)

The debate

The theory that Father Inire and the Cumaean are one kind of being was first raised on the list by Michael Andre-Driussi in 1998, though his post [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998] concerned visitors' lifespans rather than their nature. Robert Borski's early proposals, such as [Robert Borski, 1998] making them Severian's children and [Robert Borski, 2001] making Merryn their daughter, were more specific genealogical readings. The theory took shape mostly after 2006, when b sharp argued in [b sharp, 2006] that the two shapeshift rather than wear masks, and in [b sharp, 2008] that they are hierodules akin to pre-Christian gods. Lee Berman then became the main advocate of a fallen-angel or pagan-god reading, for example in [Lee Berman, 2010] and [Lee Berman, 2010], and his posts often gave a shared kind for both figures.

The opposition was led by Roy C. Lackey and Jeff Wilson, who disputed the hierodule and angel readings in [Roy C. Lackey, 2006] and [Jeff Wilson, 2010], and who argued in [Jeff Wilson, 2010] that the two are manufactured beings. Berman answered that the naming convention and the unmasked forms supported a fallen-angel reading in [Lee Berman, 2010] and [Lee Berman, 2010]. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes repeatedly demanded textual evidence, as in [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2009], where he treated the same-order claim as an assumption rather than a certainty.

The discussion moved across many threads, including "Father Inire Theory", "Appearances of Inire" and "Lake of Birds", and it rarely reached agreement. Later posts such as [Lee Berman, 2011] and [Lee Berman, 2012] pushed the identifications further, with Berman linking Inire to Abraxas and to the Old Leech. In 2018 David Stockhoff relayed Borski's Hierodule claim in [David Stockhoff, 2018], and Marc Aramini rejected it in [Marc Aramini, 2018].

For

  • Both figures show shapeshifting and bestial or serpent-like associations, which b sharp cited as evidence of a shared kind [b sharp, 2006] [b sharp, 2006].
  • Lee Berman argued that the Cumaean is shown shapeshifting and grouped with Inire, so Inire likely shares the trait [Lee Berman, 2010] [Lee Berman, 2010].
  • Berman cited Wolfe's quoted remark that pagan powers were real and the Genesis 6 parallel to support a fallen-angel reading [Lee Berman, 2010] [Lee Berman, 2010].
  • Jeff Wilson argued that Inire and the Cumaean are not widely known to be nonhuman, which fits their shared alien nature [Jeff Wilson, 2011].
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes suggested the Cumaean may be a being of power of the same order as Inire, a view he offered as an assumption [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2009].

Against

  • Roy C. Lackey argued that cacogen is only a derogatory term and that the two are Hierodules, citing an Urth passage [Roy C. Lackey, 2006].
  • Jeff Wilson argued that the two are manufactured beings that change only by aging, not by shapeshifting [Jeff Wilson, 2010].
  • Jeff Wilson described them as degraded hierodules, the worn remnants of beautiful Famulimus and Barbatus [Jeff Wilson, 2010].
  • Lackey argued that the fallen-angel account conflicts with the chronology of the black hole and the Conciliator campaign [Roy C. Lackey, 2008].
  • Marc Aramini criticized Borski's claim that the Hierodules appear as Inire and the Cumaean, saying it relies on circular reasoning [Marc Aramini, 2018].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never reached agreement on whether Inire and the Cumaean are one kind, with Berman's fallen-angel reading and Wilson's manufactured-being reading remaining opposed.
