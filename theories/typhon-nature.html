Typhon is not (only) human

Typhon was an alien, a Neighbor or a megatherian, or became one; the counter-reading is that he was fully human.

60 threads · 120 posts · for 59 / against 58 · first raised 1997-04-16 by Michael Andre-Driussi
Main voices: Larry Miller (19), David Stockhoff (18), Lee Berman (14), Gerry Quinn (13), James Wynn (9), Andrew Mason (6)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list in 1997, when David Wells asked on the Urth list where The Book of the New Sun implies that Typhon came from off Urth, saying he could not find the claim [David Wells, 1997]. It became a sustained argument in 2000 and 2001 on the Whorl list, when Robert Borski proposed that Typhon was an alien returning the Whorl to Blue-Green and that the Neighbors were his kin [Robert Borski, 2000]. Seth Lombardi, Dennis Berdanis, Alex Groce and Borski pressed Neighbor or proto-Neighbor readings, while Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, James Jordan and Borski himself disputed them, Danehy-Oakes by arguing Typhon had one head before the graft and Jordan by listing Typhon's human family and lovers [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2001] [James Jordan, 2001] [Robert Borski, 2001].

In 2002 Jeff Wilson argued that Typhon, a tyrant from another star, should still be human because his ancestors emigrated from Urth [Jeff Wilson, 2002], and Matthew Malthouse distinguished being human but not of Earth from being alien [Matthew Malthouse, 2002]. From 2008 to 2011 the megatherian version came to the fore: James Wynn suggested Typhon became a megatherian [James Wynn, 2008], Lee Berman called him a megatherian monster-god [Lee Berman, 2010], and David Stockhoff answered that megatherians need not be nonhuman [David Stockhoff, 2010]. The 2010 Short Sun discussions were the main turning point. Jane Delawney argued that Piaton's body would have rejected a nonhuman head and that the transplant examples involve only humans [Jane Delawney, 2010] [Jane Delawney, 2010], Lee Berman countered that a gut feeling against an alien Typhon was enough [Lee Berman, 2010], and Gerry Quinn largely agreed with Delawney while conceding a human from beyond Urth remained possible [Gerry Quinn, 2010].

Larry Miller revived the alien case in 2011, arguing that Typhon was an alien whose original body was a megatherian and that his people do not know he came from off-Urth [Larry Miller, 2011] [Larry Miller, 2011], while Andrew Mason and Gerry Quinn argued from naming conventions and the text that Typhon is human [Andrew Mason, 2011] [Gerry Quinn, 2012]. Marc Aramini's 2015 post treated Typhon's trajectory as moving from Satan to tyrant to father god rather than settling his origin [Marc Aramini, 2015].

For

  • Robert Borski argued Typhon was an alien who steered the Whorl home to Blue-Green, citing his remark that he was not born as he is and the doubled body parts [Robert Borski, 2000].
  • Lee Berman argued Typhon is extra-terrestrial mainly because his name is mythological rather than a saint's name, and added his statement that he was not born [Lee Berman, 2010].
  • Larry Miller argued Typhon is an alien from off-Urth, pointing to Wolfe's naming strategum and Typhon's remark that he should have left then [Larry Miller, 2011].
  • Lee Berman argued Typhon is a megatherian monster-god, citing his name, his telepathy and his resurrection of Piaton [Lee Berman, 2010].
  • David Stockhoff argued Typhon may be an outsider to Urth because he chose it as his capital and his power source is unknown [David Stockhoff, 2010].

Against

  • Jane Delawney argued that transplants stay within the human species and that an alien Typhon would have incompatible biology for receiving human organs [Jane Delawney, 2010] [Jane Delawney, 2010].
  • Jeff Wilson argued Typhon could be a human tyrant with acquired names, noting his shared anatomy with the saint-named Piaton [Jeff Wilson, 2010].
  • Gerry Quinn argued Typhon is described throughout as human, with a human face, sons, daughters and concubines [Gerry Quinn, 2012].
  • Andrew Mason argued that Commonwealth naming conventions point to a human origin, citing saint-named characters including Typhon's daughter Cilinia [Andrew Mason, 2011].
  • James Jordan argued Pas is probably human, citing Typhon's human wife, children and lover and the absence of any odd head described [James Jordan, 2001].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never settled the question, though by 2010 most posters in the Short Sun threads agreed the transplant evidence undercuts a nonhuman origin while a human from beyond Urth stayed possible.
