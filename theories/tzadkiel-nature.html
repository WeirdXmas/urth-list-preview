Tzadkiel is good or evil

Whether Tzadkiel, the Hierogrammate who judges Severian, is a benevolent angel of justice or a sinister power.

26 threads · 45 posts · for 16 / against 28 · first raised 1999-04-22 by Ori Kowarsky
Main voices: Lee Berman (13), António Pedro Marques (5), Alice Turner (4), b sharp (3), David Stockhoff (3), Ori Kowarsky (2)

The debate

The angel-or-evil question about Tzadkiel was first raised on the list on 1999-04-22, when Ori Kowarsky disputed Christopher Culver's comparison of Tzadkiel to Michael and called his angelic look a staging for Severian [Ori Kowarsky, 1999]. Alice Turner then argued in April 1999 that Tzadkiel is an angel of divine justice, citing Gustav Davidson's A Dictionary of Angels, while Jack Lyons disputed the supernatural framing [Alice Turner, 1999][Jack Lyons, 1999]. Alice Turner again argued in 1999 that Tzadkiel should be classed as an angel rather than a smerp [Alice Turner, 1999][Alice Turner, 1999].

From 2008 the debate sharpened around whether Tzadkiel is the Demiurge, a parody of God, or a fallen or false angel. thalassocrat argued he was an active promoter of deception and probably not a true angel [thalassocrat, 2008], while b sharp read the Hierogrammates as intended angels and Tzadkiel's name as an angelic signal [b sharp, 2008]. David Stockhoff proposed the Demiurge reading and Son of Witz and Jordon Flato rejected it, with Flato noting Tzadkiel never demands worship [David Stockhoff, 2008][Son of Witz, 2008][Jordon Flato, 2008]. John Watkins argued that Tzadkiel is more likely a parody or deconstruction of angels, since Wolfe depicted real angels elsewhere [John Watkins, 2008].

The 2010 Long Sun threads were the main flashpoint. John Watkins, António Pedro Marques and Jerry Friedman debated whether Tzadkiel's good intentions and the flood can be trusted or need atonement [John Watkins, 2010][António Pedro Marques, 2010][Jerry Friedman, 2010]. In July 2010 Gerry Quinn and thalassocrat argued Tzadkiel is structurally evil, while António Pedro Marques countered that the opposition to the New Sun is not admirable and Tzadkiel cannot be evil [António Pedro Marques, 2010][Gerry Quinn, 2010][thalassocrat, 2010][António Pedro Marques, 2010]. Later posters, mainly Lee Berman, proposed hybrid readings such as angel-and-demon, fallen angel, or aspect of God [Lee Berman, 2011][Lee Berman, 2013][Lee Berman, 2011].

For

  • Tzadkiel's name and its classification by Gustav Davidson's dictionary mark him as an archangel of justice [Alice Turner, 1999][Alice Turner, 2003].
  • Tzadkiel never demands worship and acknowledges his nullity before the Increate, so he is not the Demiurge [Jordon Flato, 2008].
  • Tzadkiel's demand for consent before genocide reads as Wolfe's moral endorsement of God, according to b sharp [b sharp, 2008].
  • The sailors and trial witnesses oppose the New Sun, so Tzadkiel's side looks evil in the text, argued thalassocrat and Gerry Quinn [thalassocrat, 2010][Gerry Quinn, 2010].
  • Lee Berman proposed that Tzadkiel is both angel and demon, with animal and beautiful traits marking the two kinds of being [Lee Berman, 2011].

Against

  • A fictional being cannot be a supernatural angel, argued Jack Lyons [Jack Lyons, 1999].
  • Ori Kowarsky read the angelic appearance as a staged effect and denied any link to Michael [Ori Kowarsky, 1999].
  • Tzadkiel's acts were done with good intentions, which Jerry Friedman and António Pedro Marques used to defend him from charges of evil [Jerry Friedman, 2010][António Pedro Marques, 2010].
  • An evil master would make his servants morally superior to his opponents, so Tzadkiel cannot be an evil god, argued António Pedro Marques [António Pedro Marques, 2010].
  • Son of Witz held that the Demiurge only thinks it is God, while Christ stands far above it [Son of Witz, 2008].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never settled whether Tzadkiel is an angel, a parody, an evil demiurge or a morally defensible figure, and the 2010 and 2011 threads ended with posters unconvinced.
