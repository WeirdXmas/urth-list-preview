The old Autarch (Appian)

The old Autarch whom Severian succeeds is named Appian, and his history (his absence, his bargain with Yesod) can be reconstructed.

30 threads · 49 posts · for 26 / against 22 · first raised 1998-01-27 by Michael Andre-Driussi
Main voices: David Stockhoff (7), b sharp (6), Michael Andre-Driussi (5), Roy C. Lackey (4), Rex Lycanthrosaurus (4), Lee Berman (4)

The debate

The idea that the Old Autarch is named Appian was first raised on the list by Michael Andre-Driussi, who wrote in 1998 that the name was probably Appian, citing a story in Endangered Species [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998]. Early posts mostly built on wordplay: mary whalen linked the name to the English word for bee [mary whalen, 1998], and Andre-Driussi argued that Appian derives from Latin apia, a bee, and suited a servant under the honey steward [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998]. Dave Lebling then asked about Clute's view that the old Autarch was Catherine the Weal, while noting the beekeeping and Appian clues [Dave Lebling, 1998]. In 2002 the chronology became central: Gage Kerrigan and Andre-Driussi dated Appian's reign to about 62 years before Severian, using Odilo's service and Sancha's absence [Gage Kerrigan, 2002] [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2002].

The largest discussion came in August 2006. Tony Ellis argued that the 62-year gap could not be one ruler's reign and that the boy-emperor reading had no support [Tony Ellis, 2006] [Tony Ellis, 2006]. Roy C. Lackey held that Sancha's Appian was not the Old Autarch and that the last rulers were Appian, Maruthas, the Old Autarch and Severian [Roy C. Lackey, 2006] [Roy C. Lackey, 2006]. Rex Lycanthrosaurus defended the Appian and honey steward identification, citing Wolfe's habit of leaving names unstated [Rex Lycanthrosaurus, 2006] [Rex Lycanthrosaurus, 2006], while Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued that long absences could be hidden by relativistic time [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2006]. Dan Rabin objected that Appian is named as a historical autarch while the Old Autarch is never named [Dan Rabin, 2006]. In 2011 the Lexicon Urthus was cited as identifying the two [David Stockhoff, 2011], but Dan'l Danehy-Oakes asked for actual evidence [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2011] and António Pedro Marques recalled that the list had concluded the identification was not established [António Pedro Marques, 2011]. David Stockhoff kept arguing for it in 2013 and 2018, first calling Appian a false autarch who failed the Test [David Stockhoff, 2013] and later tying him to the honey steward role [David Stockhoff, 2018].

The thread never reached agreement. Posters repeatedly returned to the same disputes over chronology, the unnamed Autarch, and whether bee and honey wordplay is enough evidence.

For

  • The name Appian is read as a pun on apian and as a Latin link to bees, which suits a honey steward [mary whalen, 1998] [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998]
  • Andre-Driussi dated Appian's reign to 66 to 62 years before Severian using Odilo's service and Sancha's absence [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2002]
  • Rex Lycanthrosaurus argued that Wolfe often leaves names unstated, so the unnamed penultimate Autarch could still be Appian [Rex Lycanthrosaurus, 2006]
  • The Lexicon Urthus was reported as identifying the Old Autarch with Appian, a reference Stockhoff cited [David Stockhoff, 2011]
  • Stockhoff argued that Appian's honey steward role and the bee myth confirm he is the Old Autarch [David Stockhoff, 2018]

Against

  • Rabin noted that Appian is named as a historical autarch while the Old Autarch is never named [Dan Rabin, 2006]
  • Ellis argued that the 62 years between Appian's reign and Severian cannot be one ruler's reign without implausible age [Tony Ellis, 2006] [Tony Ellis, 2006]
  • Lackey's timeline showed the Old Autarch was trained and kept bees about fifty years before death, leaving no room for a long absence [Roy C. Lackey, 2006]
  • Danehy-Oakes asked whether there was any actual evidence beyond the name and questioned Stockhoff's identification [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2011]
  • Marques recalled that the list had concluded the Old Autarch was not the Appian named in the text [António Pedro Marques, 2011]

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never reached consensus, the chronology and naming arguments remained unresolved, and the 2018 post supporting identification was not met with agreement.
