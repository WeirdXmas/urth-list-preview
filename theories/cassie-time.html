Cassie travels in time

Cassie Casey travels back in time (as Madame Pavlatos, or Margaret) to save Bill or fix things.

22 threads · 75 posts · for 56 / against 26 · first raised 2008-10-19 by Dave Tallman
Main voices: Dave Tallman (29), Roy C. Lackey (19), James Wynn (11), Charles Tallman (9), David Stockhoff (6), Gwern Branwen (5)

The debate

The time-travel reading first appears on the list in a December 2008 post by Dave Tallman [Dave Tallman, 2008], which says Cassie might seek out Chase to travel back and save Bill, although his earlier October 2008 post [Dave Tallman, 2008] about reality-warping and time as an illusion was the first step toward it. The idea then spread through the early 2009 threads. Tallman proposed in January 2009 that Cassie returns in time to save Bill [Dave Tallman, 2009] and that Margaret Briggs is a werewolf disguise of Cassie [Dave Tallman, 2009], while Roy C. Lackey pushed back that time travel would collapse the causal chain and that Margaret's presence proves the trip fails [Roy C. Lackey, 2009] [Roy C. Lackey, 2009].

The debate broadened in September 2009, when Charles Tallman proposed a step-by-step time-loop reconstruction in which Cassie returns to Earth, marries Bill Reis and later loops back as Margaret [Charles Tallman, 2009]. Lackey answered with timeline arithmetic and argued that Cassie's time travel failed or changed nothing [Roy C. Lackey, 2009] [Roy C. Lackey, 2009]. James Wynn offered a Woldercon-impostor alternative that rejected time travel [James Wynn, 2009], and David Stockhoff proposed a more limited trip to meet Reis [David Stockhoff, 2009]. Later, Lackey and Dave Tallman argued over whether travel runs one way or both, with ethermail replies from the future as the main evidence [Roy C. Lackey, 2009].

A turning point came in January 2010, when James Wynn cited Wolfe's Dragon*Con remark that Cassie goes to Woldercon to look for Bill Reis to argue that Pavlatos is Casey years later [James Wynn, 2010]. Gwern Branwen disputed that reading, citing Cassie's description of Pavlatos and the text's mention of photographs [Gwern Branwen, 2010] [Gwern Branwen, 2010]. In March 2010 Branwen went through the wiki's Margaret-as-time-traveller evidence point by point and found innocent explanations for each item [Gwern Branwen, 2010].

For

  • Tallman's first time-travel proposal, that Cassie returns in time to save Bill, relies on Klauser's bird-and-reflection analogy and on the Cassandra and Gray Neighbor figures [Dave Tallman, 2009].
  • Tallman argues that Margaret was the shooter who killed Chase, citing silver bullets bought by Cassie, the thin attacker who fled, and Margaret arriving fifteen minutes late [Dave Tallman, 2009].
  • Tallman relies on the hotel safe combination, the timing of Bill's visits, and the bracelet gift to argue that Margaret is a future Cassie who let her younger self enter the plot [Dave Tallman, 2009].
  • Tallman counts page-based hops and notes the sun still visible after five hops, arguing that the timeline cannot fit a single voyage [Dave Tallman, 2009].
  • James Wynn cites Sharon Bench's remark about the healed boy and Wolfe's Woldercon remark to argue that Madame Pavlatos may be Cassie from the future [James Wynn, 2009] [James Wynn, 2010].

Against

  • Lackey argues that if Cassie loops back to change her past, the causal chain collapses because Gideon's ambassadorship, the diamonds and the hopper depend on events she would alter [Roy C. Lackey, 2009].
  • Lackey argues that time-travel paradoxes persist even in fixed loops, so Margaret's presence at the end of Free Live Free shows the bank trip must fail [Roy C. Lackey, 2009].
  • Lackey argues Cassie's health, looks and timeline make it implausible that she became Rian's mother on Woldercan or that her time travel could succeed [Roy C. Lackey, 2009].
  • Lackey asks why Cassie or Margaret never used time travel to prevent Bill's death a year earlier, noting Wolfe's fixed-past rules [Roy C. Lackey, 2009].
  • James Wynn argues that a time-travel explanation fits poorly with the poisoned-bracelet attempt, since a Riese bent on murder would sit awkwardly in the timeline [James Wynn, 2009].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the time-travel reading was argued at length by Tallman, Lackey, Charles Tallman and Wynn across 2008 to 2011 and was never accepted, with most threads ending in restated positions and competing alternatives.
