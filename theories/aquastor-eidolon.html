Aquastors and eidolons are the same thing

The aquastors of the House Absolute and the eidolons of Tzadkiel's ship are the same technology.

11 threads · 13 posts · for 9 / against 2 · first raised 1997-04-11 by Michael Andre-Driussi
Main voices: Michael Andre-Driussi (2), Jeff Wilson (2), Son of Witz (2), Charles Dye (1), Alice Turner (1), Mark Millman (1)

The debate

The theory that aquastors and eidolons are the same technology was first raised on the list by Michael Andre-Driussi in 1997, who treated eidolons and aquastors as interchangeable names for phantoms [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997], while Charles Dye argued the same point from the etymology of 'aquastor' [Charles Dye, 1997]. Mark Millman developed a fuller version in 1998, arguing that aquastors are eidolon-like machine-made beings given an appointed office, citing the Tzadkiel letter's phrase 'for Urth' [Mark Millman, 1998]. The idea then lay largely dormant until 2008, when posters revived it in the context of Severian's deaths and resurrections. Son of Witz proposed Severian was an aquastor rather than an eidolon [Son of Witz, 2008] and asked where consciousness lay [Son of Witz, 2008], Roy C. Lackey argued Tzadkiel built a new aquastor body [Roy C. Lackey, 2008], and Tim O'Donnell disputed a strict separation, offering a working distinction between machine-generated physical aquastors and non-physical eidolons [O'Donnell, Tim (BOSI), 2008]. Josh Geller supported the interchangeability by comparing the terms to ghoul and ghost [Josh Geller, 2008].

Later posts applied the theory to other characters. Jeff Wilson proposed Holy Katherine was a projected eidolon or aquastor [Jeff Wilson, 2013], and Marc Aramini suggested Katherine and the eviscerated woman might be similar projections, linking the latter to Pia [Marc Aramini, 2013]. In the threads, Lee Berman argued Severian became an eidolon or aquastor aboard Tzadkiel's ship and gained his powers there, and Jeff Wilson countered that aquastors are unliving projections, so the skull from Gyoll could not be Severian's mortal remains. Berman also proposed that Jonas's spirit resembles these beings.

The record contains few explicit rebuttals of the identity claim itself; the main opposition is Tim O'Donnell's distinction, which is itself a partial disputing of the strict identity. Much of the discussion was ancillary, such as the 'pelagic' foreshadowing argument by Jeff Wilson [Jeff Wilson, 2007] and the threads that drifted into etymology and Borges.

For

  • Charles Dye argued that aquastor derives from the mist aquastors dissolve into, so the two names name one manufactured-matter technology [Charles Dye, 1997].
  • Mark Millman grounded the identity in aquastors dissolving like eidolons fade, the Tzadkiel letter's 'for Urth', and Severian's resurrection [Mark Millman, 1998].
  • Josh Geller agreed Wolfe uses the two terms interchangeably, offering a ghost and ghoul comparison as support [Josh Geller, 2008].
  • Roy C. Lackey argued Tzadkiel made the new aquastor body on the Ship, which later became the Conciliator and Apu [Roy C. Lackey, 2008].
  • Jeff Wilson argued Holy Katherine is a projected eidolon or aquastor, citing Apheta's account of Yesod and the gathering of technology into the citadel [Jeff Wilson, 2013].

Against

  • Tim O'Donnell argued the terms are not strongly distinct in Wolfe's books, but offered a working distinction between machine-generated physical aquastors and non-physical eidolons beamed into the brain [O'Donnell, Tim (BOSI), 2008].
  • Jeff Wilson countered that aquastors are unliving projections, so the skull from Gyoll cannot be the mortal remains of the original Severian.
  • Charles Dye's earlier post had kept the two terms distinct before he reversed to the identity view [Charles Dye, 1997].
  • Son of Witz placed Severian in the aquastor category rather than the eidolon one, treating the two as different [Son of Witz, 2008].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the identity was argued by several posters across 1997, 1998 and 2008, and the 2008 thread debate was left unresolved, with the strict identity view opposed by O'Donnell's distinction and not clearly settled.
