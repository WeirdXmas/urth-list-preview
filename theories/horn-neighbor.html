A Neighbor revived Horn

A Neighbor revived Horn by inhabiting his body, or Horn became a Neighbor.

55 threads · 148 posts · for 60 / against 76 · first raised 1999-10-07 by Alice Turner
Main voices: James Wynn (40), David Stockhoff (15), Gerry Quinn (15), Lee Berman (12), Jordon Flato (11), Marc Aramini (8)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by Fernando Gouvea in August 2000, who asked whether a neighbor revived Horn after he died in the pit in On Blue's Waters [Fernando Q. Gouvea, 2000]. Joe Eull later asked in 2002 whether the Neighbour's touch completes a resurrection, comparing it to Severian's rescue [Joe Eull, 2002]. Early posts on Horn's origins mostly concerned other questions, such as Neighbor-created inhumi and Horn's Neighborese, rather than revival itself [Robert Borski, 2001] [Marc Aramini, 2002].

James Wynn stated the theory explicitly in December 2004, arguing that a Neighbor dies-and-revives Horn and inhabits his body, citing the Neighbor's guilt, the handshake and shared blood, and the offer to send a spirit into Silk's dying body [James Wynn, 2004]. The theory returned in March 2009 in a burst of posts: Wynn, relaying Son of Witz, framed Horn as a clone reanimated by a Neighbor [James Wynn, 2009], and Wynn argued the greenbuck-form Neighbor became Horn [James Wynn, 2009]. David Stockhoff proposed the Neighbor imprinted Horn with a basic spark of life [David Stockhoff, 2009], and John Watkins proposed a hybrid NeighborHorn as Horn's ghost in a Neighbor body [John Watkins, 2009]. Son of Witz argued that Horn may have survived the pit and been healed over three days [Son of Witz, 2009], and Jordon Flato argued against a death-based reading [Jordon Flato, 2009].

In December 2010 Wynn argued again that a Neighbor who felt guilt repaired Horn's broken neck over three days [James Wynn, 2010], and Jane Delawney proposed that Horn's departing spirit was moved into Silk's just-dead body [Jane Delawney, 2010]. Gerry Quinn disputed this, holding that Horn died in the pit and a Neighbour now animates Silk's body [Gerry Quinn, 2010], and Roy C. Lackey argued that a Neighbour left in the pit would have had no reason to act as he did [Roy C. Lackey, 2010]. Later exchanges extended the argument to Horn as mediator and to whether his deal over Blue was legitimate [James Wynn, 2010] [Gerry Quinn, 2010] [António Pedro Marques, 2010]. In November 2011 David Stockhoff proposed the resurrected Horn was replaced by the Neighbor [David Stockhoff, 2011], António Pedro Marques listed where the case diverges from the Silk episode [António Pedro Marques, 2011], and Wynn argued the Neighbor imitates Horn out of guilt after Horn's spirit left [James Wynn, 2011]. Stockhoff later joked that Horn was not a Neighbor because he never said so to Nettle [David Stockhoff, 2011].

For

  • Horn's pit episode and the Neighbor's touch are read as completing a resurrection, compared to Severian's rescue [Joe Eull, 2002] [James Wynn, 2004].
  • The Neighbor's guilt over killing Horn, and its three-day repair of his broken neck, are cited as the motive and mechanism of revival [James Wynn, 2010] [James Wynn, 2009].
  • The Neighbor's offer in the text to send a spirit into a dying body is taken as the model for moving Horn's spirit into Silk's body [James Wynn, 2004] [Jane Delawney, 2010].
  • Babbie and Seawrack seeing Horn dead in the pit, with the chapter titled The End, is used as evidence that he died and was revived [James Wynn, 2010] [James Wynn, 2011].
  • A Neighbor sharing its soul, or imprinting basic life on Horn, is offered as the means of resurrection [David Stockhoff, 2009] [Lee Berman, 2011].

Against

  • Horn's death may be assumed by the watchers, while the Neighbors may have spent three days healing him [Son of Witz, 2009].
  • Horn never told Nettle he was a Neighbor, and the poster admits the question is unanswered [David Stockhoff, 2011].
  • A Neighbor left in the pit would have had no reason to act as he did, and a Neighbor could not legitimately represent humanity on Blue [Roy C. Lackey, 2010].
  • Horn died in the pit and it is Silk's body that a Neighbour animates, so the revival reading is disputed as misdescribing the text [Gerry Quinn, 2010].
  • Horn's Neighborliness is attributed to Passilk ancestry, and the text does not show him astral-travelling, so the Neighbor link is doubted [Robert Borski, 2001].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the theory was argued repeatedly from 2000 to 2011 and Wynn kept defending it, but Quinn, Lackey, Stockhoff, Marques and others disputed it, and the list never reached agreement.
