The exultants descend from aliens

The tall exultants descend from offworld invaders or settlers, rather than being an inbred human aristocracy.

9 threads · 9 posts · for 7 / against 2 · first raised 1997-04-21 by Michael Andre-Driussi
Main voices: Michael Andre-Driussi (4), Dave Tallman (2), J. Schultz (1), Mitchell A. Bailey (1), Roy C. Lackey (1)

The debate

The alien-origin theory was first raised on the list by Michael Andre-Driussi in April 1997, who argued that the exultants may be offworld invaders and that Typhon was an offworld ruler, citing Jonas's claim that they are new, Thecla's Swan birth sign and the height of the exultants [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997]. He followed up two days later by comparing them to Vikings or occupying forces and proposing a sacred calendar from their former home for the Swan sign, while conceding the text does not support that calendar reading [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997].

In 1998 the debate became a two-sided exchange. J. Schultz proposed that Urth is a far-future Earth and that the exultants descend from inbred aristocrats, with parallels to Athens and Theseus [J. Schultz, 1998]. Andre-Driussi replied the next day that the exultants are beings from outside the solar system who grow tall through technobloodmagic drawn from their khaibits, pointing to Severian's and Jonas's differing views of their age and the Swan constellation [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998]. Mitchell A. Bailey later used parallels between The Fifth Head of Cerberus and B/UNS to present the exultants as very tall Shadow Children, a largely playful thesis [Mitchell A. Bailey, 1999]. In 2002 Andre-Driussi argued for Typhon as an alien invader who likely brought the exultants, framing it as posthistory for Whorl [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2002].

The theory returned in 2008, when Dave Tallman argued that exultants descend from space colonist stock with genetic engineering or alien blood while armigers descend from Urth military officers, citing Thecla's height comparison and the Boy Called Frog legend [Dave Tallman, 2008]. In a follow-up he read the 'he' in Nod's line as Meschia's son and suggested exultants used alien-derived genetic engineering rather than interbreeding [Dave Tallman, 2008]. Roy C. Lackey disputed the class-height link, arguing that the tall figures in the text are exultant bastards rather than armigers [Roy C. Lackey, 2008].

The 2008 threads ('Dorcas and Fechin', 'Eschatology and Genesis', 'Sev's common lineage') carried most of the later exchange, with b sharp pressing a blood-based and flood-related reading and Jeff Wilson answering that exultant height is artificially produced via a cloned khaibit supplying growth hormone. Those threads ended without resolution, with rival lineage readings left standing.

For

  • Jonas's claim that the exultants are new, together with Thecla's Swan birth sign, was cited as pointing to an offworld origin [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997]
  • Their height was suggested as a possible adaptation to low gravity, and Typhon's remark about ships fleeing the stars was cited for an offworld ruler [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997]
  • Severian's and Jonas's differing views of exultant age and the Swan constellation in their birth zodiac were used to argue for offworld beings from outside the solar system [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998]
  • Dave Tallman argued that space colonist stock with engineering or alien blood explains exultant height, citing Thecla's height comparison in Shadow XI [Dave Tallman, 2008]
  • Typhon was proposed as an alien invader who likely brought the exultants, with the dying forests and daytime stars placed on a failing Old Sun [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2002]

Against

  • J. Schultz proposed that the exultants descend from inbred human aristocrats, with parallels to Athens and Theseus [J. Schultz, 1998]
  • Roy C. Lackey argued that armigers are not taller on average and that the tall figures in the text are exultant bastards, citing non-tall armigers such as Valeria, Lomer and Nicarete [Roy C. Lackey, 2008]
  • Andre-Driussi conceded that the text gives no support for the Swan-calendar reading of the exultants' origin [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997]

Where it stands: Still disputed: the alien-origin reading was argued repeatedly from 1997 to 2008 by a small group of posters without settling, and the lineage threads of 2008 ended in impasse.
