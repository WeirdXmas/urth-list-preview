The voices Silk hears are the gods

The voices and signs Silk receives come from Pas and Kypris (or the Outsider), not from his own mind.

13 threads · 16 posts · for 11 / against 7 · first raised 1997-04-07 by Kieran Mullen
Main voices: Marc Aramini (7), James Wynn (4), Kieran Mullen (1), Alice Turner (1), Alex David Groce (1), Michael Andre-Driussi (1)

The debate

The earliest post on the list on this idea is Kieran Mullen's 1997 reply [Kieran Mullen, 1997], which disputed Alice Turner's Silk-and-God reading and argued that the two voices reflect the Holy Spirit and a Trinity-like plurality rather than the Outsider. The gods-as-voices reading first appears in Alice Turner's 1998 post [Alice Turner, 1998], which compared Silk and Mint, guided by voices, to Iliadic heroes and tied this to Jaynes's bicameral-mind thesis. James Wynn later relayed Nastler's objection that Pas could have communicated more directly and that the Outsider sets himself apart from the Whorl's gods [James Wynn, 2005].

The theory was revived and pushed hardest from 2010 to 2014 by Marc Aramini, who proposed that the voice is the conjunction of Pas and Kypris, or Typhon and Mamelta [Marc Aramini, 2010]. He later argued that the voice is Typhon and his consort whispering to a zygote planted in Silk's brain [Marc Aramini, 2010], that the voices were preprogrammed by Pas and Kypris [Marc Aramini, 2011], and that Pas and Mamelta are prerecorded in Silk's head, tied to Crane's diagnosis [Marc Aramini, 2013]. Lee Berman offered a rival version in which the Outsider splits into pieces acting at the human level that reunite as Kypris and Pas [Lee Berman, 2010].

The main opposition came from James Wynn, who argued that Pas and Kypris, as insiders restricted to the Whorl, cannot be the Outsider, whose defining trait is being outside the Whorl, and that the identification betrays the books [James Wynn, 2010][James Wynn, 2010]. David Stockhoff questioned whether the Outsider's name fits a being inside the Whorl [David Stockhoff, 2010]. Marc Aramini's last post restated the theory with the prerecorded-messages version, and the list never settled on it [Marc Aramini, 2014].

For

  • Alice Turner argued that Silk is guided by voices in the manner of Iliadic heroes, drawing on Jaynes's thesis [Alice Turner, 1998].
  • Marc Aramini argued that the voice is the conjunction of Pas and Kypris, or Typhon and Mamelta, citing Silk's planted, grown embryo [Marc Aramini, 2010].
  • Aramini argued that the voices are preprogrammed by Pas and Kypris yet still produce a genuine enlightenment [Marc Aramini, 2011].
  • Aramini argued that the two voices are prerecorded guidance from Pas and Mamelta, linked to Crane's diagnosis of Silk's brain event [Marc Aramini, 2013].
  • Lee Berman argued that the Outsider splits into pieces that act at the human level and reunite as Kypris and Pas [Lee Berman, 2010].

Against

  • Kieran Mullen argued that the two voices reflect the Holy Spirit and a Trinity-like plurality, not the Outsider [Kieran Mullen, 1997].
  • James Wynn argued that Pas and Kypris are restricted to the Whorl and so cannot be the Outsider, whose defining trait is being outside it [James Wynn, 2010][James Wynn, 2010].
  • James Wynn argued that Pas could have communicated more directly than through the voices Silk hears [James Wynn, 2005].
  • David Stockhoff questioned whether the Outsider can be an insider god, given the irony of his name [David Stockhoff, 2010].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the gods-as-voices reading has been argued mainly by Marc Aramini over 2010 to 2014 and remains opposed by James Wynn and others, with no agreement reached.
