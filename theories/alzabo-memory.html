How the alzabo carries memories

The alzabo and the analeptic drug transfer memories by eating brain tissue, and this mechanism explains Thecla in Severian and the Autarchs' memories.

50 threads · 84 posts · for 50 / against 37 · first raised 1998-03-24 by Michael Straight
Main voices: Gerry Quinn (15), Jeff Wilson (12), Lee Berman (11), James Wynn (8), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (5), b sharp (4)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by Michael Straight in March 1998 [Michael Straight, 1998], who proposed that Severian's perfect memory explains why Thecla stays with him while the alzabo's sharing is fleeting for others, and the idea was then extended to the Autarchs' memories [Michael Straight, 1998]. Early posts broadened it: Terence Nickolette argued Thecla lives on inside Severian and that some of his memories are really hers [Terence Nickolette, 1998], and Kevin Maroney and others in 1999 discussed the alzabo's origin and name rather than its mechanism [Kevin J. Maroney, 1999]. Ouroboros The Worm defended the brain-eating memory transfer as scientifically improbable but not irrational, and David Duffy supported it with planarian experiments and science fiction antecedents [Ouroboros The Worm, 1999] [David Duffy, 1999]. Paul Duggan disputed the planarian evidence as illusory [Paul C Duggan, 2001].

In 2003 Craig Brewer asked whether Severian's near-perfect memory comes from eating the alzabo [Craig Brewer, 2003], and Don Palumbo countered that the Autarch's own words show Autarchs do not pass memories through the alzabo drug [Don Palumbo, 2003]. Around 2008 Adam Thornton described the analeptic alzabo as absorbing memories by consuming brain tissue [Adam Thornton, 2008], while Adam Thornton later argued that brain-eating was merely a competitive advantage rather than a designed transfer mechanism [Adam Thornton, 2008]. Jeff Wilson argued that the Hierogrammates and Autarchs' memories work through the same mechanism and that the remnant minds of devoured victims are records rather than survivors [Jeff Wilson, 2008] [Jeff Wilson, 2010].

The mechanism was most heavily argued in the 2011 Merger thread, where Gerry Quinn called the memory science pop-science [Gerry Quinn, 2011] and Lee Berman called the alzabo transfer diabolic rather than angelic [Lee Berman, 2011], while Gerry Quinn disputed that the distinction was meaningful [Gerry Quinn, 2011]. In 2011 the debate also split between science-fiction and magic readings of the analeptic process, with Jerry Friedman arguing it works by contagion like magic [Jerry Friedman, 2011] and Gerry Quinn arguing it is bad science [Gerry Quinn, 2011]. The thread ended without resolution.

For

  • Michael Straight argued that Severian's perfect memory explains why Thecla stays with him when others do not retain shared memories, and cited the autarchs' shared memories [Michael Straight, 1998].
  • Terence Nickolette argued Thecla lives on in Severian, citing memories such as the brothel scene that he thought were really hers [Terence Nickolette, 1998].
  • David Duffy argued brain-eating could be a plausible scientific fiction for memory transfer, citing planarian experiments and Greg Bear's Blood Music [David Duffy, 1999].
  • Jeff Wilson argued that the Hierogrammates share memories the way alzabo users do, and that Severian's inherited memories reach deep history [Jeff Wilson, 2008] [Jeff Wilson, 2010].
  • Antonin Scriabin proposed that the alzabo's devouring of a father's mind parallels Jahlee's drinking of her son's blood [Antonin Scriabin, 2013].

Against

  • Don Palumbo argued from the Autarch's own statement that Autarchs do not pass memories through the alzabo drug [Don Palumbo, 2003].
  • Paul Duggan argued that the memory-transfer effect in planarian and earthworm experiments is illusory [Paul C Duggan, 2001].
  • Adam Thornton argued that brain-eating is a competitive advantage for the alzabo rather than a designed mechanism for transferring past lives [Adam Thornton, 2008].
  • David Stockhoff argued the alzabo remains a beast with no awareness of its own, and that the planaria experiment only supports eating-based memory if memories are stored outside the brain [David Stockhoff, 2013] [David Stockhoff, 2013].
  • Gerry Quinn argued that the alzabo's chemical memory is pop-science and that the alzabo acts on predator instincts rather than merging with its victims [Gerry Quinn, 2011] [Gerry Quinn, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the mechanism was raised in 1998 and revisited through 2013, but the posters never reached agreement on whether alzabo brain-eating really transfers memories and personality, and many threads ended in impasse.
