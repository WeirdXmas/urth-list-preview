Baldanders is becoming a megatherian

Baldanders, who grows himself larger, is turning himself into something like Abaia (a sea giant), with Dr. Talos as his creation and keeper.

48 threads · 62 posts · for 45 / against 12 · first raised 1997-11-20 by Michael Straight
Main voices: Lee Berman (11), Chris P (7), b sharp (6), Gerry Quinn (6), Jeff Wilson (4), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (4)

The debate

The earliest post on the list is Michael Straight's 1997 reply [Michael Straight, 1997] about how Talos's plays end, which does not mention megatherians. The megatherian label first appears on the list in 2006, when Jeff Wilson disputed a shapeshifting claim and argued from the Greek and Latin senses of the terms and from Crowley's 'great beast' that the Lives of the Seventeen Megatherians collects black magicians such as Baldanders [Jeff Wilson, 2006].

In May 2006 the card's question was argued from several directions. David DiGiacomo proposed that Baldanders is a human who modified himself to extend his life through unending growth [David DiGiacomo, 2006]. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued he is a human who made himself a monster while following the Megatherian pattern, not a Megatherian himself [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2006]. pthwndxrclzp disputed the Megatherian reading and held that Baldanders is an alien infant, a baby Abaia, with no sign he was ever human [pthwndxrclzp, 2006]. Malcolm McGugan floated that Baldanders is Abaia himself [Malcolm McGugan, 2006].

Lee Berman took up the megatherian reading in 2010 and carried it through several posts. He first proposed that Baldanders is an aspiring megatherian, linking Juturna, Typhon and Jonas's black beans [Lee Berman, 2010], and then that he is a proto-megatherian of uncertain origin [Lee Berman, 2010]. When Dan'l Danehy-Oakes pointed out the Borges source of the name, Berman conceded that point but still called the megatherian status an open question [Lee Berman, 2010]. Dan'l also argued that Typhon and Baldanders bear monster names because they left humanity, offered as a personal reading [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2010]. In 2012 and 2013 Berman proposed that Baldanders transitions toward megatherian godhood and a path to godhood via growth, while admitting that the relationships were not fully resolved [Lee Berman, 2012][Lee Berman, 2013].

For

  • Jeff Wilson linked Baldanders to the megatherians through the meanings of the terms and suggested the Lives of the Seventeen Megatherians collects biographies of black magicians including Baldanders [Jeff Wilson, 2006].
  • Lee Berman proposed that Baldanders is an aspiring megatherian, tying him to Juturna, Typhon and Jonas's black beans [Lee Berman, 2010].
  • Berman later called Baldanders a proto-megatherian of uncertain origin, with only Typhon's Piaton part human [Lee Berman, 2010].
  • Berman argued that great size marks godhood in Wolfe and so ties Baldanders to the megatherians and godlings [Lee Berman, 2012].
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued Baldanders is a self-made monster following the Megatherian pattern for immortality [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2006].

Against

  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes denied that Baldanders is himself a Megatherian and described him as a human who imitated them [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2006].
  • pthwndxrclzp argued that Baldanders is an alien infant, a baby Abaia, and that no text indicates he was originally human [pthwndxrclzp, 2006].
  • Berman conceded the Borges source of Baldanders' name and called megatherian status an open question rather than a settled reading [Lee Berman, 2010].
  • Chris P argued that the cacogens' remark about growth extending life is ambiguous about whether it refers to Baldanders or Severian [Chris P, 2006].

Where it stands: Still disputed and largely a single advocate's project: Lee Berman revived and extended the megatherian reading from 2010 to 2013, while other posters offered rival identities and none reached consensus.
