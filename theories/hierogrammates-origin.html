The Hierogrammates were made by humans of an earlier universe

The Hierogrammates were shaped from lesser creatures by the Hieros, humans of a previous universe, and repay the debt by shaping humanity.

58 threads · 90 posts · for 37 / against 50 · first raised 1998-02-17 by Michael Andre-Driussi
Main voices: Lee Berman (12), Jeff Wilson (10), James Wynn (7), Roy C. Lackey (6), b sharp (5), John Watkins (5)

The debate

The earliest post on the list that argues the earlier-universe origin is Roy C. Lackey's 2005 post, which holds that the text does not settle whether the Hierogrammates and Hieros came from a previous universe, citing the cycle of expanding and collapsing universes and Severian's admission that he was not told what became of the previous cycle's humanity [Roy C. Lackey, 2005]. Lackey's companion post the same week framed the Hierogrammates' shaping of humanity as repayment for an old wrong [Roy C. Lackey, 2005]. Chris P disputed this framing, calling the creation a shaping that elevated each universe's humans in turn rather than a sin [Chris P, 2005], and Adam Stephanides argued the Increate never judged the shaping to be sinful [Adam Stephanides, 2005]. Earlier, in 1998 and 1999, Michael Andre-Driussi and Jonathan Laidlow had described the Hieros as sub-creations or an evolved cognate of humanity, without placing them in an earlier universe [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998] [Jonathan Laidlow, 1999].

From 2008 the previous-cycle reading became the most common version. Jeff Wilson proposed that the Hieros are humans from a previous universe cycle, grounding this in the absence of reverse speech and the need to keep Noah's God truthful [Jeff Wilson, 2008], and Dave Tallman said the Hieros were human congates of the last cycle who created the Hierogrammates from another race [Dave Tallman, 2008]. In 2010 Lee Berman argued that the Hierogrammates were created in Briah by an earlier humanity and then escaped to Yesod [Lee Berman, 2010], and Ryan Dunn tied their makers to a prior universe that imploded [Ryan Dunn, 2010] [Ryan Dunn, 2010]. Wilson later called the previous Big Bang/Big Crunch cycle the majority view while disputing an Ushas origin [Jeff Wilson, 2010].

The main opposition came from readers who rejected a human origin or the earlier-universe link. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued that future-humanity origins fail because Wolfe says Briah is a previous iteration of our universe [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2010], and David Stockhoff called the matter unsettled and the homologue reading one unaddressed possibility [David Stockhoff, 2010] [David Stockhoff, 2010]. António Pedro Marques argued the Hierogrammates were homologues of Severian's race rather than humans [António Pedro Marques, 2010], while James Wynn argued they could not have escaped from Briah into Yesod, which he took to be a meta-universe [James Wynn, 2010]. The 2010 threads on Briah and Yesod and on the Pelerine ended without agreement, with Wilson and Berman still opposed on the vanished creation [António Pedro Marques, 2010] [Lee Berman, 2010].

For

  • The Hierogrammates were made by humans of a previous universe cycle, which Lackey grounded in the cycle of expanding and collapsing universes and Severian's admission about the previous humanity [Roy C. Lackey, 2005].
  • Wilson argued the Hieros are humans from a previous cycle, citing the absence of reverse speech and the need to keep Noah's God truthful [Jeff Wilson, 2008].
  • Tallman said the Hieros were human congates of the last cycle who created the Hierogrammates from another race [Dave Tallman, 2008].
  • Berman argued the Hierogrammates were created in Briah by an earlier humanity and escaped to Yesod, so Briah precedes Yesod [Lee Berman, 2010].
  • Dunn argued the makers of the Hierogrammates were Urth's humans in a prior universe that imploded, citing the description of a divine year [Ryan Dunn, 2010].

Against

  • Chris P argued the Hierogrammates' creation was a shaping that raised each universe's humans in turn, not a sinful making by earlier humans [Chris P, 2005].
  • Stephanides argued the text does not settle Severian's success and that Ushas's humanity, not an earlier one, produces the next Hierogrammates [Adam Stephanides, 2005].
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued that the future-humanity alternative fails because Wolfe states Briah is a previous iteration of our universe, which requires iterative universes [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2010].
  • Stockhoff argued that narrative withholds information, so the Hierogrammates should not be assumed to be homologues of humans in Briah [David Stockhoff, 2010].
  • James Wynn argued the Hierogrammates could not have escaped from Briah into Yesod, so Yesod must be a meta-universe [James Wynn, 2010].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the earlier-universe origin became the most common reading from 2008 onward, but posters offered rival origins (future humanity, Ushas humans, homologues, non-human makers) and no consensus emerged.
