Pig is a godling

The blind giant Pig is a godling (one of the giant creatures of the Whorl).

19 threads · 20 posts · for 13 / against 4 · first raised 2001-02-03 by michael braithwaite
Main voices: Robert Borski (4), Dave Lebling (3), james.kellar (2), Patrick O'Leary (2), Lee Berman (2), michael braithwaite (1)

The debate

The idea that Pig is a godling first appears on the list in February 2001, when michael braithwaite linked godlings to embryos like Silk, Mucor and Pig [michael braithwaite, 2001], and mattjaq asked about Pig's godling status in the same week [mattjaq, 2001]. James Kellar floated it while arguing for a Scottish dialect [james.kellar, 2001], and Dave Lebling argued it from the pointed ears of the godling Horn meets [Dave Lebling, 2001]. Robert Borski and William Ansley took the view that Pig is a godling in On Blue's Waters and rejected Pig-as-Tartaros readings [William H. Ansley, 2001] [Robert Borski, 2001], while Lebling restated the ear evidence [Dave Lebling, 2001].

Opposition came mainly from Peter Westlake, who argued that Pig regains his sight and so cannot be the blind Tartaros [Peter Westlake, 2001], and from Patrick O'Leary, who argued that Pig's character and behavior do not fit the one known godling and that two godlings in Blood's palace would be awkward [Patrick O'Leary, 2001] [Patrick O'Leary, 2001]. Later in the list Robert Borski argued that godlings are a generic category of large beings, organic or mechanical, which Kevin Maroney disputed and Michael Straight answered with a biological-kinship view [Robert Borski, 2001] [Michael Straight, 2001]. Lee Berman revived the claim in 2010 and 2013, relating Pig's claw-like fingernails to a small godling [Lee Berman, 2010] [Lee Berman, 2013].

The discussion mostly stayed inside threads on Pig's dialect, the nature of godlings, and Pig's height, with the height question left open after a thread in which Borski suggested about ten feet and Matthew Malthouse about twenty-two [whorl-v0012-0658 thread]. Jim Jordan conceded other Long Sun points in a later exchange where O'Leary and Maroney pressed their objections [pigs, Sheep & Wolves thread].

For

  • Pig's pointed ears match the pointed ears of the godling Horn meets, which Dave Lebling argued in 2001 [Dave Lebling, 2001] [Dave Lebling, 2001].
  • William Ansley cited the pointed ears and claws given to godlings in Return to the Whorl and Pig's remark about his 'h'ears' [William H. Ansley, 2001].
  • Robert Borski quoted Wolfe's description of godlings as mostly immense and the corn farmer's remark about a smaller godling to support a medium-sized godling Pig [Robert Borski, 2001].
  • Dave Lebling argued that Pig's orders to drive people from the Whorl resemble a godling's 'Leave the Whorl!' function [Dave Lebling, 2001].
  • Lee Berman argued in 2010 that Pig's large, claw-like fingernails clue that he is essentially a small godling [Lee Berman, 2010].

Against

  • Peter Westlake argued that Pig regains his sight, which rules out the blind Tartaros identity but does not directly address godling status [Peter Westlake, 2001].
  • Patrick O'Leary argued that Pig's character and behavior do not match the one known godling and that two godlings in Blood's palace would be awkward [Patrick O'Leary, 2001] [Patrick O'Leary, 2001].
  • Kevin Maroney argued that in the Long Sun books godlings are constructed servants of Mainframe regardless of body type, so Pig's small size fits poorly [whorl-v0012-0651 thread].
  • Michael Straight doubted that a bipedal body could scale up to giant size without augmentation, granting only the low-gravity point for undines [Michael Straight, 2001].
  • Matthew Malthouse worked from Silk's height to a giant near twenty-two feet, pitting the size estimate against the small-godling reading [whorl-v0012-0658 thread].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the godling reading was argued for from 2001 to 2013 by several posters, but O'Leary, Maroney and others rejected it, and the size and constructed-versus-biological questions were left unresolved.
