The Ascians are the shadowless

The name Ascian means "shadowless", people of the tropics, a hint to the geography.

8 threads · 18 posts · for 6 / against 11 · first raised 2002-07-29 by James Jordan
Main voices: James Wynn (6), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (3), James Jordan (2), Son of Witz (2), Bratt Ian (1), Adam Stephanides (1)

The debate

The shadowless reading was first raised on the list by James Wynn, who on 2002-07-30 proposed that the Ascians take their name from darkness, linking them to Erebus and reading them as shadowless because they live in continuous dark [James Wynn, 2002]. The same day Bratt Ian proposed that the lack of shadow means lack of individuality and soul [Bratt Ian, 2002], and the equatorial reading appeared soon after in a post the record attributes to James Jordan, which placed the Ascians directly under the sun [James Jordan, 2002]. Adam Stephanides then argued that the name rests on a true etymology built on a false belief, citing Ash's speech about a man who sold his shadow [Adam Stephanides, 2002], and Wynn agreed that Wolfe knowingly exploited the equator derivation [James Wynn, 2002].

The Erebus and New Sun strand returned in later threads. Wynn tied the Ascians' shadowlessness to Severian as the New Sun and argued they reject the new order, citing the torturers' ritual of casting one's shadow on the condemned [James Wynn, 2002], and later said they lose their claim after the Deluge [James Wynn, 2002]. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued that the title's definite 'shadow of the torturer' excludes the Ascians from salvation [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002]. Wynn returned to the darkness etymology in 2010, quoting The Citadel of the Autarch to argue the Ascians are minions of Erebus [James Wynn, 2010], and Dan'l, in 2006, tied the name to Erebus and Tartaros while also noting that Commonwealthers wrongly thought the Ascians came from equatorial lands [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2006].

In January 2011 the Asian-derivation and equator questions dominated. Son of Witz proposed that 'Ascian' is a remnant of 'Asian' [Son of Witz, 2011] and later framed it as a communist metaphor [Son of Witz, 2011]; Gerry Quinn disputed the racial reading by arguing Severian's remark refers to empty eyes [Gerry Quinn, 2011]. Robin Dunn proposed a Latin origin meaning 'no shadow', tied to the equator where noon shadows vanish, citing an interview without a link [Robin Dunn, 2011], and Adam Thornton said the south-of-equator setting followed from the Ascians' northern location [Adam Thornton, 2011]. James Wynn then reported Wolfe's interview remark that the 'Asian' echo never occurred to him while writing [James Wynn, 2011].

For

  • The name Ascian means shadowless and fits an equatorial people standing directly under the sun, a geographic hint [James Jordan, 2002].
  • Noon shadows vanish near the equator, so the Latin root 'no shadow' fits the setting [Robin Dunn, 2011].
  • The Ascians are minions of Erebus, the darkness of the underworld, and their name signals that origin [James Wynn, 2010].
  • The Ascian name is a true etymology built on a false belief about people who sold their shadows, which Wolfe knowingly exploited [Adam Stephanides, 2002] [James Wynn, 2002].
  • The name echoes 'Asian' and works as a communist metaphor, supported by the Soothsayer's 'men without shadows' passage and the Mandarin-like language [Son of Witz, 2011] [Son of Witz, 2011].

Against

  • The name points to the equatorial sun, not to perpetual darkness, so the darkness etymology is weaker [James Jordan, 2002].
  • Thornton said the south-of-equator setting came from the Ascians' northern location, not from an Asian blend [Adam Thornton, 2011].
  • Quinn argued Severian's 'they all looked the same' remark refers to empty eyes, not race, so the Asian reading lacks textual support [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • Wynn reported Wolfe's interview remark that the Asian echo never occurred to him while writing [James Wynn, 2011].
  • Bratt Ian's lack-of-soul reading and Wynn's New Sun reading rest on inference rather than an explicit statement in the text [Bratt Ian, 2002] [James Wynn, 2002].

Where it stands: The geography reading (the name meaning shadowless and hinting at an equatorial people) is the most widely accepted outcome, with the Asian-derivation and communist readings rejected after Wolfe's interview remark, while the darkness, soul and New Sun readings remained disputed.
