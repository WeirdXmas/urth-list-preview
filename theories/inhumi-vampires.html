The inhumi are vampires (or fallen spirits)

The inhumi are science-fictional vampires, a mirror-image of them, or Nephilim-like fallen spirits.

33 threads · 33 posts · for 23 / against 13 · first raised 1997-01-16 by Derek Bell
Main voices: Lee Berman (10), James Jordan (4), Andrew Mason (4), Alice Turner (2), Michael Andre-Driussi (2), Adam Stephanides (2)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by Derek Bell in January 1997, who proposed that Quetzal is a vampire-like inhumu feeding on the Long Sun crew [Derek Bell, 1997], and Doug Eigsti followed the next day, reading Teasel's puncture wounds as a vampire-like attack [Doug Eigsti, 1997]. Michael Andre-Driussi added that the inhumi are the equivalent of vampires and speculated on how they might cross between worlds [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997][Michael Andre-Driussi, 2000]. Dan Rabin's 2001 post argued the inhumi are a deliberate reversal of Dracula-type vampires [Dan Rabin, 2001].

The debate broadened over the years into a contest between vampire, demon and Nephilim readings. Alice Turner argued the inhumi are demons in the Catholic tradition [Alice Turner, 2002], while Adam Stephanides held they are vampires whose cruelty mirrors human cruelty [Adam Stephanides, 2002]. Lee Berman revived the vampire and Nephilim readings in 2010, calling the inhumi fallen spirits akin to Nephilim [Lee Berman, 2010][Lee Berman, 2010], and in 2011 he argued the inhumi reverse the vampire's essence-passing [Lee Berman, 2011]. Gerry Quinn disputed the vampire reading in the 'Mucor and the devils' thread, agreeing the inhumi were on Urth from the start but not that they are vampires [Lee Berman, 2010].

James Jordan and Jerry Friedman pushed back on the Nephilim reading, with Jordan arguing that Genesis's sons of God were Sethite believers and that Nephilim means powerful men [James Jordan, 2006], and Friedman arguing that the Bible does not clearly establish what the Nephilim were [Jerry Friedman, 2010]. James Wynn and Andrew Mason disputed Berman's Genesis-based flood reading and the idea that inhumi fathered Nephilim [James Wynn, 2010][Andrew Mason, 2010]. In the 'Dorcas as vampire' thread, b sharp and Don Doggett supported vampirism with Lilith and lamia material [b sharp, 2006][Don Doggett, 2006], but the thread ended without agreement on vampirism.

For

  • Derek Bell proposed that Quetzal is a vampire-like inhumu, citing Teasel's bat bite and the vampire tradition of shape-shifting [Derek Bell, 1997].
  • Doug Eigsti argued Teasel's puncture wounds point to a vampire-like attack, citing page 62.5 of Silk [Doug Eigsti, 1997].
  • Dan Rabin proposed that the inhumi are a deliberate reversal of Dracula-type vampires, being aspiring humans who prey on the living [Dan Rabin, 2001].
  • Lee Berman argued the inhumi are fallen spirits whose vampiric nature explains Severian's flushing of Urth, citing blood bats and Dorcas and Jolenta [Lee Berman, 2010].
  • b sharp built a case through the Lilith, lamia and Jahi myths and the play's accusation that Jahi has venomous fangs [b sharp, 2006].

Against

  • Alice Turner argued the inhumi are demons, so the colonial reading of TBOTSS is incomplete [Alice Turner, 2002].
  • Adam Stephanides argued the inhumi are vampires only in the sense that their cruelty mirrors human cruelty, and that they have no souls of their own [Adam Stephanides, 2002].
  • James Jordan argued the inhumi mirror human evil rather than representing demons, and that the Outsider is God acting through figures like Scylla [James Jordan, 2000].
  • Gerry Quinn agreed the inhumi were on Urth from the start but disputed that they are vampires, and argued that the fairy-like traits cannot be assigned arbitrarily [Lee Berman, 2010][Gerry Quinn, 2012].
  • Jeff Wilson argued the shape-shifted aborigines might be brain-damaged people rather than inhumi-driven vampires [Lee Berman, 2010].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the vampire reading was proposed early and revived several times, but posters including Quinn, Jordan, Turner and Wilson never came to accept it, and most threads ended without agreement.
