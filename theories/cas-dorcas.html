Cas is Dorcas

Ouen's lost love Cas, whom Dorcas resembles, is connected to Dorcas, or is her.

5 threads · 14 posts · for 9 / against 3 · first raised 2006-08-13 by b sharp
Main voices: Lee Berman (5), Marc Aramini (2), António Pedro Marques (2), b sharp (1), Son of Witz (1), Ryan Dunn (1)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by b sharp in August 2006 [b sharp, 2006], who argued that Dorcas is Severian's grandmother and read the Cas name as authorial misdirection, with Don Doggett voicing doubts. Cliff Judge in 2010 [Cliff Judge, 2010] and Lee Berman and Son of Witz in December 2010 [Lee Berman, 2010] [Son of Witz, 2010] then argued that Cas is Dorcas in disguise, with the chapter title "Dorcas" and the boatman's search for Cas cited as clues. Ryan Dunn [Ryan Dunn, 2010] built a family tree of Dorcas, Ouen and Catherine from the text, which James Wynn disputed in the later family-tree thread.

In May 2011 the question of whether the link was obvious became the focus of a long thread opened by Jason H. [Jason H, 2011]. Marc Aramini [Marc Aramini, 2011] and Jeff Wilson [Jeff Wilson, 2011] argued Wolfe hid the connection so it is seen only in retrospect, while António Pedro Marques [António Pedro Marques, 2011] called it obvious on first reading and not a true Wolfean puzzle. Aramini [Marc Aramini, 2011] generalised identity-disguise as Wolfe's central technique, and Marques [António Pedro Marques, 2011] restated his objection to the theory's delivery.

The thread then turned to pronunciation. Lee Berman argued that American stress and vowel habits hide Cas in Dorcas, and that non-native speakers see the link more easily [Lee Berman, 2011] [Lee Berman, 2011] [Lee Berman, 2011]. The posts record this pronunciation argument running for many posts without agreement.

For

  • Son of Witz noted that the boatman searching for Cas appears under the chapter titled Dorcas before Dorcas is met [Son of Witz, 2010].
  • Lee Berman argued that Ouen knows where Dorcas's body lies and that Cas disguises her name [Lee Berman, 2010].
  • Jeff Wilson said he linked Cas with Dorcas on first reading but dismissed it as too fantastic, and cited the note in the Inn of Lost Loves [Jeff Wilson, 2011].
  • Marc Aramini argued that identity disguise is central to Wolfe's technique, citing Cas and Dorcas among other examples [Marc Aramini, 2011].
  • Ryan Dunn grounded a family tree of Dorcas as grandmother from quoted passages of the Inn of Lost Loves and the Matachin Tower [Ryan Dunn, 2010].

Against

  • António Pedro Marques said the link between old Cas and young Dorcas was obvious on first reading and that Dorcas is not really a Wolfean puzzle [António Pedro Marques, 2011].
  • Marques later clarified that his objection concerned the shared name and obvious questions with obvious answers, not the plot [António Pedro Marques, 2011].
  • Lee Berman's twin-naming argument from Borski was found unconvincing by the poster who cited it, and Jason H. argued the clues are not so elaborately hidden [Lee Berman, 2010] [Lee Berman, 2011].
  • Jerry Friedman objected to Berman's account of the Casdoe pronunciation, with the /s/ versus /z/ point quoted by Jason H. [Lee Berman, 2011] [Lee Berman, 2011].
  • James Wynn disputed Dunn's family tree, saying the text does not confirm the links and that Catherine may have borne other children [Ryan Dunn, 2010].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never reached agreement on whether the Cas-Dorcas link was hidden or obvious, and the pronunciation argument ended without consensus.
