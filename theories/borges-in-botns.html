Borges in the Sun books (Ultan is Borges)

The blind librarian Ultan is Jorge Luis Borges, and Baldanders is named from Borges's Book of Imaginary Beings.

65 threads · 78 posts · for 52 / against 18 · first raised 1997-03-13 by James Jordan
Main voices: Adam Thornton (10), James Jordan (6), Gwern Branwen (5), Michael Andre-Driussi (4), Lee Berman (4), Dan Parmenter (3)

The debate

The idea that Master Ultan is Borges was first raised on the list by James Jordan in March 1997, in a post proposing that Nessus is Buenos Aires and that Blind Ultan parodies Borges [James Jordan, 1997]. Derek Bell soon agreed that Ultan is a Borges figure, while doubting the Buenos Aires link [Derek Bell, 1997]. Alice Turner disputed the Nessus claim in the same period [Alice Turner, 1997]. In 1998 the idea spread to the main list. Jordan argued that Ultan's library is primarily modeled on Borges's library [James Jordan, 1998], Michael Andre-Driussi linked Funes and the Library of Babel to Ultan [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998], Dan Parmenter argued Ultan fits Borges's library career [Dan Parmenter, 1998], and Pedro Jorge Romero argued from a Borges poem that Ultan stands in for Borges [Pedro Jorge Romero, 1998].

The claim was repeated through 1999 and 2000, mostly as assertion. Jordan, Urash, Greene and Malthouse argued over Ultan and Cyby in the 'NORTH/SOUTH' thread, where Greene asked for textual support and Malthouse suggested Cyby was Wolfe's self-casting [James Jordan, 1999] [Greene, Carlton, 1999] [Matthew Malthouse, 1999]. Andre-Driussi compiled Borges references from Wolfe's addenda and suggested Ultan equals Borges [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2000]. In 2003 Josh Geller asked whether Master Ultan is Borges, offering no evidence [Josh Geller, 2003]. In 2007 and 2008 Adam Thornton and Stuart Hamm restated the identification in playful form, with Thornton offering no textual evidence [Stuart Hamm, 2007] [Adam Thornton, 2008]. Gwern Branwen and Matthew Keeley argued for a Borges model for the Ultan library scene [Matthew Keeley, 2008].

The biggest flare-up came in November 2009, when James Wynn called the theory thin, noting that Wolfe names Chesterton and Vance as influences but not Borges [James Wynn, 2009] [James Wynn, 2009]. Gwern Branwen answered by citing Wolfe's interview remarks, though he could not pin down which one [Gwern Branwen, 2009]. Tim O'Donnell quoted Wolfe saying he took the giant's name from the Book of Imaginary Beings [O'Donnell, Tim (BOSI), 2009]. James Jordan's claim that Ultan's library holds the Aleph book was corrected by Branwen, who pointed to a crystal cube passage instead [James Jordan, 2009] [Gwern Branwen, 2009]. In 2010 Jerry Friedman disputed the Borges identification, while Branwen argued that both libraries symbolize all libraries [Jerry Friedman, 2010] [Gwern Branwen, 2010]. Thornton said in 2011 that he came to the reading through his Borges phase [Adam Thornton, 2011].

For

  • Blind Ultan parodies Borges, as James Jordan proposed in the earliest post [James Jordan, 1997].
  • Ultan's library is primarily modeled on Borges's library, with the Borgesian framing of the series offered as support [James Jordan, 1998].
  • Ultan's blindness, his library and Borges's career as a librarian are cited as parallels [Dan Parmenter, 1998] [Adam Thornton, 2011].
  • Wolfe's own interview remarks are cited as confirming that Ultan echoes Borges, though the posters could not locate the exact quote [Gwern Branwen, 2009] [O'Donnell, Tim (BOSI), 2009].
  • Ultan's library, read through 'The Library of Babel', is treated as a Borgesian figure [Gwern Branwen, 2010] [David Stockhoff, 2010].

Against

  • Wolfe names Chesterton and Vance as influences but has never been heard naming Borges, so the basis is thin [James Wynn, 2009].
  • Being a blind librarian is too little to identify two people, and Palaemon is also blind [James Wynn, 2009].
  • Wolfe admires Borges too much to portray him as a greedy alzabo-eater, and Babel holds all books, not all libraries [Jerry Friedman, 2010].
  • The labyrinthine library may reflect early twentieth-century university architecture rather than Borges [Jeff Wilson, 2008].
  • Ultan's speech echoes Borges in content but not in style [Maru Dubshinki, 2005].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the identification was widely repeated by its advocates, especially Thornton and Branwen, and it was contested throughout, most sharply in the 2009 and 2010 threads, without a settled conclusion.
