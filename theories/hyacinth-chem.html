Hyacinth is a chem

Hyacinth, Silk's lover, is a chem (an artificial person), not a bio.

13 threads · 27 posts · for 7 / against 13 · first raised 2000-08-17 by stilskin
Main voices: James Wynn (13), Jerry Friedman (2), James Jordan (2), stilskin (1), Dennis G. Berdanis (1), Robert Borski (1)

The debate

The idea that Hyacinth is a chem was first raised on the list by stilskin in August 2000, who called her secretly a chem in a post about Horn and hybrid beings [stilskin, 2000]. A month later Dennis G. Berdanis asked whether the Hyacinth that Silk met was a chem reproduction or a clone [Dennis G. Berdanis, 2000]. The theory was argued seriously in 2002, when Robert Borski questioned Crush's chem essay and asked how a fabricated father and a dead mother at Mainframe fit it [Robert Borski, 2002]. James Wynn defended chem status, linking Hyacinth to the Hyacinthus myth and Apollo's killing of him [James Wynn, 2002], and later argued she was a male chem [James Wynn, 2002]. Jerry Friedman countered that she is most likely biologically human, citing the pilot fight, her breast enhancement and her self-deprecating remarks [Jerry Friedman, 2002].

The 2002 exchanges turned on a few textual clues. William Ansley argued that Silk's line about Hyacinth subduing the pilot alone is true either way, so it does not decide the question [William H. Ansley, 2002], and Wynn replied that the line suggests Silk suspected help [James Wynn, 2002]. Wynn also argued that her light weight is a clue to chem nature [James Wynn, 2002], while James Jordan said no passage records anyone lifting her and that her name and position work thematically without making her a chem [James Jordan, 2002] [James Jordan, 2002]. Andy Robertson argued from fusion physics that a chem's generator would make surroundings radioactive, which made a chem Hyacinth doubtful [Andy Robertson, 2002].

The theory returned in 2003 and again in 2010 and 2011, usually as a male-chem variant. Che Monro suggested Hyacinth and Chenile may have been male [Che Monro, 2003]. In 2010 Wynn proposed that Hyacinth is Fava of the Short Sun books [James Wynn, 2010], and Josh Geller reported Wolfe's reply that it was a good thing he was not on the list [Josh Geller, 2010]. In November 2011 Wynn recounted his earlier male-chem theory and rejected it while still finding her secretive [James Wynn, 2011]. Marc Aramini supported a male identity from the mirror-ring scene and the statues of youths [Marc Aramini, 2011], and Wynn argued that a male chem marriage would not be more deviant than a female-chem one [James Wynn, 2011].

For

  • Hyacinth's name and the Hyacinthus myth, with Apollo's killing of Hyacinthus, suggest Silk's lover is a chem in disguise [James Wynn, 2002] [James Wynn, 2002].
  • Horn's remark that Hyacinth performs femininity to attract Silk, and Silk's talk with Horn about male chem servants, support a male chem [James Wynn, 2002].
  • Silk's line about Hyacinth subduing the pilot alone may suggest help rather than strength, which fits the chem reading [James Wynn, 2002].
  • Hyacinth's light weight is noted as a clue linking her to Jonas's transformation [James Wynn, 2002].
  • The mirror-ring scene and statues of youths suggest a male identity, which implies a male chem [Marc Aramini, 2011].

Against

  • The pilot fight and Hyacinth's breast enhancement and deprecating remarks about appearance suggest she is biologically human [Jerry Friedman, 2002] [Jerry Friedman, 2002].
  • Silk's line about her subduing the pilot is true whether she is chem or bio, so it does not establish chem status [William H. Ansley, 2002].
  • A chem's fusion generator would make surrounding materials radioactive, which makes a chem Hyacinth doubtful [Andy Robertson, 2002].
  • Her name and chem-like positioning work thematically as a call to rescue lesser beings, without making her literally a chem [James Jordan, 2002] [James Jordan, 2002].
  • Wynn's remembered weight clue could not be relocated in the text, and he later rejected the male chem idea [James Wynn, 2010] [James Wynn, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed and never resolved: the chem and male-chem readings were argued across 2000 to 2011 without consensus, and Wynn himself later rejected the male-chem version [James Wynn, 2011].
