The hut in the jungle is in another time

The family in the hut in the Botanic Gardens' jungle (Robert, Marie and the shaman Isangoma) live in another time, perhaps our own era, and see Severian and Agia as ghosts.

39 threads · 48 posts · for 39 / against 11 · first raised 1998-07-10 by mary whalen
Main voices: Lee Berman (15), b sharp (7), Son of Witz (4), Larry Miller (4), Jerry Friedman (3), Andrew Mason (3)

The debate

The idea that the hut's inhabitants are in another time was first raised in July 1998, when Mary Whalen argued that Isangoma comes from the future rather than the past, citing his talk of hesperorns and oreodonts [mary whalen, 1998]. In March 1999 Michael Andre-Driussi proposed a chant-caught version in which some of Isangoma, Robert and Marie are caught by the chant while time travel also operates, using a Paris remark to suggest locals versus tourists [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1999]. Through 2003 to 2008 the discussion moved toward identities and location. James Wynn and Dan'l Danehy-Oakes read the figures as the Moffats or as Commonwealth citizens captured by the gardens [James Wynn, 2003] [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2003]. b sharp tied the hut to a time-structure family and to Urth as Earth's future [b sharp, 2006] [b sharp, 2008], while Duncan Truter argued that the scene must be set in Earth's past [Duncan Truter, 2008].

The ghost version gained its clearest form in November 2007, when Tony Ellis argued that the hut visitors are ghosts from Urth's future, with the Gardens bending back in time [Tony Ellis, 2007]. Stanisław Bocian, in the same week, proposed that the inhabitants are memories and that Severian and Agia travel to the past [Stanisław Bocian, 2007]. In 2010 Lee Berman argued for a time-portal reading and said the hut lies on Earth [Lee Berman, 2010] [Lee Berman, 2010]. Jeff Wilson disputed that the hut is special and noted Robert seeing Severian and Agia in real time [Jeff Wilson, 2010]. Jerry Friedman argued that the hut shows a different world rather than our future [Jerry Friedman, 2010].

From 2011 the thread was dominated by whether Isangoma is Father Inire in disguise. Andrew Mason, Lee Berman and David Stockhoff supported that identification [Andrew Mason, 2011] [Lee Berman, 2011] [David Stockhoff, 2011], while Larry Miller argued the three are real people from our time or historical missionaries [Larry Miller, 2011] [Larry Miller, 2011]. Berman revived the time-spanning structure reading in 2014 [Lee Berman, 2014]. The ghost and time-displacement readings were never settled by the list.

For

  • Isangoma comes from the future, citing his talk of hesperorns and oreodonts [mary whalen, 1998]
  • Some inhabitants may be chant-caught tourists from Urth while time travel also operates [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1999]
  • The hut visitors are ghosts from Urth's future, with the Gardens bending back in time [Tony Ellis, 2007]
  • The hut inhabitants are memories of a past journey by Severian and Agia rather than merely chant-caught Urthians [Stanisław Bocian, 2007]
  • The hut is a labyrinthine time-spanning structure, like the Last House, with upper floors lying in the future [Lee Berman, 2014] [Lee Berman, 2010]

Against

  • The hut is set in Earth's past, since Isangoma is a real South African shaman term and the air and aeroplane details fit the past [Duncan Truter, 2008]
  • The hut is not special, and any time travel lies in the Botanic Gardens as a whole, with Robert seeing Severian and Agia in real time and an anachronistic airplane [Jeff Wilson, 2010]
  • Urth is a different world, not our future, given the mixed fauna and a Nebo passage differing from the Bible [Jerry Friedman, 2010]
  • The Gardens are a South African or posthistorical theme park rather than a time structure, so the hut needs no time shift [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2003]
  • Isangoma, Robert and Marie are real people from our time observed by Severian and Agia, and the Moffat identification places them in the past [Larry Miller, 2011] [Larry Miller, 2011]

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never reached agreement, and the ghost, memory and future readings were left side by side with the Moffat and past-setting readings, while the discussion increasingly shifted to Inire identity.
