The tomb is Severian's own

The mausoleum where young Severian played, with a ship and a face like his own on it, is Severian's own tomb, built after his return.

21 threads · 33 posts · for 19 / against 11 · first raised 1997-04-16 by James Jordan
Main voices: Tony Ellis (4), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (4), Roy C. Lackey (3), Ashley Crill (3), thalassocrat (2), Jeff Wilson (2)

The debate

The idea that the tomb is Severian's own has roots in the 1997 post by James Jordan [James Jordan, 1997], who tied the rose, ship and fountain symbols on the tomb to future volumes, though he did not say Severian lay there. In 1998 J. Schultz proposed that the Conciliator's tomb held an earlier Severian who returned from Yesod [J. Schultz, 1998], and C R Culver disputed this because a Severian who failed the trial could not father children [C R Culver, 1998]. Marc Aramini added in 2002 that Severian would be placed in his tomb after being folded into Scylla or a dark swimmer [Marc Aramini, 2002], and Lisa Schaffer-Doggett argued in 2003 that the skull belongs to the original Severian [Lisa Schaffer-Doggett, 2003].

In 2005 the debate turned to Ymar. thalassocrat proposed that the childhood play-tomb is Ymar's and that Severian and Ymar are hidden family [thalassocrat, 2005]. Roy C. Lackey argued that dark coloring weighed against clone and tomb theories [Roy C. Lackey, 2005], and mournings glory objected that the door's rose and fountain clash with Ymar's reputation [mournings glory, 2005]. Tony Ellis then argued that Severian has himself tombed across history after bringing the new sun [Tony Ellis, 2005], and Don Doggett suggested the tomb may hold Ymar as well as Severian [Don Doggett, 2005].

From 2006 to 2008 posters focused on the coffins and the ship. Lackey proposed that the coffins hold Severian's own incarnations [Roy C. Lackey, 2006], while thalassocrat said the other coffins had no plausible owner [thalassocrat, 2006]. Dave Tallman proposed that Severian built the tomb himself and that its coffins may hold Odilio, Pega and Thais [Dave Tallman, 2008], but David Stockhoff argued the corpse cannot easily be an earlier Severian [David Stockhoff, 2008] and Son of Witz noted the scarred coin portrait [Son of Witz, 2008]. The ship on the tomb drew a separate dispute between Ashley Crill, Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, Jeff Wilson and Tony Ellis over whether it is a spaceship, a tender or a sailing vessel [Ashley Crill, 2007][Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2007][Jeff Wilson, 2007][Tony Ellis, 2007]. Later, John Watkins argued that a single time-travelling Severian better explains the evidence [John Watkins, 2010], and Andrew Mason read the emblem as a time-travelling Severian's tomb [Andrew Mason, 2010].

The theory was raised repeatedly across the list but was never settled. Its supporters relied on Severian's time travel and resurrection, while its critics relied on the text's coffin counts, the scarred portrait and the trial's consequences. Threads on the tomb, the ship and Ymar ended without agreement.

For

  • Tallman proposed that Severian built his own tomb after time travel and explained the changed face as an idealized portrait [Dave Tallman, 2008]
  • Ellis argued from the closing lines of Citadel and Severian's resurrection abilities that the man in the mausoleum is Severian [Tony Ellis, 2005]
  • Aramini proposed that Severian will be placed in his tomb after being folded into time-travel events [Marc Aramini, 2002]
  • Schaffer-Doggett argued that the skull belongs to the original Severian and that an adult might not recognize his childhood look-alike [Lisa Schaffer-Doggett, 2003]
  • Mason read the rose, ship and fountain emblem as the tomb of a time-travelling Severian [Andrew Mason, 2010]

Against

  • Culver argued that a Severian who failed the trial could not return and father children, so the tomb cannot hold him [C R Culver, 1998]
  • Stockhoff argued the corpse cannot easily be an earlier Severian and that the altered face reflects a godlike icon [David Stockhoff, 2008]
  • Son of Witz pointed out that the Autarch's coin portrait is scarred, which is a problem for identifying Severian's older self [Son of Witz, 2008]
  • Watkins argued that the two-Severian theory rests on Severian's own end-of-Citadel musings rather than canon [John Watkins, 2010]
  • mournings glory argued that the door's symbols clash with the tomb's supposed occupant and that the coffin counts do not fit [mournings glory, 2005]

Where it stands: Still disputed: raised repeatedly from 1997 to 2010 and never accepted, with threads on the tomb, ship and Ymar ending in impasse.
