Severian's book ends up in Ultan's library

Severian's manuscript, sent back into the library, becomes the lost Book of the New Sun of legend.

12 threads · 17 posts · for 12 / against 4 · first raised 1999-03-29 by Tony Ellis
Main voices: Tony Ellis (3), Jeff Wilson (3), James Jordan (2), David Stockhoff (2), Roy C. Lackey (1), Chris P (1)

The debate

The theory that Severian's own manuscript ends up in Ultan's library as the lost Book of the New Sun was first raised by Tony Ellis on 1999-03-29, who argued in posts [Tony Ellis, 1999] and [Tony Ellis, 1999] that Severian is the original author of a closed loop whose narrative Talos's play draws on rather than Canog's text. Roy C. Lackey disputed this at once, arguing in [Roy C. Lackey, 1999] that the account of Urth's end lies in Severian's future when he writes, so it cannot be the source, and Ellis replied in [Tony Ellis, 1999] by positing several Books of the New Sun, with Thecla's lending-library copy and the Contessa's sighting in the play as support. The thread "Eschatology and Genesis" never drew other responses, so the idea stayed a single unanswered exchange for years.

The theory resurfaced in 2010 in a looser form, when Adam Thornton proposed in [Adam Thornton, 2010] that Severian's mention of Ultan's library simply assumes his book will sit on its shelves. David Stockhoff objected in [David Stockhoff, 2010] that the book could not survive in past or future unless the Library travels through time, and James Wynn answered in [James Wynn, 2010] that the autobiography itself places readers inside Ultan's Library. The Borges strand came from James Jordan in [James Jordan, 2009], who proposed that Ultan's library holds Borges's Aleph book, and Gwern Branwen corrected him in [Gwern Branwen, 2009] by pointing to the crystal cube passage as the likelier source.

Later discussion split the idea into time-loop and survival questions. Jeff Wilson proposed in [Jeff Wilson, 2011] and [Jeff Wilson, 2011] that memoir volumes reached Ultan's library through a loop and that gaps in the narrative are lost volumes, while Gwern Branwen argued in the "Trailer of Ink" thread that the phrase derives from Ecclesiastes rather than a time loop, and David Duffy agreed that Ultan's quotation glosses that verse. David Stockhoff's [David Stockhoff, 2011] suggested the book may sit in Ultan's library, offering time travel or book delivery to explain a matching scene in the play. In the "Did the Library Survive?" thread, James Jordan's [James Jordan, 2003] argued the library survived the flood, and Matthew Malthouse's dispute in that thread countered that the books were drowned.

For

  • Tony Ellis argued Severian is the original author of a closed loop whose narrative becomes the lost Book of the New Sun [Tony Ellis, 1999] [Tony Ellis, 1999]
  • Ellis cited the Contessa's sighting of a figure in the play, which he tied to Severian's later explanation, as evidence [Tony Ellis, 1999]
  • Adam Thornton suggested Severian's reference to Ultan's library assumes his book will reach its shelves [Adam Thornton, 2010]
  • James Jordan proposed the library survived the flood and holds the copy Severian left for later generations [James Jordan, 2003]
  • Jeff Wilson proposed that a wandering trail of ink sets up a time loop delivering a memoir volume to Ultan's library [Jeff Wilson, 2011]

Against

  • Roy C. Lackey argued the account of Urth's destruction lies in Severian's future when he writes, so it cannot be the source [Roy C. Lackey, 1999]
  • David Stockhoff argued the book could not survive in past or future unless the Library travels through time [David Stockhoff, 2010]
  • Gwern Branwen argued the trailer-of-ink phrase derives from Ecclesiastes rather than a time loop [Jeff Wilson, 2011]
  • Matthew Malthouse argued the flood drowned the library's books in the mundane world even if the library is transcendental [James Jordan, 2003]
  • Gwern Branwen corrected the Aleph-book reading, noting that Borges's Library of Babel does not contain itself [Gwern Branwen, 2009]

Where it stands: Raised once in 1999 and never resolved; later posters mostly set it aside, with the time-loop reading losing ground to the Ecclesiastes reading and no consensus reached.
