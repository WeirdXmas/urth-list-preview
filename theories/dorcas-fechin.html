Dorcas is Fechin's daughter

Dorcas is the daughter (or wife) of the painter Fechin, whose pictures show her.

11 threads · 20 posts · for 15 / against 7 · first raised 1999-07-08 by Mark Millman
Main voices: thalassocrat (7), b sharp (5), Lee Berman (3), Dave Tallman (2), David Stockhoff (2), Mark Millman (1)

The debate

The idea that Fechin is Dorcas's family first surfaced in 1999, when Mark Millman [Mark Millman, 1999] conceded an earlier claim about the cloisonné work and offered Fechin as Dorcas's brother- or son-in-law, after Roy Lackey quoted Book of the New Sun I, XXII showing that Dorcas's father and brother made the piece. The daughter version was first argued in June 2008 by thalassocrat [thalassocrat, 2008], who pointed to Dorcas's artistic skill, the family's enamel trade, Ouen's cloisonné locket, Fechin's listed portraits, and Casdoe's father's remarks about Fechin's copper pan and face in the sun.

The 2008 discussion split between rival readings. b sharp [b sharp, 2008] argued that Fechin, Dorcas's husband and other figures are one shapeshifter, and objected that Fechin as her father would make Casdoe's or Becan's father implausibly old. Dave Tallman [Dave Tallman, 2008] instead made Fechin Dorcas's girlfriend and Ouen's mother. Thalassocrat defended the daughter reading with age arithmetic [thalassocrat, 2008] and answered an objection about family resemblance by arguing that red-haired ancestors can have hairless descendants [thalassocrat, 2008]. Dave Tallman disputed the family link on resemblance and on the 'Are you my uncle?' line [Dave Tallman, 2008]. In July 2008 b sharp [b sharp, 2008] proposed a fifteen-year age shift to rescue the husband reading, but thalassocrat [thalassocrat, 2008] argued Dorcas would still be too old for the story.

From 2010 onward the theory was revived in new forms. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2010] found no substantive textual support for identifying Fechin with Rudesind. Lee Berman [Lee Berman, 2011] argued that Rudesind is Fechin and therefore Dorcas's husband, citing brushes in the boy's portrait, and later [Lee Berman, 2013] suggested the girl Fechin seduces could be Dorcas. David Stockhoff supported the window-memory link [David Stockhoff, 2011] and proposed a sister or family-artist connection [David Stockhoff, 2011], while Andrew Mason proposed Fechin as father and his girlfriend as mother [Andrew Mason, 2011]. Sergei Soloviev disputed the window-memory match, listing differences in setting and circumstance [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011]. The threads on this theory mostly ended in impasse without resolution.

For

  • Thalassocrat linked Dorcas's skill and the family enamel trade to a painter father, and cited Ouen's locket and Fechin's portraits [thalassocrat, 2008].
  • Thalassocrat's age reckoning from Rudesind's age and Dorcas's birth date was offered in support of the daughter reading [thalassocrat, 2008].
  • Thalassocrat answered the resemblance objection by arguing that hairiness does not rule out a family link [thalassocrat, 2008].
  • Thalassocrat's lineage post built on Casdoe's father's remarks about Fechin's copper pan and sun-lit face, with Little Sev's question as added support [thalassocrat, 2011].
  • David Stockhoff and Andrew Mason suggested a family-artist link between Dorcas and Fechin, and Stockhoff argued that the window memory echoes Dorcas's own [David Stockhoff, 2011][Andrew Mason, 2011][David Stockhoff, 2011].

Against

  • Dave Tallman argued that the family lacks visible resemblance and that Li'l Sev's question does not imply a known uncle [Dave Tallman, 2008].
  • b sharp argued that making Fechin Dorcas's father makes Casdoe's or Becan's father implausibly old [b sharp, 2008].
  • Thalassocrat himself found that even the fifteen-year shift leaves Dorcas too old for the girl in Casdoe's father's story [thalassocrat, 2008].
  • Sergei Soloviev listed differences between Dorcas's window memory and the Fechin scene, including setting and the waiting father [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011].
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes found no substantive textual support for identifying Fechin with Rudesind, and in 1999 Mark Millman's quoted passage credited Dorcas's father and brother with the cloisonné [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2010][Mark Millman, 1999].

Where it stands: Still disputed and unresolved: the daughter reading never won agreement, and the list ended with the question open amid rival husband, girlfriend and father readings.
