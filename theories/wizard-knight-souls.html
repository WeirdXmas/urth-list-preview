Disiri gains a soul from Able

Able's blood (or his promotion) gives Disiri an immortal soul.

15 threads · 25 posts · for 17 / against 10 · first raised 2004-12-11 by Andrew Bollen
Main voices: James Wynn (4), Roy C. Lackey (4), Chris P (3), Marc Aramini (3), Bob Miller (2), John Watkins (2)

The debate

The theory that Able's blood gives Disiri an immortal soul was first raised by Andrew Bollen on 2004-12-11 [Andrew Bollen, 2004], who read The Wizard Knight as explicit that only humans have immortal souls and that Able's blood grants Disiri one. Early discussion split quickly: David Duffy and Marc Aramini offered other readings of the book's names and parallels [David Duffy, 2005] [Marc Aramini, 2005], while Chris P and Bob Miller in December 2005 argued that Disiri can gain a soul or rise like Able [Chris P, 2005] [Bob Miller, 2005].

The main opposition came from Roy C. Lackey, who on 2005-12-06 argued that Disiri did not gain a soul from Able's blood, because Able was already human when he first gave blood and Toug's transfusion to Baki conferred nothing [Roy C. Lackey, 2005]. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes instead treated the blood gift as a real supernatural reward paralleling the Eucharist [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2005]. Lackey later reversed course to propose that Able's blood gave Disiri a soul while Baki's did not [Roy C. Lackey, 2007], and in 2007 he distinguished soul from salvation, arguing Able gave a soul but not salvation [Roy C. Lackey, 2007].

The Eucharistic reading resurfaced in 2012, when James Wynn argued that Able's blood feeding of Disiri is an overtly Christian eucharistic act [James Wynn, 2012], and Daniel Petersen supported it [Daniel Petersen, 2012]. Antonin Scriabin then proposed a reciprocal blood-sacrifice reading [Antonin Scriabin, 2012]. Thread summaries show the question was debated at length in several long threads, including "TWK: The Aelf dilemma" and "The poor Aelf", without agreement.

For

  • Able's blood, or his promotion of Disiri, is read by Andrew Bollen and Chris P as conferring an immortal soul, citing Mani's question about survival and the Aelf's lack of souls [Andrew Bollen, 2004] [Chris P, 2005].
  • Roy C. Lackey proposed that Able's blood gave Disiri a soul while Baki's transfusion from Toug did not, citing Able's report and Disiri's long natural life [Roy C. Lackey, 2007].
  • James Wynn and Daniel Petersen read the blood feeding as a Eucharistic act, with Disiri raised from mud-and-leaves form to human [James Wynn, 2012] [Daniel Petersen, 2012].
  • Bob Miller argued that the Aelf are not eternally soulless, citing the Valfather's promotion of human heroes and Disiri's rise [Bob Miller, 2005].

Against

  • Roy C. Lackey argued that Able was already human when he first gave Disiri blood, and that Toug's full cup to Baki conferred no soul, so the quantity argument is absurd [Roy C. Lackey, 2005].
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes treated the blood gift as a real supernatural reward for Disiri, paralleling the Eucharist, which does not require a soul-transfer reading [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2005].
  • Stanislaus Bocian argued the Aelf have no souls and cannot ascend, and that Disiri is a new creature despite remembering her past life [Stanisław Bocian, 2007].
  • Lackey later argued that Able gave Disiri a soul but not salvation, which requires a redeemer [Roy C. Lackey, 2007].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never reached agreement on whether Able's blood or promotion gave Disiri a soul, and the question drifted into related debates about salvation and the Eucharist.
