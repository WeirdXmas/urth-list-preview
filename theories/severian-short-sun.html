Severian appears in the Sun books after his own

Severian (or his sister, or his miracles) appears in the Long Sun and Short Sun books, tying the series together.

45 threads · 52 posts · for 25 / against 19 · first raised 1997-01-18 by CoxRathvon
Main voices: Marc Aramini (6), Jerry Friedman (4), Nicholas Gevers (3), Roy C. Lackey (3), James Wynn (3), Dave Tallman (3)

The debate

The idea that Severian appears in the Short Sun books was first raised on the list in May 1998, when Dan'l Danehy-Oakes responded to Mantis's claim that Severian is an energy vampire who will appear there and asked how he would deal with the inhumi [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 1998]. Michael Straight said in 1999 that he was prejudiced against such rumors because the Short Sun feels different from the New Sun [Michael Straight, 1999]. Joel Priddy's 2001 post on a 'Severian's sister' clue and Seth Lombardi's 2003 questions about a child Silk meets as an alternate Severian kept the idea alive [Joel Priddy, 2001] [Seth Lombardi, 2003].

In 2002 the argument moved to specific mechanisms. Marc Aramini proposed that Silk appears at Severian's baptism in the Gyoll [Marc Aramini, 2002], and Roy C. Lackey objected that the drowning happened in summer while Severian found Triskele cleaning a frozen drain in winter [Roy C. Lackey, 2002]. Don Doggett argued that if Blue is Ushas, Severian would become a vanished person [Don Doggett, 2002]. Later posts shifted toward the Short Sun Severian being a different iteration: Jerry Friedman argued this in 2011, citing hints about Merryn and the age given by Hide [Jerry Friedman, 2011] [Jerry Friedman, 2011], and Marc Aramini in 2014 argued the stiff bird-legged figure in Return to the Whorl is Severian [Marc Aramini, 2014].

The 2010 Tom Hopkins thread asked why Wolfe brought Severian into the Short Sun and argued that the appearance contradicts the New Sun [Tom Hopkins, 2010]. Dave Tallman answered by identifying Silk/Horn with the aquastor Malrubius [Dave Tallman, 2010], and Matthew Keeley argued Severian withholds the meeting deliberately [Matthew Keeley, 2010]. Peter Sun Rogers later argued Silk secretly watched over Severian throughout his life [Peter Sun Rogers, 2013]. The thread ended without consensus, with the Hopkins thread concluding that Wolfe's remarks were indeterminate.

For

  • Severian's later appearance was asserted in the 1998 exchange, with Dan'l Danehy-Oakes answering a claim that he will appear in The Book of the Short Sun [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 1998].
  • Marc Aramini argued that Silk's spirit carries him to Severian's baptism in the Gyoll [Marc Aramini, 2002].
  • Dave Tallman argued that Malrubius is Silk/Horn as remembered by Severian, so the Short Sun appearance fits the New Sun [Dave Tallman, 2010].
  • Peter Sun Rogers argued that the New Sun sets up Short Sun events through Silk's hidden watch over Severian [Peter Sun Rogers, 2013].
  • Marc Aramini argued that the stiff bird-legged figure in Return to the Whorl is Severian [Marc Aramini, 2014].

Against

  • Michael Straight said he was prejudiced against rumors that Severian appears in the Short Sun because the series feels different [Michael Straight, 1999].
  • Roy C. Lackey argued the summer drowning and winter frozen drain make an exact Silk appearance at the drowning impossible [Roy C. Lackey, 2002].
  • Tom Hopkins argued the Short Sun appearance contradicts the New Sun text and is not a simple lapse by Wolfe [Tom Hopkins, 2010].
  • Jerry Friedman argued the Short Sun Severian may be a different iteration from the New Sun Severian [Jerry Friedman, 2011].
  • Pedro Pereira treated the Severian encounter as a minor tie-in from Wolfe's fancy rather than a structural link [Pedro Pereira, 2007].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never reached consensus, and the Short Sun appearance was argued both as a deliberate tie-in and as a contradiction or a different iteration of Severian.
