Time travel in An Evil Guest

Time travel explains the plot of An Evil Guest and runs in both directions.

6 threads · 5 posts · for 3 / against 2 · first raised 2009-01-16 by Dave Tallman
Main voices: Roy C. Lackey (2), Dave Tallman (1), James Wynn (1), Daniel Petersen (1)

The debate

The theory was first raised by Dave Tallman in January 2009 [Dave Tallman, 2009], who built an hour-by-hour timeline of An Evil Guest's first four days from page references and argued Reis had only about thirteen hours to plan Cassie's destruction, write a musical, commission a bracelet and travel from Berlin, which he took to imply time travel. In the thread "AEG timeline" David Duffy challenged the reading by noting Wolfe said the bracelet was commissioned months earlier, and the timeline argument was left unresolved.

In October 2009 Roy C. Lackey took up the theory from the other side, arguing the text is too thin to prove foreknowledge of a version of Bill's own execution, yet that time travel may be needed to explain the plot [Roy C. Lackey, 2009]. He then argued time travel need not run only into the past, offering the ethermail reply as a message from the future [Roy C. Lackey, 2009]. The "AEG: Mr. Pavlatos" thread saw Lackey and Tallman dispute whether travel runs one way or both, with Tallman holding that Bill has one personal timeline that must be lived out and that the past cannot be changed.

Later posters widened the field. James Wynn proposed in 2010 that the wife's name can be deduced, calling it proof that time travel works functionally in the book [James Wynn, 2010], and in later threads he moved toward impostors, changelings and alien readings rather than time travel. Daniel Petersen reframed the interplanetary mail clue raised by Antonin Scriabin as spatial bending tied to a Gideon and Bill doubling theory, grounding it in the fact that Gideon and Bill never appear together [Daniel Petersen, 2011]. The "" thread tested a Dr. Lao duplicates alternative to time travel, which Lackey eventually dropped.

For

  • Tallman's page-cited timeline argues Reis had too little time after Day 0, implying time travel [Dave Tallman, 2009]
  • Lackey argues travel must run both ways because ethermail replies come from the future [Roy C. Lackey, 2009]
  • Wynn calls the deduced wife's name the first functional proof of time travel in the book [James Wynn, 2010]
  • Petersen argues the mail clue is spatial bending that makes Gideon and Bill one person, and calls the doubling almost certain [Daniel Petersen, 2011]

Against

  • Duffy notes Wolfe said the bracelet was commissioned months earlier, undercutting Tallman's timeline [Dave Tallman, 2009]
  • Lackey argues the text is too thin to prove Bill's foreknowledge of his own execution [Roy C. Lackey, 2009]
  • Tallman argues a double could not win Cassie's love and that copies in a world are implausible
  • Wynn leaves open that the figures are clones, and later favours impostors or changelings over time travel [James Wynn, 2010]

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never settled whether time travel explains An Evil Guest or runs both ways, and most later posters moved to alternative readings such as spatial bending, impostors or duplicates.
