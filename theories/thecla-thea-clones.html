Thecla and Thea are clones

Thecla and Thea (and others in the House Absolute) are clones or kaibits of one another.

16 threads · 29 posts · for 19 / against 11 · first raised 1998-01-06 by Michael Andre-Driussi
Main voices: Lee Berman (9), b sharp (5), Roy C. Lackey (4), Dave Tallman (2), Jeff Wilson (2), David Stockhoff (2)

The debate

The earliest post on the list is Michael Andre-Driussi's 1998 proposal that Severian's mother Catherine is an escaped khaibit of the Thea and Thecla bloodline [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998]. The clone idea proper came later. In 2006 b sharp proposed a naming rule that makes kaibit names diminutives of their donors' saint names, so Thea might be Thecla's kaibit [b sharp, 2006]. Roy C. Lackey countered with the caste rules and argued Thea and Thecla could not be children of Catherine, the Castellan and Little Inire [Roy C. Lackey, 2006]. In 2008 Dave Tallman proposed that the kaibit Thais is a clone of the Thea line, drawing on saints' legends [Dave Tallman, 2008], and b sharp and Roy Lackey argued over Thais and Thea in the same period [b sharp, 2008] [Roy C. Lackey, 2008].

The 2011 discussion was the most active. Lee Berman proposed that unmentioned cloning runs through the book and that Thecla and Thea are clones of one another [Lee Berman, 2011] [Lee Berman, 2011]. Andrew Mason argued instead that their mothers were source and clone, with the kahibit becoming the husband's mistress [Andrew Mason, 2011], and David Stockhoff largely supported this while raising a legitimacy objection [David Stockhoff, 2011]. Jerry Friedman disputed the face-shape evidence, calling the two sisters more like fraternal twins [Jerry Friedman, 2011]. In 2013 Berman restated and widened the claim, naming Thea as derived from Thecla and calling the sisters a hidden clone pair [Lee Berman, 2013] [Lee Berman, 2013]. He also linked the theme to Wolfe's wider work and to autarchs' concubines and khaibits [Lee Berman, 2013]. Stockhoff questioned the name-derivation idea [David Stockhoff, 2013], and Jeff Wilson argued that foreknowledge of successors is better explained by the many-Severians hypothesis [Jeff Wilson, 2013].

By late 2013 Berman himself conceded the evidence was contradictory and suggested Wolfe deliberately withheld clone details [Lee Berman, 2013]. The thread records no moment where a Wolfe remark or new text settled the question, and the strongest support came from name-pattern arguments, not the text itself.

For

  • Names such as Thea, Pega and Thais, along with shared heights and sunken cheeks, are read as signs of clone status [Lee Berman, 2013].
  • The name-pairing rule (Pelagia and Pega, Thecla and Thea) is offered as a naming convention that would reveal clones [b sharp, 2006] [Lee Berman, 2013].
  • The half-sister relationship is treated as an unexplained euphemism that cloning could explain [Lee Berman, 2011] [Lee Berman, 2013].
  • Thea's kaibit and St. Thais share saint legends, feast days and monastic details that Tallman and b sharp cited [Dave Tallman, 2008] [b sharp, 2008].
  • Stockhoff saw a source-and-clone arrangement as a possible explanation of the sisters' relationship [David Stockhoff, 2011].

Against

  • Roy C. Lackey argued from the caste rules that Thea and Thecla cannot both be children of Catherine, the Castellan and Little Inire [Roy C. Lackey, 2006].
  • Jerry Friedman argued the face-shape evidence was mislabeled and that the sisters could be fraternal twins rather than clones [Jerry Friedman, 2011].
  • Andrew Mason argued the sisters are not source and clone because their differences are noted and their own clones exist [Andrew Mason, 2011].
  • Jeff Wilson argued that the many-Severians hypothesis explains foreknowledge and varied successors without autarch auto-cloning [Jeff Wilson, 2013].
  • Stockhoff questioned whether clone names really derive from donor names, and raised doubts about the uhlans and Severian [David Stockhoff, 2013].

Where it stands: Still disputed and unresolved: Lee Berman was the main proponent, Berman himself later conceded the evidence was contradictory [Lee Berman, 2013], and most other posters did not accept the clone reading.
