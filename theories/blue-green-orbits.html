Blue and Green are separate worlds

Blue and Green are separate planets in a double or co-orbital system, not linked as some readers proposed.

42 threads · 50 posts · for 27 / against 20 · first raised 1997-01-31 by Dave Lebling
Main voices: Adam Stephanides (7), Marc Aramini (6), Roy C. Lackey (4), Gerry Quinn (4), Jerry Friedman (3), Pedro Pereira (3)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by Dave Lebling in January 1997 [Dave Lebling, 1997], who linked Quetzal's arrival to the Blue/Green system, and it was disputed the same month by Michael Andre-Driussi [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997], who argued the two worlds are separate. The dispute resurfaced in 1998 when mary whalen proposed a double-planet arrangement with the Whorl at a Lagrange point [mary whalen, 1998], and in 1999 Kieran Cleary tied Green to Lune [Kieran Cleary, 1999] while Mitchell A. Bailey argued the text's use of conjunction rules out a Lune-Urth pairing [Mitchell A. Bailey, 1999].

From 2000 to 2002 the discussion centred on orbital mechanics. Adam Stephanides proposed a Cruithne-like horseshoe orbit [Adam Stephanides, 2000] and later argued the Short Sun orbits cannot both be independent [Adam Stephanides, 2002]. Roy C. Lackey countered with twin planets in near-congruent orbits [Roy C. Lackey, 2002], and Jerry Friedman proposed a double planet with a six-year mutual period [Jerry Friedman, 2002]. Charles Reed argued in November 2002 that Green cannot be Lune and Blue cannot be Urth [Charles Reed, 2002], and Dan'l Danehy-Oakes offered an atmospheric explanation for the climate difference [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002].

From 2007 onward posters argued over whether Wolfe had settled the matter. thalassocrat held the planets are separate [thalassocrat, 2007], and Matthew Groves cited a 2006 archived Wolfe answer calling Blue and Green not the Urth system [Matthew Groves, 2007]. Pedro Pereira disputed that this was unequivocal [Pedro Pereira, 2007] and later cited a 1997 Wolfe answer that the Blue/Green system was the intended destination [Pedro Pereira, 2007]. Later posters, including Marc Aramini, proposed Fomalhaut, a different solar system, or decoy readings [Marc Aramini, 2010] [Marc Aramini, 2011] [Marc Aramini, 2011], while Gerry Quinn and Sergei Soloviev proposed further orbital models [Gerry Quinn, 2011] [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2012].

For

  • Dave Lebling suggested Quetzal entered the Whorl only on reaching the Blue/Green system [Dave Lebling, 1997].
  • Michael Andre-Driussi defended separate worlds with textual citations and Hohmann transfer orbit reasoning [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997] [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997].
  • Charles Reed argued Green's warmer climate would require an eccentric orbit that would lose Blue's pull, so Green cannot be Blue's satellite [Charles Reed, 2002].
  • thalassocrat noted there is no hint of differing gravity and Green has a shorter year [thalassocrat, 2007].
  • Matthew Groves pointed to a 2006 archived Wolfe answer that Blue and Green are not the Urth system [Matthew Groves, 2007].

Against

  • Mitchell A. Bailey argued the word conjunction does not fit a double-planet pair like Urth and Lune [Mitchell A. Bailey, 1999].
  • Roy C. Lackey argued Blue and Green are similar in size, so Blue cannot be a larger Urth [Roy C. Lackey, 2002].
  • Jerry Friedman proposed Blue and Green form a double planet with a six-year mutual period [Jerry Friedman, 2002].
  • Adam Stephanides argued the Short Sun orbits cannot both be independent, favouring a joint system like Earth and Venus [Adam Stephanides, 2002] [Adam Stephanides, 2002].
  • Marc Aramini argued Blue and Green may be a different solar system or orbit Fomalhaut, not Urth's system [Marc Aramini, 2011] [Marc Aramini, 2010].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the thread never reached consensus, with posters repeatedly restating positions and disputing whether Wolfe's interview answers settle the question.
