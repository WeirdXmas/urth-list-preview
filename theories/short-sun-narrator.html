Who narrates the Short Sun

The narrator of the Short Sun (especially Return to the Whorl) is a composite of Horn and Silk, or Silk himself.

29 threads · 31 posts · for 19 / against 11 · first raised 2001-02-08 by Peter Westlake
Main voices: Marc Aramini (7), James Wynn (4), James Jordan (3), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (3), Michael Straight (2), Michael Andre-Driussi (2)

The debate

The earliest post on the list is Peter Westlake's 2001 dispute, which argued the narrator of Return to the Whorl is Horn rather than Silk [Peter Westlake, 2001]. The composite idea itself first appears in Binah's August 2001 post, which proposed a Horn-and-Silk soul with Silk predominant at the close [Binah, 2001]. Michael Straight then supported a merged Silk-and-Horn narrator, citing the Rose-and-Marble analogy and Horn's hybrid corn lecture as foreshadowing [Michael Straight, 2001] [Michael Straight, 2001]. Earlier in 2001, Fernando Gouvea and Michael Andre-Driussi had already proposed related readings, a mirror between the Long Sun and Short Sun books and a Silk-as-narrator reading [Fernando Q. Gouvea, 2001] [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2001].

Through the 2000s the debate shifted to whether Silk's memories or spirit survive in the narrator. James Wynn cited a dream passage as evidence of Silk's memories, without claiming it as proof [James Wynn, 2005]. Don Doggett argued that nothing in the Long Sun and Short Sun books can reliably be attributed to Silk, and later proposed that the narrator is simply the Silk of the Long Sun [Don Doggett, 2005] [Don Doggett, 2004]. James Wynn countered in 2009 that the narrator is a resurrected Horn, citing the Neighbor who claims to be Horn and similar revivals [James Wynn, 2009]. Jordon Flato argued for a voice swap between the two series [Jordon Flato, 2009].

In 2010 Son of Witz found a Silk-Horn narrator compelling for the third-person chapters [Son of Witz, 2010]. The 2011 posts centred on On Blue's Waters: Marc Aramini argued for a rebooted Silk and disputed the name Silkhorn, while Gerry Quinn proposed a fading Horn and a rising Silk [Marc Aramini, 2011] [Marc Aramini, 2011] [Gerry Quinn, 2011]. Wynn then argued the narrator is a changeling rather than Horn [James Wynn, 2011]. Aramini's 2013 post argued the Short Sun narrator behaves like Silk, not Horn [Marc Aramini, 2013]. Aramini's 2014 post argued that narrators lie through ignorance rather than fabrication [Marc Aramini, 2014]. A 2023 post argued that Babbie embodies Horn, making Horn and Silk one being [Unknown, 2023].

For

  • Binah proposed that the narrator is a composite soul of Horn and Silk, with Silk predominant at the close [Binah, 2001].
  • Michael Straight argued the narrator is a merged Silk and Horn, with Silk's soul dying into despair and Horn's spirit joining it so Silk can heal [Michael Straight, 2001] [Michael Straight, 2001].
  • Son of Witz found the Silk-Horn narrator compelling and said it validates much, though it could not verify it [Son of Witz, 2010].
  • Gerry Quinn argued On Blue's Waters is narrated by a composite Silkhorn, with Horn's personality fading and Silk's remaining [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • James Wynn cited a Return to the Whorl passage about driving Orpine's body to a grave as possible evidence that the narrator has Silk's memories [James Wynn, 2005].

Against

  • Peter Westlake argued the narrator is Horn, not Silk, since the narrator never uses the Mother's communication method [Peter Westlake, 2001].
  • James Jordan argued the narrator is mainly Horn in Silk's body, and that the narrator checks for a scar and reassures himself he is not Silk [James Jordan, 2001].
  • Don Doggett argued that Silk's spirit may not be in the Short Sun narrator, and that Silk's reality rests only on characters who claim to have known him [Don Doggett, 2005].
  • James Wynn argued the narrator is a resurrected Horn, and later a changeling rather than Horn [James Wynn, 2009] [James Wynn, 2011].
  • Marc Aramini argued the Short Sun narrator behaves more like Silk than Horn, and disputed the Silkhorn name for On Blue's Waters [Marc Aramini, 2013] [Marc Aramini, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never reached agreement, most threads ended in impasse or personal quarrels, and the Silk, Horn, Silkhorn and Neighbor readings all continued to have advocates.
