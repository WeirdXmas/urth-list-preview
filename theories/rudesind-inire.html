Rudesind is Father Inire

Rudesind, the old curator who cleans pictures in the Citadel, is Father Inire in disguise.

26 threads · 84 posts · for 42 / against 44 · first raised 2006-05-25 by b sharp
Main voices: Lee Berman (14), David Stockhoff (10), Jeff Wilson (9), Ryan Dunn (9), Roy C. Lackey (8), António Pedro Marques (7)

The debate

The theory that Rudesind is Father Inire in disguise was first raised on the list by b sharp on 2006-05-25, who listed Inire's disguises including the jungle guide, the lazaret figure and Rudesind, and proposed that Dorcas's husband is Inire too [b sharp, 2006]. Within hours mournings glory disputed it as a self-deceiving human factotum with no reason to disguise himself [mournings glory, 2006] [mournings glory, 2006], while Don Doggett kept supporting it [Don Doggett, 2006] [Don Doggett, 2006]. Roy C. Lackey pressed the question of whose body lay in place of the Boatman and objected that interspecies offspring are unlikely [Roy C. Lackey, 2006] [Roy C. Lackey, 2006].

The theory returned in 2008 and was folded into broader claims that Rudesind, Fechin, the Boatman and Dorcas's husband are one shapeshifting Hierogrammate. Dave Tallman argued that Rudesind is a guided figure steered by Inire [Dave Tallman, 2008], while Jeff Wilson objected that a dead husband cannot be immortal [Jeff Wilson, 2008] and that the theory was unfalsifiable [Jeff Wilson, 2008]. Lackey defended the splitting model by comparing it to Tzadkiel's shape-changing [Roy C. Lackey, 2008] [Roy C. Lackey, 2008].

In 2010 the idea was revived most heavily. Lee Berman and Ryan Dunn argued for Rudesind as Inire using monkey-like descriptions and the 'blue Urth' phrase [Lee Berman, 2010] [Ryan Dunn, 2010] [Ryan Dunn, 2010], while David Stockhoff, John Watkins and António Pedro Marques objected that the resemblance was coincidental and that the identification added nothing [David Stockhoff, 2010] [John Watkins, 2010] [António Pedro Marques, 2010]. Jeff Wilson argued that Rudesind was too tall to be the bent little man [Jeff Wilson, 2010], and in 2011 Berman and Gerry Quinn disputed whether the physical resemblance was real [Lee Berman, 2011] [Gerry Quinn, 2011]. Later posts from 2013 to 2014 by Berman and Wilson restated the theory, with Wilson proposing Rudesind as a clone [Lee Berman, 2013] [Jeff Wilson, 2014].

For

  • Rudesind's old, bent, monkey-like appearance matches the description of Father Inire, as Lee Berman argued [Lee Berman, 2010] [Lee Berman, 2011].
  • Rudesind's directions to Severian and his role at the House Absolute, which Jeff Wilson cited as work only Inire could direct [Jeff Wilson, 2010] [Jeff Wilson, 2010].
  • The masks motif recurs across the books, which Ryan Dunn and Jeff Wilson took as support for hidden identities [Ryan Dunn, 2010] [Jeff Wilson, 2010].
  • Rudesind's remark that he carries Father Inire's letter, which Ryan Dunn read as showing Inire is his master [Ryan Dunn, 2010].
  • Dorcas's husband's wording about the averns and his small size and bow legs match Severian's description of Inire, according to b sharp [b sharp, 2008].

Against

  • Rudesind has no reason to disguise himself as a human painter or to keep the disguise after Severian takes the throne, as mournings glory argued [mournings glory, 2006].
  • Interspecies offspring with Dorcas are unlikely, and Severian's resemblance to Ouen argues against shapeshifting, according to Roy C. Lackey [Roy C. Lackey, 2006].
  • The Inire seen by the younger husband may be remembered as smaller after aging, and a dead husband cannot be immortal, as Jeff Wilson argued [Jeff Wilson, 2008].
  • Rudesind is too tall to be the bent little man, and Thecla's imprint only belatedly recognises the Autarch, per Jeff Wilson [Jeff Wilson, 2010].
  • Wolfe lets characters reveal themselves through speech, and Rudesind's manner and letter do not match Inire's behaviour, as David Stockhoff argued [David Stockhoff, 2010].

Where it stands: Still disputed and never settled: the threads ended in impasse or stalemate, with most of the strongest objections (physical mismatch, lack of textual support, unfalsifiability) left unanswered by the identification's supporters.
