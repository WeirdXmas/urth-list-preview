The drugged egg in "Seven American Nights"

Which night Nadan ate the drugged sugar egg can be worked out, and it decides which events are hallucinations.

7 threads · 6 posts · for 5 / against 0 · first raised 1997-08-14 by Alice Turner
Main voices: Dave Tallman (3), Alice Turner (1), Peter Cash (1), Stone Ox (1)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by Alice Turner on 1997-08-14, who reported Joan Gordon's 1986 reader's guide argument that the sixth egg held the hallucinogen, so Nadan lost a day because the story describes only six nights [Alice Turner, 1997]. Peter Cash took this up the next day, arguing that the egg-eating count is off and testing where a missing night could fit around Nadan's date with Ardis and the psychedelic egg, while also raising questions about the curator, Easter timing and a possible drugging [Peter Cash, 1997].

The discussion moved on in 2002, when Stone Ox supported the theory with a calendar argument that the missing night ends a repertory theater run on Sunday. Stone Ox also answered David DiGiacomo's objection that the egg had not yet taken effect, arguing that Nadan saw Ardis's head on a beast's torso after the laser shot and suggesting Ardis is a con artist [Stone Ox, 2002]. In 2008 Dave Tallman proposed a different reading in which the journal is a forgery and the eggs were drugged by secret police, grounding this in Nadan's teetotalism and the werewolf episode [Dave Tallman, 2008]. Over the following two days Tallman refined the claim into a poisoning plot and a Holy Week parallel scheme, reading the Good Friday parade and the Last Supper meal as allegorical support [Dave Tallman, 2008] [Dave Tallman, 2008].

The posts record these posts as proposals and supporting replies, and the thread summaries add that the egg-night question was otherwise argued in threads where Robert Borski proposed excised journal passages and Matthew Davis's counting argument was withdrawn. Tallman's forgery posts were not answered by any recorded poster, so that line closed in impasse, and the egg question itself ended without a clear resolution.

Against

  • David DiGiacomo objected that the egg had not yet taken effect at the point Nadan saw the hallucination, which Stone Ox answered [Stone Ox, 2002]

Where it stands: Still unresolved: the egg-night question was argued by a few posters and never settled, and Tallman's related forgery and poisoning reading ended in impasse with no recorded response.
