Why Jahlee attacked Nettle

Jahlee's attack on Nettle was jealousy over the Rajan, and how she reached Blue.

5 threads · 12 posts · for 11 / against 2 · first raised 2001-02-14 by Peter Westlake
Main voices: Roy C. Lackey (5), Johan Akesson (2), thalassocrat (2), James Wynn (2), Peter Westlake (1), Dave Lebling (1)

The debate

The theory was first raised on 2001-02-14 by Dave Lebling, who argued that Jahlee attacks Nettle out of unrequited love for Horn and that Horn kills her in response [Dave Lebling, 2001]. The same day Peter Westlake defended a more layered version, with Jahlee biting Nettle out of jealousy over the Rajan and as a rival to Nettle [Peter Westlake, 2001]. Johan Akesson then framed the attack as a crime of passion rather than inhuman nature, citing two passages on pages 315 and 316 about Jahlee wanting Horn/Silk for a long time [Johan Akesson, 2001] [Johan Akesson, 2001].

The discussion moved mostly to Jahlee's route to Blue in 2005. Roy C. Lackey asked how a child-sized Green inhuma could have bitten infant Sinew and proposed she arrived by lander [Roy C. Lackey, 2005], then argued she travelled by lander both ways [Roy C. Lackey, 2005]. Thalassocrat disputed that Silkhorn's lander remark shows any lander traffic and called the remark a lie [thalassocrat, 2005]. James Wynn also argued for lander travel, tying it to maturation rates and the timeline of the Short Sun books [James Wynn, 2005], and thalassocrat proposed Jahlee was born before the Exodus in a Neighbors' breeding program [thalassocrat, 2005]. The long "Travelin' Jahlee" thread carried most of this back and forth without resolution.

Lackey later tied the attack to motive, arguing that Jahlee's attack on Nettle and her final revelation were driven by inherited grief for her children and jealousy rather than hunger, citing her dying words and Silkhorn's last entry [Roy C. Lackey, 2005]. In 2011 Wynn questioned the flight idea and suggested Jahlee's reverence might refer to her mother hopping a lander [James Wynn, 2011].

For

  • Lebling argued the attack came from unrequited love for Horn, and that Horn killed her for breaking the agreement not to touch his family [Dave Lebling, 2001].
  • Westlake argued the bite was dramatically motivated by Jahlee's attraction to the Rajan, her rivalry with Nettle and her vanity [Peter Westlake, 2001].
  • Akesson quoted Jahlee on pages 315 and 316 saying she had long wanted Horn and that he had too many wives in Gaon, supporting a crime of passion [Johan Akesson, 2001] [Johan Akesson, 2001].
  • Lackey argued the attack was driven by inherited jealousy and grief for lost children, based on her dying words and Silkhorn's last entry [Roy C. Lackey, 2005].
  • Lackey argued the timeline only works if Jahlee travelled to Blue by lander both ways, possibly placed aboard by others [Roy C. Lackey, 2005].

Against

  • Thalassocrat argued Silkhorn's lander remark does not show inhumi reached the Whorl by lander, since he later says the Neighbors took them there [thalassocrat, 2005].
  • Wynn questioned the feasibility of a winged inhuman crossing between planets, noting that falling inhumi would not burn up in the atmosphere [James Wynn, 2011].
  • Wynn asked when inhumi become pregnant, raising doubts about the lander timeline he otherwise supported [James Wynn, 2005].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the motive reading (jealousy, passion, or unrequited love) drew several supporters, but the lander and timeline questions stayed unresolved after long threads without consensus.
