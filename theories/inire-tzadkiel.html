Father Inire is a lesser Tzadkiel

Father Inire is a Hierogrammate or a weaker being of Tzadkiel's kind, and his mirrors and portals are a lesser version of Tzadkiel's.

24 threads · 45 posts · for 27 / against 17 · first raised 1999-06-12 by Mitchell A. Bailey
Main voices: Lee Berman (20), Roy C. Lackey (4), John Watkins (4), James Wynn (4), Jeff Wilson (3), b sharp (2)

The debate

The earliest post on the list that touches on this theory is Mitchell A. Bailey's 1999 post [Mitchell A. Bailey, 1999], which made Inire an emissary of the Hierogrammates rather than a lesser Tzadkiel. The Tzadkiel comparison came in with b sharp in 2006, who said Inire resembled a shapeshifting Tzadkiel [b sharp, 2006]. In 2009 b sharp stated the weaker-version claim directly, arguing that the Autarch and Inire are weaker versions of Tzadkiel through shape-changing and multiple roles, and defended it against David Stockhoff's doubts [b sharp, 2009].

In 2010 the theory was taken up mostly by Lee Berman, who argued that Inire's portals are inferior to Tzadkiel's, citing Severian's remark on the portal [Lee Berman, 2010], and earlier described Tzadkiel's portals as an advanced version of Inire's [Lee Berman, 2010]. Berman also tied the portal comparison to the mirror work in Lee Berman's mirror argument [Lee Berman, 2010]. James Wynn proposed that Inire shapeshifts and is therefore a Hierogrammate, citing recurring reddish and monkey signifiers [James Wynn, 2010]. Opponents answered on textual grounds: John Watkins argued that Inire appears to be a Hierodule whose illusions hide only facial features [John Watkins, 2010], and Roy C. Lackey called the fallen-Hierogrammate reading unsupported by the text [Roy C. Lackey, 2010].

Roy C. Lackey supported b sharp's wider identification of Inire with Rudesind and the Boatman as one Hierogrammate able to take any form [Roy C. Lackey, 2008], while Jeff Wilson questioned whether Inire belongs to the shape-sharing race at all [Jeff Wilson, 2008]. Andrew Mason later argued that Inire belongs to the same species as B and F and so is not a hierarch [Andrew Mason, 2011]. Berman's 2012 claim that Inire is a pale reflection of Tzadkiel [Lee Berman, 2012] and his 2014 class-of-shapeshifters argument [Lee Berman, 2014] restated the theory, while Marc Aramini said the link to Tzadkiel's shapeshifting did not persuade him despite Inire's ape associations [Marc Aramini, 2014].

For

  • b sharp argued that Inire and Tzadkiel are weaker versions of one another through shape-changing and multiple roles [b sharp, 2009]
  • Berman argued that Inire's portals are inferior to Tzadkiel's, citing Severian's remark about the portal [Lee Berman, 2010]
  • Berman described Tzadkiel's portals as an advanced version of Inire's [Lee Berman, 2010]
  • Berman later called Inire a pale reflection of Tzadkiel, with the Autarch as a further weakened reflection [Lee Berman, 2012]
  • Lackey argued that Inire is a Hierogrammate able to take any form, as Tzadkiel is [Roy C. Lackey, 2008]

Against

  • Watkins argued that Inire appears to be a Hierodule whose illusions hide only facial features, not a Tzadkiel-style shapeshifter [John Watkins, 2010]
  • Watkins noted that Inire's bent-old-man disguises undercut a Tzadkiel-style shapeshifter reading [John Watkins, 2010]
  • Lackey denied that Inire is a shapeshifter and called the fallen-Hierogrammate reading unsupported by the text [Roy C. Lackey, 2010]
  • Mason argued that Inire belongs to the same species as B and F, so is not a hierarch, perhaps an adopted child of the hierogrammates [Andrew Mason, 2011]
  • Wilson questioned whether Inire can belong to the shape-sharing Hierogrammate race at all [Jeff Wilson, 2008]

Where it stands: Still disputed: the weaker-Tzadkiel version was pressed mainly by b sharp and Lee Berman, most other posters rejected it or the Hierogrammate identification, and the list ended in impasse.
