Bax and his twin George

In The Sorcerer's House, Bax cons his twin George and George's wife Millie, or impersonates George (a Jacob and Esau story); some readers make George the werewolf.

6 threads · 33 posts · for 26 / against 11 · first raised 2010-03-19 by Matthew Keeley
Main voices: Gwern Branwen (9), James Wynn (8), Thomas Bitterman (6), Craig Brewer (4), thalassocrat (4), Dave Tallman (3)

The debate

The idea that Bax impersonates or replaces his twin George was first raised on the list by Matthew Keeley on 2010-03-19, who asked whether George really wrote the last letter and whether Bax is replacing George as writer [Matthew Keeley, 2010]. The same day James Crossley floated an evil-twin idea [James Crossley, 2010]. Within days, Gwern Branwen argued that Bax wrote the final letter while impersonating George [Gwern Branwen, 2010], and thalassocrat argued that Bax planned from the start to lure George to Medicine Hat, kill him and take over his life [thalassocrat, 2010]. Thalassocrat later argued that the replacement needed no faerie [thalassocrat, 2010] and that the plan was made in advance [thalassocrat, 2010].

In April 2010 the theory split into several versions. James Wynn argued that George is the werewolf impersonating Bax, and that the twins are Jacob and Esau types [James Wynn, 2010][James Wynn, 2010]. Dave Tallman recast the story as Bax cheating George out of inheritance and blessing [Dave Tallman, 2010], then argued the twins may be reconciled rather than one killing the other [Dave Tallman, 2010]. Craig Brewer proposed that the whole story is a con by Bax against George and Millie, or a psychological allegory [Craig Brewer, 2010][Craig Brewer, 2010]. Thomas Bitterman argued that Bax forged the corroborating documents and staged the psychic's letter [Thomas Bitterman, 2010], and that the letters never happened and Bax is the Compiler [Thomas Bitterman, 2010]. Gwern Branwen rejected the Jacob and Esau and birthright readings [Gwern Branwen, 2010] and proposed that Bax owes George a debt [Gwern Branwen, 2010].

The discussion repeatedly turned on whether the letters can be trusted. Dave Tallman relied on George's challenge letter as independent evidence and argued that making Bax the compiler would destroy suspension of disbelief [Dave Tallman, 2010]. Gwern Branwen defended the framing story as the most plausible account [Gwern Branwen, 2010]. Later readings included Eugene Zaretskiy's view that Bax planned from the first letter [Eugene Zaretskiy, 2010], Gwern Branwen's 2012 reading of Bax as a plain evil twin who murders his brother [Gwern Branwen, 2012], and Marc Aramini's 2018 claim that the Millie Dunn letter may be a lure for Bax [Marc Aramini, 2018]. The threads ended without agreement.

For

  • Bax's first letter presents George as a source of funds, and the seven-year wait until Bax could be declared legally dead suggests a planned killing [thalassocrat, 2010].
  • The style, sign-off and sudden change of affection in the final letter are cited as signs that Bax wrote it as George [Gwern Branwen, 2010].
  • Directions Bax gives George in the second letter are read as meant to bring George to the house to be murdered [James Wynn, 2010].
  • Bax's earlier jailing for fraud, Millie's visits to a psychic and her jealousy of Doris are cited for a con against George and Millie [Craig Brewer, 2010].
  • Calculated kindness in Bax's early letters is read as concealing a plan to replace George and woo Millie [Eugene Zaretskiy, 2010].

Against

  • George's challenge letter on pp. 261-262 is treated as independent evidence that the supernatural claims are true [Dave Tallman, 2010].
  • Making Bax the compiler would turn the supporting evidence into lies, which Dave Tallman says breaks the reading [Dave Tallman, 2010].
  • The framing story is judged more plausible than the alternatives, so the compiler's account need not be doubted [Gwern Branwen, 2010].
  • It is odd that George would write from inside the house and plan an illegal duel instead of confronting Bax, Thomas Bitterman argues [Thomas Bitterman, 2010].
  • The psychic letter and Doris's postscript are, on Bitterman's reading, easy to forge, so the corroborating evidence is unreliable [Thomas Bitterman, 2010].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the Bax-killed-George reading recurred across six threads and many posts, but the list never reached agreement on whether George is dead, whether Bax compiled the letters, or whether the supernatural events are real.
