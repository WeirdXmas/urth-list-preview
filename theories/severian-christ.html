Severian as a Christ figure

Severian is consciously built as a Christ figure, or as a deliberately flawed or inverted one: torturer turned saviour, resurrected, bringing the New Sun.

160 threads · 294 posts · for 121 / against 175 · first raised 1996-12-07 by Charles Dye
Main voices: Son of Witz (34), David Stockhoff (28), John Watkins (25), James Wynn (18), Lee Berman (18), James Jordan (14)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by Charles Dye in December 1996, who linked Severian's wounds to Graves's King Jesus and Claudius [Charles Dye, 1996]. Through 1997 the debate moved to the Urth list, where James Jordan argued Severian is a Christian figure rather than a Christ figure [James Jordan, 1997], and Michael Straight and Kevin McGuire argued for a lesser replica or a death-and-resurrection reading [Michael Straight, 1997][Kevin McGuire, 1997]. Jordan then reversed course to call Severian a Christ figure without being a Christian [James Jordan, 1997], and the Typhon temptation became the main textual evidence, pressed by Peter Stephenson, Kieran Mullen and Kevin Maroney [Peter Stephenson, 1998][Kieran Mullen, 1999][Kevin J. Maroney, 1999].

From 2002 the argument intensified around Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, who argued Severian's career parallels Christ's from baptism to resurrection [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002], while Adam Stephanides granted parallels but denied literal identity [Adam Stephanides, 2002]. Roy C. Lackey and Chris P disputed the Typhon-as-Satan reading [Roy C. Lackey, 2003][Chris P, 2003], and Andrew Bollen and Matthew Weber argued over whether Severian resembles Christ or St. Peter at Gethsemane [Andrew Bollen, 2003][Matthew Weber, 2003]. In 2004 Danehy-Oakes argued Severian is a typological Christ figure while Chris P proposed Shiva as a model [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2004][Chris P, 2004].

The 2005 to 2014 discussion became more fragmented. Thalassocrat argued Severian is a god rather than a Christ type [thalassocrat, 2005], Son of Witz pushed Severian as Christ and then the label quasi-Christ [Son of Witz, 2009][Son of Witz, 2009], and Marc Aramini argued for an explicitly Judeo-Christian savior against Lee Berman's pagan-god reading [Marc Aramini, 2014][Lee Berman, 2014]. Several posts from 2008 to 2012 treated the Logos and the Son [Son of Witz, 2008][Son of Witz, 2008], and Lackey continued to reject Christ parallels [David Stockhoff, 2009]. The Urth-as-past-iteration question and the absence of Christ on Urth recurred in threads such as "Urth - Earth links" and "eschatology and genesis" without resolution.

For

  • Severian's healing, raising of the dead, water-into-wine and repeated returns from death are cited as Christ parallels [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002][Marc Aramini, 2014][Son of Witz, 2008].
  • The Typhon temptation in the desert replays Christ's temptation, argued by Peter Stephenson, Kieran Mullen and Marc Aramini [Peter Stephenson, 1998][Kieran Mullen, 1999][Marc Aramini, 2012].
  • Severian's forehead stigmata, chest wound and lameness are read as Christ-like wounds, as Jane Delawney argued [Jane Delawney, 2010][Jane Delawney, 2011].
  • Severian as Conciliator is linked to Mark's Jesus reluctant to work miracles, as Adam Stephanides argued [Adam Stephanides, 2002].
  • James Jordan and Son of Witz read Severian as a new Christ or hypostasis of the Logos, with Son of Witz citing his repeated deaths and resurrections [James Jordan, 2002][Son of Witz, 2008].

Against

  • Severian's universe lacks Christ and redemption, since Tzad's remarks and the Key to the Universe passage show no Christ on Urth, as Ori Kowarsky argued [Ori Kowarsky, 1999].
  • Severian's violence, including the killing of the Governess and his fighting at arrest, sets him apart from Christ and aligns him with St. Peter, as Andrew Bollen and Matthew Weber argued [Andrew Bollen, 2003][Matthew Weber, 2003].
  • Typhon's role toward Severian is Satan's role toward Jesus, while Severian's temptation mirrors Christ's, so Severian cannot be part Satan, as Roy Lackey argued [Roy C. Lackey, 2003][Chris P, 2003].
  • Severian is a god who performs miracles by his own power, not a Christ type, as thalassocrat argued [thalassocrat, 2005][thalassocrat, 2010].
  • Severian is a Christian figure, not Christ, since Christ would be the one who redeems, as Jordan and Josh Young argued [James Jordan, 1997][Josh Young, 2006].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never reached consensus on whether Severian is Christ, a Christ figure, a Christian figure, or a pagan or alternative figure, and most threads ended in impasse.

Written by claude-haiku-5-5 from the cards of 90 posts.

Evidence usually cited

  • Resurrections (his own and those he performs), the New Sun as Second Coming, Wolfe's Catholicism.
  • Counter-reading: Severian as reverse Christ or Antichrist (torturer, killer, unrepentant), or as a man who takes part in Christ's work without being a figure for him.
