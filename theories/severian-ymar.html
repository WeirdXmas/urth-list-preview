Severian and Ymar are related

The Autarch Ymar, whom Severian meets in the past, is his ancestor, his clone or himself.

28 threads · 36 posts · for 27 / against 10 · first raised 1998-06-22 by Dan Parmenter
Main voices: Don Doggett (11), Lisa Schaffer-Doggett (7), Marc Aramini (3), Roy C. Lackey (3), Lee Berman (3), thalassocrat (2)

The debate

The earliest post on the list is Dan Parmenter's 1998 proposal [Dan Parmenter, 1998], which argued that Ymar became autarch after Severian's advice; the relation to Severian was not yet the central claim. Dan Rabin's 1999 post [Dan Rabin, 1999] tied Severian's prison scene to Ymar's legitimacy. Don Doggett then proposed in 2002 that Ymar is Severian's original, through Typhon and Herod's bloodline [Don Doggett, 2002], and Marc Aramini questioned the clone idea and asked for internal textual evidence [Marc Aramini, 2002].

The 2003 debate was the main period of argument. Lisa Schaffer-Doggett argued that Typhon is Severian's ancestor through Ymar, citing Graves's The White Goddess and the Frog tale [Lisa Schaffer-Doggett, 2003], and defended genetic relation against Aramini's warning that Graves-based parallels are weak evidence [Marc Aramini, 2003][Lisa Schaffer-Doggett, 2003][Lisa Schaffer-Doggett, 2003]. Later that year she proposed that Ymar was the first Severian, and in September that Severian is Ymar's clone [Lisa Schaffer-Doggett, 2003][Lisa Schaffer-Doggett, 2003]. Chris P rejected the Ymar-clone and same-person readings in 2004, arguing Severian was autarch more than once [Chris P, 2004].

The 2005-2008 posts mostly came from Don Doggett, with pushback from Roy C. Lackey and mournings glory. Doggett proposed Severian as a clone of Ymar and then as the same person as Ymar [Don Doggett, 2005][Don Doggett, 2005]. Lackey argued from Severian's dark hair and pale skin against the clone readings [Roy C. Lackey, 2005], and mournings glory argued the tomb's symbols do not fit Ymar [mournings glory, 2005]. Lackey also argued that Ymar's memories faded before reaching Severian [Roy C. Lackey, 2006], while Doggett argued that Severian's memories may partly belong to Ymar [Don Doggett, 2006]. In 2007 Doggett cited a 1982 interview to argue the assassins at the Secret House were after Severian [Don Doggett, 2007]. In 2011 Lee Berman and Gerry Quinn posted on Ymar as a first version of Severian and on the Frog tale [Lee Berman, 2011][Gerry Quinn, 2011][Lee Berman, 2011], and James Wynn disputed the Ymar-as-last-Autarch reading [James Wynn, 2011]. Wynn and David Stockhoff later took up the Typhon family tree, though Stockhoff argued it will always stay obscure [James Wynn, 2011][David Stockhoff, 2011].

For

  • Severian's memories and situation were read by Don Doggett as partly Ymar's, and Doggett's twin-and-coffin reading linked the tomb to Ymar [Don Doggett, 2006][Don Doggett, 2006].
  • Lisa Schaffer-Doggett argued the Frog story makes Ymar Typhon's son and that the two share too many parallels to be different people [Lisa Schaffer-Doggett, 2003].
  • Schaffer-Doggett argued that Wolfe deliberately blurred whether the first Severian was erased [Lisa Schaffer-Doggett, 2003].
  • Lee Berman argued Ymar parallels the boy Frog, making Frog a little Severian [Lee Berman, 2011].
  • Stockhoff agreed that Typhon's family tree spreads across the Sun books, linking Severian to Typhon and Ymar [David Stockhoff, 2011].

Against

  • Marc Aramini argued that comparing Silk and Severian to Graves's Typhon and Malrubius is risky without evidence Wolfe read Graves [Marc Aramini, 2003].
  • Chris P argued that Severian was autarch more than once and rejected the Ymar-was-Severian and clone readings [Chris P, 2004].
  • Roy C. Lackey argued Severian's dark coloring weighs against Severian being a clone of Typhon or Ymar [Roy C. Lackey, 2005].
  • mournings glory argued that the childhood play-tomb's rose and fountain symbols clash with Ymar's reputation as a stinking, unbathed man [mournings glory, 2005].
  • Lackey argued that Severian's partial memories came from eating Thecla and Appian, not from Ymar [Roy C. Lackey, 2006].

Where it stands: Still disputed and never settled: the theory was raised in 1998 and 2002, argued hardest from 2003 to 2006, and in 2011 Wynn remained doubtful while the thread ended in impasse without consensus.
