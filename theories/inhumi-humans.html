The inhumi are degenerate humans

The inhumi are the remnants of humans who did not advance, or become fully human in dream-travel.

8 threads · 9 posts · for 4 / against 4 · first raised 2009-04-28 by Jordon Flato
Main voices: Marc Aramini (5), Jordon Flato (1), James Wynn (1), Lee Berman (1), David Stockhoff (1)

The debate

The theory that the inhumi are degenerate humans was first raised on the list by Jordon Flato, who in 2009 proposed that a creature on Horn's ship might be a Neighbor or inhumi remnant [Jordon Flato, 2009]. The more developed form came from Marc Aramini, who in 2011 argued that inhumi are surviving humans on a degenerate evolutionary branch [Marc Aramini, 2011] and then that they are the remnants of humans who failed to advance [Marc Aramini, 2011]. James Wynn disputed the dream-travel version in 2010, arguing that an inhumi in dream-travel is fully human and that the Rajan would have met such people [James Wynn, 2010].

In 2012 the idea was tied to Quetzal's tree story: Aramini read men climbing the trees of green as becoming the vanished people, with vines as the inhumi's parasitic ancestors [Marc Aramini, 2012]. David Stockhoff disputed this reading as too complicated and doubted that Wolfe intends an evolutionary metaphor [David Stockhoff, 2012]. Later that year Aramini described the inhumi as vestigial humanity that cannot breed without fresh human blood [Marc Aramini, 2012], and Lee Berman agreed, pointing to the reptilian description against their mammal-like mating [Lee Berman, 2012]. Several threads summarized on the list, including the 'Rajan and Food' and 'Like a Good Neighbor' threads, mostly ended without agreement over how the transformations between trees, vines and inhumi work.

In October 2013 Aramini offered an ontogeny-recapitulates-phylogeny reading of the inhumi's mating ritual, as a debased but vestigial branch shaped by divergent evolution [Marc Aramini, 2013]. The record shows no post that answers this directly, and the theory's support is essentially Aramini's own across the list.

For

  • Aramini argued the Whorl's long orbit around Urth would allow Quetzal to evolve aboard and that inhumi are surviving humans on a degenerate branch [Marc Aramini, 2011].
  • Aramini argued the inhumi are remnants of humans who failed to advance and that their planet-jumping is unnatural for them [Marc Aramini, 2011].
  • Aramini read Quetzal's tree image as an evolutionary account in which men become the vanished people and vine-parasites become the inhumi [Marc Aramini, 2012].
  • Aramini described the inhumi as vestigial humanity that cannot breed without fresh human blood [Marc Aramini, 2012].
  • Berman agreed the inhumi are vestigial humanity, citing their reptilian description against their mammal-like mating and amphibian-style water reproduction [Lee Berman, 2012].

Against

  • Wynn argued that if inhumi were active on Severian's Urth, the Rajan should have met some, since an inhumi in dream-travel is fully human and could bear children [James Wynn, 2010].
  • Stockhoff argued the evolutionary reading of Quetzal's story is too complicated and that the story should be simpler, with a moral like Genesis [David Stockhoff, 2012].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the theory was argued almost entirely by Marc Aramini, with Lee Berman's agreement and Wynn and Stockhoff's objections, and the list never settled the question.
