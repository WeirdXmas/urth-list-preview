The black beans grow into sea monsters

The black beans that grow monstrous plants are the origin of sea monsters like Erebus.

21 threads · 25 posts · for 20 / against 4 · first raised 1997-04-14 in the thread "Jonas & black beans"
Main voices: Jeff Wilson (6), Lee Berman (5), Gerry Quinn (3), C R Culver (2), Jonathan Goodwin (2), Kieran Cleary (1)

The debate

The sea-monster claim first appears on the list in Kieran Cleary's February 1998 post [Kieran Cleary, 1998], which states that the black beans were the origin of sea-dwelling monsters such as Erebus but offers no textual evidence and admits confusion about the Green Room remark. The same week, C R Culver [C R Culver, 1998] read the bean tale as a black hole planted by tyrannical rulers, and Charles Dye [Charles Dye, 1998] linked the beans to Abaia, Erebus and Arioch instead, so the bean story was being read in several ways from the start.

In 1999 to 2001 the debate turned to other readings. Ori Kowarsky [Ori Kowarsky, 1999] tied the beans to embryos and Erebus and Baldanders as Severian's siblings, Tony Ellis [Tony Ellis, 2001] argued the beans concern Erebus's arrival on Urth and are separate from the black hole, and Jeff Wilson [Jeff Wilson, 2001] proposed an inverted Jack and the Beanstalk allusion. In December 2008 the idea was revived by Matthew King [Matthew King, 2008], who quoted Jonas's story and argued the beans were the source of monsters such as Abaia. The same week John Smith [John Smith, 2008] connected the beans to the undines, Dave Tallman [Dave Tallman, 2008] offered a chain of speculation involving Cilinia, and David Stockhoff [David Stockhoff, 2008] doubted that the beans could become seventeen megatherians. Jeff Wilson [Jeff Wilson, 2008] then tied Abaia's arrival to the bean story.

From 2010 onward the sea-monster origin was one option among many. Jonathan Goodwin [Jonathan Goodwin, 2010] asked whether the beans were seeds that grew into the undersea monsters, Lee Berman [Lee Berman, 2010] and Jeff Wilson [Jeff Wilson, 2010] proposed other links to the megatherians and the sun's black hole, and Berman [Lee Berman, 2011] argued that Typhon and the Mandragora were grown from black beans. In September 2013 Jerry Friedman [Jerry Friedman, 2013] raised the sea-monster origin again, recalling Robert Borski's view, and Gerry Quinn [Gerry Quinn, 2013] proposed that the beans were seeds of Erebus and Abaia, while also offering a nanotech alternative.

For

  • Kieran Cleary asserted in 1998 that the black beans were the origin of sea-dwelling monsters like Erebus, without citing textual evidence [Kieran Cleary, 1998]
  • Matthew King quoted Jonas's black bean story and argued the beans were the source of sea monsters such as Abaia [Matthew King, 2008]
  • John Smith linked the undines to the black beans from the stars, reading the tale as a sign of outside visitors [John Smith, 2008]
  • Gerry Quinn proposed that the black beans are seeds that grew into the undersea lords Erebus and Abaia [Gerry Quinn, 2013]
  • Jerry Friedman speculated the beans may have grown into the sea monsters, and recalled Robert Borski holding that view [Jerry Friedman, 2013]

Against

  • Tony Ellis argued the black beans are a separate matter from the black hole and tied them to Erebus's arrival on Urth rather than to the sea monsters' origin [Tony Ellis, 2001]
  • Charles Dye offered an alternative reading linking the beans to Abaia, Erebus and Arioch while not treating them as the origin of the monsters [Charles Dye, 1998]
  • David Stockhoff doubted that the black beans could become seventeen megatherians or indicate both Abaia and a black hole in the sun [David Stockhoff, 2008]
  • Jonathan Goodwin questioned why the bean woman refers to the undersea monsters, asking whether the beans were seeds that grew into them without settling the point [Jonathan Goodwin, 2010]

Where it stands: Never settled: the list raised the sea-monster origin repeatedly from 1998 to 2013, but posters kept offering rival readings of the bean tale, so it remains disputed and was never adopted by the list as a consensus.
