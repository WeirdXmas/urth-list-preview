Tzadkiel appeared in Severian's childhood

Tzadkiel appeared to Severian earlier in disguise (the childhood hero or another figure).

8 threads · 9 posts · for 4 / against 6 · first raised 2010-08-09 by Lee Berman
Main voices: Lee Berman (8), David Stockhoff (1), James Wynn (1)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by Lee Berman in August 2010 [Lee Berman, 2010], who argued that Tzadkiel's words about being a childhood hero referred to Severian's race rather than a disguised Severian acquaintance. David Stockhoff supported the reading the same day, calling the disguise a clue to a larger story spanning universes [David Stockhoff, 2010]. The idea then shifted from a childhood figure toward an acolyte: in October 2011 Berman argued that Hethor's fawning and Tzadkiel's claim of being Severian's acolyte pointed to a hidden identity [Lee Berman, 2011], and James Wynn had earlier proposed Inire as Tzadkiel in disguise [James Wynn, 2011].

Through 2012 to 2015 Berman remained the main proponent, repeatedly tying Tzadkiel to Hethor. In January 2012 he disputed the claim that Tzadkiel's ship caused the eclipse, citing the acolyte remark and the ship's detour to drop Severian on Urth [Lee Berman, 2012]. In December 2013 he proposed that Hethor is a fallen piece of Tzadkiel, the acolyte Tzadkiel named [Lee Berman, 2013], and in September 2014 he speculated that Hethor and Tzadkiel may be one figure, arguing Hethor's attempts on Severian's life could be godhood tests [Lee Berman, 2014]. Marc Aramini objected that Hethor is too debased to be a guardian angel, and Berman answered by citing the fallen-angel tradition of mating with human women [Lee Berman, 2014]. In April 2015 Berman defended the acolyte reading in a discussion of the name Latro [Lee Berman, 2015].

the list's thread summaries describe the discussion as broad but inconclusive. In the 'Tzadkiel's form' thread Mark Millman drew on Wolfe's essay on onomastics, and in the 'Father Inire as Dionysus' thread Gerry Quinn, Sergei Soloviev and Andrew Mason argued that Hethor and Inire are distinct and the evidence is thin. The '' thread had Berman, Jerry Friedman and David Stockhoff push back on textual grounds before drifting into off-topic film chat.

For

  • Tzadkiel said he was a childhood hero and an acolyte of Severian, which Berman and Stockhoff took as signs of a disguise [Lee Berman, 2010] [David Stockhoff, 2010]
  • Hethor's fawning 'Master' talk, his service on Tzadkiel's ship and his disappearance when Severian meets Jonah were cited as evidence that Hethor is Tzadkiel [Lee Berman, 2013] [Lee Berman, 2014]
  • Tzadkiel's many forms on the Ship and the fallen-angel tradition of mating with human women were offered as support for a disguised Tzadkiel on Urth [Lee Berman, 2014]
  • Inire's mirrors and monsters parallel Hethor's, and Berman linked the Father Inire figures to a natural budding process like Tzadkiel's finger [Lee Berman, 2013]
  • Ryan Dunn's time-loop and mirror arguments were used in the thread to support Inire as a non-human form of Tzadkiel

Against

  • Aramini argued Hethor's character is too debased to be a guardian angel and that the Autarch is confused with God [Lee Berman, 2014]
  • Berman's own ship-detour and acolyte-remark reading was disputed by posters who preferred other explanations for the eclipse [Lee Berman, 2012]
  • Gerry Quinn, Sergei Soloviev and Andrew Mason argued in the Inire thread that Hethor and Inire are distinct figures and the evidence is thin [Lee Berman, 2011]
  • John Watkins and others disputed the textual support for Inire as Tzadkiel in the thread
  • Jerry Friedman and David Stockhoff pushed back on textual grounds against the Inire-Tzadkiel link

Where it stands: Still disputed: Berman kept proposing Tzadkiel as a disguised acolyte or Hethor across 2010 to 2015, but the list never reached agreement and most other posters did not adopt the reading.
