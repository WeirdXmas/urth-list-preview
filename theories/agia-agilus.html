Agia and Agilus are twins of one make

The twins Agia and Agilus are more than human siblings (alike by design, able to swap places, or not true twins).

4 threads · 16 posts · for 11 / against 6 · first raised 2010-06-17 by Ryan Dunn
Main voices: David Stockhoff (6), Ryan Dunn (4), David Duffy (2), Jerry Friedman (1), Josh Geller (1), Gerry Quinn (1)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by Ryan Dunn on 2010-06-17 [Ryan Dunn, 2010], who argued that Agia is a robot and that twins share the same make-up, citing a visible ribbon on Agilus's face and the sharp sound of Agia's head striking the wall. David Stockhoff then proposed in the same week that the twins are linked by impersonation and sexual attraction [David Stockhoff, 2010], and later that they are not true twins at all, possibly a chem and a human under false names [David Stockhoff, 2010]. Jerry Friedman disputed the robot reading early on, pointing to blood when Agilus was beheaded and Agia's ability to turn pale [Jerry Friedman, 2010].

The discussion grew most intense on 2010-06-18 and 2010-06-19. Josh Geller and Ryan Dunn supported the robot reading, with Dunn later proposing that Agilus rather than Agia is the robot [Josh Geller, 2010] [Ryan Dunn, 2010]. Stockhoff argued that Agia could be a robot and still call herself Agilus's sister, and then argued she is holy as a Heirodule created directly by the gods, a sacred-incest reading [David Stockhoff, 2010] [David Stockhoff, 2010]. Gerry Quinn proposed that both twins are paracoitae from Hethor's box [Gerry Quinn, 2010], and Jeff Wilson proposed a bred line of made people engendered to be replaced by clones [Jeff Wilson, 2010].

Later posts moved toward ordinary genetics and textual scrutiny. David Duffy argued the twins could be genetically similar given khaibit engineering [David Duffy, 2010], and in 2011 he disputed Ryan Dunn's reading of the head-striking sound, arguing Agia was stunned and that the twins are not ordinary twins [David Duffy, 2011]. Lee Berman linked the twins' engineered twinhood to a purpose in the text [Lee Berman, 2011]. The thread-level summaries show the question was debated repeatedly without consensus, with the 'Mystery of Agia' thread also treating her identity as open [Ryan Dunn, 2010].

For

  • Ryan Dunn argued Agia is a robot, citing the ribbon on Agilus's face, the sharp head-striking sound, and that twins share the same make-up [Ryan Dunn, 2010].
  • Josh Geller argued that robot twins would differ from Jonas, who received human spare parts, because they would have been built as they are [Josh Geller, 2010].
  • Ryan Dunn argued that Agia and Agilus are not human, sensing something too off about them [Ryan Dunn, 2010].
  • Gerry Quinn argued both twins could be paracoitae from Hethor's box, male and female paracoitae looking alike [Gerry Quinn, 2010].
  • David Duffy argued the twins could be genetically identical given khaibit engineering in Urth [David Duffy, 2010].

Against

  • Jerry Friedman argued Agia is not a robot because Severian saw blood when Agilus was beheaded and Agia can turn pale [Jerry Friedman, 2010].
  • Jerry Friedman noted Agilus looks like Agia, so they may be human siblings, and Severian never mentions Hethor's purple eyes [Jerry Friedman, 2010].
  • David Stockhoff argued Agilus and Agia may be false names of a chem and a human, since Agilus bled flesh and blood [David Stockhoff, 2010].
  • David Stockhoff argued Agia can be a robot and still call herself Agilus's sister, answering Mo Holkar's objection about 'the same flesh' [David Stockhoff, 2010].
  • David Duffy argued the head-striking sound shows Agia was stunned and that the twins are not ordinary twins [David Duffy, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never reached consensus, with robot, human, paracoita, holy and genetic-twin readings all left open after repeated discussion across several threads.
