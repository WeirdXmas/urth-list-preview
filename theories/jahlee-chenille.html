Jahlee carries Chenille's soul

The inhuma Jahlee carries Chenille's soul, from her mother's feeding on Chenille.

10 threads · 25 posts · for 17 / against 10 · first raised 2005-08-01 by James Wynn
Main voices: Marc Aramini (9), James Wynn (8), Roy C. Lackey (3), Stuart Hamm (2), thalassocrat (1), Chris P (1)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by James Wynn in August 2005 [James Wynn, 2005], who argued that Kypris, Hyacinth, Mamelta and Silk's mother share one soul and that Jahlee's drunk voice and red hair suggest Chenille's soul. Roy C. Lackey disputed it at once, arguing the Rajan's present-tense descriptions signal nothing about continuous action [Roy C. Lackey, 2005], and James Wynn replied that the Rajan explicitly takes Jahlee's voice for Chenille's [James Wynn, 2005]. Lackey then shifted to a softer version, saying Chenille drank only once yet Jahlee still carries something of her essence [Roy C. Lackey, 2005].

The idea of a blood-drinking parent was taken up by thalassocrat, who proposed that Jahlee's parent drank Chenille's blood and tested the timing against the colonists' departure and Sinew's infancy [thalassocrat, 2005]. In 2009 the theory was revived in the feeding form: Stuart Hamm backed it by pointing to the scene where the Raja takes Jahlee's voice for Chenille's [Stuart Hamm, 2009], James Wynn compared it to Sinew's soul within Krait [James Wynn, 2009], and John Watkins proposed a twin-like copy of soul, making Jahlee effectively Chenille [John Watkins, 2009]. Chris P. disputed that Chenille became an inhumi at all and rejected identifying Krait with Sinew or Jahlee with Chenille [Chris P, 2009].

From 2011 Marc Aramini and António Pedro Marques argued that Jahlee resembles Chenille because she fed on the grieving Chenille after her release from Green [Marc Aramini, 2011][António Pedro Marques, 2011], while Sergei Soloviev disputed that Chenille ever escaped Green and placed her captive at Sinew and Bala's house in Abanja [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011]. Aramini then moved to an offscreen Chenille in Dorp, drunk on rust, as the victim [Marc Aramini, 2011][Marc Aramini, 2011]. Through 2013 and 2014 Aramini, Andrew Mason and Wynn kept advancing variants, with Mason proposing each inhumu's spirit derives from someone its parent fed on [Andrew Mason, 2013], and Aramini claiming Chenille was bled by Jahlee in a Dorp alley [Marc Aramini, 2013][Marc Aramini, 2014]. The discussion never converged; the threads ended with competing source-claims left open.

For

  • Stuart Hamm supported the feeding theory by citing the Raja's mistaking Jahlee's voice for Chenille's [Stuart Hamm, 2009].
  • James Wynn argued that Jahlee's voice and red hair, and the Rajan's description of her, point to Chenille's soul [James Wynn, 2005].
  • James Wynn argued that the Rajan explicitly takes Jahlee's voice for Chenille's and that Jahlee was not trying to mimic her [James Wynn, 2005].
  • Roy C. Lackey argued that Chenille drank only once yet Jahlee carries something of her essence, citing Return to the Whorl and On Blue's Waters [Roy C. Lackey, 2005].
  • Marc Aramini argued that Jahlee resembles Chenille and drinks heavily because she fed on the grieving Chenille, and that the narrator's mistake is a clue [Marc Aramini, 2011][James Wynn, 2011].

Against

  • Roy C. Lackey argued the Rajan's present-tense descriptions of Jahlee signal nothing about continuous action and that Jahlee does not contain Chenille's soul [Roy C. Lackey, 2005].
  • Chris P. argued Chenille is not an inhumi and that Jahlee does not hold Chenille's soul, allowing only a loose sense of Sinew within Krait [Chris P, 2009].
  • Sergei Soloviev argued the text never says Chenille escaped Green and shows her held captive in Abanja [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011].
  • James Wynn disputed Aramini's variant by noting the text gives no sign that inhumi absorb souls [James Wynn, 2009].
  • Marc Aramini's offscreen-Chenille-in-Dorp variant was disputed on the ground that the narrator's remark only compares Jahlee to a drunk Chenille [Marc Aramini, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never reached agreement, and the posters who raised it (Wynn, Aramini, Marques, Hamm) faced steady pushback from Lackey, Chris P. and Soloviev, with the threads ending in competing readings.
