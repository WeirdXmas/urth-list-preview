Juturna is Severian's sister

Juturna, the undine who saves Severian from drowning, is his sister or kin, and her help (water-breathing, saving his child) follows from it.

61 threads · 92 posts · for 72 / against 20 · first raised 1998-01-29 by Michael Andre-Driussi
Main voices: Jeff Wilson (25), Lee Berman (15), Roy C. Lackey (8), Dave Tallman (7), Gerry Quinn (6), Marc Aramini (5)

The debate

The sister theory was first put forward by Stuart Hamm on 2008-07-08, who said Juturna is Severian's sister while admitting he only suspected Father Inire was an inhumu [Stuart Hamm, 2008]. Jeff Wilson quickly took up the idea as a "missing sister" in reply to Russell Wodell about the flood ending [Jeff Wilson, 2008], and b sharp asked him to justify it seriously [b sharp, 2008]. Wilson answered by citing undine coloring, Juturna's inconsistent loyalty, and her passing water-breathing to Severian, which he tied to Severian's underwater time in The Urth of the New Sun [Jeff Wilson, 2008].

The idea shifted over the following years. b sharp offered a rival reading that Juturna, Severian and the undines are all pinched-off versions of Abaia [b sharp, 2008], and Wilson replied that they were probably half-siblings linked by a longevity-through-growth theme [Jeff Wilson, 2008]. Wilson later argued for a twin sister given over to Abaia [Jeff Wilson, 2010], and Lee Berman argued in 2010 that the mutual ardor between Severian and Juturna signals a family incest curse that the New Sun breaks [Lee Berman, 2010]. Wilson proposed Juturna as Severa, citing folklore of stolen children [Jeff Wilson, 2010], and joked that dark hair in a light-haired family implies hidden paternity [Jeff Wilson, 2010]. The colour-based arguments were widely disputed in the thread on hair and names.

In 2011 the theory was contested again. Gerry Quinn called the sister-identification heuristic thin and rejected a sister reading for Merryn [Gerry Quinn, 2011], and David Stockhoff argued that Severian's attraction cannot be assumed, and need not be reciprocated [David Stockhoff, 2011]. Marc Aramini disputed the idea that every woman Severian desires is a relative under an incest curse [Marc Aramini, 2011]. Wilson revived the sister reading, citing water-breathing after the flood as a sign of kinship [Jeff Wilson, 2011], while Quinn argued Juturna is spawned by sea monsters [Gerry Quinn, 2011].

For

  • Stuart Hamm first proposed that Juturna is Severian's sister and mentioned a parallel with Father Inire [Stuart Hamm, 2008].
  • Jeff Wilson argued that undine-hood explains Juturna's coloring and her passing water-breathing to Severian, which supports kinship [Jeff Wilson, 2008].
  • Jeff Wilson argued that a family tie would explain Juturna's double-agent strategy and the strength of her attraction to Severian [Jeff Wilson, 2010].
  • Lee Berman argued that the mutual sexual ardor between Severian and Juturna signals a family incest curse [Lee Berman, 2010].
  • Jeff Wilson argued that the water-breathing after the flood is a sign of kinship with Juturna [Jeff Wilson, 2011].

Against

  • b sharp asked Jeff Wilson for evidence and whether he meant the sister claim seriously [b sharp, 2008].
  • b sharp proposed that Juturna, Severian and the undines are all pinched-off versions of Abaia, a shared origin rather than sibling ties [b sharp, 2008].
  • Gerry Quinn called the sister-identification heuristic thin and rejected reasoning from missing sexual attraction [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • David Stockhoff argued that attraction is not necessarily reciprocated and that consummation seems impossible, challenging the kinship reasoning [David Stockhoff, 2011].
  • Marc Aramini disputed the idea of universal incest, arguing that one incest case does not imply it everywhere [Marc Aramini, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed and never settled: the sister reading was raised repeatedly by Jeff Wilson and others but drew sustained objections, and the final thread on it ended in impasse with Anthony Davis's question about who Juturna is left unanswered.
