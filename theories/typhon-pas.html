Typhon became Pas

Typhon, the two-headed ancient ruler of The Urth of the New Sun, is the same person as the Monarch Typhon who built the Whorl and became its god Pas in the Long Sun.

106 threads · 179 posts · for 87 / against 90 · first raised 1996-12-04 by James Jordan
Main voices: James Wynn (31), Lee Berman (23), David Stockhoff (12), Gerry Quinn (12), Chris P (11), António Pedro Marques (10)

The debate

James Jordan first put the idea in its Christian-allegory form in December 1996, tying Pas to the devil [James Jordan, 1996]. Within weeks Dave Lebling argued that the Long Sun Typhon is Pas, citing the two-headed references and the Exodus dramatis personae entry [Dave Lebling, 1997], and Doug Eigsti added that Scylla in LAKE identifies Daddy as Typhon [Doug Eigsti, 1997]. Ranjit Bhatnagar backed it with the two-headed image and Typhon's grafted-head account [Ranjit Bhatnagar, 1997]. Kevin Maroney later stressed that Lake of the Long Sun is the only explicit textual link between the series [Kevin J. Maroney, 1999]. Early objections came from Michael Andre-Driussi, who pointed to the single appearance of Scylla in the New Sun and to timescale problems [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997], and from Michael Richman, who argued that Pas's benevolence does not fit Typhon's self-regard [Michael Richman, 1997].

From 2000 to 2003 the theory was developed in more elaborate forms. Nicholas Gevers argued that Typhon asset-stripped Urth to build the Whorl and left an abandoned version of himself [Nicholas Gevers, 2000]. Michael Andre-Driussi argued that vanity led Typhon to make Pas two-headed [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2002], while Joe Cilluffo and Roy Lackey proposed that Pas is a digital copy of Typhon or his family kept in Mainframe [Joe Cilluffo, 2002] [Roy C. Lackey, 2002]. Lackey also argued that Typhon in the New Sun and Pas in the Long Sun are distinct characters [Roy C. Lackey, 2002]. James Wynn and Chris P then argued over genetics and Silk's origin in March 2003 [James Jordan, 2003] [Chris P, 2003]. From 2005 the debate turned to clones. Marc Aramini argued that Pas is the genetic child of Typhon and Kypris [Marc Aramini, 2011] and that Typhon becomes Pas [Marc Aramini, 2008]. James Wynn argued that Typhon was rescanned as Pas after taking Piaton's body [James Wynn, 2005]. Chris P and Roy Lackey disputed these readings on textual and motive grounds [Chris P, 2005] [Chris P, 2005] [Roy C. Lackey, 2005].

Later posts largely moved away from a plain identity. Lee Berman tied Pas to Pan, Osiris and Typhon-Set rather than to Typhon himself [Lee Berman, 2012] [Lee Berman, 2014]. Several long threads, including 'Shaving Clones' and 'Clones of the Long Sun', ended in impasse with the clone theory still pressed by Wynn and rejected by others. The identity was never confirmed by the text, and posters kept citing the same few passages.

For

  • Dave Lebling cited the two-headed Pas references and the Exodus dramatis personae entry to argue that the Long Sun Typhon is Pas [Dave Lebling, 1997].
  • Doug Eigsti argued that Scylla's remark in LAKE, that Daddy was Typhon, confirms the identification [Doug Eigsti, 1997].
  • Kevin Maroney noted that Lake of the Long Sun explicitly names Pas as formerly Typhon [Kevin J. Maroney, 1999].
  • Nicholas Gevers grounded the identity in Typhon's taxes in children and his prestige projects, answering how Urth could fund the Whorl [Nicholas Gevers, 2000].
  • Marc Aramini argued that Pas is the genetic child of Typhon and Kypris, citing Lemur's remark and the destroyed embryos [Marc Aramini, 2011].

Against

  • Michael Andre-Driussi cited a textual parallel in the Long Sun and noted that Scylla appears only once in the New Sun, disputing the identity [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997].
  • Michael Richman argued that Pas shows some benevolence while Typhon cares only for himself [Michael Richman, 1997].
  • Roy Lackey argued that the Silk-Pas-Typhon identity makes no sense and that Silkhorn denies being Pas [Roy C. Lackey, 2005].
  • Chris P argued that Quetzal could have known Pas without knowing Typhon, and that Typhon would not have given Pas a different face from his own [Chris P, 2005].
  • Lebling himself argued that the Pas-Typhon timeline is implausibly short [Dave Lebling, 1997].

Where it stands: Still disputed when the record ends: the identity was argued for and against for nearly two decades, with the Lake passage the only explicit link and most later posters treating the rest as unproven or rejecting it.
