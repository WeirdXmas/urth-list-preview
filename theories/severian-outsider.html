Severian is an aspect of the Outsider

Severian (as the New Sun) is a form or aspect of the Outsider of the Long Sun, so the god Silk meets is in part Severian.

33 threads · 37 posts · for 21 / against 10 · first raised 1997-04-06 by Alice Turner
Main voices: Lee Berman (7), Alice Turner (5), James Jordan (4), Andrew Mason (3), Michael Andre-Driussi (2), shannon wilde (2)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the Whorl list in April 1997, when Alice Turner argued from the multiple voices in the Lake passage that the Outsider is Severian [Alice Turner, 1997]. The same week Keith Fields argued from paired voices and Severian's withheld excruciations [Keith Fields, 1997], and Michael Andre-Driussi disputed it with timeline objections [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997]. Turner later developed a fuller version in which Severian becomes a god through successive deaths [Alice Turner, 1997]. A June 1997 set of answers attributed to Wolfe stated that Severian is a form of the Outsider, and posters cited it from then on [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997][Alice Turner, 1997]. Kevin J. Maroney rejected the idea in 1997, arguing that the Long Sun is a composed text in which the Outsider is not Severian [Kevin J. Maroney, 1997].

The idea went quiet on the list for several years and returned in 2000 and 2002. In the Whorl list E Dorian argued from shifting pronouns in the altar scene [E Dorian, 2000], and James Jordan answered that Severian is an agent of the Outsider rather than the Outsider himself [James Jordan, 2000]. Peter Westlake disputed both, saying 'them' refers to the Outsider and the baletiger [Peter Westlake, 2000]. From 2006 onward Urth-list posters returned to the reading. Thomas Gamble proposed it in 2008 [thomas gamble, 2008], and Matthew Keeley offered a tentative reading of the interview remark as Severian being made in the Outsider's image [Matthew Keeley, 2008]. Son of Witz cited the same remark in 2009 [Son of Witz, 2009].

The heaviest discussion came in 2010 to 2012. Lee Berman repeatedly argued that Severian is an aspect of the Outsider, citing mixed parentage and Wolfe's remark [Lee Berman, 2010], and later the androgynous voices of the enlightenment [Lee Berman, 2011]. Andrew Mason read Severian as a Christ figure and a form of the Outsider [Andrew Mason, 2011], while Marc Aramini [Marc Aramini, 2011] and Antonin Scriabin [Antonin Scriabin, 2011] argued that the Outsider is the truer Christ figure. Son of Witz declined to reopen his earlier Christ argument and asserted the Outsider embodiment instead [Son of Witz, 2010]. Mason later proposed that the Oannes passage ties Severian to the Increate [Andrew Mason, 2012]. Berman's 2012 dispute about whether Severian's healing is divine power [Lee Berman, 2012] is among the last posts on the list.

For

  • Alice Turner argued the Outsider's multiple voices in the Lake passage identify it as Severian [Alice Turner, 1997].
  • Posters repeatedly cited a Wolfe-attributed answer that Severian is a form of the Outsider [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997][Son of Witz, 2009][Andrew Mason, 2011].
  • Lee Berman argued Severian is the Theoanthropos, pointing to mixed parentage, unnarrated acts of torture and mercy, and possible multiple copies [Lee Berman, 2010].
  • Berman also argued that the male and female whispers during Severian's enlightenment belong to one androgynous Outsider [Lee Berman, 2011].
  • E Dorian read the shifting 'I', 'we' and 'he' in the New Sun books as evidence that Severian is more than one person [E Dorian, 2000].

Against

  • Michael Andre-Driussi argued that Severian is not the Outsider and raised timeline objections [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997].
  • Kevin J. Maroney argued the Outsider is a burst blood vessel in Silk's head and that the polyphonic enlightenment does not link the Outsider to Severian [Kevin J. Maroney, 1997].
  • Marc Aramini and Antonin Scriabin argued the Outsider is a more apt Christ figure than Severian [Marc Aramini, 2011][Antonin Scriabin, 2011].
  • Peter Westlake argued the 'them' in the altar scene refers to the Outsider and the baletiger, and that Severian speaks of the Increate as a man would of a deity [Peter Westlake, 2000].
  • James Jordan argued Severian is an agent of the Outsider rather than the Outsider himself [James Jordan, 2000].

Where it stands: Still disputed and never settled: the theory was raised repeatedly from 1997 to 2012, the Wolfe-attributed remark was read in different ways, and the posts show no consensus.
