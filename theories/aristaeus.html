The Long Sun follows the myth of Aristaeus

The plot of the Long Sun is mapped onto the life of Aristaeus.

10 threads · 17 posts · for 9 / against 6 · first raised 2009-03-19 by David Stockhoff
Main voices: James Wynn (12), David Stockhoff (2), Marc Aramini (2), Lee Berman (1), Daniel Petersen (1)

The debate

The Aristaeus theory was first raised on the list by David Stockhoff, who on 2009-03-19 argued that Horn corresponds to Actaeon and Silk to Aristaeus and asked James Wynn for a more suggestive source [David Stockhoff, 2009]. Wynn's reply that day widened the claim to Wolfe's Whorl books following Robert Graves's retelling of the myth, with Tussah, Quetzal and Pas mapped onto Apollo, Dionysus and Zeus [James Wynn, 2009]. Stockhoff then noted the correspondence had been plain to him and said he would read Graves [David Stockhoff, 2009].

Over 2010 and 2011 Wynn repeated and extended the claim in many threads, linking Silk and the Rajan to Dionysus and Aristaeus, arguing that Silk is a clone of Typhon built on Aristaeus, and tying Long Sun to Graves's account [James Wynn, 2010] [James Wynn, 2010] [James Wynn, 2011]. Lee Berman proposed that the Neighbors are symbiotes tied to Dionysus and Pan, grounding this in Graves's Aristaeus material [Lee Berman, 2010]. Marc Aramini used the same myth to identify Incus as female and Maytera Corn, a method Wynn defended in November 2011 [Marc Aramini, 2011] [James Wynn, 2011].

Opposition was mostly sceptical rather than a rival mapping. Wynn himself disputed the allegory reading and called Dionysian readings of the Short Sun's bread and wine scenes overt Eucharist [James Wynn, 2010] [James Wynn, 2012]. Marc Aramini's hybridization theory of the inhumi was dismissed by Wynn as too fragile [James Wynn, 2010]. In 2012 Daniel Petersen supported a blend in which the pagan myth is absorbed into the Christ story, citing C.S. Lewis [Daniel Petersen, 2012].

For

  • Stockhoff first tied Horn to Actaeon and Silk to Aristaeus, noting the mapping is broad enough that many gunslinger plots could fit [David Stockhoff, 2009].
  • Wynn argued the Whorl gods follow Graves's Greek Myths, citing Herodotus, Pindar, and Wolfe's recommendation of the book [James Wynn, 2009].
  • Wynn linked Silk and the Rajan to Dionysus and Aristaeus, citing Pan and Priapus material and a claimed Pas-Pan link [James Wynn, 2010] [James Wynn, 2011].
  • Aramini traced Long Sun names such as Rose, Mint and Marble to the Aristaeus myth via Graves [Marc Aramini, 2011].
  • Berman used Graves's Aristaeus material and Pan myths to argue the Neighbors are symbiotes [Lee Berman, 2010].

Against

  • Wynn's claim that Long Sun is an allegory of Greek myth was disputed by himself, who said the parallels did not make it allegory [James Wynn, 2010].
  • Marc Aramini's hybridization theory of the inhumi was called too fragile, since one weak element collapses the whole [James Wynn, 2010].
  • Gerry Quinn objected to the Dionysus link and was answered by Wynn with Lewis's view of Dionysus [James Wynn, 2010].
  • Wynn's Eucharist reading of the Short Sun's bread and wine scenes was offered as an alternative to Petersen's Dionysian reading [James Wynn, 2012].
  • Several posters in later threads, including Gerry Quinn and Jack Smith, objected to outside mythological sources as a method [James Wynn, 2010].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the mapping stayed largely Wynn's position through 2009 to 2012, with a few supporters and critics but no broad agreement, and the list's later threads drifted into other topics.
