Weer is a murderer

Weer committed one or more killings that he never states outright (Lois at the grave, the coldhouse prank); the counter-reading is that he is not a murderer.

32 threads · 44 posts · for 15 / against 22 · first raised 1997-05-03 by Reusch
Main voices: Michael Andre-Driussi (12), Alex David Groce (3), Stone Ox (3), Dave Tallman (3), Reusch (2), Tony Ellis (2)

The debate

The theory first appears on the list when Reusch asked in May 1997 whether Weer murdered Sherry Gold and Margaret Lorn/Price in Peace, while doubting evidence beyond Bobbie Black and the freezer figure [Reusch, 1997]. Michael Andre-Driussi listed the murder suspects and the father's possible death in a hunting accident [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997]. In June 1997 Andre-Driussi reported a Wolfe chat remark that seemed to deny Weer was a mass murderer, and Jason Voegele pressed Wolfe on whether he denied the killings or only the mass murder [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997] [Jason Voegele, 1997]. In 1998 Tony Ellis cited an interview answer that Weer killed no other characters, and Michael Straight replied that the answer only denied many killings, pointing to the boy at the top of the stairs [Tony Ellis, 1998] [Michael Straight, 1998].

From 1998 to 2000 the discussion split between a minimal and a broader reading. Alex David Groce and Roy Lackey limited Weer's plausible culpability to the stairs boy and possibly the frozen man [Alex David Groce, 1999] [Roy C. Lackey, 1999], and Ed van der Winden argued Weer was already dead and repeating his life [Ed van der Winden, 1999]. Christopher Culver argued that Wolfe's tight-lipped reaction to the librarian question implied the idea was not implausible [C R Culver, 1999], and Josh Levitan and Kevin Maroney argued over whether she was killed and whether that counted as murder [Josh Levitan, 2000] [Kevin J. Maroney, 2000]. In late 2000 the coldhouse prank became the main focus: Adam Stephanides argued the text could not settle Weer's guilt, while Michael Straight argued aesthetic coherence favoured Weer as culprit [Adam Stephanides, 2000] [Michael Straight, 2000].

In 2002 the gold-hunt climax in Peace produced a new Lois-at-the-grave version. Andre-Driussi argued the diggers unearth a coffin, which Stone Ox used to support a scenario where Weer shot Lois accidentally and buried her under a porch-step stone [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2002] [Stone Ox, 2002]. Lackey rejected that by pointing to the order in which Lois explains her gun [Roy C. Lackey, 2002]. Later posters returned to the theory in new forms: Dave Tallman argued in 2008 that Weer did not kill Lois [Dave Tallman, 2008], James Wynn argued in 2011 that Weer killed Lois and hid it [James Wynn, 2011], and Eric Bourland in 2012 asked for a full tally of the deaths [Eric Bourland, 2012].

For

  • Reusch doubted only the Bobbie Black and freezer deaths but raised Sherry Gold's murder as a question [Reusch, 1997].
  • Michael Straight argued the boy at the top of the stairs was a character Weer plainly, if accidentally, killed [Michael Straight, 1998].
  • Christopher Culver argued Wolfe left it to readers to decide whether Weer killed the librarian [Christopher Culver, 2000].
  • Stone Ox argued Weer laid the flat stone over Lois's grave, so only he would know it marked a burial [Stone Ox, 2002].
  • James Wynn argued Wolfe deliberately leaves the cover-up unexplained, so readers must work out that Weer killed Lois [James Wynn, 2011].

Against

  • Tony Ellis cited a Wolfe interview answer that Weer killed no other characters in Peace [Tony Ellis, 1998].
  • Alex David Groce argued Weer did not kill anyone in Peace and that the Professor killed Aunt Olivia [Alex David Groce, 1998].
  • Roy Lackey argued Weer's only plausible culpability is the stairs boy and possibly the frozen man, and rejected the mass-murderer idea [Roy C. Lackey, 1999].
  • Michael Andre-Driussi argued the coldhouse prankster was not Weer and that Weer is agitated by it from guilt over Bobby Black [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2000].
  • Roy Lackey argued Weer did not kill Lois accidentally, since Lois explains her gun only after Weer takes it [Roy C. Lackey, 2002].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never reached agreement, and posters split between narrow readings (only the stairs boy, or a librarian/Lois killing) and denials that Weer is a murderer at all.
