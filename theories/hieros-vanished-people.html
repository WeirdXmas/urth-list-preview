The Hieros are the Vanished People

The Hierogrammates or the Hieros are, or are related to, the Vanished People (the Neighbors) of Blue in the Short Sun, linking the two series.

4 threads · 4 posts · for 1 / against 3 · first raised 2011-11-05 in the thread "vanished people=Hieros"
Main voices: Jeff Wilson (2), David Stockhoff (1), James Wynn (1)

The debate

The theory was first raised by Jeff Wilson on 2011-11-06, when he proposed the name Hieros for the vanished people of Wolfe's Long Sun series, defending it against David Stockhoff's suggestion of Yesodis [Jeff Wilson, 2011]. The same day Wilson disputed a proposed doubled-figure reading of the optical illusion puzzle, arguing the two images are not one intended person [Jeff Wilson, 2011]. Stockhoff then argued that the rocket lander in the Green city is an iconic sign tying the vanished people's cities to the Sun Cycle and Hieros, replying to Marc Aramini's objection that the narrator refuses identifications [David Stockhoff, 2011]. James Wynn, on 2011-11-10, argued the vanished people of Green could be Hieros, since a diarist after bodily destruction and orbit might not remark on gravity changes, answering Gerry Quinn [James Wynn, 2011].

the list's thread summaries show the discussion broadened well beyond the four posts. One long thread, begun from a remembered Wolfe interview, had Aramini and Larry Miller raise the interview constraint while Wilson offered sweeping reconciling theories, and Lee Berman and Quinn pushed the Green Man as shackled or staged. Another thread, started by Nick Lee proposing the Green Man as Vertumnus or Inire, saw long disputes between Aramini and António Pedro Marques over whether the City of the Inhumi is Nessus, with Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, Stockhoff and Roy C. Lackey pressing textual objections and Stockhoff offering a cycle-to-cycle theory. A third thread had Wilson argue hierodules come from Yesod and that Inire should count as a Yesodi and probably a hierarch, while Andrew Mason disputed both claims and argued Inire belongs to the same species as B and F.

Across these threads the posters did not reach agreement, and the summaries record entrenched positions, personal sniping and side quarrels. The question of Inire's status and whether the created races should be called Hieros was left unresolved.

For

  • Wilson proposed the name Hieros for the vanished people, comparing it to plural national names like Iraqis and Saudis [Jeff Wilson, 2011]
  • Stockhoff argued the rocket lander in the Green city identifies the vanished people's cities with the Sun Cycle and Hieros [David Stockhoff, 2011]
  • Wynn argued a diarist after bodily destruction and time in orbit might not remark on altered gravity, so the vanished people could be Hieros [James Wynn, 2011]
  • Wilson argued hierodules come from Yesod and spend time in Briah, so Inire should count as a Yesodi and probably a hierarch, per the thread summary for
  • Mason proposed the Hieros were super-evolved humans of a former cycle, with hierodules as non-human beings they shaped, per the thread summary for

Against

  • Wilson argued the two images in the optical illusion do not show the same person, rejecting a doubled-figure reading [Jeff Wilson, 2011]
  • Quinn argued people would notice altered gravity, which Wynn answered by noting weightlessness could make it less salient [James Wynn, 2011]
  • Aramini objected that the narrator refuses identifications, which Stockhoff answered with the Statue of Liberty analogy [David Stockhoff, 2011]
  • Mason disputed that all hierodules come from Yesod and that Inire is a hierarch, arguing he belongs to the same species as B and F, per the thread summary for
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, Stockhoff and Lackey pressed textual objections in the Nick Lee Vertumnus thread, per its thread summary

Where it stands: Still disputed: raised by Wilson in 2011 and debated in several threads with entrenched positions, and the record shows no settled conclusion.
