Marsch was replaced by V.R.T.

In "V.R.T.", the anthropologist John Marsch has been killed and replaced by the aboriginal shapechanger Victor (V.R.T.), who later appears as "Marsch" in the first novella.

77 threads · 131 posts · for 67 / against 79 · first raised 1998-05-30 by Robert Borski
Main voices: Marc Aramini (27), Lee Berman (25), Gerry Quinn (22), Tony Ellis (13), Robert Borski (11), adam louis stephanides (6)

The debate

The theory was first raised on 1998-05-30 by Robert Borski [Robert Borski, 1998], who proposed that the shrike sentence in V.R.T.'s composition book, completed by Marsch's impalement, shows Victor killing Marsch and taking his identity. In the following days Michael Andre-Driussi argued for an April 12 rifle killing [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998], while Adam Stephanides argued for a self-defence killing after April 21 and disputed the cat-following idea [adam louis stephanides, 1998][adam louis stephanides, 1998]. Borski also raised a knife killing and Marsch's homosexuality [Robert Borski, 1998], and Mary Whalen attributed the death to a tiger [mary whalen, 1998].

The debate became more focused after Borski cited Gene Wolfe's Nova Express interview, in which Wolfe reportedly said V.R.T. killed Marsch and assumed his name [Robert Borski, 2000]. Later posters repeatedly returned to that remark. In 2005 Tony Ellis argued that Victor became Marsch, relying on the handwriting match [Tony Ellis, 2005]. In July 2006 Ori Kowarsky proposed a double-blending of Marsch and Victor after a cat bite, which Daniel D Jones and Adam Thornton disputed with rival replacement and bite-order accounts [Ori Kowarsky, 2006][Daniel D Jones, 2006][Adam Thornton, 2006]. In 2007 Adam Stephanides split the April 23 journal entry between Marsch and V.R.T., and Ellis rejected that split [Adam Stephanides, 2007][Tony Ellis, 2007]. Lee Berman later treated Wolfe's interview remark as a slip that favoured replacement [Lee Berman, 2010].

From 2011 onward the argument broadened into debates about Shadow Children and abos as a whole. Gerry Quinn and Ellis argued that the text does not support alien replacement, while Berman and Marc Aramini pressed shadow-child and imitator readings [Gerry Quinn, 2011][Tony Ellis, 2011][Marc Aramini, 2013][Marc Aramini, 2014]. Several threads, including 'Overthinking/Underthinking' and the 2014 Technology Review thread, ended in impasse without shared agreement.

For

  • Wolfe's interview remark, as quoted by Borski, says V.R.T. killed Marsch and assumed his name, which posters cited as decisive support [Robert Borski, 2000].
  • The change in handwriting between the early and later journal entries was taken as evidence of a switch of writer [Robert Borski, 2000][Tony Ellis, 2007].
  • Lee Berman and Marc Aramini read Wolfe's later interview statement that Marsch was replaced by a shadowchild as backing for a shape-shifting replacement [Lee Berman, 2011][Marc Aramini, 2013].
  • Ori Kowarsky and Adam Thornton proposed that the cat's bite on Victor or Marsch explains a merged or replaced identity [Ori Kowarsky, 2006][Adam Thornton, 2006].
  • Tony Ellis argued that the aboriginal mimic can replace Marsch as a human mimic with limited passing ability [Tony Ellis, 2006][Tony Ellis, 2006].

Against

  • Adam Stephanides argued that the text has no Cassilla evidence in two passages and that V.R.T.'s motive is unlikely [adam louis stephanides, 1998].
  • Jerry Friedman noted that Victor's green eyes appear in the journals, so the Marsch-killed-Victor reading would require revised entries [Jerry Friedman, 2002].
  • Ellis noted that characters call him Doctor Marsch before he meets V.R.T., so a replacement would not keep the surname [Tony Ellis, 2000].
  • Ellis argued that the cat does not bite anyone in the story, undermining the bite mechanism [Tony Ellis, 2007].
  • Gerry Quinn argued that the text does not show alien concepts of death or motherhood, and that Lee Berman's shapeshifter reading rests on loose evidence [Gerry Quinn, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never reached consensus, most posters held to their readings, and the Wolfe-interview question remained unresolved.
