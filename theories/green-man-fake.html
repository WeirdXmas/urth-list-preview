The Green Man is a fraud

The Green Man at Saltus is not what he says (a liar, an engineered creature, a fraud as a prophet) rather than a genuine time traveller from a green future.

8 threads · 19 posts · for 4 / against 13 · first raised 1999-06-02 by Peter Stephenson
Main voices: Lee Berman (5), Jerry Friedman (4), Jeff Wilson (3), António Pedro Marques (2), Peter Stephenson (1), Marc Aramini (1)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by Peter Stephenson on 1999-06-02, who argued that the green man is a fraud at predicting personal histories and that no one in the book has genuine supernatural sight [Peter Stephenson, 1999]. The idea lay largely dormant until January 2011, when Lee Berman opened a long debate by arguing that the Green Man is a fake, since internal algae cannot power a warm-blooded moving body and the figure calls himself a liar [Lee Berman, 2011]. Jeff Wilson, Jerry Friedman, António Pedro Marques and Pedro Pereira then argued the biology and physics in both directions, with Wilson suggesting an evergreen-like evolved plant [Jeff Wilson, 2011] and Friedman proposing engineered algae that etiolate [Jerry Friedman, 2011]. Wilson also argued the Green Man is lying about his supernatural nature, citing his admitted lying and his practice of frightening patrons with dire fortunes [Jeff Wilson, 2011].

The debate turned on several points that the posters returned to repeatedly. Gerry Quinn argued that Wolfe is not writing hard SF and that the Green Man never promises rescue, only correct information [Gerry Quinn, 2011]. Berman countered that the Green Man's intact teeth and pagan horned-god name show he is a deceiver and trickster rather than an evolved human [Lee Berman, 2011]. Friedman offered a reading in which the liar remark is a plain fortune-telling joke spoken while the Green Man's foot is trapped [Jerry Friedman, 2011], and he later suggested the Green Man may be a plant put in place by the New Sun's rivals [Jerry Friedman, 2011]. In the Fifth Head of Cerberus thread, Dan'l Danehy-Oakes doubted that any green race exists because the only example is reported by a self-confessed liar [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2011], and Friedman proposed that the claim may be a fabrication or a showman's rumour [Jerry Friedman, 2011].

Later posts moved the theme into wider readings. Jeff Wilson proposed that the Green Man stands for good genetic engineering, set against the bad engineering of the Yesodis [Jeff Wilson, 2011], and Berman later tied him to a plant-animal theme with Pan and Dionysus [Lee Berman, 2014]. Marc Aramini's earlier speculation that the Green Man is a plant-human hybrid descended from the Vanished People was noted but never confirmed by Wolfe [Marc Aramini, 2002].

For

  • Lee Berman argued that internal algae cannot supply the energy a warm-blooded, moving body needs, so the Green Man is a fake [Lee Berman, 2011].
  • Berman cited the Green Man's self-admitted lying, his laugh, and his unfulfilled promise to rescue Severian as signs of a deceiver [Lee Berman, 2011].
  • Jeff Wilson argued the Green Man lies about his supernatural nature, citing his admission of lying to escape a trap and his practice of frightening off patrons with dire fortunes [Jeff Wilson, 2011].
  • Jerry Friedman argued that the Green Man's teeth and digestive make-up are better explained by deliberate genetic engineering and that he may be a planted fake [Jerry Friedman, 2011].
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes questioned whether any green race exists, since the only green man seen is reported by a self-confessed liar [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2011].

Against

  • Gerry Quinn argued Wolfe is not writing hard SF, so energy objections do not apply, and read the laughter as despair at Severian's ignorance [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • Jeff Wilson proposed that a plant could evolve animation by being active only part of the year, so the energy objection does not rule out a natural plant [Jeff Wilson, 2011].
  • António Pedro Marques argued that photosynthesis need not rely only on chlorophyll and that the being could be engineered rather than impossible [António Pedro Marques, 2011].
  • Jerry Friedman offered that the liar remark may be an ordinary fortune-telling disclaimer read in-world, not a hint from Wolfe [Jerry Friedman, 2011].
  • Pedro Pereira argued that Wolfe's books are fantasy rather than hard SF and that readers over-analyse his science [Pedro Pereira, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the thread debates ended without agreement, with posters largely agreeing only that the Green Man's nature and reliability remain unresolved.
