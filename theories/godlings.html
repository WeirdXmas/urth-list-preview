What the godlings are

The godlings were designed as bodies for the gods of Mainframe, or as their messengers.

32 threads · 33 posts · for 20 / against 15 · first raised 2001-02-03 by james.kellar
Main voices: Robert Borski (9), Lee Berman (4), Dave Lebling (3), James Jordan (3), Dan Parmenter (2), Marc Aramini (2)

The debate

The theory that godlings are bodies for Mainframe's gods or their messengers takes shape on the list mostly through the post-2000 Long Sun and Short Sun discussion, though the earliest post, [james.kellar, 2001] by james.kellar, only asks whether the Neighbours sent representatives and links a godling to Return to the Whorl without stating the theory. Robert Borski's posts [Robert Borski, 2001] and [Robert Borski, 2001] argue that Pig is a godling controlled by Mainframe, and Borski's later work on godling size [Robert Borski, 2001] and [Robert Borski, 2001] moves toward treating godling as a generic term for large beings, organic or mechanical. Kevin J. Maroney disputed that broader reading, arguing in [Kevin J. Maroney, 2001] that godling means a constructed servant of Mainframe whatever its body type.

The discussion broadened in the 2001 Whorl threads around gravity and body size. George Terry [George Terry, 2001] and James Jordan [James Jordan, 2001] proposed that weaker gravity at altitude or near the pole could allow giant godlings, while Michael Straight [Michael Straight, 2001] granted the low-gravity point but doubted a bipedal body could scale up. William H. Ansley [William H. Ansley, 2001] argued the godlings are biological beings that grow rather than chems or taluses. Pedro Pereira [Pedro Pereira, 2008] later stated outright that godlings are genetically engineered artificial creatures sent by Mainframe.

The theory was revived in later years by Lee Berman [Lee Berman, 2011], who proposed that godlings are downloaded Mainframe gods in enlarged human bodies, and Gerry Quinn [Gerry Quinn, 2011], who relayed a reading of godlings as messengers who carry instructions and heavy hardware. Quinn later disputed the power of godlings in Return to the Whorl [Gerry Quinn, 2012], and David Stockhoff [David Stockhoff, 2012] challenged calling the Outsider a godling at all.

For

  • Robert Borski argued that Pig is a godling and that all godlings are controlled by Mainframe [Robert Borski, 2001].
  • Michael Straight argued that Pig's godlingness implies biological kinship with the big gods [Michael Straight, 2001].
  • Pedro Pereira argued the godlings are artificial creatures engineered by Mainframe [Pedro Pereira, 2008].
  • Lee Berman proposed that godlings are downloaded Mainframe gods in enlarged human bodies, grounding this in naming patterns and an interview remark [Lee Berman, 2011].
  • Gerry Quinn relayed the reading that godlings are messengers from the gods or Mainframe who deliver instructions and carry hardware [Gerry Quinn, 2011].

Against

  • Kevin J. Maroney argued that godling means a constructed servant of Mainframe regardless of body type, so Pig's small size fits poorly [Kevin J. Maroney, 2001].
  • William H. Ansley argued the godlings are most likely biological beings that grow, rejecting the cargo-technology and talus explanations [William H. Ansley, 2001].
  • Robert Borski's own later posts argued godling is a generic Whorl term for large beings, organic or mechanical, which cuts against a Mainframe-messenger reading [Robert Borski, 2001].
  • James Jordan disputed Borski's claim about the Mother hosting Scylla, noting Scylla is a human pre-adolescent who would not think he killed Silk [James Jordan, 2001].
  • Gerry Quinn disputed the godlings' power in Return to the Whorl, noting the text leaves godling undefined [Gerry Quinn, 2012].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never settled whether godlings are Mainframe's bodies, messengers, constructed servants, or biological beings, and posters kept their own readings through the 2001 threads and the later Berman and Quinn exchanges.
