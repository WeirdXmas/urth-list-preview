A single Wolfean universe

Most of Wolfe's science fiction (and some fantasy) shares one universe and timeline.

23 threads · 25 posts · for 9 / against 13 · first raised 1997-07-01 by Alastair Reynolds
Main voices: Marty Light (3), António Pedro Marques (3), James Jordan (2), Pedro Pereira (2), Milton Jackson (2), Alastair Reynolds (1)

The debate

The earliest post on the list is Alastair Reynolds's 1997 question about whether The Whorl and The Fifth Head of Cerberus share a universe or only a theme [Alastair Reynolds, 1997]. Marty Light's 1997 posts were the first to argue a link between two Wolfe stories, proposing that The Hero as Werewolf and Seven American Nights share a world because of shared lupine characters and a Garou name [Marty Light, 1997][Marty Light, 1997]. The first explicit claim that all of Wolfe's works share one universe came as a tongue-in-cheek extension by James Jordan in 2002, based on Marc's remark about memory at the dawn and end of time [James Jordan, 2002]. Talarican and Jordan extended the link to The Fifth Head of Cerberus and the Long and Short Sun books in 2000 and 2002 [Talarican, 2000][James Jordan, 2002].

The argument recurred through the 2000s. Adam Thornton and b sharp argued that Fifth Head is closely tied to the Short Sun and New Sun books [Adam Thornton, 2006][b sharp, 2006], and Pedro Pereira used a Wolfe interview remark about not remembering how much time has passed to argue that Urth is our Earth [Pedro Pereira, 2008][Pedro Pereira, 2008]. Ryan Dunn tied Briah and Urth to a single cosmic succession [Ryan Dunn, 2010]. Opponents such as Tony Ellis and Jeff Veyera had already denied that the Whorl and Severian's universes were the same, grounding this in Yesod and in the mirror-based travel between worlds [Tony Ellis, 1998][Jeff Veyera, 2003].

In 2010 Sean Ako proposed a single timeline for all of Wolfe's stories, citing coincidences and recurring names [sean ako, 2010]. Milton Jackson supported the idea by comparing Wolfe to Burroughs but then argued that a shared universe would not make Wolfe unoriginal [Milton Jackson, 2010][Milton Jackson, 2010]. James Wynn disputed the link between Severian and Cassie [James Wynn, 2010], and António Pedro Marques argued that an Increate could not be incarnate in several universes [António Pedro Marques, 2010]. Craig Brewer and Gerry Quinn later argued that the double-planet pattern is symbolic or too far apart in time to be placed in one universe [Craig Brewer, 2011][Gerry Quinn, 2011]. Wolfe's own answers, quoted in the Antonin Scriabin thread, denied a literal identity between the worlds, so the question was largely settled on that point.

For

  • Jordan argued in 2002 that Marc's remark about memory at the dawn and end of time hints that the Soldier books share Urth's universe [James Jordan, 2002].
  • Marty Light proposed that The Hero as Werewolf and Seven American Nights share a world, citing shared lupine characters and a Garou name [Marty Light, 1997].
  • Talarican argued that Fifth Head is a much earlier, post-modified part of the universe of The Book of the New Sun [Talarican, 2000].
  • Pedro Pereira argued that Urth is our Earth in the future, based on Wolfe's remark about the passage of time [Pedro Pereira, 2008].
  • Sean Ako proposed that all of Wolfe's stories and novels sit on one timeline, based on recurring coincidences and similar names [sean ako, 2010].

Against

  • Tony Ellis argued that the White Fountain draws its power from another universe, so Severian returns to a different universe from the one he left [Tony Ellis, 1998].
  • Jeff Veyera argued that Tzadkiel crosses universes by mirror-like means while the Whorl stays within one universe [Jeff Veyera, 2003].
  • Craig Brewer argued that the double-planet and blue/green motif is symbolic rather than evidence of a shared physical universe [Craig Brewer, 2011].
  • Gerry Quinn argued that Fifth Head is hard to place in the same universe as the New Sun because of the vast time span [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • Milton Jackson argued that Wolfe would not be unoriginal to keep his works in one shared universe, pointing to the New Sun and Soldier books as examples [Milton Jackson, 2010].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the link was raised repeatedly from 1997 to 2014 but never agreed, and most posters who addressed the whole corpus doubted it, with the discussion ending in impasse and Wolfe's quoted remarks left open.
