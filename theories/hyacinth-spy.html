Hyacinth is a spy for Trivigaunte

Hyacinth is a Trivigauntine spy.

5 threads · 4 posts · for 2 / against 1 · first raised 1997-01-05 by CoxRathvon
Main voices: Dave Lebling (2), CoxRathvon (1), Peter Cash (1)

The debate

The theory that Hyacinth is a Trivigaunte spy was first raised on the list by Peter Cash in January 1997, who proposed it and tied it to her relationship with Silk and her habit of bedding powerful men [Peter Cash, 1997]. Days earlier, CoxRathvon had already entertained Cash's Trivagaunti-spy idea while interpreting the top-of-the-airship conversation between Silk and Horn [CoxRathvon, 1997], so the notion was circulating before Cash spelled it out.

Later in January 1997 Dave Lebling picked up the idea from a different angle, noting that Crane admits Hyacinth worked for him in the submarine, which would make her indirectly a spy for Trivigaunte [Dave Lebling, 1997]. Lebling's next post, in February 1997, disputed other Long Sun theories, including that Hyacinth is possessed by Kypris, but did not revisit the spy claim on the list [Dave Lebling, 1997].

The wider thread '' on Hy as a flawed bride in an allegory of the church and Christ includes Alex David Groce floating her as a Trivigaunti spy leader in Short Sun, though that discussion was mainly about the allegorical reading.

For

  • Peter Cash proposed that Hyacinth was a Trivigaunte spy whose relationship with Silk served covert political activity, citing her bedding powerful men and asking where the black mechanics got their skills [Peter Cash, 1997].
  • CoxRathvon supported the idea that Hyacinth may have been a Trivagaunti spy while reading the Silk-Horn airship conversation [CoxRathvon, 1997].
  • Dave Lebling argued that Crane admits Hyacinth worked for him in the submarine, making her indirectly a Trivigaunte spy [Dave Lebling, 1997].
  • Alex David Groce floated Hyacinth as a Trivigaunti spy leader in Short Sun during the bride-allegory thread.

Against

  • Dave Lebling's February 1997 post disputed other Hyacinth theories, such as her being possessed by Kypris, but the posts record no direct rebuttal of the spy claim [Dave Lebling, 1997].

Where it stands: Raised by a few posters in 1997 and left unresolved, with no record post rejecting it and no later discussion of it beyond a passing mention in a broader thread.
