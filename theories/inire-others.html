Father Inire in other disguises

Father Inire hides behind other characters (Master Ultan, Master Palaemon, Ceryx, Cyriaca's uncle and others).

32 threads · 59 posts · for 39 / against 17 · first raised 1999-04-21 by Dan Rabin
Main voices: Lee Berman (20), b sharp (9), David Stockhoff (7), Ryan Dunn (5), Jeff Wilson (4), Roy C. Lackey (3)

The debate

The idea that Father Inire hides behind other characters was first raised on the list by Dan Rabin, who in 1999 suggested the staff-carrying Ceryx might be a dark counterpart to Inire [Dan Rabin, 1999]. Mitchell A. Bailey followed later that year with a puzzle linking Inire to the artist Fechin through the portrait motif [Mitchell A. Bailey, 1999]. The theory took off in 2006 when b sharp argued that Dorcas' husband, the boatman and Maxellindis' uncle might all be Inire [b sharp, 2006], and that Fechin, Rudesind and Inire are related [b sharp, 2006].

From 2008 onward the argument broadened and hardened. b sharp proposed that Rudesind, Dorcas' husband and Fechin are shape-shifting forms of Inire [b sharp, 2008], and Roy C. Lackey supported this on the basis of the men's similar simian physiques [Roy C. Lackey, 2008]. Jeff Wilson disputed it as unfalsifiable and noted that a dead husband cannot be immortal [Jeff Wilson, 2008] [Jeff Wilson, 2008]. In 2010 Lee Berman became the theory's main proponent, arguing for Fechin, Ultan, Palaemon and Cyriaca's uncle as Inire variants [Lee Berman, 2010] [Lee Berman, 2010]. Opponents such as David Stockhoff, John Watkins and Roy Lackey repeatedly objected that the textual support was thin and that the identifications were too loose [David Stockhoff, 2010] [John Watkins, 2010] [Roy C. Lackey, 2010].

The debate sprawled across many long threads, including 'Father Inire as Dionysus', 'Appearances of Inire' and 'Father Inire Theory', and ended in impasse each time. Candidate identities kept shifting, with Ceryx, Ultan, Fechin, Palaemon, Cyriaca's uncle and Dr. Talos all proposed at different points [Son of Witz, 2010] [Lee Berman, 2011] [Lee Berman, 2011]. Few posts on the list concede the point, and the theory's proponents kept reviving it into the 2010s.

For

  • Dan Rabin tied Ceryx to Inire through the Lexicon Urthus gloss of the name and Severian's sense that Ceryx had travelled similar paths [Dan Rabin, 1999].
  • b sharp cited the uncle's missing description, the boatman's frailty and the 'calls himself' remark to argue Maxellindis' uncle is Inire [b sharp, 2006].
  • Lee Berman pointed to Inire's first description as old, bent, monkey-like and drawn to young girls, plus shared traits such as boats, art and red hair [Lee Berman, 2010].
  • Roy C. Lackey argued that conflicting accounts of the men's lives can all be true if Inire is a Hierogrammate with several selves [Roy C. Lackey, 2008].
  • James Wynn cited reddish furry and monkey signifiers recurring among characters who never share a room with Inire [James Wynn, 2010].

Against

  • Jeff Wilson noted that a dead husband cannot be immortal and that the scrawny Inire could be a remembered, smaller figure [Jeff Wilson, 2008].
  • Jeff Wilson argued Eata kept Maxellindis' uncle under observation on their boat for four or five years, overlapping Inire's service [Jeff Wilson, 2010].
  • John Watkins argued that Inire appears to be a Hierodule whose illusions hide only facial features, with no textual proof of shape-shifting [John Watkins, 2010].
  • Roy C. Lackey noted Fechin's handsome face and tall stature contradict Inire's described smallness [Roy C. Lackey, 2010].
  • David Stockhoff warned that applying the conservation-of-characters principle too broadly would make almost any male character Inire, even Severian [David Stockhoff, 2010].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the theory was raised repeatedly from 1999 to 2018 and was never accepted, with most threads ending in impasse and only its proponents, chiefly Lee Berman and b sharp, continuing to argue for it.
