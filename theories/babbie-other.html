What Babbie is

The hus Babbie is more than an animal (a Neighbor in disguise, the eight-legged creature sent by Mucor, the one who maimed Seawrack).

24 threads · 28 posts · for 18 / against 10 · first raised 1999-09-29 by Robert Borski
Main voices: Robert Borski (10), William H. Ansley (2), James Wynn (2), Dave Lebling (1), Bill Carmichael (1), Nicholas Gevers (1)

The debate

The theory that Babbie is more than an animal first appears on the list in 1999, when Robert Borski relays Alex Groce and Dan Rabin's comparison of Babbie to Circe and Dave Lebling disputes the huss reading by arguing Babbie's arm was mangled and amputated and that Babbie may be a vehicle for Mucor [Robert Borski, 1999][Dave Lebling, 1999]. Bill Carmichael then proposed in November 1999 that Babbie tore off Seawrack's arm during a struggle aboard the boat [Bill Carmichael, 1999], a claim later repeated by Scott Wowra, b sharp, and thalassocrat, who cite blood on the deck and the stump [Scott Wowra, 2009][b sharp, 2009][thalassocrat, 2011].

In August 2000 the discussion centered on the eight-legged creature that accompanies Horn. Nicholas Gevers proposed the Neighbors arranged the transference [Nicholas Gevers, 2000], while Robert Borski argued Babbie is a Neighbor in disguise and that the leaping creature is Babbie [Robert Borski, 2000][Robert Borski, 2000]. Alice Turner disputed this, calling the creature Babbie sent by Mucor and rejecting the Scylla-in-Oreb reading [Alice Turner, 2000], and William Ansley backed the Babbie identification by arguing Babbie was under Mucor's control [William H. Ansley, 2000]. Borski later extended the Neighbor idea into a shapeshifting claim, arguing Babbie and Pig are linked and that Babbie returns to Mucor's island [Robert Borski, 2001][Robert Borski, 2001].

From 2003 to 2012 the theory was contested on the urth list. Marc Aramini and Roy Lackey argued against Horn-in-Babbie readings [Marc Aramini, 2003][Roy C. Lackey, 2011], James Wynn proposed that the Rajan asked Mucor to send Babbie [James Wynn, 2011], and António Pedro Marques and Lee Berman extended the Neighbor and card-deck readings [António Pedro Marques, 2011][Lee Berman, 2011]. The 2012 Seawrack's Arm thread turned on whether Babbie attacked Seawrack or the pirate girl was killed by Horn's gunshot, with David Stockhoff and Mark Millman offering alternatives while Sergei Soloviev defended the textual basis for Babbie's mutilation [David Stockhoff, 2012][Mark Millman, 2012][Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2012].

For

  • Babbie tore off Seawrack's missing arm during a struggle aboard the boat, supported by blood on the deck and Seawrack's gills [Bill Carmichael, 1999].
  • Babbie's arm was mangled and amputated, not bitten off, which Lebling compares to Triskele's amputation [Dave Lebling, 1999].
  • Babbie is a Neighbor in disguise, linked to the sloop, pit and forest scenes by quoted passages [Robert Borski, 2000].
  • Babbie's extra limbs and the Neighbors' hiding inside multi-limbed animals support the host reading [Brian Lovely, 2009].
  • Babbie's guilty behaviour on return and the bleeding traces on the sloop support the mutilation reading [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2012].

Against

  • Alice Turner argued the eight-legged creature is Babbie sent by Mucor and that the Scylla-in-Oreb reading is unnecessary [Alice Turner, 2000].
  • Marc Aramini argued Horn, not Babbie, is the one whose soul moves, given the visible tree sending point [Marc Aramini, 2003].
  • Roy Lackey argued Babbie's spirit-travel body and Silk's ignorance of Babbie before Dorp make a Horn-in-Babbie reading untenable [Roy C. Lackey, 2011].
  • David Stockhoff argued the arm could come from a gunshot, and that Babbie may have attacked the pirate girl Horn shot [David Stockhoff, 2012].
  • Mark Millman proposed the pirate girl's body was used by the Mother to create Seawrack, so Babbie attacks without recognising her [Mark Millman, 2012].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the Babbie-severed-the-arm reading recurs from 1999 to 2012 but was never settled, and the Neighbor and Horn-in-Babbie readings were contested throughout.
