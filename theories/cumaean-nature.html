What the Cumaean is

The Cumaean is more than a sibyl (an inhumu, the Echidna of the Long Sun, a being who appears as snakes or the Old Leech, Merryn's mother).

34 threads · 51 posts · for 32 / against 19 · first raised 1998-01-13 by C R Culver
Main voices: Lee Berman (17), Robert Borski (9), b sharp (6), Gerry Quinn (5), Dan Parmenter (4), David Stockhoff (4)

The debate

The theory first appears on the list as a 1998 question: C R Culver asked whether Madregot helped Merryn and the Cumaean survive Urth's transformation [C R Culver, 1998]. The inhumu idea itself was floated by Alice Turner, who linked the Cumaean to Virgil's guide role and to the Sybil in Eliot's epigraph [Alice Turner, 1998]. Mary whalen replied that the text on page 357 calls the Cumaean a hierodule, which contradicts the proposal [mary whalen, 1998]. Robert Borski then proposed that the Cumaean's true name is Camoena and that she may experience time backwards [Robert Borski, 1998], and that Merryn is a cacogen with a mythic name [Robert Borski, 1998].

In 2000 to 2002 Dan Parmenter proposed that the Cumaean is an Inhuma, while admitting the idea might be obviously wrong [Dan Parmenter, 2000]. Borski argued that Merryn is the daughter of the Cumaean and Father Inire, citing Merryn's trouble with animals and Jahlee passing through a locked door [Robert Borski, 2001]. The 2001 exchanges turned on the word hierodule: Borski said Severian's use of it undercut the inhuma idea [Robert Borski, 2001], and Parmenter answered that Hierodule covers all aliens, citing Dr Talos's term extrasolarians [Dan Parmenter, 2001]. Parmenter later conceded that the word names the aliens from Yesod [Dan Parmenter, 2001]. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes revived the question in 2002, grounding it in the Cumaean's reptilian form and her long age [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002].

From 2006 to 2010 the debate moved to Father Inire and the Cumaean as shapeshifters or fallen angels. b sharp argued their snake-like and monkey-like forms show shapeshifting [b sharp, 2006] and proposed that the Cumaean appears as snakes and as the Old Leech [b sharp, 2008]. Roy Lackey disputed the fallen-angel idea on chronology [Roy C. Lackey, 2008], and David Stockhoff argued the Cumaean is a different kind of being from the Inhumi, citing her unmasked face and lack of wings [David Stockhoff, 2009]. Lee Berman's 2010 Grand Unified Theory placed the Cumaean among pagan gods and fallen angels [Lee Berman, 2010], and Jeff Wilson argued that Inire is a robot while standing figuratively for Severian's mother [Jeff Wilson, 2010]. From 2011 to 2014 Berman argued that the Old Leech is the Cumaean in disguise [Lee Berman, 2012] [Lee Berman, 2012], which Bruno de Albuquerque Furtado disputed on textual grounds, citing the blood bat as a vampire sign [Bruno de Albuquerque Furtado, 2012]. Berman then linked the Cumaean to Echidna and Typhon [Lee Berman, 2011] [Lee Berman, 2013] [Lee Berman, 2014] [Lee Berman, 2014]. Gerry Quinn repeatedly rejected the link, saying the two have different personalities and that snakes and caves are weak clues [Gerry Quinn, 2014] [Gerry Quinn, 2014] [Gerry Quinn, 2014] [Gerry Quinn, 2014].

For

  • Berman linked the Cumaean to Echidna through snake-associated traits, caves, witchcraft and human sacrifice [Lee Berman, 2014].
  • The Cumaean's reptilian form with many faces and reported centuries-long age were cited as grounds for the inhuma idea [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002].
  • b sharp argued that Inire and the Cumaean shift shape rather than wear masks, using their snake-like and monkey-like forms [b sharp, 2006].
  • b sharp proposed the Cumaean appears as snakes and as the Old Leech, citing the roof seance in the Stone Town [b sharp, 2008].
  • Parmenter argued that Hierodule covers all alien beings, including the Inhumi, citing Dr Talos's term extrasolarians [Dan Parmenter, 2001].

Against

  • Mary whalen cited page 357 of The Urth of the New Sun, where the Cumaean is explicitly called a hierodule [mary whalen, 1998].
  • Borski pointed to Severian calling the Cumaean a hierodule as undercutting the inhuma idea [Robert Borski, 2001].
  • Stockhoff noted the Cumaean's unmasked face and lack of wings, arguing she is not an Inhumu [David Stockhoff, 2009].
  • Quinn argued the Cumaean and Echidna have different personalities and that shared snakes, caves and witchcraft are too weak to identify one character with the other [Gerry Quinn, 2014] [Gerry Quinn, 2014].
  • Bruno de Albuquerque Furtado argued that the Old Leech's blood focus and the blood bat at the ziggurat are vampire signs that conflict with a Cumaean identity [Bruno de Albuquerque Furtado, 2012].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the theory was raised repeatedly from 1998 to 2014 and never gained consensus, and most posters who engaged with the later Echidna and Old Leech versions rejected them.
