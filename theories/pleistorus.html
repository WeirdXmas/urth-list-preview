Pleistorus is Ahura Mazda

The Thracian god Pleistorus is Ahura Mazda in disguise.

7 threads · 8 posts · for 8 / against 0 · first raised 2003-05-10 by Marc Aramini
Main voices: Marc Aramini (8)

The debate

The theory was first raised on 2003-05-10 by Marc Aramini, who argued that Latro in Soldier of Arete parallels Heracles and linked Ares to Zoroastrianism through Pleistorus as Ahura Mazda, while conceding Latro cannot literally be Heracles [Marc Aramini, 2003]. Over 2006 he extended it: Latro might be a trial incarnation of Pleistorus testing human life before Christ, tied to an Ares-possession explanation of the amnesia [Marc Aramini, 2006], and then Pleistorus was framed as Ahura Mazda incognito, a god changed by human experience who incarnates among Rome's Jews, with bets on later Latro-book events [Marc Aramini, 2006]. He admitted the link rested on a line or two in Soldier of Arete and compared it to Wolfe's hint-dropping [Marc Aramini, 2006].

In 2011 Aramini returned to the idea in several posts. He proposed Latro is Ares or Pleistorus trapped in flesh, perhaps Ahura Mazda incognito, citing Latro's Olympic success and Sisyphus' boulder [Marc Aramini, 2011]. He also argued Pleistorus is a battle god, perhaps the Thracian form of Ares, and answered António Pedro Marques's point about Ares as a god of fields by suggesting the Soldier books may not distinguish Ares from Pleistorus [Marc Aramini, 2011]. Later that year he tied the idea to his wider claim that identity puzzles are central to Wolfe's work, responding to Sergei Soloviev [Marc Aramini, 2011]. In 2013 he argued the gods are real and that Pleistorus merges into Ahura Mazda, dismissing Christopher Simon's head-injury explanation [Marc Aramini, 2013].

Across the threads listed on the list, the idea drew the most pushback where readers doubted the premise that gods can change. Jeff Wilson argued in the 'changing the immutable gods' thread that a mortal body cannot carry a god's altered character, and Chris warned that the idea was being treated as settled. In the Hegesistratus thread, Chris offered the alternatives of Ares possessing Latro or Latro merely resembling Pleistorus, while Stanislaus argued for Ares possession. James Wynn suggested Dionysus instead of Ares. The posts record these exchanges only in thread summaries, with no post ids, and none of them reached agreement.

For

  • Latro parallels Heracles, citing Pindaros calling him Heracles and his finishing Sisyphus' task, and Pleistorus is linked to Ahura Mazda [Marc Aramini, 2003]
  • Ares possession or Pleistorus incarnation could explain Latro's amnesia, depending on how long Pleistorus was absent [Marc Aramini, 2006]
  • Gods can change through human experience, so Pleistorus can become Ahura Mazda incognito, and Aramini bet on later Latro-book events [Marc Aramini, 2006]
  • Following small hints such as the tree and Eleanor Bold can reveal unifying themes in Wolfe, as with the Pleistorus link [Marc Aramini, 2006]
  • Pleistorus is a battle god, perhaps the Thracian form of Ares, and the text makes him the missing figure who is Ahura Mazda [Marc Aramini, 2011]

Against

  • Jeff Wilson argued in the 'changing the immutable gods' thread that a god's altered character cannot be carried by a mortal body, and Aramini's reply that gods can change is recorded at [Marc Aramini, 2006]
  • Marques's remark that Ares was originally a god of fields was answered by Aramini, who suggested the distinction may not apply in the Soldier books [Marc Aramini, 2011]
  • Soloviev's remark about Cas and Dorcas prompted Aramini's broader claim that identity puzzles drive the plot, which the thread disputed [Marc Aramini, 2011]
  • Christopher Simon argued that Latro's head trauma could explain the gods, a reading Aramini dismissed as superficial [Marc Aramini, 2013]
  • Chris warned that a reader had moved from speculating about the Ahuramazda identity to treating it as settled, noting characters voice ideas built on misunderstandings, as recorded in the 'changing the immutable gods' thread [Marc Aramini, 2006]

Where it stands: Still disputed: raised repeatedly by Marc Aramini from 2003 to 2013 with no agreement from other posters, and the thread discussions ended in impasse or drifted to other topics.
