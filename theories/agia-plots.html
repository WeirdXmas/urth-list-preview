Agia plotted against Severian all along

Agia's pursuit of Severian is part of a larger plan (she planted the Claw, belongs to a conspiracy against the New Sun, or later tries to kill Valeria).

21 threads · 44 posts · for 24 / against 17 · first raised 1998-10-11 by Robert Borski
Main voices: Lee Berman (9), David Stockhoff (6), Jeff Wilson (5), b sharp (4), Robert Borski (3), Son of Witz (3)

The debate

The theory was first raised on 11 October 1998, when Robert Borski proposed that Agia placed the Claw in Severian's sabertache after the altar crash [Robert Borski, 1998]. Charles Dye then argued that she could have slipped it in while carrying him [Charles Dye, 1998], and later proposed that she was after his keys instead [Charles Dye, 1998]. Alex David Groce backed the Claw-search reading without new textual evidence [Alex David Groce, 1998]. Michael Andre-Driussi offered a version in which Agia knowingly plants the Claw and then changes her plan [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998]. Mark Millman objected that the 'Where did you get it?' test cannot be passed by any physical answer, such as planting [Mark Millman, 1998].

In 2006 the thread shifted to a sorcerer-cult reading. Macronaut linked Agia and Agilus to a cult opposed to the New Sun through the Jurupari design [Macronaut, 2006]. b sharp argued that Agia's lucivee and cry of sacrilege tie her to the jungle sorcerers [b sharp, 2006][b sharp, 2006], and suggested the Secret House assassin might be her [b sharp, 2006]. Roy C. Lackey disputed this, saying Severian sees the attacker's face and that the weapon was a batardeau knife [Roy C. Lackey, 2006].

In 2010 the discussion centred on whether Agia was a robot, an assassin or part of a mystery cult. Cliff Judge floated that she carried the Claw as an unwitting tool of divine will [Cliff Judge, 2010]. Jeff Wilson disputed the cult and halo readings as backlight and dust [Jeff Wilson, 2010], and Ryan Dunn argued she was more than woman or assassin [Ryan Dunn, 2010]. David Stockhoff proposed she was made as an assassin [David Stockhoff, 2010], while Jerry Friedman argued her hostility came from Thecla's anger, Agilus's death and Severian's survival [Jerry Friedman, 2010]. Jeff Wilson proposed that Agia and Agilus were anti-New Sun conspirators [Jeff Wilson, 2010].

In 2011 Lee Berman argued that the assassin who kills Valeria is Agia, citing the poisoned blades and the stab wound [Lee Berman, 2011], and later proposed she poisons Valeria as revenge for Agilus [Lee Berman, 2011]. Son of Witz argued that her pursuit of Severian goes beyond revenge [Son of Witz, 2011], and António Pedro Marques said the revenge explanation fails because Severian was innocent of the killing [António Pedro Marques, 2011]. Jeff Wilson replied that Agia spares Severian because she lacks means for prolonged revenge and that Vodalus planted the torture idea [Jeff Wilson, 2011]. David Stockhoff questioned whether the Valeria theory explains anything beyond tying up loose threads [David Stockhoff, 2011]. Berman's further posts argued that Inire controls events and that Agia killed Valeria to avenge Agilus [Lee Berman, 2011], and in 2013 he linked the twins to an anti-New Sun cult [Lee Berman, 2013].

For

  • Agia could have slipped the Claw into Severian's sabertache while carrying him after the altar crash [Robert Borski, 1998][Charles Dye, 1998]
  • Agia's lucivee matches the sorcerers' jungle saddle weapon, and her crooked dagger and athame tie her to the cult [b sharp, 2006][b sharp, 2006]
  • Agia's cry of sacrilege and devil-marked coin link her to the cult and jungle sorcerers [b sharp, 2006]
  • The poisoned blades, the throne-room sequence and the stab wound mirroring Severian's avern wound suggest Agia is Valeria's assassin [Lee Berman, 2011][Lee Berman, 2011]
  • Hethor's similarly intense fixation on Severian suggests Agia's motive goes beyond revenge [Son of Witz, 2011]

Against

  • Famulimus's test is a question rather than a literal query, so no physical answer such as planting the Claw could pass it [Mark Millman, 1998]
  • Severian sees the assassin's face and the attacker carried a batardeau knife, so there is no link to Agia [Roy C. Lackey, 2006]
  • Agia lacks the means for prolonged revenge and spares Severian because she is impulsive, with Vodalus planting the torture idea [Jeff Wilson, 2011]
  • The aureole and dust readings are backlight and mist, not a cult sign [Jeff Wilson, 2010]
  • Agia's hostility to Severian is explained by Thecla's anger, Agilus's death and Severian's survival, without a larger plan [Jerry Friedman, 2010]

Where it stands: Still disputed: the planted-Claw reading was raised in 1998 and passed without consensus, and the later cult, assassin and Valeria readings were pressed repeatedly by a few posters but disputed by others, so the list never settled it.
