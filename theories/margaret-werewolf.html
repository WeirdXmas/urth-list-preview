Margaret Briggs is a werewolf

Margaret Briggs is a werewolf (and a spy).

12 threads · 25 posts · for 24 / against 5 · first raised 2008-10-16 by Henry Eissler
Main voices: Dave Tallman (16), Roy C. Lackey (3), thalassocrat (2), Stephen Hoy (2), Charles Tallman (2), Henry Eissler (1)

The debate

The earliest post on the list on this theory is by Henry Eissler on 2008-10-16, who links Margaret Briggs to a banshee washer figure rather than to a werewolf [Henry Eissler, 2008]. The werewolf claim itself first appears in Dave Tallman's post of 2008-10-18, which cites her grey appearance, colorless face, bobbing walk and early knowledge of Jimmy Warshawsky's death [Dave Tallman, 2008]. Tallman then added the initials M.B., her misreported name and her role in the killings, and by 2008-10-25 he was calling her a werewolf and double agent [Dave Tallman, 2008] [Dave Tallman, 2008].

In January 2009 the theory became more elaborate. Tallman argued that Margaret is Cassie travelling back in time as a werewolf, and that she was the shooter who killed Chase [Dave Tallman, 2009] [Dave Tallman, 2009]. Tallman also argued that Chase's werewolf speech points to Margaret, and that she is Cassie's twin soul [Dave Tallman, 2009]. Roy C. Lackey disputed that Chase would have kept Margaret around [Roy C. Lackey, 2009], while Tom Foster supported the werewolf reading with the man leading the wolf on a leash [Tom Foster, 2009]. Thalassocrat had earlier proposed a clone reading [thalassocrat, 2008], and Stephen Hoy argued in April 2009 that Margaret is a bain-sidh and possibly a werewolf, based on her seamstress and laundress self-description [Stephen Hoy, 2009].

From September 2009 Tallman, Lackey and Charles Tallman traded the werewolf and time-travel readings. Charles Tallman argued Margaret is future Cassie [Charles Tallman, 2009], while Tallman argued she can shape-shift and that Margaret is a werewolf [Dave Tallman, 2009] [Dave Tallman, 2009]. Lackey countered that no werewolf was demonstrable, then argued Margaret is a werewolf and a future Cassie by pointing to parallels with Al and her eating sandwiches [Dave Tallman, 2009] [Roy C. Lackey, 2009]. Gwern Branwen's March 2010 post went through the evidence point by point and offered innocent explanations, and it is the last post on the list on the theory [Gwern Branwen, 2010].

For

  • Tallman cites her grey clothing, colorless face, bobbing walk and the initials M.B. as signs she is a werewolf [Dave Tallman, 2008] [Dave Tallman, 2008]
  • Tallman notes she knew of Warshawsky's death early and that her misreported name fits a disguise [Dave Tallman, 2008] [Dave Tallman, 2008]
  • Tallman argues Chase's werewolf speech at the Silent Woman points to Margaret [Dave Tallman, 2009]
  • Lackey argues Margaret is a werewolf and a future Cassie, citing werewolf traits on p. 206 and her bobbing that echoes Ebony's wake [Roy C. Lackey, 2009]
  • Stockhoff notes werewolves in the story could heal wounds by rearranging their cells, so Margaret could have been the wounded shooter [David Stockhoff, 2009]

Against

  • Gwern Branwen argues each clue has an innocent explanation, including her churchly prudence, her singing and the sandwich timing [Gwern Branwen, 2010]
  • Gwern notes that Chase's attacker was described as a large man, which does not fit Margaret [Gwern Branwen, 2010]
  • Lackey argues Reis's sources would have checked Margaret's background and that Chase removed her as a spy, not as a werewolf [Roy C. Lackey, 2009]
  • Tallman reports Lackey's claim that no werewolf was demonstrable in the text, which Tallman answered by citing the book's descriptions [Dave Tallman, 2009]

Where it stands: Still disputed: the theory was never settled, Gwern's 2010 rebuttal stands against the werewolf reading, and the posters split without consensus.
