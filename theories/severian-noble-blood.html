Severian has noble or divine blood

Severian's parents (or ancestors) were exultants, armigers, aliens or something more than human, like the hidden parentage of mythic heroes.

21 threads · 23 posts · for 13 / against 7 · first raised 1997-04-13 by Alice Turner
Main voices: Lee Berman (11), Alice Turner (1), C R Culver (1), urth-errors (1), Dave Lebling (1), Roy C. Lackey (1)

The debate

The theory that Severian has noble or divine blood was first raised on the list by Alice Turner in 1997, who cast him as a mortal who becomes a demigod through successive deaths [Alice Turner, 1997]. The lineage version took shape in January 1998, when C R Culver and urth-errors asked who Severian's father was and proposed that his mother Katherine was a captured Exultant, with the father possibly an Exultant too, based on Severian's height [C R Culver, 1998][urth-errors, 1998]. Dave Lebling then weighed Clute's idea that the old Autarch is Catherine, citing Severian's height as a hint of a tall mother [Dave Lebling, 1998].

The discussion grew in 2006 to 2010. Roy C. Lackey reconstructed a family tree around an exultant or khaibit-linked Catherine [Roy C. Lackey, 2006], and Dave Tallman proposed that his mother was an exultant of the House Absolute who fled to the past [Dave Tallman, 2008]. John Smith and Jack Smith turned the height argument into an armiger or exultant-mother claim, with Jack Smith adding a dying-sun nutrition explanation [John Smith, 2008][Jack Smith, 2010]. Opposition came from Mark Millman, who held that Wolfe states the exultant blood outright, and from David Wells, who argued the exultants are not truly of this Urth and that Severian was made common to fit his role [Mark Millman, 2008][David Wells, 2008]. Lee Berman then pressed an alien or divine lineage across many posts, including a hidden House of Typhon versus House of Severian structure [Lee Berman, 2010][Lee Berman, 2010].

Later posts shifted from lineage to the demigod pattern. Lee Berman linked Severian to Heracles, Perseus and Theseus and argued he is an immortal demi-god [Lee Berman, 2013][Lee Berman, 2014], while John Watkins and Marc Aramini disputed the Increate or alien readings [John Watkins, 2009][Marc Aramini, 2010]. Several threads, including "Sev's common lineage", "Ouen's Catherine" and "The Maids of Contessa Carina", ended in impasse with rival Catherine identifications unresolved [Roy C. Lackey, 2006][Dave Tallman, 2008].

For

  • Severian's height is taken as evidence of an exultant or armiger mother, which Culver, Lebling and Jack Smith cite [C R Culver, 1998][Dave Lebling, 1998][Jack Smith, 2010].
  • Mark Millman argues Wolfe states exultant blood outright, so no hidden source needs to be sought [Mark Millman, 2008].
  • Lee Berman argues the mythic hero pattern of hidden divine or royal parentage fits Severian [Lee Berman, 2010][Lee Berman, 2013].
  • Dave Tallman argues the maids of Contessa Carina mark the woman on the bridge as Catherine, an exultant [Dave Tallman, 2008].
  • Lee Berman argues Baldanders, like Severian, appeared without known parentage, so special ancestry is needed for such figures [Lee Berman, 2010].

Against

  • David Wells argues the exultants are not truly of this Urth and that Severian was made common to fit his role [David Wells, 2008].
  • John Watkins argues Severian is a demi-god linked to the sun but not an incarnation of the Increate, rejecting the divine-lineage reading [John Watkins, 2009].
  • Marc Aramini argues that at Yesod Zak a part of Tzadkiel stands in and bears the cross for Severian [Marc Aramini, 2010].
  • Roy C. Lackey raised textual gaps, such as Nessus's drowning and Inire's absence, that a Catherine identification would need to explain [Roy C. Lackey, 2006].
  • In the Lamarck thread, Jeff Wilson argued that Commonwealth nutrition stunts stature, undercutting the height argument [Jack Smith, 2010].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never reached consensus on Severian's parentage, and the lineage threads ended in impasse with most posters unpersuaded.
