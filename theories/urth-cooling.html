Why Urth is cold and dying

Urth's condition (a cooling core, no magnetic field) comes from human exploitation as well as the dying sun.

11 threads · 16 posts · for 2 / against 12 · first raised 1998-02-06 by Jason Voegele
Main voices: Jeff Wilson (5), Mitchell A. Bailey (4), Jason Voegele (1), C R Culver (1), Peter Westlake (1), Greene, Carlton (1)

The debate

The theory first appears on the list on 1999-03-31, when Mitchell A. Bailey proposed that geothermal exploitation accelerated Urth's cooling, arguing from petrified strata at Casdoe's cliff and from the forgotten history and legends of the books that Urth lies millions of years in the future [Mitchell A. Bailey, 1999]. The idea then went quiet on the list until 2006, when the 'Urth geochronology' thread began with Jeffrey Lefstin arguing that Urth must be far older than the text's 'perhaps a million years'. Macronaut replied that intelligent beings could have accelerated the cooling, using the dying sun as a parallel for human action producing natural-seeming effects [Macronaut, 2006]. Jeff Wilson answered that the cooling is not natural at all, since the Great Beast Erebus lives in a volcanic mountain and drains Urth's energy, and he attributed the cooling model to 19th-century books [Jeff Wilson, 2006].

In December 2008 the theory returned through the 'House Absolute' thread. Jordon Flato asked whether others recalled a passage saying ancient peoples used up Urth's core energies, so that they were exhausted rather than fading [Jordon Flato, 2008]. Jeff Wilson disputed the premise that the core is cooling, citing the size of the mountains of Abaia and Erebus and suggesting that Ultan and Palaemon took outdated theories from library books [Jeff Wilson, 2008]. Tim Walters clarified that his point concerned textual evidence for human-caused core cooling, and he rejected Wilson's use of petrification timescales, saying one sediment layer proves nothing about a whole cliff [Tim Walters, 2008]. Wilson then rejected core cooling from any cause and argued that the dimming of the old Sun and environmental damage explain the surface cooling without core evidence [Jeff Wilson, 2008].

In December 2010 Wilson revived his geothermal reading in the 'lupine crisis of faith' thread, arguing that Erebus and his periscii already draw on Urth's geothermal energy and that the Urth scientists wrongly assumed the internal fires had died [Jeff Wilson, 2010]. Across the record, the main supporters of human causation were Bailey, Macronaut, Flato and Walters, who mostly argued from the text and from analogy, while Wilson was the most persistent opponent, arguing from the Erebus passages, library sources and the surface-cooling explanation.

For

  • Bailey argued in 1999 that geothermal exploitation accelerated Urth's cooling, citing petrified strata and the legends of the Historians [Mitchell A. Bailey, 1999].
  • Macronaut argued that intelligent beings could have accelerated the cooling, using the dying sun as a parallel for human action [Macronaut, 2006].
  • Flato asked whether a passage says ancient peoples exhausted Urth's core energies, linking this to a sun dying by design rather than by decline [Jordon Flato, 2008].
  • Walters argued that the text gives evidence for human-caused cooling of Urth's core, and clarified that his point was not about the civilization's ignorance [Tim Walters, 2008].

Against

  • Wilson argued that Urth's apparent geological inactivity is not natural cooling, because Erebus drains energy from a volcanic mountain, and the cooling model came from 19th-century books [Jeff Wilson, 2006].
  • Wilson argued that the mountains of Abaia and Erebus are too large to be natural and suggested tidal and geothermal power, while doubting that the core is cooling [Jeff Wilson, 2008].
  • Wilson argued that there is no core cooling from any cause and that the dimming of the old Sun and environmental damage explain the surface cooling [Jeff Wilson, 2008].
  • Wilson argued that Erebus and his periscii already tap Urth's geothermal energy, and that the internal fires had not died as the Urth scientists assumed [Jeff Wilson, 2010].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the theory was raised once in 1999, then revived in the 2006 and 2008 threads, where Macronaut, Flato and Walters supported human causation and Wilson consistently rejected core cooling, and the list never settled the question.
