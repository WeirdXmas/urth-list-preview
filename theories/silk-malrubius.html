Silk is Malrubius

The ghost of Malrubius who appears to Severian is Silk (or Horn) astral-travelling.

49 threads · 69 posts · for 40 / against 31 · first raised 1998-10-16 in the thread "Malrubius"
Main voices: Lisa Schaffer-Doggett (7), Marc Aramini (6), Michael Andre-Driussi (5), Andrew Bollen (5), thalassocrat (5), Dave Tallman (5)

The debate

The theory that the ghost of Malrubius is Silk (or Horn) astral-travelling was first floated on the list by Christopher Culver in 2000 as a reading of Severian's visions, but the earliest explicit identification came from Ian Smith in 2001 ( is a whorl post, so the first Malrubius-as-Horn/Silk question is Ian Smith's [Ian Smith, 2001]), with BMeyer7 asking the same [BMeyer7, 2001] and William Ansley claiming Wolfe had confirmed it [William H. Ansley, 2001]. Nicholas Gevers disputed this at once, arguing the aquastor Malrubius in The Book of the New Sun contradicts it [Nicholas Gevers, 2001]. Craig Brewer raised a Wolfe interview remark linking Silk to Master Malrubius in 2002 [Craig Brewer, 2002], and Culver and Michael Andre-Driussi took opposite sides on whether Severian's visions were really Silk [Christopher Culver, 2002][Michael Andre-Driussi, 2002].

The 2003 discussion on the Urth list was the main period of debate. Marc Aramini and Chris P argued for the Silk-astral reading, pointing to Wolfe's SFBC catalog comments about the secret of Malrubius' ghost [Marc Aramini, 2003][Chris P, 2003], while Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, maa32 and Roy Lackey rejected it on the basis of the Hieros aquastor explanation, the hierodules who maintain Malrubius's image, and Silk's character [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2003][Marc Aramini, 2003][Roy C. Lackey, 2003]. Lisa Schaffer-Doggett and Andrew Bollen tried intermediate positions, such as Silk as Malrubius through time travel or a disguise used on Gurloes [Lisa Schaffer-Doggett, 2003][Andrew Bollen, 2003]. Later Dave Tallman revived the claim in 2010, arguing that Severian's memories shape the aquastor Malrubius and that his promise to write nothing about Silk/Horn is a lie [Dave Tallman, 2010][Dave Tallman, 2010]. Peter Sun Rogers extended it in 2013 to a single shared soul or archetype [Peter Sun Rogers, 2013].

A long run of posts in 2011 by Larry Miller, Nick Lee and António Pedro Marques turned on Wolfe's Gevers interview answer, 'Yes but not a leading part', which posters read in different ways [Larry Miller, 2011][Nick Lee, 2011][António Pedro Marques, 2011]. The list never reached agreement, and most threads ended in impasse.

For

  • Silk astral-travelling was offered as the reading of 'Malrubius' ghost' in 2003 by Chris P, who treated it as a euphemism [Chris P, 2003].
  • Marc Aramini cited Wolfe's SFBC catalog remark urging readers to discover the secret of Malrubius' ghost as evidence against a circumstantial reading [Marc Aramini, 2003].
  • Dave Tallman argued in 2010 that Silk/Horn is identified as Malrubius in The Book of the New Sun and that Severian's memories shape the aquastor [Dave Tallman, 2010][David Stockhoff, 2010].
  • Dave Tallman argued that the astral Silk looks like the real Master Malrubius, so Severian mistakes him for the dead man's ghost [Dave Tallman, 2010].
  • Nick Lee argued the only plausible appearance of Silk or Horn as Malrubius's ghost is on page 77 of The Shadow of the Torturer [Nick Lee, 2011].

Against

  • Nicholas Gevers argued that Horn's astral self being Malrubius's ghost contradicts what The Book of the New Sun says about the aquastor Malrubius [Nicholas Gevers, 2001].
  • Michael Andre-Driussi argued the Silk-astral reading causes too much trouble and the timeline of Silk's visit to the dog conflicts with Malrubius dying years earlier [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2002][Michael Andre-Driussi, 2003].
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued the ghost was not meant to be Silk, and that a retcon is bad art unless intratextual evidence shows a lie [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2003].
  • Roy C. Lackey argued Malrubius was a torturer-guild Master who maimed and killed, while Silk's nature opposes such acts [Roy C. Lackey, 2003].
  • Lisa Schaffer-Doggett noted that Silk's appearance as Malrubius in Return to the Whorl predates the aquastor, and that the hierodules maintain Malrubius's image [Lisa Schaffer-Doggett, 2003][Lisa Schaffer-Doggett, 2003].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never reached consensus, most threads ended in impasse, and the theory was raised repeatedly from 2001 to 2013 without being settled.
