The prisoner in the next cell is V.R.T.'s mother

The woman in the cell next to V.R.T.'s is his mother.

7 threads · 7 posts · for 6 / against 1 · first raised 1998-05-27 by Tony Ellis
Main voices: Robert Borski (3), Tony Ellis (2), Alice Turner (1), Michael Andre-Driussi (1), Gerry Quinn (1)

The debate

The theory that the prisoner in the next cell is V.R.T.'s mother was first raised in May 1998 by Tony Ellis, who argued that the female prisoner is his mother and rejected Robert Borski's suggestion that the lady in pink is Celestine Etienne, on the grounds that she is too young to be David's mother [Tony Ellis, 1998]. Ellis based the claim on the prisoner's illiteracy and petty theft, V.R.T.'s belief that his mother is alive and returns to town in winter, and the Roncevaux remark [Tony Ellis, 1998].

Borski responded the next day with a different mother theory, proposing that V.R.T.'s absentee mother is a shape-changing figure named Three Faces, and he also suggested the woman in the next cell could be V.R.T.'s mother instead of David's [Robert Borski, 1998]. Within a day Ellis returned to defend the idea, citing the letter's description of an illiterate woman, the tapping madman mentioned five times, and the Annese talking drums [Tony Ellis, 1998]. Borski then raised objections about how V.R.T.'s mother could have reached Sainte Croix and passed customs, while still backing the idea [Robert Borski, 1998].

In June 1998 Alice Turner said she believed the woman in the next cell is likely his mother, while limiting her objection to the Aunt Jeanine theory rather than to V.R.T.'s half-abo state [Alice Turner, 1998]. Later discussion took the idea in other directions: Michael Andre-Driussi in November 2000 proposed that the prisoner is the hero of Marsch's "A Story" in veiled autobiographical form [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2000], and in 2013 Gerry Quinn questioned whether the novel settles V.R.T.'s parentage at all [Gerry Quinn, 2013]. The posts record no post that rejects the mother identification outright.

For

  • Ellis argued the prisoner is V.R.T.'s mother based on her illiteracy, petty theft, and V.R.T.'s belief that his mother returns in winter [Tony Ellis, 1998]
  • Ellis cited the illiterate Annese thief from Port-Mimizon, the tapping madman, and the Annese talking drums as support [Tony Ellis, 1998]
  • Borski backed the identification and offered recalculated Croixian ages to argue Celestine Etienne is David's mother instead [Robert Borski, 1998]
  • Turner said she believes the woman in the next cell is likely V.R.T.'s mother [Alice Turner, 1998]

Against

  • Borski questioned how V.R.T.'s mother could have reached Sainte Croix and passed customs [Robert Borski, 1998]
  • Borski proposed a different mother, Three Faces, a shape-changing Aboriginal woman, as a rival identification [Robert Borski, 1998]
  • Gerry Quinn argued the novel does not settle V.R.T.'s parentage, which weakens any single mother identification [Gerry Quinn, 2013]

Where it stands: Raised in 1998 and supported by a few posters, but with no settled consensus; the record shows it was argued briefly and then largely left aside, with later posts moving to other readings.
