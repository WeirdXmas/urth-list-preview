The Mother is Scylla (or an undine)

The sea monster called the Mother is Scylla (of the Whorl or of Urth) or an undine, and the black pirate ship is her creation.

36 threads · 46 posts · for 32 / against 7 · first raised 1998-06-14 by J. Schultz
Main voices: Robert Borski (10), Lee Berman (9), James Wynn (4), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (3), Alex David Groce (2), Alice Turner (2)

The debate

The theory was first raised on 14 June 1998 by J. Schultz, who proposed that the creature in the buried town is an ancient evil and that Scylla of the Whorl and Scylla of Urth are the same figure [J. Schultz, 1998]. The idea became a Long Sun question in 1999, when Alice Turner floated Seawrack's mother as an undine [Alice Turner, 1999] and Alex David Groce proposed the Mother as an undine-maker like Erebus or Abaia [Alex David Groce, 1999]. In August 2000 Robert Borski proposed that the Mother is Typhon's daughter Scylla [Robert Borski, 2000], then that she is Scylla III, a recarnified copy of Scylla II [Robert Borski, 2000]. Alice Turner later dropped the undine version [Alice Turner, 2000].

From 2001 to 2002 the debate split between Borski's alien-family reading and sceptics. Adam Stephanides suggested a telepathic link between Scylla-in-Oreb and the Mother as an alternative [Adam Stephanides, 2001], and Dan'l Danehy-Oakes disputed the reading of Oreb's 'Find god' line [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2001]. James Jordan objected that Scylla is a human pre-adolescent who could not be hosted by the Mother [James Jordan, 2001]. Michael Straight proposed in 2002 that the Mother and Scylla are both undines [Michael Straight, 2002].

In 2010 and 2011 James Wynn and Lee Berman argued over whether the Mother is the megatherian Scylla [James Wynn, 2010] [Lee Berman, 2010] [James Wynn, 2010]. Berman later argued that the ambiguity about which sea being is which is deliberate [Lee Berman, 2010]. Daniel Petersen and Lee Berman then read the Mother and Great Scylla as Lovecraftian leviathans [Lee Berman, 2011] [Daniel Petersen, 2011], while Andrew Mason argued they were sisters [Andrew Mason, 2011] and James Wynn argued they might be one being [James Wynn, 2011]. From 2012 the focus moved to the black pirate ship: Berman argued it is the Mother herself [Lee Berman, 2012], David Stockhoff supported that reading while noting the shooting scene cuts against it [David Stockhoff, 2012], and Mark Millman proposed the pirate girl's body was remade by the Mother as Seawrack [Mark Millman, 2012].

The 2009 posts by b sharp first put forward the pirate-ship link [b sharp, 2009] [b sharp, 2009]. The theory was taken up again in 2013 and 2014 [Lee Berman, 2013] [Lee Berman, 2014], and most threads, including those on Seawrack, sea monsters and Inhumi, ended without a settled reading.

For

  • Scylla of the Whorl is the same as Scylla of Urth, the girl in Jonas's inset tale, according to the 1998 proposal [J. Schultz, 1998].
  • Horn's comparisons of the Mother to Scylla, and the red imagery in Incanto's dream, were cited for identifying the Mother as Typhon's daughter Scylla [Robert Borski, 2000].
  • The glossary entry and Scylla's meaning 'she who rends' were cited for the claim that the Mother is Scylla III [Robert Borski, 2000].
  • Seawrack's descriptions of the enormous underwater Mother were used to argue she is the same being as Scylla [Robert Borski, 2000].
  • The black pirate ship is the Mother herself, linked to Great Scylla, Abaia and Erebus, according to Lee Berman [Lee Berman, 2012].

Against

  • Scylla is a very human pre-adolescent, and Pig recognizes the Narrator as Silk, so the Mother cannot host Scylla [James Jordan, 2001].
  • Danehy-Oakes argued that Oreb's 'Find god' line does not support Scylla seeking the Mother [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2001].
  • Stephanides offered a telepathic-contact explanation as an alternative to a direct identity [Adam Stephanides, 2001].
  • Lee Berman argued that the ambiguity about the sea beings is deliberate and not a sign of a single identity [Lee Berman, 2010].
  • David Stockhoff noted that the shooting scene cuts against the pirate ship being the Mother [David Stockhoff, 2012].

Where it stands: Still disputed and never settled: the theory was raised repeatedly over 1998 to 2014 in many variants, but the list did not reach agreement on whether the Mother is Scylla, an undine, or the pirate ship.
