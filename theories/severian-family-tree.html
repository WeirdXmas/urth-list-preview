Severian's family tree

Severian's lineage can be reconstructed from clues scattered through the books, connecting him to Dorcas, Ouen, Catherine, the Autarchs and others; an umbrella for the more specific family theories.

97 threads · 125 posts · for 69 / against 54 · first raised 1997-06-30 in the thread "Severian's family"
Main voices: Lee Berman (39), b sharp (13), Marc Aramini (7), Andrew Mason (6), David Stockhoff (6), James Wynn (5)

The debate

The family-tree idea was first raised in 1997, when Michael Andre-Driussi proposed naming conventions for Severian's upper-class family and linked names such as Thea, Thecla and Valeria to etymology [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997]. In 1998 the theory widened: J. Schultz argued Severian might be his own ancestor through the Conciliator's tomb [J. Schultz, 1998], and Michael Straight suggested Typhon regards Severian as the Conciliator's descendant [Michael Straight, 1998]. The same year mary whalen and Tony Ellis argued over whether Severian's descendants are all men on Urth or his children by Apheta [mary whalen, 1998][Tony Ellis, 1998].

From 1999 to 2005 posters tied the family to other characters. Robert Borski linked Becan's father with Severian's family and proposed Dorcas was pregnant [Robert Borski, 1999], while Mitchell A. Bailey explored time-loop readings with no ancestry [Mitchell A. Bailey, 1999]. Don Doggett and Lisa Schaffer-Doggett argued for Typhon and Ymar as ancestors [Don Doggett, 2002][Lisa Schaffer-Doggett, 2003], and Marc Aramini and Roy C. Lackey disputed the genetic readings, citing the risk of relying on Robert Graves and the text [Marc Aramini, 2003][Roy C. Lackey, 2003]. In 2005 James Wynn defended Severian's crypt as Typhon's family mausoleum, but mournings glory disputed it using the coffin counts and the bronze door [James Wynn, 2005][mournings glory, 2005].

From 2006 b sharp drove a long run of family-tree proposals, including pagan-god matings, Father Inire linked to Abaia, and Valeria dropped from the tree [b sharp, 2006][b sharp, 2006][b sharp, 2006]. Roy C. Lackey questioned whether Thecla is related to Severian through the genealogical folio, later shifting to Thea [Roy C. Lackey, 2006][Roy C. Lackey, 2006]. In 2008 thalassocrat argued the lineage explains Severian's silence and family crime, and that it fits the ages without time travel [thalassocrat, 2008][thalassocrat, 2008], which b sharp disputed [b sharp, 2008]. From 2010 Lee Berman made the most sustained case for hidden kinship, proposing Juturna, Jolenta and Thecla as relatives and an incest curse, in threads that Gerry Quinn and Son of Witz disputed [Lee Berman, 2010][Gerry Quinn, 2011][Son of Witz, 2011]. In 2013 Michael Thayer's clone theory and David Stockhoff's responses about the mausoleum and Valeria's family followed [David Stockhoff, 2013][Jeff Wilson, 2013], and in 2014 Stockhoff argued any lineage theory must sit inside a Catholic frame [David Stockhoff, 2014].

For

  • Severian's family names and clues in the text suggest a deliberate pattern, as Andre-Driussi argued [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997].
  • Typhon's family and Ymar are proposed as ancestors, supported by Graves parallels and the Autarchs as placeholders [Don Doggett, 2002][Lisa Schaffer-Doggett, 2003].
  • James Wynn argued the Severian crypt is Typhon's family mausoleum, citing the scenes of finding it [James Wynn, 2005].
  • b sharp's family tree ties Valeria, Casdoe, Thecla and Inire into incest and curse readings, with Plan B as a looser fallback [b sharp, 2006][b sharp, 2006].
  • thalassocrat argued the lineage fits the ages and explains theft and silence in the family [thalassocrat, 2008][thalassocrat, 2008].

Against

  • mary whalen and Tony Ellis disputed each other's readings of who the descendants are, and Ellis's textual case rests on the priest passage [mary whalen, 1998][Tony Ellis, 1998].
  • Marc Aramini warned that comparing Silk to Typhon through Graves rests on no evidence Wolfe read it [Marc Aramini, 2003].
  • mournings glory argued the crypt differs from Typhon's mausoleum by coffin counts, Cilinia's grave and the door symbols [mournings glory, 2005].
  • Gerry Quinn argued the spread of Severian's lovers across classes and places makes close kinship improbable [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • David Stockhoff disputed the time-dislocated mausoleum and the clone theory on its location and mechanics [David Stockhoff, 2013][Jeff Wilson, 2013].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the family-tree readings never reached consensus, most threads ended in impasse, and several proposals were revised or dropped by their own authors.
