Silk's mother is Mamelta (or Kypris)

Silk's biological mother is Mamelta, the sleeper from the ship, or Kypris herself (or another woman), which explains his origin as a "bio" child.

17 threads · 30 posts · for 18 / against 14 · first raised 1997-02-05 by Michael Andre-Driussi
Main voices: Marc Aramini (15), Andrew Mason (5), Gerry Quinn (5), James Wynn (2), Michael Andre-Driussi (1), Alice Turner (1)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list on 1997-02-05, when Michael Andre-Driussi proposed in [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997] that Silk's ghostpike visions are staged from his own mind, with Kypris and Hyacinth as possible stand-ins for his parents. Alice Turner disputed Kypris as his mother in [Alice Turner, 1997], noting that Silk fails to recognise the blonde as Kypris. The idea stayed dormant for years and was then revived as the list turned to close reading of the Long Sun books. In 2005 James Wynn in [James Wynn, 2005] and [James Wynn, 2005] argued instead for a Typhon clone and for Mamelta as the biological mother, while Malcolm McGugan in [Malcolm McGugan, 2005] argued from Litany passages that Mamelta is Kypris and Silk's mother.

From 2008 Marc Aramini became the main proponent, posting repeatedly that Silk's blue eyes point to a blue-eyed mother such as Kypris, Mamelta or Mamelta-as-Kypris, as in [Marc Aramini, 2008] [Marc Aramini, 2008] and [Marc Aramini, 2010]. In December 2010 Gerry Quinn in [Gerry Quinn, 2010] and Andrew Mason in [Andrew Mason, 2010] disputed the identification on grounds of Mamelta's ordinary background, and Aramini answered in [Marc Aramini, 2010] by conceding that Typhon's powers over sleepers could explain the mismatch. In January and February 2011 Roy C. Lackey in [Roy C. Lackey, 2011] and Gerry Quinn in [Gerry Quinn, 2011] challenged the blue-eyed evidence and the four-parent reading, while Andrew Mason in [Andrew Mason, 2011] proposed that Mamelta could be the mother without being Kypris, with Typhon as father by in vitro fertilisation.

Later threads kept the theory alive. Aramini restated it in [Marc Aramini, 2011] in October 2011 and [Marc Aramini, 2013] in November 2013, and in several threads he argued it against Quinn, Lackey, James Wynn and António Pedro Marques, who mostly relied on naturalistic readings of the dreams. The discussion never reached agreement, and many threads ended with the parties restating their positions.

For

  • Silk's blue eyes, and the rarity of blue eyes in the Whorl, are cited as a link to a blue-eyed mother such as Kypris or Mamelta [Marc Aramini, 2008][Marc Aramini, 2010]
  • Dream sequences and Litany passages are read as equating Mamelta, Kypris, Hyacinth and Mother, supporting an identification [Marc Aramini, 2011][Marc Aramini, 2010][Marc Aramini, 2013]
  • Litany passages linking Kypris with Hyacinth and Mother are presented as textual support [Malcolm McGugan, 2005][Marc Aramini, 2013]
  • Four parents named in the text, with Silk recognising them, are cited as showing the parentage question matters [Marc Aramini, 2011]
  • Silk's need to hide his identity from the Ayuntamiento is argued to follow from Kypris being his genetic mother [Ashley Crill, 2011]

Against

  • Mamelta's technician life and ordinary past do not fit Kypris's likely left-behind history, according to Andrew Mason and Gerry Quinn [Andrew Mason, 2010][Gerry Quinn, 2010]
  • Blue eyes are common in Viron, and the Calde text ties Kypris to Mamelta and Hyacinth but not to Chenille, according to Roy C. Lackey [Roy C. Lackey, 2011]
  • Silk's visions do not identify Kypris as his mother, and the four parents are two adoptive and two biological, according to Gerry Quinn [Gerry Quinn, 2011]
  • Mamelta could be Silk's mother without being Kypris, with Typhon as father by in vitro fertilisation, argued by Andrew Mason [Andrew Mason, 2011]
  • Mamelta's memories and motives are unclear, and the sealed-chamber reasoning is weak, argued by Gerry Quinn [Gerry Quinn, 2011][Gerry Quinn, 2010]

Where it stands: Still disputed and never settled: Marc Aramini kept proposing it from 2008 to 2013, while Gerry Quinn, Roy C. Lackey and Andrew Mason repeatedly rejected it, and no consensus emerged.
