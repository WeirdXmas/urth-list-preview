Severian is Apu-Punchau

The figure Severian and Dorcas see at the stone town (Apu-Punchau) is Severian himself, visiting from his own future, which is why the apparition seems to know him.

50 threads · 65 posts · for 39 / against 20 · first raised 1997-05-18 by Dan Rabin
Main voices: Michael Andre-Driussi (7), Son of Witz (6), Lee Berman (6), David Stockhoff (5), Marc Aramini (3), James Wynn (3)

The debate

The theory that Severian is Apu-Punchau, the apparition at the stone town, first appears on the list in 1997, when Michael Andre-Driussi proposed that Apu-Punchau is Severian in another time [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997], and he kept returning to it with a second Severian and sliver-selves [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997][Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998]. In January 1998 C R Culver argued Apu-Punchau was an earlier iteration of Severian [C R Culver, 1998], while Dave Lebling countered that Apu-Punchau is Severian's own future self [Dave Lebling, 1998], a split that frames much of what follows.

The question came back repeatedly over the following decade, with many posters offering variants. In 2003 Marc Aramini and Lisa Schaffer-Doggett traded positions on whether Severian is the original Apu-Punchau or whether an earlier failed Severian, possibly Ymar, was [Marc Aramini, 2003][Lisa Schaffer-Doggett, 2003]. In 2008 the Apu-Punchau eclipse debate drew in Eric Ortlund and b sharp, who argued Severian's body or subconscious caused it, and Matthew Groves and Roy Lackey, who favoured the Ship [Eric Ortlund, 2008][b sharp, 2008][Matthew Groves, 2008]. Son of Witz later argued Apu Punchau is a symbol of the Old Sun and that Severian becomes him through a time loop [Son of Witz, 2008].

From 2010 onward posters increasingly tied the theory to parallel timelines, the Conciliator and the black hole. Jeff Wilson argued that Severian is the Conciliator and Apu Punchau [Jeff Wilson, 2010], while John Watkins argued a single time-travelling Severian explains the tomb and Apu Punchau [John Watkins, 2010]. James Wynn disputed that the clues made the time travel derivable [James Wynn, 2009]. Lee Berman and Bruno de Albuquerque Furtado proposed Severian as one of Apu Punchau's incarnations or as the sun-god's inspiration [Lee Berman, 2012][Bruno de Albuquerque Furtado, 2012], while Dave Tallman proposed the Hierogrammates staged the eclipse to save him [Dave Tallman, 2008].

For

  • Severian's time travel and resurrections make him a plausible source for a future-self identity for Apu-Punchau [Jeff Wilson, 2010][Ryan Dunn, 2011]
  • Andre-Driussi's 1997 reading links Apu-Punchau to a repeated Severian through the time loop, and Dave Lebling argued the same future-self mechanism [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997][Dave Lebling, 1998]
  • Lee Berman argued that Severian and Apu Punchau share agency in the time travel and resurrection of Lake of Birds [Lee Berman, 2012]
  • Ryan Dunn suggested Inire as a time-travelling twin who could have allowed Severian to become Apu Punchau [Ryan Dunn, 2011]
  • Marc Aramini cited the Cumaean's vivimancer description of Apu Punchau as support for Severian being the original Apu-Punchau [Marc Aramini, 2003]

Against

  • Dave Lebling argued Apu-Punchau is Severian's future self rather than an earlier iteration, disputing C R Culver [Dave Lebling, 1998][C R Culver, 1998]
  • Lisa Schaffer-Doggett argued the Cumaen sent Apu-Punchau into the future and that Severian did not cause the miracle [Lisa Schaffer-Doggett, 2003][Lisa Schaffer-Doggett, 2003]
  • Roy Lackey argued the eclipse was staged by the Ship and that a Severian-caused eclipse was unlikely [Eric Ortlund, 2008][Roy C. Lackey, 2010]
  • Matthew Groves argued Severian's body or shadow cannot cast the required darkness [Matthew Groves, 2008]
  • James Wynn argued the clues did not make Severian's time travel derivable [James Wynn, 2009][James Wynn, 2011]

Where it stands: Still disputed: the posts record many competing variants (earlier iteration, future self, sliver, aquastor merger, Old Sun symbol) and the threads end without consensus.
