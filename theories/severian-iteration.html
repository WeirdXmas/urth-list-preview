Severian lives his life more than once

The Severian of the books is a second or later iteration of a Severian whose first life went differently, and the books show traces of the earlier version.

71 threads · 87 posts · for 55 / against 27 · first raised 1997-07-15 by Michael Andre-Driussi
Main voices: Son of Witz (7), Ryan Dunn (7), Michael Andre-Driussi (6), David Stockhoff (6), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (5), Roy C. Lackey (5)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by Michael Andre-Driussi in July 1997, who linked Apu Punchau and a second Severian to identity-hijacking motifs [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997]. In January 1998 C R Culver argued that Apu-Punchau was Severian of an earlier iteration of Briah, pointing to The Citadel of the Autarch [C R Culver, 1998], and then disputed that a New Sun was brought in the earlier iteration [C R Culver, 1998]. Dave Lebling proposed in May 1998 that Severian draws his power from the White Fountain because he created it in his past [Dave Lebling, 1998], while Dan'l Danehy-Oakes proposed labeling resurrected Severians Sev2.1, Sev2.2 and so on [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 1998].

The discussion grew most intense in 2003 and from 2006 to 2010. In July 2003 Michael Andre-Driussi argued the Claw is a fragment of the first Severian [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2003], Lisa Schaffer-Doggett supported a separate Ymar as the first Severian [Lisa Schaffer-Doggett, 2003], and James Jordan argued the Claw is derived from the present Severian alone [James Jordan, 2003]. In 2004 Roy C. Lackey and Chris P disputed whether Severian was autarch more than once [Roy C. Lackey, 2004][Chris P, 2004]. From 2008 Son of Witz and David Stockhoff argued over whether Severian is the First Severian reshaped by time travelers [Son of Witz, 2008][David Stockhoff, 2008], and Jordon Flato and Son of Witz developed amphisbaena and Conciliator loop readings [Jordon Flato, 2008][Jordon Flato, 2008]. In 2010 Ryan Dunn and Dave Tallman extended the idea to Inire, Rudesind and Silk/Malrubius [Ryan Dunn, 2010][Dave Tallman, 2010].

Opponents repeatedly argued that the first Severian failed or passed the Yesod test, or that he was replaced rather than preserved. Tony Ellis argued the first Severian passed [Tony Ellis, 2007], while Joel Sieh and Roy C. Lackey argued he failed [Joel Sieh, 2007][Roy C. Lackey, 2006]. Critics such as John Watkins and b sharp rejected the multiple-Severian reading as too simple, preferring finite lives tied to five coffins [b sharp, 2008][John Watkins, 2010]. Later threads on the Short Sun and clones, such as Jerry Friedman's argument that the Short Sun Severian is a different iteration [Jerry Friedman, 2011], and the Five Severians thread, ended with the puzzle unresolved.

For

  • Michael Andre-Driussi proposed in 1997 that Apu Punchau is a second Severian and linked this to identity-hijacking motifs [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997].
  • C R Culver argued Apu-Punchau was Severian of an earlier iteration of Briah, citing Citadel chapter XXXVIII [C R Culver, 1998].
  • Michael Andre-Driussi argued the Claw is a fragmentary presence of the first Severian [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2003].
  • Son of Witz and David Stockhoff argued the narrator is the First Severian reshaped by time travelers, citing the self-cancellation scene and Apu Punchau encounter [Son of Witz, 2008][David Stockhoff, 2008].
  • Jerry Friedman argued the Short Sun Severian may be a different iteration, citing hints about Merryn, Triskele and the red-trimmed hoods [Jerry Friedman, 2011].

Against

  • C R Culver disputed that a New Sun was brought in the earlier iteration, since Tzadkiel would not need to rework the timeline [C R Culver, 1998].
  • Roy C. Lackey argued the first Severian on page 270 was replaced and does not count, so the present Severian lacks his memories [Roy C. Lackey, 2004].
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued that earlier Severian versions would imply history can change, which conflicts with Wolfe's time travel [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2001].
  • James Jordan argued the Claw is a sacrament derived from the present Severian, not from any previous Severian [James Jordan, 2003].
  • b sharp argued against repeated iterations, proposing that Severian's lives are finite and tied to five coffins [b sharp, 2008].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the theory was raised repeatedly from 1997 to 2015 and never gained consensus, with posters on each side citing textual passages and most threads ending unresolved.
