The old boatman is Dorcas's husband and Severian's grandfather

The old man rowing the Lake of Birds in search of his drowned wife is Dorcas's husband, Ouen's father and so Severian's grandfather.

34 threads · 61 posts · for 38 / against 21 · first raised 1999-04-07 by Michael Andre-Driussi
Main voices: Lee Berman (16), b sharp (15), Roy C. Lackey (4), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (4), Jeff Wilson (4), Gerry Quinn (4)

The debate

The idea that the old boatman of the Lake of Birds is Dorcas's husband and so Severian's grandfather surfaces on the list first in Roy C. Lackey's 2006 post, which argues he searched the river for her body [Roy C. Lackey, 2006]. It was not new then: Michael Andre-Driussi had already in 1999 argued that Dorcas was accidentally murdered by her husband, a point that bears on the same figure [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1999]. Lackey repeated and defended the husband identification in 2007, adding that the theory needs no time travel [Roy C. Lackey, 2007], and Paul B. offered an alternative reading in which the boatman searches for Dorcas's corpse in his own story [Paul B, 2007].

From 2006 onward, b sharp, a frequent opponent, argued the tar-stained boatman is Maxellindis' uncle rather than Dorcas's husband, or that he is Father Inire in disguise [b sharp, 2006][b sharp, 2006]. Tony Ellis disputed the grandfather claim in 2007, noting Severian fails to recognise the man later and that the husband was an ex-shopkeeper, not a river man [Tony Ellis, 2007]. In 2010 John Watkins proposed the boatman is Dorcas's husband and Severian's grandfather, against Lee Berman's Inire reading [John Watkins, 2010], and Jack Smith backed the paternal-grandparent version [Jack Smith, 2010]. Ryan Dunn and Lee Berman then developed family-tree arguments [Ryan Dunn, 2010][Lee Berman, 2010].

From 2011 Lee Berman became the main proponent of Father Inire as Dorcas's husband, against Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, who argued the mourned husband is dead while Inire is alive [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2011][Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2011]. Marc Aramini sided with the objection [Marc Aramini, 2011]. Gerry Quinn argued that the resemblance case is circular [Gerry Quinn, 2011]. Many threads ended in impasse, and the proponents of Inire never converged with the husband-as-boatman camp.

For

  • Dorcas's husband searched the river for her body, which Lackey grounded in the boatman's daily searching and his remarks about women underwater [Roy C. Lackey, 2006].
  • The boatman's story of Dorcas being dropped into the sea is taken as evidence that he is her husband, with Andre-Driussi arguing she was accidentally murdered by him [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1999].
  • Dorcas and the boatman are Ouen's parents, and Ouen fathered Severian, which Jack Smith backed from the boatman's mention of his dead wife and Ouen's locket [Jack Smith, 2010][Ryan Dunn, 2010].
  • John Watkins argued the boatman is Dorcas's husband and Severian's grandfather, citing Dorcas as an echo of the Blessed Mother and Inire's disguises as evidence against shapeshifting [John Watkins, 2010].
  • Lee Berman argued Ultan may be Severian's maternal grandfather and the boatman his paternal grandfather [Ryan Dunn, 2011].

Against

  • Tony Ellis argued Severian fails to recognise the boatman in the Gardens, and the husband was an ex-shopkeeper rather than a river man [Tony Ellis, 2007].
  • b sharp argued the boatman is Maxellindis' uncle, citing tar-stained clothes and the undine remark [b sharp, 2006].
  • Paul B. argued the boatman is recounting his own search for Dorcas's corpse rather than Severian's drowning [Paul B, 2007].
  • Jeff Wilson argued that Dorcas's husband may have been a self-deceiver who blamed his neglect for her death, which undercuts the search reading [Jeff Wilson, 2010].
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued Dorcas is mourning a husband who is dead at the book's end, which conflicts with identifying him as the living Inire [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the boatman-as-husband reading had supporters from 2006 to 2011 but no consensus, and rival identities such as Inire and Maxellindis' uncle were never settled.
