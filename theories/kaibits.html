Kaibits and clones in the House Absolute

Several women of the court (Carina, Thea, Thais) are kaibits, clones of nobler women, and the books hide this.

10 threads · 16 posts · for 14 / against 3 · first raised 2006-05-29 by b sharp
Main voices: b sharp (14), Dave Tallman (3), Roy C. Lackey (1)

The debate

The theory was first raised on 2006-05-29 by b sharp, who proposed a naming rule under which kaibit names are diminutives of their cell donors' saint names, making Josepha/Josephina and Pega/Pelagia clone pairs [b sharp, 2006]. The same day b sharp proposed that Countess Carina is a kaibit of Severian's mother Catherine [b sharp, 2006]. Over the following months b sharp argued that the maid playing Saint Katharine was the real Catherine and that the woman in the coffin was a kaibit [b sharp, 2006], and later that Catherine was an exultant with the Maid as her kaibit [b sharp, 2006].

In 2008 the discussion shifted toward Carina and Thais. b sharp argued that Catherine and Carina are clones in a kaibit relationship and admitted the evidence was thin [b sharp, 2008]. Dave Tallman proposed that Severian's mother was an exultant who fled to the past as Catherine, with kaibit maids made for her and serving as St. Katharine [Dave Tallman, 2008]. b sharp suggested that Valeria might be a clone of Catherine and that kaibits take shortened names from their mistresses, such as Thecla-Thea and Thais [b sharp, 2008]. Thais as Thea's kaibit was pressed by b sharp [b sharp, 2008] and by Tallman, who linked Thais to the saints Pelagia, Pega, Thea and Valentina [Dave Tallman, 2008], while b sharp also loosely tied Thea's kaibit to St. Thais [b sharp, 2008].

Roy C. Lackey was the main opponent. He disputed Tallman's identification of the loggia woman with Countess Carina, arguing that the Play cannot be mapped selectively onto Urth Cycle figures and that the Countess is only escorted, not tortured [Roy C. Lackey, 2008]. b sharp questioned whether a kaibit's cell donor must be an exultant or armigette and asked for text checks on whether Severian is the son and whether Tallman conflates Catherine and Carina [b sharp, 2008]. Roy's objection that only exultants have khaibits was answered in b sharp's own later posts [b sharp, 2006]. Several threads ("The Maids of Contessa Carina", "Ouen's Catherine", "Severian's family tree") ended in impasse, with Roy C. Lackey and Jeff Wilson pressing textual and genetic objections.

For

  • Kaibits are named as diminutives of their cell donors' saint names, so Josepha and Pega pair with Josephina and Pelagia [b sharp, 2006].
  • Countess Carina may be a kaibit of Severian's mother Catherine, based on Path of Air scenes and the Rules of Names [b sharp, 2006].
  • Catherine was an exultant whose kaibit maid, not Catherine, appeared as Saint Katharine, a reading supported by resemblances among several women [b sharp, 2006].
  • Tallman argued that Catherine fled to the past as an exultant, with kaibit maids made for her and serving as St. Katharine [Dave Tallman, 2008].
  • Tallman linked the kaibit Thais to the Thea clone line through the legends of Pelagia, Pega, Thea and Valentina [Dave Tallman, 2008].

Against

  • Roy C. Lackey argued that mapping Play characters onto Urth Cycle figures cannot be selectively valid and that the Countess is never tortured [Roy C. Lackey, 2008].
  • b sharp's own posts raised the objection that a kaibit's cell donor might be an armigette rather than an exultant, and asked whether Severian is really the son [b sharp, 2008].
  • Roy C. Lackey questioned the Thais-as-Thea's-kaibit explanation and the time-portal account [Roy C. Lackey, 2008].
  • Roy C. Lackey pressed the textual gaps that a Catherine identification would have to explain, such as Nessus's drowning and Inire's absence [b sharp, 2008].
  • Thread-level objections from Jeff Wilson and Roy C. Lackey argued from genetics and text that Severian's mother and the family links could not be settled [b sharp, 2006].

Where it stands: Still disputed and never resolved: the proposals were argued across many threads over 2006 to 2008, with posters including b sharp, Dave Tallman and Roy C. Lackey staying divided and most threads ending in impasse.
