Krait is Horn's son

The inhumu Krait is Horn's son (through Sinew, or by blood), and his age relative to Sinew matters.

49 threads · 69 posts · for 42 / against 23 · first raised 1999-09-28 by Alex David Groce
Main voices: Alice Turner (9), Dennis G. Berdanis (4), Lee Berman (4), Gerry Quinn (4), Alex David Groce (3), William H. Ansley (3)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by Alex David Groce in September 1999 in the [Alex David Groce, 1999] post, which guessed that Krait took Sinew's personality by first feeding on him, making him Horn's son. Within days Patrick O'Leary ([Patrick O'Leary, 1999]) offered a blood-based version, citing Horn's denial in The Book of the Long Sun that Krait nearly killed Sinew, and Alice Turner ([Alice Turner, 1999]) moved the discussion toward hibernation of imprisoned inhumi rather than parentage. Through 2000 and 2001 the list kept returning to the question with variants: Dennis Berdanis ([Dennis G. Berdanis, 2000] [Dennis G. Berdanis, 2000]) proposed that Krait's mother drew part of Sinew's soul, Alice Turner ([Alice Turner, 2000] [Alice Turner, 2000]) asked whether Horn is Krait's father, and Ian Smith ([Ian Smith, 2000]) argued the opposite, that Sinew and Krait are separate persons.

Later posts shifted to spirit-based and possession-based versions. Adam Stephanides ([Adam Stephanides, 2001]) proposed that Krait is Horn's son by spirit because the inhumi take children's spirits, and William H. Ansley ([William H. Ansley, 2001]) supported it. In 2002 Michael Straight ([Michael Straight, 2002]) and Jerry Friedman ([Jerry Friedman, 2002]) argued over why Krait's relationship with Horn was left undeveloped, with Friedman holding that Krait carries Sinew's soul. In 2009 the theory was rearticulated in threads on Blood Pudding and the Short Sun inhumi, where Stuart Hamm ([Stuart Hamm, 2009]), James Wynn ([James Wynn, 2009] [James Wynn, 2009]) and John Watkins ([John Watkins, 2009]) argued for shared or copied souls, and Chris P. disputed the identity claims.

In 2011 Lee Berman ([Lee Berman, 2011]) and James Wynn ([James Wynn, 2011]) argued that Krait becomes Horn's son by acting like one, while Gerry Quinn ([Gerry Quinn, 2011]) and David Stockhoff ([David Stockhoff, 2011]) debated a Neighbour replacing Horn in the pit. Marc Aramini ([Marc Aramini, 2011]) and Antonio Pedro Marques ([António Pedro Marques, 2011] [António Pedro Marques, 2011]) argued over whether Krait resembles Sinew because of feeding near the reproductive cycle. The last post on the list, by Andrew Mason ([Andrew Mason, 2013]) in 2013, again reads Krait as Horn's son through Sinew. Across the period the debate was mostly among a small set of repeat posters and never produced a shared reading of the parentage question.

For

  • Krait's mother drinking Sinew's blood or soul would make Krait a Sinew-derived inhumu, as Groce and later Stuart Hamm argued [Alex David Groce, 1999] [Stuart Hamm, 2009].
  • Horn calls Krait his son in the resurrection passage, which James Wynn cited for a shared-identity reading [James Wynn, 2009] [James Wynn, 2011].
  • The inhumi take children's spirits from the humans whose blood they drink, so Krait's spirit matches Sinew's and makes him Horn's son in spirit [Adam Stephanides, 2001] [William H. Ansley, 2001].
  • Krait acting like Horn's son, and Horn coming to regard him as more his son than Sinew, supports a parentage-by-conduct reading [James Wynn, 2011] [Lee Berman, 2011].
  • Jahlee's bite on Krait as a toddler or in the egg would make him Sinew's child, as Alice Turner and Peter Westlake suggested [Alice Turner, 2000] [Peter Westlake, 2000].

Against

  • Ian Smith and Dennis Berdanis argued in 2000 that Sinew and Krait are separate people, citing passages where they act apart [Ian Smith, 2000] [Dennis G. Berdanis, 2000].
  • Jerry Friedman held that Krait carries Sinew's soul, so there is no need for a separate Horn-Krait relationship [James Wynn, 2009] [Jerry Friedman, 2002].
  • Chris P. allowed only a loose sense in which Sinew is within Krait, disputing the identity claims [James Wynn, 2009] [John Watkins, 2009].
  • Gerry Quinn disputed the replacement idea, holding that Horn is still Horn and the Neighbor version would make the Neighbours cruel or irrational [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • Marc Aramini and António Pedro Marques doubted that Krait resembles Sinew at all, noting that disguise may be random [António Pedro Marques, 2011] [Marc Aramini, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never settled whether Krait is Horn's son, Sinew's copy, or a separate person, and the debate ran through 2013 without consensus.
