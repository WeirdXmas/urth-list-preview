Severian is his own ancestor

Through time travel Severian is his own father or grandfather.

8 threads · 12 posts · for 6 / against 1 · first raised 1998-05-03 by J. Schultz
Main voices: Roy C. Lackey (2), Che Monro (2), Gerry Quinn (2), J. Schultz (1), Mitchell A. Bailey (1), James Jordan (1)

The debate

The idea was first raised on the list by J. Schultz on 1998-05-03, who proposed that the Conciliator's tomb might hold an earlier Severian who returned from Yesod and fathered a line, making Severian his own ancestor [J. Schultz, 1998]. Mitchell A. Bailey followed in 1999 with a casualty-loop version, comparing it to Heinlein's "All You Zombies" and asking about Charon's parents [Mitchell A. Bailey, 1999]. The same month Roy C. Lackey backed Jonathan Laidlow's grandfather version, citing the raft passage where Severian's resemblance to the Autarch's portrait is noticed [Roy C. Lackey, 1999].

In 2002 the grandfather idea shifted to Dorcas: James Jordan floated it while asking about Thais [James Jordan, 2002], and Lackey then retracted it because the timing of the basket baby made its parentage impossible [Roy C. Lackey, 2002]. In 2003 Che Monro raised a self-father version with Dorcas as mother but questioned whether identical chromosome halves make it biologically possible, and the exchange stayed playful [Che Monro, 2003][Che Monro, 2003].

The theory returned in 2010 with Cliff Judge reading the Apheta scene as possibly making Severian his own father [Cliff Judge, 2010] and David Stockhoff asking whether a closed causal loop could create all time, with Catherine or Apheta as the mother [David Stockhoff, 2010]. Gerry Quinn then relayed John Watkins's time-travelling-sperm grandfather joke and his symmetry rationale, which Watkins said was as plausible as serious theories [Gerry Quinn, 2010][Gerry Quinn, 2010]. Adam Thornton argued the grandfather version in 2011, citing Wolfe's fondness for filk and the multiple iterations of Severian [Adam Thornton, 2011].

For

  • Schultz argued that Katharine is a robot and that the Conciliator's tomb may hold an earlier returned Severian who fathered a line [J. Schultz, 1998].
  • Bailey argued that a casualty loop could make Severian the sole ancestor of his lineage [Mitchell A. Bailey, 1999].
  • Lackey cited Severian's resemblance to the Autarch's portrait on the raft and the earlier Odilo recognition as setup for a family-resemblance reading [Roy C. Lackey, 1999].
  • Judge argued that the Apheta scene, where Severian says what was conceived was himself, may mean he is his own father [Cliff Judge, 2010].
  • Watkins argued through symmetry, Severian's uncontrolled powers and Dorcas as grandmother that he could be his own grandfather [Gerry Quinn, 2010].

Against

  • Lackey retracted the grandfather-through-Dorcas idea because the timing of the basket baby makes its parentage impossible [Roy C. Lackey, 2002].
  • Monro asked whether identical chromosome halves make self-parentage biologically possible [Che Monro, 2003].
  • Judge himself weighed the self-father reading against a simpler reading in which the conceived thing is the white fountain [Cliff Judge, 2010].

Where it stands: Raised repeatedly from 1998 to 2011 by different posters in several versions, but never accepted by the list; the later posts were mostly playful or speculative, and no clue was settled.
