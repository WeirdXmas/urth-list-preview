Silk and Pike are linked by blood

Silk and Patera Pike are brother clones, or Pike is Silk's father, or an older Silk appears as Pike's ghost.

4 threads · 12 posts · for 3 / against 8 · first raised 2002-12-04 by Marc Aramini
Main voices: James Wynn (4), Marc Aramini (2), Jerry Friedman (2), David Stockhoff (2), Dave Tallman (1), Gerry Quinn (1)

The debate

The theory was first raised on 2002-12-04 by Marc Aramini, who proposed that the ghost of Patera Pike in the Long Sun books is a much older Silk returning astrally to an earlier life [Marc Aramini, 2002]. Dave Tallman then offered a related version in 2010, suggesting Silk mistakes his older self for Pike and that Severian meets an early iteration of him [Dave Tallman, 2010]. The theory was revived in November 2011, when Marc Aramini supported Tallman's reading of the Pike and Oreb apparition in Lake of the Long Sun as Silk's older self traveling back in time [Marc Aramini, 2011].

The main shift came from James Wynn, who in the same week disputed the time-travel reading and proposed instead that Silk mistook an older, slightly altered version of himself for Pike [James Wynn, 2011]. He then argued that Silk and Pike are brother clones and that Wolfe planned Horn's transformation into Silk from the start, citing Silk's struggle over calling Blood "son" [James Wynn, 2011]. Jerry Friedman countered that the passage concerns why Silk said "sir", and noted a discrepancy in Oreb's astral form in Return to the Whorl [Jerry Friedman, 2011]. David Stockhoff argued that Wolfe deliberately highlights the word "son" as a pointer, while conceding it is not enough to build a case on [David Stockhoff, 2011].

The debate continued through mid-November 2011, with Wynn insisting that overt facts such as Pike's callotte left behind by the Rajan point to a hidden identity [James Wynn, 2011], and Gerry Quinn replying that the time-travel reading still needs explaining and asking why the Rajan wears the raven [Gerry Quinn, 2011]. Wynn later defended a reading in which Silk misidentifies the bird, citing Pike's white hair and missing eye [James Wynn, 2011]. The thread ended in drift and impasse, with no consensus.

For

  • Marc Aramini argued in 2002 that the aged augur on the steps and parallels with Horn and Nettle support a much older Silk appearing as Pike's ghost [Marc Aramini, 2002].
  • Dave Tallman grounded the idea in resemblances characters notice, such as Severian's near-photographic memory and the Ouen comparison [Dave Tallman, 2010].
  • Marc Aramini supported the time-travel reading, citing Krait's paradox remark and a tunic clue in Exodus from the Long Sun [Marc Aramini, 2011].
  • James Wynn argued that Wolfe planned Horn's transformation into Silk and that Silk's struggle over calling Blood "son" points to Silk and Pike being brother clones [James Wynn, 2011].
  • David Stockhoff argued that Wolfe deliberately highlights "son" as a signal that Blood may be Silk's genetic son if Silk is Pike [David Stockhoff, 2011].

Against

  • James Wynn argued the text does not say Silk merely thought the figure looked like Pike, and that Silk mistook an older altered self for Pike [James Wynn, 2011].
  • Jerry Friedman argued the "son" passage is about why Silk said "sir", and noted Oreb's astral form is a fat bird in Return to the Whorl [Jerry Friedman, 2011].
  • Jerry Friedman argued that the Silk-Pike resemblance goes unremarked because characters would not mention it, so its absence in Rose's viewpoint is not evidence [Jerry Friedman, 2011].
  • Gerry Quinn argued the time-travel reading still needs explaining, and asked why the Rajan wears the raven while Pike's resemblance to Silk stays unexplained [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • David Stockhoff conceded the "son" wording is only a pointer and offered no textual evidence beyond quoted remarks about budding creatures [David Stockhoff, 2011][David Stockhoff, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed: raised in 2002, revived in 2011 and left in drift and impasse, with the Short Sun material said to be needed to settle it.
