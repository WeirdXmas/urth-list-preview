Silk is a clone of Typhon

Silk (or his father Tussah, or Fish) is a clone or genetic copy of Typhon, made as a body for Pas; this is why Silk resembles Typhon.

79 threads · 272 posts · for 126 / against 171 · first raised 1997-04-21 by Michael Andre-Driussi
Main voices: James Wynn (95), Marc Aramini (45), Gerry Quinn (36), David Stockhoff (19), António Pedro Marques (16), Lisa Schaffer-Doggett (14)

The debate

The clone question was first raised on the Whorl list in 2000, when David Wells asked whether Silk's embryos were clones of the original Typhon body [David Wells, 2000]. The first explicit proposal that Silk is a clone from Typhon's cells came in 2002 from Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, who also argued at length about Horn and memory transfer [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002]. Don Doggett tied the clone idea to Herod's bloodline and Ymar [Don Doggett, 2002], and Lisa Schaffer-Doggett then built a full 'Plan of Pas' around it in 2003 [Lisa Schaffer-Doggett, 2003]. James Wynn and Roy C. Lackey backed the idea with the shared reddish-blond beard and Typhon's obsession with his face [James Wynn, 2003] [Roy C. Lackey, 2003], while James Jordan answered that Pas could be Typhon in disguise despite differences in age and hair [James Jordan, 2003].

The 2005 'Shaving Clones' thread was the main turning point. After Wolfe's ambiguous answer about Silk's ancestry, James Wynn pressed the Typhon-clone reading, arguing that Tussah's 'son not of my body' line was deliberately misleading [James Wynn, 2005] and that Tussah knew Silk was a clone [James Wynn, 2005]. Roy Lackey said he had never made the clone claim and that a Tussah-Typhon link had no textual support [Roy C. Lackey, 2005], and Chris P questioned why Typhon would clone a son rather than himself [Chris P, 2005]. Stanisław Bocian argued that the wording did not rule out a biological child [Stanisław Bocian, 2005]. A 2006 post described the earlier 'clone wars' as a heated run of posts [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2006].

From 2009 to 2011 the theory spread to Horn, Fish and Pas variants. In 2009 James Wynn and Son of Witz proposed Horn as a clone of Typhon [James Wynn, 2009], and Son of Witz later disputed it for missing the clues [Son of Witz, 2009]. John Watkins suggested Silk as a powered-up clone rather than a biological son [John Watkins, 2009]. Marc Aramini argued Silk is Typhon's biological son [Marc Aramini, 2010] [Marc Aramini, 2011] and later that he is a love child rather than a clone [Marc Aramini, 2011], while Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued for a Typhon clone [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2011]. Ashley Crill proposed a black-market embryo, not a clone [Ashley Crill, 2011]. The threads on the Calde answer, the face of Pas and Babbie repeatedly ended in impasse.

For

  • Lisa Schaffer-Doggett cites shared traits such as blond hair, height and a reddish-blond beard tied to Graves' King Jesus [Lisa Schaffer-Doggett, 2003].
  • James Wynn argues the matching 'harvest-red' beard in Graves links Silk to sacred kingship and the Typhon clone idea [James Wynn, 2003].
  • Roy C. Lackey argues Silk is Typhon's double, pointing to the painting Silk destroyed and to the fact that no one remarks on the resemblance [Roy C. Lackey, 2003].
  • James Wynn argues Wolfe's Calde answer was deliberately misleading and that Typhon cloned his family for the voyage [James Wynn, 2005].
  • Dave Tallman argues that if Silk is a clone, the Mainframe spirits he meets cannot be his biological parents, which points toward a clone lineage [Dave Tallman, 2010].

Against

  • Charles Reed argues Silk's biological parents cannot be identified from the text, so any royal link is speculation [Charles Reed, 2002].
  • James Jordan argues that Pas's two heads, white hair and Silk's youth differ enough that the resemblance could go unnoticed, so Typhon in disguise is not a clone [James Jordan, 2003].
  • Roy Lackey argues the clone claim has no textual support and that Calde was Silk's adoptive spiritual father [Roy C. Lackey, 2005].
  • Chris P asks why Typhon would clone a son rather than himself and argues the Calde answer does not support the clone reading [Chris P, 2005] [Chris P, 2005].
  • Marc Aramini argues Silk and Severian cannot be clones because of hair colour, and that Silk is Typhon's biological son [Marc Aramini, 2006] [Marc Aramini, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the clone reading was pressed by James Wynn and several others from 2002 to 2011 but never gained agreement, and most critics, including Marc Aramini and Ashley Crill, favoured a biological son or a non-clone reading.
