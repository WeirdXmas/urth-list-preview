Oreb is possessed by a god

Silk's bird Oreb is at times ridden by a god (Scylla or Pas), which explains his words and knowledge.

43 threads · 75 posts · for 41 / against 32 · first raised 1997-03-10 by Kieran Mullen
Main voices: James Wynn (22), Robert Borski (7), Gerry Quinn (6), stilskin (5), Roy C. Lackey (5), Marc Aramini (4)

The debate

The idea that Oreb is ridden by a god was first raised on the list by Kieran Mullen in 1997, who proposed Oreb sees Silk as a god, a view that is only loosely connected to the later possession claims [Kieran Mullen, 1997]. The full possession theory took shape in 2000, when Robert Borski argued that Scylla rides Oreb, citing the story 'The Night Chough' and Oreb's visions [Robert Borski, 2000], and Michael Andre-Driussi agreed that Scylla was the top candidate while excluding Pas [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2000]. Borski kept pressing the case through 2000 and 2001, using Oreb's speech as a mouthpiece for Scylla [Robert Borski, 2000][Robert Borski, 2000], while stilskin and Alex David Groce questioned it, stilskin arguing Scylla's personality clashes with Oreb's and proposing instead a Mainframe god or a piece of Pas [stilskin, 2000][stilskin, 2000].

The discussion then moved to mechanisms in 2002, when Andrew Bollen and Roy C. Lackey proposed Scylla possessed Oreb at specific points in Nightside the Long Sun and at the Grand Manteion sacrifice [Andrew Bollen, 2002][Roy C. Lackey, 2002]. Nigel Price proposed a lossy 'human JPEG' transfer and Dan Meliza an interference-pattern model for Oreb [Nigel Price, 2002][Dan Meliza, 2002]. From 2005 to 2011 James Wynn became the main advocate of Pas, not Scylla, as the possessing god, citing Silk's butcher experience and black birds as Pas's signatories [James Wynn, 2005][James Wynn, 2009][James Wynn, 2011], while Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, David Stockhoff, Gerry Quinn and António Pedro Marques disputed that Oreb was possessed at all, arguing he was playing dead [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2005][António Pedro Marques, 2011][Gerry Quinn, 2011]. In 2013 Larry Miller proposed possession at the sacrifice and Marc Aramini and Andrew Mason proposed Scylla possession during The Night Chough, with Miller disputing timing links [Larry Miller, 2013][Marc Aramini, 2013][Larry Miller, 2013].

The debate has repeatedly turned to Oreb's apparent death and resurrection, and to whether the bird's lies or forgetting can be read as possession, as in the 2011 thread on Mucor and Oreb in the manse [Roy C. Lackey, 2011][James Wynn, 2011]. Across the threads, posters rarely changed sides, and the central question remained contested.

For

  • Robert Borski argued Scylla rides Oreb, citing Oreb's visions of Scylla's face in 'The Night Chough' and Moonrat's account of the bird leading a girl [Robert Borski, 2000].
  • Borski also argued the bird's line 'That was the bird. And not the bird.' shows Scylla speaking through him [Robert Borski, 2000].
  • Michael Andre-Driussi argued Oreb is sometimes ridden by a god, as Chenille is by Kypris and Tick by Sphigx, with Scylla the top candidate [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2000].
  • Nigel Price and Dan Meliza argued Scylla moved into Oreb through a lossy personality transfer or fragment upload [Nigel Price, 2002][Dan Meliza, 2002].
  • Roy C. Lackey proposed Scylla possessed Oreb at the Grand Manteion sacrifice, seeking a glass to re-upload herself [Roy C. Lackey, 2002].

Against

  • Alex David Groce argued the Scylla references in the Long Sun and Short Sun books are not convincing evidence that Scylla possesses Oreb [Alex David Groce, 2000].
  • stilskin argued Scylla's bloodthirsty personality clashes with Oreb's wise character, so Oreb is not ridden by her [stilskin, 2000].
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued possession by a Mainframe god is documented only once and under extreme conditions, and asked whether Oreb was simply playing dead [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2005].
  • Gerry Quinn and António Pedro Marques argued Silk would have known a dead bird from a fainted one, so possession is not needed [António Pedro Marques, 2011][Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • James Wynn argued Pas, not Scylla, was the possessing entity, since Scylla's possessions only began after Pas's resurrection [James Wynn, 2005][James Wynn, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never settled whether Oreb is possessed at all, and if so by Scylla or Pas, with posters largely holding their positions across the 1997 to 2013 discussions.
