Chenille is Tussah's daughter

Chenille is Tussah's natural daughter, and so Silk's sister.

12 threads · 18 posts · for 10 / against 6 · first raised 1997-02-06 by Dave Lebling
Main voices: James Wynn (5), Marc Aramini (3), Roy C. Lackey (2), Dave Tallman (2), David Stockhoff (2), Dave Lebling (1)

The debate

The theory that Chenille is Tussah's natural daughter, and so Silk's sister, appears on the list first in a 1997 post by Dave Lebling [Dave Lebling, 1997], who disputed the Molpe reading but agreed Chenille is Tussah's normal daughter on the basis of matching facial descriptions. In 2002 Roy C. Lackey argued for it directly, grounding it in Silk's remarks about the calde's bust and in the will [Roy C. Lackey, 2002], while James Wynn argued the opposite that Chenille is genetically engineered because Tussah is an inhume [James Wynn, 2002]. The later discussion, which the thread summaries place mostly in 2004 to 2011, moved from parentage to a clone or Typhon-based origin. Wynn proposed a clone of Tussah from his X chromosome [James Wynn, 2004], then argued Silk's father is Lemur in a chem body, which made the natural-daughter reading doubtful [James Wynn, 2011]. Lackey and Dave Tallman in 2010 disputed Chenille's knife skill as a sign of parentage, and Lackey proposed Tussah as Silk's unacknowledged foster father [Roy C. Lackey, 2010][Dave Tallman, 2010].

Marc Aramini and Tim O'Donnell then took up related family readings. Aramini proposed Chenille as Silk's step sister [Marc Aramini, 2010] and later argued Mint's 'older sister' line is a deliberate clue [Marc Aramini, 2011]. O'Donnell proposed Auk and Chenille as Tussah's own children, reading the phrase 'son not of my body' [Tim O'Donnell, 2011]. Wynn argued Chenille is a daughter of Typhon [James Wynn, 2011]. Much of the wider exchange is recorded only in thread summaries, where the family-relation question was entangled with disputes over Silk's origin and over the character list's authorship.

The 2010 names thread brought in a different line. Lee Berman linked the names Silk, Chenille and Tussah through caterpillar, silk and plant senses [Lee Berman, 2010], Dave Tallman supported a deliberate naming pattern [Dave Tallman, 2010], and David Stockhoff favoured the caterpillar plant link [David Stockhoff, 2010]. Dave Lebling's 1997 post and the 1998 glossary by mary whalen [mary whalen, 1998] were the earliest name-based material.

For

  • Dave Lebling argued that Chenille is Tussah's normal daughter, citing identical facial descriptions [Dave Lebling, 1997].
  • Roy C. Lackey argued Chenille is Tussah's daughter and Silk an adopted embryo, citing the calde's bust, Chenille's face and the will [Roy C. Lackey, 2002].
  • Lackey proposed that Tussah is Chenille's unacknowledged natural father, and Silk's unacknowledged foster father [Roy C. Lackey, 2010].
  • Dave Tallman argued Tussah is Chenille's natural father through shared Typhon-clone genes with frozen embryos [Dave Tallman, 2010].
  • Tim O'Donnell proposed that Auk and Chenille are Tussah's children, reading 'son not of my body' as ruling out a son from his body [Tim O'Donnell, 2011].

Against

  • James Wynn argued Chenille is engineered and cannot be Tussah's natural daughter because Tussah is an inhume [James Wynn, 2002].
  • Wynn argued Chenille is a clone of Tussah from his X chromosome only [James Wynn, 2004].
  • Wynn argued the father in the Calde chapter 9 reunion is Lemur in a chem body, making the natural-daughter reading doubtful [James Wynn, 2011].
  • Marc Aramini argued Chenille would be Silk's step sister rather than a full or clone sister [Marc Aramini, 2010].
  • Lackey questioned Tallman's knife-skill argument, pointing out Silk's biological father is the blue-eyed man in his vision [Roy C. Lackey, 2010].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the thread summaries record no agreement, and the record leaves the authorship of the Exodus character list that calls Chenille Tussah's natural daughter open.
