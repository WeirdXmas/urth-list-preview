Tzadkiel re-created Severian

The Severian who returns from Yesod was re-created by Tzadkiel rather than resurrected in the same body.

4 threads · 5 posts · for 2 / against 2 · first raised 2008-12-03 by Son of Witz
Main voices: David Stockhoff (3), Son of Witz (1), Lee Berman (1)

The debate

The theory that Tzadkiel re-created Severian first appears on the list in a December 2008 post by Son of Witz, who proposed that the returning Severian is the First Severian reshaped by time travelers rather than a new body [Son of Witz, 2008]. David Stockhoff raised the same day a closely related reading, that the Citadel claim should be read as the First Severian reshaped, and framed the Tzadkiel resurrection argument as a point to weigh against a multiple-Severians idea [David Stockhoff, 2008]. Both posts argued from the self-cancellation scene and the Apu Punchau encounter [Son of Witz, 2008] [David Stockhoff, 2008].

A week later Stockhoff questioned why Severian's face changed after Yesod and what the corpse in the tomb signifies, noting that Severian had been gone only weeks or months so age cannot explain the change [David Stockhoff, 2008]. In December 2008 he then disputed the time-walker explanation, arguing the corpse is hard to read as an earlier Severian and that the altered face reflects a godlike icon, answering a quoted reply that the tomb holds Odilio, Pega and Thais [David Stockhoff, 2008]. The posts record no reply from the proponents in these posts.

In April 2012 Lee Berman shifted the question toward divinity, arguing that Severian's resurrection power is divine, citing the ending of The Urth of the New Sun and Tzadkiel's service to him [Lee Berman, 2012]. The threads on Abaia and the undines and on fairies and Wolfe drifted into origins, Christ-figure parallels and advancement debates, and the posts record no consensus on the re-creation point.

For

  • Son of Witz argued the narrator is literally the same person as the First Severian reshaped by time travelers, defended by the self-cancellation scene and the Apu Punchau encounter [Son of Witz, 2008]
  • Stockhoff, in his first post, read the Citadel claim as the First Severian reshaped and cited the Tzadkiel resurrection argument [David Stockhoff, 2008]
  • Lee Berman argued Severian's resurrection power is divine, citing the ending of The Urth of the New Sun and Tzadkiel serving him [Lee Berman, 2012]

Against

  • Stockhoff rejected the multiple-Severians idea as too simple while weighing the Tzadkiel resurrection argument [David Stockhoff, 2008]
  • Stockhoff argued the corpse in the tomb cannot easily be an earlier Severian, citing the TCotA tomb passage and listing problems with the time-walker explanation [David Stockhoff, 2008]
  • Stockhoff argued the altered face after Yesod reflects a godlike icon, answering the quoted reply that portraits idealize faces [David Stockhoff, 2008]
  • Stockhoff questioned the re-creation account, noting age cannot explain a face change after only weeks or months [David Stockhoff, 2008]

Where it stands: Raised once in 2008 and mostly left unresolved, with the 2012 divine-power post shifting attention rather than settling whether Tzadkiel re-created Severian.
