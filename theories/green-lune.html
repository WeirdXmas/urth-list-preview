Green is Lune

Green, the jungle planet of the inhumi, is Lune, Urth's moon, which was greened in the New Sun era.

54 threads · 66 posts · for 31 / against 32 · first raised 1997-02-05 by xury
Main voices: Marc Aramini (8), Kieran Cleary (6), Jeff Wilson (6), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (5), Jerry Friedman (5), David Stockhoff (4)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by xury in February 1997, asking whether the Long Sun planet Blue could be Urth and Green its Lune [xury, 1997], and John Eric Ivancich followed in October 1997 with a related question about the Moon being forested [John Eric Ivancich, 1997]. Kieran Cleary took the Green=Luna question up in 1998 and 1999, first as an open bet and then as a claim that Merryn and Sev can only appear in that system if Green is Lune [Kieran Cleary, 1999]. Mitchell A. Bailey disputed it on astronomical grounds, arguing the book's word 'conjunction' does not fit a double-planet system [Mitchell A. Bailey, 1999]. Dan Rabin argued in 2001 that Green is the forested Moon, using Wolfe's usage of 'Green' for any bright green body.

The debate grew sharply in 2002, mostly in the 'Green ain't Lune' thread. Roy C. Lackey argued from Hoof's and Silkhorn's size remarks that Lune cannot be Green's moon [Roy C. Lackey, 2002], Marc Aramini countered with apparent-size and distance arguments [Marc Aramini, 2002], and Jerry Friedman argued that apparent size is unknowable from space and proposed a double planet with a six-year period [Jerry Friedman, 2002]. Jerry Friedman's orbital calculations in March 2002 concluded that the gravitational forces would destabilize the system [Jerry Friedman, 2002]. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued that a terraformed Lune lacks the open water life needs and that the theory has no textual support [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002]. Charles Reed's orbital reasoning in November 2002 was a major contribution against the theory [Charles Reed, 2002].

Later posters continued the dispute. Marc Aramini claimed in 2011 that Wolfe had privately said Green is Urth, while James B. Jordan and others disputed that account [Marc Aramini, 2011]. In 2011 many posters, including Jeff Wilson, Lee Berman and David Stockhoff, worked on the question of how Lune could be green, through domes, algae, or a larger Lune [Jeff Wilson, 2011] [Lee Berman, 2011] [David Stockhoff, 2011]. Andreas Johansson revived the Green-is-Lune idea in October 2013 with a Blue-as-Urth variant [Andreas Johansson, 2013], and Lee Berman disputed it [Lee Berman, 2013].

For

  • Kieran Cleary argued in 1999 that Merryn and Sev can only appear in the Blue/Green system if Green is Lune [Kieran Cleary, 1999].
  • Dan Rabin explained in 2001 that the 'Green' is the forested Moon, since Wolfe uses the word for any bright green celestial body.
  • Roy C. Lackey argued in 2002 that Silkhorn's and Hoof's size remarks do not show Green is larger than Lune [Roy C. Lackey, 2002].
  • Lee Berman argued in 2011 that the Whorl-as-Lune theory weakens while Urth-as-Green and Lune-as-Blue gain probability [Lee Berman, 2011].
  • Andreas Johansson proposed in 2013 that the green world is really Lune, with Blue being Urth after the flood [Andreas Johansson, 2013].

Against

  • Mitchell A. Bailey argued that the book's word 'conjunction' does not fit a double-planet system [Mitchell A. Bailey, 1999].
  • Jerry Friedman's orbital mechanics concluded that the gravitational forces would destabilize the system [Jerry Friedman, 2002].
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued that a terraformed Lune lacks the open water Urth-like life needs and that the theory has no textual support [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002].
  • Charles Reed argued that Green's warmer climate requires an eccentric orbit that would lose Blue's pull, and that Green and Lune exist concurrently [Charles Reed, 2002].
  • Jerry Friedman argued that Green is sometimes seen full on the opposite side of Blue from the Short Sun, so the celestial mechanics cannot be made to work [Jerry Friedman, 2002].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the theory was never settled, the 2002 and 2011 threads ended without consensus, and posters on both sides still disagreed by 2013.
