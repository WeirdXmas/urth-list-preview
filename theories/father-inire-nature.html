What Father Inire is

Father Inire is not human (a hierodule, a cacogen, a fallen angel or a pagan god) and appears in many disguises across the books.

82 threads · 207 posts · for 107 / against 104 · first raised 1998-10-12 by Robert Borski
Main voices: Lee Berman (61), Jeff Wilson (21), b sharp (18), Roy C. Lackey (16), Ryan Dunn (15), David Stockhoff (14)

The debate

The idea that Father Inire is non-human first appears on the list in 1998, when Robert Borski [Robert Borski, 1998] proposed that Inire is the father of the cacogen children and that named characters are alien disguises. In 1999 Borski [Robert Borski, 1999] argued that Inire appears in disguise several times, and Mitchell A. Bailey [Mitchell A. Bailey, 1999] called Inire a non-human hierodule of a different race serving on Urth for centuries.

The theory was pressed hardest in 2006 and 2008. b sharp proposed a list of disguises, including the jungle guide, a monkey-like figure and Rudesind [b sharp, 2006], and later argued that Dorcas's husband is Inire [b sharp, 2008]. Roy C. Lackey and Jeff Wilson took opposing sides on shapeshifting and on the bier scene, with Wilson offering prosaic explanations [Jeff Wilson, 2008]. Lackey later charged that Wilson's method made the theory unfalsifiable [Roy C. Lackey, 2008]. Dave Tallman linked Inire's race to the hierodules' origins [Dave Tallman, 2008], and Lackey identified Inire with the hierodule vessel that carried Severian [Roy C. Lackey, 2008].

From 2010 to 2011 Lee Berman became the main proponent, arguing that Inire is a fallen pagan or Dionysus-like god [Lee Berman, 2010][Lee Berman, 2011] and linking him to Inuus, Faunus and Pan [Lee Berman, 2011]. Ryan Dunn backed Rudesind-as-Inire [Ryan Dunn, 2010], and Andrew Mason argued that Inire is the masked jungle guide and possibly Isangoma [Andrew Mason, 2011][Andrew Mason, 2011]. Berman's 2013 post [Lee Berman, 2013] proposed that Father Inire is not human, and Jeff Wilson [Jeff Wilson, 2014] suggested Inire's cloning technology explains Rudesind. In 2014 and 2018 Marc Aramini disputed the shapeshifter reading [Marc Aramini, 2014] and Borski's hierodule claims [Marc Aramini, 2018].

For

  • Borski's 1998 post proposed Inire as father of the cacogen children, the earliest version of the theory [Robert Borski, 1998].
  • Berman linked Inire's name to Inuus, Faunus and Pan, and Inire's monkey traits to shapeshifting pagan gods [Lee Berman, 2011][Lee Berman, 2014].
  • Lackey argued that the summoned ship in Citadel was the hierodule vessel that carried Severian, making Inire an alien [Roy C. Lackey, 2008].
  • Berman argued that Inire, Hethor and Tzadkiel are one class of angelic shapeshifter, citing small, old, bent figures and mastery of mirrors [Lee Berman, 2011].
  • Dave Tallman used the lifespan remarks about dogs to argue Inire's shorter-lived race shares an origin with the hierodules [Dave Tallman, 2008].

Against

  • Jeff Wilson argued Inire is a human actor hiding nonhuman proportions under a hood, and that a twenty-year lifespan cannot fit a thousand years of service [Jeff Wilson, 2008].
  • Lackey argued Inire is not a shapeshifter because Severian would have disclosed such a power in his manuscripts [Roy C. Lackey, 2006][Andrew Mason, 2011].
  • Lackey argued that interspecies offspring with Dorcas is unlikely, undercutting the shapeshifting husband reading [Roy C. Lackey, 2006].
  • mournings glory argued Rudesind is a self-deceiving human factotum with no reason to disguise himself [mournings glory, 2006][mournings glory, 2006].
  • Wilson questioned the alien reading by noting that dark skin alone does not mark a character as non-human [Jeff Wilson, 2006].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the threads ended without consensus, and in 2014 and 2018 posters such as Aramini still rejected the shapeshifting and hierodule readings.
