Silk's body was meant for Pas

Silk's body was prepared as a host for Pas (or contains part of him), and Silk and Pas may merge in Mainframe.

32 threads · 50 posts · for 24 / against 21 · first raised 1997-06-05 by Michael Straight
Main voices: Marc Aramini (9), Gerry Quinn (8), Michael Straight (7), James Wynn (6), Alice Turner (2), James Jordan (2)

The debate

The host-body idea was first raised on 1997-06-05 by Michael Straight in a numbered set of Long Sun points that cited Kypris's offer to Silk to be Pas [Michael Straight, 1997]. Straight expanded it in 1998, arguing that Typhon and company tried to install Allfather Pas as the starcrossers' divine ruler [Michael Straight, 1998], and in November 1998 he floated the view that Silk was meant to be Pas's host body while defending Exodus's abrupt close [Michael Straight, 1998]. Donn Seeley later proposed that Silk was created as a host for Pas, grounding the guess in quoted passages from the Short Sun books [Donn Seeley, 2000].

The idea returned in 2002, when Straight asked whether Silk was a godling and argued that he was the body planned for Pas, drawing a symmetry with Piaton, an intended host that turned the tables on Typhon [Michael Straight, 2002]. James Jordan disputed the paternity side of this, arguing that Pas cannot be Silk's father because Pas is still alive when Silk sees his dead biological parents [James Jordan, 2002]. In 2009 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes reported a widely held list belief that the embryo was meant to serve as Pas's own body, without offering evidence [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2009]. Stuart Hamm, John Watkins and Son of Witz then argued over whether Pas was loaded into Silk or Silk into Pas at Mainframe, and whether Pas is hidden in Silk, with the text cited only thinly [Stuart Hamm, 2009] [John Watkins, 2009] [Son of Witz, 2009].

In January 2011 the theory was mainly tested against resemblance. Roy C. Lackey argued that in two Exodus scenes only Piaton's face, not Silk's, appears among Pas's heads [Roy C. Lackey, 2011], while António Pedro Marques cited a cut paragraph contrasting Silk's features with Great Pas's and suggested Silk could be Pas incarnate [António Pedro Marques, 2011]. Gerry Quinn proposed a Silk-Pas merge in Mainframe [Gerry Quinn, 2011] and later argued that the embryo was not central to Pas's plan [Gerry Quinn, 2011] and that Silk was not Pas's biological child [Gerry Quinn, 2011]. Marc Aramini instead argued for Silk as Pas's natural son [Marc Aramini, 2011], and in November Larry Miller proposed that Silk was a body grown for Pas to occupy [Larry Miller, 2011]. The Exodus thread also saw Alice Turner reject the Silk-Pas amalgam as too obviously a demonic offer of world control, noting that the ending left Hy's allegiance and the taluses unresolved [Alice Turner, 1998], and Kieran Cleary suggest the downloaded personality might have been grafted onto an incomplete Pas persona, relying on a hazy recollection [Michael Straight, 1998].

For

  • Silk's near-miraculous charisma and his birth timing were cited as signs that he was the body planned for Pas [Michael Straight, 2002].
  • Straight argued by symmetry with Piaton, an intended host body that turned the tables on Typhon [Michael Straight, 2002].
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes reported a widely held list belief that the embryo was meant to be Pas's own body on arrival [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2009].
  • Larry Miller argued that Typhon is an alien and so cannot be the same being as Pas, making Silk a grown body for Pas to occupy [Larry Miller, 2011].
  • António Pedro Marques pointed to a cut paragraph contrasting Silk's features with Great Pas's, suggesting Silk could be Pas incarnate [António Pedro Marques, 2011].

Against

  • James Jordan argued that Pas cannot be Silk's father because Pas is still alive when Silk sees his dead biological parents, and that the birth timing belongs to the Outsider's plan [James Jordan, 2002].
  • Roy C. Lackey argued that Silk does not look like Pas, since only Piaton's face appears among Pas's heads in two Exodus scenes [Roy C. Lackey, 2011].
  • Gerry Quinn argued that the First Exodus timing left no room for a long scouting plan, so the embryo could not have been central to Pas's plan [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • Alice Turner argued that the Silk-Pas amalgam was too obviously a demonic offer of world control and that Exodus leaves too much open [Alice Turner, 1998].
  • Don Doggett argued that the Outsider subverted Pas's plan from the start, so Silk's body was not simply Pas's [Don Doggett, 2002].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the host-body theory was raised from 1997 to 2011 and was repeatedly tested against the text, but the list never reached agreement, and most of the later discussion folded it into broader debates over Silk's parentage.
