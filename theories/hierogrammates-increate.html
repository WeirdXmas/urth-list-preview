The Hierogrammates serve the Increate

The Hierogrammates act for the Increate (God), with or without knowing it; the counter-reading is that they pursue their own ends.

41 threads · 74 posts · for 21 / against 49 · first raised 1998-10-15 by mary whalen
Main voices: Roy C. Lackey (18), John Watkins (7), thalassocrat (5), Son of Witz (5), Lee Berman (5), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (4)

The debate

The idea was first raised in 1999 by Nicholas Gevers, who argued that the Hierogrammates, though scheming, act as instruments of the Increate within a Sefirotic hierarchy of universes [Nicholas Gevers, 1999]. In the same year he proposed a Catholic-style model in which the Increate's influence passes through mediating intermediaries, and he faulted Ori's blunter framing [Nicholas Gevers, 1999]. The theory lay quiet until 2005, when Roy C. Lackey revived it, arguing that the Hierogrammates receive direct orders from the Increate and that they stopped Typhon [Roy C. Lackey, 2005][Roy C. Lackey, 2005]. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes disputed the reading by noting that Catholics also offer suffering to Jesus, so the offering line shows no privileged access [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2005]. Maru Dubshinki and Adam Stephanides argued instead that the angel lacked the Increate's confidence or that the Increate never sanctioned the shaping of humanity [Maru Dubshinki, 2005][Adam Stephanides, 2005], while Andrew Kertesz argued the Hierogrammates could be wise while working against the Increate's will [Andrew Kertesz, 2005].

The debate broadened in 2006 and 2008. Lackey reported Peter Wright's reading in Attending Daedalus, in which the Hierogrammates deceive humanity, and Stephanides answered that the religious elements read as sincere [Roy C. Lackey, 2006][Adam Stephanides, 2006]. In January 2008 thalassocrat argued the Hierogrammates are malicious parasitic creatures rather than angels [thalassocrat, 2008], and Lackey disputed this by citing Tzadkiel's sacrifice and Severian's healings as signs of the Increate's involvement [Roy C. Lackey, 2008]. The long 2008 threads saw further positions: John Watkins argued that the Hierogrammates serve the Increate's will through habituation, a claim Paul B repeatedly rejected as erasing morality [John Watkins, 2008][Paul B, 2008], while Jeff Wilson called them false gods built out of hubris [Jeff Wilson, 2008]. Lackey kept defending the direct-orders reading, relying on the dictionary sense of 'rescript' [Roy C. Lackey, 2008].

From 2008 to 2011 the argument continued through Stockhoff, Son of Witz, Craig Brewer, Lee Berman and others. David Stockhoff and Son of Witz clashed over whether the Hierogrammates and Hierodules serve the Increate at all, with Son of Witz citing the Christian symbolism [David Stockhoff, 2008][Son of Witz, 2008]. Brewer and Son of Witz argued over whether Wright's atheist reading misses a divine-plan layer [Craig Brewer, 2010][Son of Witz, 2010]. Berman argued the Hierogrammates may be divine judges above the fray and later that angelic status follows from allegiance to the Increate [Lee Berman, 2010][Lee Berman, 2011]. Multiple threads, including 'What's So Great About Ushas?' and 'the Epitome', ended without agreement.

For

  • Gevers argued the Hierogrammates act as instruments of the Increate within a Sefirotic hierarchy, even while scheming [Nicholas Gevers, 1999].
  • Lackey argued that the Hierogrammates receive direct orders from the Increate, citing the Urth death-agonies line and the Christian imagery of the Sun Cycle [Roy C. Lackey, 2005].
  • Lackey argued that the Hierogrammates, acting for the Increate, stopped Typhon, and that Severian's account would otherwise be a farce [Roy C. Lackey, 2005].
  • Lackey argued from the dictionary senses of 'rescript' that the phrase implies direct contact with the Increate [Roy C. Lackey, 2008].
  • b sharp argued that Wolfe intended the Hierogrammates as angels closer to God, with moral authority above humans [b sharp, 2008].

Against

  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued that the death-agonies line shows no privileged access to God's will, since Catholics likewise offer suffering to Jesus [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2005].
  • Stephanides argued that the Increate never judged the shaping of humanity to be a sin and that the text shows no direct divine intervention [Adam Stephanides, 2005].
  • Paul B argued that the Hierogrammates are not shown to know the Increate's Plan, the only evidence being a line about offering agonies [Paul B, 2008].
  • thalassocrat argued that the Hierogrammates were not created by the Increate, show no holiness, and pursue a self-serving agenda [thalassocrat, 2008].
  • Watkins argued that the Hierogrammates are only guessing at the Increate's will, so their genocide lacks justification [John Watkins, 2008].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the theory was never settled, the threads on it mostly ended in impasse, and the posters who argued for direct service (Gevers, Lackey, b sharp) faced sustained opposition from Stephanides, Paul B, Watkins and others.
