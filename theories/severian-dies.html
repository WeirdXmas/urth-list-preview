Severian died and was brought back

Severian died at some point (drowning in the Gyoll as a boy, or later) and what continues is a resurrected or re-created Severian.

41 threads · 71 posts · for 50 / against 17 · first raised 1997-06-25 by Kevin McGuire
Main voices: Jeff Wilson (11), Lee Berman (11), Roy C. Lackey (7), Son of Witz (6), David Stockhoff (6), Dave Tallman (6)

The debate

The earliest post on the list touching on death and resurrection is Kevin McGuire's 1997 Christ-figure argument, which treats the Apu Punchau sequence as evidence [Kevin McGuire, 1997]. The specific claim that Severian drowned as a boy and was resurrected was first put by Greene Carlton in 1999, who read the skull Severian finds in the Gyoll as his own [Greene, Carlton, 1999]. Through 2002 to 2007 the idea was mostly tied to other threads: Dan'l Danehy-Oakes and Roy C. Lackey argued over whether the drowning fell on the day Severian reached Nessus and in which season [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002][Roy C. Lackey, 2002], and Dave Tallman later suggested a drowned predecessor became an aquastor who saved him [Dave Tallman, 2008]. The theory became central in December 2008, when a burst of posts argued whether the body Severian occupies after his fall from the sailship is an aquastor, an eidolon, or a self-resurrected original. Son of Witz proposed the Gyoll resurrection [Son of Witz, 2008], Lane Haygood argued Severian died and was resurrected in the Gyoll [Lane Haygood, 2008], and Roy Lackey argued he died on the Ship and was revived as an aquastor [Roy C. Lackey, 2008]. David Stockhoff disputed the aquastor body reading, saying the text leaves it open [David Stockhoff, 2008].

In 2010 John Watkins's claim of an artificially resurrected personality prompted James Wynn to propose a drowning at the start of The Shadow of the Torturer [James Wynn, 2010] and Josh Geller to reject the drowning as unnecessary [Josh Geller, 2010]. Lee Berman argued repeatedly that Severian dies and is resurrected several times as an eidolon, with his powers granted aboard Tzadkiel's ship [Lee Berman, 2010]. Tony Ellis opposed the aquastor reading, citing Famulimus's 'son your mother bore' line to argue Severian self-resurrects in the same body [Tony Ellis, 2010], and Jeff Wilson argued the Gyoll skull cannot be his mortal remains if the original reached the Ship [Jeff Wilson, 2010]. In 2011 Marc Aramini asked whether the underwater skull shows Severian was an eidolon from the start [Marc Aramini, 2011], and in 2013 David Stockhoff and Wilson debated whether he is an eidolon, a time-copy, or an aquastor duplicate rather than a clone [David Stockhoff, 2013][Jeff Wilson, 2013].

The strongest support came from posters who read the drowning, the skull and the Claw resurrections as textual evidence: Fred Kiesche backed a reading that the drowning is followed by a dying man's hallucination [Fred Kiesche, 2008], and Lee Berman argued Severian is immortal and in self-denial [Lee Berman, 2011]. Opponents relied on the text's account of self-resurrection and on the cost of multiplying copies: Ellis [Tony Ellis, 2010], Stockhoff's argument that the deaths are plot puzzles rather than climaxes [David Stockhoff, 2008], Tallman's point that aquastors can age and die [Dave Tallman, 2008], and Stockhoff's Occam's Razor objection to the clone-storage version [David Stockhoff, 2013].

For

  • Greene Carlton proposed that the skull Severian finds in the Gyoll is his own, implying he drowned as a boy and was resurrected [Greene, Carlton, 1999].
  • Son of Witz proposed that Severian died and was resurrected in the opening Gyoll swim, leaving the skull behind [Son of Witz, 2008].
  • Roy C. Lackey argued Severian died on the Ship and was revived as an aquastor, citing surviving injuries that make survival plausible [Roy C. Lackey, 2008].
  • Lee Berman argued Severian dies and is resurrected several times as an eidolon, with his powers bestowed on the Ship [Lee Berman, 2010].
  • Fred Kiesche backed the reading that Severian drowns in the Gyoll and the rest of the books are a dying man's hallucination [Fred Kiesche, 2008].

Against

  • Tony Ellis argued Famulimus's 'son your mother bore' line shows Severian self-resurrects in the same body, so the aquastor theory is unnecessary [Tony Ellis, 2010].
  • Josh Geller argued the drowning is not needed because Severian was resurrected, and rejected the artificial-presence reading [Josh Geller, 2010].
  • David Stockhoff argued Severian's deaths and resurrections are plot puzzles rather than climaxes, so no atonement is implied [David Stockhoff, 2008].
  • Dave Tallman disputed that aquastors or Severian are immortal, noting aquastors can age, be injured and die [Dave Tallman, 2008].
  • David Stockhoff argued against the clone-storage version on Occam's Razor grounds, since a simpler coffin-corpse explanation fits better [David Stockhoff, 2013].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never reached consensus, the aquastor, eidolon and self-resurrection readings each kept their defenders, and the drowning question recurred over fifteen years without being settled.
