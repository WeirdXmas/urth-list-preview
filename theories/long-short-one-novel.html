Long Sun and Short Sun are one work

The Long Sun and Short Sun are one novel, with authorship and narration mirroring each other.

8 threads · 9 posts · for 7 / against 1 · first raised 2001-02-20 in the thread "New Sun/Long Sun/Short Sun as whole (RTTW spoilers)"
Main voices: James Wynn (4), Fernando Q. Gouvea (2), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (1), Don Doggett (1), Stuart Hamm (1)

The debate

The theory that the Long Sun and Short Sun form one novel was first raised on the list by Fernando Q. Gouvea in February 2001, who argued that Horn exists within Silk and that the two books mirror each other as narrator-subject reversals [Fernando Q. Gouvea, 2001][Fernando Q. Gouvea, 2001]. The idea was not revisited substantively until 2004, when James Wynn argued the two books were conceived as one unified story, citing foreshadowing such as Horn imitating Silk and Quetzal's attempt to lead citizens to Green [James Wynn, 2004]. In March 2005 Wynn asked which series readers preferred and compared the two to the Star Wars sequels, while Dan'l Danehy-Oakes agreed they form a single novel, offering only an analogy to reading Urth of the New Sun without the New Sun books [James Wynn, 2005][Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2005].

In 2009 Don Doggett argued that Short Sun continues Long Sun's epilogue and supplies the revelations needed to resolve its plot, responding to Gwern Branwen's point that Long Sun's narrator is unreliable [Don Doggett, 2009]. Stuart Hamm agreed, saying he would take both as a single desert-island book [Stuart Hamm, 2009]. Wynn's 2010 grand unified theory, which linked Pike's ghost, time-travel and Hyacinth to Fava and the Simon Magus story, extended the single-novel claim to a seven-volume novel using textual clues such as the flying Oreb and the green umbrella [James Wynn, 2010]. The posts record the broader threads as largely agreement without substantive debate, with the Grand Unified Theory threads ending in impasse [James Wynn, 2005][James Wynn, 2010].

The only substantive support on the list comes from Gouvea, Wynn, Doggett and Hamm; no post on the list argues against the single-novel view directly. The Horn Dies and mind-body threads mention Wynn's insistence that The Knight and The Wizard Knight must be read as one story, but the record does not show that point being contested on the Long and Short Sun question itself.

For

  • Horn exists within Silk at the end of The Book of the Long Sun, and the symmetric authorship (Silk writing about Horn, Horn writing about Silk) mirrors across the two books [Fernando Q. Gouvea, 2001][Fernando Q. Gouvea, 2001].
  • The two books were conceived as one unified story, with Horn imitating Silk and Quetzal's attempt to lead citizens to Green as foreshadowing [James Wynn, 2004].
  • Reading the Short Sun without the Long Sun is compared to reading Urth of the New Sun without the New Sun books, the poster's only stated ground [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2005].
  • Short Sun continues Long Sun's epilogue and supplies the revelations needed to resolve Long Sun's plot, and Long Sun's narrator is unreliable [Don Doggett, 2009].
  • Wynn's seven-volume reading uses textual clues across both series, such as the flying Oreb, Hyacinth's light weight and a Calde of the Long Sun quotation [James Wynn, 2010].

Where it stands: Widely accepted among the posters who addressed it, with Gouvea, Wynn, Danehy-Oakes, Doggett and Hamm in agreement and no post on the list disputing it, though the support is thin and mostly asserted rather than argued in detail.
