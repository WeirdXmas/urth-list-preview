The Drotte-Roche mix-up: clue or slip

On the first pages of The Shadow of the Torturer Severian swaps Drotte and Roche (who said the men carried pikes). One side reads it as deliberate, a sign that Severian's "perfect" memory fails or that time passes while he writes; the other as an ordinary author's or editor's slip.

7 threads · 16 posts · for 4 / against 12 · first raised 1999-10-18 in the thread "DROTTE VS. ROCHE"
Main voices: David Stockhoff (4), Tony Ellis (3), James Wynn (3), Jeff Wilson (2), António Pedro Marques (2), Jeff Veyera (1)

The debate

The discussion began with Jeff Veyera in 2003, who proposed that Severian's claim to forget nothing is a grim joke and that the faulty memory may belong to a tampered timeline, citing the pikes differing across worlds [Jeff Veyera, 2003]. Jeff Wilson followed in 2008 with a redaction theory that a fourth boy's dialogue is misassigned to hide his presence [Jeff Wilson, 2008], and in 2009 argued that the Roche/Drotte mismatch shows a false recall the narrator records as accurate [Jeff Wilson, 2009].

The main debate came in April 2011, when Tony Ellis argued the swap is an ordinary writer's or editor's slip, noting Severian attributes the pikes to Roche and then, two paragraphs later, to Drotte [Tony Ellis, 2011][Tony Ellis, 2011]. António Pedro Marques countered that the mixup is too prominent for an accident and could be a deliberate device that deceives the reader [António Pedro Marques, 2011], while Lee Berman argued Severian's claimed perfect memory makes it possibly purposeful [Lee Berman, 2011]. David Stockhoff pressed the editorial and authorial-error reading and questioned whether publisher reordering could have caused the confusion [David Stockhoff, 2011][David Stockhoff, 2011].

James Wynn extended the question to Exodus from the Long Sun, where a similar uncorrected Marble/Mint error suggested a Drotte/Roche typo could also be an uncorrected printing error [James Wynn, 2011][James Wynn, 2011], and he offered the deliberate-act option as well [James Wynn, 2011]. Stockhoff and Ellis treated the swap as a typo, while Marques and Berman kept the in-world reading open [David Stockhoff, 2011][António Pedro Marques, 2011]. The threads ended without agreement.

For

  • Severian's claim of perfect memory makes the Drotte/Roche swap possibly purposeful, as Lee Berman argued [Lee Berman, 2011].
  • Jeff Wilson read the mismatch as a false recall the narrator records as accurate, grounded in immersion in his past persona [Jeff Wilson, 2009].
  • António Pedro Marques argued the mixup is too prominent and carefully proofread to be an accident and may be a deceptive narrative device [António Pedro Marques, 2011].
  • James Wynn suggested the swap may be a deliberate act placed right after the perfect-memory claim [James Wynn, 2011].
  • Jeff Veyera linked the faulty memory to a tampered timeline, citing the pikes differing across worlds [Jeff Veyera, 2003].

Against

  • Tony Ellis argued the swap is an ordinary authorial slip, since Severian's pike sighting is attributed to Roche and then, two paragraphs later, to Drotte [Tony Ellis, 2011][Tony Ellis, 2011].
  • Tony Ellis held that Severian would have marked any interruption, so a memory lapse does not explain the change [Tony Ellis, 2011].
  • David Stockhoff argued that every series has a few bloopers and the error rate looks evenly spread, fitting a typo [David Stockhoff, 2011].
  • David Stockhoff proposed that an editorial or narrator slip is more plausible than a clue, pointing to a Calde audio narrator who voiced Blood without naming him [David Stockhoff, 2011].
  • James Wynn noted that Tor leaves Long Sun typos uncorrected, so the Drotte/Roche swap may be a simple printing error [James Wynn, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never reached agreement, with posters split between a typo or editorial error and a deliberate or memory-based reading, and the Marble/Mint parallel leaning some toward an uncorrected typo.
