Thecla lives on inside Severian

Thecla, whose flesh Severian ate at the alzabo feast, is more than a memory in him (the Claw revived her within him, or her voice shapes the narration).

40 threads · 55 posts · for 29 / against 22 · first raised 1998-01-06 by Michael Andre-Driussi
Main voices: Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (5), Jeff Wilson (4), James Jordan (3), Roy C. Lackey (3), Michael Andre-Driussi (2), Michael Straight (2)

The debate

The theory that Thecla lives on inside Severian was first raised on the list by Michael Andre-Driussi in January 1998, who proposed an exultant family background for Severian's mother Catherine [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998]. The idea was soon framed as a resurrection by the Claw, with Tony Ellis and Michael Andre-Driussi both arguing that the Claw revived her [Tony Ellis, 1998] [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998]. Early debate in March 1998 turned on why Thecla stays with Severian when the alzabo's shared memories should fade for others, with Michael Straight and Dan Parmenter answering through Severian's perfect memory [Michael Straight, 1998] [Dan Parmenter, 1998], and adam louis stephanides asking for a textual passage saying she is wholly resurrected [adam louis stephanides, 1998].

The discussion then broadened into the question of whether Thecla's memories intrude on the narration. Jon Camfield and Tony Ellis argued over the 'father's mounted guard' pebbles passage in 1998 [Jon Camfield, 1998] [Tony Ellis, 1998], and Mark Millman and Jeff Wilson revived the intrusion reading in 2006 and 2011 [Mark Millman, 2006] [Jeff Wilson, 2011]. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued in 2007 and 2010 that Severian's Thecla intimacy memories might be fantasies absorbed from her, and that Thecla is fully alive in him [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2007] [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2011]. Roy C. Lackey pressed a pronoun-shift argument in 2008 [Roy C. Lackey, 2008], and in 2008 John Watkins and Jeff Wilson argued Thecla's personality dominates the absorbed Autarch memories [John Watkins, 2008] [Jeff Wilson, 2008].

Later posts ranged from Alan Lewis's claim in 2004 that Thecla's soul is dormant in Severian [Alan Lewis, 2004] to Dan'l Danehy-Oakes's 2013 argument that the forest feast perverts the Eucharist so Severian's copy revives her more fully, which Ross Arlen Tieken supported [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2013] [Ross Arlen Tieken, 2013]. Jeff Wilson disputed a resurrection of the Autarchs' minds but accepted a single Thecla will with confused memories [Jeff Wilson, 2010].

For

  • Severian's perfect memory is offered as the reason Thecla stays with him when others lose the shared memories of the alzabo meal [Michael Straight, 1998] [Dan Parmenter, 1998] [Michael Straight, 1998].
  • The Claw is said to have brought Thecla back to life in his mind, a reading argued by Michael Andre-Driussi and Dave Lebling [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998] [Dave Lebling, 1998].
  • Jeff Wilson argues that only Thecla and the immediate predecessor are active memories and that Thecla appears most often because she is the only passenger memory on view [Jeff Wilson, 2008].
  • Jeff Wilson and David Stockhoff argue Thecla's memories intrude into the narration, making passages like the pebbles scene nonsensical or counterfactual [Jeff Wilson, 2011] [David Stockhoff, 2011].
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argues Severian's resurrection power lets Thecla live again inside him, unlike the former Autarchs [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2010].

Against

  • Tony Ellis and Matthew Malthouse argued the 'father's mounted guard' phrase is a brief slip into Thecla's voice, not a shift to Severian's father speaking [Tony Ellis, 1998] [Jon Camfield, 1998].
  • Jeff Wilson disputed that the Autarchs' minds survive through the alzabo analeptic, noting too few years for hundreds of personalities [Jeff Wilson, 2010].
  • James Wynn argued that a typo claim about Thecla and Thea would need to persist across several editions [James Wynn, 2008].
  • Allan Anderson argued that Severian's reticence about Thecla is a narrative device prompting critical reading, not a fact about her presence [Allan Anderson, 2007].
  • Matthew Knapton argued Severian deceives himself about Thecla's death and their relationship, rather than having a faulty memory [Matthew Knapton, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the posters divided between a Thecla resurrected or living inside Severian, a memory-only presence, and a narrative intrusion reading, and the list reached no consensus.
