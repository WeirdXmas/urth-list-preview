Sandwalker and Eastwind switch identities

Sandwalker and Eastwind switch identities at the end of "A Story", through a bite.

30 threads · 60 posts · for 38 / against 30 · first raised 1998-06-02 by Michael Andre-Driussi
Main voices: Marc Aramini (31), Gerry Quinn (15), Michael Andre-Driussi (7), Tony Ellis (4), James Wynn (4), António Pedro Marques (3)

The debate

The switch reading was first put forward on the list by Marc Aramini in March 2013, when he argued in a post on The Fifth Head of Cerberus that Eastwind survives thinking he is Sandwalker [Marc Aramini, 2013] and, in the same run, that the bite serves as the switch [Marc Aramini, 2013]. Earlier posts had already linked the brothers to Marsch: Maclean Jessica argued in 2007 that Marsch is Sandwalker or Eastwind, citing the cat bite and the lone survivor [Maclean Jessica, 2007], and Tony Ellis disputed the bite itself, saying the cat bites no one in the story [Tony Ellis, 2007].

Discussion picked up in April 2013, when Aramini argued that Sandwalker dies while Eastwind lives [Marc Aramini, 2013], and Gerry Quinn countered that the drowned boy is Eastwind and that a drug made him receptive to the suggestion that he was Sandwalker [Gerry Quinn, 2013]. Richard Simon later proposed that a Shadow Child took Eastwind's form after Eastwind was killed [Richard Simon, 2014]. Quinn also argued that the narrated events in the Sandwalker story should be taken as true [Gerry Quinn, 2014].

In July and August 2014 Aramini restated the claim in several posts, saying Eastwind and Sandwalker are switched after the cat bite [Marc Aramini, 2014], and that Sandwalker, Eastwind and Marsch are all switched by a bite [Marc Aramini, 2014] [Marc Aramini, 2014]. Quinn rejected the reading, arguing that the Sainte Anne aborigines are the only imitative species [Gerry Quinn, 2014]. Aramini then reworded the ending so that each brother believes himself the other [Marc Aramini, 2014] and declined to argue further [Marc Aramini, 2014].

For

  • Aramini proposed that Eastwind survives thinking he is Sandwalker, with the airborne figure being the survivor [Marc Aramini, 2013].
  • Aramini argued that the bite switches Sandwalker, Eastwind and Marsch, and that the white brides are organisms that fit this cycle [Marc Aramini, 2014] [Marc Aramini, 2014].
  • Aramini read Eastwind's lack of testicles, together with his survival, as symbolizing a non-sexual propagation cycle, and António Pedro Marques supported that reading [Marc Aramini, 2014] [António Pedro Marques, 2014].
  • Aramini pointed to parallels such as the heron-like walk, Port Mimizon and the bite as linking Marsch to Eastwind and Sandwalker [Marc Aramini, 2014].
  • Quinn agreed that Eastwind thinks he is Sandwalker after drug-induced suggestion, which backs part of the switch [Gerry Quinn, 2013].

Against

  • Maclean Jessica's original 2007 claim was disputed by Tony Ellis, who argued the cat does not bite anyone in the fifth head story [Tony Ellis, 2007].
  • Aramini argued that Sandwalker, not Eastwind, is the one who dies, which cuts against a survival-swap reading [Marc Aramini, 2013].
  • Quinn argued that the drowned boy is Eastwind and that Sandwalker killed Eastwind before he could speak [Gerry Quinn, 2013].
  • Quinn disputed the bite-based switch and argued the Sainte Anne aborigines are the only imitative species, not two species [Gerry Quinn, 2014].
  • Quinn argued that a third-person telling would have been read as fact, so the Sandwalker story's events should be taken as true rather than as unreliable legend [Gerry Quinn, 2014].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the switch was raised by Aramini and defended over 2013 and 2014, but Quinn and Ellis kept rejecting it and the list never reached agreement, with Aramini ending the exchange without further argument [Marc Aramini, 2014].
