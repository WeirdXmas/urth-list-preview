Pig and Silk

Pig's story is tied to Silk's (Pig attacked Silk at the manse; Silk possesses Pig; Horn's gift of an eye to Pig restores Silk).

51 threads · 67 posts · for 34 / against 24 · first raised 1999-10-19 by William H. Ansley
Main voices: Marc Aramini (9), Robert Borski (7), Adam Stephanides (7), James Jordan (6), Michael Andre-Driussi (5), Michael Straight (5)

The debate

The Pig-and-Silk link was first raised on 1999-10-19, when William H. Ansley argued that Horn gave his eye to Pig rather than to Marble [William H. Ansley, 1999], and Alice Turner supported the idea that Pig stays blind until an eye exchange [Alice Turner, 1999]. The theory widened in February 2001, during the Return to the Whorl discussion. Tim Boolos asked why Silk possessed Pig [Tim Boolos, 2001], and Joel Priddy answered that Silk was a god who possessed Pig for no special reason [Joel Priddy, 2001]. Jeffrey Meyers reported that the eye gift is explicitly stated in a late chapter, which Adam Stephanides had denied [Jeffrey Meyers, 2001]. Robert Borski argued that Pig killed the augur who was really Silk [Robert Borski, 2001] and later that Pig assassinated Silk at the manse [Robert Borski, 2001], while Alex David Groce [Alex David Groce, 2001] and Dave Lebling [Dave Lebling, 2001] disputed the killing reading.

In 2002 the debate moved to Silk-23 and Mainframe transfers. James Jordan proposed that Silk-in-Pig moves into Silk [James Jordan, 2002], and Adam Stephanides listed five textual problems with the idea that Horn plans to move Silk-in-Pig into Silk's body [Adam Stephanides, 2002]. Michael Andre-Driussi argued that Horn himself quixotically sacrifices an eye to free Silk-in-Pig [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2002], while Michael Straight argued Horn wants Silk-in-Pig out rather than possessed [Michael Straight, 2002]. Marc Aramini, with Mooncalf, revived the Pig-eye restoration idea in 2003, arguing that Silk's quest completes when Pig regains his eyes [Marc Aramini, 2003] and that Silk's divine part was trapped in Pig [Mooncalf, 2003]. In 2005 Aramini proposed that Silver Silk was downloaded into Pig [Marc Aramini, 2005], and Stephanides rejected the timing as incompatible with the bandage and Pig's later speech [Adam Stephanides, 2005]. Later posters such as John Watkins [John Watkins, 2009], Aramini [Marc Aramini, 2011] and Roy Lackey [Roy C. Lackey, 2011] kept variants alive, and Aramini's 2012 post [Marc Aramini, 2012] conflated Silk with Babbie and blind Pig.

The main arguments for were the explicit eye-gift statement and the Silk-possesses-Pig reading, while the main objections concerned the lack of textual support for the transfer routes, the timing problems, and the blindness of other candidates. The thread never reached agreement on how or whether Silk's soul passes through Pig's eye.

For

  • Horn gave his eye to Pig rather than to Marble, as Ansley argued from the Galatians passage and other text [William H. Ansley, 1999].
  • Jeffrey Meyers reported that the eye gift to Pig is explicitly stated in a late chapter of Return to the Whorl [Jeffrey Meyers, 2001].
  • Joel Priddy argued Silk, a god, possessed Pig and that Silk's wounds healed once the controlling mind stopped binding them [Joel Priddy, 2001].
  • Marc Aramini argued that the ending of Return to the Whorl saves Silk when Pig's eye restores him to his own body [Marc Aramini, 2011].
  • Mooncalf proposed that Silk erased himself and that his divine part was trapped in Pig when Pig lost his eyes [Mooncalf, 2003].

Against

  • Adam Stephanides said the eye transfer to Pig is never stated in the text and suspected the editors invented the mystery [Jeffrey Meyers, 2001].
  • Adam Stephanides argued that Silver Silk could not move from Pig into Horn at the glass scenes because the bandage is still on [Adam Stephanides, 2005].
  • Adam Stephanides listed five textual problems with the theory that Horn plans to move Silk-in-Pig into Silk's body [Adam Stephanides, 2002].
  • Dave Lebling rejected the theory that Pig killed Silk as implausible [Dave Lebling, 2001].
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued Tartaros is incurably blind and so cannot see through Pig's eyes [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2001]; Roy Lackey argued the reboot of Silver Silk did not depend on Pig's eyesight [Roy C. Lackey, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the eye gift to Pig is widely treated as stated in the text, but the routes by which Silk's soul or copy passes through Pig remain unresolved and contested.
