The Whorl travels near light speed

The Whorl travelled near the speed of light, so far more time passed outside than inside.

21 threads · 25 posts · for 14 / against 10 · first raised 1999-10-12 by Kevin J. Maroney
Main voices: Michael Andre-Driussi (2), James Wynn (2), Gwern Branwen (2), Lee Berman (2), Jack Smith (2), Sergei SOLOVIEV (2)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by Kevin J. Maroney in 1999, who proposed that relativistic distortion explained the Whorl's long transit and the timing of Blue and Green [Kevin J. Maroney, 1999]. Talarican followed in 2001 with a detailed case for relativistic velocity, using the 332-year subjective voyage and Urth's roughly thousand-year interval [Talarican, 2001]. Through 2002 Michael Andre-Driussi and Charles Reed took opposing views, with Andre-Driussi calculating about 687 light years at 0.98 to 0.99 c and Reed insisting the Whorl orbited the Short Sun at sub-light speeds [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2002][Charles Reed, 2002].

The discussion grew over 2006 to 2011, when thalassocrat, Gwern Branwen, Roy C. Lackey, Jack Smith, and Jane Delawney argued that near-light speed travel explained the gap between the Whorl's roughly 300 ship years and the much longer Urth interval [thalassocrat, 2006][Roy C. Lackey, 2007][Jack Smith, 2010][Jane Delawney, 2011]. Gwern Branwen later reversed course, arguing that slowing the ship would require impossible energy [Gwern Branwen, 2010]. Sergei Soloviev and David Duffy questioned the fusion energy budget and proposed a slower 0.1 c trip without dilation [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011][David Duffy, 2011], while Jeff Wilson argued that relativistic effects would limit the acceleration itself [Jeff Wilson, 2011].

Later posts from 2011 to 2013 brought Gerry Quinn, Lee Berman, and Andreas Johansson back to time dilation, with Quinn proposing it for the 300 versus 2000-year gap and Johansson raising it as an answer to the chronology [Gerry Quinn, 2011][Lee Berman, 2013][Andreas Johansson, 2013]. The thread debates record no decisive text or Wolfe remark settling the matter; the strongest textual anchor cited was Wolfe's interview and the Urth chronology, which posters read in opposing ways.

For

  • Maroney proposed relativistic distortion of the Whorl's transit as the explanation for the long gap [Kevin J. Maroney, 1999]
  • Talarican argued from the 332-year voyage and Urth's roughly thousand-year interval that the Whorl attained relativistic velocity [Talarican, 2001]
  • Andre-Driussi calculated about 687 light years for Urth to Bleen at 0.98 to 0.99 c with 10:1 dilation [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2002]
  • Lackey recalled an interview with Jim Jordan in which Wolfe seemed to agree that relativistic flight explained the thousand-year gap [Roy C. Lackey, 2007]
  • Jack Smith worked through the energy and acceleration phases needed to fit the voyage into 300 ship years [Jack Smith, 2010]

Against

  • Reed argued the narrator should be taken at face value and the Whorl orbited the Short Sun at sub-light speed [Charles Reed, 2002]
  • Gwern Branwen argued that slowing the Whorl would need near-light speeds and impossible energy, so no significant dilation occurred [Gwern Branwen, 2010]
  • Sergei Soloviev argued that fusion cannot supply enough energy to accelerate the Whorl near light speed [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011]
  • David Duffy proposed a 50-70 light year trip at 0.1 c, avoiding relativistic dilation entirely [David Duffy, 2011]
  • Jeff Wilson argued that time dilation and length contraction would make the Whorl's acceleration non-constant as seen from outside [Jeff Wilson, 2011]

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never reached agreement, several posters abandoned or reversed the theory, and it was raised repeatedly without a settled answer.
