The Whorl and Tzadkiel's ship are related

The Whorl is a precursor or corrupt copy of Tzadkiel's ship.

8 threads · 7 posts · for 2 / against 5 · first raised 2002-03-26 by Dan'l Danehy-Oakes
Main voices: Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (1), Roy C. Lackey (1), Lisa Schaffer-Doggett (1), Jeff Veyera (1), James Wynn (1), Chris P (1)

The debate

The theory that the Whorl is a precursor or corrupt copy of Tzadkiel's ship surfaces on the list first in March 2002, when Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued the Whorl returns to Urth because Tzadkiel or its masters reroute it to repopulate the world [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002]. Michael Andre-Driussi, in the Space Vampires thread, pressed the same colonization-ark reading, citing the Talos play, the sailors' wager, and guards' reports of ships during the deluge, and Matthew Malthouse backed the Tzadkiel-ark view [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002]. The debate sharpened in August 2003, when Lisa Schaffer-Doggett reported that Don saw the corrupted-ship idea as a likely scenario and suggested the Whorl might be a lesser imitation of Tzadkiel's ship, linking Silk's departure to Yesod [Lisa Schaffer-Doggett, 2003]. Ashley Crill's 'One Ship' argument, that the two are one ship with Tzadkiel's as archetype, was the most-cited version of this idea in the Two Ships and related threads, and James Jordan and Schaffer-Doggett backed it.

Opponents argued mainly from physics and textual evidence. Roy C. Lackey held that the Whorl seen from Blue cannot have been carried inside Tzadkiel's ship, and that Jonas is a chem rather than a suit like Sidero [Roy C. Lackey, 2003]. Jeff Veyera argued that Tzadkiel crossed between universes while the Whorl never left normal space, so a hyperspace-capable ship would not have been a generational starship [Jeff Veyera, 2003]. James Wynn and Chris argued that FTL would not make generation ships obsolete, with Wynn suggesting the Whorl could be a primitive Tzadkiel, a position that partly conceded the link while keeping it open [James Wynn, 2003][Chris P, 2003]. Sergei Soloviev and Gerry Quinn also rejected or qualified the identification in the Science Fiction and Fantasy Question thread, with Quinn noting that the Ship is not the only interstellar craft [Lee Berman, 2012].

The later discussion largely consisted of threads on FTL speed, chems on Urth, and general Sun-series inconsistencies, in which the precursor reading was often treated as one option among several. Lee Berman's 2012 post judged the link possible in principle but probably unintended, since the two ships share little structure or function, and grounded this in the absence of textual clues, especially at the end of Return to the Whorl [Lee Berman, 2012]. The record contains no later turning point that settled the question.

For

  • The Whorl is a corrupted or imitation version of Tzadkiel's ship, supported by Lisa Schaffer-Doggett's report of Don's agreement [Lisa Schaffer-Doggett, 2003].
  • The Whorl returns to Urth because Tzadkiel or its masters reroute it to repopulate the world, a motive Dan'l Danehy-Oakes called more plausible than Typhon's concern for posterity [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002].
  • Guards' reports of ships during the deluge and the Talos play point to Tzadkiel's ship as the ark, as Michael Andre-Driussi argued [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002].
  • Ashley Crill's One Ship theory treats Tzadkiel's ship as the archetype of the Whorl, a reading James Jordan backed.
  • James Wynn suggested the Whorl could be a primitive Tzadkiel, a precursor reading that partly accepts the link [James Wynn, 2003].

Against

  • The Whorl seen from Blue cannot have been carried inside Tzadkiel's ship, according to Roy C. Lackey, who cited the Whorl's naked-eye visibility in Exodus [Roy C. Lackey, 2003].
  • Tzadkiel crossed between universes while the Whorl never left Briah, so the two are different kinds of ship, as Jeff Veyera argued [Jeff Veyera, 2003].
  • An FTL ship would not make generation ships obsolete on the same world, a jet-and-triplane point Chris made against the link [Chris P, 2003].
  • Lee Berman argued Wolfe probably did not intend a precursor relation because the two ships share little structure or function and the text gives no clues [Lee Berman, 2012].
  • Gerry Quinn and António Pedro Marques disputed the identification on the Whorl's asteroid shape, lack of sails and limited lifespan, and Quinn noted the Ship is not the only interstellar craft [Lee Berman, 2012].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the theory was raised repeatedly from 2002 to 2012 and never reached consensus, with most later posters treating the precursor link as possible but unconfirmed by the text.
