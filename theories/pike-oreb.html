Pike's ghost is in Oreb

Patera Pike's ghost lives on in Oreb, or acts through him.

11 threads · 14 posts · for 4 / against 9 · first raised 2008-08-13 in the thread "A second dream team: Mucor, Oreb, and Pike"
Main voices: James Wynn (6), Marc Aramini (5), Roy C. Lackey (2), Dave Tallman (1), Gerry Quinn (1), Larry Miller (1)

The debate

The theory that Patera Pike's ghost lives on in Oreb, or acts through him, first appears on the list in September 2008, when James Wynn argued that Oreb's astral form reflects possession by Scylla and that the ghost-Oreb encounter is Pike rather than a rationalization [James Wynn, 2008]. Through 2011 Wynn kept developing related readings, proposing that Pike's ghost is the Rajan and Oreb passing through and that the flying bird Silk sees is Pike's astral Oreb [James Wynn, 2011][James Wynn, 2011]. Roy Lackey and Gerry Quinn later answered that Mucor, Oreb and Pike's ghost must be explained together or kept separate, and Lackey argued the manse sounds were Pike's ghost rather than Oreb and Mucor [Gerry Quinn, 2011][Roy C. Lackey, 2011].

The debate grew heated across overlapping threads in late 2011. Dave Tallman and Marc Aramini supported a reading in which the Pike and Oreb apparition is Silk's older self travelling back in time, while Aramini also proposed mainframe properties and mistaken identity as alternatives [Marc Aramini, 2011][Marc Aramini, 2011]. Wynn countered that the Mucor standing over Silk is the Mucor Horn consulted, and Lackey declined to keep trading arguments with him, promising a later solution that avoids time travel [James Wynn, 2011][Roy C. Lackey, 2011]. Lackey and Gerry Quinn disputed the timeline with Wynn, and the thread on Pike's ghost concluded that Wynn conceded the timeline point while insisting Mucor and Silk may be lying [James Wynn, 2011].

In 2013 the theory was revived in a narrower form when Marc Aramini proposed that Oreb possessed Pike, the figure glimpsed in a calotte, and Larry Miller disputed the link on timing grounds, since Pike appears minutes and pages after Oreb's descent [Marc Aramini, 2013][Larry Miller, 2013]. Across the threads, Pedro Pereira argued for physical ghosts in the Solar Cycle, and Tallman later revised his own view so that Silk could have been mistaken for Pike [James Wynn, 2011]. The discussion repeatedly ended without agreement.

For

  • Pike's ghost is the Rajan and Oreb passing through, per Wynn's reading of the Long and Short Sun clone and dream-travel clues [James Wynn, 2011].
  • Pike's ghost waves and vanishes like a beamed-up figure, making a supernatural reading of it less fitting [James Wynn, 2011].
  • The Oreb flying from the window is most likely Pike's astral companion, citing the limited number of night choughs in the books [James Wynn, 2011].
  • Oreb possessed Pike, the figure glimpsed in the calotte, which Aramini linked to an old woman's story in In Green's Jungles [Marc Aramini, 2013].
  • Mucor possessing Oreb and Pike's ghost being a real ghost, proposed by Tallman, with Gerry Quinn adding that Pike's ghost is Mucor's grandfather [James Wynn, 2008][Gerry Quinn, 2011].

Against

  • Pike's ghost, Mucor and Oreb must be explained together, and the rival view is no less speculative [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • The manse sounds were Pike's ghost, since the ghost appeared minutes later while Oreb and Mucor were alive [Roy C. Lackey, 2011].
  • Pike appears minutes and pages after Oreb's descent, so the events are separate [Larry Miller, 2013].
  • Lackey's solution avoids time travel and separates the Mucor and Oreb events from Pike's ghost, which the text itself divides [Roy C. Lackey, 2011].
  • Pike's ghost is not a conventional ghost but a figure that could be a supernatural or naturalistic ghost, which Wynn disputed on the basis of Oreb's later astral appearance [James Wynn, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the theory was never agreed, most threads ended in impasse, and the posters divided over time travel, astral projection, real ghosts and separate events.
