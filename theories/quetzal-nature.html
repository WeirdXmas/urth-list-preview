Quetzal's nature and aims

Prolocutor Quetzal is an inhumu who has been aboard the Whorl since the start (or replaced a human), a serpent figure whose aims tie into the Plan of Pas.

67 threads · 85 posts · for 50 / against 24 · first raised 1996-12-03 by James Jordan
Main voices: James Wynn (11), James Jordan (9), Dave Lebling (5), Michael Andre-Driussi (5), Charles Reed (5), Lisa Schaffer-Doggett (4)

The debate

The idea that Quetzal is an inhumu was first raised on the list by James Jordan on 1996-12-03, who called him a converted vampire possibly behind Typhon's ship [James Jordan, 1996], and Jordan developed the converted-inhumi reading in January 1997 with Catholic typology [James Jordan, 1997]. Derek Bell added a vampire-like inhumi feeding on the crew [Derek Bell, 1997], while Jeffrey Meyers argued Quetzal was benevolent rather than sinister [Jeffrey Meyers, 1997] and Dave Lebling questioned what his intent was [Dave Lebling, 1997].

In 1997 the discussion centred on serpent imagery and Quetzalcoatl. Charles Dye said the shapeshifting figure in Lake was an aquastor, not Quetzal [Charles Dye, 1997], while Lebling argued the Calde serpent scene first reveals Quetzal as an inhumu [Dave Lebling, 1997]. Lebling also linked the name to Quetzalcoatl and proposed Quetzal had been aboard for most of the Whorl's time in Blue and Green [Dave Lebling, 1997] [Dave Lebling, 1997]. Alice Turner defended the claim that Quetzal was aboard from the start [Alice Turner, 1997]. Michael Andre-Driussi mapped Quetzal onto the brazen serpent in a Moses reading [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997], which Kieran Mullen rejected as devilish [Kieran Mullen, 1997]. Mary Whalen's 1998 posts on Quetzalcoatl worship were informational only [mary whalen, 1998].

From 2002 to 2003 the Urth question and the Typhon link dominated. Charles Reed argued Quetzal could not have been on Urth or the Whorl from the beginning [Charles Reed, 2002], and James Jordan agreed that the text shows no inhumi on Urth [James Jordan, 2002]. Jeff Veyera argued Quetzal manipulated Typhon from Urth via the cobra in the A-man and Wo-man story [Jeff Veyera, 2002]. James Wynn conceded that a quoted Wolfe remark, that Typhon did not know of the inhumi, undercut his manipulation theory [James Wynn, 2002]. In 2003 Lisa Schaffer-Doggett and James Wynn proposed Typhon or clone links [Lisa Schaffer-Doggett, 2003] [James Wynn, 2003], Jordan granted only a symbolic link [James Jordan, 2003], and Roy Lackey argued the timeline made a feeding link impossible [Roy C. Lackey, 2003].

From 2009 to 2011 the replacement and impersonation readings took over. Ashley Crill and Stuart Hamm proposed an inhumi returning with an embryo in a revised First Exodus theory [Ashley Crill, 2011] [Stuart Hamm, 2011], and Crill argued Quetzal could have been replaced early in his career [Ashley Crill, 2011]. Roy Lackey argued he was a physical inhumu passing for human [Roy C. Lackey, 2011]. Gerry Quinn and António Pedro Marques questioned his plans and whether he was too weak to plan them [Gerry Quinn, 2011] [António Pedro Marques, 2011]. Thread summaries record the Wolfe remark on Quetzal's late arrival as a settling point, though these threads drifted without a clear agreement. Lee Berman later separated Remora from Quetzal's kind [Lee Berman, 2013].

For

  • Quetzal feeds on the crew, and Teasel's possible bat bite fits the vampire tradition [Derek Bell, 1997].
  • Quetzal may be a converted inhumi, with Catholic typology of a good or bad Pope as the frame [James Jordan, 1997].
  • The serpent scene in Calde is the first point where Quetzal is revealed as an inhumu [Dave Lebling, 1997].
  • The name Quetzal points to Quetzalcoatl, with the quetzal bird as that god's symbol [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2001] [Dave Lebling, 1997].
  • Typhon could have been manipulated from Urth by an inhumi Quetzal, based on the cobra in the A-man and Wo-man story [Jeff Veyera, 2002].

Against

  • Quetzal may be benevolent, since he struggles between blood-hunger and protecting the group [Jeffrey Meyers, 1997].
  • The shapeshifting ghost in Lake is an aquastor rather than Quetzal in disguise [Charles Dye, 1997].
  • Quetzal's serpentine image marks him as devilish, which rules him out as a Moses figure [Kieran Mullen, 1997].
  • The text gives no evidence of inhumi on Urth or of Quetzal being aboard from the beginning [Charles Reed, 2002] [James Jordan, 2002].
  • A quoted Wolfe remark that Typhon did not know of the inhumi undercuts the Typhon-manipulation reading [James Wynn, 2002] [James Jordan, 2003].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never reached agreement on Quetzal's origin or aims, though the Wolfe remark about Typhon and the late-arrival remark narrowed the options and most posters moved away from a from-the-start reading.
