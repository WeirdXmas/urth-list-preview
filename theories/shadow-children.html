Who the Shadow Children were

The Shadow Children of "A Story" are the indigenous shapechangers (or earlier human settlers who degenerated, or a group mind spread by infection).

84 threads · 194 posts · for 135 / against 93 · first raised 1998-05-19 by Robert Borski
Main voices: Lee Berman (48), Marc Aramini (44), Gerry Quinn (43), Tony Ellis (28), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (10), James Wynn (9)

The debate

The earliest post on the list is Robert Borski's 1998 post [Robert Borski, 1998], which treats the Shadow Children as possibly paired planets or moons rather than shapechangers. The indigenous-shapechanger framing first appears in Tony Ellis's post of 1998-06-02, [Tony Ellis, 1998], which contrasts indigenous shape-changers with an earlier human colonization. Other 1998 posts offered a range of rival readings: Neal Smith linked the Shadow Children's drug to native spaciness in [Neal Smith, 1998], Alice Turner suggested the Shadow Children smoke an addictive substance in [Alice Turner, 1998], and mary whalen argued in [mary whalen, 1998] that they were drug-addicted technological humans from Earth.

The debate split early between indigenous and degenerate-human readings. Mitchell A. Bailey argued in [Mitchell A. Bailey, 1999] that the Shadow Children are degenerate drugged humans, while Ellis argued in [Tony Ellis, 1999] that they were St Anne's first inhabitants, citing the Old Wise One's remark 'we were always here'. Adam Stephanides argued in [Adam Stephanides, 2002] that the text says the ancestors arrived in a starcrosser, and in 2002 Ellis and Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued over French colonists versus indigenous origins in [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002] and [Tony Ellis, 2002]. Ellis proposed indigenous readings again in [Tony Ellis, 2006] in 2006.

From 2008 to 2014 the question was reopened in the context of VRT and Marsch. E argued in [E, 2008] that the narrator of 'A Story' was a shadowchild, which James Crossley disputed in [James Crossley, 2008] with an account of original inhabitants who mimicked colonists. Lee Berman then argued in [Lee Berman, 2010] and [Lee Berman, 2014] that the Shadow Children are a native shape-changing people or a primitive form of the Abos, while James Wynn in [James Wynn, 2010] defended stunted spaceship descendants. Marc Aramini's 2013-2014 posts, such as [Marc Aramini, 2013] and [Marc Aramini, 2014], moved toward parasitic and infection readings. Several threads, including 'Shadow Children and Inhumi', 'Atlantis and Gondwanaland' and 'Fifth Head of Cerberus: Shadowchildren vs. Abos', ended without agreement.

For

  • Ellis argued in [Tony Ellis, 2006] that the Shadow Children are the native creatures of St Anne, citing the Old Wise One's claim that his people were always there.
  • Ellis argued in [Tony Ellis, 1999] that the Shadow Children were St Anne's first inhabitants, citing witness accounts from Mary Blount and Robert Culot.
  • Lee Berman argued in [Lee Berman, 2010] that the Shadow Children are a native shape-changing people of Ste. Anne, with the aborigines as their human descendants.
  • Crossley argued in [James Crossley, 2008] that the shadow-children were the planet's original inhabitants who took humanoid forms by mimicking colonists.
  • b sharp argued in [b sharp, 2008] that the shadow children became the abos by mimicking the first colonists, and extended the view to replaced humans.

Against

  • Bailey argued in [Mitchell A. Bailey, 1999] that the Shadow Children are degenerate drugged humans, with the shape-changing abos as their ancestors.
  • Gerry Quinn argued in [Gerry Quinn, 2012] that the Shadow Children are devolved human descendants and that the Old Wise One's remarks are confused.
  • Ellis argued in [Tony Ellis, 2011] that the Shadow Children are deluded fantasists who wrongly believe they are human.
  • Stephanides argued in [Adam Stephanides, 2002] that the text says the Shadow Children's ancestors arrived in a starcrosser, meaning a spaceship.
  • Wynn argued in [James Wynn, 2010] that the Shadow Children are stunted descendants of spaceship humans, doubting the Old Wise One's account.

Where it stands: Still disputed: the discussion ran from 1998 to 2014 across many threads and ended in impasse each time, with no shared reading reached.
