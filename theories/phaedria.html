Phaedria is Number Five's sister

Phaedria is Number Five's sister or half-sister.

18 threads · 19 posts · for 12 / against 7 · first raised 1998-04-04 by Robert Borski
Main voices: Robert Borski (7), Lee Berman (4), Tony Ellis (2), Jim Raylor (2), Gerry Quinn (2), John Bishop (1)

The debate

The theory that Phaedria is Number Five's sister was first raised in April 1998 by Robert Borski, who proposed a numbering scheme for the clones in The Fifth Head of Cerberus and argued she is Number Five's biological sister, citing Maitre's brokered children, her broken ankle, and the Phaedra name [Robert Borski, 1998]. Tony Ellis disputed the genealogy, arguing that Mr. Million is counted twice and that an earlier Wolfe must stand behind him [Tony Ellis, 1998][Tony Ellis, 1998]. Borski replied that the generations fit within 140 years and cited page references on the family and the incest connotations of the name [Robert Borski, 1998]. Over the next days the exchange shifted: Ellis defended Phaedria as Maitre's sister, while Borski argued from her violet eyes that Maitre is her father [Tony Ellis, 1998][Robert Borski, 1998].

Later posts in the list pursued the theory less directly. Borski proposed that David and Phaedria may be twins and tied the names to incest imagery [Robert Borski, 1998], and he argued for a horizontal configuration in which Phaedria is the virgin [Robert Borski, 1998]. Mark Millman disputed the natural origin of her violet eyes, suggesting cosmetic surgery [Mark Millman, 1998]. In 2002 Jerry Friedman reported that a source glossed Phaedrus as 'wolf' in support of Borski's half-sister claim, though a phaedrusdeinus.org page called that etymology false [Jerry Friedman, 2002]. Interest then moved to comparisons with Anne Shirley, Thecla, and the paracoita, and to the ending's 'us' and child [Jim Raylor, 2005][Lee Berman, 2010][Gerry Quinn, 2010].

The thread debates most often mentioned the sister theory only in passing, and the record contains no post that confirms it. Its main attention went to other readings of Phaedria, such as the child she is with, Number 6 or the narrator, and to the ankle being literal [James Wynn, 2010][Gerry Quinn, 2014].

For

  • Robert Borski first proposed that Phaedria is Number Five's biological sister, citing Maitre's brokered children, her broken ankle, and the Phaedra name [Robert Borski, 1998].
  • Borski argued that the generations fit within 140 years and cited page references on the family and the broken ankle [Robert Borski, 1998].
  • Jerry Friedman reported that Phaedrus was said to mean 'wolf', which Borski used to support the half-sister claim [Jerry Friedman, 2002].

Against

  • Tony Ellis argued the generation count was flawed because Mr. Million is counted twice and an earlier Wolfe must stand behind him [Tony Ellis, 1998][Tony Ellis, 1998].
  • Ellis argued that Phaedria is Maitre's sister rather than Number Five's, pointing to the ankle injury and family resemblance [Tony Ellis, 1998].
  • Mark Millman argued that Phaedria's violet eyes may come from cosmetic surgery, so the eye-color evidence for kinship is uncertain [Mark Millman, 1998].
  • A phaedrusdeinus.org page cited by Friedman called the 'wolf' etymology false [Jerry Friedman, 2002].

Where it stands: Raised by Borski in 1998 and argued mainly between him and Ellis in that year, then largely dropped; the list never reached agreement and the record shows no later consensus.
