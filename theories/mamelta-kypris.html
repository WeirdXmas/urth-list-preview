Mamelta is Kypris

Mamelta, the sleeper from the ship, is (or carries) the goddess Kypris.

24 threads · 52 posts · for 27 / against 25 · first raised 1997-02-23 by Alice Turner
Main voices: Marc Aramini (25), James Wynn (9), Gerry Quinn (7), Lee Berman (4), Roy C. Lackey (3), Andrew Mason (2)

The debate

The theory that Mamelta is Kypris was first raised on the list in July 2005, when James Wynn recalled an earlier discussion and asked for the original posts to be reposted [James Wynn, 2005]. Marc Aramini then proposed it directly, matching names, blue eyes, dark hair and Silk's attraction to her, and grounding it in the ladder scene and Silk's wish to stay awake until his mother came [Marc Aramini, 2005]. Malcolm McGugan added Litany passages linking Kypris with Hyacinth and Mother [Malcolm McGugan, 2005]. Roy C. Lackey was the first to dispute it, using height, since Hyacinth/Kypris is described as short and Mamelta is tall [Roy C. Lackey, 2005]. Wynn replied that souls in the Mainframe appear as their owners see themselves, so looks do not decide identity [James Wynn, 2005], and Lackey answered that Mamelta's Chenille appearance was a single disguise and that Jahlee did not carry Chenille's soul [Roy C. Lackey, 2005].

From 2008 to 2011 Aramini was the theory's main and most persistent advocate, repeatedly restating it with quotations from Litany and Epiphany of the Long Sun [Marc Aramini, 2008] [Marc Aramini, 2008] [Marc Aramini, 2011]. Gerry Quinn became its principal critic in December 2010, arguing that Mamelta is an ordinary Sleeper with an ordinary technician's past and that her speech and memories do not fit Kypris [Gerry Quinn, 2010] [Gerry Quinn, 2010]. Andrew Mason accepted that Mamelta might be Silk's mother but not Kypris [Andrew Mason, 2010], and Roy Lackey pressed the rarity-of-blue-eyes objection [Roy C. Lackey, 2011]. Aramini responded with dream-equation arguments and the claim that Mucor's possession or Typhon's powers could explain the discrepancies [Marc Aramini, 2010] [Gerry Quinn, 2011].

The discussion stayed mostly between Aramini and Quinn through the 2010s. Aramini's later posts ran to 2013, when he asserted that Wolfe's symbols are literal and that Kypris is Hyacinth, Mamelta, Chenille and Silk's mother [Marc Aramini, 2013] [Marc Aramini, 2013]. Quinn disputed this as a cipher reading that leaves characters no life [Gerry Quinn, 2013]. Daniel Petersen partly backed Aramini's reading of the Outsider's role [Daniel Petersen, 2013], and Lee Berman offered a broader line from Mamelta through Hyacinth and Kypris to Seawrack [Lee Berman, 2013]. The posts do not show the question being settled.

For

  • Mamelta and Hyacinth share blue eyes and dark hair, and Silk is drawn to Mamelta as he was to the mother he seeks, per Marc Aramini [Marc Aramini, 2005] [Marc Aramini, 2010].
  • The ladder scene, Silk's sense of home there, and the vision that he will not sleep until his mother comes are cited as linking Mamelta to Kypris [Marc Aramini, 2005] [Malcolm McGugan, 2005].
  • Litany and Epiphany passages in which Kypris is called Hyacinth and Mother are read as linking Kypris to Mamelta [Marc Aramini, 2010] [Marc Aramini, 2008].
  • Souls in the Mainframe appear as their owners see themselves, so Mamelta's looks need not match Kypris's, per James Wynn [James Wynn, 2005].
  • Aramini argued that dream sequences equating Mamelta, Kypris and Mother should be taken literally, as Wolfe elides and writes cryptically [Marc Aramini, 2011] [Marc Aramini, 2011].

Against

  • Mamelta is tall while Hyacinth/Kypris is short and petite, per Roy Lackey using Chenille's remark and the airship scene [Roy C. Lackey, 2005].
  • Mamelta is an ordinary Sleeper with an ordinary Urth life and training as a computer technician, per Gerry Quinn [Gerry Quinn, 2010] [Gerry Quinn, 2013].
  • Mamelta's speech, memories and manner do not match Kypris's character and history, per Gerry Quinn [Gerry Quinn, 2010].
  • Blue eyes are common in Viron and the Calde text ties Kypris to Mamelta and Hyacinth but not to Chenille, per Roy Lackey [Roy C. Lackey, 2011].
  • Kypris's hair colour is never stated in the text, which Aramini conceded weakens the link [Marc Aramini, 2011]; Andrew Mason accepted Mamelta as Silk's mother but not Kypris [Andrew Mason, 2010].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the theory was pressed by Marc Aramini across many years and opposed by Gerry Quinn, Roy Lackey and others, and the list never reached agreement.
