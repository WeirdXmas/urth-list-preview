Pas is a Neighbor

Pas, the god of the Whorl, is (or is in league with) a Neighbor.

11 threads · 12 posts · for 5 / against 7 · first raised 2000-08-11 in the thread "Typhon's Home: Pas as Neighbor"
Main voices: Robert Borski (4), James Jordan (3), Kevin J. Maroney (2), Alex David Groce (1), Michael Straight (1), Michael Andre-Driussi (1)

The debate

The theory that Pas is a Neighbor was first raised on the list by Robert Borski, who in March 2001 argued that Pas is a Neighbor returning home, citing why the Whorl targeted the Blue-Green system and the shared two heads of Pas and the Neighbors. Kevin J. Maroney initially questioned the claim and proposed instead that Horn fears he is no longer human after translation into a new body. Within days, Alex David Groce and Maroney himself disputed the idea, Groce arguing Typhon in The Sword of the Lictor does not resemble a Neighbor, and Maroney noting the second head is explicitly artificial.

The debate broadened in March 2001 when James Jordan argued Pas is probably human, listing objections such as Typhon's human wife, children and lover, and then relayed a scenario, drawn from Borski, in which Pas learned Neighbor language and passed it to Horn through a Silk download. Borski later proposed that the Neighbour Windcloud in Return to the Whorl might be Pas himself, given the meaning of Typhon/typhoon. In April 2001 Borski restated his view that Pas and the Mainframe gods are shape-shifting Neighbor-type aliens, while another poster rejected the wider Pig and Mother theories that accompanied it.

In July 2002 the theory was pulled into a dispute over Silk-23 and Hyacinth's death. Michael Straight argued that Pas and the Neighbors work in league [Michael Straight, 2002], while Michael Andre-Driussi countered that they work at cross-purposes [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2002]. In 2011 Lee Berman noted that Borski's book The Long and Short of It repeats the claim that Pas is a Neighbor, though mainly discusses other works [Lee Berman, 2011]. Later threads show the idea returning to smaller questions, such as Horn's Neighborly ability and astral travel, where Michael Straight disputed the wider Pas-as-Neighbor reading.

For

  • Pas is a Neighbor returning home, which would explain why the Whorl targeted the Blue-Green system and the shared two heads of Pas and the Neighbors.
  • Hide's dream in Return to the Whorl, Horn's Neighborese, and Scylla's knowledge of the language are cited as support.
  • Windcloud in Return to the Whorl may be Pas, based on the meaning of Typhon/typhoon and Pas's depiction as a whirlwind.
  • Pas and the other Mainframe gods are shape-shifting Neighbor-type aliens, consistent with Typhon grafted onto Piaton and the Mother-Daughter identities.
  • Pas learned Neighbor language from the Neighbors and passed it to Horn via a Silk download, a scenario relayed by James Jordan.

Against

  • Typhon in The Sword of the Lictor does not resemble a Neighbor, and a human head grafted onto a Neighbor seems implausible.
  • The second head of Pas is explicitly artificial, and the Blue-Green choice lacks evidence.
  • Pas is probably human, given Typhon's human wife, children and lover and the lack of any odd head described.
  • Horn's fear may be about losing humanity after translation rather than any Neighbor link.
  • Pig's clear recognition of the Narrator as Silk and Scylla's human pre-adolescence undercut the wider Neighbor-type alien reading.

Where it stands: Still disputed and mostly rejected: the Neighbor theory was argued by Borski over several years but most posters on the list, including Groce, Maroney and Jordan, disputed it, and it was never resolved.
