Pike's ghost is Quetzal

The ghost of Patera Pike that Silk meets is Quetzal in disguise.

29 threads · 28 posts · for 20 / against 5 · first raised 1997-01-25 by Fernando Q. Gouvea
Main voices: Michael Andre-Driussi (5), Alice Turner (4), Reusch (4), Dave Lebling (3), James Jordan (3), David Langford (2)

The debate

The theory that Patera Pike's ghost is Quetzal in disguise was first raised on the list by Fernando Q. Gouvea in January 1997, who called it a possible shapeshifting act but noted a missing motive [Fernando Q. Gouvea, 1997]. Within days Alice Turner proposed Quetzal impersonating Pike to explain the graffiti campaign, while Michael Andre-Driussi, citing Quetzal's disappearing habits, argued for the same reading [Alice Turner, 1997][Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997][Alice Turner, 1997]. Support came from CoxRathvon and Ranjit Bhatnagar, and in April 1997 Andre-Driussi and Kieran Cleary offered variants such as an accidental return from Main Frame or Quetzal wearing Pike's former body [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997][Kieran Cleary, 1997].

Skeptics pushed back early. Dave Lebling questioned whether Pike ever really met a vampire and noted Pike only warned of devils [Dave Lebling, 1997], and Andre-Driussi later objected that the double-dipping was implausible [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997]. James Jordan and James Wynn argued the figure is a genuine ghost, Jordan citing Wolfe's own belief in ghosts [James Jordan, 1997][James Wynn, 2010]. The turning point came in 2000, when David Langford cited a 1997 list answer by Wolfe denying the Quetzal identification, and then argued the denial might not settle the question because Wolfe had said Quetzal was not the only one of his kind [David Langford, 2000][David Langford, 2000].

Later posts mostly used the theory as a premise rather than arguing it directly. Andrew Mason's 2013 threads treated Quetzal's source as Pike, while Wynn argued Typhon stands behind Pike and Quetzal [Andrew Mason, 2013][James Wynn, 2011]. Most of the thread arguments were about narrator reliability and reread details rather than the ghost itself.

For

  • Quetzal's habit of disappearing and reappearing fits the figure seen in the ghost scene [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997]
  • Alice Turner argued Quetzal flew in to look at Silk, whose name was graffitied as a calde candidate, then impersonated Pike [Alice Turner, 1997]
  • Andre-Driussi argued Quetzal accidentally brought Pike back from Main Frame, so the apparition is a real ghost of Pike [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997]
  • Cleary proposed that Quetzal took Pike's older body to hide his unchanging youth [Kieran Cleary, 1997]
  • Mason suggested Quetzal's source is Pike, citing Silk mistaking Quetzal for Pike [Andrew Mason, 2013]

Against

  • Jordan argued Pike's ghost should be read simply as a ghost, drawing on Wolfe's stated belief in ghosts [James Jordan, 1997]
  • Lebling noted Pike only told Silk that devils infiltrated the whorl, questioning a Quetzal-vampire link [Dave Lebling, 1997]
  • Langford cited Wolfe's 1997 list answer denying that Pike's ghost is Quetzal [David Langford, 2000]
  • Wynn argued the apparition is a genuine disembodied spirit, citing Wolfe's questionnaire answers and an interview calling Pike a real ghost [James Wynn, 2010]
  • Andre-Driussi argued the double-dipping charade would be implausible and asked what Quetzal would gain by it [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997]

Where it stands: Still disputed: the theory was argued repeatedly in 1997 and drew a published denial from Wolfe as cited by Langford in 2000, and later posts treated it as one option among others rather than settled.
