Hethor is Father Inire

Hethor, the stuttering sailor, is Father Inire in disguise.

12 threads · 32 posts · for 21 / against 13 · first raised 2006-05-24 by b sharp
Main voices: Lee Berman (17), David Stockhoff (9), Sergei SOLOVIEV (3), António Pedro Marques (3), b sharp (2), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (1)

The debate

The idea that Hethor is Father Inire was first raised in 2006 by b sharp, who grouped the human-scale shapeshifters Cumaean, Father Inire and Hethor as therians on the basis of snake, monkey and slug associations and shining eyes [b sharp, 2006]. The specific Inire-Hethor link came in July 2010, when Lee Berman proposed that Inire, Hethor and Tzadkiel's mirrors connect as teleporters and suggested a hidden structure centred on Inire [Lee Berman, 2010]. Berman then defended the mirror-monster connection against David Stockhoff's radiation reading of the teratoid glyph [Lee Berman, 2010], and in December 2010 he asked whether Lucifer can travel through mirrors [Lee Berman, 2010].

The theory was argued most intensely in late October 2011. Berman proposed that Inire is Hethor reborn, a former ship's officer exiled to Urth, pointing to the sailor references, lost doll and fascination with young women [Lee Berman, 2011]. Stockhoff first supported a greater-Inire reading and then proposed Hethor as a lesser Inire [David Stockhoff, 2011] [David Stockhoff, 2011], but later disputed identity and argued that Hethor lacks Inire's judging authority [David Stockhoff, 2011] [David Stockhoff, 2011]. Sergei Soloviev and António Pedro Marques pushed back, with Soloviev arguing that Hethor's invented past and human emotions would be wasted effort if he were an alien [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011] [António Pedro Marques, 2011] and that the book repeatedly reports Inire as alien [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011]. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes and Jerry Friedman disputed the claim that shared service on Tzadkiel's ship makes Hethor identical with Tzadkiel or Inire [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2011] [Jerry Friedman, 2011].

In December 2011 and later, the debate shifted to Agia and to a split-character model. Berman argued that Inire controls Agia and that Hethor and Inire are one character split into sub-characters [Lee Berman, 2011] [Lee Berman, 2011]. Stockhoff countered that the pan-Inire model explains too much without an endpoint [David Stockhoff, 2011] and that Agia's mercy is hard to accept [David Stockhoff, 2011]. In 2013 aaron and Stockhoff agreed that Hethor may be related to Inire, with Stockhoff noting that Inire's suspected appearances are optional clues [aaron, 2013] [David Stockhoff, 2013]. Berman's 2014 post proposed that Inire and Hethor are the same character, citing Hethor's mutiny and the jiber that resembles him [Lee Berman, 2014].

The remaining threads on Isangoma, Rudesind, the teratoid glyph and the Minotaur reused the same claims about Inire's disguises and ended without agreement. The record does not show any turning point that settled the question.

For

  • Berman linked Hethor and Inire through the mirror-master and mirror-monster parallel and the teleporter idea [Lee Berman, 2010] [Lee Berman, 2010]
  • Berman argued that both Hethor and Inire share a sailor past and a mutiny connected to Tzadkiel's ship, so Hethor is Inire reborn [Lee Berman, 2011] [Lee Berman, 2014]
  • Berman cited Inire's letter placing him with the Ascians and the cat's ambiguous fate as links to Hethor [Lee Berman, 2011] [Lee Berman, 2014]
  • Stockhoff proposed Hethor as a lesser Inire in the same way Agia is a lesser Vodalus [David Stockhoff, 2011]
  • Stockhoff argued that Hethor and Inire are thematically redundant, so one character could fill both roles [António Pedro Marques, 2011]

Against

  • Soloviev argued that Hethor's invented sailor past and human emotions would be wasted effort if he were an alien like Inire [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011] [António Pedro Marques, 2011]
  • Soloviev held that the book repeatedly reports Inire as alien, and that Hethor's manner differs from Inire's [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011] [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011]
  • Stockhoff argued that Hethor holds no judging authority, while Inire is chained to Urth [David Stockhoff, 2011]
  • Friedman asked for evidence that Hethor crewed the Ship and argued that only one crewman makes many time trips [Jerry Friedman, 2011]
  • Danehy-Oakes argued that shared service on the ship is too weak a basis for identity [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2011]

Where it stands: Still disputed and not resolved; the list's most active posters, including Stockhoff and Soloviev, ended up rejecting or doubting the identity while Berman kept proposing it.
