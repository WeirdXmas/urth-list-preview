The Rajan is a Neighbor

The Rajan of Gaon (Horn/Silk) is a Neighbor, or can travel in time like them.

18 threads · 43 posts · for 10 / against 30 · first raised 2005-06-23 by James Wynn
Main voices: James Wynn (26), Gerry Quinn (7), António Pedro Marques (5), David Stockhoff (2), John Watkins (1), Son of Witz (1)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by James Wynn in June 2005, in a post arguing that Tussah is Typhon and Silk is Typhon's son [James Wynn, 2005], and it was then pushed in August 2005 through a soul-travel argument in which the Rajan time-travels like the Neighbors [James Wynn, 2005]. The earliest posts used the theory to explain Horn, Silk and the Raja, with Wynn shifting between clone, Trinity and Neighbor-in-Silk versions [James Wynn, 2009] [James Wynn, 2009] [James Wynn, 2009]. In March 2009 Son of Witz and Roy Lackey pressed for textual support, and Wynn conceded a corrected printing removed one key passage [Son of Witz, 2009] [James Wynn, 2009]. In April 2009 Wynn added the Rajan's barely eating and sudden ravenous appetite as evidence, while John Watkins offered a simpler explanation [James Wynn, 2009] [John Watkins, 2009].

From 2010 onward Wynn kept proposing that the Rajan and Neighbors time-travel, citing Scylla, dream travel, Oreb and Pike's ghost, and the Mother [James Wynn, 2010] [James Wynn, 2011] [James Wynn, 2011]. Opponents, most often António Pedro Marques, Gerry Quinn and David Stockhoff, argued that the evidence was thin, that the second Oreb and the grandmother's tale had other explanations, and that the hat points to Pike rather than to a time-travelling Rajan [António Pedro Marques, 2010] [António Pedro Marques, 2011] [Gerry Quinn, 2011] [David Stockhoff, 2011]. The November 2011 exchanges were the longest stretch, and they ranged from Pike's calotte and Mucor's possible presence to whether the Rajan in On Blue's Waters could be an amnesiac Neighbor, where Quinn argued that Silk already holds Horn and Marques argued for Neighbourly [James Wynn, 2011] [James Wynn, 2011] [Gerry Quinn, 2011] [António Pedro Marques, 2011].

The record shows the time-travel and Neighbor readings being argued at length but never settled. Several threads named on the list, including Urth Digest Vol 56 Issue 37 and Travelin' Jahlee, ended in impasse without any side conceding the central readings. Wynn's answer that the combined evidence supports time travel was met by objections that no single item proves it [James Wynn, 2011] [António Pedro Marques, 2011].

For

  • Wynn argued the Rajan's time travel is shown by Silk's sighting of Pike's ghost, the ghostly Oreb, and the grandmother's childhood story [James Wynn, 2005].
  • Wynn cited the Rajan's barely eating and sudden ravenous appetite in Spring as a further sign he is not merely human [James Wynn, 2009].
  • Wynn argued the Rajan in On Blue's Waters could be a Neighbour with amnesia, pointing to the Mother's worshippers, a childhood among trees and Able's archery [James Wynn, 2011].
  • Wynn argued that the Rajan's and Oreb's travels are unhinged from the timeline, so Rigoglio's voyage should be read as instantaneous travel [James Wynn, 2010].
  • Marques argued that the second Oreb is the strongest evidence for Rajan's time-travel manipulation in the Short Sun, since no other explanation fits [António Pedro Marques, 2011].

Against

  • Marques argued that Rajan's presence at the Red Sun needs no time travel and the grandmother's tale can be manipulation [António Pedro Marques, 2011].
  • Quinn argued that the Rajan cannot be a Neighbour because Silk already has Horn in his body and there is no room for a second passenger [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • Quinn argued that the hat Silk sees dropped by the ghost points to Pike rather than to a time-travelling Rajan [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • Watkins offered a simpler account of appetite and fasting, suggesting Silk's relation to food could explain the Rajan's eating pattern [John Watkins, 2009].
  • Stockhoff argued that the Rajan's time machine lacks precise controls, and that Wolfe excludes temporal paradoxes [David Stockhoff, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the time-travel and Neighbor readings were argued across many years and threads and most posters who engaged, notably Marques, Quinn and Stockhoff, remained unpersuaded, with no consensus reached.
