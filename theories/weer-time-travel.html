Weer travels back into his past

Weer's haunting of his past involves real travel in time.

10 threads · 11 posts · for 8 / against 4 · first raised 1999-01-27 by William H. Ansley
Main voices: Michael Andre-Driussi (4), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (3), William H. Ansley (2), C R Culver (1), Roy C. Lackey (1), Dave Tallman (1)

The debate

The earliest post on the list is William H. Ansley's January 1999 reply [William H. Ansley, 1999], which disputes a time-travel reading and argues that Weer's mental state in Peace is memory retrieval, with the doctor visits extrapolated from remembered consultations. C R Culver is the first to propose real time travel, suggesting a temporal cross-over linked to the haunted hotel in Aunt Bella's article [C R Culver, 1999]. Ansley's rejection of the rigor mortis explanation, based on the grown elm over the grave, appears in the same first exchange [William H. Ansley, 1999].

The idea returned in 2000 to 2002 in several forms. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued that Weer is a ghost narrating from beyond the grave and set out five possible readings of his time-shifting [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2000]. Michael Andre-Driussi argued that Weer may talk to real ghosts through time travel [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2001], proposed a time-splice to explain the office scene [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2002], and argued that the three visits to Dr. Van Ness fall into 1953, 1963 and 1973, making the time-jumping paradoxical [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2002]. Roy C. Lackey countered with a solipsistic reading in which Weer is dead and stuck in a frametale [Roy C. Lackey, 2002], and Ansley later argued that an underweight Weer in the Orb paperback weakens a time-splice at the doctor's office [William H. Ansley, 2002].

In 2008 Dave Tallman proposed that Miss Hadow follows Weer across time-jumps, possibly as a psychopomp, grounding this in Weer's use of maiden names and the quiet of his office [Dave Tallman, 2008]. The 'One Devil' discussion that followed drifted toward secretaries and birthday dating without settling the time-travel question. Danehy-Oakes later argued for purgatorial punishment rather than time travel [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2001]. Discussion of the time-travel reading stayed on the same disputed points across the years.

For

  • C R Culver proposed that the haunted hotel in Aunt Bella's article may be a temporal cross-over, so Weer's haunting could involve real time travel [C R Culver, 1999].
  • Michael Andre-Driussi proposed that Weer's office scene is a time-splice, and that the Van Ness visits fall in separate decades with paradoxical time-jumping [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2002] [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2002].
  • Andre-Driussi suggested that Weer may really speak with ghosts through time travel, which he presented as one reading of the text [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2001].
  • Dave Tallman argued that Miss Hadow follows Weer across time-jumps, citing Weer's maiden-name habit and the quiet office [Dave Tallman, 2008].
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes set out five readings of Weer's time-shifting, including ones that treat the shifts as real [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2000].

Against

  • William H. Ansley argued that Weer's mental state is memory retrieval, with doctor visits extrapolated from real remembered consultations [William H. Ansley, 1999].
  • Roy C. Lackey argued that Weer is dead and stuck in a frametale, so the stroke and talks with dead figures are products of memory, not time travel [Roy C. Lackey, 2002].
  • Danehy-Oakes argued that Weer knows he is dead and that his time under the tree is purgatorial punishment, not travel [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2001].
  • Ansley argued that if the Orb paperback reads underweight, Weer is always thin, which weakens the time-splice at the doctor's office [William H. Ansley, 2002].
  • Ansley rejected the rigor mortis explanation, pointing to the grown elm on Weer's grave as evidence against it [William H. Ansley, 1999].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the time-travel reading was raised repeatedly from 1999 to 2008 but never resolved, with memory, solipsistic and purgatorial readings competing against it.
