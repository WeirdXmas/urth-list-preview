Where Severian was born

Severian was not born in the Matachin Tower but brought there, and his birth can be placed (in a cell, among the Pelerines, elsewhere).

20 threads · 28 posts · for 14 / against 13 · first raised 1998-10-07 by Jon Camfield
Main voices: Roy C. Lackey (10), David Stockhoff (4), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (2), Daniel D Jones (2), Gerry Quinn (2), Lee Berman (2)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by Jon Camfield in October 1998, who speculated that Catherine bore Severian and a sister in the Obuilette and that the infant went to the Seekers, the healers, the Pelerines or the Echopraxia [Jon Camfield, 1998]. In 1999 the basket-in-Oldgate question was pushed further: Mitchell A. Bailey argued that if the basket infant is Catherine, Severian would have no ancestry and his birth would parallel the Virgin Birth [Mitchell A. Bailey, 1999], while Peter Westlake suggested Dorcas may have been too old to be the basket's mother [Peter Westlake, 1999]. The sharper form of the theory, that Severian was not born in the Matachin Tower, was put by Roy C. Lackey in November 1999, who argued from the guild's recruiting age limits and Severian's breast-feeding memory among gray cell walls [Roy C. Lackey, 1999]. Lackey returned to it in 2000, arguing that Severian's first-person narration is written in hindsight and is unreliable on such points [Roy C. Lackey, 2000].

The discussion became most active in October 2006. Lackey restated the case from the nursing memory and the height-bar rule for conscripted children [Roy C. Lackey, 2006], then argued that the past participle 'borne' means carried, not born, so the 'borne by Catherine' line does not place a birth in the Tower [Roy C. Lackey, 2006] [Roy C. Lackey, 2006]. Tim Walters rejected this, arguing from dictionary usage that 'borne by' is a deliberate way of saying Catherine gave birth in the cell [Tim Walters, 2006]. Daniel D Jones argued that the guild's rule about torturers' children refers to their own offspring, so a Tower birth is not excluded, and that a pregnant prisoner could give birth before a Caesarean, reconciling the wet-nurse passage [Daniel D Jones, 2006] [Daniel D Jones, 2006]. Lackey then conceded that Severian was most likely born in a cell, so his earlier view was probably wrong [Roy C. Lackey, 2006], but a day later he again argued that the wet-nurse passage conflicts with the guild's rule and that Severian's own memory separates his birth from the guild's taken children [Roy C. Lackey, 2006].

For

  • Severian's memory of nursing at his mother's breast in a cell suggests he was not born in the Tower, given the guild's rule that taken children know no breasts [Roy C. Lackey, 1999] [Roy C. Lackey, 2006].
  • Recruiting and height rules for conscripted children imply that a newborn could not have been taken into the guild directly [Roy C. Lackey, 1999] [Roy C. Lackey, 2006].
  • The word 'borne' in the 'borne by Catherine' phrase may mean carried rather than born, so the line does not place a birth in the Tower [Roy C. Lackey, 2006] [Roy C. Lackey, 2006].
  • Severian's narration is written in hindsight and may be unreliable about his own origins, so the Tower birth account need not be trusted [Roy C. Lackey, 2000].
  • A baby of a tortured woman could not be taken into the guild without nursing, so the wet-nurse passage points to a birth outside the guild's taking [Roy C. Lackey, 2006].

Against

  • The phrase 'borne by' is used in dictionaries and ordinary usage for giving birth, so the passage refers to Catherine bearing Severian in the cell [Tim Walters, 2006].
  • The guild rule about torturers' children refers to offspring of torturers with wives or lovers, so it does not exclude a birth in the Tower [Daniel D Jones, 2006].
  • A pregnant prisoner awaiting sentence could give birth before a Caesarean, which reconciles the wet-nurse passage with a birth to Catherine while imprisoned [Daniel D Jones, 2006].
  • Dorcas may have been older than assumed, so the basket infant may not be a child of hers and the cell-birth reading is not needed for that point [Peter Westlake, 1999].
  • Lackey's own later post concluded that the cell-birth reading is most likely, undercutting his earlier position [Roy C. Lackey, 2006].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never settled on a birth place, Lackey himself oscillated between not-Tower and cell-birth, and the main opponents Walters and Jones remained unpersuaded.
