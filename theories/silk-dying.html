Silk's spirit is dying while his body lives

Silk's soul is departing or dead while his body is healthy.

9 threads · 10 posts · for 6 / against 4 · first raised 2001-02-09 by Adam Stephanides
Main voices: Adam Stephanides (2), Robert Borski (2), Michael Straight (1), Stephen Case (1), Andy Robertson (1), Chris P (1)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by Adam Stephanides in February 2001, who argued that the narrator of The Book of the Long Sun is Silk, a merged Silk-Horn spirit, with Horn's spirit partly departing later [Adam Stephanides, 2001]. Robert Borski offered a rival version the next day, proposing that Silk's spirit was transferred into the dying Horn by Neighborly technomagic [Robert Borski, 2001]. Stephanides then disputed the Horn-lives reading of the ending, arguing that Horn, who knows he is in Silk's body, would not deny Silk's death [Adam Stephanides, 2001]. Borski's April 2001 post first used wording close to the card's statement, reading the Neighbor's "two dying men" phrase as Silk's spirit dying while his body stays healthy [Robert Borski, 2001].

The idea was taken up in the Return to the Whorl and Book of the Long Sun discussions. Michael Straight argued in August 2001 that the narrator merges Silk and Horn, with Silk's soul dying into despair and Horn's spirit joining him until Silk heals [Michael Straight, 2001]. In July 2002 Stephen Case proposed that Silk's soul was dying at the end of the series, which he linked to the Neighbors' remark about transferring Horn into a healthy body and asked readers for objections [Stephen Case, 2002]. Andy Robertson disputed this a few days later, holding that Hyacinth was a bad woman whose death left Silk inwardly dead, so the spirit died when she died rather than at the end of the series [Andy Robertson, 2002].

In December 2004 Chris P recast the question in soul-versus-psyche terms, setting out variants on which element survives in Silk's body [Chris P, 2004]. The debate returned in December 2010, when Gerry Quinn argued that Silk cut his own face in despair without meaning to kill himself, so his spirit was dying while his body survived [Gerry Quinn, 2010]. James Wynn replied the same week that Silk's spirit died and the Neighbor animates his body, citing Horn's death in the pit and Wolfe's confirmation about the floater leap [James Wynn, 2010]. The threads summarised on the list, including Horn Lives and Suicide & Despair, mostly ended in impasse rather than agreement.

For

  • Horn fails to recognize he is in Silk's body and his appearance changes on astral trips, which Stephanides took to support a merged spirit [Adam Stephanides, 2001].
  • Borski read the Neighbor's "two dying men" phrase as consistent with Silk's spirit dying while his body remains healthy [Robert Borski, 2001].
  • Michael Straight argued that Silk's soul died into despair and Horn's spirit joined him until Silk healed [Michael Straight, 2001].
  • Case took the Neighbors' remark about a healthy body with a dying soul as support for his reading that Silk's soul was dying [Stephen Case, 2002].
  • Wynn cited Horn's death in the pit, the Rajan's writing and Wolfe's confirmation about Mucor to argue the Neighbor animates Silk's body [James Wynn, 2010].

Against

  • Stephanides argued that Horn, who knows he is in Silk's body, would not deny Silk's death [Adam Stephanides, 2001].
  • Robertson held that Silk's spirit died when Hyacinth died, not at the end of the series, and that she was a bad woman who ruined him [Andy Robertson, 2002].
  • Quinn argued that the face-cutting shows Silk meant only to hurt himself, not to kill himself before the Neighbor intervened [Gerry Quinn, 2010].
  • Schabe's rebuttal denied that Silk bleeds to death, which Borski had to answer when defending the cut-himself reading [Robert Borski, 2001].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the theory was raised repeatedly from 2001 to 2010 with competing variants, and the list never reached agreement.
