Seven American Nights is a forgery

The journal of "Seven American Nights" is a police forgery, and Ardis Dahl is the werebeast Nadan shot.

52 threads · 60 posts · for 53 / against 18 · first raised 1997-08-13 by Peter Cash
Main voices: Dave Tallman (25), Stone Ox (8), Marty Light (7), Michael Andre-Driussi (6), Robert Borski (6), Peter Cash (5)

The debate

The forgery idea has an earlier relative: in 1997 Peter Cash proposed that the investigator may have fabricated the whole story to extract money from the boy's mother [Peter Cash, 1997]. The Ardis-as-werebeast claim was first put on the list by Robert Borski in May 2002, who argued that Ardis is the creature Nadan shot on the night of the first egg, citing the full moon, the Dahls' refusal to admit Nadan, and a laser-burn wound [Robert Borski, 2002]. Michael Andre-Driussi supported it by comparing the burn to the Tzadkiel ship attack in URTH [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2002], and Peter Westlake supported it by noting Ardis switching off the light as Nadan reached for her laces [Peter Westlake, 2002]. David DiGiacomo disputed it, arguing that a laser burn would have shown the creature was still alive and that the filthy clothing and blunt muzzle do not fit Ardis [David DiGiacomo, 2002], while Stone Ox answered that Nadan saw Ardis's head on a beast's torso after the shot [Stone Ox, 2002].

The police-forgery version was developed by Dave Tallman from May 2008. In his first statement he argued that secret police drugged Nadan with eggs and doctored his journal, and that his account of Ardis is a lie [Dave Tallman, 2008]. He followed with a numbered list of clues, including a red beam mistaken for moonlight, lucky breaks, the curator's questions and the prose style [Dave Tallman, 2008], and later reframed the eggs as a poisoning attempt the police then guarded him from [Dave Tallman, 2008]. Roy C. Lackey disputed the premise, arguing the U.S. government had no reason to forge a notebook since no investigator was expected to look for Nadan [Roy C. Lackey, 2008], and he argued the private investigator himself is the likelier forger [Roy C. Lackey, 2008]. Tallman's arguments also turned to Holy Week parallels, including a Palm Sunday entry and a Judas-like Ardis [Dave Tallman, 2008] [Dave Tallman, 2008].

The theory got its most visible moment in August 2010, when Tallman announced that his WolfeWiki forgery explanation had won a mock prize from a blog [Dave Tallman, 2010]. Mo Holkar said he had moved from skeptical to largely convinced and planned to reread the story for remaining problems [Mo Holkar / UKG, 2010]. Tallman kept pressing the forgery of the final day in the procession thread [Dave Tallman, 2010], but the list never reached agreement on the forgery or on the Ardis identification, and the two halves of the theory were argued by different posters.

For

  • Nadan's teetotalism and the implausible werewolf episode suggest his account is unreliable, which Tallman used to argue the journal was doctored [Dave Tallman, 2008].
  • A numbered list of clues (red beam mistaken for moonlight, lucky breaks, curator's questions, prose style) was offered as internal evidence of forgery [Dave Tallman, 2008].
  • Holy Week parallels such as the Palm Sunday entry, the Judas-like Ardis and the Last Supper meal were cited as evidence that the final journal section is constructed [Dave Tallman, 2008] [Dave Tallman, 2008].
  • The laser burn between Ardis's breasts was said to match the Tzadkiel ship wound, supporting that she is the werebeast Nadan shot [Robert Borski, 2002] [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2002].
  • Ardis switching off the light as Nadan reached for her laces was read as a small clue that the fantastic episode is real [Peter Westlake, 2002].

Against

  • The U.S. government would have had no reason to forge the notebook, since no investigator was expected to look for Nadan [Roy C. Lackey, 2008].
  • The private investigator's conclusion that Nadan left for the north predates the notebook, so the notebook's forgery would not undermine the sleuthing, and the investigator is the likelier forger [Roy C. Lackey, 2008].
  • A laser burn would have shown the creature was still alive, and the filthy clothing and blunt muzzle Nadan describes do not fit Ardis [David DiGiacomo, 2002].
  • Lackey objected that a machine-written climax trivializes Nadan's experience, which argues against the final pages being forged [Dave Tallman, 2008].
  • Stone Ox reads the scene differently, seeing Ardis's head on a beast's torso after the shot, which conflicts with the forgery-based account of her [Stone Ox, 2002].

Where it stands: Still disputed: Tallman's forgery reading gained one stated supporter (Mo Holkar) but drew objections from Lackey and DiGiacomo, and the Ardis werebeast identification remained contested with no consensus.
