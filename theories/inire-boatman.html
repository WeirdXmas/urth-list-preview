The boatman is Father Inire

The old boatman on the Lake of Birds (Dorcas's widower) is Father Inire in disguise, which makes Inire Dorcas's husband.

27 threads · 63 posts · for 38 / against 26 · first raised 2006-05-25 by b sharp
Main voices: Lee Berman (24), b sharp (9), Roy C. Lackey (7), Gerry Quinn (7), Ryan Dunn (5), Jeff Wilson (3)

The debate

The theory that the old boatman of the Lake of Birds is Father Inire, making Inire Dorcas's husband, was first raised by b sharp on 2006-05-25, who argued that Inire appears in several disguises including the old jungle guide, Rudesind and the boatman [b sharp, 2006][b sharp, 2006]. Roy C. Lackey took up the masks idea the same day, questioning why Severian would not notice a resemblance between the dead Boatman and Rudesind [Roy C. Lackey, 2006], and then disputed the shapeshifter premise on grounds of interspecies breeding [Roy C. Lackey, 2006]. b sharp kept developing the idea over 2006 and 2008, linking Fechin, Dorcas' husband and Inire through shared traits such as losing possessions, museum ties and a monkey-like look [b sharp, 2008][b sharp, 2008].

The 2008 bier debate was a turning point in the early phase. b sharp argued the dead husband's youthful face showed a deliberate fantastic act [b sharp, 2008][b sharp, 2008], while Jeff Wilson countered that rigor mortis timing and prosaic explanations ruled out a disguise [Jeff Wilson, 2008]. Lee Berman revived the idea from 2010 onward, claiming the Boatman is an Inire form and proposing an Erebus-Charon link [Lee Berman, 2010][Lee Berman, 2010]. John Watkins and Roy C. Lackey disputed it that August, calling the link speculation and noting the absence of textual evidence [John Watkins, 2010][Roy C. Lackey, 2010]. Ryan Dunn added a Urth-of-the-New-Sun key argument from Zak's shapeshifting [Ryan Dunn, 2010].

From 2011 the theory was pressed hardest by Lee Berman, who extended it to Dorcas's husband, the jungle guide Isangoma and Severian's grandfather [Lee Berman, 2011][Lee Berman, 2011][Lee Berman, 2011]. Opponents included Larry Miller, Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, who argued the mourning Dorcas shows the husband is dead while Inire lives [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2011], Marc Aramini and Gerry Quinn, who said the resemblances were circular and the theory faced serious consistency problems [Gerry Quinn, 2011][Gerry Quinn, 2011]. Jerry Friedman and Sergei Soloviev also disputed it on narrative and thematic grounds [Jerry Friedman, 2011][Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011].

For

  • The old boatman and Inire share small size, bent or twisted bodies, monkey-like features and a Charon-like role, per b sharp's list [b sharp, 2006][b sharp, 2006].
  • The boatman's own words about Inire's wry neck and bow legs and his claim to have seen the averns planted are cited as matching clues [Lee Berman, 2011][Lee Berman, 2011].
  • Dorcas's husband's youthful face in death and his unexplained survival are read as a deliberate disguise, per b sharp [b sharp, 2008].
  • Shared traits such as folksy speech, art, boats and attraction to young women link Rudesind, Fechin and the Boatman to Inire [Lee Berman, 2010][Lee Berman, 2010].
  • Zak's shapeshifting and the monkey-like sightings in the original four books are offered as support by Ryan Dunn [Ryan Dunn, 2010].

Against

  • Dorcas mourns a husband who is dead at the end of the book, which contradicts Inire being the husband [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2011][Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2011].
  • Inire's shapeshifting is not established in the New Sun text, and interspecies viable progeny seems unlikely [Roy C. Lackey, 2006][Andrew Mason, 2010].
  • Rigor mortis timing and prosaic explanations undercut the claim that the dead man is a disguised Inire [Jeff Wilson, 2008].
  • The resemblances are circular and thin, since nothing shows Rudesind or the husband resembles Inire [Gerry Quinn, 2011][Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • Accepting the theory implies Severian has a partly diabolic origin, which conflicts with the story's message [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011][Roy C. Lackey, 2010].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the theory was raised repeatedly from 2006 to 2014 and drew steady opposition, and posters never reached agreement, with the most vocal proponent Lee Berman still defending it at the end of the record.
