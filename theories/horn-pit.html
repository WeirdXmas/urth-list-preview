Horn died in the pit

Horn died in the pit in Green's jungles (and was revived, or replaced); the counter-reading is that he survived.

33 threads · 78 posts · for 37 / against 42 · first raised 1999-10-12 by Michael Andre-Driussi
Main voices: James Wynn (17), Gerry Quinn (15), Marc Aramini (14), David Stockhoff (3), António Pedro Marques (3), Timothy Reilly (2)

The debate

The earliest post on the list that raises the pit-death idea is a 2000 question from Fernando Q. Gouvea, who asks whether a neighbor revived Horn after he died in the pit [Fernando Q. Gouvea, 2000]. In 2002 Andy Robertson called the pit episode a death-and-resurrection experience [Andy Robertson, 2002], and Joe Eull asked whether it was only a half resurrection [Joe Eull, 2002]. The theory was taken up at length from 2009 to 2011. Lee Berman argued Horn died and was brought back to life [Lee Berman, 2010], and James Wynn argued he died and was revived by a Neighbor who felt guilt [James Wynn, 2010]. Roy Lackey disputed the revival version, saying Horn's spirit went into Silk's body [Roy C. Lackey, 2010], and Dan'l Danehy-Oakes asked why the Neighbors would not simply resurrect him [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2010].

The debate sharpened in November 2011 in the 'Like a Good Neighbor' threads. Marc Aramini, Berman and Wynn cited the chapter title 'The End', the line that the pit was his grave, and the deathly images [Marc Aramini, 2011][Marc Aramini, 2011][Lee Berman, 2011]. Wynn also pointed to Seawrack's diagnosis and the narrator's emphasis on the fall [James Wynn, 2011]. Gerry Quinn replied that Horn was still alive, citing the sequence in which Horn wakes thirsty before a figure touches his forehead [Gerry Quinn, 2011], and that Seawrack merely assumed he was dead [Gerry Quinn, 2011]. Sergei Soloviev argued that unconsciousness is easily confused with death [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011]. David Stockhoff and Wynn offered variants in which Horn survives, the Neighbors trap him deliberately, or his spirit persists [David Stockhoff, 2011][James Wynn, 2011][Gerry Quinn, 2011].

Later the ring became a test case. Larry Miller proposed that the ring enabled the resurrection [Larry Miller, 2011], but Soloviev noted that Horn received the ring only after leaving the pit [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011]. The discussion never reached agreement, and most exchanges turned to personal disputes.

For

  • Wynn argues from Krait's question about whether Horn saw a corpse and whether being dead makes a difference [James Wynn, 2011].
  • Aramini and Berman cite the chapter title 'The End', the line that the pit was Horn's grave, and the deathly imagery [Marc Aramini, 2011][Marc Aramini, 2011].
  • Berman says the chapter title, formatting and dialogue are clear signals from Wolfe and that the question should not be debated [Lee Berman, 2011].
  • Wynn argues that the narration makes such a fuss over the fall that it must be a definite death [James Wynn, 2011].
  • Wynn and Robertson note that Babbie and Seawrack saw Horn dead in the pit, and Robertson reads the episode as death and resurrection [James Wynn, 2010][Andy Robertson, 2002].

Against

  • Quinn argues the Neighbor was not acting and Horn woke sitting up and thirsty before any figure touched him [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • Quinn notes Horn later betrayed his family, was taken to a hell planet, and that Seawrack merely thought him dead [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • Soloviev argues that people who have lost consciousness are often mistaken for dead and that vegetative patients can wake years later [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011].
  • Quinn points to the Neighbor bending over Horn and the absence of any clear sign of death [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • Soloviev points out that Horn got the ring only after leaving the pit, undermining the resurrection account [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed and unresolved: the 2011 Like a Good Neighbor exchanges ended in impasse with Aramini, Berman and Wynn on one side and Quinn, Soloviev and others on the other.
