Severian remembers his mother

Severian's earliest memories (nursing, a woman in a cell) are of his own mother, held in the torturers' tower before he was given to the guild.

7 threads · 10 posts · for 3 / against 6 · first raised 1998-10-02 by Robert Borski
Main voices: Robert Borski (1), Jim Henley (1), Michael Straight (1), Roy C. Lackey (1), Chris Mulder (1), Jeff Wilson (1)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by Robert Borski in 1998, when he proposed in a post on Barbea and Severian's sister that prenatal memories of his mother and sister explain the 'could not remember when' passage [Robert Borski, 1998]. The idea that Severian's nursing memory is of his actual mother was developed mainly in 2006, when Jeff Wilson argued that the apparent contradiction between two passages could be resolved, with the mother first confined, nursing him in her cell, and executed later [Jeff Wilson, 2006]. Jeff Wilson's reconciliation drew sharp replies from Daniel D Jones, who read the infant's breast memory as showing Severian was still with his mother, and from Nathan Spears, who limited the wet-nurse premise to nursing inside the Tower [Daniel D Jones, 2006] [Nathan Spears, 2006].

The main opposition came from Roy C. Lackey, who argued that the recovered memory of warm milk from a veiled woman's breast does not contradict the claim that torturers' children know no breasts, and that Severian's inference that the woman was his mother rests on deduced background [Roy C. Lackey, 2006]. Chris Mulder backed a weaning-based reading, holding that children seized with their mothers were weaned before capture [Chris Mulder, 2006]. Early in 1999, Jeremy Crampton's claim that Severian's breastfeeding memory contradicts never knowing his mother was disputed by Michael Straight, who offered a consistent sentence stating both [Michael Straight, 1999], and Jim Henley disputed the cited page 15 passage as proof of a mother memory [Jim Henley, 1999].

In the 2006 'littlest apprentices' discussion, the arguments largely ran in circles: Brent Dyer called the inconsistency deliberate, and Nathan Spears conceded the point to end the debate [Daniel D Jones, 2006] [Nathan Spears, 2006]. Later, in 2010, Andrew Mason asked whether the maid playing Catherine is literally Severian's mother or only identified with the beheaded Saint Katharine, calling the idea a brilliant explanation for the name but questioning its logic [Andrew Mason, 2010]. The b sharp post on the pebble memory and the five coffins linked the mother's cell to the pebble-piling memory, but did not resolve the question [b sharp, 2006].

For

  • Robert Borski proposed in 1998 that Severian's prenatal memories of his mother and sister explain the 'could not remember when' passage [Robert Borski, 1998].
  • Jeff Wilson argued that 'take' may mean surgical removal or guild induction at different times, so the mother could have nursed him in her cell before being executed [Jeff Wilson, 2006].
  • Daniel D Jones read the infant breast memory as showing Severian was still with his mother, not yet taken by the guild [Daniel D Jones, 2006].
  • Nathan Spears argued the statements about infants and wet-nurses can be consistent if the wet-nurse premise is limited to nursing inside the Tower [Nathan Spears, 2006].
  • Roy C. Lackey argued that the veiled woman in the memory is his mother by deduction from background knowledge, not from the memory's details [Roy C. Lackey, 2006].

Against

  • Jeremy Crampton argued that Severian's breastfeeding memory contradicts his statement that he never knew his mother [Michael Straight, 1999].
  • Jim Henley argued that a lie requires knowing a statement is false, and that the cited page 15 passage does not prove a memory of his mother [Jim Henley, 1999].
  • Michael Straight offered the sentence 'I have memories of breastfeeding, but I never knew my mother' as a consistent statement, not a contradiction [Michael Straight, 1999].
  • Chris Mulder argued that the torturers' children who know no breast were seized as small children and weaned before capture, so the nursing memory is not a contradiction [Chris Mulder, 2006].
  • Mark Millman relayed Borski's rebuttal that Severian's sense of familiarity echoes Valeria rather than a buried memory of his mother [Robert Borski, 1998].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list ended in impasse, with posters such as Lackey and Jones still disagreeing, and several readers still finding the passages in tension.
