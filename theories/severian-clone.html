Severian is a clone

Severian is one of a series of clones (or otherwise manufactured), made for the role he plays.

36 threads · 63 posts · for 43 / against 17 · first raised 1999-04-22 by Ori Kowarsky
Main voices: James Wynn (13), David Stockhoff (12), Lee Berman (7), Roy C. Lackey (4), Dave Tallman (4), Jeff Wilson (4)

The debate

The clone idea was first raised on 1999-04-22 by Ori Kowarsky, who proposed that Severian is a genetically engineered clone of Number Five with alzabo and abo tissue, and built the case on the aquastor machine and the shared dream with Baldanders [Ori Kowarsky, 1999]. He extended it a week later to a homunculus in the Citadel as Severian's fetal clone [Ori Kowarsky, 1999].

From 2002 to 2003 the theory shifted to a Typhon-Ymar family line. Don Doggett argued that Ymar and Severian are linked dynastically through Typhon, as clones or sons, pointing to parallels between their lives and shared Guild and Autarch roles [Don Doggett, 2002]. Marc Aramini endorsed the idea [Marc Aramini, 2002], and Lisa Schaffer-Doggett drew out a kinship chain in which Typhon fathered Ymar and Severian is Ymar's clone [Lisa Schaffer-Doggett, 2003]. Roy C. Lackey and James Wynn objected, Lackey on Christ-parallel grounds [Roy C. Lackey, 2003] and Wynn on the clone's differing appearance [James Wynn, 2003].

In 2005 Don Doggett revived the Ymar line, suggesting Piaton might be Severian's father and that Severian could be a clone or vessel for the first Autarch [Don Doggett, 2005]. James Wynn then proposed Severian as a clone of Piaton, making Typhon his biological father [James Wynn, 2005], and later argued for a clone of Frog [James Wynn, 2010]. Roy Lackey disputed the Ymar and Typhon clone readings on the grounds of Severian's dark hair and pale skin [Roy C. Lackey, 2005], and Marc Aramini separately concluded Silk and Severian cannot be clones on hair colour [Marc Aramini, 2006]. The 2013 'Five Severians' debate around the Citadel mausoleum drew many new proponents, including Michael Thayer's five-clone reading [Michael Thayer, 2013] and Charles Gillingham's robot-clone reading [Charles Gillingham, 2013], while Lee Berman tied the clone idea to the khaibit and the title The Shadow of the Torturer [Lee Berman, 2013] [David Stockhoff, 2013].

The 2013 thread turned on rival explanations. David Stockhoff argued against cloned coffins on Occam's Razor grounds and proposed that the mausoleum holds eidolons rather than clones [David Stockhoff, 2013] [David Stockhoff, 2013], and Jeff Wilson argued Severian is an aquastor duplicate, not a clone [Jeff Wilson, 2013]. Thayer defended the hidden clone-laboratory reading against the Occam's Razor objection, citing the book's pattern of technology disguised as myth [Michael Thayer, 2013]. The debate closed without agreement, and Charles Gillingham's suspended-animation reading [Charles Gillingham, 2013] was one of the last proposals on the list.

For

  • Severian's resurrection power and the aquastor, shared-dream and Jonas-account evidence were offered to argue he is Number Five's genetically engineered clone [Ori Kowarsky, 1999].
  • Parallels between Severian's and Ymar's lives, and their shared Guild and Autarch roles, were cited to argue for a dynastic clone link through Typhon [Don Doggett, 2002] [Don Doggett, 2005].
  • A kinship chain through Quetzal's mother, Typhon fathering Ymar, and Ymar's clone being Severian was offered as a family-tree case [Lisa Schaffer-Doggett, 2003].
  • Severian's leading the narrator and Scylla to a crypt at the end of The Short Sun was cited to argue he is a clone of Piaton [James Wynn, 2005].
  • Lee Berman argued the Latin and Arabic meanings of larva and khaibit show Severian was always meant to be a clone, and that the title The Shadow of the Torturer makes it explicit [Lee Berman, 2012] [Lee Berman, 2013].

Against

  • Roy C. Lackey argued that Christ-like figures cannot be part Satan, so Severian cannot be a clone of Typhon's child [Roy C. Lackey, 2003].
  • James Wynn argued that clones of Typhon would not look so different from him, undermining the Typhon-Severian clone reading [James Wynn, 2003].
  • Roy C. Lackey argued that Severian's dark hair and pale skin weigh against his being a clone of Typhon or Ymar [Roy C. Lackey, 2005].
  • David Stockhoff argued on Occam's Razor grounds that the coffins need not be clone storage, since a simpler corpse explanation fits and no other character sees the mausoleum [David Stockhoff, 2013] [David Stockhoff, 2013].
  • Jeff Wilson argued Severian is an aquastor duplicate, citing the dead bodies left behind in the text, and so not a clone [Jeff Wilson, 2013].

Where it stands: Still disputed and never settled: the clone reading recurred from 1999 to 2013 with several proponents, but posters disputed it on hair colour, appearance, chronology and Occam's Razor grounds, and the list ended without agreement.
