Hyacinth is a Magdalene figure

Hyacinth plays Mary Magdalene (or the Church, or Helena to Silk's Simon Magus).

8 threads · 9 posts · for 7 / against 2 · first raised 1997-03-12 by Kieran Mullen
Main voices: James Wynn (3), James Jordan (2), Kieran Mullen (1), Michael Straight (1), Thomas Bitterman (1), Lee Berman (1)

The debate

The earliest post on the list is Kieran Mullen's 1997 proposal [Kieran Mullen, 1997] that Hyacinth stands for the Church as bride, and the Magdalene reading first appears in James Jordan's 2000 post [James Jordan, 2000], which offers Silk and Hyacinth as Christ and Mary Magdalene figures.

In 2002 the idea was taken up in the Long Sun discussions. James Jordan argued that Silk's despair stemmed from grief over Hyacinth's death and that Hyacinth was loyal, not a ruinous woman [James Jordan, 2002]. Michael Straight supported a Church reading by citing Ephesians on Christ and the Church [Michael Straight, 2002], and Thomas Bitterman framed the Long Sun books as an alternate history of Christ marrying Mary Magdalene [Thomas Bitterman, 2002].

From 2010 the discussion shifted toward Simon Magus and Helena. James Wynn proposed that Silk and Hyacinth retell the Gnostic story [James Wynn, 2010], then in later posts argued that Hy's identity as Fava fits that play but reveals little thematically [James Wynn, 2010]. In 2013 Lee Berman proposed a different reading, that Hyacinth may be a transgender prostitute [Lee Berman, 2013]. The Heavy Hyacinth thread, in which Tom and Jordan disputed a Magdalene and Christ-marriage reading, ended with no clear resolution on the literal question.

For

  • Kieran Mullen argued that Hyacinth represents the Church as the forgiven bride, tied to Christian and Catholic resonance in the names [Kieran Mullen, 1997].
  • James Jordan offered Silk and Hyacinth as Christ and Mary Magdalene figures, tracing Silk's airship temptations to the gospel temptations [James Jordan, 2000].
  • Michael Straight extended the Christ and Church reading, citing Ephesians to argue the Silk-Hyacinth relationship was disastrous for Silk [Michael Straight, 2002].
  • Thomas Bitterman proposed Hyacinth as Mary Magdalene in a Christ-type pattern, offering it as a thematic rather than literal reading [Thomas Bitterman, 2002].
  • James Wynn's Simon Magus and Helena reading supplied a related Gnostic parallel to the Silk-Hyacinth pairing [James Wynn, 2010].

Against

  • James Jordan argued that Hyacinth was loyal and not a bad woman who ruined Silk, cutting against a cast of her as disastrous [James Jordan, 2002].
  • James Wynn argued that Hy's Fava identity says little about Silk or the Rajan and fits the Simon Magus play rather than a Magdalene reading [James Wynn, 2010].
  • Lee Berman put forward a separate account of Hyacinth as a transgender prostitute, moving away from the Magdalene framing [Lee Berman, 2013].
  • In the Heavy Hyacinth discussion, James Wynn and James Jordan disagreed over whether Hy is literally a chem, with Jordan keeping her role thematic [Thomas Bitterman, 2002].

Where it stands: Still disputed and never settled: the Magdalene reading was raised in 2000 and 2002 and then largely gave way to Simon Magus and Helena and other readings, with no consensus reached.
