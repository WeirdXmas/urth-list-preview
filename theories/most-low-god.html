The Most Low God is lying

In The Wizard Knight, the god who speaks of the Most High God (or calls himself the Most Low) is lying about himself.

8 threads · 15 posts · for 6 / against 7 · first raised 2005-01-03 by stilskin
Main voices: James Wynn (3), Jerry Friedman (2), Chris P (1), Nathan Spears (1), stilskin (1), Stone Ox (1)

The debate

The lying reading was argued on the list by Dave Lebling in early January 2005 ([Dave Lebling, 2005]), who agreed with a point James Wynn had quoted that the Most Low God is a liar and extended it to his self-worship, saying he either deludedly believes or falsely claims to be the Most High God. The idea was then pursued mostly in the Aelf and cosmology threads, and it resurfaced in December 2005 when Chris P argued that the Most Low God lies when he says that seeing his back would reveal the Most High God ([Chris P, 2005]).

Wynn was the main dissenter. In December 2005 he argued that the Most Low God is not a lying figure but part of a progression of elevation and transformation, pointing out that the back he offers shows only maggots ([James Wynn, 2005]). In 2012 he argued that the Sun Cycle feels gnostic without making Wolfe gnostic, and that the worlds seem created by the Most High God ([James Wynn, 2012]). The record does not show him directly answering later posts on the lie itself.

The theory was revived in 2009, when John Watkins argued that the lowest deity, who claims to be the Most High, is lying about his rank, which the text makes fairly clear ([John Watkins, 2009]). David Stockhoff agreed that the text shows the lie, and he responded to Son of Witz's point about the hierarchy being more complex ([David Stockhoff, 2009]). In 2012 Jerry Friedman agreed with Marc Aramini that the low god deceives worshippers, citing his punishment for blasphemy as evidence of a higher god in charge ([Jerry Friedman, 2012]).

For

  • The Most Low God is so morally deficient that he can find no worship object better than himself, so he either deludedly believes or falsely claims to be the Most High God ([Dave Lebling, 2005]).
  • Chris P argues the Most Low God's claim that seeing his back would reveal the Most High God is a lie ([Chris P, 2005]).
  • John Watkins argues the text makes the lowest deity's false claim to be the Most High fairly clear ([John Watkins, 2009]).
  • David Stockhoff says the text clearly shows the Most Low God's lie about being the Most High God ([David Stockhoff, 2009]).
  • Jerry Friedman argues the low god is punished for blasphemy, which shows a higher god is in charge and that the low god misjudges his own interests ([Jerry Friedman, 2012]).

Against

  • James Wynn argues the Most Low God is part of a progression of elevation and transformation rather than a liar, noting that the back he offers shows only maggots ([James Wynn, 2005]).
  • James Wynn argues the worlds seem created by the Most High God rather than by inhabitants, which he finds unlike any gnosticism he knows, so the lie reading does not follow from a gnostic frame ([James Wynn, 2012]).

Where it stands: Still disputed: the lie reading gained support from Lebling, Chris P, Watkins, Stockhoff and Friedman across 2005 to 2012, but Wynn dissented throughout and the threads ended in disagreement.
