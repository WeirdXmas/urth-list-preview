The colonists are the abos (Veil's Hypothesis)

The original French colonists of Sainte Anne were killed and replaced by the shapeshifting natives, who now believe themselves human.

43 threads · 51 posts · for 25 / against 27 · first raised 1998-06-01 by adam louis stephanides
Main voices: Lee Berman (12), Gerry Quinn (8), Tony Ellis (4), Jeff Wilson (4), adam louis stephanides (3), Robert Borski (3)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list on 1 June 1998, when adam louis stephanides asked about Liev's Postpostulate in The Fifth Head of Cerberus and linked it to the Veil's Hypothesis passage [adam louis stephanides, 1998]. Within a week, Adam Stephanides argued that Liev's Postpostulate was a milder variant of Veil's Hypothesis rather than its reverse [adam louis stephanides, 1998], and Tony Ellis made the same case [Alice Turner, 1998]. Robert Borski proposed that Aunt Jeannine was herself an alien aboriginal who validated Veil's Hypothesis [Robert Borski, 1998], and Alice Turner proposed that aboriginals descend from Earth colonists who went native [Alice Turner, 1998]. Through 1999 to 2002 the debate moved to whether the abos are descended from Earth colonists, with Mitchell A. Bailey disputing that view [Mitchell A. Bailey, 1999], and Tony Ellis and Jeff Wilson clashing over whether such descent would leave any trace in the text or the archaeology [Tony Ellis, 2002] [Jeff Wilson, 2002]. Adam Stephanides then proposed in 2002 that the unnamed Liev idea reverses Veil's Hypothesis, with humans killing the abos [Adam Stephanides, 2002].

From 2005 to 2011 the discussion turned to the aboriginals' intelligence and to specific textual clues. Maru Dubshinki and Chris P disputed Veil's account of the aboriginals as naive or folktale-based [Maru Dubshinki, 2005] [Chris P, 2005], and Jim Raylor called Veil's hypothesis the key solution [Jim Raylor, 2005]. Lee Berman argued that Veil's Hypothesis was simply correct for St. Croix and that Aunt Jeannine is Dr. Veil [Lee Berman, 2010] [Lee Berman, 2010], while Tony Ellis argued that VRT's poor handwriting refuted it [Tony Ellis, 2011]. Gerry Quinn argued that Veil's Hypothesis was largely or partly false [Gerry Quinn, 2011] [Gerry Quinn, 2011], and Berman replied that it must be wholly true or wholly false [Lee Berman, 2011]. Tony Ellis proposed that the humans themselves degenerated into the aboriginals [Tony Ellis, 2011].

The 2010 to 2014 posts, many written around the 2014 Technology Review interview, repeated these lines. Berman argued that Marsch was replaced by an aboriginal and defended the plausibility of this with an anthropological parallel [Lee Berman, 2014] [Lee Berman, 2014]. Quinn argued that Veil's wholesale replacement was silly and that a twenty light-year travel delay explained the facts [Gerry Quinn, 2014], and that the Shadow Children in Short Sun are degenerate humans [Gerry Quinn, 2014]. Marc Aramini proposed a mechanistic reading of the mysteries and a Frenchman's Landing version of Veil's Hypothesis [Marc Aramini, 2013], and Berman extended the theory to wider alien replacement of humanity [Lee Berman, 2014].

For

  • Lee Berman argued that the Shadow Children and the inhumi are imitators who replaced colonists, citing the replacement of Dr. Marsch by VRT and the deception motif [Lee Berman, 2010].
  • Berman argued that the inhumi cannot be perfect imitators because they keep the ability to shift back to their original form [Lee Berman, 2010].
  • b sharp argued that the shadow children mimicked the first colonists and that later colonists were replaced by abos who kept their shapeshifting [b sharp, 2008].
  • Gerry Quinn argued that natives merged with colonists and replaced some, such as Marsch [Gerry Quinn, 2010].
  • Marc Aramini argued that Trenchard and the Frenchman's Landing people are adapted abos [Marc Aramini, 2013].

Against

  • Adam Stephanides argued that Liev's Postpostulate is a milder form of Veil's Hypothesis that leaves the thoughts-influence-reality passage unexplained [adam louis stephanides, 1998].
  • Tony Ellis argued that VRT's poor handwriting would be shared by all natives if the hypothesis were true, so his case refutes it [Tony Ellis, 2011].
  • Jeff Wilson argued that the absence of any trace of such colonists in the text or archaeology undercuts the colonist theory [Jeff Wilson, 2002].
  • Gerry Quinn argued that the replacement of Marsch is silly and that the twenty light-year travel delay explains the facts [Gerry Quinn, 2014].
  • James Wynn argued that the aborigines cannot use their imitative abilities, so Veil's paradox is answered and the hypothesis fails [James Wynn, 2010].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the theory was never settled, with the list split between those who accepted Veil's replacement story and those who called it partly true, silly or unsupported, and the 2014 threads ended in impasse.
