Number Five's identity

Number Five is a clone (of a line of clones) and his relationships (to V.R.T., to Phaedria) say more about him.

28 threads · 33 posts · for 19 / against 11 · first raised 1998-04-04 by Robert Borski
Main voices: Michael Andre-Driussi (5), Robert Borski (4), James Wynn (4), Tony Ellis (3), Jerry Friedman (3), James Jordan (2)

The debate

Robert Borski first raised the Phaedria-as-sister idea in April 1998, proposing a genealogy for The Fifth Head of Cerberus that rested on Maitre's brokered children, Phaedria's broken ankle and the Phaedra name [Robert Borski, 1998]. Pedro Jorge Romero the same day read the number Five as a generation count, not a family position, arguing David is not a clone because he is named and blond [Pedro Jorge Romero, 1998]. Tony Ellis disputed the sister reading, citing Dr Marsch's remark and the three heads of Cerberus [Tony Ellis, 1998], and Borski answered with page references and the incest connotations of the name [Robert Borski, 1998].

The debate broadened over the next years. Borski later made David the son of Celestine Etienne from V.R.T. and suggested David and Phaedria might be twins [Robert Borski, 1998]. Michael Andre-Driussi argued that Number Five and Marsch/V.R.T. are unexamined twins, linked by parallel murders, disguises and dreams [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998], and he pressed a telepathic link in 2000 and 2002 [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2000] [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2002]. Jerry Friedman disputed the robot-massacre parallel, noting that the robots in the book act differently [Jerry Friedman, 2002].

In April 2002 the clone-of-Wolfe reading surfaced. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued Number Five is a clone of Gene Wolfe through name wordplay [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002], and John Barach added name links to John Marsch [John Barach, 2002]. Michael Straight disputed it as a joke [Michael Straight, 2002], while James Jordan supported it [James Jordan, 2002]. Later posters moved to Doctor Who parallels [Gerry Quinn, 2011], to a plural cloned-being reading that Marc Aramini contested [Lee Berman, 2013] [Marc Aramini, 2013], and to Lee Berman's view that Number Five is more reliable than V.R.T. [Lee Berman, 2014].

For

  • Borski argued Phaedria is Number Five's sister from Maitre's brokered children, the broken ankle and the Phaedra name [Robert Borski, 1998].
  • Romero argued the number Five counts clone generations and that David is not a clone because he is named and blond [Pedro Jorge Romero, 1998].
  • Borski proposed David's mother is Celestine Etienne and that David and Phaedria may be twins [Robert Borski, 1998].
  • Andre-Driussi argued Number Five and V.R.T. are twins through parallel murders, disguises, imprisonments and dreams [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998].
  • Danehy-Oakes argued Number Five is a clone of Gene Wolfe, resting on a pun on Gene and Eugene [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002].

Against

  • Ellis disputed the sister reading, citing Dr Marsch's remark and the three heads of Cerberus [Tony Ellis, 1998].
  • Friedman disputed the robot parallel, saying the robot scenes in the book differ [Jerry Friedman, 2002].
  • Straight called the name business a joke and denied any real relation to Wolfe [Michael Straight, 2002].
  • Ellis disputed the Antichrist reading, arguing 666 and Cerberus point to Greek myth rather than Christian hell [Tony Ellis, 2002].
  • Aramini argued the final line refers to shadow children, not a cloned Number Five [Marc Aramini, 2013].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the sister, twin and clone readings were each raised repeatedly over 1998 to 2014 and each drew objections, and no thread reached agreement.
