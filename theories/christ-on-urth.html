Christ existed in Urth's past

Urth's history includes the historical Jesus and Christianity, now forgotten or folded into later religion.

37 threads · 58 posts · for 24 / against 32 · first raised 1999-04-29 by Nicholas Gevers
Main voices: Lee Berman (14), Gerry Quinn (7), António Pedro Marques (5), Jeff Wilson (4), Son of Witz (4), Andrew Mason (4)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by Nicholas Gevers in 1999, who argued that Christian allusions in The Book of the New Sun imply Christianity once existed on Urth and faded into abeyance [Nicholas Gevers, 1999]. Ori Kowarsky replied the same day, conceding that an Urth without a Christian past looked untenable while proposing that Tzad comes from a universe where Christ existed [Ori Kowarsky, 1999]. Craig Christensen then asked whether Christianity existed long ago and was forgotten, citing Dorcas's memory of the rood [Craig Christensen, 1999], and Michael Andre-Driussi argued that the Conciliator's church and names like Holy Katharine show Christianity shaped Urth's past [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1999].

In the 2000s the question split into several camps. Jeff Wilson argued that Urth's history includes a Christian era and that the millions-of-years figure is figurative [Jeff Wilson, 2008], while thalassocrat argued Urth had no Jesus and that Silk's crucified possessed man is the true incarnation seen from outside the universe [thalassocrat, 2006]. Marc Aramini proposed in 2008 that Christ existed in Urth's past, grounding this in the jungle hut scene [Marc Aramini, 2008], and Jordon Flato asked whether a historic Christ could exist there [Jordon Flato, 2008]. Dave Tallman argued Jesus was forgotten on Urth while Christian customs persisted [Dave Tallman, 2008].

The debate became heavily active in 2010 to 2012. Andrew Mason argued that Urth must once have had a Jesus-analogue [Andrew Mason, 2010], António Pedro Marques argued Urth probably had a historical Jesus behind its Christian lore [António Pedro Marques, 2010], and Jeff Wilson argued Christianity died out around the First Empire [Jeff Wilson, 2010]. Lee Berman repeatedly argued that Jesus never appeared on Urth [Lee Berman, 2010] and that Christ is absent from the Urth phase text [Lee Berman, 2010]. Gerry Quinn cited Silk's vision of Jesus entering Jerusalem on Palm Sunday as evidence that Christ had already appeared [Gerry Quinn, 2012], and Marc Aramini repeated that Jesus existed within Urth's universe [Marc Aramini, 2011]. In 2014 Lee Berman still argued Urth and Briah await Earth for Christ [Lee Berman, 2014].

For

  • Gevers argued Christian allusions in the Urth books imply Christianity was once present and faded into abeyance, citing missionaries in the Jungle Garden and Genesis references [Nicholas Gevers, 1999].
  • Andre-Driussi cited Dorcas's family's enamelled crucifixes and the pelerines' cross-form sigil as evidence that Christianity shaped Urth's past [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1999].
  • Aramini pointed to Robert and Mary as missionaries, arguing that Christ must also be present on ancient Urth [Marc Aramini, 2011].
  • Quinn cited Silk's vision of Jesus entering Jerusalem on Palm Sunday as evidence that Jesus existed in Urth's universe [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • Marques argued that Christ-related lore such as the Judas tree and Mary could not exist without a historical Jesus [António Pedro Marques, 2010].

Against

  • Ori Kowarsky proposed that Tzad comes from a universe where Christ existed, unlike Severian's, where the series implies he did not [Ori Kowarsky, 1999].
  • Lee Berman argued that Jesus never appeared on Urth and that the Urth phase text contains no Jesus [Lee Berman, 2010][Lee Berman, 2010].
  • Jeff Wilson argued that Christianity died out on Urth during the First Empire and never returned [Jeff Wilson, 2010].
  • Jerry Friedman argued that the missionaries may follow a Christianity-like religion that never mentions Jesus [Jerry Friedman, 2011].
  • Gerry Quinn argued that the Outsider cult succeeded Christianity and that the Chrasmological passages do not show a Christ on Urth [Gerry Quinn, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the thread never reached agreement, with posters split between Christ having existed on Urth, Christianity being forgotten, and Urth having no Jesus at all.
