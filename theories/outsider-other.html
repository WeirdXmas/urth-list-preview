The Outsider is someone else

The Outsider is a pagan or lesser being (Dionysus, Typhon, Pas and Kypris together) rather than God.

51 threads · 83 posts · for 49 / against 34 · first raised 1997-04-11 by Alice Turner
Main voices: Lee Berman (23), Marc Aramini (11), James Wynn (8), David Stockhoff (5), Andrew Mason (5), Gerry Quinn (5)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by David Wells, who on 1997-04-14 proposed that the Long Sun Outsider is Typhon, a view that Alice Turner's earlier reply [Alice Turner, 1997] addressed by arguing that Outsider worship predates Typhon. Wells extended the idea within a week, suggesting Typhon exists as emergency backup forms [David Wells, 1997], and Michael Andre-Driussi answered that a backup god would need a biological basis and suggested Typhon as Pas might play the Outsider [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997].

The idea returned in 2002 and 2003. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued that Kypris is becoming the Outsider [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002], and James Wynn replied that the Outsider is God and Pas a blasphemous imitation [James Wynn, 2002]. Matthew Malthouse argued that Typhon did not believe in the Outsider at all [Matthew Malthouse, 2002], and Wynn later held that Kypris has become the Outsider [James Wynn, 2003].

From 2008 to 2011 the debate centred on whether Pas and Kypris, or Typhon and Mamelta, speak as the Outsider in Silk's enlightenment. Marc Aramini proposed such readings [Marc Aramini, 2010][Marc Aramini, 2010], while Wynn objected that an insider cannot be the Outsider, whose defining trait is being outside the Whorl [James Wynn, 2010][James Wynn, 2010]. Roy Lackey denied that Pas pulls the Outsider's strings [Roy C. Lackey, 2010]. Lee Berman pushed a parallel Dionysus reading from late 2010 [Lee Berman, 2010][Lee Berman, 2010][Lee Berman, 2010], which Gerry Quinn and Jerry Friedman disputed as too thin or as two distinct gods [Gerry Quinn, 2010][Jerry Friedman, 2010]. Threads such as "Dionysus and the Outsider" and "christ, already" ended without agreement.

Later posts moved toward the Outsider as a lesser deity. Berman argued the Outsider may be less than the Increate [Lee Berman, 2013], Daniel Petersen agreed with Aramini's reading of the Outsider's role [Daniel Petersen, 2013], and Wynn rejected the Dionysus link in 2012 [James Wynn, 2012]. Across the years the thread lists record repeated disputes over the same passages rather than a turning point that settled the question.

For

  • Typhon was proposed as the Outsider, and a backup form of Typhon was offered as the enlightening voice [David Wells, 1997][David Wells, 1997]
  • Kypris was argued to be becoming the Outsider, and later to have become him [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002][James Wynn, 2003]
  • The Outsider was linked to Dionysus, son of Thyone, on textual grounds from Return to the Whorl and interview remarks [Lee Berman, 2010][Lee Berman, 2010]
  • The Outsider was argued to be a lesser deity than the Increate, so that deviation from his plan is possible [Lee Berman, 2013]
  • The Outsider was argued to absorb Typhon's demon family into godhood [John Watkins, 2009]

Against

  • Outsider is God and Pas a blasphemous imitation, from black-mass and Mithraic allusions [James Wynn, 2002]
  • Pas is the Demiurge and the Outsider is God, with Gnostic readings rejected [Josh Geller, 2002]
  • The Outsider's defining trait is being outside the Whorl, so Pas and Kypris cannot be him [James Wynn, 2010][James Wynn, 2010]
  • The Dionysus link is too thin, since shared symbols like wine prove no real connection [Gerry Quinn, 2010][Gerry Quinn, 2010][Gerry Quinn, 2011]
  • Pas is not the Outsider because the Outsider created humanity and Silk's own statements say so [Roy C. Lackey, 2010][Roy C. Lackey, 2010]

Where it stands: Still disputed: the Outsider-as-lesser-being and Dionysus readings were proposed repeatedly but never won consensus, and the Outsider-as-God view stayed a standing objection.
