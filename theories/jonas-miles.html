Miles is Jonas

Miles, the dead soldier revived by the Claw, carries Jonas's mind (Jonas returning through Severian), or Jonas is otherwise present in him.

23 threads · 28 posts · for 18 / against 6 · first raised 1997-04-14 by Kieran Cleary
Main voices: Michael Andre-Driussi (7), James Wynn (3), Peter Westlake (2), Roy C. Lackey (2), Michael Dale (2), Andrew Mason (2)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by Kieran Cleary, who in April 1997 asked how Jonas relates to Miles [Kieran Cleary, 1997], and was quickly taken up by Michael Andre-Driussi in April 1997, who offered a happy and a disturbing reading of Jonas returning in a dead soldier [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997]. In 1998 C R Culver and J. Schultz proposed that the Claw revived a dead soldier and that Jonas returned in him, with Culver grounding this in Severian's isolation [C R Culver, 1998] and Schultz answering Culver and Mantis [J. Schultz, 1998]. Peter Westlake framed the idea as Jonas really present in Miles [Peter Westlake, 1998].

The main 1998 dispute concerned the mirror scene in The Citadel of the Autarch. Tony Ellis argued that Miles sees Jolenta's face, confirming possession by Jonas [Tony Ellis, 1998], while Peter Westlake argued the mirror shows Miles's own face and that the red-gold-haired girl memory concerns Jonas [Peter Westlake, 1998]. Later threads were largely sparked by Andrew Mason, who first disputed the Miles-Jonas link in 2011 and then reversed to propose that Miles may be Jonas [Andrew Mason, 2011] [Andrew Mason, 2011], and by Roy C. Lackey, who proposed in 2002 that Jonas's software was installed in a reanimated corpse [Roy C. Lackey, 2002].

Other posters proposed varied mechanisms: Tim O'Donnell argued Jonas possessed Miles after Severian revived the body, grounded in the phantom cat of 'The Cat' [O'Donnell, Tim (BOSI), 2009]; Dave Tallman proposed possession by Heirogrammate technology [Dave Tallman, 2008]; Michael Dale proposed Jonas absorbed parts of Miles after a crash [Michael Dale, 2007]; and Lee Berman argued Miles is a vehicle for Jonas's memory [Lee Berman, 2011]. Marc Aramini questioned the identity on the basis of the sailor's Wellerisms [Marc Aramini, 2011], which Mason answered by noting several sailors speak them [Andrew Mason, 2011].

For

  • Jonas is said to be present in Miles through the mirror scene and the soldier's red-gold-haired girl memory, as Ellis argued [Tony Ellis, 1998]
  • Culver and Schultz argued the Claw revived a dead soldier opening a door for Jonas to return, grounded in Severian's isolation and the flesh-eaters' talk of souls in cells [C R Culver, 1998] [J. Schultz, 1998]
  • Tallman and O'Donnell argued possession by Jonas, using the cat's invisibility and Heirogrammate technology as analogies [O'Donnell, Tim (BOSI), 2009] [Dave Tallman, 2008]
  • Mason argued Severian's theory of mirrors as non-spatial devices means Jonas never left, citing Long Sun parallels of beings in two places at once [Andrew Mason, 2011]
  • Berman argued the sailor's talk and ship imagery point to Ascian imagery, linking Miles to Jonas's memory [Lee Berman, 2011]

Against

  • Westlake argued the mirror scene shows Miles's own face and that the soldier's memory concerns Jonas, not Jolenta [Peter Westlake, 1998]
  • Mason first argued the Wellerisms tied to Jonas are spoken by several sailors, so they do not link Miles to Jonas [Andrew Mason, 2011]
  • Lackey and Friedman rejected the Pinocchio-style reading and disputed that Jonas became Miles through repair [James Wynn, 2002] [Roy C. Lackey, 2002]
  • Wilson disputed that the Endangered Species cat is the same creature as the phantom cat, reducing O'Donnell's bridge [O'Donnell, Tim (BOSI), 2009]
  • Marc Aramini questioned whether the soldier Miles is really a returned Jonas [Marc Aramini, 2011]

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list shows many competing mechanisms (possession, revival, absorption, memory vehicle) and no consensus, with the mirror-scene reading largely settled in Westlake's favor and several threads ending without agreement.
