Severian descends from Typhon

Severian is Typhon's son, clone or descendant (often through Ymar), which is why his face and body resemble the ancient ruler's.

35 threads · 49 posts · for 26 / against 16 · first raised 1998-06-22 by Michael Straight
Main voices: James Wynn (9), Lisa Schaffer-Doggett (6), Don Doggett (5), Marc Aramini (4), Roy C. Lackey (3), Lee Berman (3)

The debate

The theory first appears on the list in 1998, when Michael Straight [Michael Straight, 1998] argued that Typhon, recognising Severian, takes him to be the Conciliator's descendant, or suspects the Conciliator is long-lived. In 2001 Michael Andre-Driussi [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2001] proposed that Severian might be Tartaros, son of Typhon, reincarnated by old-fashioned means. In 2002 Don Doggett [Don Doggett, 2002] [Don Doggett, 2002] tied Typhon to Ymar and Severian through a dynastic line rather than direct sonship.

From 2003 the debate centred on Lisa Schaffer-Doggett, who argued that Severian is Typhon's son and that Ymar and Severian share too many parallels to be different people [Lisa Schaffer-Doggett, 2003] [Lisa Schaffer-Doggett, 2003] [Lisa Schaffer-Doggett, 2003]. James Wynn disputed the Ymar-Typhon-Severian family line and the clone idea, arguing that clones would not look so different [James Wynn, 2003] [James Wynn, 2003]. Roy C. Lackey argued that a Christ-like Severian cannot be part Satan and that Silk cannot be a Typhon clone [Roy C. Lackey, 2003]. In 2005 the Wynns proposed that Severian is a clone of Piaton, whom they identified as Typhon's son Hieros, citing the crypt Severian leads Scylla to in The Short Sun [James Wynn, 2005] [James Wynn, 2005]. Mournings Glory disputed that the tomb is the Typhon family mausoleum, citing coffin counts and Cilinia's grave [mournings glory, 2005]. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes mocked the clone idea with the Spanish Barber joke [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2005].

Later posts kept the question open. Marc Aramini [Marc Aramini, 2010] [Marc Aramini, 2010] pressed the mausoleum link between Severian's face and Typhon's family in 2010. Jane Delawney [Jane Delawney, 2010] and Son of Witz [Son of Witz, 2010] proposed alternative kinship routes, while Gerry Quinn [Gerry Quinn, 2011] argued that Frog is Romulus rather than Ymar or Typhon in disguise. In 2013 Aramini [Marc Aramini, 2013] argued that a Matachin tomb would imply a genetic link, and in 2014 he revived the Don Doggett debate and proposed Pia as Severian's sister [Marc Aramini, 2014].

For

  • Severian and Ymar share too many parallels to be separate people, and the Frog story makes Ymar Typhon's son [Lisa Schaffer-Doggett, 2003].
  • Wynn read the Short Sun crypt as Typhon's family mausoleum and so as a link to Severian [James Wynn, 2005].
  • Wynn cited a Wolfe interview saying Pas is Typhon, which he took to support Severian's link to Typhon's line [James Wynn, 2005].
  • Scylla's casket lies in the mausoleum Severian later claims, which Delawney took to show a blood relation to Typhon [Jane Delawney, 2010].
  • Aramini argued that Severian's face appearing in the family crypt suggests a relation to Typhon's family [Marc Aramini, 2010].

Against

  • Lackey argued that Severian's mountain temptation mirrors Jesus's temptation by Satan, so a Christ-like figure cannot be part Satan, and that Silk's sainthood rules out a Typhon clone [Roy C. Lackey, 2003].
  • Mournings Glory argued that the crypt Severian uses differs from the mausoleum Horn visits, citing differing coffin counts and Cilinia's grave [mournings glory, 2005].
  • Lackey argued that the dark hair and pale skin of Severian and Ymar weigh against clone or descendant readings [Roy C. Lackey, 2005].
  • Wynn argued that clones would not look as different as Severian and Typhon do [James Wynn, 2003].
  • Gerry Quinn argued that Frog is clearly Romulus and rejected the Ymar and Typhon reading of the Frog tale [Gerry Quinn, 2011] [Gerry Quinn, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the theory was raised repeatedly from 1998 to 2014 with many proponents and critics, but the list never reached consensus.
