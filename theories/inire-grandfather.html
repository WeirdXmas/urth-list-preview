Father Inire is Severian's ancestor

Father Inire is Severian's grandfather or ancestor, which gives Severian a non-human strain.

15 threads · 21 posts · for 9 / against 10 · first raised 2007-11-27 by Roy C. Lackey
Main voices: Lee Berman (6), John Watkins (3), David Stockhoff (3), Roy C. Lackey (2), Andrew Mason (2), Son of Witz (1)

The debate

The ancestry idea first appears on the list in Lee Berman's post of 2010-07-09, which proposes that the old boatman is Inire and that Little Severian is a cousin, in a family-tree theory [Lee Berman, 2010]. Berman's 2010-12-04 post added a line through Dorcas's husband, grounded in Borski's Moses reading and Severian's resemblance to Greek heroes [Lee Berman, 2010]. Earlier, John Watkins had argued in July 2010 that Severian is Inire outright, a different claim that others quickly contested [John Watkins, 2010][Josh Geller, 2010]. The earliest Rudesind post, by Roy C. Lackey, is a separate idea about steering rather than ancestry [Roy C. Lackey, 2007].

The discussion turned critical in August 2010, when Lackey disputed Berman's grandfather claim as lacking textual evidence and noted the speculation piled upon speculation [Roy C. Lackey, 2010]. Andrew Mason argued that Inire is not a shapeshifter, citing his masks and hoods, and questioned whether a human-alien breeding link is workable [Andrew Mason, 2010]. Watkins defended the wider identity claims by answering that people in these books disguise themselves and lie [John Watkins, 2010]. Berman extended the theory in 2011, linking the boatman to the Jungle Guide and to the averns, and using a Heracles and Theseus pattern [Lee Berman, 2011]. Berman also used Ceryx-Typhon and Simon Magus parallels [Lee Berman, 2011].

In October 2011 David Stockhoff argued that the boatman's death contradicts the grandfather reading and that no clear evidence shows Inire helping Severian [David Stockhoff, 2011], then partly retracted that claim [David Stockhoff, 2011]. Sergei Soloviev objected that identifying Inire with Severian's grandfather obscures Wolfe's message [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011]. Berman's April 2012 post recast Inire as an ancestor of Severian within a pre-Christian trinity linked to Dionysus [Lee Berman, 2012]. Across the threads, the 'Boatman as Inire' thread saw Jack Smith and Gerry Quinn offer family-tree ideas while Watkins, Lackey and Jeff Wilson disputed the identification, and the 'Father Inire Theory' thread ended with Berman calling it too sprawling to state whole.

For

  • The boatman is Inire and Little Severian is a cousin, proposed in a family-tree theory [Lee Berman, 2010].
  • Dorcas's husband line descends from Inire, with the incest framed as a deliberate effect in first person [Lee Berman, 2010].
  • The boatman claimed to have seen Inire plant the averns, which Berman cites for the grandfather link [Lee Berman, 2011].
  • Inire could disguise himself and lie, so Severian's different looks do not rule out the identification [John Watkins, 2010].
  • Inire's role as creator and caretaker, and an implied ancestry of Severian, are given as grounds for the claim [Lee Berman, 2012].

Against

  • Lackey found no textual evidence for the grandfather or shape-changer claims and called the speculation excessive [Roy C. Lackey, 2010].
  • Mason argued Inire is not a shapeshifter, citing his masks, and doubted that human-alien breeding works [Andrew Mason, 2010].
  • Stockhoff argued that the boatman's death contradicts the grandfather reading and no clear evidence shows Inire helped Severian [David Stockhoff, 2011].
  • Soloviev objected that identifying Inire with Severian's grandfather obscures Wolfe's message [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011].
  • Watkins and Wilson disputed the boatman identification on textual grounds in the 'Boatman as Inire' thread, which the record reports without a single post id for each.

Where it stands: Still disputed: the idea was pushed mainly by Lee Berman across many threads, which ended in impasse, and the posters who responded mostly rejected it.
