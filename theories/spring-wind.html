Spring Wind is Typhon

Spring Wind, the ruler in the stone-town legend, is Typhon (perhaps modelled on Alexander).

24 threads · 56 posts · for 23 / against 32 · first raised 2002-05-06 by Michael Andre-Driussi
Main voices: Lee Berman (18), James Wynn (16), Andrew Mason (10), Gerry Quinn (8), Marc Aramini (4), Son of Witz (2)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by Matthew Weber in 2003, who responded to an earlier post's claim that Spring Wind is Typhon [Matthew Weber, 2003], though the earliest posts already on the Frog and Fish story came from Michael Andre-Driussi and Josh Geller in 2002 [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2002][Josh Geller, 2002]. The idea became a serious theme in 2010, when James Wynn argued that Spring Wind, son of Early Summer in the inset legend, is Typhon and that Typhon was not born on Urth [James Wynn, 2010]. Gerry Quinn and Andrew Mason pressed back, Quinn on the weak etymology and the lack of any evidence that Spring Wind ruled Urth [Gerry Quinn, 2010][Gerry Quinn, 2010], and Mason on a Romulus-and-Remus mapping that gives Spring Wind the role of Mars [Andrew Mason, 2010].

Lee Berman was the most frequent supporter, first offering a benign Typhon-Set reading and then linking Typhon to wind through Return to the Whorl [Lee Berman, 2010][Lee Berman, 2010], and later proposing that Spring Wind is a younger Typhon whose life allegorizes Alexander [Lee Berman, 2012]. Wynn kept extending the theory with Alexander and Midas links and an argument that the name is a deliberate authorial construction, against Quinn's view that the name is inherited legend material [James Wynn, 2011][James Wynn, 2011][Gerry Quinn, 2011]. Quinn's Romulus reading of Frog, based on the basket, wolf-rearing and twin-killing parallels, became the main rival, and Nick Lee and Marc Aramini later took the Mars line too [Gerry Quinn, 2011][Nick Lee, 2011].

The discussion stayed unresolved. Posters repeatedly restated their positions across threads such as Spring Wind, Grand Unified Theory and Wind god, and later digests show Aramini arguing for a Green Man reading and a Mars-born Spring Wind [Marc Aramini, 2014][Marc Aramini, 2014]. No thread recorded a decisive new clue, and the debate turned on whether the Romulus parallels or the Typhon etymology better explain the text.

For

  • Spring Wind's name is treated as an overt reference to Typhon, which Wynn argued needs no further proof [James Wynn, 2010].
  • Typhon's link to winds, via the real etymological connection to typhoon, supports identifying Spring Wind with him [James Wynn, 2010].
  • Berman argued that Return to the Whorl repeatedly links Typhon/Pas to wind, and that Pas's name derives from Pazuzu [Lee Berman, 2010].
  • Wynn argued that the Frog story's naming pattern and the Spring Wind name point to a deliberate authorial construction rather than folk material [James Wynn, 2011].
  • Berman proposed that Typhon's life allegorizes Alexander, with Spring Wind as a younger Typhon [Lee Berman, 2012].

Against

  • Quinn argued the Sword of the Lictor story shows only that Spring Wind is extraterrestrial and warlike, not that he ruled Urth [Gerry Quinn, 2010].
  • Quinn pointed to the missing second head, the absence of any mention of the father, and the lack of evidence that Spring Wind ruled Urth [Gerry Quinn, 2010].
  • Mason and Quinn argued the Romulus and Remus and Mowgli parallels explain the Spring Wind tale, with Spring Wind as Mars [Andrew Mason, 2010][Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • Quinn argued that Typhon's wind link is too weak, and that the name Spring Wind and the Frog story are inherited legend material [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • Wynn's own Hercules and Ymar identifications were disputed by Quinn, who said Frog is clearly Romulus [Gerry Quinn, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed: most posters did not accept the Spring Wind is Typhon reading, and the threads ended without agreement, with several positions simply restated.
