Dorcas is Severian's grandmother

Dorcas, drowned and revived in the Lake of Birds, is Severian's paternal grandmother; her son is Ouen, Severian's father, so Severian's love affair with her is unwitting incest.

57 threads · 92 posts · for 43 / against 42 · first raised 1997-02-07 by Michael Andre-Driussi
Main voices: Lee Berman (14), Ryan Dunn (12), James Wynn (10), António Pedro Marques (8), b sharp (6), Gerry Quinn (5)

The debate

The idea that Dorcas is Severian's grandmother appears on the list first in a February 1997 post by Michael Andre-Driussi, who proposed a family tree with Ouen as father, Catherine as mother, Dorcas as paternal grandmother and the Lake boatman as paternal grandfather [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997]. In July 1997 he again cited Manlove's essay as having missed the grandmother point [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997], and in 1998 Jon Camfield proposed the same paternal-grandmother link, grounding it in the Claw passages about the 'father's mounted guard' and the missing-sister problem [Jon Camfield, 1998]. Over the following years the theory was disputed by Don Doggett, who rejected incest readings and suggested a Typhon-based Ymar link [Don Doggett, 2002], and Marc Aramini and Lisa Schaffer-Doggett and James Wynn offered Dorcas as mother or sister instead [Marc Aramini, 2002][Lisa Schaffer-Doggett, 2003][James Wynn, 2003].

The 2006 to 2008 posts by b sharp revived and extended the theory, treating the incest as an Oedipal curse and Dorcas as grandmother [b sharp, 2006][b sharp, 2008], while Josh Geller and brunians objected that the clues were too weak to show authorial intent [Josh Geller, 2008]. In 2010 a long wave of threads (notably 'Dorcas (was: The mystery of the image of an astronaut cleaned by Rudesind)' and 'Severian's Family. What the text shows...') pitted Ryan Dunn and Gerry Quinn, who cited the locket identification and the innkeeper's remark as canon [Gerry Quinn, 2010][Ryan Dunn, 2010], against James Wynn and António Pedro Marques, who argued the text does not confirm the links and that the grandmother reading was a late, weak puzzle [James Wynn, 2010][António Pedro Marques, 2010]. Tony Ellis and Ryan Dunn countered that readers solved it in the 1980s, citing a 1991 Vector article [Tony Ellis, 2010][Tony Ellis, 2010].

Later posts moved the debate toward the evidence standard: Jack Smith and António Pedro Marques discussed how the clues surfaced [António Pedro Marques, 2011], Marc Aramini called identity-shifting central to Wolfe's work [Marc Aramini, 2011], and Craig Brewer in 2012 used the theory as the main example of a puzzle that must be read rather than interpreted [Craig Brewer, 2012].

For

  • Michael Andre-Driussi proposed the family tree with Dorcas as paternal grandmother in 1997, citing Shadow and Claw passages [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997].
  • Jon Camfield grounded the paternal-grandmother link in the 'father's mounted guard' detail and the memory-mixing after the House Absolute performance [Jon Camfield, 1998].
  • b sharp argued the family's incest is an Oedipal curse and that Severian withholds the grandmother relation in his narration [b sharp, 2006][b sharp, 2008].
  • Ryan Dunn and Gerry Quinn presented the locket identification and the Matachin Tower birth account as textual support for the Dorcas-Ouen-Severian chain [Gerry Quinn, 2010][Ryan Dunn, 2010].
  • Lee Berman argued an Autarch with Severian's curiosity would have searched the records, so the omission itself signals the secret [Lee Berman, 2010].

Against

  • Don Doggett called the revelation a plot-breaking, ham-handed dud and rejected Dorcas as grandmother [Don Doggett, 2006][Don Doggett, 2002].
  • James Wynn argued the text does not confirm Ouen as father or Dorcas as grandmother and that Catherine may have borne other children [James Wynn, 2010].
  • António Pedro Marques argued the grandmother link adds family-tree data while the Rudesind-Inire identification only subtracts a character, and that such patterns can be found in almost anything [António Pedro Marques, 2010][António Pedro Marques, 2010].
  • Gerry Quinn argued the grandmother claim is not essential to the story and has little narrative payoff [Gerry Quinn, 2010].
  • Josh Geller argued the proposed sexual and kinship clues are too weak to show Wolfe intended them [Josh Geller, 2008].

Where it stands: Widely treated by later posters as an accepted solution, with Jerry Friedman and Marc Aramini citing it as a shared conclusion, yet repeatedly disputed in the 2010 threads over whether the text confirms it and how it was recognised.

Evidence usually cited

  • Dorcas remembers a husband and a baby son, and an old man searches for his drowned wife.
  • Ouen resembles Severian and has a dead lover named Cas whom Dorcas resembles.
