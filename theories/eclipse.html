What caused the eclipse at Apu-Punchau

The eclipse during the Apu-Punchau scene was caused by Tzadkiel's ship passing the sun, or by Severian himself.

22 threads · 44 posts · for 28 / against 19 · first raised 1997-04-14 in the thread "Witches & eclipse-maker"
Main voices: b sharp (8), Joel Priddy (4), Michael Andre-Driussi (4), Roy C. Lackey (4), Dave Tallman (4), Jeff Wilson (4)

The debate

The idea that Tzadkiel's ship caused the Apu-Punchau eclipse was first raised on 1997-05-18, when Dan Rabin argued that the large opaque ship passed between the sun and the town [Dan Rabin, 1997]. The same day Joel Priddy offered rival readings of Severian's time magic or the Hierodules' ship [Joel Priddy, 1997], and in 1998 Michael Andre-Driussi and Tony A. West added sail-based and Hierodule-contrived variants [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998] [Tony A. West, 1998]. In 1999 Andre-Driussi worked out how large the sail would need to be at low orbit [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1999], and Mark Millman proposed a larger ship farther from Urth [Mark Millman, 1999]. Discussion stayed sparse until 2006, when Dan'l Danehy-Oakes and Rex Racer tied the eclipse to the black hole and the New Sun's approach [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2006] [Rex Racer, 2006].

The theory was most heavily argued in June 2008, when a burst of posts set the Ship against Severian and against a natural lunar eclipse. Roy C. Lackey argued the eclipse was staged, since the sun returned too quickly [Roy C. Lackey, 2008], while Dave Tallman argued it was an ordinary lunar eclipse timed by Juturna [Dave Tallman, 2008]. Jeff Wilson argued that a ship casting that shadow would need to be miles wide [Jeff Wilson, 2008], and Eric Ortlund and b sharp argued Severian's own body or emotions caused it [Eric Ortlund, 2008] [b sharp, 2008]. Matthew Groves backed the Ship as the more plausible option against the Severian-as-shadow idea [Matthew Groves, 2008].

From 2010 to 2012 Lee Berman and Jeff Wilson revived the debate, with Berman arguing that Severian's naturalistic powers explain the events [Lee Berman, 2010] [Lee Berman, 2012] and Wilson denying that the Ship could cast the shadow [Jeff Wilson, 2012]. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes proposed the Megatherians as a local, smaller cause [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2012]. The participating summaries describe the threads ending in impasse, with each side keeping its own interpretation.

For

  • The text's line about an opaque body between the Old Sun and Urth supports a ship causing the eclipse [Dan Rabin, 1997].
  • Andre-Driussi's calculations show a sail of modest size near Earth could produce a total eclipse [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1999].
  • Dave Tallman cited a line in which a hand says the vessel sometimes blocks stars from view, supporting a ship moving into the inner system [Dave Tallman, 2008].
  • Lackey argued the sun returned too quickly for a natural eclipse and the Ship could move faster than the moon [Roy C. Lackey, 2008].
  • Groves argued the afterword nearly states that the Ship is the cause [Matthew Groves, 2008].

Against

  • Wilson calculated that a ship casting the described shadow would need to be far wider than a mile [Jeff Wilson, 2008].
  • Wilson cited the moon's size and the crew's walking-time remarks against the ship's role [Jeff Wilson, 2012].
  • Berman argued Severian's powers are naturalistic and emotion-driven, and that the ship's detour to drop Severian on Urth makes it an unlikely cause [Lee Berman, 2012].
  • b sharp judged the timing too coincidental, since Severian cannot die and needs no rescue [b sharp, 2008].
  • Tallman argued the eclipse was an ordinary lunar eclipse timed by Juturna [Dave Tallman, 2008].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never reached consensus, and the rival readings of ship, Severian, Hierodules, Megatherians and natural lunar eclipse all remained open.
