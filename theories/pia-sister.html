Pia is Severian's sister

Pia, the chained girl on Lake Diuturna, is Severian's sister.

7 threads · 7 posts · for 6 / against 2 · first raised 2000-08-13 by A Aramini
Main voices: Marc Aramini (6), A Aramini (1), Talarican (1)

The debate

The idea that Pia, the chained girl at Lake Diuturna, is Severian's sister was first raised on the list by A Aramini in August 2000 [A Aramini, 2000], who cited a Dante Purgatorio figure named Pia, the Severa/Severian twin motif, and matching physical descriptions. Talarican relayed the same idea a few days later as Severa, Severian's lost sister, and paired it with a chronology of the guild masters [Talarican, 2000]. Marc Aramini then folded the sister reading into a broader shadow-family argument in late 2000 [Marc Aramini, 2000], and in 2010 tied Pia's possible identity to the Fomalhaut and fish-and-frog material [Marc Aramini, 2010].

Over the following decade Marc Aramini remained the main proponent, using Pia's dark hair and pale skin and his own question to Wolfe about her to support the reading [Marc Aramini, 2013]. In 2011 he also used Pia's genetic description to argue against a sweeping incest-curse reading [Marc Aramini, 2011]. The 2014 revival of a 2002 Typhon-as-father thread saw him argue that Pia, not Scylla, fits better as sister, citing the lake people's fish imagery, Severian's frog-like swimming and Pia's age and build [Marc Aramini, 2014].

The posts record no post by another poster that supports the sister identification, and the threads on undines, time and the 2002 lineage revival treated the idea mainly as one thread among others, with Gerry Quinn and Lee Berman rejecting the incest-curse framework more broadly [Marc Aramini, 2011]. The discussion largely ended in impasse and drifted toward Christology and free will.

For

  • Pia's matching physical description and the Severa/Severian twin motif were cited as grounds for a sister identity [A Aramini, 2000].
  • Talarican relayed the reading that Pia is a lost sister named Severa, adding guild chronology to the case [Talarican, 2000].
  • Marc Aramini tied Pia to the shadow-family and foundling reading of Severian's origins [Marc Aramini, 2000].
  • Pia's dark hair and pale skin were cited as matching Severian's genetic description, and the poster questioned Wolfe directly about Pia [Marc Aramini, 2011][Marc Aramini, 2013].
  • Pia, rather than Scylla, was argued to fit as sister based on fish imagery, frog swimming and age [Marc Aramini, 2014].

Against

  • Marc Aramini doubted how Pia reached the lake people, a gap in the original case [A Aramini, 2000].
  • Severian's black hair was argued to undercut the golden-glow marker, complicating genetic readings of his relatives [Marc Aramini, 2011].
  • Gerry Quinn and Marc Aramini rejected the sweeping incest-curse reading that supports the sister idea [Marc Aramini, 2011].

Where it stands: Raised by several posters but never widely accepted; still held mainly by Marc Aramini, with other posters leaving it unresolved in the threads that touched it.
