A black hole is killing the sun

The old sun is dying because a black hole was placed in it (by the Hierogrammates or as punishment), not from natural ageing.

45 threads · 79 posts · for 42 / against 33 · first raised 1997-04-04 by Michael Andre-Driussi
Main voices: Gerry Quinn (7), Jeff Wilson (6), Lee Berman (6), mary whalen (5), Roy C. Lackey (5), Michael Andre-Driussi (4)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by Michael Andre-Driussi in 1997, who argued that the dying sun's black hole needed very long timescales [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997], and then explicitly by him in January 1998, claiming the Old Sun was killed by a black hole placed in it by someone [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998]. Early 1998 posts by C R Culver proposed that a black hole fell in or was planted by tyrannical monarchs [C R Culver, 1998][C R Culver, 1998], and Jon Camfield and Michael Andre-Driussi proposed deliberate placement for cooling or energy [Jon Camfield, 1998][Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998].

The physics of the black hole dominated 1998, with Mary Whalen, Jason Voegele, Damien Broderick and Matthew Freestone disputing how fast a black hole would consume the sun and whether a wormhole was the better device [mary whalen, 1998][Jason Voegele, 1998][Damien Broderick, 1998][Matthew Freestone, 1998]. In 2006 the question of who placed the hole was taken up in the 'Severian's miraculous eclipse' thread, where Rex Racer and Dan'l Danehy-Oakes proposed Tzadkiel's ship or Typhon's scientists [Rex Racer, 2006][Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2006], and mournings glory disputed Typhon's role [mournings glory, 2006]. Lee Berman's 2010 and 2011 posts argued the hole was a natural event [Lee Berman, 2010][Lee Berman, 2011].

A turning point came in 2008, when Roy C. Lackey cited the condemnation passage in The Urth of the New Sun and argued the Hierogrammates placed the hole [Roy C. Lackey, 2008][Dave Tallman, 2008]; John Watkins conceded the Hierogrammates likely did it after Apheta's quoted line, though the motive stayed unknown [John Watkins, 2008]. Paul B disputed the motive as implausible [Paul B, 2008][Paul B, 2008], and Jeff Wilson argued that transporting a hole was possible in the books [Jeff Wilson, 2008]. The 2011 Gerry Quinn and Sergei Soloviev exchange brought in red-giant objections [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011][Gerry Quinn, 2011].

For

  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes and Dave Tallman argued that the sun's hole was placed deliberately, citing Apheta's wording and the condemnation passage [Dave Tallman, 2008][Roy C. Lackey, 2008].
  • Roy C. Lackey argued the Hierogrammates condemned Urth and placed the hole, reading Apheta's use of 'we' as pointing to them [Roy C. Lackey, 2008][Roy C. Lackey, 2008].
  • John Watkins conceded that Apheta's quoted line makes the black hole a Hierogrammate act [John Watkins, 2008].
  • Jeff Wilson argued that antigravity devices in the books could make a placed black hole plausible [Jeff Wilson, 2008].
  • Adam Stephanides argued the Increate's sentence was the black hole punishment, with the New Sun lifting it [Adam Stephanides, 2005].

Against

  • Mary Whalen argued that a black hole inside the sun would consume it within seconds and so cannot explain its slow dying [mary whalen, 1998][mary whalen, 1998].
  • Jason Voegele argued that a small black hole would absorb matter only slowly, citing Brin and Simmons [Jason Voegele, 1998].
  • Paul B argued the Hierogrammates could have achieved their goal more simply and that Typhon had no motive [Paul B, 2008][Paul B, 2008].
  • mournings glory argued that Severian's remark about the sky people's ancestors shows Typhon had no direct role in the wounding [mournings glory, 2006].
  • Lee Berman argued the black hole need not have been placed by anyone and questioned the assumption of deliberate placement [Lee Berman, 2010][Lee Berman, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never settled who placed the black hole or whether it is a black hole at all, and most later posters treated the placed-hole reading as one option among several, with Lackey and Watkins leaning toward the Hierogrammates.
