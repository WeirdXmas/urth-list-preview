Tzadkiel's ship travels in time

Tzadkiel's ship moves outside ordinary space and time, which explains the duplicate Severians, Jonas and Hethor's arrival on Urth and other time anomalies.

26 threads · 29 posts · for 15 / against 12 · first raised 1998-07-09 by Michael Andre-Driussi
Main voices: Lee Berman (6), Gerry Quinn (3), Jeff Wilson (2), Tony Ellis (2), Michael Andre-Driussi (1), Roy C. Lackey (1)

The debate

The theory first appears on the list in 1998, when Michael Andre-Driussi [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998] read Severian's ship time and Yesod through an Egyptian afterlife model, and he later admitted this might be a tangent. The first post that states the card's claim directly is Roy C. Lackey's 1999 post [Roy C. Lackey, 1999], which says the ship operates outside space and time and links this to the duplicate selves and the blackout. Peter Stephenson [Peter Stephenson, 1999] joked that the Matachin tower might be Jonas's ship after a time-warp, and Josh Levitan [Josh Levitan, 2000] pushed back on related Whorl secrets in 2000.

From 2005 to 2007 the debate moved to relativity and faster-than-light travel. Andrew Kertesz [Andrew Kertesz, 2005] proposed a relativistic time debt that sends the ship backward, and thalassocrat [thalassocrat, 2006] argued that modest constant acceleration could compress a thousand-year Whorl voyage. Andy Robertson [Andy Robertson, 2006] argued that in the Sun books FTL and time travel are the same thing, and Jeff Wilson [Jeff Wilson, 2007] argued that time travel arises as a side effect of FTL combined with relativity. Nessun Saprà [Nessun Saprà, 2007] cited a line from the captain to argue that the Tzadkiel is a single ship across all times.

From 2010 onward the posts grew more varied. Cliff Judge [Cliff Judge, 2010] treated Tzadkiel as a God figure, and Jeff Wilson [Jeff Wilson, 2010] tied the blue-shifted view of creation to Yesod. Lee Berman argued that the ship travels outside time [Lee Berman, 2010] and that time runs backward on it [Lee Berman, 2010] [Lee Berman, 2010], while Tony Ellis disputed these readings [Tony Ellis, 2010] [Tony Ellis, 2010]. Berman then proposed that the ship is a shape-shifted Tzadkiel [Lee Berman, 2012], and Gerry Quinn [Gerry Quinn, 2011] argued against the single-ship reading, which Berman had pressed [Lee Berman, 2011]. Nathan C. Tresch [Nathan C. Tresch, 2012] proposed that the Whorl transforms into the Ship over time. The thread never reached agreement.

For

  • Lackey argued from Gunnie's explanation that the ship operates outside space and time, allowing duplicate selves [Roy C. Lackey, 1999].
  • Kertesz argued that faster-than-light travel incurs a relativistic time debt, so the ship arrives before it departs [Andrew Kertesz, 2005].
  • Robertson argued that faster-than-light travel is routine in the Sun books and that in those books it is the same as time travel [Andy Robertson, 2006].
  • Wilson cited Severian's descriptions of the outside universe and the flying saucer episode, whose timelines reverse relative to Urth time, as evidence for time travel from FTL [Jeff Wilson, 2007].
  • Berman argued that Severian's own past is reversed on the swift-sailing ship and that Yesod lies in the ship's past [Lee Berman, 2010] [Lee Berman, 2010].

Against

  • Levitan argued that hibernation and the Rajan's remarks fit a warmth or sunlight dependence rather than a secret tied to time travel [Josh Levitan, 2000].
  • Ellis argued that humanity could create the Hierogrammates within a Briahtic universe, so the ship's travel to Yesod does not contradict Tzadkiel's remarks [Tony Ellis, 2010].
  • Friedman argued that the transporter room dates from Jonas's time and not from his ship [Jerry Friedman, 2010].
  • Quinn argued that several ships are lost in time rather than a single Tzadkiel vessel, citing a stranger's ships lost between the suns and Jonas's crashed ship [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • Quinn argued that Tzadkiel came from nearer our time and that the Ship never appears as a white fountain, so the quasar reading fails [Gerry Quinn, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the theory was raised repeatedly from 1998 to 2012 and gained variants, but the list never reached agreement on whether the ship goes back in time, moves outside time, or is one vessel.
