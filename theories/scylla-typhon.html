Scylla is Typhon's daughter

Scylla, goddess of the Whorl, is Typhon's daughter Scylla, and is linked to the Scylla of the sea.

21 threads · 21 posts · for 15 / against 4 · first raised 1997-04-10 by Kieran Cleary
Main voices: Lee Berman (8), Robert Borski (2), Kieran Cleary (1), Michael Andre-Driussi (1), J. Schultz (1), Greene, Carlton (1)

The debate

The idea that Scylla, the goddess of the Whorl, is Typhon's daughter, and linked to the sea monster Scylla, first appears on the list in April 1997, when Kieran Cleary identified Scylla as the daughter in Rose's passage [Kieran Cleary, 1997]. Michael Andre-Driussi's 1998 post on Typhon and his rebels is a related early contribution [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998], and J. Schultz that June proposed the Whorl's Scylla as Typhon's daughter, grounding it in Lovecraftian hints and Wolfe's habit of tying characters together [J. Schultz, 1998]. Carlton Greene asked in 1999 whether the sea-monster Scylla might be Typhon's daughter Scyla, citing no passages [Greene, Carlton, 1999]. Robert Borski made the most substantive early case in 2000, arguing the entity Horn and Seawrack call the Mother is Typhon's daughter Scylla, based on red imagery and Horn's comparisons [Robert Borski, 2000]. Ian Smith floated the same identification shortly after [Ian Smith, 2000].

The debate split over the years. Borski himself later disputed the Scylla identity, arguing that the Scylla of The Book of the Long Sun is Pas's daughter Cilinia [Robert Borski, 2001], and Roy C. Lackey later argued Typhon could not be the two-headed Scylla of the ship [Roy C. Lackey, 2010]. Michael Straight and James Jordan offered their own readings in 2002 [Michael Straight, 2002] [James Jordan, 2002]. From 2008 onward Lee Berman became the main proponent, linking Scylla to Echidna, Abaia, the Cumaean and the black-bean origin, and proposing that Scylla is Echidna's daughter [Lee Berman, 2010] [Lee Berman, 2014]. Pedro Pereira argued that Typhon's daughter took the name Scylla after the sea monster [Pedro Pereira, 2008]. António Pedro Marques supported a Typhon-family reading by citing Scylla's line 'Eat sheep. Eat boy.' [António Pedro Marques, 2011], while Gerry Quinn suggested Typhon may not have recognised the name Cilinia took [Gerry Quinn, 2011].

Most of the later discussion remained contested rather than resolved. Berman's arguments rested on Greek myth and Gaon Echidna worship, and posters including James Wynn and Gerry Quinn disputed name-based and mythological readings, with Quinn rejecting the Typhon-Echidna sibling link [Lee Berman, 2010] [Lee Berman, 2013]. Jonathan Goodwin's 2014 question about Typhon's calm over sea monsters prompted Berman's answer that Typhon is their father via Scylla [Lee Berman, 2014], and Marques conceded the sibling link was unproven in the 'And Now For Something Completely' thread.

For

  • Kieran Cleary identified Scylla as the daughter in Rose's passage, the earliest support on the list [Kieran Cleary, 1997].
  • Robert Borski argued the Mother is Typhon's daughter Scylla, citing matching red imagery and Horn's own comparisons of the Mother to Scylla [Robert Borski, 2000].
  • Lee Berman argued Scylla is Echidna's daughter and that the sea monsters of Urth came from Blue, grounded in Gaon's worship of Echidna and the names of her seven children [Lee Berman, 2010].
  • Lee Berman argued Typhon's confidence in the sea monsters comes from being their father, citing the Greek Typhon myth and Long Sun and Short Sun passages on Scylla [Lee Berman, 2014].
  • António Pedro Marques read Scylla's 'Eat sheep. Eat boy.' as implying cannibalism within the Typhon family [António Pedro Marques, 2011].

Against

  • Robert Borski later argued the Scylla of The Book of the Long Sun is Pas's daughter Cilinia, not Typhon's daughter [Robert Borski, 2001].
  • Roy C. Lackey argued Rigoglio could not have recognised Typhon in the two-headed figure because the ship's gods never followed the colonists to Blue [Roy C. Lackey, 2010].
  • James Wynn objected that an insider god like Scylla cannot be the Outsider, since the Outsider's defining trait is being outside the Whorl [Lee Berman, 2010].
  • Gerry Quinn disputed name-based readings and the Typhon-Echidna sibling link, and questioned whether Typhon knew of Cilinia's affair with the monster Scylla [Lee Berman, 2010] [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • Gerry Quinn rejected Typhon's relative humanity and imagined the sea monsters as nanotech singularity beings, away from the Scylla identification [Lee Berman, 2013].

Where it stands: Still disputed: raised in 1997 and argued by Berman through the 2010s, but posters including Quinn, Wynn and Borski never accepted it, and Marques conceded the sibling link was unproven.
