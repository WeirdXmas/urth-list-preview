The inhumi evolved from vines on Green

The inhumi are native to Green, evolved from parasitic vines, and can breed only there.

40 threads · 77 posts · for 46 / against 32 · first raised 1997-02-05 by xury
Main voices: James Wynn (16), Lee Berman (13), Marc Aramini (12), David Stockhoff (11), Gerry Quinn (5), Robert Borski (3)

The debate

The vine-origin idea first appears on the list in March 2002, when Marc Aramini argued that the trees and inhumu of The Book of the Short Sun are tied to a plant-based biology [Marc Aramini, 2002]. In November 2002 he went further, proposing that the inhumi evolved from tree-planting vines [Marc Aramini, 2002]. The Green-only breeding claim has an earlier history on the Whorl list: Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued in 2001 that the Neighbors state outright that inhumi can breed only on Green [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2001], while Robert Borski countered that they also breed in Blue's sewage and stagnant water [Robert Borski, 2001].

The idea was revived heavily in 2009. Brian Lovely proposed that the Neighbors are trees and the inhumi parasitic vines [Brian Lovely, 2009] [Brian Lovely, 2009], and David Stockhoff independently argued that the inhumi evolved from parasitic vines [David Stockhoff, 2009]. James Wynn and Tim O'Donnell objected that the theory could not explain how inhumi become vines or change back into winged reptiles [James Wynn, 2009] [O'Donnell, Tim (BOSI), 2009]. Matthew Groves offered an alternative: the inhumi look vine-like because they feed on Green's trees [Matthew Groves, 2009]. Stockhoff's 2008 predecessor, thalassocrat, had argued that inhumi must breed on Green and are ferried there by the Neighbors [thalassocrat, 2008].

From 2010 to 2011 Aramini, Lee Berman and Wynn kept the theory alive, often tangled with Dionysus readings [James Wynn, 2011] [Lee Berman, 2011]. Aramini refined it to liana vines as primitive, blood-free inhumi [Marc Aramini, 2011] [Marc Aramini, 2011], and in November 2011 argued that the inhumi are native to Green [Marc Aramini, 2011]. That month brought a burst of rival versions: Stockhoff proposed slug-like bugs merging with plants [David Stockhoff, 2011], James Wynn restated that inhumi become vines by feeding on Neighbors [James Wynn, 2011], and Stockhoff and Wynn each attacked Aramini's lianas-eating-animals account [David Stockhoff, 2011] [James Wynn, 2011]. Gerry Quinn, who rejected a literal tree-and-vine reading from the start, argued that Wolfe would have stated any rapid evolution outright [Gerry Quinn, 2011]. In December 2012 Andrew Mason backed a Green origin [Andrew Mason, 2012], and Stockhoff proposed descent from Green's vines with a space-travel route [David Stockhoff, 2012], then disputed Aramini's evolutionary reading of Quetzal's tree story [David Stockhoff, 2012].

Several long threads on these questions, including 'Like a good Neighbor', 'Rajan and Food', 'Dionysus' and 'Crocagators', ended without agreement. The 'Quetzal On Urth?' thread was settled by a quoted Wolfe answer about Quetzal's late arrival on the Whorl rather than by any vine evidence.

For

  • Aramini argued that inhumi are native to Green, citing a triumphant claim after they reached Urth as a sign of Greenish origin [Marc Aramini, 2011].
  • Danehy-Oakes argued the Neighbors state outright that inhumi breed only on Green, citing the word 'must' in Return to the Whorl [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2001].
  • Brian Lovely proposed that the inhumi are parasitic vines, grounding the idea in the series' tree and vine imagery [Brian Lovely, 2009] [Brian Lovely, 2009].
  • Thalassocrat argued that inhumi reproduce only on Green and that the Neighbors physically carry them to the Whorl [thalassocrat, 2008].
  • Andrew Mason argued the inhumi come from Green, citing Quetzal's wish for humans to go there and Jahlee's astral journey to a Green cliff-tower [Andrew Mason, 2012].

Against

  • Gerry Quinn argued the lianas are metaphors and that Wolfe, as an SF writer, would have clearly indicated any rapid plant-animal evolution [Gerry Quinn, 2011] [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • James Wynn and Tim O'Donnell argued that the theory cannot explain how inhumi become vines or change back into winged, clawed reptiles [James Wynn, 2009] [O'Donnell, Tim (BOSI), 2009].
  • Stockhoff argued that if inhumi became vines by feeding on Neighbors, Pajarocu would be full of lianas the Neighbors would burn, and that the theory fails on the setting [David Stockhoff, 2011].
  • Stockhoff argued that cold makes breeding on Blue impossible, which undercuts the Green-only framing when inhumi are said to breed on Blue [David Stockhoff, 2011].
  • Robert Borski argued that inhumi breed in sewage and stagnant water on Blue, not only on Green [Robert Borski, 2001].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the theory was never accepted by the list, its main proponents (Aramini, Lovely, Stockhoff, Wynn) kept disagreeing on mechanism, and the threads where it was argued ended without consensus.
