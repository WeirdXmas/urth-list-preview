Seawrack's arm and the pirate woman

Seawrack was made from the pirate woman Horn killed, and Babbie tore off her arm.

14 threads · 24 posts · for 16 / against 11 · first raised 1999-10-05 by Alice Turner
Main voices: David Stockhoff (6), b sharp (3), Sergei SOLOVIEV (3), Alice Turner (2), Robert Borski (2), James Wynn (2)

The debate

The idea that Babbie tore off Seawrack's arm was first raised on 1999-10-05 when Alice Turner asked why Seawrack lacks the double limbs of the Neighbors and whether that shows she is not native [Alice Turner, 1999]. William H. Ansley then asked on 1999-11-14 why Seawrack's lost arm troubles no one in the text, which framed the question [William H. Ansley, 1999]. Bill Carmichael answered on 1999-11-15 that Babbie tore the arm off during a struggle aboard the boat while Horn slept [Bill Carmichael, 1999], and Alice Turner backed the view that the arm is really missing [Alice Turner, 1999]. Alex Groce offered a rival 1999 reading in which Mother arranged the loss to win Horn's sympathy [Alex David Groce, 1999], and Robert Borski in 2000 and 2001 argued that Scylla or the Mother maimed Seawrack instead [Robert Borski, 2000][Robert Borski, 2001].

The pirate-woman link entered the list in 2009, when James Wynn first proposed that Seawrack is built from the corpse of a pirate woman Horn killed [James Wynn, 2009]. b sharp developed this with a corpse-construction account and linked the black pirate ship to Abaia [b sharp, 2009], and later argued the wounded swimmer Horn shot is Seawrack herself, attacked by Babbie [b sharp, 2009]. Scott Wowra offered textual support by citing dried blood on the stump on page 155 [Scott Wowra, 2009]. Wynn returned to the idea in 2010 [James Wynn, 2010], and thalassocrat revived the Babbie explanation in 2011 [thalassocrat, 2011].

The 2011-2012 discussion was the main turning point and centred on whether the pirate girl Horn shot became Seawrack. Lee Berman argued that Horn's shot severed Seawrack's arm and that the Mother linked the ship and Seawrack [Lee Berman, 2011]. David Stockhoff, Marc Aramini and Mark Millman then developed versions in which the pirate girl herself becomes Seawrack [David Stockhoff, 2012][Marc Aramini, 2012][Mark Millman, 2012], and Stockhoff argued that a gunshot or Babbie's bite could explain the arm [David Stockhoff, 2012]. Sergei Soloviev disputed this reading on textual grounds [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2012][Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2012], and António Pedro Marques argued the narrator's account may be unreliable and that no alien physiology is needed [António Pedro Marques, 2012][António Pedro Marques, 2012]. Stockhoff's later post on the pirate woman's body being inhabited by another being was also weighed against limb-regrowth and gun problems [David Stockhoff, 2012].

For

  • Carmichael argued that blood on the deck, Seawrack's gills and the Mother's ban on raw fish point to Babbie tearing off the arm [Bill Carmichael, 1999].
  • Alice Turner argued that Krait's distrust and the multi-limbed native fauna suggest the arm loss is real [Alice Turner, 1999].
  • Wowra cited dried blood on the stump on page 155 as evidence for Babbie's attack [Scott Wowra, 2009].
  • Wynn linked Seawrack's appearance and golden-green colour to the pirate woman Horn killed [James Wynn, 2009].
  • Berman argued that the freshly healed wound and Horn's shot point to the pirate girl's death [Lee Berman, 2011].

Against

  • Soloviev argued that Babbie's guilt on Seawrack's return only makes sense if he injured her earlier at the sloop [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2012].
  • Soloviev argued that bleeding traces on the sloop and other textual details support the narrator's account, and called the pirate-girl conjecture disrespectful to the text [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2012].
  • Marques argued that the narrator's belief about the mutilation does not settle the matter and the account may be false [António Pedro Marques, 2012].
  • Marques argued that Seawrack's ordinary human healing and lack of other damage mean no alien physiology is needed [António Pedro Marques, 2012].
  • Stockhoff noted that limb regrowth and gun problems remain difficulties for the pirate-girl theory [David Stockhoff, 2012].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the arm and pirate-woman theories were argued across many threads, and none reached consensus.
