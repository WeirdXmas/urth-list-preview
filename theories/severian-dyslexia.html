Severian is spatially dyslexic

Severian confuses left and right and misreads directions, which explains contradictions in his account of places.

5 threads · 6 posts · for 3 / against 2 · first raised 1997-04-17 by Charles Dye
Main voices: Charles Dye (4), C R Culver (1), Tony Ellis (1)

The debate

The theory that Severian is spatially dyslexic was first raised on the list in April 1997 by Charles Dye, who forwarded a suggestion attributed to Reusch that Severian confuses left and right and gets lost despite precise directions [Charles Dye, 1997]. The idea resurfaced in February 1998, when Charles Dye reported Jason Voegele's proposal and cited the battle Severian sees northeast from Typhon's west-facing left eye, along with repeated lost-while-following-directions episodes [Charles Dye, 1998]. Tony Ellis was the main opponent, arguing that Severian's accurate navigation, deductions and self-described perfect memory contradict the dyslexia idea [Tony Ellis, 1998]. CRCulver disputed a related pole-reversal explanation on timescale grounds and noted that the dyslexia explanation had come up before [C R Culver, 1998].

In the same week Charles Dye moved from reporting the idea to defending it as a deliberate pattern, suggesting a dyslexic detective rather than faulty memory, pointing to Idas's motive to mislead, the symmetrical Hypogeum and Triste-le-Roy, and a good leg, bad leg injury pattern in answer to Ellis [Charles Dye, 1998]. Dye returned to the theory in August 1999, asking which leg is wounded at Orithiya and how Typhon's left eye can see a northeast battle, and linking the matter to Dante and Matthew 4 echoes [Charles Dye, 1999]. The debates in the threads Genetics and Spatial Dyslexia and Of lost books and millenia were largely parallel, with Mark Millman and Jim Jordan raising evolution and Lamarckism rather than engaging the dyslexia claim directly.

For

  • Severian confuses left and right and gets lost despite precise directions, as Reusch suggested via Dye [Charles Dye, 1997].
  • Jason Voegele proposed the dyslexia explanation for the left-right and compass confusions in the Typhon sequence, as reported by Dye [Charles Dye, 1998].
  • The northeast battle seen from Typhon's west-facing left eye is cited as a misperception [Charles Dye, 1998][Charles Dye, 1999].
  • Misperceptions of places follow a symmetrical pattern, with Idas's motive to mislead, supporting a dyslexic detective reading [Charles Dye, 1998].
  • A good leg, bad leg pattern in injuries across characters is offered as further support [Charles Dye, 1998].

Against

  • Severian's accurate navigation and deductions contradict the dyslexia idea [Tony Ellis, 1998].
  • Severian's perfect memory, which he mentions himself, is cited against the claim of misreading places [Tony Ellis, 1998].
  • A magnetic pole reversal, offered as an alternative cause, would take at least 100,000 years, so it cannot explain the confusion [C R Culver, 1998].
  • Severian's deduction of Terminus Est's location in the Hypogeum Apotropaic shows accurate spatial reasoning [Tony Ellis, 1998].

Where it stands: Raised once in 1997 and repeatedly revived through 1999 by Charles Dye and others, but still disputed with no consensus reached, as the discussion never resolved the navigation and memory objections.
