Who Doris is

Doris, the girl in Charlie Turner's letter in Peace, is Den Weer's daughter by Sherry Gold (or Sherry herself, Tilly's daughter, a Cinderella figure, or Charlie's invention).

23 threads · 33 posts · for 21 / against 14 · first raised 1997-05-03 by Reusch
Main voices: Michael Andre-Driussi (15), Roy C. Lackey (6), Robert Borski (5), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (3), Stone Ox (3), Marc Aramini (2)

The debate

The idea that Doris, the carnie girl in Charlie Turner's letter in Peace, is Den Weer's daughter first appears on the list in October 2000, when Michael Andre-Driussi proposed that she is Den's daughter with Sherry Gold or Sherry herself, a Cinderella figure whose identity Den's past hints at [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2000]. He made this reply to Bill S. and Roy Lackey, who thought Den invents the Charlie visit, and argued that astral characters seeing invented events would break the novel's rules [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2000]. Andre-Driussi then used age arithmetic from Roy's timeline to rule out both Sherry and a hypothetical daughter of hers [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2000].

The question came back in summer 2002, when Robert Borski argued the daughter theory and proposed Margaret Lorn Price as the woman seeking Doris, citing Charlie's letter, Den's interest in Doris and Lorn family details [Robert Borski, 2002]. Roy Lackey disputed it on timeline grounds [Roy C. Lackey, 2002], Adam Stephanides argued Doris is a fiction Charlie invented [Adam Stephanides, 2002], and Dan'l Danehy-Oakes first called the near-certainty overstated because the D'Or link is weak [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002], then called the argument plausible but lacking a smoking gun [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002]. Borski then argued that Den may be the hidden father behind the 'Mr. Mason' who supposedly fathered Doris and Candy [Robert Borski, 2002], and Lackey replied that Candy and Arline were also daughters of Mason and that Weer never married [Roy C. Lackey, 2002].

A related thread shifted the question to the sepia photographs. Andre-Driussi proposed that the normal-sized sepia man is Tilly, making Doris Tilly's daughter [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2002], and Borski supported this from the pharmacy relatives and the giant's clothing and bed [Robert Borski, 2002]. Lackey objected on chronology and on a passage describing the man as slouched and of average height, while StoneOx17 offered a publicity stand-in posed for scale; that thread ended without resolution. Andre-Driussi later proposed that Doris is Sherry Gold, whose 1974 death is compressed into the letter tale [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2002], and then framed the Doris section as a Cinderella retelling in a Dickens-like letter-story [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2002]. Marc Aramini revived the question in 2013, proposing that Doris may be Sherry's child through time displacement, after working through the ages and dates [Marc Aramini, 2013].

For

  • Robert Borski argued Doris is Den Weer's daughter by Sherry Gold, citing Charlie's letter, Den's interest in Doris and Lorn family details [Robert Borski, 2002].
  • Michael Andre-Driussi proposed Doris as Den's daughter with Sherry Gold, or Sherry herself, as a Cinderella figure whose identity Den's past hints at [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2000].
  • Borski suggested the 'Mr. Mason' who supposedly fathered Doris and Candy is a nom de convenience hiding Den Weer [Robert Borski, 2002].
  • Andre-Driussi and Borski supported Doris as Tilly's daughter, using the sepia-photo evidence of the pharmacy relatives and the giant's clothing and bed [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2002][Robert Borski, 2002].
  • Andre-Driussi later proposed Doris is Sherry Gold herself, with her death in 1974 compressed into the letter tale [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2002].

Against

  • Roy Lackey argued the timeline makes it impossible for Sherry to be Doris's mother, working through the ages of Weer and Sherry and the dating of the Tilly tale [Roy C. Lackey, 2002].
  • Andre-Driussi used age arithmetic on Roy's timeline to rule out both Sherry and her hypothetical daughter as Doris [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2000].
  • Adam Stephanides argued Weer's coldness at Doris's death and his inadequate gifts make the theory incoherent, and that Doris is a fiction Charlie invented [Adam Stephanides, 2002].
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes noted the D'Or connection is too weak, and that Doris means gift in Greek and was an Oceanid, which might suggest different parentage [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002][Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002].
  • Lackey argued that Candy and Arline were also daughters of Mr. Mason and that Weer never married, and that the sepia photos show three people rather than Doris [Roy C. Lackey, 2002][Roy C. Lackey, 2002].

Where it stands: Still disputed and unresolved: the daughter theory was never accepted, Borski's near-certainty drew repeated objections, and the threads ended with the timeline and photo evidence still contested.
