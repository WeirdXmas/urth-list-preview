Severian's journey follows Hercules

Severian's adventures parallel the labours of Hercules.

22 threads · 21 posts · for 13 / against 10 · first raised 1998-06-15 by Michael Andre-Driussi
Main voices: Lee Berman (10), James Wynn (4), mournings glory (2), Marc Aramini (2), David Stockhoff (2), Michael Andre-Driussi (1)

The debate

The earliest Hercules-linked post in this record is by Michael Andre-Driussi in 1998, who read the iron dog in The Fifth Head of Cerberus against Heracles bringing Cerberus up from Hades [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998]. That post concerns the novella rather than Severian's journey. Hercules readings of Wolfe's protagonists then appear in 2003 on Latro, first from mournings glory and then from Marc Aramini [mournings glory, 2003] [Marc Aramini, 2003]. The Severian version of the theory was pressed mainly from December 2010 onward. Lee Berman first argued that Severian parallels Hercules' twelve labors, citing Nessus and Palaemon [Lee Berman, 2010], and then linked Severian's divine-impersonation birth to Hercules and to Jesus and Joseph [Lee Berman, 2010].

The discussion grew in January 2011, when Berman tied Severian's labors and Nessus's disguised parentage to Hercules and drew on The Town That Forgot Fauna [Lee Berman, 2011] [Lee Berman, 2011]. David Stockhoff responded that modeling Severian on Hercules let Wolfe build an episodic narrative, and asked whether anyone had matched the labors one for one beyond the Augean Stables [David Stockhoff, 2011]. Berman continued the case in October 2011, claiming Severian is a half-god hero like Heracles and Theseus [Lee Berman, 2011], and in another post he argued that Hercules fits Severian's life better than the Iliad parallels James Wynn had proposed, though only seven or eight labors match [Lee Berman, 2011]. Berman repeated the Heracles claim in 2014 [Lee Berman, 2014] and defended a Hercules-Jesus prototype against Marc Aramini in October 2014 [Lee Berman, 2014].

Opposition came from several posters. James Wynn rejected Hercules as a Christ-figure parallel, since Mithras and Hercules are also called Christ-figures [James Wynn, 2010]. John Watkins argued that Typhon maps more directly onto Prometheus and that Hercules rescuing Prometheus breaks the Christ analogy [John Watkins, 2010]. Stockhoff held that Typhon resembles Alexander rather than Hercules and rejected Hercules-style semi-divinity [David Stockhoff, 2011]. Andrew Mason argued that Spring Wind fits Mars better than Hercules, in a Frog and Fish exchange where Wynn had also disputed Hercules readings [Andrew Mason, 2011] [James Wynn, 2011]. Berman's Nessus-as-Hercules reading was also argued in the South America thread, where Jerry Friedman doubted the wider Buenos Aires link [Lee Berman, 2011].

The threads that carried the theory ended without agreement. The City On Green and Father Inire threads fragmented, and the Ouen and Catherine thread ended in disagreement after Berman invoked myth for Severian's paternity [Lee Berman, 2010]. Most of the debate stayed between Berman and a few critics rather than becoming a settled reading.

For

  • Berman argued Severian's twelve labors parallel Hercules' labors, citing Nessus and Palaemon [Lee Berman, 2010].
  • Berman drew parallels between Nessus's twin and disguised parentage, the Town That Forgot Fauna links, and Hercules' labors [Lee Berman, 2011] [Lee Berman, 2011].
  • Berman argued Severian's divine-impersonation birth resembles Hercules and the Jesus and Joseph pattern [Lee Berman, 2010].
  • Stockhoff argued that modeling Severian on Hercules gave Wolfe an episodic narrative with a higher purpose [David Stockhoff, 2011].
  • Berman argued Severian is a half-god hero like Heracles and Theseus, and that Hercules fits Severian's life better than the Iliad parallels [Lee Berman, 2011] [Lee Berman, 2011].

Against

  • Wynn argued that the Christ-figure label is overstated, since Mithras and Hercules are also called Christ-figures [James Wynn, 2010].
  • Watkins argued that Hercules rescuing Prometheus breaks the Christ analogy and that Typhon maps onto Prometheus [John Watkins, 2010].
  • Stockhoff argued Typhon resembles Alexander and rejected Hercules-style semi-divinity [David Stockhoff, 2011].
  • Mason argued that Spring Wind fits Mars better than Hercules [Andrew Mason, 2011].
  • Mason called the Severian-Ouen link a mistake rather than a deliberate trail, undermining the mythic paternity reading that Berman built on Hercules [Lee Berman, 2010].

Where it stands: Still disputed: Lee Berman championed the Severian-Hercules parallel through 2014, but critics such as Wynn, Watkins and Stockhoff rejected or qualified it, and the threads ended without consensus.
