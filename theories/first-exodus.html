An earlier exodus

An earlier wave of humans reached Blue and Green before the Whorl's colonists.

3 threads · 6 posts · for 1 / against 5 · first raised 2011-02-09 by Gerry Quinn
Main voices: Gerry Quinn (3), António Pedro Marques (2), David Stockhoff (1)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by Ashley Crill, whose First Exodus idea (Inhumi reaching the Whorl in an unremembered exodus) is the source of the card's wording; the earliest post on the list that discusses it is Gerry Quinn's dispute of February 2011 [Gerry Quinn, 2011]. Quinn objected that nobody in the books recalls such an exodus or the dangers of Green, and rejected the Silk-as-clone idea [Gerry Quinn, 2011]. Discussion in February 2011 turned to the revised form of the theory: Quinn argued that Pas's plan could not have allowed a long scouting period because the Whorl was already disintegrating, so any colony-leader embryos would have been implanted about a decade before arrival [Gerry Quinn, 2011]. António Pedro Marques pressed textual objections, questioning how Tussah could know of Viron's corruption and whether personality follows from parentage, and rejected multiple Silk-like figures because Horn would have mentioned them [António Pedro Marques, 2011].

The February 2011 exchanges also took up Typhon's motives and identity questions. Marques clarified that his argument concerned Typhon's motives rather than Pas's intentions, and restated that there was no reason Typhon would want Pas to possess a hypothetical heir of Typhon's line [António Pedro Marques, 2011]. Quinn defended a revised version identifying the Outsider with Baldanders and dismissing the Dionysus references as minor, and rejected the claim that Silk's adoptive mother is Hyacinth [Gerry Quinn, 2011]. The thread summaries record that Marc Aramini argued Silk is Typhon's son and asked why Typhon would send his own son's embryo, with no clear resolution [Gerry Quinn, 2011].

In May 2012 the theory's thread moved away from parentage. David Stockhoff proposed, from the hollowed-asteroid terrarium design, that Typhon's earlier ships may have used FTL by pure acceleration, a point linked to the earlier exodus question only indirectly [David Stockhoff, 2012]. Daniel Petersen and Jeff Wilson then drifted the discussion toward Urth as a decayed backwater of a collapsed empire, leaving the hollow-asteroid question unresolved [David Stockhoff, 2012].

For

  • Ashley Crill's revised First Exodus theory has Tussah sending a scouting party that retrieved a special embryo, with Silk linked to Typhon and Kypris, as recorded in the thread summary of 'First Exodus theory revised'.
  • Marc Aramini argued that Silk is Typhon's son, asking why Typhon would send his own son's embryo, as recorded in the thread summary of 'First Exodus'.
  • Quinn's scouting-exodus variant was offered as a way to reconcile the plan with the timing of the Whorl's decline, according to the thread summary of 'First Exodus' [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • Stuart Hamm echoed the theory in the 'First Exodus theory revised' thread, per the thread summary.

Against

  • Gerry Quinn argued that nobody in the books remembers such an exodus or the dangers of Green [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • Quinn argued that the Whorl was already disintegrating, so a long scouting period could not have been part of Pas's plan [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • António Pedro Marques questioned how Tussah could know Viron's Ayuntamiento was corrupt and whether personality follows from biological parentage [António Pedro Marques, 2011].
  • Marques rejected multiple Silk-like figures because Horn would have mentioned them [António Pedro Marques, 2011].
  • Lee Berman and Gerry Quinn objected that the cities would have remembered such an exodus, as recorded in the thread summary of 'First Exodus'.

Where it stands: Still disputed and unresolved: the thread ended in impasse over Silk's parentage and the theory was not widely accepted, with the 2011 threads closing without agreement and the 2012 post on FTL by acceleration leaving the earlier exodus question aside.
