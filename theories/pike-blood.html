Pike is Blood's father

Patera Pike is the father of Blood, the crime lord.

14 threads · 16 posts · for 11 / against 2 · first raised 1997-06-14 by Michael Andre-Driussi
Main voices: Michael Andre-Driussi (3), James Wynn (3), Patrick O'Leary (1), Alice Turner (1), Dave Lebling (1), Peter Westlake (1)

The debate

The theory that Patera Pike is Blood's father was first raised on the list by Patrick O'Leary on 1997-06-14, who argued that Pike may be Blood's father, hinted at in the text [Patrick O'Leary, 1997]. The same day Michael Andre-Driussi's published answers to reader questions were reported as naming Pike as Blood's father, and Alice Turner praised that answer [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997][Alice Turner, 1997]. Dave Lebling praised the revelation the next day [Dave Lebling, 1997]. Andre-Driussi then developed the idea himself, proposing that Pike's ghost, arriving after Mucor, may be a sinful lover of Maytera Rose and possibly Blood's father [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997][Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997]. Peter Westlake and mary whalen added textual hints in 1998, the latter citing the shared initial letters of Pike, Rose and Blood as very slight support [Peter Westlake, 1998][mary whalen, 1998].

The debate was revived on the Urth list in 2002, when Dan'l Danehy-Oakes relayed a claim that Wolfe endorsed the theory in the 'Train from Wolfeland' interview [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002]. In 2010 James Wynn and Andrew Mason argued the point again, with Mason citing Wolfe's confirmation [James Wynn, 2010]. Wynn also linked Pike, Blood and Rose of Sharon to the spear that pierced Christ's side [James Wynn, 2010]. In 2011 the discussion shifted toward Silk as a clone or relation of Pike, with Wynn proposing that Silk is a clone of Typhon [James Wynn, 2011], David Stockhoff reading Silk's use of 'son' as a pointer to Blood being Silk's son [David Stockhoff, 2011], and Andrew Mason arguing that Wolfe may think he has disclosed the Pike paternity [Andrew Mason, 2011].

Larry Miller disputed the family links around Mucor, while Gerry Quinn supported Miller's reading and suggested Pike's ghost is a clue that Blood is Pike's son [Larry Miller, 2011][Gerry Quinn, 2011]. The threads summarised for this theory ended without agreement; the Urth Digest thread and the Pike's ghost thread turned to acrimony and jokes, and the Silk's origin thread stayed divided [James Wynn, 2010][Gerry Quinn, 2011].

For

  • Patrick O'Leary argued in 1997 that Pike may be Blood's father, hinted at in the text, and linked this to Silk and Severian as surrogate God figures [Patrick O'Leary, 1997].
  • Michael Andre-Driussi proposed that Pike's ghost, arriving after Mucor, is a sinful lover of Maytera Rose and possibly Blood's father, drawing on the vampire temptress pattern and Silk's enlightenment after sex [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997].
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes relayed Roy C's claim that Wolfe agreed Pike is Blood's father, quoting the Outsider's fondness for repentant sinners from the 'Train from Wolfeland' answers [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002].
  • Andrew Mason argued that Wolfe confirmed Patera Pike as Blood's father, a point James Wynn accepted while offering analogies to support it [James Wynn, 2010].
  • Gerry Quinn suggested Pike's ghost is a clue that Blood is Pike's son, citing Marble calling Mucor her granddaughter and the equal weight Long Sun gives adoptive kinship [Gerry Quinn, 2011].

Against

  • Larry Miller argued Mucor is not Blood's daughter and so has no familial relation to Pike, citing Silk's theorising near the end of Lake chapter 9 [Larry Miller, 2011].
  • David Stockhoff stated that the 'son' wording is only a pointer and not enough to build a case on, responding to Jerry Friedman's reading of Silk's 'sir' [David Stockhoff, 2011].
  • Lee Berman and Jerry Friedman questioned how obvious the Christian symbolism behind the Pike, Blood and Rose names is [James Wynn, 2010].
  • James Wynn's clone-of-Typhon proposal and his dream-travel reading pushed the paternity question elsewhere, and Roy Lackey and Gerry Quinn argued the Silkhorn timeline rules dream-travel out [James Wynn, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list reports Wolfe's endorsement as a point posters cite, but the threads on Pike's paternity ended without agreement and the case rests largely on the 1997 answers and the 2002 relay.
