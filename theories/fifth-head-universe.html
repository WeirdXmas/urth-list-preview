The Fifth Head of Cerberus shares a universe with the Sun books

The Fifth Head of Cerberus is set in the same universe as the Sun books (or the reverse is argued).

20 threads · 21 posts · for 8 / against 9 · first raised 1996-12-03 by James Jordan
Main voices: Adam Thornton (3), b sharp (3), James Jordan (2), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (2), Roy C. Lackey (2), Patrick O'Leary (1)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by James Jordan in December 1996, who proposed that the Fifth Head of Cerberus novellas are set outside the Urth universe and asked for concrete evidence linking Ste. Anne and Ste. Claire with Green and Blue [James Jordan, 1996]. Patrick O'Leary in 1997 asked whether links between Blue and Green and Sainte-Anne and Sainte-Croix had been dropped [Patrick O'Leary, 1997], and Alastair Reynolds questioned whether Whorl and Fifth Head share one universe [Alastair Reynolds, 1997]. The question came back in 2002, when Andy Robertson asked whether the book shares a universe with the New Sun books, quoting Wolfe's answer 'I don't know' from a Nick Gevers interview [Andy Robertson, 2002]. Nicholas Gevers argued that the paired double-world systems and Robert Borski's correspondences such as starcrossers support placing the book in the Briah Cycle [Nicholas Gevers, 2002], while Dan'l Danehy-Oakes disputed that it is set in the same universe and called it a poor entry point [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002].

Through 2002 to 2006 the debate returned repeatedly. James Jordan argued that the Long Sun was originally titled 'Starcrosser's Landfall' and linked it to the Fifth Head [James Jordan, 2002]. Roy C. Lackey disputed placing the book in Urth's past, citing theological problems and Earth references such as Ireland and New York [Roy C. Lackey, 2002][Roy C. Lackey, 2006]. Joe Cilluffo proposed the book was a dry run for the Urth cycle [Joe Cilluffo, 2003]. b sharp was the main proponent in 2006, arguing from scenes in the Sun books and the mechanical men of Tzadkiel that the books share one universe [b sharp, 2006][b sharp, 2006], and Dan'l Danehy-Oakes countered that Wolfe's published statements that the book is not set in the Solar past carry weight [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2006]. Jeffrey Lefstin argued that the shared shapeshifting and double worlds are a reused conceit rather than a hidden puzzle [Jeffrey Lefstin, 2006].

In 2011 Gerry Quinn argued the two settings are hard to place in one universe given the time gap [Gerry Quinn, 2011], and in the later threads Chris and James Wynn reported Wolfe's denial that the Blue and Green worlds equal the Fifth Head system [Adrian Robert, 2005]. The Blushas thread saw Jerry Friedman question whether Wolfe intends the analogies at all, citing his non-answer in an interview [Jerry Friedman, 2002].

For

  • Blue and Green and the Fifth Head's Ste. Anne and Ste. Croix share parallels such as binary planets, shape-shifting natives and the term starcrosser, which Adrian Robert listed [Adrian Robert, 2005].
  • Gevers argued that paired double-world systems across the Urth and Whorl series, and Borski's noted correspondences, justify placing the book in the Briah Cycle [Nicholas Gevers, 2002].
  • b sharp argued that three scenes in the Sun books and the mechanical men of Tzadkiel show the books share one universe [b sharp, 2006][b sharp, 2006].
  • Andy Robertson and Adam Thornton asked whether Blue is Ste. Croix and Green is Ste. Anne, weighing hypotheses about the inhumu and the ruins [Andy Robertson, 2002][Adam Thornton, 2006].
  • James Jordan argued the Long Sun was originally titled 'Starcrosser's Landfall', linking it to the Fifth Head [James Jordan, 2002].

Against

  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued the Fifth Head seems set in a different universe and that Wolfe's published statements carry weight [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002][Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2006].
  • Roy C. Lackey argued the book cannot be part of Urth's past, citing theology, colonial names and Severian's silence [Roy C. Lackey, 2002][Roy C. Lackey, 2006].
  • William H. Ansley denied the two sets of books share one universe while noting thematic parallels [William H. Ansley, 2004].
  • Jeffrey Lefstin argued the parallels are a reused conceit, citing the ship in 'Silhouette' and the duel in 'Tracking Song' [Jeffrey Lefstin, 2006].
  • Gerry Quinn argued the events are separated by an enormous span of time and the planets share little beyond being habitable twins [Gerry Quinn, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never settled the question, and Wolfe's 'I don't know' and denial of literal identity were cited by both sides without resolution.
