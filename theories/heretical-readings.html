Gnostic and heretical readings

Wolfe's books contain (or invite) Gnostic or heretical theology, deliberately or not.

154 threads · 200 posts · for 98 / against 111 · first raised 1997-02-07 by Michael Andre-Driussi
Main voices: Lee Berman (34), James Wynn (31), David Stockhoff (21), Alice Turner (18), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (11), James Jordan (9)

The debate

The earliest post in this record is Michael Andre-Driussi's 1997 retelling of Plato's Cave, which reads the fire in The Book of the Long Sun as a Gnostic pretender to sunlight without citing Long Sun text [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997]. In the same year James Jordan asked whether the Gnostic and Byzantine allusions in The Book of the New Sun were decoration or something the New Sun must act against [James Jordan, 1997], and then argued that the book is a Christian creational universe with Severian as a Christian figure rather than a Christ figure [James Jordan, 1997]. Jordan kept this line in later 1997 posts, citing Wolfe's own remarks about Yesod and Beriakh meaning only heaven and earth [James Jordan, 1997].

From 1998 to 2003 the theory mostly took the form of heresy-naming. Alice Turner posted the Oxford entry on Severian, pointing to the Severians sect as a source for the name [Alice Turner, 1998], and later argued it signalled a heretical sect [Alice Turner, 1998]. Alex David Groce answered that the New Sun is philosophically anti-Manichean [Alex David Groce, 1998]. In 2000 the Pelagian reading arrived: Turner tied Vodalus's pass-phrase to Pelagius [Alice Turner, 2000], while Groce, Carlton Greene and Adam Stephanides took opposing views on whether Severian or Vodalus embodies the heresy [Alex David Groce, 2000] [Greene, Carlton, 2000] [Adam Stephanides, 2000]. Turner also read Pullman's trilogy as a syncretic Gnostic and Manichaean religion [Alice Turner, 2001], which Stephanides disputed [Adam Stephanides, 2001].

The Long Sun debates of 2002 were the main turning point. Kevin J. Maroney argued that Pas as demiurge makes the series an attack on Gnosticism [Kevin J. Maroney, 2002], while Turner called Wolfe orthodox rather than a real Gnostic [Alice Turner, 2002]. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued the books literalize Gnosticism [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002], and James Jordan replied that the Long Sun shows a gnostic stage on the way to Christianity [James Jordan, 2002]. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes later argued that Wolfe embeds a Gnostic cosmology inside a Christian universe [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2004]. From 2005 on, posters applied the theory to other books: thalassocrat called Wolfe's cosmology Arian and Adoptionist [thalassocrat, 2005], James Wynn read Severian and Thecla as a Gnostic syzygy [James Wynn, 2010], and David Stockhoff and Lee Berman linked Typhon, the Outsider and Pas to Gnostic demiurge figures [David Stockhoff, 2011] [Lee Berman, 2012]. Stockhoff's objection that Gnosticism is a loose family of motifs rather than a uniform system [David Stockhoff, 2009] came up in several later threads.

Later posts mostly repeated the earlier exchange. In 2013 Marc Aramini proposed that Gnostic play is central to the Latro books [Marc Aramini, 2013], and in 2014 he asserted that Catholic and heretical ideologies are valid interpretive keys [Marc Aramini, 2014]. Threads such as Wolfe Heretic? and Gnosticism in BotNS ended in impasse, with much of the talk moving to the history of religion rather than close reading of the books.

For

  • The Book of the Long Sun's Outsider as true God and Pas as Demiurge literalize Gnosticism, as Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued in 2002 [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002].
  • Severian's name may derive from the Severians sect, which Alice Turner argued from the OED entry [Alice Turner, 1998].
  • Severian and Thecla form a Gnostic syzygy in James Wynn's reading [James Wynn, 2010].
  • The Wizard Knight's world reads as Gnostic in structure, with matter as evil, according to Daniel Petersen [Daniel Petersen, 2012].
  • David Stockhoff argued that Gnostic imagery in BotNS sets failed approaches to the Increate against the proper one [David Stockhoff, 2009].

Against

  • James Jordan argued that The Book of the New Sun is a Christian creational universe and that Severian is a Christian figure, not a gnostic one [James Jordan, 1997].
  • Jordan relayed Wolfe's statement that Yesod and Beriakh mean only heaven and earth, supporting a Catholic reading [James Jordan, 1997].
  • Alex David Groce argued that the New Sun is philosophically anti-Manichean, since the Pancreator fills the God and Creator roles [Alex David Groce, 1998].
  • Alice Turner cited Wolfe's interview remarks in the Crowley chapbook, where he rejects Gnostic intent while calling the metaphor useful [Alice Turner, 2003].
  • Alice Turner held that Wolfe is orthodox rather than a real Gnostic, noting the missing Sophia figure [Alice Turner, 2002].

Where it stands: Still disputed: posters from 1997 to 2014 kept proposing and rejecting Gnostic and heretical readings, the Long Sun debate of 2002 and the later threads ended without consensus, and the most recent posts left the matter open.
