Auk's origin

Auk was an embryo carried by Mint, or is Silk's twin in a mythic pattern (dividing the deeds of Hephaestus).

11 threads · 13 posts · for 10 / against 3 · first raised 1997-02-07 by Michael Andre-Driussi
Main voices: James Wynn (5), Andrew Mason (4), Michael Andre-Driussi (2), Brownes3 (2), David Stockhoff (1)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list on 1997-04-11 and 1997-02-07 only indirectly: Michael Andre-Driussi's reply [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997] disputed arguments about Mint and Auk without setting out an embryo idea. The embryo proposal itself first appears in Brownes3's 1998 post, which proposed that Auk is an embryo like Chenille and Silk and grounded this in his staying power, his engineering theophany and a remembered mother named Lilly [Brownes3, 1998]. The idea lay dormant for over a decade until Andrew Mason revived it in December 2010, proposing Auk as an embryo whose surrogate was Mint, citing Crane's petri-dish remark and Tartaros's handling of Hyacinth [Andrew Mason, 2010].

In 2011 Mason developed the embryo reading further. He argued that Auk calls Mint 'Mother', that Marble's unfinished remark bears on Auk's parentage, and that Mint's virginity despite witnessing theophanies fits a surrogacy, while admitting the sponsor and motive stayed unknown [Andrew Mason, 2011]. He also argued that the sealed numbered sections underground hold sleepers and embryos, and that Silk realised he was once an embryo, extending the idea to Villus [Andrew Mason, 2011]. A separate line of argument, led by James Wynn, moved from embryo to mythic pairing: Silk and Auk as twins dividing Hephaestus's deeds, with mirrored shriving and solving of Blood's House as evidence [James Wynn, 2010] [James Wynn, 2010]. Wynn later developed this with Hammerstone and Sand as twins [James Wynn, 2011] and with a Zeus-bound-to-a-chair mapping [James Wynn, 2011].

Discussion stayed mostly between a few posters rather than broadening. Mason also argued Auk is Silk's dark twin, correcting the sequence of Auk's shriving of Silk and tying the twinning to hair colour [Andrew Mason, 2010]. Wynn's later posts on Silk's leg injury treated the mythic pattern as continuing to hold [James Wynn, 2011]. The 2011 threads on mysteries in Long Sun, which included Andrew Mason's Mint-surrogate proposal, ended without consensus, with several questions left as loose ends. Mason and Wynn both appear in the thread that mixes family-relation theories and meta-arguments about critics' motives, which remained unresolved.

For

  • Brownes3 proposed that Auk is an embryo like Chenille and Silk, citing his staying power when injured and a remembered mother named Lilly [Brownes3, 1998]
  • Andrew Mason argued Auk is an embryo with Mint as surrogate, citing Crane's petri-dish remark and the claim that Tartaros's hand-holding is not possession [Andrew Mason, 2010]
  • Mason grounded the surrogacy in Auk calling Mint 'Mother', Marble's unfinished remark about Auk's parentage and Mint's virginity despite theophanies [Andrew Mason, 2011]
  • Mason extended the embryo idea to Villus, using the sealed numbered sections, the Rigoglio scene, the glossary and Echidna's confusion of Villus with Auk [Andrew Mason, 2011]
  • James Wynn argued that Silk and Auk are twins who divide Hephaestus's deeds, citing their mirrored roles in shriving and in the solving of Blood's House [James Wynn, 2010] [James Wynn, 2010]

Against

  • Michael Andre-Driussi disputed the Mint and Auk arguments, rebutting them with cited passages and chapters [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997]
  • Andrew Mason's dark-twin reading corrected the sequence of Wynn's account of shriving and tied the twinning to hair colour, a variant rather than a rejection [Andrew Mason, 2010]
  • David Stockhoff disputed a related reading of Agia's weapons, arguing that a bequest implies death and so a living mother Cyriaca is unlikely [David Stockhoff, 2011]

Where it stands: Still disputed and unresolved: the embryo reading rests mainly on Brownes3 and Andrew Mason's posts, the mythic-twin variant was pursued by James Wynn with little uptake, and the threads ended in impasse without consensus.
