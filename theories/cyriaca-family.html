Cyriaca is Severian's aunt (or mother)

Cyriaca, the armigette of the Claw's story, is related to Severian (as aunt, or in some versions mother) or to Agia and Agilus.

23 threads · 34 posts · for 27 / against 8 · first raised 1998-05-03 by C R Culver
Main voices: Lee Berman (17), b sharp (5), David Stockhoff (5), Alice Turner (2), C R Culver (1), Mitchell A. Bailey (1)

The debate

The earliest kinship claim on the list is by Michael Andre-Driussi in 1999, who proposed that Cyriaca is Severian's mother, arguing she wants him as a dreamchild [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1999]. Earlier, in 1998, C R Culver only raised the idea that the first Severian may have saved Cyriaca from the archon, without any kinship claim [C R Culver, 1998]. In the same 1999 exchange, Mitchell A. Bailey proposed a non-literal maternal link [Mitchell A. Bailey, 1999], while Alice Turner disputed the related Catherine identification on etymological and textual grounds [Alice Turner, 1999].

The theory was revived in 2006 by b sharp, who proposed that Cyriaca is the aunt or mother of Agia and Agilus and related to Catherine [b sharp, 2006][b sharp, 2008]. Roy C. Lackey disputed that Cyriaca could be a twin's sister, citing her postulancy, age and the bequest of the rag shop [Roy C. Lackey, 2006]. Josh Geller and Tim O'Donnell questioned the textual support, and O'Donnell admitted the evidence was circumstantial and unconfirmed by Wolfe [Josh Geller, 2008][O'Donnell, Tim (BOSI), 2008]. In 2010 Lee Berman became the main proponent, arguing Cyriaca is Severian's aunt or relative through resemblance, hair colour and Ultan's family ties [Lee Berman, 2010][Lee Berman, 2010][Lee Berman, 2010], with David Stockhoff supporting Ultan as her uncle [David Stockhoff, 2010].

In 2011 Berman argued Cyriaca is Agia's mother who left the twins the shop [Lee Berman, 2011], and proposed a series of identities for her uncle, including Father Inire and Appian [Lee Berman, 2011][David Stockhoff, 2011]. David Stockhoff disputed these points, arguing that a bequest implies death and that the uncle links to Inire only by puzzling inference [David Stockhoff, 2011][David Stockhoff, 2011][David Stockhoff, 2011]. The threads on these claims ended in impasse, with the evidence standard the main point of disagreement.

For

  • Andre-Driussi proposed Cyriaca as Severian's mother, citing her wish to keep him as a dreamchild [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1999].
  • b sharp grounded the aunt claim in matching ages, heights, hair, face traits and Pelerine ties, while conceding no explicit statement exists [b sharp, 2008].
  • Lee Berman cited Cyriaca's resemblance to Catherine, her age and hair as grounds for a family tie [Lee Berman, 2010].
  • Berman argued that Ultan's family ties and his resemblance to the woman in the coffin support Cyriaca being Severian's aunt [Lee Berman, 2010].
  • Bailey argued that Cyriaca's extended musings about being Severian's mother would otherwise serve no purpose, implying a figurative link [Mitchell A. Bailey, 1999].

Against

  • Turner argued from etymology that Cyriaca and Catherine are not the same person and that Cyriaca's known story makes her an unlikely mother [Alice Turner, 1999].
  • Roy C. Lackey argued that Cyriaca's Pelerine postulancy, age when leaving the order and the bequest of the rag shop make her unlikely to be a twin's sister [Roy C. Lackey, 2006].
  • Josh Geller asked directly for textual evidence of the kinship and sexual relationships [Josh Geller, 2008].
  • O'Donnell called the evidence circumstantial and unconfirmed by Wolfe, and was skeptical that Wolfe intended any kinship [O'Donnell, Tim (BOSI), 2008].
  • David Stockhoff argued that a bequest implies death, so Cyriaca as a living mother of Agia is doubtful, and questioned whether the Inire links explain more than loose threads [David Stockhoff, 2011][David Stockhoff, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the kinship claims were repeatedly proposed by Lee Berman and b sharp but never accepted, and most threads ended in impasse without textual confirmation.
