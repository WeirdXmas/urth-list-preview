Wolfe layers myths for depth

Wolfe deliberately layers older myths and tales into his stories to give them depth of time.

49 threads · 48 posts · for 27 / against 17 · first raised 2000-04-14 by Jack and Melissa Holcomb
Main voices: James Wynn (10), David Stockhoff (10), Lee Berman (4), b sharp (3), Andy Robertson (2), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (2)

The debate

The earliest post on the list that discusses deliberate myth layering is John Bishop's 2001 proposal that Wolfe layers easy and hard mysteries so that readers who spot simple clues are led toward a larger unsolved Mystery [John Bishop, 2001]. Earlier posts touched on individual borrowings without making the general claim: Alice Turner tied the limp motif across classical myth and Long Sun [Alice Turner, 2000], and Andrew Bollen argued for Robert Graves as a source [Andrew Bollen, 2002]. The card wording 'layers older tales for depth of time' is first matched by Sergei SOLOVIEV in 2011, who argued that the Book of the New Sun reworks older tales in layers and that searching for one source misses the method [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011].

Discussion picked up in 2009, when David Stockhoff argued that Wolfe rewrites and mixes myths, so that Pas resembles Zeus and the Neighbors resemble Irish Little People [David Stockhoff, 2009], and that Wolfe's myth-saturated technique recombines motifs like language [David Stockhoff, 2009]. The main turning point came in 2011. Soloviev cited The Tale of the Student and His Son and Fish and Frog against Gerry Quinn's demand for one clear source [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011], and James Wynn argued that Pas combines Greek, Pan and Zeus-and-Thetis material, with Wolfe deliberately layering many myths [James Wynn, 2011]. Lee Berman then argued that the allusions shift from Greco-Roman to Egyptian myth, backed by the shared names Typhon and Sphinx [Lee Berman, 2011].

Opposition came mainly from Quinn, who insisted on literal one-to-one correspondences, and from Marc Aramini, who doubted Wolfe could intend so many allusions while keeping control of the story [James Wynn, 2011]. Wynn later argued that the names Juno and Mars were chosen for specific objects rather than inherited corruptions [James Wynn, 2011]. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes suggested a syncretic mixing of traditions instead [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2011], and Dan'l and Wynn later disputed how heavily The Wizard draws on Dionysus, with Dan'l favouring a middle position [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2012][James Wynn, 2012].

For

  • Bishop argued that Wolfe layers easy and hard mysteries, with simple clues such as the faked Necronomicon rewarding readers [John Bishop, 2001].
  • Stockhoff argued that Wolfe rewrites and mixes myths, so Pas resembles Zeus and other figures echo Christ or angels [David Stockhoff, 2009].
  • Soloviev argued that the stories rework older tales in layers, citing Student and his Son keeping the Athens and Minotaur story and Fish and Frog combining Romulus and Remus with Jungle Book dialogue [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011].
  • Wynn argued that Pas's name, his link to Pan's death and a Zeus-bound-and-rescued parallel show deliberate layering of many myths [James Wynn, 2011].
  • Berman argued that Wolfe deliberately shifts mythic allusions from Greco-Roman myth to Egyptian myth, supported by the shared names Typhon and Sphinx [Lee Berman, 2011].

Against

  • Quinn insisted on one clear source for the Romulus and Mowgli material, which Soloviev answered by arguing for layering [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011].
  • Wynn argued that Juno, Mars and the Frog tale's names were deliberate authorial choices rather than corruptions, and that Romulus parallels were confirmation bias [James Wynn, 2011][James Wynn, 2011].
  • Aramini doubted that Wolfe could intend so many allusions while keeping control of his story [James Wynn, 2011].
  • Holcomb argued that the flood parallels in Pratchett's library scene come from common Near Eastern flood myths rather than Wolfe [Jack and Melissa Holcomb, 2000].
  • Wynn argued that Wolfe's remark that the gods are real but unworthy of worship does not by itself make his views gnostic [James Wynn, 2012].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the layering claim gained support from Soloviev, Wynn and Berman, but the threads on Frog and Fish, The Wizard and the Romulus material ended without agreement, and Stockhoff endorsed Wynn's myth-as-eternal-pattern view as the thread closed [David Stockhoff, 2011].
