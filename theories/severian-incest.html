Severian is the child of incest

Severian's family carries a pattern of incest (his affair with Dorcas, his own birth), reflecting a family curse or design.

30 threads · 34 posts · for 19 / against 12 · first raised 1998-04-08 by Robert Borski
Main voices: Lee Berman (11), David Stockhoff (6), b sharp (3), Gerry Quinn (3), Robert Borski (2), Marc Aramini (2)

The debate

The idea that Severian himself is the product of incest was first raised on the list by Jonathan Laidlow in 1999, who asked where the text supports the claim that Dorcas's unborn child is Severian's mother Katherine and would make Severian the product of incest with Dorcas and Ouen [Jonathan Laidlow, 1999]. Marc Aramini took up the Dorcas-as-mother reading in 2002, arguing her distorted maternal love fits Wolfe's intentions [Marc Aramini, 2002]. In 2006 b sharp moved the incest into the family history, first calling Severian's unwitting incest with Dorcas a matter of evil [b sharp, 2006] and then proposing that Dorcas is his grandmother and that the family incest is a curse like Oedipus's [b sharp, 2006].

Roy C. Lackey disputed b sharp's reading, arguing that a Severian who fathers himself through incest would be no righteous Noah [Roy C. Lackey, 2006]. Thalassocrat supported a lineage reading in 2008, tying Severian's silence about Catherine to incest and to theft and crime running through the family [thalassocrat, 2008]. From 2010 to 2011 Lee Berman made the family curse the centre of his case: Juturna as a missing sister whose ardor signals a curse the New Sun breaks [Lee Berman, 2010], Severian's family under an incest curse with hidden hints in the sexual details [Lee Berman, 2010], and a chaste marriage to Valeria possibly breaking the curse [Lee Berman, 2011].

Opposition grew in the same period. Gerry Quinn rejected a curse linking Severian's relatives, saying the women reflect a social cross-section of the Commonwealth [Gerry Quinn, 2011]. Marc Aramini argued that one case of incest does not imply universal incest, pointing to the varied social spread of the women and Severian's black hair [Marc Aramini, 2011]. Sergei Soloviev criticised readers who treat hidden incest as the story's secret [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011]. Berman later pressed the Dorcas-grandmother reading [Lee Berman, 2011] and Severian-as-god reading tied to incest with Thecla [Lee Berman, 2014], the last record post on the theory in 2014.

For

  • Jonathan Laidlow asked whether Dorcas's unborn child is Katherine, which would make Severian the product of incest with Dorcas and Ouen [Jonathan Laidlow, 1999].
  • Marc Aramini argued that Dorcas must be Severian's mother, since her love is distorted maternal love [Marc Aramini, 2002].
  • b sharp read Dorcas as Severian's grandmother and the family incest as a curse like Oedipus's [b sharp, 2006].
  • thalassocrat argued that Severian's incestuous lineage explains his silence about Catherine and that theft runs through the family [thalassocrat, 2008].
  • Lee Berman proposed that Juturna is Severian's missing sister and that the mutual ardor signals a family incest curse [Lee Berman, 2010].

Against

  • Roy C. Lackey argued that Severian as a self-fathering New Sun through incest makes him no righteous Noah [Roy C. Lackey, 2006].
  • Gerry Quinn rejected an incest curse, arguing the women Severian is drawn to reflect a social cross-section of the Commonwealth [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • Marc Aramini argued that one case of incest does not imply universal incest and cited Severian's black hair against the golden-glow marker [Marc Aramini, 2011].
  • Sergei Soloviev disputed readers who treat hidden incest and secret pet theories as the point of Wolfe's books [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011].
  • Gerry Quinn argued that extraordinary hypotheses need extraordinary evidence and that perversity need not be everywhere in the text [Gerry Quinn, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed and never accepted: the theory had sustained advocacy from a few posters, especially Lee Berman, but the discussion ended in impasse with most posters rejecting a family-wide curse.
