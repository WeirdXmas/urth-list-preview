Briah and Yesod are successive universes

The universe of the books (Briah) is one in a cycle of universes; Yesod is the next one (or a higher one), whose time runs differently, and our own universe may be an earlier Briah.

80 threads · 165 posts · for 71 / against 98 · first raised 1997-04-04 by Michael Andre-Driussi
Main voices: Lee Berman (38), David Stockhoff (17), Michael Andre-Driussi (15), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (15), António Pedro Marques (15), Tony Ellis (11)

The debate

The theory that Briah and Yesod are successive universes was raised early on the list by Michael Andre-Driussi, who in 1997 argued that Yesod-time runs opposite to Briah-time [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997], and who by 1998 proposed Yesod and Abaddon as successive cosmic cycles of one universe [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998]. Through 1998 the list split over a three-tiered cosmology, with Andre-Driussi placing Briah between Yesod and a subspace while Tony Ellis argued Yesod is both higher and lower than Briah [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998] [Tony Ellis, 1998]. James Jordan disputed the sequence in 2000 by reading Briah as earth and Yesod as heaven [James Jordan, 2000], and in 2002 he quoted a Wolfe interview calling Briah a long past universe we repeat [James Jordan, 2002].

The 2006 exchanges between b sharp, Dan'l Danehy-Oakes and Tony Ellis pitted a future-of-Briah reading against a separate-universe reading [b sharp, 2006] [Tony Ellis, 2006]. The 2010 August thread, the longest cluster on the list, saw Lee Berman argue Yesod is the next universe after Briah [Lee Berman, 2010], while Tony Ellis, David Stockhoff, Jeff Wilson and Dan'l Danehy-Oakes offered rival readings such as hyperspace enclosing successive Briahs, a cyclic pocket continuum, Yesod as our universe, and Yesod existing before and after each Briah [Tony Ellis, 2010] [Jeff Wilson, 2010] [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2010] [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2010]. Several posters, including Berman, shifted their own views during the thread [Lee Berman, 2010].

Later posts continued the disagreement. Cliff Judge argued Yesod is a future cycle of our universe [Cliff Judge, 2010], while David Stockhoff argued Briah is cyclical and Yesod is not [David Stockhoff, 2008]. James Wynn argued the Hierogrammates reach simultaneous universes from Yesod, and Stockhoff countered that universes are played out in parallel rather than cyclically [James Wynn, 2008] [David Stockhoff, 2008]. Gerry Quinn disputed cyclic readings in 2014 [Gerry Quinn, 2014].

For

  • Yesod-time runs opposite to Briah-time, which Andre-Driussi proposed as early as 1997 [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997].
  • Andre-Driussi's Non-Multiverse Model made Yesod and Abaddon successive cosmic cycles that reverse time after each bounce, based on Paul Davies' bounce theory [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998].
  • Lee Berman argued Yesod is the next universe after Briah, with Briah as the past manvantara, reading the flowering metaphor and Malrubius's account of the Hierogrammates [Lee Berman, 2010].
  • Jeff Wilson argued Yesod can be cyclic, with Briah-formed pocket continuums dissipating and later refugees creating new ones [Jeff Wilson, 2010].
  • Cliff Judge proposed Yesod as a future cycle of our universe, grounded in Famulimus's cycle account and the manvantara term [Cliff Judge, 2010].

Against

  • Tony Ellis argued Yesod is a separate universe adjacent to ours and that Earth cannot lie in it, citing Severian's phrase 'the universe higher than our own' [Tony Ellis, 2006].
  • James Jordan argued from a Wolfe interview that Briah is a long past universe we repeat rather than a future of ours [James Jordan, 2002].
  • David Stockhoff argued Yesod is an over-universe where time is not constant, so universes are played out in parallel, not cyclically [David Stockhoff, 2008].
  • James Wynn argued the Hierogrammates can move among coexisting universes reachable from Yesod, making a strict succession merely theoretical [James Wynn, 2008].
  • Gerry Quinn argued that Yesod is separate from the Briah sequence and probably not cyclic, and that visible galaxies show our universe is young [Gerry Quinn, 2014].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never reached agreement on whether Briah and Yesod are successive, simultaneous, or hierarchical, and the discussion ended with competing readings unresolved.
