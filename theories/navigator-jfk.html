The Navigator is John F. Kennedy

The navigator whose funeral Oringa describes in the antechamber is John F. Kennedy (the black caisson, the walking mourners), and the prisoners' ancestors were brought from our time; Robert Borski argued it in Solar Labyrinth.

7 threads · 7 posts · for 4 / against 1 · first raised 1999-03-17 by Robert Borski
Main voices: Robert Borski (2), Michael Andre-Driussi (1), b sharp (1), Michael Thayer (1), Marc Aramini (1), Lee Berman (1)

The debate

Robert Borski first proposed in a 1999-03-17 post [Robert Borski, 1999] that the navigator whose funeral Oringa describes is John F. Kennedy, with the prisoners' ancestors apported from our time, grounding this in the black caisson and walking mourners of Kennedy's 1963 funeral. Within the same month of June 1999, Borski's critic-side reply [Robert Borski, 1999] set out three tests any crashed-starship origin must meet, and Michael Andre-Driussi [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1999] offered a rival reading in which the navigator was a single survivor, Kim Lee Soong, and a JFK and Henry the Navigator composite.

The idea resurfaced years later in a 2006 post by b sharp [b sharp, 2006], who defended Borski's identification by listing 1960s pop culture references in the book while faulting the cryptonymic linking as overly aggressive. In 2013 the theory was revisited in a discussion of whether the novels allude to twentieth-century history at all, where Michael Thayer [Michael Thayer, 2013] asked whether anyone agreed with Borski's argument and Marc Aramini [Marc Aramini, 2013] sorted JFK among candidate references. The thread 'Thecla's Identity' and the 'Navigator' thread, which pressed Mitchell Bailey's and CRCulver's objections, carried the debate further; the 'Are old questions OK?' thread recorded Scott K. disputing the JFK reading.

In 2014 Lee Berman [Lee Berman, 2014] defended Borski's reading as a genuine veiled allusion while doubting it, and the '' thread recorded Wolfe mocking symbol hunters who match characters to real figures such as the navigator-as-JFK theory, which Frederick Norwood took as confirmation that such symbolism fuels speculation.

For

  • Borski argues the black caisson and walking mourners match Kennedy's 1963 funeral, and cites Gunnie's remarks about apports [Robert Borski, 1999].
  • b sharp defends Borski's identification by listing 1960s pop culture references in the book [b sharp, 2006].
  • Lee Berman argues Wolfe sets real puzzles and symbols, so Borski's Kennedy funeral allusion is a genuine veiled meaning even though Berman doubts it [Lee Berman, 2014].
  • Michael Andre-Driussi's composite reading keeps a JFK element alongside a single starship survivor, based on the painted ceiling and generation spacing [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1999].

Against

  • Borski's own critic-side reply notes the girl's mention of the navigator does not by itself establish starship crew, and asks how survivors could hold a funeral and why mourners wear black [Robert Borski, 1999].
  • Mitchell Bailey replied that Severian's remark about black refers to fuligin and questioned the crew's appearance [b sharp, 2006].
  • Marc Aramini asked for sorting of candidate references and held judgment on the Thecla-Rosenberg idea until the candidates were weighed [Marc Aramini, 2013].
  • Scott K. disputed the JFK reading of the Navigator in the 'Are old questions OK?' thread [b sharp, 2006].

Where it stands: Still disputed and largely unresolved: the idea was raised by Borski in 1999, got a few defenders such as b sharp and Lee Berman, and was met with objections, with most threads ending in impasse rather than acceptance.
