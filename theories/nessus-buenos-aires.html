Nessus is Buenos Aires

The Commonwealth lies in South America and Nessus is far-future Buenos Aires, on the Gyoll (the Rio de la Plata).

33 threads · 49 posts · for 24 / against 24 · first raised 1997-02-22 by Joshua A. Solomon
Main voices: Adam Thornton (5), David Stockhoff (5), Lee Berman (5), James Jordan (4), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (4), Jerry Friedman (3)

The debate

The Nessus-is-Buenos-Aires theory was first raised on the Whorl list by Joshua A. Solomon in February 1997, who proposed the Parana and Uruguay as the Nessus and Gyoll rivers [Joshua A. Solomon, 1997]. James Jordan answered the same month, adding the Borges link via Blind Ultan and a previous-oscillation explanation [James Jordan, 1997], and Dave Lebling pushed a layered Constantinople-over-Alexandria-over-Buenos Aires reading [Dave Lebling, 1997]. Alice Turner and Scott Dalrymple disputed the identification in favor of Alexandria or simply a South American Buenos Aires [Alice Turner, 1997] [Scott Dalrymple, 1997].

The debate continued on Urth through 1998 and 2008, with Matthew Malthouse arguing that Nessus cannot be placed on Earth at all [Matthew Malthouse, 1998], and mary whalen defending a lower-sea-level South American Commonwealth [mary whalen, 1998]. A long December 2008 House Absolute thread brought in Josh Geller, David Stockhoff and Son of Witz, who argued from river geography and tectonic drift against the Buenos Aires reading [David Stockhoff, 2008] [Son of Witz, 2008] [Josh Geller, 2008]. Adam Thornton revived the Borges reading in 2011 [Adam Thornton, 2011], and Lee Berman and Jerry Friedman then argued the South American parallels in detail, with Friedman disputing how close the correspondence is [Lee Berman, 2011] [Jerry Friedman, 2011].

The Ascians thread quoted a Wolfe interview saying the setting is South America with Byzantium as parallel, which posters treated as settling the geography question [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2006]. The later Centipede Press newsletter threads saw Gerry Quinn and António Pedro Marques reject the Amazon-style reading [Gerry Quinn, 2020] [António Pedro Marques, 2020]. The Buenos Aires identification persisted among its defenders but was never accepted by all posters, and some who doubted it did so on tectonic or geological grounds.

For

  • Joshua A. Solomon first proposed the Parana and Uruguay rivers as Nessus and Gyoll, citing the delta and the town of Salto [Joshua A. Solomon, 1997].
  • James Jordan linked the Nos Aires pun and layered cities in the mountains to Buenos Aires, and tied Blind Ultan to Borges [James Jordan, 1997] [James Jordan, 1999].
  • Lee Berman argued Nessus takes its geography from South America, citing the pampas, Gyoll, Salto and the cataracts [Lee Berman, 2011].
  • Lee Berman identified Gyoll with the Rio de la Plata near Buenos Aires and noted the estuary's proximity to Nessus [Lee Berman, 2014].
  • Pedro Pereira argued Wolfe clearly places Nessus in South America and that the Moon Lune may once have been closer [Pedro Pereira, 2007].

Against

  • Alice Turner argued Nessus is in a general way Alexandria rather than Buenos Aires [Alice Turner, 1997].
  • Scott Dalrymple noted the Alexandrian library no longer exists and that north and south are arbitrary, so the South American reading is weak [Scott Dalrymple, 1997].
  • Matthew Malthouse argued Urth is geologically very old and that Nessus cannot be placed on Earth, citing the coastline and Plate river [Matthew Malthouse, 1998].
  • Josh Geller and David Stockhoff argued from river directions that Nessus would have moved, so Buenos Aires cannot be it on present geography [Josh Geller, 2008] [David Stockhoff, 2008].
  • Jerry Friedman argued the Commonwealth is not a mirror of South America, citing Gyoll's westward flow and the sea's position west of Nessus [Jerry Friedman, 2011] [Jerry Friedman, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed: defenders kept the Buenos Aires reading and its Borges link, while critics such as Friedman, Stockhoff and Malthouse argued the correspondence is loose or the geography unreliable, and the list never reached agreement.
