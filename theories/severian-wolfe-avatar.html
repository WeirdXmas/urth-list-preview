Severian is an avatar of Wolfe

Severian (and other narrators) is a self-portrait of Wolfe, whose own concerns shape the character.

20 threads · 19 posts · for 16 / against 2 · first raised 1999-03-22 by Charles Dye
Main voices: Lee Berman (8), b sharp (4), Jeff Wilson (2), Charles Dye (1), Geoff Alex Cohen (1), Nigel Price (1)

The debate

The idea that Severian is a self-portrait of Wolfe was first put forward on the list by b sharp in August 2006, who argued that Wolfe's authorial narcissism, seen in cameo touches and wolves, makes Severian a demi-god avatar even though he is a morally flawed figure [b sharp, 2006]. Earlier in 1999, Geoff Alex Cohen had proposed a cameo for Wolfe in the figure of Cyby, a related but narrower self-insertion claim [Geoff Alex Cohen, 1999]. b sharp kept building on the idea in 2006, linking a bronze face and the five coffins to Wolfe's cameo and to Wolfe's marriage [b sharp, 2006] [b sharp, 2006].

The theory was restated in several forms over the years. In 2008 b sharp used the card's wording while disputing the SevN repetition reading, arguing instead for finite lives tied to the coffins [b sharp, 2008]. In 2010 Lee Berman made the theory his main argument, comparing Severian to Number Five from The Fifth Head of Cerberus [Lee Berman, 2010] and reading Severian's silences and the Thecla exchange as hiding autobiographical incest hints [Lee Berman, 2010]. In January 2011 aaron asked whether posters had seen photographs of Wolfe as a young man, suggesting his description of Severian fit Wolfe [aaron, 2011], and Berman followed with an argument that Severian's reactions hide the author's own experience [Lee Berman, 2011]. Jeff Wilson supported the wider self-insertion idea in 2013, pointing to Wolfe's own remark that his insertion into the book is Cyby [Jeff Wilson, 2013].

Berman extended the claim in 2013 to the mausoleum's coffins, arguing they hold Wolfe avatars such as Number Five, Weer and Horn [Lee Berman, 2013], and that Wolfe's admission about Maitre's name supports a self-insertion reading [Lee Berman, 2013]. Berman also argued that Weer, Severian and Horn express self-condemnation [Lee Berman, 2013]. The Five Severians thread ended in disagreement, with Marc Aramini dismissing the mapping of characters onto Wolfe's life as barren and favouring structural patterns, and the Father Inire and Close Reading threads did not settle the question either.

For

  • Wolfe's authorial narcissism and cameo touches make Severian a demi-god avatar of the author [b sharp, 2006]
  • Severian's coy silences and reactions to Thecla's clone hide the author's own experience [Lee Berman, 2011]
  • Severian resembles Number Five, the Wolfe avatar in The Fifth Head of Cerberus, and the coffins link the two stories [Lee Berman, 2010]
  • Severian's description could fit Wolfe as a young man, which fits Wolfe placing himself in his stories [aaron, 2011]
  • The five coffins may hold Wolfe avatars such as Number Five, Weer and Horn [Lee Berman, 2013]

Against

  • b sharp rejected the SevN repetition reading and argued that Severian's lives are finite and tied to the coffins, reading them as a nod to The Fifth Head of Cerberus rather than clones [b sharp, 2008]
  • Jeff Wilson's support rests on the Cyby cameo rather than on Severian himself, so the avatar claim there is narrower [Jeff Wilson, 2013]

Where it stands: Still disputed: Lee Berman was its main proponent across many years, while others offered only partial support or rejected the reading, and the threads it appeared in ended without agreement.
