Number Five is named for Gene Wolfe

The narrator of the first novella is named Gene (or Jean) Wolfe, a self-reference.

21 threads · 32 posts · for 26 / against 7 · first raised 1998-03-29 by Damien Broderick
Main voices: John Barach (5), Lee Berman (5), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (4), Jerry Friedman (4), Damien Broderick (2), Robert Borski (2)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by Damien Broderick in March 1998, who named Gene Wolfe as the narrator of The Fifth Head of Cerberus in passing while reading Alden Dennis Weer as a memory palace [Damien Broderick, 1998]. The next day Robert Borski gave the naming argument, linking Aunt Jeannine's remark and the house name Maison du chien to a narrator named Gene with surname Wolfe [Robert Borski, 1998]. Ron Crown proposed Jean Wolfe from sibling-naming patterns [Ron Crown, 1998], Tony Ellis said he had missed the allusion [Tony Ellis, 1998], and Broderick added that Aunt Jeannine is a feminized Gene and asked whether Wolfe's baptismal name is Eugene [Damien Broderick, 1998].

In 2002 the debate split. Jeff Wilson argued that the narrator's name is Aubrey Wolfson and that Wolfe's appearance does not match the book's description [Jeff Wilson, 2002]. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes went further and proposed that Number Five is a clone of Wolfe himself, reading a pun on gene and wolf [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002], while John Barach linked the name to Gene and Jean and to the Johns in 'A Story' [John Barach, 2002]. Michael Straight called the name link only a joke and asked whether Danehy-Oakes was serious [Michael Straight, 2002], and James Jordan supported the clone reading using Wolfe's views on Original Sin [James Jordan, 2002].

From 2005 on, Barach argued the father is Jean, a homophone of Gene, and that the family is surnamed Wolfe [John Barach, 2005]. Maru Dubshinki disputed this, noting that Clute only says the narrator 'could' be Wolfe [Maru Dubshinki, 2005] and that Number Five finds no books by a Wolfe in the library, while Jeanette also goes by Dr. Veil [Maru Dubshinki, 2005]. Lee Berman later cited Wolfe's admission that Maitre's name solves to Gene Wolfe in arguing that the fifth head is Gene Wolfe [Lee Berman, 2013], and Barach revived the library-shelf clue in 2014 [John Barach, 2014], which Andrew Bollen questioned [Andrew Bollen, 2014].

For

  • Robert Borski argued the narrator is named Gene, with surname Wolfe, using Aunt Jeannine's remark and the Maison du chien house name [Robert Borski, 1998].
  • Damien Broderick cited the father's line that Number Five shares his name and Marsch's remark that the aunt is a version of the father [Damien Broderick, 1998].
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes proposed Number Five as a clone of Gene Wolfe, grounded in wordplay on Gene as well-born [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002].
  • John Barach argued the father is Jean, a homophone of Gene, using the Maison du chien house name, library shelving and Aunt Jeanette [John Barach, 2005].
  • Berman cited Wolfe's remark that Maitre's name solves to Gene Wolfe, and the title's mismatch of five heads, to argue the fifth head is Wolfe [Lee Berman, 2013].

Against

  • Jeff Wilson argued the narrator is Aubrey Wolfson and that Wolfe's appearance does not match the book's description [Jeff Wilson, 2002].
  • Michael Straight called the name link only a joke and said the character bears no real relation to Wolfe [Michael Straight, 2002].
  • Maru Dubshinki noted that the Clute quotation says only that the narrator 'could' be Wolfe [Maru Dubshinki, 2005].
  • Dubshinki argued that Number Five finds no books under the Wolfe name in the library and that Jeanette also goes by Dr. Veil, so her surname is not Wolfe [Maru Dubshinki, 2005].
  • Michael Straight argued the link to Wolfe is symbolic rather than literal and cautioned against expecting a family name to carry across generations [Michael Straight, 2002].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the theory was argued repeatedly from 1998 to 2014 without consensus, and posters such as Jerry Friedman treated Number Five's surname as Wolfe as a shared conclusion [Jerry Friedman, 2011] while others rejected the name link [Michael Straight, 2002].
