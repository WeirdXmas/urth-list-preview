The Whorl is a hollowed asteroid

The Whorl was made by hollowing out an asteroid.

8 threads · 8 posts · for 2 / against 2 · first raised 1997-10-06 by John Eric Ivancich
Main voices: Jeff Wilson (2), John Eric Ivancich (1), Michael Andre-Driussi (1), Craig Brewer (1), James Wynn (1), Matthew Groves (1)

The debate

The asteroid origin was first raised as a question by John Eric Ivancich on 1997-10-06, in a post asking whether the Whorl is a hollowed asteroid and what Short Sun signifies [John Eric Ivancich, 1997]. Michael Andre-Driussi took it up in 1997 and said an old chem soldier had described the Whorl's asteroid origin, while using rotating-habitat gravity models to work through how floaters could lift off [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997].

The theory came back in 2009 and 2010 mainly in questions about shape and scale rather than origin. Craig Brewer asked whether the Whorl is a true cylinder or an oblong hollowed asteroid, citing an inhumi's description [Craig Brewer, 2009]. Matthew Groves described a craggy, asteroid-like interior and then conceded he had no answer to the objection that shiprock could not bear gravity [Matthew Groves, 2009]. James Wynn argued that the mountains were carved from asteroid material rather than built up by geological pressure [James Wynn, 2009].

Jeff Wilson used the asteroid origin in 2010 to argue that the Wall around Nessus cannot be as large as the Whorl [Jeff Wilson, 2010], and later said the Whorl is a hollow body made from an asteroid that need not keep the asteroid's original dimensions [Jeff Wilson, 2010]. Jack Smith argued that the Whorl is a cylinder shaped from a raw asteroid, too small to be an excavated Lune, estimating at least 200 miles in length [Jack Smith, 2010]. Several threads (Shape of the Whorl, the wall and baldanders, and ) took up the question, with Roy Lackey citing a proper-names list for asteroid origin and David Stockhoff suggesting the ambiguous term shipstone might hide asteroid matter, but none reached agreement.

Against

  • Groves conceded he had no answer to the objection that shiprock asteroid material could not bear gravity [Matthew Groves, 2009]

Where it stands: Still disputed: the asteroid origin was raised in 1997 and recurs as a working premise for several posters, but the shape, scale and construction of the Whorl were never settled and the list reached no consensus.
