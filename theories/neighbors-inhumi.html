The Neighbors and the inhumi are related

The Neighbors and the inhumi are related (the Neighbors created, carried or keep the inhumi, perhaps to test human souls).

81 threads · 117 posts · for 65 / against 43 · first raised 1999-09-29 by Dan Rabin
Main voices: James Wynn (18), Lee Berman (12), David Stockhoff (9), Gerry Quinn (9), Marc Aramini (6), thalassocrat (6)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the Whorl list in September 1999 by Dan Rabin, who speculated that the inhumi gained intelligence from their diet and that the Neighbors' departure pushed them to seek another race [Dan Rabin, 1999]. Over the following years the question of a link between the two groups took many forms: a Neighbor-created biological experiment [Jeffrey Meyers, 2000], Neighbors who are themselves inhumi [Nicholas Gevers, 2000], and an inhumi secret about the Neighbors' dark side [Adam Stephanides, 2001]. In 2002 Ryan Thistlethwaite stated the relation directly, citing Horn's name exchange with a Neighbor [Ryan Thistlethwaite, 2002].

The debate sharpened around how inhumi moved between Green and the Whorl or Blue. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes reported a Return to the Whorl line saying the Neighbors infected the Whorl with inhumi [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002], and Charles Reed argued the Neighbors seeded the Whorl via landers under the Plan of Pas [Charles Reed, 2002]. In 2005 thalassocrat and Roy Lackey proposed that Neighbors physically shuttled inhumi by ship [thalassocrat, 2005][thalassocrat, 2005][Roy C. Lackey, 2005], while Don Doggett and James Wynn offered rival explanations [Don Doggett, 2005][James Wynn, 2009]. The Whorl-entry question returned in 2010 and 2011, when James Wynn argued the quoted passage says only that the Neighbors infected humanity [James Wynn, 2011] and Gerry Quinn insisted they must have visited the Whorl [Gerry Quinn, 2011]. Wynn repeatedly argued for dream-travel instead of spaceships [James Wynn, 2011][James Wynn, 2011].

From 2009 onward posters increasingly took the link as a tree-and-vine ecology or a symbiotic cycle. Brian Lovely and James Wynn debated trees versus vines [Brian Lovely, 2009][James Wynn, 2009], Lee Berman proposed symbiosis [Lee Berman, 2010][Lee Berman, 2010], and Marc Aramini and Lee Berman later argued the Neighbors were corrupted by the Inhumi on Green [Marc Aramini, 2013][Lee Berman, 2013]. Later posts mostly disputed the link, with Andrew Mason rejecting it in 2013 [Andrew Mason, 2013].

For

  • Dan Rabin proposed that the Neighbors' departure pressured the inhumi to find another race, which ties the two groups together [Dan Rabin, 1999].
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes reported a Return to the Whorl line in which a Neighbor says they infected the Whorl's people with inhumi [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002].
  • Ryan Thistlethwaite proposed the relation directly, citing Horn's name exchange with a Neighbor [Ryan Thistlethwaite, 2002].
  • Thalassocrat and Roy Lackey argued the Neighbors shuttled inhumi between Green and the Whorl by ship [thalassocrat, 2005][Roy C. Lackey, 2005].
  • Jordon Flato argued the Neighbors enslaved the inhumi, which ties their fates together [Jordon Flato, 2009].

Against

  • Adam Stephanides argued that enslaved inhumi would not remember the Neighbors as benevolent, disputing the Neighbors' role in their rise [Adam Stephanides, 2000].
  • James Wynn argued the Neighbors could not have had spaceships and that dream-travel explains interplanetary movement, so inhumi need not be carried [James Wynn, 2011][James Wynn, 2011].
  • James Wynn disputed Gerry Quinn's reading that the Neighbors brought the inhumi to the Whorl, saying the quoted line only mentions infecting humanity [James Wynn, 2011].
  • Nicholas Gevers argued that teleportation would leave Blue far more infested, undercutting a Neighbor-carried account [Nicholas Gevers, 2001].
  • Andrew Mason argued the Neighbours and inhumi remain separate entities, citing the Rajan's words [Andrew Mason, 2013].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never reached agreement on whether the Neighbors created, carried or kept the inhumi, and the question was argued across many years with the transport and origin accounts continuing to conflict.
