Why Auk kidnapped Hyacinth

Auk took Hyacinth because Tartaros told him to, or by mistake.

10 threads · 11 posts · for 4 / against 7 · first raised 1997-07-31 by Alice Turner
Main voices: Andrew Mason (5), James Wynn (4), Alice Turner (1), Michael Andre-Driussi (1), Lee Berman (1)

The debate

The theory was first raised in 1997 when Alice Turner, in [Alice Turner, 1997], disputed Wolfe's published answer about why Auk kidnapped and released Hyacinth, arguing that Tartaros staying with Auk would have exposed any error sooner and that the novelist needed her off-stage. Michael Andre-Driussi replied in [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997] that Auk's violence comes from a primal layer of his own character rather than from Tartaros.

The question returned in 2005, when James Wynn asked in [James Wynn, 2005] how Silk could see gods after his wedding night, floating Auk's concussion as an exception to the rule. In 2010 Wynn argued in [James Wynn, 2010] that Tartaros told Auk to bring a woman and that Auk released her once his brain injury healed. In [James Wynn, 2010] he went further and proposed that Auk is a clone of Tartaros, grounding this in Wolfe's Q&A answer on the kidnapping and release. In [James Wynn, 2010], within the Embryos thread, he disputed David Stockhoff's suggestion that Tartaros possesses Auk, separating possession from Auk thinking more clearly.

Andrew Mason supplied the main rival account. In [Andrew Mason, 2010] he argued that Tartaros heals Auk's mind so that the release can be explained, and that the hand-holding is not possession. From January 2011 he argued in [Andrew Mason, 2011] that Auk was sent to protect Chenille, so grabbing Hyacinth was a mistake, and suggested Auk may be Tartaros's son. In [Andrew Mason, 2011] he argued that Auk misread Tartaros's 'the woman' as meaning Hyacinth when Chenille had vanished, a simpler explanation than time travel. In [Andrew Mason, 2011] he argued that Auk misheard Tartaros saying a woman is vital, citing the later rule that every man must bring a woman.

The record shows no participant other than Wynn and Mason posting on the kidnapping motive after 2005, so the debate stayed between a told-to reading and a mistake reading.

For

  • Tartaros told Auk to bring a woman, and Auk released her after his brain injury healed, an answer the text cannot supply [James Wynn, 2010]
  • Auk is a clone of Tartaros, a view grounded in Wolfe's Q&A answer on the kidnapping and release [James Wynn, 2010]
  • Tartaros heals Auk's mind, which explains the instruction to let Hyacinth go, and his hand-holding is not possession [Andrew Mason, 2010]
  • Auk was sent to protect Chenille, so grabbing Hyacinth was a mistake about which woman Tartaros meant [Andrew Mason, 2011]
  • Auk misread 'the woman' as Hyacinth after Chenille vanished, and later released her without argument, a simpler account than time travel [Andrew Mason, 2011]

Against

  • Auk's error would have been obvious sooner because Tartaros stayed with him through the week, so the published answer is disingenuous [Alice Turner, 1997]
  • The real reason for Hyacinth's removal was the novelist needing her off-stage for Silk's movement, not any plot logic about Auk [Alice Turner, 1997]
  • Auk's abduction of Hyacinth comes from a primal, uninhibited layer of Auk, not from Tartaros [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997]
  • Possession is not the same as Auk thinking more clearly or receiving messages from Tartaros, so possession does not explain the instruction [James Wynn, 2010]
  • Auk's concussion may be what confuses the rule about seeing gods, rather than any instruction from Tartaros [James Wynn, 2005]

Where it stands: Still disputed: the told-to, clone and mistake readings were each argued by a single poster in 2010-2011 and none gained agreement on the list.
