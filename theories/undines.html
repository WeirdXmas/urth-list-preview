What the undines want

The undines (Juturna and her sisters) serve Abaia or act on their own; their saving of Severian from drowning has a hidden purpose (luring him, or a baptism).

71 threads · 121 posts · for 85 / against 42 · first raised 1998-01-27 by C R Culver
Main voices: Jeff Wilson (26), Lee Berman (25), David Stockhoff (10), Alice Turner (8), b sharp (7), mournings glory (6)

The debate

The theory was first raised in January 1998 on the Urth list. C R Culver argued that the undine who saved Severian as a child was a tool of Tzadkiel, so his survival was fated [C R Culver, 1998]. Two days later Dave Lebling argued instead that the undine was luring Severian toward evil, since the agents of evil in the books try to win him over rather than kill him [Dave Lebling, 1998]. Peter Stephenson later framed the undine as a Faustian tempter, asking whether she was Mephistopheles or Helen [Peter Stephenson, 1998], and adam louis stephanides questioned whether the rescue was caused by the earlier Severian at all [adam louis stephanides, 1998].

In 2002, during the thread on the undines of Briah, the debate turned to what undines are. Robert Borski argued they were alien beings with reversed time [Robert Borski, 2002], Alice Turner argued they were aliens and not homo sapiens [Alice Turner, 2002], and Josh Geller argued they were plausibly female humans [Josh Geller, 2002]. Jeff Wilson then argued they serve Abaia and are not high-tech enslavers, since Juturna says she serves Abaia [Jeff Wilson, 2002]. Robert Borski's name-based and time-based readings were the main alternatives in that thread [Robert Borski, 2002].

From 2006 the discussion centred on whose agents the undines are and what they want. Roy C. Lackey argued the undines foresaw Severian's rise to Autarch and helped it, citing Malrubius's line about the paramours of Abaia who spared him [Roy C. Lackey, 2006]. Chris P answered that the undines are more primordial than Abaia and need not serve his interests [Chris P, 2006], and Jeff Wilson argued that the undines must have foresight of their own [Jeff Wilson, 2006]. mournings glory argued the undines serve the Hierodules rather than the Megatherians [mournings glory, 2006], and b sharp argued that the undines were forced into compliance and that the New Sun freed them [b sharp, 2006]. In 2008 Jordon Flato proposed the undines want to enslave decaying humanity rather than destroy it [Jordon Flato, 2008], while Chris P argued they are not servants of Abaia [Chris P, 2006]. In 2010 Jeff Wilson proposed Juturna is Severian's twin and that the undines convert humans to breathe water [Jeff Wilson, 2010] [Jeff Wilson, 2010], and Lee Berman argued Severian's first miracle occurred during the undine rescue [Lee Berman, 2010]. Lee Berman again linked the rescue to Severian's eidolon revivals in 2013 [Lee Berman, 2013].

The most distinctive reading came in 2014, when marcobadie argued that the rescue from water is Severian's baptism by immersion and that the Autarch's coin is a confirmation [marcobadie, 2014] [marcobadie, 2014]. The discussion never reached consensus across the threads, which ended in impasse on the sibling, Hierodule, foresight and baptism readings alike.

For

  • Lackey argued the undines foresaw Severian's rise and helped it, citing the paramours of Abaia who spared him in The Citadel of the Autarch [Roy C. Lackey, 2006].
  • Juturna's own remarks about serving Abaia and her mistreatment by him were cited to argue the undines were forced into compliance [b sharp, 2006] [Jeff Wilson, 2002].
  • Flato argued the undines and Abaia want to keep humanity alive to enslave it, so a flood would leave them nothing to play with [Jordon Flato, 2008].
  • Mournings glory argued from Famulimus's remark about the water women and from the Hierodules' knowledge of Severian's encounter with Juturna that the undines serve the Hierodules [mournings glory, 2006] [mournings glory, 2006].
  • Marcobadie argued that the undine's rescue of Severian is a baptism by immersion and a sacramental bond to Abaia's followers [marcobadie, 2014] [marcobadie, 2014].

Against

  • Culver argued the undine was a tool of Tzadkiel, so the rescue was fated rather than serving a hidden purpose [C R Culver, 1998].
  • Lebling argued the undine was luring Severian to evil, since the agents of evil in the books try to win him over rather than kill him [Dave Lebling, 1998].
  • Jeff Wilson argued the undines are not enslavers and that Juturna serves Abaia rather than running gadgetry [Jeff Wilson, 2002].
  • Chris P argued the undines are more primordial than Abaia and need not serve his interests, and later that they are not his servants [Chris P, 2006] [Chris P, 2006].
  • Stockhoff argued Abaia and the undines are not the cause of humanity's disgrace and may be aliens seeking control of the Autarchs rather than acting on Abaia's behalf [David Stockhoff, 2008].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never settled whether the undines act for Abaia, the Hierodules or themselves, or whether the rescue has a hidden purpose such as luring or baptism, and most threads ended without consensus.
