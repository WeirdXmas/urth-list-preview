The meaning of "Autarch"

The title Autarch means "self-ruler", ironically plural in Severian's case.

8 threads · 8 posts · for 2 / against 5 · first raised 1998-05-16 by Michael Andre-Driussi
Main voices: Michael Andre-Driussi (1), Michael Straight (1), Mark Millman (1), J. Schultz (1), Matthew Malthouse (1), Jeff Wilson (1)

The debate

the list's earliest post is from 1998-05-16, when Michael Andre-Driussi retracted an earlier reading of the Autarch's closing line, which concerns flight to Yesod and not the meaning of the title [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998]. The self-rule idea first appears on 1998-07-19, when J. Schultz argued that the title is thematic, symbolizing Severian's self-rule as he casts off the guild, the Archon and Typhon [J. Schultz, 1998]. Other 1998 posts by Michael Straight and Mark Millman concern how the Autarch's successors are verified through the words of command, not the title's meaning [Michael Straight, 1998] [Mark Millman, 1998].

The theory as worded here was put forward by Matthew Malthouse on 2004-12-05. He argued that 'self ruler' is the more literal sense of Wolfe's word, that the form is ironic because the Autarch is legion rather than singular, and that Wolfe uses 'monarch' for absolute rulers elsewhere [Matthew Malthouse, 2004]. In 2010 the question of whether 'autarch' was a generic word for any ruler in Severian's era came up. Jeff Wilson disputed that generic claim, citing the brown book's use of 'king' and arguing that Ymar's memories are not the only source for ruler titles [Jeff Wilson, 2010]. Roy C. Lackey replied that the word was a catch-all for ruler in that era, while people in Typhon's day called him monarch, quoting a line in which Severian tells Ymar he will become the monarch [Roy C. Lackey, 2010]. Andrew Mason then supported the self-ruler sense, citing Jonas and Cyriaca, who call ancient rulers autarchs and then note that the usage is strictly wrong [Andrew Mason, 2010].

Much of the wider discussion ran through threads rather than the card's posts. In the 'Autarchs' thread, António Pedro Marques and Andrew Mason argued that the office is non-hereditary and that 'autarch' means self-ruler, often misread as a royal title, while Lee Berman questioned how autarchs differ from ordinary despots. The later '' thread traded dictionary, Google Books and Byzantine usage evidence on the word's origin without settling it.

For

  • Schultz argued the title symbolizes Severian's self-rule as he casts off outside authorities [J. Schultz, 1998]
  • Malthouse argued the etymology of autarchy supports the literal self-ruler sense, and that the form is ironic because the Autarch is legion [Matthew Malthouse, 2004]
  • Malthouse contrasted 'autarch' with 'monarch', which Wolfe uses for absolute rulers, to argue for the self-ruler sense [Matthew Malthouse, 2004]
  • Mason argued the proper sense is a ruler who rules himself, as Ymar and his successors took it, and cited Jonas and Cyriaca calling ancient rulers autarchs while noting this is strictly wrong [Andrew Mason, 2010]

Against

  • Lackey argued the word functioned as a catch-all for ruler in Severian's era, with people in Typhon's day calling him monarch [Roy C. Lackey, 2010]
  • Wilson challenged the generic-ruler claim by citing the brown book's use of 'king' and noting Ymar's memories are not the only source for titles [Jeff Wilson, 2010]

Where it stands: Still disputed: the self-ruler reading has supporters in Malthouse and Mason, but the 2010 exchange with Wilson and Lackey left the usage question unsettled, and the thread discussions ended without consensus.
