Horn is (un)reliable as narrator

Whether Horn's account in the Short Sun can be trusted.

15 threads · 10 posts · for 5 / against 4 · first raised 1997-01-25 by James Jordan
Main voices: James Jordan (1), Dave Lebling (1), Alex David Groce (1), Dan Parmenter (1), Adam Stephanides (1), Charles Reed (1)

The debate

The theory that Horn is an unreliable narrator in the Short Sun was not raised as a single question at first; the list's earliest post on Horn's reliability is James Jordan's 1997 argument that Horn is reliable, so Silk's story can be trusted [James Jordan, 1997]. Dave Lebling soon added that Horn tries to be an accurate narrator showing Silk's faults [Dave Lebling, 1997], and Alex David Groce proposed in 2000 that the Virgil allusion makes Horn a truthful prophetic narrator [Alex David Groce, 2000]. Rival positions then appeared: Dan Parmenter called Horn the least reliable Wolfe narrator in 2001 [Dan Parmenter, 2001], and Charles Reed argued in 2003 that Horn claims accuracy only about what he wrote down, not infallible memory [Charles Reed, 2003].

Later posts moved the question onto identity and memory. James Wynn argued in 2005 that Horn's denial of being Silk is unreliable [James Wynn, 2005], and Roy Lackey and Wynn disputed whether Horn claims perfect recall like Severian. Michael Straight and Adam Stephanides debated whether Horn is Silk with Horn's spirit, with Stephanides noting that Horn would not say so unless he consciously knew it [David Stockhoff, 2010]. Philip Loguinov argued in the Sinew paternity thread that Horn is unreliable and does not know. In the Alice Turner and Groce exchange over whether Horn's account is factual, the question was left open.

Lee Berman's 2011 claim that Horn's account of cursed gifts is reliable is the main defence of trust after the early years [Lee Berman, 2011]. Marc Aramini's 2013 parallel that Silk lies to himself as Horn does shows the theory being carried into the Short Sun's characterisation [Marc Aramini, 2013]. The threads summarised show the question returning repeatedly without any settled answer.

For

  • Horn is reliable and Silk's story can be trusted, according to James Jordan's argument from Wolfe's rejection of duplicitous narrators and the Gospel analogy [James Jordan, 1997]
  • Horn tries to be an accurate narrator who shows Silk's warts, as Dave Lebling argued [Dave Lebling, 1997]
  • Alex David Groce proposed that the Virgil horn-gate allusion makes Horn a truthful narrator of prophecy and underlying events [Alex David Groce, 2000]
  • Lee Berman argued that Horn's account of the barnacled Neighbor and of cursed gifts can be trusted [Lee Berman, 2011]
  • Michael Straight argued that leaving Horn's reliability open strengthens The Book of Silk

Against

  • Dan Parmenter argued that Horn is the least reliable Wolfe narrator, citing Severian's precedent and a prophecy Horn calls charlatanry [Dan Parmenter, 2001]
  • Charles Reed argued that Horn's memory is unreliable, citing gaps and invented conversations, and that Horn claims accuracy only about what he wrote down [Charles Reed, 2003]
  • James Wynn argued that Horn's denial of being Silk is not complete and so unreliable [James Wynn, 2005]
  • Philip Loguinov argued in the Sinew thread that Horn is an unreliable narrator who does not know
  • Alice Turner argued that Horn, like Matthew, invented events out of evangelical zeal and so is not factual

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never settled whether Horn is reliable, with Jordan, Groce and Berman defending trust and Parmenter, Reed, Wynn and Loguinov doubting it, and several threads ended in impasse.
