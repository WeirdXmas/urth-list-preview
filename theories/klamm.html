Klamm is Kissinger

In There Are Doors, Klamm is patterned on Henry Kissinger (and Kafka's Klamm), and is the same man as Mr. Green at another time.

10 threads · 16 posts · for 12 / against 3 · first raised 2003-01-14 by Michael Andre-Driussi
Main voices: Michael Andre-Driussi (4), Adam Stephanides (4), Chris P (4), Roy C. Lackey (3), Dan (2), Joe Cilluffo (1)

The debate

The Kissinger identification first appears on the list in January 2003, when Michael Andre-Driussi proposed in [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2003] that Herr Klamm is patterned on Kissinger, alongside a Graves-inspired pagan reading of the novel. Adam Stephanides and Joe Cilluffo then took up the name question: Stephanides argued in [Adam Stephanides, 2003] that Klamm is drawn from Kafka's The Castle, and Cilluffo in [Joe Cilluffo, 2003] reread the figure as a soft, unsympathetic Kissinger-like character. Stephanides later in [Adam Stephanides, 2003] and [Adam Stephanides, 2003] pressed the Kafka link, citing matching descriptions of the two men's faces and the Castle parallels.

The Kissinger reading returned in February 2003, when Alice Turner in [Alice Turner, 2003] proposed an allegory of the Nixon White House merged with Kafka's Castle, with Klamm as Kissinger and North as Ollie North, while admitting the conceit was unsuccessful. The thread summary notes she later clarified Ollie was a general symbol rather than a literal Watergate link. The Klamm-Green identity question was argued separately: Chris P in [Chris P, 2003] tentatively proposed Klamm and Green are one person, Dan asked the same question in [Dan, 2005], and Dan again in [Dan, 2005] tied it to Klamm calling Green Herr K.

The Kissinger idea received little sustained support beyond its proposers and was overtaken in the list's discussion by the Kafka-derived name, Cybele priest, Visitor and Attis readings. Posters did not reach agreement on whether Klamm and Green are the same man, and the record contains no decisive Wolfe remark on Kissinger.

For

  • Michael Andre-Driussi suggested Herr Klamm is patterned on Kissinger in the January 2003 post [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2003].
  • Joe Cilluffo proposed Klamm may be modelled on Kissinger, reading both as soft and unsympathetic figures [Joe Cilluffo, 2003].
  • Alice Turner read the novel as a Nixon White House allegory with Klamm standing for Kissinger [Alice Turner, 2003].
  • Adam Stephanides found the Kissinger idea plausible in discussing Mantis's suggestion [Adam Stephanides, 2003].

Against

  • Adam Stephanides argued Klamm is drawn from Kafka's The Castle, where the protagonist is always called K., rather than from Kissinger [Adam Stephanides, 2003].
  • Stephanides cited matching descriptions of the two men's faces and moustaches to tie Klamm to Kafka's Klamm, not Kissinger [Adam Stephanides, 2003].
  • Alice Turner admitted the merged Nixon-Castle conceit was unsuccessful and said she would not argue it [Alice Turner, 2003].

Where it stands: Still unresolved and raised only sporadically: the Kissinger reading was proposed in 2003 by a few posters and never widely adopted, with the Kafka-derived name and the Klamm-Green identity question drawing more discussion.
