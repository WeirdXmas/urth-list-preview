The numbers under the Seal of Pas are a cipher

The numbers under the Seal of Pas form a cryptogram.

8 threads · 15 posts · for 10 / against 5 · first raised 2008-07-04 in the thread "whorl numbering and the seal of pas"
Main voices: Lee Berman (4), Gerry Quinn (3), Marc Aramini (3), Dave Tallman (1), Jack Smith (1), Gwern Branwen (1)

The debate

The theory that the numbers under the Seal of Pas form a cipher was first raised on the list by Dave Tallman, who proposed in 2008 that the underground number rows in Long Sun could be a cipher and that Silk's addition sign might sum them [Dave Tallman, 2008]. The question returned in 2010, when Lee Berman argued the sequence was a long-unsolved puzzle with a probable hidden answer [Lee Berman, 2010], and Josh Geller and Thomas Bitterman argued the digits encoded something, the latter suggesting a product code with checksums [Josh Geller, 2010] [Thomas Bitterman, 2010]. Berman then argued more explicitly for a deliberate cryptogram, grounded in the nine four-digit groups each summing under 27, while testing guesses such as Typhon-Pas and GENE WOLFE [Lee Berman, 2010].

Opposition came mainly from Gerry Quinn, who argued the numbers might have low entropy as verisimilitude without encoded meaning [Gerry Quinn, 2010], and later read them as barcode-like identifiers [Gerry Quinn, 2011]. Jack Smith supported the decoding view, arguing the numbers must specify stored people, citing 0401, while admitting he could not decipher them [Jack Smith, 2010]. Gwern Branwen tested whether Wolfe's name fit the digits through sums, mod values and a binary reading, admitting the regularities might be numerology [Gwern Branwen, 2010]. Jeff Wilson's entropy measurements, as reported in the Wolfe's Puzzles thread, pointed to low randomness, though Quinn kept doubting specific meaning.

In the later years Marc Aramini revived the cipher idea, first suggesting gematria [Marc Aramini, 2014], then proposing Thetis as a keyword [Marc Aramini, 2014], and finally arguing the string was probably a traditional cipher rather than coordinates [Marc Aramini, 2014]. Berman also linked the numbers to Revelation's Seventh Seal in 2011 [Lee Berman, 2011], which Quinn disputed by arguing the numbers were meaningless as a hidden meaning [Gerry Quinn, 2011]. Several threads, including Seal of Pas and Wolfe's Attitude toward his Readers, ended without agreement.

For

  • Dave Tallman first proposed the rows of numbers might be a cipher and that Silk's addition sign could sum them [Dave Tallman, 2008].
  • Lee Berman argued the numbers are a deliberate cryptogram, grounded in the nine four-digit groups each summing under 27 [Lee Berman, 2010].
  • Jack Smith argued the numbers must specify stored people, citing 0401 as evidence the sequence is a code [Jack Smith, 2010].
  • Thomas Bitterman argued the groups are a product code with checksums, supported by observations about doubled digits and digit sums [Thomas Bitterman, 2010].
  • Marc Aramini suggested a number-letter cipher or keyword such as Thetis might decode the numerals [Marc Aramini, 2014] [Marc Aramini, 2014].

Against

  • Gerry Quinn argued the numbers may have low entropy to add verisimilitude without encoding any specific meaning [Gerry Quinn, 2010].
  • Gerry Quinn argued the numbers are barcode-like identifiers, each seal differing, rather than a fixed sequence [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • Gwern Branwen objected that a fixed sequence cannot be a serial number and that earlier ASCII and EBCDIC decodings failed, per the Seal of Pas thread [Gwern Branwen, 2010].
  • Gerry Quinn argued the numbers are deliberately meaningless, so any hidden meaning is an irony about Wolfe as author [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • Marc Aramini argued coordinates make little narrative sense for the Whorl, favouring a traditional cipher reading, which is a point made in favour rather than against; Quinn's entropy-based doubts were answered by Jeff Wilson's measurements [Marc Aramini, 2014].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the cipher reading was raised repeatedly from 2008 to 2014 and never solved, and posters including Quinn kept doubting that the digits encode anything specific.
