The inhumi take their minds from their victims

The inhumi have no minds of their own; their intelligence (and their children's spirits) come from the blood of those they feed on.

107 threads · 128 posts · for 69 / against 45 · first raised 1997-06-17 by Michael Andre-Driussi
Main voices: Adam Stephanides (13), Lee Berman (13), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (9), Marc Aramini (8), James Wynn (8), James Jordan (6)

The debate

The theory that the inhumi take their minds from their victims was first raised on the list on 1997-06-17 by Michael Andre-Driussi, though his post [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997] only argued that a passage in Exodus from the Long Sun concerns character rather than intelligence. The idea took shape in September and October 1999, when Alex David Groce [Alex David Groce, 1999] and Patrick O'Leary [Patrick O'Leary, 1999] offered that inhumi acquire personality from the first blood they drink, and Dave Lebling [Dave Lebling, 1999] proposed that they maintain intelligence by drinking intelligent creatures' blood. William H. Ansley [William H. Ansley, 1999] and Endymion9 [Endymion9, 1999] then backed the human-blood version, and Ansley [William H. Ansley, 2000] tied the secret to protecting humans from the bite.

The spring 2001 Return to the Whorl discussion produced the most variants. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2001] argued that inheritance from the parents they fed on explained intelligence, while Adam Stephanides [Adam Stephanides, 2001] proposed that inhumi take the children's spirits from their victims, and Dan'l Danehy-Oakes [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2001] argued that stealing blood steals the spirit. Michael Straight [Michael Straight, 2002] and Andy Robertson [Andy Robertson, 2002] later applied the idea to Horn and Silk, but Straight [Michael Straight, 2002] disputed Robertson's dead-inhumu reading. In 2009 and 2010 James Wynn [James Wynn, 2009][James Wynn, 2010] and Lee Berman [Lee Berman, 2010] extended the notion into soul absorption and Neighbor ensoulment, while Andrew Mason [Andrew Mason, 2010] and António Pedro Marques [António Pedro Marques, 2011] disputed the soul-transfer model.

Opponents mostly argued that the inhumi keep their own minds or nature. Adam Stephanides [Adam Stephanides, 2002] said the inhumi have only souls stolen from humans, Jerry Friedman [Jerry Friedman, 2002] argued their minds are genuine, and Chris P [Chris P, 2004] said the animal soul remains. Later discussion of Silk/Horn and the Shadow Children in the 2011 to 2013 period, including David Stockhoff [David Stockhoff, 2013] and Antonin Scriabin [Antonin Scriabin, 2013] on conscious selves created by drinking blood, kept the theory alive without settling it.

For

  • Inhumi intelligence or personality comes from the blood they drink, as Groce [Alex David Groce, 1999] and Lebling [Dave Lebling, 1999] argued in 1999.
  • Ansley [William H. Ansley, 1999] cited Krait's secret passage from On Blue's Waters and a parallel with Powers' nephilim as support for human-blood intelligence.
  • Stephanides [Adam Stephanides, 2001] proposed that inhumi take children's spirits from victims, linking Krait to Sinew through the spirit match.
  • Danehy-Oakes [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2001] grounded the spiritual version in the breath-spirit-life equation and Fava's remark about what inhumi last consumed.
  • Stockhoff [David Stockhoff, 2013] and Scriabin [Antonin Scriabin, 2013] read Jahlee's remark that without drinking blood the inhumi would remain beasts as confirming the view.

Against

  • Horn's dismissal of the inhumi as no more intelligent than dogs was disputed by David Wells [David Wells, 1999], who cited one inhumi passing as a senior clergyman for thirty years.
  • Groce [Alex David Groce, 1999] argued that unearthed inhumi revert to normal after blood, so they are not reduced to animals without it.
  • Stephanides [Adam Stephanides, 2002] argued that inhumi have only souls stolen from humans, not souls of their own.
  • Friedman [Jerry Friedman, 2002] argued that the inhumi's human appearance during astral travel shows their minds are genuine.
  • Marques [António Pedro Marques, 2011] argued that the inhumi's biology does not change when they feed, only their souls, so the blood model is incomplete.

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list raised and reworked the blood-derived intelligence idea repeatedly from 1997 through 2013 without reaching agreement, and the debate ended in competing variants rather than consensus.
