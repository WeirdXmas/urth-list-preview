The Fortunate Cloud and the Quasar are one ship

The ship names the Fortunate Cloud and the Quasar refer to the same vessel.

12 threads · 20 posts · for 14 / against 10 · first raised 1998-03-15 by C R Culver
Main voices: Lee Berman (7), Gerry Quinn (4), C R Culver (2), b sharp (2), Sergei SOLOVIEV (2), Michael Andre-Driussi (1)

The debate

The same-ship reading first appears on the list in March 1998, when Michael Andre-Driussi argued in a sarcastic reply that Fortunate Cloud and Quasar are the same ship, pointing to a web of coincidences including the names and the old men on the Lake of Birds [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998]. The earliest post on the list is C R Culver's query about whether Hethor's ship passed the Quasar rather than being it [C R Culver, 1998]. Within days Michael Straight argued the two names could be translations of one ship name, since a star is a gas cloud that condenses fortunately [Michael Straight, 1998], while Alice Turner and Culver argued the ships were different, noting that sailors and ships change ports and that Wolfe may not have planned the Ship of Tzadkiel when writing the early books [Alice Turner, 1998] [C R Culver, 1998].

In 1999 Roy C. Lackey linked the Quasar, the Fortunate Cloud and Tzadkiel's ship as one vessel, citing Sidero's remark that all ships are one ship [Roy C. Lackey, 1999]. The idea returned in 2006, when b sharp proposed that Hethor was captain of the Quasar and that the Citadel is an alternate version of it [b sharp, 2006]. In 2010 the discussion moved to Gerry Quinn's suggestion that the ship is named the Fortunate Cloud and that Jonas was a crewman on it [Gerry Quinn, 2010] [Gerry Quinn, 2010], though Quinn also doubted the Chinese-sounding name was Korean [Gerry Quinn, 2010]. In October 2011 Lee Berman argued that Quasar is an apt name for Tzadkiel's vessel and that Hethor and Jonas were shipmates on it [Lee Berman, 2011] [Lee Berman, 2011].

The 2011 posts split. Sergei Soloviev argued that Quasar belongs to space-opera naming and that other starcrossers like it existed in the past [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011] [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011], while Quinn argued that many ships lost in time exist in the book and that the Fortunate Cloud may not be one of them [Gerry Quinn, 2011]. The thread summaries show the wider Father Inire, Hethor and Fortunate Cloud threads ending without agreement, with the Chinese reading of the name gaining some support but little textual argument.

For

  • Sidero's remark that all ships are one ship is cited as support for identifying the Quasar, the Fortunate Cloud and Tzadkiel's ship [Roy C. Lackey, 1999]
  • Quasar is argued to be an apt name for Tzadkiel's vessel, linked to a 1970s-80s science fiction trope of a quasar and black hole joining universes [Lee Berman, 2011]
  • Hethor is argued to be a shipmate of Jonas on the Quasar, supported by look-alikes aboard the ship and the Quasar as a white fountain [Lee Berman, 2011]
  • Jonas is argued to have been a crewman of the Fortunate Cloud, which explains his knowledge of the mirror transporter [Gerry Quinn, 2010]
  • Hethor is proposed as a former captain of the Quasar, with the Citadel described as an alternate version of that ship [b sharp, 2006]

Against

  • Alice Turner argued the two are different ships, and that the mundane ships she describes differ from Tzadkiel's unique vessel [Alice Turner, 1998]
  • C R Culver argued the ships may be different, since Wolfe may not have planned the Ship of Tzadkiel when writing the earlier books [C R Culver, 1998]
  • Sergei Soloviev argued that Quasar is space-opera naming and cannot be the name of Tzadkiel's vessel [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011]
  • Soloviev argued that Citadel ships are landers that cannot reach the stars, and that other starcrossers like Quasar likely existed [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011]
  • Quinn argued that many ships lost in time exist in the book and that the Fortunate Cloud may not be one of them [Gerry Quinn, 2011]

Where it stands: Still disputed and never settled: the same-ship reading was raised in 1998 and revived in 2011, but the list ended with posters split and no consensus.
