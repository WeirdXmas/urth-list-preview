The mandragora is linked to Typhon

The mandragora in the jar is related to Typhon or to Severian.

15 threads · 41 posts · for 23 / against 21 · first raised 1998-06-29 by Peter Stephenson
Main voices: Larry Miller (9), Lee Berman (8), Jerry Friedman (6), Gerry Quinn (6), Dave Tallman (4), Michael Andre-Driussi (2)

The debate

The earliest post in this record that bears on the mandragora is Peter Stephenson's 1998 proposal that the homunculus line, the mandragora and Eliot's Cumaean allusion link Severian's journey to artificial versus natural life [Peter Stephenson, 1998]; it does not yet name Typhon. Michael Andre-Driussi (1998) instead read the mandragora chamber as a sign of a stillborn twin of Severian [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998]. The Typhon link appears in 2008, when b sharp argued the mandragora is probably related to Typhon, citing telepathy shared by the two [b sharp, 2008]. Lee Berman then made it central in 2010, proposing that Typhon and the Mandragora are linked through telepathy, clairvoyance and cloning [Lee Berman, 2010], and Jane Delawney argued separately for a twin sister, with the mandragora as the twin [Jane Delawney, 2010].

In late September 2010 the discussion turned toward clones of Severian. Dave Tallman proposed the mandragora is a spare clone brother of Severian rather than a link to Typhon [Dave Tallman, 2010], and Anthony Davis raised a clone or embryo of Severian, admitting no textual evidence [Anthony Davis, 2010]. Tallman repeated the idea with kinship passages as support [Dave Tallman, 2010]. Ryan Dunn rejected the kinship clue and suggested the mandragora is Father Inire [Ryan Dunn, 2010], while Jeff Wilson and David Stockhoff took more skeptical views of the mandragora's abilities [Jeff Wilson, 2010] [David Stockhoff, 2010].

From late 2011 the Typhon reading dominated. Larry Miller proposed the mandragora was Typhon's discarded clone body [Larry Miller, 2011] and a proto-chem Typhon built to download into [Larry Miller, 2011], and Lee Berman argued Typhon and the mandragora were grown from black beans [Lee Berman, 2011]. Gerry Quinn disputed the proto-chem reading [Gerry Quinn, 2011] and argued the homunculus was no proof of Typhon's cloning, since the name was a standard term for homunculi [Gerry Quinn, 2011]. Jerry Friedman pressed etymology and folklore against any Typhon-only reading, citing alchemical homunculus lore [Jerry Friedman, 2011] and the chance nature of such word links [Jerry Friedman, 2011] [Jerry Friedman, 2011]. Gerry Quinn argued the mandragora predates the Autarchs [Gerry Quinn, 2011] and later rejected the suite-dating argument [Gerry Quinn, 2011]. In 2012 Lee Berman linked the Cumaean Sibyl to the Mandragora in a Lake of Birds discussion [Lee Berman, 2012] [Bruno de Albuquerque Furtado, 2012]. A 2023 post offered a mandrake-root-to-homunculus idea without textual support [Unknown, 2023].

For

  • Stephenson's homunculus and Cumaean allusions tie the mandragora to Severian's artificial-life theme [Peter Stephenson, 1998].
  • b sharp noted that Typhon and the Mandragora are the only telepathic characters [b sharp, 2008].
  • Lee Berman linked telepathy, clairvoyance and revival to a Typhon clone on Urth [Lee Berman, 2010].
  • Larry Miller cited Silk's possession of chems and Echidna's Prime Calcula to argue for a proto-chem Typhon built [Larry Miller, 2011].
  • Lee Berman argued the mandrake root and etymology point to Typhon as the maker of the homunculus [Lee Berman, 2011].

Against

  • Jeff Wilson argued the mandragora is an inert object that only echoes Severian's expectations [Jeff Wilson, 2010].
  • Ryan Dunn argued the mandragora may be Father Inire and that the kinship talk should be taken at face value [Ryan Dunn, 2010].
  • Gerry Quinn held the homunculus name is a standard term for homunculi and nothing ties the fetus to Typhon [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • Jerry Friedman argued mandrake folklore allows many connections and that a shared word does not prove a Typhon link [Jerry Friedman, 2011] [Jerry Friedman, 2011].
  • Gerry Quinn noted Typhon already occupies Piaton's body and never asks Severian about a homunculus [Gerry Quinn, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the Typhon link was argued repeatedly from 2008 to 2012 by Berman, Miller and others, with rival clone, twin and Inire readings, and the list never settled on one reading.
