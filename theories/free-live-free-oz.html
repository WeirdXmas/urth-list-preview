Free Live Free retells The Wizard of Oz

Free Live Free is a loose retelling of The Wonderful Wizard of Oz.

16 threads · 18 posts · for 9 / against 7 · first raised 1998-09-23 by Tony Ellis
Main voices: William H. Ansley (4), James Jordan (4), Michael Andre-Driussi (4), Tony Ellis (1), Mitchell A. Bailey (1), Nigel Price (1)

The debate

Tony Ellis first raised the Oz reading in the earliest record post, a 1998 question to David Wells that noted John Clute's view that the novel retells The Wonderful Wizard of Oz [Tony Ellis, 1998]. William H. Ansley then disputed the common view in February 1999, arguing the only clear Oz reference was a clown's parody song about a brain, heart and nerve [William H. Ansley, 1999], and in a follow-up he partly retracted this after recalling Little Ozzie singing 'We're Off to See the Wizard' [William H. Ansley, 1999].

The theory was developed mainly by James Jordan from January 2000 onward. He argued that the novel's house, hotel, asylum and High Country track Oz's journey and that the four main characters match the Seven Deadly Sins and Oz figures [James Jordan, 2001]. He also cited the name Glinda with an eye and the opening arrival at the house as a journey to Oz [James Jordan, 2000], and suggested a reader keep Oz in mind when reading the book [James Jordan, 2001]. Michael Andre-Driussi added a 1985 Clute review calling the novel a loose fantasia on the 1939 film [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2000].

The main opposition came from Michael Straight and Ansley. Straight argued the other characters' flaws are moral rather than lacks of courage, brains or heart, and proposed a general four-on-a-quest allusion instead [Michael Straight, 2000]. Ansley called the claim too vague to be informative [William H. Ansley, 2000] and disputed the later-book links, arguing that the film matters more than Baum's books, with the ruby slippers detail as his example [William H. Ansley, 2001]. The threads tended to end without a settled reading.

For

  • Clute is cited as holding that the novel retells The Wonderful Wizard of Oz [Tony Ellis, 1998].
  • Jordan maps the novel's locations and four main characters onto Oz's journey and the Seven Deadly Sins [James Jordan, 2001].
  • Jordan cites the Glinda name and the arrival at the house as a journey to Oz [James Jordan, 2000].
  • Andre-Driussi quotes Clute's 1985 review describing the book as a loose fantasia on the 1939 Oz film [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2000].
  • Ansley recalled that Little Ozzie sings 'We're Off to See the Wizard' in the book [William H. Ansley, 1999].

Against

  • Ansley argues the only clear Oz reference is a clown's parody song, with other resemblances too vague to notice [William H. Ansley, 1999].
  • Straight argues the characters' flaws are moral rather than lacks of courage, brains or heart [Michael Straight, 2000].
  • Ansley says the claim is vague enough to fit many books [William H. Ansley, 2000].
  • Ansley argues the Oz links to later books are looser and that the film matters more than the books [William H. Ansley, 2001].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the theory was raised in 1998 and supported mainly by Jordan, but Ansley and Straight contested it, and the threads ended without a settled reading.
