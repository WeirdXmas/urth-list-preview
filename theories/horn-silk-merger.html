Horn and Silk are merged

The narrator of the Short Sun, Horn, ends up in Silk's body (or Silk's mind in his), becoming a merged person ("Silkhorn", the Rajan).

178 threads · 261 posts · for 146 / against 126 · first raised 1999-10-04 by Dave Lebling
Main voices: Marc Aramini (36), Adam Stephanides (33), Michael Straight (22), Roy C. Lackey (21), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (15), James Wynn (14)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list in October 1999, when Dave Lebling suggested that Horn's mind is in Silk's body, citing Horn's beard and the boat's size on a book cover [Dave Lebling, 1999]. The same week Alice Turner proposed the same idea while admitting the reveal was far off [Alice Turner, 1999], and Alex David Groce argued that Silk and Horn are combined into one character, pointing to the "caught the ball" line [Alex David Groce, 1999].

In 2000 and 2001 the theory spread through many variants. William H. Ansley argued Horn's mind was transferred into Silk's discarded body, citing Horn's added height and bad ankle [William H. Ansley, 1999]. Tim Boolos proposed that Horn's spirit was loaded into Silk's body at Hyacinth's death [Tim Boolos, 2000], and Michael Andre-Driussi argued for a transfer by the Neighbors [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2000]. Adam Stephanides argued that the narrator is really a merged Silk-Horn spirit, citing Horn's failure to recognize he is in Silk's body [Adam Stephanides, 2001]. Dissent came from Ansley, who later doubted that Horn's spirit occupies Silk's body, citing Horn's voice blurring with Babbie's [William H. Ansley, 2000], and from David Wells, who argued Horn's spirit is only partly Horn's [David Wells, 2000]. Other posters argued for a Silk spirit leaving or a Horn spirit merging with Silk's, including Robert Borski [Robert Borski, 2001] and James Jordan, who held that Horn lives on in Silk's body [James Jordan, 2001].

From 2004 to 2013 the debate moved to the Short Sun books and to whether Silver Silk is downloaded into Pig. Roy C. Lackey cited a passage in which Silkhorn describes himself as nearly bald at thirty-five, treating it as a description of Horn's body [Roy C. Lackey, 2004]. Marc Aramini proposed a chain of transfers ending in a copy of Silk [Marc Aramini, 2005], and Stephanides rejected it on timing grounds because the bandage is still on during the glass scenes [Adam Stephanides, 2005]. Chris P argued that a three-entity reading is harder to sustain [Chris P, 2005]. In 2009 and 2010 James Wynn and Jordon Flato disputed the Horn-to-SilkHorn reading [James Wynn, 2009] [Jordon Flato, 2009], and Jane Delawney and Gerry Quinn proposed that a Neighbour moved Horn into Silk's body after Silk's death [Jane Delawney, 2010] [Gerry Quinn, 2010]. In 2013 Lee Berman argued that Silkhorn is a true blend of Silk and Horn, while Aramini argued they are one merged identity [Lee Berman, 2013] [Marc Aramini, 2013].

From 2018 to 2023 the theory was restated rather than resolved. Ab de Vos argued that Silk is Horn, since giving up Silk would kill Horn [Ab de Vos, 2018], and Aramini argued that Horn's spirit merges into Silk with the narration hiding it [Marc Aramini, 2018]. The last post, from 2023, argued that Babbie is Horn's spirit and that Horn and Silk are the same being [Unknown, 2023].

For

  • Lebling proposed that Horn's mind is in Silk's body, citing Horn's beard and the size of the boat on the cover [Dave Lebling, 1999].
  • Ansley argued that Horn's mind was transferred into Silk's discarded body, pointing to Horn's added height and bad ankle [William H. Ansley, 1999].
  • Stephanides argued that Horn fails to recognize he is in Silk's body and that his appearance changes on astral trips [Adam Stephanides, 2001].
  • Lackey cited a passage describing Silkhorn as nearly bald at thirty-five, treating it as a description of Horn's body [Roy C. Lackey, 2004].
  • Lee Berman argued that Silkhorn's insistence in old Viron and his killing of Jahlee show a true blend of Silk and Horn [Lee Berman, 2013].

Against

  • Ansley later doubted that Horn's spirit occupies Silk's body, citing the blurring of Horn's and Babbie's identities in On Blue's Waters [William H. Ansley, 2000].
  • Wells argued that Horn's spirit is only partly Horn's, so the spirit is not wholly in Silk's body [David Wells, 2000].
  • Stephanides rejected the Silver Silk download scenario because the bandage is still on during the glass scenes and Silk speaks through Pig later [Adam Stephanides, 2005].
  • Chris P argued that the thread used soul, spirit and identity loosely, so a three-entity reading is harder to sustain [Chris P, 2005].
  • Wynn argued that the Horn-to-SilkHorn reading is insufficient, and after a corrected printing said he would have to rewrite his greenbuck claim [James Wynn, 2009] [James Wynn, 2009].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never reached agreement on whether Horn's mind or spirit is in Silk's body, and the discussions in the Short Sun threads ended without resolution.
