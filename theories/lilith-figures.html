Lilith figures

Some of Wolfe's women (Jahi in Dr. Talos's play, Agia, Jane Doe) are versions of Lilith.

26 threads · 33 posts · for 22 / against 13 · first raised 1997-11-19 by Tony Ellis
Main voices: Roy C. Lackey (7), Michael Andre-Driussi (6), Michael Straight (4), Lee Berman (4), James Jordan (3), Adam Stephanides (3)

The debate

The theory was first raised on 1997-11-19 by Tony Ellis, who read Jahi in Dr. Talos's play as Lilith and treated the play as a retelling of Genesis and Severian's story [Tony Ellis, 1997]. Alice Turner then placed Jahi in a Zoroastrian creation frame, calling her a Lilith-like figure [Alice Turner, 1997], while Michael Andre-Driussi and C R Culver argued for Agia (and Dorcas) as Lilith and Eve [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997] [C R Culver, 1997]. Andre-Driussi later argued that Agia is Lilith while Jolenta only plays her [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997], and in 1998 Turner linked Lilith's legend to Babylonian Jahi [Alice Turner, 1998].

The theory widened in 2001 in the Copperhead discussion, where Roy C. Lackey argued that the naked woman, and later Jane Doe, is Lilith [Roy C. Lackey, 2001] [Roy C. Lackey, 2001]. Michael Andre-Driussi called Jane Doe an archetypal Lilith [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2001], and James Jordan argued that Lilith and Adam are unfallen parallel-world counterparts [James Jordan, 2001]. Michael Straight disputed the timeline, arguing that Lilith's presence conflicts with the Fall and Cain's murder [Michael Straight, 2001]. Andre-Driussi reported that Wolfe said he never finished LILITH and argued that Jane Doe has a Lilith aspect [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2001]. Kevin J. Maroney and Straight then argued over whether the Adam and Lilith pairing is Manichaean, a side question that did not settle whether the women are Lilith [Kevin J. Maroney, 2001] [Michael Straight, 2001].

Later posts moved to other characters and to the Jahi name. Jordan proposed Scylla as a Lilith-like mother figure in 2002 [James Jordan, 2002]. In 2010 Lee Berman argued that Jahi is a Lilith cognate in Zoroastrian lore [Lee Berman, 2010] [Lee Berman, 2010], while Jeff Wilson questioned whether Jahi is a demon or a Lilith figure, pointing to the play's casting of demons [Jeff Wilson, 2010]. Berman continued in 2012, linking Meschia, Meschiane and Jahi to Adam, Eve and Lilith in the Short Sun material [Lee Berman, 2012]. The theory was not settled, and the posts that followed mostly re-raised the same readings.

For

  • Jahi is Lilith, a reading Tony Ellis first put forward in 1997 [Tony Ellis, 1997].
  • Agia is Lilith and Jolenta only plays her, as Andre-Driussi argued [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997].
  • The naked woman in Copperhead is Lilith, and Jane Doe is an archetypal Lilith according to Lackey and Andre-Driussi [Roy C. Lackey, 2001] [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2001].
  • Jahi is a Lilith cognate in Zoroastrian tradition, which Berman argued in 2010 [Lee Berman, 2010].
  • Meschia, Meschiane and Jahi map onto Adam, Eve and Lilith in the Short Sun material, according to Berman [Lee Berman, 2012].

Against

  • Jahi is a demon rather than a Lilith figure, since the play casts other characters as demons, Wilson argued [Jeff Wilson, 2010].
  • Lilith's presence before the Fall conflicts with the murder of Cain, Straight argued [Michael Straight, 2001].
  • The Lilith reading of Copperhead's pre-Fall setting seems inelegant to Straight, who found both timeline explanations unconvincing [Michael Straight, 2001] [Michael Straight, 2001].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the Jahi and Agia readings were raised in 1997, the Copperhead debate of 2001 widened the theory without settling it, and the 2010 to 2014 posts repeated the same claims without consensus.
