The Green Man repays Severian across time

The Green Man comes from the future of Ushas and repays Severian's gift of half a whetstone by intervening at another point in Severian's life.

21 threads · 27 posts · for 17 / against 9 · first raised 1998-01-22 by Michael Andre-Driussi
Main voices: Jeff Wilson (4), Roy C. Lackey (4), Marc Aramini (3), Lee Berman (3), Michael Andre-Driussi (2), Stone Ox (2)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by Michael Andre-Driussi in January 1998, when he offered a personal view that the Green Man and Master Ash were later far-future helpers [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998]. The specific repayment version came in May 1999, when he argued that the Green Man's half-whetstone exchange was unequal and that he therefore searches through time for a moment when Severian is captive [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1999]. Peter Stephenson answered that the Green Man's 'above ground' remark for Agia is a generic oracle's platitude rather than real foresight [Peter Stephenson, 1999].

From 2003 to 2007 the discussion turned to mechanics. Stone Ox proposed a time loop in which the restitution must happen before Severian frees the Green Man [Stone Ox, 2003], then asked in December 2007 whether a Green Man wandering through time could have freed the first Severian in the mausoleum episode [Stone Ox, 2007]. Jeff Wilson reversed the idea, suggesting the Green Man lurked in the Gyoll to derail Severian [Jeff Wilson, 2007], while Roy C. Lackey placed him as a far-future child of the New Sun who rescued Severian in the northern jungle [Roy C. Lackey, 2007].

From 2008 onward most posts addressed the Green Man's origin rather than the repayment. Lackey argued he descended from Ushas's new peoples and came from the Ushas future [Roy C. Lackey, 2008] [Roy C. Lackey, 2008], while Tony Ellis argued that the Green Man future does not produce the Hierogrammates [Tony Ellis, 2008]. Lee Berman then read the Green Man as a constructed disguise and a liar working toward a destructive future [Lee Berman, 2011], and Gerry Quinn held that he travels the Corridors of Time on foot [Gerry Quinn, 2011]. Later Jeff Wilson and Lee Berman discussed reverse time travel and multiple branchings as ways for the Green Man to reach earlier points [Jeff Wilson, 2014] [Lee Berman, 2014].

For

  • Andre-Driussi argued that the unequal whetstone exchange drives the Green Man to search through time for a moment when Severian is captive, so he can repay him [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1999].
  • Stone Ox proposed that the Green Man's restitution occurs in a time loop before Severian frees him, and suggested a green face in the antechamber is him seeking that chance [Stone Ox, 2003].
  • Stone Ox asked whether the Green Man, moving through time to free a captive Severian, could have freed the first Severian in the mausoleum episode [Stone Ox, 2007].
  • Lackey described the Green Man as a far-future being who rescued Severian in the northern jungle, which he cited as intervention across time [Roy C. Lackey, 2007].
  • Wilson argued that the Green Man's reverse time travel up and down the Corridors lets Severian reach branches that were previously ruled out [Jeff Wilson, 2014].

Against

  • Stephenson argued the Green Man's prophecy is a generic platitude, not foresight into particular lives, which weakens any claim that he targets specific moments [Peter Stephenson, 1999].
  • Wilson proposed that the Green Man lurked in the Gyoll to derail Severian rather than save him, a reading opposed to repayment [Jeff Wilson, 2007].
  • Ellis argued the Green Man future does not produce the Hierogrammates, since Severian's own path leads to a different future [Tony Ellis, 2008].
  • Lackey argued the books show only two possible futures, Ragnarok and Ushas, with no evidence of multiple futures after the white hole arrives [Roy C. Lackey, 2008].
  • Berman argued the Green Man is a self-admitted liar and disguise, a trick to make Severian favour a future that destroys humanity [Lee Berman, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed: it was raised by Andre-Driussi in 1999 and revisited in a few later threads, but most posters moved to debating the Green Man's origin, nature and mobility, and no consensus on repayment across time was reached.
