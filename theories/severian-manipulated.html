Severian is manipulated by outside powers

Severian's life is arranged by outside forces (the Hierogrammates, Father Inire, the Vodalarii, the megatherians), so his choices are less free than they seem.

48 threads · 68 posts · for 40 / against 26 · first raised 1998-01-29 by David Wells
Main voices: Son of Witz (12), Ryan Dunn (9), Craig Brewer (5), Lee Berman (5), Roy C. Lackey (4), Chris P (4)

The debate

The idea that outside forces arrange Severian's life was first raised by David Wells in 1998, who argued that free will looks illusory in his life and grounded this in episodes such as the Undine rescue, the Citadel gun shot and the avern duel [David Wells, 1998]. Roy C. Lackey extended it in 1999 by reading Inire as an instrument of divine justice and Severian as a dupe of the Increate [Roy C. Lackey, 1999]. Ori Kowarsky disputed this by treating the hierodules as material, fallible manipulators rather than angels [Ori Kowarsky, 1999], and Lackey later described Severian as a self-deceiving but roughly accurate narrator [Roy C. Lackey, 1999].

From 2005 to 2008 the theory was pursued through several specific mechanisms. Alan Lewis argued Severian was engineered to make his choice [Alan Lewis, 2005], and Chris P proposed Hierogrammate-engineered embryos [Chris P, 2005] before disputing total manipulation [Chris P, 2006]. Dave Tallman rejected the genetic-puppet reading because it empties the epic of point [Dave Tallman, 2008]. Jeff Wilson proposed Inire steers Severian through the Houses [Jeff Wilson, 2008], while Son of Witz argued Severian is steered almost completely by a higher hand, a claim that became the centre of the long Christ-figure debates involving Craig Brewer and John Watkins [Son of Witz, 2008][Craig Brewer, 2008][John Watkins, 2008]. In 2010 to 2011 Ryan Dunn pressed a designed puppet-master plan [Ryan Dunn, 2010][Ryan Dunn, 2011], Lee Berman argued that opposing forces watch and steer Severian [Lee Berman, 2010], and Gerry Quinn and Jane Delawney disputed the reading that Hierodules control him [Gerry Quinn, 2011][Jane Delawney, 2010]. Peter Wright's book Attending Daedalus, which reads manipulation as the core of the New Sun, was a recurring focus [b sharp, 2006].

From 2011 onward the discussion narrowed to Inire, resurrection and chess imagery. Lee Berman argued Inire guided Severian to Dorcas's body [Lee Berman, 2013] and that Severian is a pawn promoted after Yesod, a reading Robert Pirkola supported [Lee Berman, 2014][Robert Pirkola, 2014]. Jerry Friedman repeatedly asked for concrete examples of manipulation beyond the obvious ones [Jerry Friedman, 2011], and Lee Berman's Inire arguments were challenged for over-reading [Roy C. Lackey, 2010]. Overall, most threads, including those on Inire, Watchman, Ushas and the Christ figure, ended in impasse without consensus.

For

  • David Wells argued that Severian's life is steered by outside forces, making free will look illusory [David Wells, 1998].
  • Son of Witz argued Severian is steered almost completely by a higher hand, citing the many coincidental encounters in the book [Son of Witz, 2008].
  • Ryan Dunn argued Severian's journey is a designed master plan with a hidden puppet master, citing dream puppet shows and Dr. Talos's play [Ryan Dunn, 2010].
  • Lee Berman argued that opposing forces watch and steer Severian, citing the avern duel and resurrection episodes [Lee Berman, 2010].
  • Lee Berman argued Inire guided Severian to Dorcas's body, citing the finger-pinching scene with Tzadkiel and the necromancers Ceryx and the Cumaean [Lee Berman, 2013].

Against

  • Chris P argued that manipulation removes free will and makes the story uninteresting, pointing to false religions in the Long Sun books [Chris P, 2007].
  • Dave Tallman argued that engineering Severian as a genetic puppet makes the epic pointless and pointed to foreshadowed side effects [Dave Tallman, 2008].
  • Ori Kowarsky argued the hierodules are material, fallible manipulators rather than angels [Ori Kowarsky, 1999].
  • Gerry Quinn argued that a reading making everyone a puppet is uninteresting and diminishes Dorcas and the visible story [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • Roy C. Lackey argued against Inire manipulating Severian across parallel timelines by asking how the deeds fit his known whereabouts [Roy C. Lackey, 2010].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the idea was raised in 1998, developed through many threads, and never won consensus, with most discussions ending in impasse.
