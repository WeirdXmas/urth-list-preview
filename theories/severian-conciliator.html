Severian is the Conciliator

The Conciliator of legend is Severian himself, sent back in time; the Claw's power and the Conciliator's cult come from him in a causal loop.

86 threads · 109 posts · for 68 / against 39 · first raised 1997-04-07 by Doug Eigsti
Main voices: Son of Witz (15), Michael Straight (9), Marc Aramini (9), James Jordan (7), Jordon Flato (7), Lee Berman (5)

The debate

The theory that Severian is the Conciliator, sent back in time, first appears on the list in 1997 when Doug Eigsti disputes it, arguing the Outsider is the supreme Creator while Severian is at most a Conciliator [Doug Eigsti, 1997]. Through 1997 and 1998 the debate on the early Urth list is mostly a dispute about whether Severian is a Christ figure or the Conciliator at all, with James Jordan, C R Culver and Alice Turner replying point by point [James Jordan, 1997] [C R Culver, 1998] [Alice Turner, 1997].

In June 1998 the question turns to textual clues. Peter Grimes argues the healing powers come from Severian himself, and John Moar argues the volume titles mark Severian rather than the Claw, which adam louis stephanides supports [Peter Grimes, 1998] [John Moar, 1998] [adam louis stephanides, 1998]. Dan Rabin's reading of the prayer passages is disputed by Michael Straight, who reads them as ordinary prayer, while Tony Ellis argues from "The Key to the Universe" that the Conciliator and New Sun are one figure [Dan Rabin, 1998] [Michael Straight, 1998] [Tony Ellis, 1998]. In 2003 Marc Aramini and Josh Geller argue Typhon recognizes Severian as the Conciliator, while Benjamin Martin doubts the text supports the link at all [Marc Aramini, 2003] [Benjamin Martin, 2003] [Josh Geller, 2003].

From 2008 onward the theory becomes a time-loop argument. Jordon Flato, Son of Witz and Marc Aramini propose that Severian and the Conciliator form a causal loop, with Son of Witz asking which came first [Jordon Flato, 2008] [Son of Witz, 2008] [Marc Aramini, 2008]. Opponents such as Roy C. Lackey, Dave Lebling and Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argue Severian is only Christ-like or a conciliator between Man and the Hierogrammates [Roy C. Lackey, 2009] [Dave Lebling, 2008] [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2007]. Lee Berman's 2010 argument that Severian is the Theoanthropos and Conciliator, and Andrew Mason's 2012 claim that the Conciliator is Severian from the start, keep the question open [Lee Berman, 2010] [Andrew Mason, 2012].

For

  • Typhon's recognition of Severian and the New Sun's kneeling is read by Marc Aramini as showing Typhon knows Severian is the Conciliator [Marc Aramini, 2003] [Marc Aramini, 2003].
  • The volume titles of The Book of the New Sun follow a torturer, lictor, autarch pattern that marks Severian and not the Claw, according to John Moar and adam louis stephanides [John Moar, 1998] [adam louis stephanides, 1998].
  • The Dark Sun image of the fuliginous cloak and Agia's remarks about time foreshadow Severian as Conciliator, per Marc Aramini [Marc Aramini, 2003].
  • Severian's healing powers and the Claw's time-reversing power come from Severian himself, a point Peter Grimes and Ryan Dunn make from the Triskele episode [Peter Grimes, 1998] [Ryan Dunn, 2010].
  • The Conciliator is Severian from the start, citing Typhon's recognition and the Claw at the end of the book, as argued by Andrew Mason [Andrew Mason, 2012].

Against

  • Michael Straight reads Conciliator passages as Wolfe's coy way of referring to Jesus, not to Severian [Michael Straight, 1999].
  • Tony Ellis argues Severian's Earth shares our history, so a Christ figure must exist there too, not Severian [Tony Ellis, 1999].
  • Benjamin Martin doubts the text gives any evidence Severian is the Conciliator and suspects Wolfe devised the link later [Benjamin Martin, 2003].
  • Dave Lebling argues Severian is only a Christ-like figure because the Conciliator is Christ, and that nuns predate his time [Dave Lebling, 2008].
  • Lisa Schaffer-Doggett argues the Cumaen sent Apu-Punchau into the future and that an earlier failed Severian, possibly Ymar, was the original figure [Lisa Schaffer-Doggett, 2003] [Lisa Schaffer-Doggett, 2003].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the theory was argued by many posters across two decades, but the list never reached agreement, and the strongest opposition came from readers who saw Severian as Christ-like rather than the Conciliator.

Written by claude-haiku-5-5 from the cards of 90 posts.

Evidence usually cited

  • In The Urth of the New Sun Severian travels to the distant past and performs the deeds later attributed to the Conciliator.
  • The Claw's miracles track Severian's own presence and need.
