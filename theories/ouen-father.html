Ouen is Severian's father

Ouen, the waiter at the Inn of Lost Loves, is Severian's father (and Dorcas's son).

62 threads · 88 posts · for 63 / against 28 · first raised 1997-02-07 by Michael Andre-Driussi
Main voices: Lee Berman (16), James Wynn (12), Roy C. Lackey (7), Ryan Dunn (7), David Stockhoff (6), Michael Andre-Driussi (5)

The debate

The theory was first raised on 1998-01-04 by urth-errors, who answered a question about Severian's father by naming Ouen, the waiter at the Inn of the Lost Loves, and citing pages 305-306 of the hardcover Citadel of the Autarch [urth-errors, 1998]. Michael Andre-Driussi followed within days, proposing Cath as Severian's mother and Merryn as his twin, and building on the Inn revelation [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998]. Robert Borski later argued that a balding, blue-eyed figure in Citadel is Ouen, citing Severian's physical description and the Welsh form of Ouen as Eugene [Robert Borski, 1999]. Roy C. Lackey and Andre-Driussi backed this in late 1999, with Lackey pointing to Citadel's recognition of Ouen and a breastfeeding memory in Claw [Roy C. Lackey, 1999][Michael Andre-Driussi, 1999].

In 1999 Borski also objected to Roy Lackey's family tree on timing grounds: Dorcas would have to bear a child that Severian fathers decades later, and Severian is absent for forty years [Robert Borski, 1999]. The 2005 list poll thread saw Jim Raylor question Borski's Ouen basis and propose a poll, while the Wynns put forward Typhon and Piaton as candidates [Jim Raylor, 2005][James Wynn, 2005]. Alan Lewis backed Ouen with the Citadel line about literacy [Alan Lewis, 2005], and Sarah Dorrance-Minch asked for the rationale [Sarah Dorrance-Minch, 2005]. From 2008 to 2010 the theory was argued most heavily: Henry Eissler and Roy Lackey defended Ouen as father against a mounted-guard reading [Henry Eissler, 2008][Roy C. Lackey, 2008], while Jack Smith and Ryan Dunn supported Dorcas and the boatman as grandparents [Jack Smith, 2010][Ryan Dunn, 2010]. Jane Delawney and Andrew Mason argued over whether Ouen already knows Dorcas is his mother [Jane Delawney, 2010][Andrew Mason, 2010].

Opponents included Don Doggett, who rejected Ouen as father in 2002 [Don Doggett, 2002], and Lisa Schaffer-Doggett, who suggested Dorcas as mother or sister [Lisa Schaffer-Doggett, 2003]. James Wynn disputed the Dorcas and Ouen links repeatedly, arguing the text does not confirm them [James Wynn, 2003][James Wynn, 2010]. Andrew Mason called the Ouen-Severian link a mistake rather than a trail [Andrew Mason, 2010]. Lee Berman proposed reading the unresolved paternity through myth [Lee Berman, 2010], and in 2013 proposed that the vizier Father fathered Ouen [Lee Berman, 2013]. The theory was never settled; the list moved to rival candidates and the question stayed open.

For

  • Borski cited Severian's description of a balding, blue-eyed man and the Welsh form of Ouen as Eugene as evidence that Ouen is the father [Robert Borski, 1999].
  • Alan Lewis cited a Citadel line in which Ouen admits he can read and write despite seeming dull as a clue to his importance [Alan Lewis, 2005].
  • Urth-errors and Ryan Dunn relied on the Inn of the Lost Loves revelation and Ouen's locket and resemblance to Severian [urth-errors, 1998][Ryan Dunn, 2010].
  • Ryan Dunn argued Severian shares the profile Ouen is described as having, and linked this to the Matachin Tower account of pregnant prisoners [Ryan Dunn, 2010].
  • Jack Smith cited the Inn note calling Dorcas 'my mother come again' and the boatman's mention of his dead wife Cas [Jack Smith, 2010].

Against

  • Borski argued that timing problems, with Dorcas bearing a child that Severian fathers decades later, undermine Roy Lackey's genealogy [Robert Borski, 1999].
  • James Wynn argued the text does not confirm Ouen as Severian's father and that Catherine may have borne other children [James Wynn, 2010].
  • Andrew Mason called the Ouen-Severian link a mistake and offered Severian's twinhood and time journeys as simpler answers [Andrew Mason, 2010].
  • Son of Witz noted Ouen's balding and age clash with Severian's youth when floating Ouen as a brother instead [Son of Witz, 2010].
  • Don Doggett rejected Ouen as father on incest and genealogy grounds and proposed Typhon-linked kinship instead [Don Doggett, 2002].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the theory was raised in 1998 and widely discussed through 2010, but the list never reached agreement and later posts offered rival candidates such as Typhon, Piaton and the vizier Father.
