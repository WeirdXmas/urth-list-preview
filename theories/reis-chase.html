Reis and Gideon Chase

The identities of Bill Reis and Gideon Chase blur (one impersonates the other; Reis becomes the volcano god; Chase is one of the evil guests).

35 threads · 83 posts · for 54 / against 27 · first raised 2008-09-25 by Greg Bates
Main voices: Dave Tallman (33), Roy C. Lackey (16), James Wynn (14), Mo Holkar / UKG (3), John Watkins (3), Nigel Price (3)

The debate

The idea that Reis and Gideon Chase blur into one another surfaces first on the list in Greg Bates's 2008 post that treats Chase as one of the evil guests [Greg Bates, 2008], and Dave Tallman's December 2008 proposal that the Volcano God is Reis's spirit [Dave Tallman, 2008] is an early version of the Reis-as-volcano-god strand. Tallman also argued that Chase knew of Reis's scheme and let it unfold [Dave Tallman, 2009], while Roy C. Lackey argued that Gideon engineered Reis's downfall and set him up as bait [Roy C. Lackey, 2009]. Lackey's objection that Tallman's reading of the Reis-Gideon link leaves Reis's plan unexplained was met by Tallman's claim that Reis's own megalomania caused his troubles [Dave Tallman, 2009].

The identity-swap strand became concrete in September 2009, when James Wynn proposed that the man killed on the island was Gideon Chase in disguise, so that Reis was the one who later went to Woldercon [James Wynn, 2009]. Wynn then argued that Chase impersonated Reis, citing Reis's missing son and the Pygmalion parallel [James Wynn, 2009], and that Reis might be dead with Chase as the impostor [James Wynn, 2009]. Lackey rejected each version on physical and textual grounds, including body mass, a missing prosthetic leg, Cassie's grief, and a talking picture on the book's second page [Roy C. Lackey, 2009] [Roy C. Lackey, 2009]. Wynn kept insisting the theory was a hypothesis rather than a conclusion [James Wynn, 2009], and the Dragon*Con thread ended without agreement.

Later posters revived the link with different arguments. Wynn proposed that Reis, Gideon Chase and King Kanoa might be one figure [James Wynn, 2010], which Gwern Branwen disputed on economic and motive grounds [Gwern Branwen, 2010]. In 2011 Daniel Petersen supported the idea that Gideon and Bill are one person, noting they never appear together [Daniel Petersen, 2011]. In 2018 Robert Pirkola questioned Eric Bourland's claim that Gideon is Gene Wolfe [Robert Pirkola, 2018], and in 2019 Marc Aramini announced a Perseus-based reading that casts Gideon and Reis as destroyer and gold-bearing figures who become friends [Marc Aramini, 2019].

For

  • Wynn argued the dead man was Chase in disguise, which would make Reis the one who later went to Woldercon [James Wynn, 2009].
  • Wynn argued Chase impersonated Reis, citing Reis's missing son, Chase's unexplained absence afterward, and a Pygmalion parallel [James Wynn, 2009].
  • Tallman argued Chase knew of Reis's scheme and did nothing for Cassie, which makes him look more sinister [Dave Tallman, 2009].
  • Lackey argued Gideon secretly set Reis up as a trap, recruiting Cassie as bait [Roy C. Lackey, 2009].
  • Petersen argued that Gideon and Bill are one person because the two never appear together, unlike Margaret and Cassie [Daniel Petersen, 2011].

Against

  • Lackey argued Reis is too big to be Gideon's disguise, Chase's missing leg would show, and shape-changers lose control at death [Roy C. Lackey, 2009].
  • Lackey argued Reis had no need to impersonate Gideon, since his fortune could buy passage to Woldercan [Roy C. Lackey, 2009].
  • Lackey argued that a talking picture on the book's second page shows a media report of the ambassador's return, which rules out the disguise [Roy C. Lackey, 2009].
  • Tallman argued Reis's reckless choices, not Chase's plan, caused the trouble with hiding Cassie near the island [Dave Tallman, 2009].
  • Lackey argued Reis's wedding plans on the island show he did not die there, and that the Reis-Gideon swap is unnecessary [Roy C. Lackey, 2009].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the Reis-Gideon identity swap was argued across 2008 to 2019 without consensus, with Lackey and Wynn the main opponents and later posters only restating the reading.
