Stubb's sin

Stubb's main temptation in Free Live Free is pride rather than envy.

4 threads · 5 posts · for 4 / against 1 · first raised 2000-01-26 by Michael Straight
Main voices: Michael Straight (3), Marc Aramini (1), Michael Andre-Driussi (1)

The debate

The pride reading of Stubb was first raised by Michael Straight in January 2000, in a post that proposed Stubb's main temptation as pride while mostly arguing time-travel questions [Michael Straight, 2000]. The idea returned in April 2001, when Marc Aramini answered a recollection of a Wolfe interview that named Stubb's sin as envy and argued that Stubb embodies envy mixed with pride [Marc Aramini, 2001]. The same week Straight took up the question directly, arguing that Stubb's temptation appeals to a wish to seem clever and essential, so it fits Pride rather than Envy, and testing each character's temptation against the Seven Deadly Sins [Michael Straight, 2001].

The discussion turned on whether the deadly sins overlap. The following day Straight argued that the sins in the novel overlap, so Stubb's temptation may show Pride rather than Envy, and that Serpentina's humbled speech on human power corrects her own pride [Michael Straight, 2001]. The posts in this period were largely a response to the interview clipping, which the threads report as assigning Stubb to envy and Serpentina to pride, and the clipping's status as authorial intent was disputed among posters. The thread on the clipping stayed short and ended without resolution, with James Jordan finding Stubb's envy hard to see and Straight disputing the neat mapping.

The broader Free Live Free thread asked whether the Quadrumvirate maps onto the seven deadly sins, but it was mostly taken up with time-travel mechanics and an Oz reading. In December 2002 Michael Andre-Driussi proposed that Stubb is the Tin Woodman in an Oz reading, a different casting that does not address the pride-versus-envy question [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2002]. The Oz thread was contested, and the sins question was not revisited on the list.

For

  • Stubb's temptation appeals to his wish to seem clever and essential, which Straight took to fit pride rather than envy [Michael Straight, 2001].
  • Because the sins overlap, Stubb's temptation may show pride rather than envy, as Straight argued [Michael Straight, 2001].
  • Straight's first post proposed pride as Stubb's main temptation, in a thread otherwise devoted to time travel [Michael Straight, 2000].

Against

  • Aramini argued that Stubb embodies envy mixed with pride, not pride alone [Marc Aramini, 2001].
  • Aramini answered a recollection that Wolfe named Stubb's sin as envy, which supports the envy reading [Marc Aramini, 2001].
  • Straight's own argument rests on his reading of the sins overlapping, which the record does not show Wolfe endorsing [Michael Straight, 2001].

Where it stands: Still disputed and unresolved: pride was proposed by Straight and envy was defended by Aramini and by the recalled interview, and the threads ended without consensus.
