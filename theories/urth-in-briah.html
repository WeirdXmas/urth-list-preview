Our Earth is Urth's past (or not)

Urth is our Earth in the far future and our universe is Briah (or an earlier Briah); the counter-reading puts Earth in another cycle.

96 threads · 143 posts · for 75 / against 75 · first raised 1997-04-16 by James Jordan
Main voices: Lee Berman (42), David Stockhoff (16), b sharp (15), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (8), António Pedro Marques (7), Son of Witz (6)

The debate

The theory was first raised by James Jordan in April 1997, who argued that Urth is Earth in a future oscillation of an cyclic universe, with Urth/Briah preceding our present cycle [James Jordan, 1997]. Early posts mostly came at it from other directions: Kevin Maroney argued in 1999 that Urth is our Earth in the far future, citing the Terminus Est inscription and the Aldrin photograph [Kevin J. Maroney, 1999], and Matthew Malthouse argued in 2002 that the internal evidence places the books in Earth's very far future [Matthew Malthouse, 2002].

From 2006 the debate sharpened around the past-or-future question. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued that Urth is Earth in a previous cosmic cycle, billions of years older, with the changed spelling marking the difference [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2006], and Roy C. Lackey argued that Earth is in Urth's past, citing Severian's remark that ancient peoples were wiser [Roy C. Lackey, 2006]. b sharp moved between readings, first placing Earth's past in Yesod [b sharp, 2006] and then arguing that Earth lies in Urth's future [b sharp, 2006]. In December 2008 a long run of posts followed a Wolfe interview describing Urth as an earlier cycle: Jeff Wilson called that claim retroactive continuity tied to the Noah covenant [Jeff Wilson, 2008], while David Stockhoff pointed to the translator's note in The Book of the New Sun as saying the manuscript comes from the future [David Stockhoff, 2008]. Pedro Pereira argued that Urth is simply our Earth in the future [Pedro Pereira, 2008].

The 2010 to 2014 threads brought in more models. Lee Berman argued repeatedly that Urth is a past iteration of Earth, citing the Norse norn name and the South American geography [Lee Berman, 2010] [Lee Berman, 2012], and later proposed a spiral of adjacent universes [Lee Berman, 2010]. Cliff Judge proposed that Yesod is a future cycle of our universe [Cliff Judge, 2010]. Craig Brewer asked for textual support for the far-future reading [Craig Brewer, 2012], and Dan'l Danehy-Oakes replied that Wolfe is evasive but does not lie [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2012]. Marc Aramini argued that the name Urth is a phonetic pun on Earth [Marc Aramini, 2014], and Berman replied by citing a Jordan interview calling Urth a long past universe [Lee Berman, 2014]. The 2020 post on the compass convention is off-topic to the theory [Norwood, Frederick Hudson, 2020].

For

  • James Jordan argued that Urth is Earth in a future oscillation, with Urth/Briah preceding our cycle, to explain how the manuscript survives [James Jordan, 1997].
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued that Urth is Earth in a previous cycle, a billion or more years older, with the spelling change marking it as not our Earth [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2006].
  • Roy C. Lackey argued that Earth is in Urth's past, citing Severian's line about ancient peoples being wiser and the brown book's title [Roy C. Lackey, 2006].
  • Kevin Maroney argued that Urth is our Earth in the far future, citing the Terminus Est inscription and the Aldrin photograph [Kevin J. Maroney, 1999].
  • Lee Berman argued that Urth is a past iteration of Earth, citing the Norse norn name and South American geography [Lee Berman, 2010] [Lee Berman, 2012].

Against

  • Pedro Pereira argued that Urth is the same universe as Earth, relying on Wolfe's remark that he does not remember how much time has passed [Pedro Pereira, 2008] [Pedro Pereira, 2008].
  • David Stockhoff argued that the translator's note signed by Wolfe says the manuscript comes from the future, which conflicts with the claim of an earlier cycle [David Stockhoff, 2008].
  • David Stockhoff cited the lunar module photograph as the plainest problem for the later-universe reading [David Stockhoff, 2008].
  • Jeff Wilson argued that the claim of an earlier cycle is likely retroactive continuity, added after drowning Urth would break God's promise to Noah [Jeff Wilson, 2008].
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued that Wolfe is tricky and evasive in interviews but does not outright lie, so the future reading need not assume a lie [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2012].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never reached agreement, and the past-cycle and future-Earth camps kept restating their positions across the 2006, 2008, 2010 and 2012 to 2014 threads.
