Valeria is related to Catherine

Valeria, Severian's consort, is Catherine's mother (or her clone), which makes her a close relative of Severian.

16 threads · 20 posts · for 14 / against 8 · first raised 1998-10-09 by Robert Borski
Main voices: Roy C. Lackey (5), b sharp (4), Lee Berman (4), Robert Borski (3), Dave Tallman (2), Kieran Mullen (1)

The debate

The theory was first raised on 1998-10-09 by Robert Borski [Robert Borski, 1998], who argued from Ouen's testimony in The Citadel of the Autarch that Catherine was already fleeing the Pelerines when she met Ouen and that Valeria is her mother. The same day he went further, proposing Catherine as the daughter of Valeria and her second husband Caesidius, drawing on Eata's remark about Caesidius's looks, Ouen's comment on an old-fashioned name, and Catherine's access to the Corridors of Time [Robert Borski, 1998]. Kieran Mullen objected that this would make Severian's two lovers grandmother and granddaughter and raised a time problem [Kieran Mullen, 1998], and Charles Dye pressed the timeline, noting Valeria is younger than Catherine when Severian meets both and marries long after Catherine's confinement [Charles Dye, 1998]. Borski repeated the Caesidius line in 1998 [Robert Borski, 1998].

The idea returned in 2008, when Dave Tallman proposed Catherine as Valeria's daughter by her second husband, citing a Path of Air glimpse and a resemblance between two Chatelaines, while conceding problems [Dave Tallman, 2008]. b sharp then reversed the direction, making Catherine the mother of Valeria and proposing a Catherine clone in the pale-gown corpse [b sharp, 2008]. Roy C. Lackey rejected this, arguing that Severian would have recognized his own mother living in Valeria's tower [Roy C. Lackey, 2008], and later argued that Ouen was Valeria's only possible father match, so her father must be someone else [Roy C. Lackey, 2008]. Discussion then shifted to Valeria as a clone of Catherine, which b sharp argued from her youthful look and name-pairing [b sharp, 2008][b sharp, 2008], and to b sharp's reading of the loggia woman as a Catherine-linked figure [b sharp, 2008]. Tallman later proposed Valeria as Severian's mother through a Catherine embryo, linked to Typhon's era [Dave Tallman, 2008].

Later posters mostly treated the family links as one option among many. Son of Witz argued that the bloodline puzzles were secondary to the book's vertical and transcendental concerns [Son of Witz, 2008]. Marques suggested Valeria Severa and a possible Wolfe-family link [António Pedro Marques, 2010]. Lee Berman in 2011 cited Valeria's unexplained barrenness and possible kinship with Catherine, noted by Andre-Driussi and Borski, within an anti-Oedipus reading [Lee Berman, 2011], and later tied Valeria to Witches' saint lore and to Catherine's staged death [Lee Berman, 2011][David Stockhoff, 2011][Lee Berman, 2011]. The list never settled the question.

For

  • Borski argued from Ouen's testimony that Catherine was a fugitive who would accept shelter, placing Valeria's connection in the Ouen episode [Robert Borski, 1998].
  • Borski cited Caesidius's looks, Ouen's comment on an old-fashioned name, and Catherine's access to the Corridors of Time to support Caesidius as Catherine's father [Robert Borski, 1998].
  • Tallman built his daughter-by-second-husband case on a Path of Air glimpse and a resemblance between two Chatelaines [Dave Tallman, 2008].
  • b sharp grounded the Catherine-as-mother reading in the old-fashioned names and clothes, the corpse's face seen in Ultan's, and the tribade remark before the Thecla feast [b sharp, 2008].
  • Berman pointed to Valeria's unexplained barrenness and possible kinship with Catherine noted by others as support for a family link [Lee Berman, 2011].

Against

  • Mullen objected that the theory makes Severian's two lovers grandmother and granddaughter and that the timing does not work [Kieran Mullen, 1998].
  • Dye argued from the timeline that Valeria is younger than Catherine when Severian meets both and marries long after Catherine's confinement [Charles Dye, 1998].
  • Lackey argued that Catherine living in Valeria's tower would mean Severian met his mother and never recognized her [Roy C. Lackey, 2008].
  • Lackey argued that Ouen is Valeria's only possible father match, so Valeria's father must be someone else [Roy C. Lackey, 2008].
  • Son of Witz argued that the bloodline puzzles are mere details beside the book's vertical, transcendental meaning [Son of Witz, 2008].

Where it stands: Still disputed and never resolved: the list raised the Valeria-Catherine kinship repeatedly from 1998 to 2011, with mother, daughter, and clone variants argued, and the mother-of-Catherine reading was contested by Mullen and Lackey.
