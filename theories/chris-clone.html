Chris is a clone of himself

Chris, the narrator of Pirate Freedom, is a clone or his own father through time travel.

12 threads · 16 posts · for 8 / against 7 · first raised 2007-11-30 by thalassocrat
Main voices: Jerry Friedman (5), thalassocrat (3), Matthew Keeley (2), Andrew Bollen (2), Fernando Q. Gouvea (1), Henry Eissler (1)

The debate

The clone idea for Chris in Pirate Freedom was first raised by thalassocrat on 2007-11-30 [thalassocrat, 2007], who pointed to the father's remark about engineering Chris's height, the absence of any mother, and a parallel with the clone-training father in The Fifth Head of Cerberus. Within weeks thalassocrat also argued that Burt could be the father and that Chris might be his own father through Ignacio [thalassocrat, 2007], while Fernando Q. Gouvea disputed the whole reading, arguing that Chris is a flawed 18th-century man and that Wolfe avoids time-travel paradoxes [Fernando Q. Gouvea, 2007]. Matthew Keeley's questions about priesthood, the beating and whether Chris could erase himself [Matthew Keeley, 2008] fed a long run of speculation in which the clone reading stayed unresolved.

The theory returned in 2010 and 2011 when Jerry Friedman proposed Chris as a possible clone, linking it to the 666 Saltimbanque Street address and to the Antichrist idea for the Fifth Head narrator [Jerry Friedman, 2010][Jerry Friedman, 2011]. The 2012 cluster is the main turning point: Friedman disputed the genetics of Chris being his own child with Novia [Jerry Friedman, 2012], Andrew Bollen first argued Chris is his own father [Andrew Bollen, 2012] and then weighed cloning against a hybrid option [Andrew Bollen, 2012], and Dave Tallman argued the bootstrap paradox ruled out self-fatherhood and named Burt as the likely father [Dave Tallman, 2012]. Gwern Branwen then argued that the cloning and 'made man' evidence was unconvincing and that the timeslip was poorly handled [Gwern Branwen, 2012], and Friedman answered that the evidence was weak and that an alternate-universe reading was possible [Jerry Friedman, 2012].

Across the record the clone reading was defended mainly by its proposers, with Paul Witcover answering only that the other person is Chris himself and Matthew Groves noting that the missing mother is suggestive but not proof [thalassocrat, 2007]. Opponents such as Gouvea, Henry Eissler and Gwern relied on the character's moral complexity, the genetic implausibility and the handling of time travel. The thread summaries record repeated drift away from the question into surnames, Dago slurs and redemption, without any consensus.

For

  • Thalassocrat first proposed a clone reading, citing the father's remark about engineering Chris's height and the lack of any mother [thalassocrat, 2007].
  • Friedman argued the Fifth Head narrator and Chris may both be clones, tying the idea to the 666 address and the Antichrist theme [Jerry Friedman, 2010][Jerry Friedman, 2011].
  • Henry Eissler suggested Chris may be a clone rather than genetically engineered, which would make look-alike figures plausible [Henry Eissler, 2008].
  • Andrew Bollen considered cloning or an engineered hybrid as the best fit for Chris's parentage, citing his self-description as a half-human monster [Andrew Bollen, 2012].
  • Matthew Groves argued the missing mother is suggestive rather than proof she never existed [thalassocrat, 2007].

Against

  • Gouvea argued Chris is a flawed 18th-century man rather than a clone, and that Wolfe avoids time-travel paradox puzzles [Fernando Q. Gouvea, 2007].
  • Friedman argued the genetics of Chris being his own child with Novia are drastically unlikely, so the parentage is hard to explain [Jerry Friedman, 2012].
  • Dave Tallman argued that Chris cannot be his own father because it would be a bootstrap paradox Wolfe avoids, proposing Burt instead [Dave Tallman, 2012].
  • Gwern Branwen argued the cloning and 'made man' evidence is unconvincing and the timeslip is handled poorly [Gwern Branwen, 2012].
  • Friedman later argued the evidence is weak and that an alternate-universe reading with monorails and engineering is possible [Jerry Friedman, 2012].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the clone reading was proposed repeatedly from 2007 to 2012 but never gained agreement, and the list ended with posters still divided.
