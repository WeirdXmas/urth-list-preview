Wolfe's narrators are unreliable by design

Wolfe's first-person narrators are unreliable by design, and the reader is meant to see past them.

91 threads · 68 posts · for 30 / against 33 · first raised 1997-05-05 by Michael Andre-Driussi
Main voices: Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (7), Marc Aramini (6), James Wynn (6), Lee Berman (6), Gerry Quinn (5), Jerry Friedman (4)

The debate

The theory as worded here (Wolfe's narrators are unreliable by design, with the reader meant to see past them) is not stated in any single early card; the earliest related post is Michael Andre-Driussi's 1997 PEACE discussion recap, which records readers' suspicions about Weer, and the first post to take up a general narrator-reliability point is James Laidlow's 1999 post on Severian [Jonathan Laidlow, 1999]. Early exchanges turned the question into a debate over whether Wolfe's narrators lie or only omit: Alex David Groce argued in 1999 that they mostly tell the truth while leaving things out, so unreliability is hard to detect [Alex David Groce, 1999], and James Jordan framed them as ordinary people in extraordinary situations [James Jordan, 1999]. Adam Stephanides in 2000 argued instead that Wolfe deconstructs narrative conventions [Adam Stephanides, 2000].

The discussion shifted in the 2000s and 2010s to particular narrators. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued in 2003 that Wolfe writes in good faith while the Narrator is in bad faith [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2003], and in 2007 that Able of The Wizard Knight is unreliable [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2007]; Adam Stephanides replied that Wolfe's later narrators are not generally unreliable [Adam Stephanides, 2007]. Roy Lackey defended Able's lack of guile [Roy C. Lackey, 2005]. In 2011 Marc Aramini argued that Wolfe's narrators are unreliable for specific motives [Marc Aramini, 2011], while James Wynn defined unreliability in a weaker sense [James Wynn, 2011]. Gerry Quinn argued in 2014 that the label is a misnomer and that the narrators are realistic human tellers [António Pedro Marques, 2014], and Marc Aramini argued that Wolfe's narrators suppress rather than fabricate [Marc Aramini, 2014].

The Fifth Head of Cerberus and Long Sun/Short Sun generated the most sustained disputes, including the Wolfe-surname identity debates (JWillard [JWillard, 2005], Maru Dubshinki [Maru Dubshinki, 2005]) and Lee Berman's readings of inhumi and Fifth Head narrators as liars [Lee Berman, 2011] [Lee Berman, 2014]. The Lupine and Vrt cases were also argued at length [Gerry Quinn, 2011] [Jerry Friedman, 2011].

For

  • Wolfe's narrators can be read as unreliable in the sense that they conceal or distort, as Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued for Severian and Able [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2003] [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2007].
  • James Wynn argued that Wolfe signals unreliable first-person narration openly, and listed Weer, VRT, Latro, Bax and Marsch as examples [James Wynn, 2005] [James Wynn, 2011].
  • Marc Aramini argued that Wolfe's narrators are unreliable for specific motives, such as Weer's denial and Silk's refusal to accept Horn's death [Marc Aramini, 2011].
  • Lee Berman argued that Wolfe withholds cloning details so that Severian and Number Five stay unreliable and horror is sustained [Lee Berman, 2013].
  • Marc Aramini argued that Silk is an unreliable narrator in denial, citing shifts in voice between the Long Sun books [Marc Aramini, 2013].

Against

  • Alex David Groce argued that Wolfe's narrators mostly tell the truth while omitting things, so unreliability is hard to detect [Alex David Groce, 1999].
  • Adam Stephanides argued that Wolfe's later narrators are not generally unreliable and that Able's treatment of inferiors is not parody [Adam Stephanides, 2007].
  • Gerry Quinn argued that the label is a misnomer, since Wolfe's narrators are realistic limited tellers and their hidden meanings are thematic [António Pedro Marques, 2014] [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • Roy Lackey argued that Able is not an unreliable narrator because he lacks guile and the text has no outside God [Roy C. Lackey, 2005].
  • Gerry Quinn and Lee Berman disputed whether Aunt Jeannine is unreliable, with Quinn saying her account is largely accurate [Gerry Quinn, 2011] [Lee Berman, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never reached agreement on whether Wolfe's narrators are unreliable by design, and most threads ended in impasse, drift, or personal exchanges.
