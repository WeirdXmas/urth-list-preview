The Autarchs' memories live in Severian

Severian's sense of having lived before comes from the memories of past Autarchs he takes in with the Autarch's drug, which in some versions revive them inside him.

21 threads · 27 posts · for 13 / against 9 · first raised 1998-07-14 in the thread "Urth condemned, the Autarch's memories"
Main voices: Jeff Wilson (6), James Jordan (3), Chris P (3), David Duffy (2), Mitchell A. Bailey (1), Matthew Malthouse (1)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by Mitchell A. Bailey in 1999 in a post about Father Inire and the Epitome, though his wording concerns memory transfer shaping the Epitome rather than Severian's sense of prior life [Mitchell A. Bailey, 1999]. The idea that Severian lives with the Autarchs' memories took clearer form in 2003, when Chris P argued that faint traces of earlier autarchs' memories come through the alzabo and cause Severian's feeling of having lived before [Chris P, 2003], and James Jordan argued that the memories come to life inside him, citing Thecla [James Jordan, 2003]. Jordan's Eucharist-based reading had already appeared in 2000 [James Jordan, 2000].

In the 2003 thread, Matthew Malthouse disputed the special status of Severian's inheritance, noting that no textual clue shows it differs from his predecessors' [Matthew Malthouse, 2003], and Jordan answered by citing Thecla's coming to life within him [James Jordan, 2003]. Chris P then retreated, saying autarch memories fade with each generation and that he could not find the passage he had relied on [Chris P, 2003]. Roy C. Lackey later disputed the claim that Severian carries all past autarchs' memories, arguing the 'first Severian' on page 270 was replaced and does not count [Roy C. Lackey, 2004]. The 'Sulpicius' and 'Appian' threads carried this dispute further without consensus.

From 2006 onward the discussion spread to other readings. Matthew DeLuca and Dan'l Danehy-Oakes read Severian's childhood memories as belonging to several Severians and the Autarchs [Matthew DeLuca, 2006] [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2006]. Jeff Wilson argued from Thecla's presence in his memory and from the ten-year gap that the Autarch's hosted minds shape his 'we' and personality [Jeff Wilson, 2010], and he later proposed that Autarchs add memories of freshly dead people [Jeff Wilson, 2010]. Jane Delawney disputed the picture by arguing that only Ymar started the tradition [Jane Delawney, 2010], and Andrew Mason argued that an infant memory belongs to Thecla or the Old Autarch rather than to a time-travel episode [Andrew Mason, 2010].

For

  • Severian's sense of prior life is attributed to traces of earlier autarchs' memories passed through the alzabo [Chris P, 2003].
  • The absorbed memories of predecessors are argued to come to life inside Severian, with Thecla cited as the clue [James Jordan, 2003].
  • Severian's childhood memories are read as inherited Autarchial memories that he may eventually merge into one persona [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2006].
  • Severian's eidetic memory and use of faded memories at his trial is linked to Autarchs absorbing the memories of the freshly dead [Jeff Wilson, 2010].
  • Severian's inherited memories are argued to reach into deep history, including uncarved mountains [Jeff Wilson, 2010].

Against

  • No textual clue is found showing Severian's inheritance differs from his predecessors', so the trial-passing memory effect cannot be special to him [Matthew Malthouse, 2003].
  • Chris P admitted he could not find the passage he had relied on for the idea that Severian's memories come to life uniquely [Chris P, 2003].
  • The 'first Severian' on page 270 was replaced, so Severian does not hold memories of becoming autarch [Roy C. Lackey, 2004].
  • Ymar alone started the autarch tradition, since Typhon's two intact heads show he was not eaten, so later memory inheritance is not universal [Jane Delawney, 2010].
  • The infant screaming memory is more plausibly Thecla's or the Old Autarch's than a time-travel case, and the basket imagery does not link to Dorcas [Andrew Mason, 2010].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the 2003 thread ended with the key textual clue unfound, and later posts kept the question open without consensus.
