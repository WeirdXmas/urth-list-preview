The Outsider is the Christian God

The Outsider, the god outside the Whorl who enlightens Silk, is the Christian God (the Increate of the New Sun).

72 threads · 137 posts · for 50 / against 83 · first raised 1996-12-19 by Doug Eigsti
Main voices: Lee Berman (24), Gerry Quinn (15), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (14), David Stockhoff (11), Andrew Mason (11), James Jordan (8)

The debate

The theory that the Outsider is the Christian God was first raised on the list by Doug Eigsti in December 1996, who linked the Void Cross Silk wears to Pas as a Christian-opposed devil and cited Nightside passages placing the Outsider outside the Whorl [Doug Eigsti, 1996]. Within weeks James Jordan and Michael Andre-Driussi argued from Silk's arc, Catholic theology and Christian allusion that the Outsider is God, while Doug Eigsti and Jordan's rival Alga contested whether the Increate is the true Creator [James Jordan, 1997][Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997][Doug Eigsti, 1997]. In 1997 Patrick O'Leary and Kevin J. Maroney also called the Outsider God, though Maroney added a burst blood vessel reading, and Michael Straight objected that Wolfe's own interview statement that the Outsider is God closes off readings the text allows [Patrick O'Leary, 1997][Kevin J. Maroney, 1997][Michael Straight, 1997].

The debate kept returning to whether the Outsider is God, the Increate, or a Gnostic or Dionysian figure. In 1998 and 2002 Gnostic readings appeared, with Thomas Bitterman and Alice Turner mapping Pas as Demiurge and the Outsider as true god, and Josh Geller disputing it [Thomas Bitterman, 2002][Alice Turner, 2002][Josh Geller, 2002]. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued in 2004 and 2005 for an orthodox Christian frame containing a Gnostic cosmos, and Malcolm McGugan objected that divine intent in the Whorl is at most indirect [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2004][Malcolm McGugan, 2005]. From 2010 Lee Berman pushed a Dionysian reading, and Son of Witz, Craig Brewer and Andrew Mason argued the Outsider is Christ or the Trinity's Father, Holy Ghost or Logos [Son of Witz, 2010][Son of Witz, 2010][António Pedro Marques, 2010][Andrew Mason, 2010].

The later years show the same split. In 2011 and 2012 Andrew Mason and Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued the Outsider is the Christian God, while Lee Berman argued it is a dark pagan figure and not the Increate, with Daniel Petersen and Jerry Friedman answering on God's darkness and mercy [Andrew Mason, 2012][Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2012][Lee Berman, 2012][Daniel Petersen, 2012][Jerry Friedman, 2012]. Several threads ("christ, already", "Oannes") ended without agreement, and the posts record no decisive clue or Wolfe remark that settled the question.

For

  • The Outsider is God in Wolfe's Long Sun, as Silk's arc and Catholic theology suggest, according to James Jordan and Michael Andre-Driussi [James Jordan, 1997][Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997].
  • Patrick O'Leary and Kevin J. Maroney argued the Outsider is plainly God, with Maroney adding a burst blood vessel explanation for Silk's vision [Patrick O'Leary, 1997][Kevin J. Maroney, 1997].
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued that the Outsider is both the Christian God and the Gnostic true god, with Pas as Demiurge, inside an orthodox Christian universe [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2004][Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2005].
  • Andrew Mason argued the Outsider is the Christian God or Increate, with pagan gods as its aspects, citing crosses, saints and scripture quotations [Andrew Mason, 2010][Andrew Mason, 2012].
  • Daniel Petersen argued that the Outsider resembles the biblical God and that Silk and Severian are Christ-like figures rather than Christ [Daniel Petersen, 2012].

Against

  • Michael Straight objected that Wolfe's interview statement that the Outsider is God forecloses readings and that the narrators leave God's existence open [Michael Straight, 1997].
  • Lee Berman argued that the Outsider is a dark pagan or Dionysian god, not the Christian God or Increate, citing Genesis and the Old Testament [Lee Berman, 2012][Lee Berman, 2012][Lee Berman, 2011].
  • Malcolm McGugan argued that divine intent in the Whorl is at most indirect, close to a pantheist view, so the Outsider is not acting directly as the Christian God [Malcolm McGugan, 2005].
  • Gerry Quinn argued the Outsider's Christian-sounding references are weaker than the pagan and Dionysian ones and that the Outsider is an aspect of the True God, not the Christian God [Gerry Quinn, 2011][Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • Lee Berman and Antonin Scriabin argued that the Outsider's dark, wrathful character and Dionysian links make it unlike the Christian God, with Scriabin favouring a Christ-like figure instead [Antonin Scriabin, 2011][Lee Berman, 2012].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never reached agreement, with posters on both sides still arguing from the text in the last threads ("christ, already", "Oannes") and no decisive clue settling it.
