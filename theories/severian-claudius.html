Severian is modelled on Claudius

Severian as narrator echoes Robert Graves's I, Claudius.

9 threads · 12 posts · for 5 / against 2 · first raised 1996-12-07 by Charles Dye
Main voices: Charles Dye (7), Alex David Groce (2), Ron Hale-Evans (2), Daniel Fusch (1)

The debate

The idea that Severian echoes Graves's Claudius first appears on the list on 1996-12-07, when Charles Dye listed Severian and Claudius alongside Christ and Silk in a discussion of wounds, linking Silk to Marcus Aurelius [Charles Dye, 1996]. In 1997 Dye, Jim Jordan and Ranjit Bhatnagar returned to the idea, suggesting that Graves's Claudius novels might explain Severian's leg and facial wounds [Charles Dye, 1997], and Dye began comparing Silk with Marcus Aurelius and Severian with Claudius [Charles Dye, 1997].

In 1998 Alex David Groce asked directly whether the list had explored parallels between Severian and Claudius, citing the lamed emperor, the prophetic opening and wives named Valeria [Alex David Groce, 1998]. Dye supported the link by setting a coin scene in Wolfe beside a coin passage in Claudius the God [Charles Dye, 1998]. In 1999 the topic peaked: Dye proposed that Severian is modelled on Claudius and matched Master Ultan's reading room to Graves's Sulpicius [Charles Dye, 1999], and Groce argued for explicit patterning, adding the Sybil's prophecy and Valeria's name [Alex David Groce, 1999]. Daniel Fusch agreed only in general terms, pointing to the Roman imagery [Daniel Fusch, 1999].

Ron Hale-Evans was the main dissenter. He argued that Severian fits Claudius less well, since Severian leads armies and is not a bumbling fool, and then proposed Marcus Aurelius as the better model [Ron Hale-Evans, 1999][Ron Hale-Evans, 1999]. In the separate Contessa thread, Dye's side remark about Severian's recognition of his own image on the chrisos drew a dispute from Lackey's camp [Charles Dye, 1999]. The posts record no decisive new clue, and the discussion ended with the parallels suggested but not settled.

For

  • Dye first linked Severian's wounds to Graves's Claudius in the earliest post on the list [Charles Dye, 1996].
  • Dye argued that a coin scene in Wolfe echoes a coin passage in Claudius the God [Charles Dye, 1998].
  • Dye proposed that Severian is modelled on Claudius, pairing Master Ultan's reading room with Graves's Sulpicius [Charles Dye, 1999].
  • Groce argued Severian is explicitly patterned on Claudius, citing the Sybil's prophecy and the battle descriptions [Alex David Groce, 1999].
  • Groce raised the lamed emperor, prophetic opening and Valeria parallels as grounds for the link [Alex David Groce, 1998].

Against

  • Hale-Evans argued Severian leads armies and is not a bumbling fool, so he fits Claudius less well [Ron Hale-Evans, 1999].
  • Hale-Evans argued Severian resembles Marcus Aurelius more than Claudius because he leads a charge in Citadel [Ron Hale-Evans, 1999].
  • Dye himself argued that Patera Silk fits Marcus Aurelius better than Severian does [Charles Dye, 1999].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the Claudius parallel was repeatedly raised and supported by Dye and Groce, but the posts record it as unresolved, with Hale-Evans preferring Marcus Aurelius and no consensus reached.
