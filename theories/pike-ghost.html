What Pike's ghost is

Pike's ghost is a real ghost, an aquastor, a projection raised by Mucor, or a time-travelling Silk or Rajan.

43 threads · 103 posts · for 51 / against 51 · first raised 1997-01-23 in the thread "Patera Pike's Ghost"
Main voices: James Wynn (25), Gerry Quinn (24), Andrew Mason (7), Michael Andre-Driussi (6), Dave Tallman (6), Alice Turner (5)

The debate

The theory was first raised in January 1997, when Doug Eigsti argued that Pike's apparition belongs to the same supernatural category as Mucor's asomatous travel [Doug Eigsti, 1997]. Within days the debate split between Quetzal in disguise, raised by Fernando Gouvea [Fernando Q. Gouvea, 1997] and Alice Turner [Alice Turner, 1997], and an aquastor, argued by Charles Dye [Charles Dye, 1997] and Dave Lebling [Dave Lebling, 1997]. Michael Andre-Driussi backed Quetzal and questioned the implausible double-dipping an aquastor reading would need [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997] [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997].

The 1997 discussion turned on Wolfe's published answers. Lebling welcomed the reported answer that the ghost was not an aquastor [Dave Lebling, 1997], and David Langford later cited Wolfe's 1997 denial of Quetzal, though he also argued in 2000 that Wolfe's remark about other beings of Quetzal's kind might leave the question open [David Langford, 2000] [David Langford, 2000]. James Jordan argued the figure should be read simply as a ghost, drawing on Wolfe's own belief in ghosts [James Jordan, 1997]. In 1998 Greg Neyman floated a projection from a MainFrame copy [Greg Neyman, 1998], and Peter Westlake linked the ghost to Mucor [Peter Westlake, 1998]. Nicholas Gevers and Andre-Driussi proposed possession or Q-biting explanations in 2000 [Nicholas Gevers, 2000] [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2000].

From 2002 the older-Silk and Rajan readings took over. Marc Aramini proposed an older Silk returning astrally [Marc Aramini, 2002], and Dave Tallman argued in 2008 that Mucor stirred up a real ghost [Dave Tallman, 2008] and later that Pike was Silk in a calotte disguise [Dave Tallman, 2008]. James Wynn proposed in 2008 that the ghost was the Raja travelling in spirit [James Wynn, 2008]. In 2010 and 2011 Wynn made the Rajan-as-Pike reading central across many threads [James Wynn, 2010] [James Wynn, 2011] [James Wynn, 2011], while Tallman, Roy Lackey and Gerry Quinn offered rival readings of a future Silk, a ghost, and a hat-linked figure [Dave Tallman, 2010] [Roy C. Lackey, 2010] [Gerry Quinn, 2011]. Later threads on the manse sounds, the second Oreb and Mucor's family ties added further positions [Roy C. Lackey, 2011] [Larry Miller, 2011] [Andrew Mason, 2011].

No consensus emerged. Threads repeatedly ended in impasse, with Wynn and Quinn repeating positions and some posters accusing others of evasion, and the 2023 post by Andre-Driussi on Mucor reaching back to Pike shows the question still being raised [Andrew Mason, 2012].

For

  • Pike's ghost is a real spirit of the dead, citing Mucor's disembodied appearances and the afterlife in Silk's vision [Andrew Mason, 2011].
  • Wynn argued that Wolfe's 'real ghost' remark and the non-Gothic scene support a genuine disembodied spirit [James Wynn, 2010].
  • Roy Lackey reconstructed Wolfe's answers as eliminating an aquastor and Quetzal, leaving a ghost [Roy C. Lackey, 2010].
  • Wynn argued that the Rajan time-travels and that the Pike's room visit is time travel, citing the grandmother's tale and muddled timelines [James Wynn, 2011].
  • Wynn argued that Mucor summoned Pike's ghost, with her dream-travel explaining the mist-like disappearance [James Wynn, 2011] [James Wynn, 2011].

Against

  • Lebling welcomed Wolfe's reported answer that the ghost was not an aquastor [Dave Lebling, 1997].
  • Langford corrected the claim that the ghost is Quetzal by citing Wolfe's 1997 denial [David Langford, 2000].
  • Gerry Quinn argued Pike's ghost need not be a time traveller and that the Neighbours likely phased in from a parallel universe [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • Quinn argued Silk cannot time-travel back as an aged Pike and that the hat falling from the ghost points to Pike, not the Rajan [Gerry Quinn, 2011] [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • Quinn and Lackey argued the Silkhorn timeline rules out dream-travel time travel, and Dave Tallman later weakened the real-ghost reading by suggesting Silk may have been mistaken for Pike [Gerry Quinn, 2011] [Dave Tallman, 2010].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never reached agreement, with the aquastor, Quetzal, real-ghost, Mucor and time-travel readings each kept by some posters and most threads ending in impasse.
