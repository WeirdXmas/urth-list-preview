Hierodules live backwards in time

The hierodules (Ossipago, Barbatus, Famulimus) experience time in reverse or come from the future.

13 threads · 19 posts · for 16 / against 6 · first raised 1998-08-30 by Jon Camfield
Main voices: Don Doggett (5), thalassocrat (5), Jon Camfield (4), Jeff Wilson (2), b sharp (2), Mark Millman (1)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by Jon Camfield on 1998-08-30 [Jon Camfield, 1998], who argued that the Hierodules' repeated appearances reflect a gap between perceived and absolute time, grounded in the Tzadkiel's time-travel and their rescue of Severian at Apu-Punchau [Jon Camfield, 1998]. The same year CRCulver's puzzlement about the Hierodules' childlike look and short lives drew Mark Millman's four explanations, including non-human aging and a larval stage [Mark Millman, 1998], and Robert Borski extended the backward-time idea to the Cumaean [Robert Borski, 1998]. Peter Westlake's suggestion that Baldanders is simply mistaken about their lifespans is noted in the 'Relative & Absolute' thread summary.

The idea took its fullest shape in May 2006, when Don Doggett argued the Hierodules age forward while travelling backward through time by machine, arriving from the future as guides for Severian's early reign [Don Doggett, 2006]. Doggett's position was pressed by thalassocrat, who questioned how backward-moving beings could hold coherent conversations with Severian and why their meetings fall out of sequence [thalassocrat, 2006], and by b sharp, who argued instead that the Hierodules move only backward and cast them as chess pawns [b sharp, 2006][b sharp, 2006]. Roy C. Lackey offered a counterclockwise-time version grounded in their role as Severian's counselors [Roy C. Lackey, 2006]. thalassocrat later disputed b sharp's account, arguing that Barbatus's remark shows the Hierodules know both past and future [thalassocrat, 2006], and proposed that the forward-travel view is more plausible [thalassocrat, 2006].

Later posts shifted toward loop mechanics. Jeff Wilson proposed backward time-jumps across Severian's life, grounded in the lack of reversed speech or objects in the text [Jeff Wilson, 2008]. Dave Tallman argued that steering agents avoid intractable loops through repeated iterations [Dave Tallman, 2008]. Jane Delawney proposed that the Ascians, who live backwards, know Severian is the New Sun because they lived his future [Jane Delawney, 2010]. The main threads ended without agreement, with posters citing the same passages to opposite conclusions.

For

  • The Hierodules' repeated appearances in The Urth of the New Sun suggest a gap between perceived and absolute time, so their last meeting with Severian is not final [Jon Camfield, 1998]
  • Hierodules aging forward while travelling backward by machine would explain why they can serve as Severian's guides during his early reign [Don Doggett, 2006]
  • Counterclockwise time would explain how the Hierodules know of Severian's later encounters, since they counselled him during his autarchy [Roy C. Lackey, 2006]
  • Tzadkiel's and Typhon's chess remarks cast the Hierodules as pawns moving opposite to Severian in time [b sharp, 2006][b sharp, 2006]
  • The absence of reverse speech or reversed objects in the text, and the need to keep Noah's God truthful, support backward time-jumps across Severian's life [Jeff Wilson, 2008]

Against

  • The Hierodules could not converse coherently with Severian if each instant ran backward, so the backward-travel account is absurd [Don Doggett, 2006][thalassocrat, 2006]
  • Their out-of-sequence meetings with Severian and Baldanders clash with a simple backward-travel explanation [thalassocrat, 2006]
  • Barbatus's remark in The Sword of the Lictor shows the Hierodules already know both past and future, undermining the pawn account [thalassocrat, 2006]
  • Ossipago reverses the Hierodules' time-flow only in dealings with ordinary people, a suggestion from the archives that complicates the simple model [thalassocrat, 2006]
  • Severian's inconsistent accounts of the same events may reflect ordinary authorial error rather than time-loop mechanics [thalassocrat, 2006]

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never converged on backward or forward travel, and the proposals remain separate hypotheses with no consensus.
