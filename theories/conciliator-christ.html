The Conciliator stands for Christ

The Conciliator of the Commonwealth's religion is a figure for Christ, or a far-future memory of him; the counter-reading is that he is an earthly messiah only.

50 threads · 68 posts · for 30 / against 32 · first raised 1997-06-28 by Paul C Duggan
Main voices: Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (9), Marc Aramini (9), James Jordan (6), Lee Berman (6), António Pedro Marques (4), Dave Lebling (3)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by Paul C Duggan in June 1997, who disputed that Severian is not a Christ figure and argued he is a little Christ bringing temporal salvation [Paul C Duggan, 1997]. Alice Turner and James Jordan that summer linked Agia's Theoanthropos reference to Jesus or to composite heroes like Gilgamesh and Herakles [Alice Turner, 1997][James Jordan, 1997]. Through 1998 and 1999 Michael Straight, Dave Lebling, Tony Ellis and Roy C. Lackey debated whether the Conciliator is Jesus, Christ-like, or an earthly messiah [Michael Straight, 1998][Tony Ellis, 1999][Roy C. Lackey, 1999].

The debate sharpened in 2002 and 2006. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued a Christ-like figure need not be Christ, citing Catholic sacramental theology and Wolfe's distinction between Christ figures and Christian figures [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002], while Adam Stephanides asked for textual grounds and Jordan proposed the Theoanthropos as an earlier incarnation of the Second Person [James Jordan, 2002][Adam Stephanides, 2002]. Roy C. Lackey and Jeff Wilson clashed over whether the Conciliator's sacrifice is Christian [Roy C. Lackey, 2008][Jeff Wilson, 2008], and Craig Brewer and Son of Witz repeated the Christian-figure versus Christ-figure split [Craig Brewer, 2008][Son of Witz, 2008].

From 2010 onward the thread became one of the longest on the list, with Dave Lebling recalling that the early books seemed to point to Jesus [Dave Lebling, 2010], Antonio Pedro Marques arguing the Conciliator is a red herring rather than the local Jesus [António Pedro Marques, 2010][António Pedro Marques, 2010], and Lee Berman, Andrew Mason and Jerry Friedman offering composite or retroactive readings [Lee Berman, 2010][Andrew Mason, 2010][Jerry Friedman, 2010]. Marc Aramini's 2011 posts argued both for a syncretic blend of historical Christ and legend and against a straightforward Christ type [Marc Aramini, 2011][Marc Aramini, 2011], and Lee Berman argued the Theanthropos remark is an ironic reference to the Conciliator [Lee Berman, 2011]. Marc Aramini's 2014 suggestion that the Conciliator and the theoanthropos are not distinct in the first four books was offered tentatively [Marc Aramini, 2014].

For the theory, posters cited Wolfe's interview remarks, Christian sacramental and liturgical parallels, and the Christ-like acts of the Conciliator [John Watkins, 2010][Son of Witz, 2008][Lee Berman, 2010]. Against it, posters cited the Conciliator's lack of Jesus' teachings, the ironic or non-Christian readings of the Theoanthropos, and the claim that Severian is not literally Christ [António Pedro Marques, 2010][Lee Berman, 2011][Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2014].

For

  • Severian and the Conciliator parallel Christ's death, resurrection and reconciliation work, as posters argued from the text and the Easter liturgy [Son of Witz, 2008][John Watkins, 2010].
  • Wolfe's Urth parallels Earth's history closely enough that a Christ figure must have existed there, as Dan'l Danehy-Oakes and Andrew Mason argued [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2006][Andrew Mason, 2010].
  • Dave Lebling recalled that the references to the Conciliator seemed to point to Jesus on first reading [Dave Lebling, 2010].
  • Marc Aramini argued the Conciliator blends a historical Christ with legendary returns and that Severian's salvation is imitative [Marc Aramini, 2011][Marc Aramini, 2011].
  • Lee Berman and Eugene Zaretskiy argued the Conciliator is the Jesus-like figure while Severian is non-divine [Eugene Zaretskiy, 2010].

Against

  • Roy C. Lackey argued the Conciliator is an earthly messiah, not an eschatological Christ, and that his sacrifice differs from Christ's voluntary one [Roy C. Lackey, 1999][Roy C. Lackey, 2008].
  • Antonio Pedro Marques argued the Conciliator is a red herring because Urth's church and cult do not match the promise of a local Jesus [António Pedro Marques, 2010][António Pedro Marques, 2010].
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued Christ-like need not mean Christ, and later that the text makes clear the Conciliator is not Christ [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002][Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2014].
  • Marc Aramini argued the Conciliator is not Christ or a Christ type and is a specifically Christian figure tied to one religion, not a generic savior [Marc Aramini, 2011][Marc Aramini, 2011].
  • Craig Brewer and Matthew Weber argued Severian is a Christian rather than a Christ figure, and that the Outsider cannot be simply identified with Christ [Craig Brewer, 2008][Matthew Weber, 2010].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never reached agreement, with posters split between Christ, Christ-like, Christian-figure, earthly-messiah and red-herring readings across the 1997-2014 threads.
