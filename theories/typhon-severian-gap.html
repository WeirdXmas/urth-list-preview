How long between Typhon and Severian

The span between Typhon's empire and Severian's time can be estimated from clues, from about a thousand years to many thousands.

13 threads · 14 posts · for 7 / against 6 · first raised 1998-01-30 by C R Culver
Main voices: Dave Lebling (2), Gerry Quinn (2), C R Culver (1), Tony Ellis (1), Paul C Duggan (1), mary whalen (1)

The debate

The earliest post on the list is from C R Culver on 1998-01-30, who argued that a New Sun was not brought in the earlier iteration and put the Typhon-to-Severian gap at about a chiliad, citing Mantis's figure of 1000 years between Ymar and Severian [C R Culver, 1998]. The same day Dave Lebling disputed that figure, arguing that carved mountains and geology need far more time, and reasoned that about a hundred ten-year mountains would fit only in a thousand-year span [Dave Lebling, 1998][Dave Lebling, 1998]. Tony Ellis and Paul C Duggan then argued from Wolfe's own offhand answer of 1000 to 2000 years, with Duggan insisting it applies only to Typhon to Severian and not to the New Sun-to-present gap [Tony Ellis, 1998][Paul C Duggan, 1998]. Mary Whalen made the carving argument again in 1998, pointing to Lexicon Urthus [mary whalen, 1998].

The discussion turned on the Whorl books from 2001. Michael Straight argued that time dilation during the Whorl's voyage could account for the gap, and Michael Andre-Driussi backed the idea using the calendar and a god's remark about 1000 years [Michael Straight, 2001][Michael Andre-Driussi, 2001]. Marc Aramini argued in 2003 that monsters changing quickly and Maytera Marble's internal clock allow the span to be much longer than a thousand years [Marc Aramini, 2003]. Rex Racer in 2006 proposed roughly 3000 to 4000 years from the Whorl's flight and Silk's phrase about many centuries [Rex Racer, 2006]. Later, James Wynn estimated 1000 to 2000 years and noted the books never fix the gap [James Wynn, 2010], while Gerry Quinn argued from the visibility of Sol that the Whorl travelled only about 50 light years, so Typhon lived only about 350 years before Severian [Gerry Quinn, 2011][Gerry Quinn, 2011].

The threads show a long, unresolved dispute. Lebling and CRCulver traded views on Wolfe's stated two-millennia figure, with CRCulver citing a People magazine interview and Lebling answering with geology and archaeology [Dave Lebling, 1998]. Jim Jordan later argued that Wolfe's figure refers to Typhon's time and that Urth is much younger than Mantis's estimate. The Nessus thread ended with agreement that time dilation covers the gap, though the 1997 priority claim stayed open.

For

  • Time dilation during the Whorl's voyage could explain a large gap between Horn's Urth and Severian's era, computed from the ship's speed [Michael Straight, 2001].
  • The calendar's roughly 300 years and a god's comment about 1000 years supply figures for estimating the Whorl's time dilation [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2001].
  • Monsters can change within one generation and Maytera Marble's internal clock suggests the span may be far longer than a thousand years [Marc Aramini, 2003].
  • Carved mountains and geological or archaeological evidence require much more time than two thousand years [Dave Lebling, 1998][Dave Lebling, 1998].
  • Silk's phrase about many centuries at Nessus, plus the Whorl's calendar, points to roughly 3000 to 4000 years [Rex Racer, 2006].

Against

  • Wolfe's own offhand answer of 1000 to 2000 years refers only to Typhon to Severian and not to the future setting [Tony Ellis, 1998][Paul C Duggan, 1998].
  • Gerry Quinn argues from the visibility of Sol that the Whorl travelled only about 50 light years, leaving about 350 years between Typhon and Severian [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • Wolfe is said to be loose with physics that depends on knowledge no one has within the story, so the chronology should not be trusted [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • Mantis's figure of 1000 years between Ymar and Severian gives about a chiliad between Typhon and Severian [C R Culver, 1998].
  • Jim Jordan argues Urth is far younger than Mantis's estimate, with human culture dating only to about 6000 BC.

Where it stands: Still disputed: posters disagree between about 350 years and many thousands, and the threads ended without consensus, though the Nessus thread reached agreement that time dilation covers the gap.
