Aunt Jeannine is an abo

Aunt Jeannine is an abo, not a human.

33 threads · 36 posts · for 18 / against 20 · first raised 1998-04-02 by Robert Borski
Main voices: Robert Borski (11), mary whalen (6), Alice Turner (5), Lee Berman (5), Pedro Jorge Romero (3), William H. Ansley (3)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list in April 1998 by Robert Borski [Robert Borski, 1998], in a debate about whether the narrator of The Fifth Head of Cerberus is Gene Wolfe. Early replies from Tony Ellis [Tony Ellis, 1998] and Alice Turner [Alice Turner, 1998] disputed the clone and genealogy counts, and Alice Turner later called the Aunt Jeannine theory preposterous [Alice Turner, 1998]. Borski's June 1998 argument that Jeannine is an alien aboriginal, built on clues about her legs, money and the name Aubrey Veil, marked the shift from genealogy to a hidden-identity reading [Robert Borski, 1998].

In June and July 1998 the theory picked up supporters. Borski argued she is the baby in the photograph aged 140 [Robert Borski, 1998], and mary whalen supported it with the spiral staircase as a helix metaphor [mary whalen, 1998] and then with a parlor-magic image on page 52 of the Orb edition [mary whalen, 1998]. Dan Parmenter said the hypothesis was gaining credibility while suggesting the novel might be deliberately ambiguous [Dan Parmenter, 1998]. Debate reappeared in the 2010s, when Lee Berman argued Aunt Jeannine is Dr Veil and rejected her as an unreliable narrator [Lee Berman, 2010] [Lee Berman, 2010], and Gerry Quinn argued against Berman's view of her reliability [Gerry Quinn, 2011] [Lee Berman, 2011]. In 2013 Marc Aramini proposed she is an outcross of a clone line tied to Mr. Million's original [Marc Aramini, 2013].

The posts record no consensus. Posters on both sides kept repeating their points across the decades, and the thread summaries end in impasse or disagreement over textual evidence rather than agreement.

For

  • Borski argued that Jeannine's legs, money worries, pseudonym and Victor's phrasing hide an alien aboriginal identity [Robert Borski, 1998].
  • Borski argued that Jeannine is the baby in the photograph, aged 140, and that eoliths and artifacts fit the abo reading [Robert Borski, 1998].
  • mary whalen read the spiral staircase as a DNA helix in which Jeannine floats down the centre [mary whalen, 1998].
  • mary whalen used the page 52 parlor-magic comparison of Jeannine's withered legs to argue she is an aboriginal impostor [mary whalen, 1998].
  • Marc Aramini argued Jeannine is a likely outcross of a clone line, linked to the dream scene with the broken-necked man [Marc Aramini, 2013].

Against

  • Alice Turner argued that Jeannine is an outcropping with her own mother rather than a clone or abo, and relied on age estimates [Alice Turner, 1998].
  • Alice Turner called the Aunt Jeannine theory preposterous while accepting the V.R.T. half-abo idea [Alice Turner, 1998].
  • Alice Turner argued that Jeannine's withered legs are no evidence of a hidden identity [Alice Turner, 1998].
  • Lee Berman argued from the text that the inhumi keep the ability to shift back, and that Aunt Jeannine is Dr Veil [Lee Berman, 2010].
  • Gerry Quinn argued that Jeannine is not an unreliable narrator, since her claims about Veil are largely accurate [Gerry Quinn, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the theory was never settled, and posters on both sides repeated their points across the 1998 and 2010s debates without reaching agreement.
