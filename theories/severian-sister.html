Severian's lost sister

Severian had a missing twin sister (posters call her Severa) who appears in the books under another name; candidates include Merryn, Jolenta, Juturna, Pia and Valeria (after Valeria Severa, wife of the emperor Valentinian).

90 threads · 140 posts · for 87 / against 60 · first raised 1997-05-23 by Geoff Alex Cohen
Main voices: Lee Berman (36), David Stockhoff (23), Jeff Wilson (12), Gerry Quinn (11), b sharp (7), Robert Borski (6)

The debate

The sister theory was first raised on the list in May 1997, when Geoff Alex Cohen reported a live chat in which Wolfe remarked that Merryn is almost certainly Severian's sister [Geoff Alex Cohen, 1997]. Cohen then proposed Merryn as the sister in June 1997, reasoning from the twin-like names Severian and Severa and from the practice of sending girls of condemned prisoners to the witches [Geoff Alex Cohen, 1997]. Michael Andre-Driussi responded the same day by quoting Clute and Feeley on Severian's family and raising the sister question [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997].

In 1998 the candidates multiplied. Jon Camfield proposed Thecla as a half-sister [Jon Camfield, 1998], and Robert Borski argued that Barbea, a khaibit in the House Azure, is Severa [Robert Borski, 1998]. C R Culver worried this would undercut Merryn [C R Culver, 1998], and Mark Millman relayed Borski's rebuttal that Severian's familiarity echoed Valeria rather than a buried memory [Mark Millman, 1998]. Borski later argued that Merryn is the daughter of the Cumaean and an alien rather than Severa [Robert Borski, 2001]. In 2001 Adam Stephanides argued that Merryn's 'brother' line does confirm the sibling relation [Adam Stephanides, 2001], and Matthew Malthouse proposed a biological sibling tie from an early passage on guild assignments [Matthew Malthouse, 2001].

From 2008 the discussion shifted to Jolenta, Juturna and Valeria. b sharp pressed Jolenta as Severa [b sharp, 2008], Stuart Hamm proposed Juturna [Stuart Hamm, 2008], and Jeff Wilson argued for Juturna as a shapeshifting undine sister [Jeff Wilson, 2008]. Lee Berman took up Jolenta as Severa from 2010 onward, citing hair, age and incest themes [Lee Berman, 2010], and later argued that Merryn is old and disguised [Lee Berman, 2011]. David Stockhoff proposed Merryn as Severa in 2011 [David Stockhoff, 2011] and later argued that Severa's absence points to a doubled Severian [David Stockhoff, 2011]. Marc Aramini proposed Pia in 2000 and again in 2013 [A Aramini, 2000][Marc Aramini, 2013].

The thread stayed unresolved, with the evidence standard disputed. Roy Lackey argued that the text gives no objective reason for Merryn's unchanged appearance and that confirming the link would make the mystery anticlimactic [Roy C. Lackey, 2007]. Tim O'Donnell called the supporting evidence circumstantial and said Wolfe is not confirming it [O'Donnell, Tim (BOSI), 2008], while b sharp rejected the golden-hair arguments against Jolenta [b sharp, 2006].

For

  • Wolfe is reported to have said Merryn is almost certainly Severian's sister in a 1997 chat [Geoff Alex Cohen, 1997]
  • Severian and Severa are twin-like names, and girls of condemned prisoners went to the witches, so Merryn fits as the sister [Geoff Alex Cohen, 1997]
  • Merryn's 'brother' line in the Short Sun dialogue is taken to confirm the sibling tie [Adam Stephanides, 2001]
  • An early passage on which children of prisoners go to the torturers and which to the witches supports a biological sibling relation [Matthew Malthouse, 2001]
  • Jolenta's age, hair and stage role are cited as evidence she is Severa, Severian's twin [Lee Berman, 2010][Larry Miller, 2011]

Against

  • The homunculus's room held very old objects, and Merryn may be far older than Severian, so the sibling reading is doubtful [Peter Westlake, 1998]
  • Borski argued Merryn is the daughter of the Cumaean and an alien, not Severa [Robert Borski, 2001]
  • Roy Lackey argued the text gives no objective reason for Merryn's unchanged appearance, and confirming the link would undercut the mandragora clue [Roy C. Lackey, 2007]
  • b sharp's golden-hair argument rejects Borski's candidates Jolenta and Barbea as Severa [b sharp, 2006]
  • Tim O'Donnell called the evidence for Severian's family circumstantial and said Wolfe is not confirming it [O'Donnell, Tim (BOSI), 2008]

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never reached agreement, and the identity of Severa was spread across Merryn, Jolenta, Juturna, Pia, Valeria and others without consensus.
