Typhon's purpose for the Whorl

Typhon built the Whorl and sent it out to recolonize Urth (or Ushas), not only to found new worlds, and chose its destination deliberately.

82 threads · 114 posts · for 72 / against 40 · first raised 1997-05-09 by Scott Dalrymple
Main voices: James Wynn (16), Roy C. Lackey (8), Lee Berman (7), David Stockhoff (7), Gerry Quinn (7), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (5)

The debate

The earliest post on the list is Scott Dalrymple's 1997 proposal that Typhon launched the Whorl and fashioned Pas after himself [Scott Dalrymple, 1997]. Kieran Cleary then asked the same month whether Typhon aimed for the Blue/Green system [Kieran Cleary, 1997], and John Eric Ivancich asked whether Typhon meant to repopulate or retake Urth [John Eric Ivancich, 1997]. In 1999 the question split into rival motives: Greg Neyman proposed a Trojan horse against the Inhumi [Greg Neyman, 1999], Alex David Groce argued the Whorl spread Pas's glory [Alex David Groce, 1999], and Tom Urash argued that Typhon and his children never boarded it [Urash, Tom, 1999].

From 2000 to 2003 the debate turned to the Blue/Green destination and to Typhon's religion. Adam Stephanides argued the choice was deliberate and offered an orbital explanation [Adam Stephanides, 2000], while Robert Borski and Groce proposed Typhon was a Neighbor returning home [Robert Borski, 2000][Alex David Groce, 2001]. Jeff Wilson argued the Whorl might return to Urth [Jeff Wilson, 2001], and Nicholas Gevers proposed an asset-stripped Urth [Nicholas Gevers, 2000]. In November 2002 a long thread produced several motives: Roy C. Lackey argued for vanity and a return to Urth [Roy C. Lackey, 2002][Roy C. Lackey, 2002], Jeff Veyera proposed an original starcrosser purpose [Jeff Veyera, 2002], and Lisa Schaffer-Doggett and Michael Andre-Driussi argued for a hedge or colonial project [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2003][Lisa Schaffer-Doggett, 2003]. Jerry Friedman and Stephanides disputed the Gnostic and cosmic readings [Jerry Friedman, 2002][Adam Stephanides, 2002].

In 2007 to 2011 Dan argued Typhon hijacked the Whorl late in his decline to recolonize Earth [Dan, 2007][Dan, 2007], and Gwern Branwen disputed this, holding that a costly generation ship fits Typhon's prime [Gwern Branwen, 2007]. Pedro Pereira cited a Wolfe interview naming immortality as the motive and argued Typhon wanted worship [Pedro Pereira, 2007], but later admitted the interview answer was too evasive to settle the question [Pedro Pereira, 2007]. Son of Witz proposed a return to Urth as Ushas [Son of Witz, 2009], which Jordon Flato rejected [Jordon Flato, 2009]. Gerry Quinn argued that the Whorl could not practically return to repopulate Urth [Gerry Quinn, 2011]. In 2010 thalassocrat, David Stockhoff, Jane Delawney and Lee Berman offered museum, self-aggrandizement, last-message and sleeper-mission readings [thalassocrat, 2010][David Stockhoff, 2010][Jane Delawney, 2010][Lee Berman, 2010]. In 2013 Stockhoff and Antonin Scriabin proposed that Typhon sent many Whorls [David Stockhoff, 2013][Antonin Scriabin, 2013], while Andreas Johansson proposed a colonization of a planet like pristine Urth [Andreas Johansson, 2013].

For

  • Typhon's arrogance and drive for power make a deliberate, self-serving destination plausible, according to Dan and Stockhoff [Dan, 2007][David Stockhoff, 2010].
  • Pedro Pereira cited a Wolfe interview naming Typhon's desire for immortality as the reason for the Whorl [Pedro Pereira, 2007].
  • Stephanides argued that an improbable Blue-Green system points to Typhon's choice, using a Cruithne-like orbit as a mechanism [Adam Stephanides, 2000].
  • Roy Lackey cited Typhon's statement that he missed the ships leaving Urth to argue he built the Whorl out of vanity [Roy C. Lackey, 2002].
  • Scriabin argued that concurrent Long Sun and Short Sun events imply a launch long before Typhon's awakening, consistent with many Whorls [Antonin Scriabin, 2013].

Against

  • Gwern Branwen argued a costly generation ship is a hubristic project of Typhon's prime, not a hijack amid collapse [Gwern Branwen, 2007].
  • Gerry Quinn argued a relativistic detour or return is impractical and Typhon never mentions such a plan when he meets Severian [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • Jordon Flato argued that a Typhon-return plan conflicts with the books' themes and that the Hierodules would have hidden the black hole fix from him [Jordon Flato, 2009].
  • Urash argued that Typhon and his children never boarded the Whorl, only software copies were planted in Mainframe [Urash, Tom, 1999].
  • Stockhoff argued that Wolfe may not have thought hard about the Whorl's purpose at all [David Stockhoff, 2010].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the motive and destination were raised repeatedly from 1997 to 2013, with posters offering many rival readings, and the list never settled on one.
