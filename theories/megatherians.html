The megatherians' place in the cosmos

The megatherians (Abaia, Erebus and others) are alien powers with their own cosmic role, not simply devils opposed to the Hierogrammates.

50 threads · 128 posts · for 85 / against 58 · first raised 1999-03-25 by Robert Borski
Main voices: Lee Berman (32), James Wynn (18), David Stockhoff (14), Jerry Friedman (14), Gerry Quinn (11), Jeff Wilson (10)

The debate

The alien-powers idea was first raised in 1999, when Mitchell A. Bailey proposed that the megatherians are Erebus, Abaia and the other powers themselves [Mitchell A. Bailey, 1999], and Tony Ellis argued that the Erebus entities are alien lifeforms, citing Jonas's inset tale of the woman who returned from space with beans [Tony Ellis, 1999]. Daniel Fusch called that reading farfetched though interesting [Daniel Fusch, 1999].

The idea returned in 2005 and after. Chris P argued that Abaia and Erebus are aliens and questioned whether cacogens fits them, pointing to the life cycle of Baldanders's people [Chris P, 2005]. Andy Robertson offered non-supernatural godlike beings [Andy Robertson, 2005]. In 2008, John Smith proposed Abaia as an alien monster from the stars, linked to the black beans [John Smith, 2008], and David Stockhoff suggested the powers may not be native to Urth [David Stockhoff, 2008] [David Stockhoff, 2008]. Jordon Flato floated alien invaders [Jordon Flato, 2009]. Dave Lebling argued instead that the megatherians are fallen angels like devils [Dave Lebling, 2008]. Later, Lee Berman framed them as an angel-and-fallen-angel pair with the Hierogrammates [Lee Berman, 2011], and Larry Miller argued that Typhon's original body was a megatherian [Larry Miller, 2011].

The main rivals were human-origin and symbolic readings. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued that Baldanders is a self-made human monster following the Megatherian pattern for immortality [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2006], and later that the Megatherians are demonic counterparts of the cacogens [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2007]. Chris P argued the megatherians are ideas rather than individuals [Chris P, 2004], b sharp mapped them onto communist states [b sharp, 2006], and David Stockhoff proposed twisted human rulers of a future Urth [David Stockhoff, 2011]. Lee Berman also argued that the megatherians and aliens are all human in origin [Lee Berman, 2011]. The threads 'do the Hierogrammates care about the megatherians?' and 'Lives of the Great Beasts' pitted James Wynn against Lee Berman and Gerry Quinn over whether these beings are alien great beasts, and both ended without agreement [James Wynn, 2011].

The debate never reached consensus. Posters kept citing the same Jonas tale and the same Typhon and Scylla passages to reach opposite conclusions, and the alien reading and the human-origin reading both persisted to the end of the record.

For

  • Ellis argued the Erebus entities are alien lifeforms, citing Jonas's magic-beans tale in The Shadow of the Torturer [Tony Ellis, 1999].
  • John Smith argued Abaia is an alien monster from the stars, quoting the woman who returned with black beans [John Smith, 2008].
  • Stockhoff argued Abaia and Erebus may not be native to Urth, and that Vodalus describes powers from Erebus wanting humanity purified [David Stockhoff, 2008] [David Stockhoff, 2008].
  • Flato suggested Abaia and Erebus could be alien invaders, citing references to other sentient species in the Book [Jordon Flato, 2009].
  • Lee Berman and Dave Lebling compared the megatherians to fallen angels, which would give them their own cosmic standing [Lee Berman, 2011] [Dave Lebling, 2008].

Against

  • Fusch called the alien-lifeform idea farfetched, though interesting [Daniel Fusch, 1999].
  • James Wynn argued the megatherians are powers on Urth like Typhon without human followers, so the Hierogrammates likely do not care about them [James Wynn, 2011].
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued Baldanders is a self-made human monster and the megatherians are demonic counterparts of the cacogens [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2006] [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2007].
  • Chris P argued the megatherians are ideas or ideologies such as totalitarianism rather than individual beings [Chris P, 2004].
  • Stockhoff and Lee Berman argued the megatherians and aliens are mutated humans who grew large in pursuit of immortality [David Stockhoff, 2011] [Lee Berman, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the alien and human-origin readings both persisted through the record, and no consensus emerged.
