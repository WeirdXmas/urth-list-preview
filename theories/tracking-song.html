Tracking Song and its world

"Tracking Song" is set on another world (an asteroid or moon), Cutthroat is a dog or a dogman, and the story reworks The Call of the Wild.

69 threads · 79 posts · for 66 / against 32 · first raised 1997-08-03 by Michael Andre-Driussi
Main voices: Michael Andre-Driussi (15), Matthew Groves (15), Alice Turner (12), mary whalen (12), Roy C. Lackey (12), Robert Borski (6)

The debate

The compound theory was first raised in pieces in 1997-1998, before the setting, dog and London claims were joined. Michael Andre-Driussi proposed Cutthroat as a dead sailor [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997] and Alice Turner argued he fell from the Great Sleigh [Alice Turner, 1997]. Dave Lebling proposed in 1998 that the narrator is a dog-man on a world that may not be Earth, citing two moons and odd trees [Dave Lebling, 1998]. Alice Turner called the domestic-dog idea new and worth developing [Alice Turner, 1998], and John Bishop argued the setting was another Mars because Phobos and Deimos would not look as Wolfe's text suggests [John Bishop, 1998].

The setting drew the most sustained argument. Michael Andre-Driussi argued for an Ice Age world and then a Mars-like world with a terraforming mirror, raising objections to tidal-lock and Roche-limit problems [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997] [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998]. Bishop argued for North America [John Bishop, 1997]. Dave Lebling suggested in 2007 that the setting could be an asteroid with moons, citing the Great Sleigh's two-week circuit of the body [Dave Lebling, 2007], and Daniel D Jones later questioned whether an asteroid that size could hold an atmosphere [Dave Lebling, 2007]. In 2014 Antonin Scriabin argued for a distant planet or moon, pressing the time gap between colonists and the rescue team [Antonin Scriabin, 2014]. Matthew Groves later disputed the priority of the dog idea, pointing to an archived Lebling post [Matthew Groves, 2007], which led to a thread over attribution.

The Call of the Wild reading and the dog reading were argued mainly in June 2007. Matthew Groves argued Cutthroat is a sleigh dog and that the story is a reversed Buck narrative [Matthew Groves, 2007] [Matthew Groves, 2007]. Roy C. Lackey agreed the story models London but said the Great Sleigh link was unconvincing [Roy C. Lackey, 2007]. Matthew King argued the arc resembles White Fang more than Call of the Wild [Matthew King, 2007]. Roy Lackey rejected the dog-derived beastman reading using Cutthroat's off-world gear [Roy C. Lackey, 2007], and Groves then argued Cutthroat is a man, not a dog [Matthew Groves, 2007]. The 2007 exchanges ended with the dog reading largely abandoned, and the setting question was never settled.

For

  • Dave Lebling proposed in 1998 that the narrator is a dog-man and that the world may not be Earth, citing two moons and odd trees [Dave Lebling, 1998].
  • Dave Lebling suggested the setting could be an asteroid, citing the Great Sleigh's two-week circuit of the body [Dave Lebling, 2007].
  • Matthew Groves argued Cutthroat is a sleigh dog and that the story is a reversed counterpart to The Call of the Wild [Matthew Groves, 2007] [Matthew Groves, 2007].
  • Roy C. Lackey agreed the story is modeled on Jack London's novel and pointed to the fourteen-dog sled team detail [Roy C. Lackey, 2007].
  • Antonin Scriabin argued the time gap between colonists and rescue team implies a distant planet or moon rather than Earth's moon [Antonin Scriabin, 2014].

Against

  • Michael Andre-Driussi argued the world is Mars-like with a terraforming mirror, citing radiation, day length and the Roche limit against a tide-locked satellite [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998].
  • Roy C. Lackey disputed the beastman reading, citing Cutthroat's off-world suit, pocket knife, firestarter and recorder [Roy C. Lackey, 2007].
  • Matthew Groves later argued Cutthroat is a man, not a dog, and that the Sleigh dogs are men [Matthew Groves, 2007].
  • Roy C. Lackey argued Cutthroat is a man like us, not a dog with an ancestor, citing the wolfmen's tribal use of the word man [Roy C. Lackey, 2007].
  • Matthew King argued Cutthroat's arc resembles White Fang rather than The Call of the Wild [Matthew King, 2007].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the setting, dog and London readings were never settled, and in the 2007 exchanges Cutthroat was more often argued to be a man than a dog.
