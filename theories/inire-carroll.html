Father Inire is Lewis Carroll

Father Inire, with his mirrors, is modelled on Lewis Carroll.

5 threads · 6 posts · for 2 / against 1 · first raised 1998-04-23 by William H. Ansley
Main voices: William H. Ansley (1), Michael Andre-Driussi (1), Dan Parmenter (1), Charles Dye (1), Alex David Groce (1), Dave Tallman (1)

The debate

The Carroll link to Father Inire was first raised on the list by William H. Ansley in April 1998, in a syllabus proposal that listed Carroll's Alice books among influences and noted Father Inire's resemblance to Carroll [William H. Ansley, 1998]. The same week Michael Andre-Driussi reported a consensus linking Lewis Carroll to Father Inire while relaying a 1983 Wolfe interview about Peake [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998]. Dan Parmenter then asked whether Wolfe had confirmed the modelling, calling it vaguer than the Borges-Ultan parallel [Dan Parmenter, 1998].

The discussion became more specific in September 1998, when Charles Dye argued that Father Inire is unlike Carroll in manner, since he takes Domnina away and frightens her, yet mirrors and little girls still link them, and he pointed to the word 'pleasance' as a further Carrollian touch [Charles Dye, 1998]. The thread 'Musings on the Hierodule fieldguide' shows that a joke about Dodgson drew a misreading as an accusation of child abuse, which Ansley cleared up while agreeing that a Carroll connection exists [Charles Dye, 1998]. In April 1999 Alex David Groce defended Father Inire in a thread mainly about Peace, naming Barrie and Flann O'Brien as sources [Alex David Groce, 1999]. The last post, in June 2008, came from Dave Tallman, who asked whether readers of 'The Cat' had noticed Alice-like echoes, especially the Cheshire Cat, and listed six parallels [Dave Tallman, 2008].

Across the record the link was mostly raised as a resemblance rather than tested, with few posters supplying evidence beyond the mirrors and the child-related details. The posts do not record any Wolfe confirmation of the modelling, and the only direct question about that was left unanswered.

For

  • Ansley says Father Inire resembles Carroll and proposes Carroll's Alice books as an influence on Wolfe [William H. Ansley, 1998].
  • Driussi reports a consensus linking Carroll to Father Inire [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998].
  • Dye cites shared mirrors and little girls, plus the word 'pleasance', as Carrollian links [Charles Dye, 1998].
  • Tallman lists six parallels with Alice in Wonderland, including the cat's vanishing and mirrors opening another world [Dave Tallman, 2008].

Against

  • Dye argues Father Inire is unlike Carroll in manner, since he takes Domnina away, frightens her and speaks general relativity [Charles Dye, 1998].
  • Parmenter notes that Wolfe's confirmation is absent and finds the Carroll link vaguer than the Ultan parallel [Dan Parmenter, 1998].

Where it stands: Still unconfirmed and loosely held: the link was repeatedly asserted as a resemblance, with Ansley accepting a Carroll connection but no Wolfe statement ever confirming that Father Inire is modelled on Carroll.
