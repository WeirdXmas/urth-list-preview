Who Hethor is

Hethor, the stuttering sailor who follows Severian, is a face of something larger (a fallen counterpart of Tzadkiel, a crewman from a ship, the older or younger self of another character).

32 threads · 61 posts · for 38 / against 25 · first raised 1998-03-14 by Michael Andre-Driussi
Main voices: Lee Berman (23), Michael Andre-Driussi (5), C R Culver (5), David Stockhoff (5), Gerry Quinn (4), Tony Ellis (3)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list on 1998-03-14 by Michael Andre-Driussi, who argued that the Antechamber prisoners descend from a starship crew and tied Hethor to that crew [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998]. Early 1998 posts mostly argued about Hethor's name and language rather than his larger identity: J. Schultz proposed he was a fugitive captain of the Quazar [J. Schultz, 1998], and Andre-Driussi later linked him to Yesod and the ship Tzadkiel [mournings glory, 2006]. The 'face of Tzadkiel' framing first appears in 2003, when Andre-Driussi proposed Hethor as one of Tzadkiel's many faces [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2003].

From 2006 the theory was revived on the list. Mournings glory argued that Hethor and Purn are younger and older versions of one man [mournings glory, 2006], and b sharp proposed Hethor as a shapeshifting former ship captain possibly like the fallen angel Tzadkiel [b sharp, 2006]. Macronaut suggested Hethor could have been a crewman on Tzadkiel's ship without special powers [Macronaut, 2006], and Matthew King floated a fallen counterpart of Tzadkiel [Matthew King, 2006]. From 2010 to 2014 Lee Berman became the main proponent, arguing that Hethor was Tzadkiel's second-in-command, a broken-off piece of Tzadkiel, or a form of Tzadkiel himself [Lee Berman, 2011] [Lee Berman, 2013] [Lee Berman, 2014].

Many posters resisted. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes conceded Hethor once crewed the Ship but denied that shared service made him identical to Tzadkiel or Father Inire [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2011], and Jerry Friedman asked for evidence and argued that only one crewman makes many time trips [Jerry Friedman, 2011]. Marc Aramini held that Hethor's debased character is unfit for a guardian angel [Marc Aramini, 2014], and David Stockhoff read Hethor as a symbol of lust, power and technology whose ship crashed on Urth [David Stockhoff, 2011]. Berman defended the link against these objections, citing Tzadkiel's many forms and the fallen-angel tradition [Lee Berman, 2014].

For

  • Hethor and Purn may be younger and older versions of one man, given a six-point case, including a linguistic link proposed later [mournings glory, 2006] [mournings glory, 2006].
  • Hethor may have been a crewman on Tzadkiel's ship, possibly travelling back in time [Macronaut, 2006] [Matthew King, 2006].
  • Hethor may be a broken-off piece of Tzadkiel, or a form of Tzadkiel, based on his mutiny talk and his mirrors [Lee Berman, 2013] [Lee Berman, 2014].
  • Hethor, like Father Inire, came from Tzadkiel's ship, as shown by the mutiny, the look-alike jiber and Inire's letter [Lee Berman, 2014] [Lee Berman, 2011].
  • Hethor's mirrors, his fondness for dolls and his service to Severian are cited as signs of a single angelic shapeshifter [Lee Berman, 2011] [Lee Berman, 2011].

Against

  • Shared service on the ship is too weak a basis for identity with Tzadkiel or Father Inire, since many people crewed it [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2011].
  • Lost ships are many, and only one crewman making many time trips needs evidence [Jerry Friedman, 2011].
  • Hethor's debased character is too unfit for a guardian angel, and his manipulation by Agia argues against it [Marc Aramini, 2014].
  • Hethor is a symbol of lust and technology whose ship crashed on Urth, and he seems content as Agia's lover [David Stockhoff, 2011].
  • Hethor's ship was lost in time like Jonas's, rather than being Tzadkiel's ship, and the old-fashioned names point to a nearer Urth time [Gerry Quinn, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed: Berman and a few posters kept the Tzadkiel link alive for over a decade, while most who replied, including Stockhoff, Friedman and Aramini, did not accept it, and the threads ended in impasse.
