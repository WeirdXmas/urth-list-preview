Michael Mauss is Mickey Mouse

In "Three Fingers", Michael Mauss is literally (or metaphorically) Mickey Mouse; the rival readings make him insane or sane and really caught by the "Mickey Mafia".

4 threads · 4 posts · for 4 / against 2 · first raised 1998-06-14 by adam louis stephanides
Main voices: adam louis stephanides (4), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (3)

The debate

The Mickey Mouse idea first appears on the list on 1998-06-14, when adam louis stephanides laid out three competing readings of the ending of "Three Fingers": Michael Mauss is insane, Michael is sane and really caught by the Mickey Mafia, or Michael is literally Mickey Mouse [adam louis stephanides, 1998]. The next day Dan'l Danehy-Oakes took the literal side, arguing the Disney characters are real and not pretending, and rejected the hallucination reading [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 1998].

Stephanides then shifted to a metaphorical version, arguing that Michael rather than the corporate suits in the final scene is in touch with the mythical sources Mickey derives from, extending an earlier afterthought about the unmasking scene [adam louis stephanides, 1998]. On 1998-06-16 Danehy-Oakes answered Dan Parmenter by reading the paunchy suited figures as villain archetypes (the Queen, Hook and Wolf) rather than Disney characters in masks, a move that cuts against the Mickey identification of those figures [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 1998]. The record contains only four posts, so the longer thread exchanges are not separately cited.

For

  • Danehy-Oakes argued the story should be read literally, with the Disney characters real, grounding this in a 'Roach Principle' that surface meaning is most likely the deep meaning [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 1998].
  • Stephanides argued the metaphorical version, that Michael is closer to Mickey's mythic sources than the corporate figures are [adam louis stephanides, 1998].

Against

  • Stephanides's first reading held that Michael is insane, grounded in the sedative reserpine used as a tranquilizer [adam louis stephanides, 1998].
  • Stephanides rejected the literal Mickey reading on the implausibility of the Disney plot and on Michael being human [adam louis stephanides, 1998].
  • Danehy-Oakes later read the suited figures as villain archetypes who look that way because of their world, not as Disney characters in masks [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 1998].

Where it stands: Raised in 1998 and still disputed when the record ends: the posters restated their readings without reaching agreement, and the theory was never widely accepted.
