Blue is Ushas

The planet Blue of the Short Sun is Ushas (the renewed Urth after the New Sun), linking the two series.

126 threads · 169 posts · for 77 / against 83 · first raised 1997-02-05 by xury
Main voices: Marc Aramini (37), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (17), Lee Berman (15), Don Doggett (10), Roy C. Lackey (8), James Wynn (7)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by xury in February 1997, who asked whether the Long Sun planet Blue could be Severian's Urth, and Kieran Cleary followed that month by proposing Blue is Ushas to tie the Long Sun books to The Book of the New Sun [xury, 1997][Kieran Cleary, 1997]. Through 1998 to 2000, Nicholas Gevers, Kevin J. Maroney, Jim Henley and john pirker kept the Blue-as-Ushas and Blue-as-Urth readings alive, with Maroney arguing relativistic distortion and Henley floating a cold-blooded inhumi reading [Nicholas Gevers, 1999][Kevin J. Maroney, 1999][Jim Henley, 2000][john pirker, 2000]. Interest peaked in 2002 when Marc Aramini posted a long run of proposals, including a maize-genetics reading of On Blue's Waters and a claim that Blue is Urth, drawing responses from Andy Robertson and Joe Cilluffo [Marc Aramini, 2002][Andy Robertson, 2002][Marc Aramini, 2002][Joe Cilluffo, 2002].

Disputes grew through 2003 to 2011. Jeff Veyera and David DiGiacomo argued against the Green-Urth, Blue-Ushas pairing on astronomical and textual grounds, and Roy C. Lackey rejected a Silk-Typhon link [Jeff Veyera, 2003][Roy C. Lackey, 2003]. In December 2007 Chris P, Matthew Groves and Pedro Pereira debated whether Wolfe had unequivocally denied the Blue-Green-Urth identity, with Groves citing a 2006 archived answer to James Jordan [Matthew Groves, 2007][Pedro Pereira, 2007]. In 2010 and 2011 Marc Aramini, Lee Berman and James Wynn repeatedly reversed positions, for example Aramini moving from Green-is-Urth to Blue-is-not-Urth and Berman arguing Blue is Ushas, a reborn planet [Marc Aramini, 2010][Lee Berman, 2011]. Later posts by Andreas Johansson, Gerry Quinn and Dan Harris revived symbolic and time-travel readings in 2013 and 2014 [Andreas Johansson, 2013][Gerry Quinn, 2013][Dan Harris, 2014].

The thread digests describe the discussion as repeatedly stalling. Several threads ended in impasse, with posters disputing the same passages and little consensus [Chris P, 2007][Jane Delawney, 2011]. Some threads turned personal, and a newcomer was redirected to existing archives in the last post of the set [Scyetletull letetuwathe, 2004].

For

  • Blue is Ushas, with the Whorl returning to a renewed Urth, was argued by Kieran Cleary and later by Marc Aramini using the maize and tree-hybridization passages [Kieran Cleary, 1997][Marc Aramini, 2002][Marc Aramini, 2002].
  • Blue could be a pre-Flood or flooded later world after a brighter sun replaced the dying one, citing Severian's mild equatorial summers and the green moon [Scyetletull letetuwathe, 2004].
  • Lee Berman listed six similarities between Blue and Ushas, including a flooded world, separated islands, refugees from a spaceship and mermaids [Lee Berman, 2011].
  • Matthew Groves argued that the Book of the Short Sun is thematically about returning, supported by the corn genetics and hyacinth symbols at the book's end [Matthew Groves, 2009].
  • Andreas Johansson proposed a symbolic link in which Typhon sent the Whorl to colonize a planet resembling a pristine Urth, and Gerry Quinn supported the subtler reading [Andreas Johansson, 2013][Gerry Quinn, 2013].

Against

  • Jeff Veyera argued that Hoof describes Green as bigger on the Red Sun whorl, undercutting the Green-Urth, Blue-Ushas identification [Jeff Veyera, 2003].
  • David DiGiacomo argued that travel between the two worlds would be impossible if they already occupied Urth and Lune.
  • Pedro Pereira and Chris P argued that Wolfe never clearly denied the solar-system link, and Chris compared the theory to a Templar conspiracy, so the Blue-Green system is not Urth remains unproven [Pedro Pereira, 2007][Chris P, 2007].
  • James Wynn argued that Blue is not Ushas, citing Quetzal's Whorl politics on Green and Wolfe's ironic reply that Urth is Green [James Wynn, 2010].
  • Jordon Flato rejected the Blue-Ushas idea by arguing Typhon could not have known the fix for the black hole [Jordon Flato, 2009].

Where it stands: Still disputed and never settled: the list stalled in repeated impasses with no shared conclusion, and the Blue-as-Urth or Blue-as-Ushas reading was opposed by many posters from 1999 to 2014 and never accepted by consensus.
