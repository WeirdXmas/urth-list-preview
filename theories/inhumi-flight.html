How the inhumi cross between worlds

The inhumi do not really fly between Blue and Green; they lie about it and travel by lander or by dream.

51 threads · 86 posts · for 52 / against 43 · first raised 2000-02-11 by Alice Turner
Main voices: Lee Berman (19), James Wynn (11), James Jordan (6), Nicholas Gevers (6), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (5), Roy C. Lackey (5)

The debate

The idea that the inhumi only pretend to fly between Blue and Green surfaces early in the Whorl list's record. Robert Borski first proposed in 2000 that the wing-flight is colonist folklore and that the inhumi reach Blue by an oneiric transport like Incanto's [Robert Borski, 2000]. Alice Turner's relay of the Birthday Bear's views on inhumi cycles between the worlds is the earliest post on the list [Alice Turner, 2000]. Through 2001 the Long Sun list argued mostly about teleportation versus passive astral travel, with Nicholas Gevers, James Jordan, Adam Stephanides and William Ansley contesting the mechanism [Nicholas Gevers, 2001] [James Jordan, 2001] [Adam Stephanides, 2001].

The lander version of the theory took shape in the Short Sun era. Thalassocrat argued in 2007 that the inhumi lie about flying and that only Auk's lander could carry them [thalassocrat, 2007], and later proposed Auk reprogrammed the lander for inhumi travel [thalassocrat, 2007]. Eric Ortlund countered that the inhumi may be moved by Neighbor spaceships, citing their arrival in waves every few years [Eric Ortlund, 2008]. Roy Lackey disputed the lie reading, arguing the void-flight could not be literal without waiting for conjunction and comparing it to astral travel [Roy C. Lackey, 2007]. Lee Berman then backed Thalassocrat's lander version in 2010 [Lee Berman, 2010], and in 2011 the theory became central to a long debate in which Berman, Craig Brewer and Gerry Quinn argued the inhumi cannot survive space or fly unaided, while Dan'l Danehy-Oakes and David Stockhoff defended flight as true or at least a serious reading [Lee Berman, 2011] [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2011] [Gerry Quinn, 2011].

In 2012 Berman proposed that the secret is that the inhumi stow away on human ships, citing Krait's hitchhiking onto Horn's boat and a Nick Gevers interview [Lee Berman, 2012] [Lee Berman, 2012]. Antonio Pedro Marques then argued that the inhumi likely do travel by flight, pointing to their altitude flights and their surge during conjunction [António Pedro Marques, 2012]. The question was still being reargued in 2013 [Lee Berman, 2013], with no post on the list settling it.

For

  • Borski proposed that the flight is folklore and that the inhumi reach Blue by an oneiric transport [Robert Borski, 2000].
  • Thalassocrat argued that the physics and the lander's capacity show the inhumi cannot fly between Blue and Green, so only Auk's lander could carry them [thalassocrat, 2007].
  • Craig Brewer cited Fava's death in the snow during astral travel and Jahlee's remark about thin air as signs the space-travel claim is a myth [Craig Brewer, 2009].
  • Lee Berman argued the inhumi cannot survive the cold of space and that the flight account must be a lie, pointing to Fava lying dead in the snow [Lee Berman, 2011].
  • Lee Berman argued that Krait's hitching a ride onto Horn's boat and then the lander shows the inhumi's skill is stowing away rather than technology [Lee Berman, 2012].

Against

  • Eric Ortlund proposed that the Neighbors carried inhumi physically by spaceship, citing their arrival in waves every few years [Eric Ortlund, 2008].
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued the inhumi really fly through space because their consistent accounts are better explained by truth than by a conspiracy [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2011].
  • Roy Lackey argued the void-flight cannot be literal without conjunction timing, comparing it to astral travel rather than a lander [Roy C. Lackey, 2007].
  • James Wynn argued the inhumi story about Blue is true because attacks would need landers or Dream-Travel to reach Blue [James Wynn, 2009].
  • António Pedro Marques argued the inhumi likely do travel between Blue and Green, reasoning from their flight at altitude and their surge during conjunction [António Pedro Marques, 2012].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the lie-and-lander or lie-and-dream reading was never settled, and the list ended with the flight question open, with Berman and Thalassocrat's lander version and the flight defenders holding their positions.
