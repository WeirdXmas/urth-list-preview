Merryn is Severian's sister

Merryn, the Cumaean's acolyte, is Severian's lost sister (or otherwise his kin).

42 threads · 52 posts · for 19 / against 27 · first raised 1997-05-23 by Geoff Alex Cohen
Main voices: Lee Berman (8), Robert Borski (7), Gerry Quinn (5), David Stockhoff (5), Geoff Alex Cohen (2), Michael Andre-Driussi (2)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by Geoff Alex Cohen in May 1997, who reported a remark from a live chat with Wolfe that Merryn is almost certainly Severian's sister [Geoff Alex Cohen, 1997], and in June 1997 he argued for it from the names Severian and Severa and the female children sent to the witches [Geoff Alex Cohen, 1997]. Michael Andre-Driussi soon backed a version of it, offering Clute and Feeley essays and Ava's question about the names [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997], and in January 1998 he proposed Merryn as Severian's twin sister [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998]. Terence Nickolette challenged the family reading on textual grounds [Terence Nickolette, 1998].

Through 1998 to 2001 the argument split into rival candidates. Robert Borski proposed Barbea as Severa [Robert Borski, 1998] and later argued Merryn is the daughter of the Cumaean and an alien [Robert Borski, 2001]. Kieran Mullen and Peter Westlake disputed Merryn's sisterhood [Kieran Mullen, 1998] [Peter Westlake, 1998], while Matthew Malthouse and Adam Stephanides defended a sibling reading from the text [Matthew Malthouse, 2001] [Adam Stephanides, 2001]. In 2006 to 2007 the question of Merryn's unchanged appearance and failure to recognise Severian took over, with Roy Lackey, mournings glory and Maclean Jessica arguing about memory and age [Roy C. Lackey, 2006] [mournings glory, 2006] [Maclean Jessica, 2007], and Ian S proposed time travel [Ian S, 2007].

From 2010 onward Lee Berman, David Stockhoff and Gerry Quinn fought over whether Severa is Merryn, Jolenta or someone else. Berman argued for Jolenta [Lee Berman, 2010] [Lee Berman, 2011], Stockhoff for Merryn [David Stockhoff, 2011] [David Stockhoff, 2011], and Quinn disputed any sexual or witch-based reasoning [Gerry Quinn, 2011] [Gerry Quinn, 2011]. Threads such as "Severa" and "Jahlee and Merryn" ended in impasse [David Stockhoff, 2011] [Lee Berman, 2011].

For

  • Wolfe is reported to have said Merryn is almost certainly or probably Severian's sister, cited by several posters [Geoff Alex Cohen, 1997] [mary whalen, 1998] [António Pedro Marques, 2010].
  • Severian and Severa are twin-like names, and female children of prisoners went to the witches, so Merryn as a witch fits the sister role [Geoff Alex Cohen, 1997] [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • Merryn's line calling Severian her brother, and the sibling hints in the text, are read as confirming kinship [Adam Stephanides, 2001] [Tom Hopkins, 2010].
  • The Inn of Lost Loves revelation and matching ages of Severian and Merryn support a twin reading, according to Andre-Driussi [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998].
  • Merryn's failure to change in appearance across years and her witch status are cited as signs she is an unusual relative [Maclean Jessica, 2007] [Roy C. Lackey, 2007].

Against

  • Merryn's age and unchanged appearance make her an unlikely sister, and she does not recognise Severian in the Stone Town [Roy C. Lackey, 2006] [Lee Berman, 2011].
  • Severian's many sexual partners cannot all be kin, and a sexual relation cannot show a sibling bond [Gerry Quinn, 2011] [David Stockhoff, 2011].
  • Severian's silence about a sister in the New Sun argues against any sibling, as Jeff Wilson suggested in a thread [Lee Berman, 2010].
  • Merryn's supposed sibling line may only mean torturers in general, so it does not prove kinship [Adam Stephanides, 2001].
  • Peter Westlake and Kieran Mullen argued the homunculus and the textual drift away from family make the sibling reading weak [Peter Westlake, 1998] [Kieran Mullen, 1998].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never settled whether Merryn is Severian's sister, and the rival candidates Barbea, Jolenta and Severa kept the question open through 2014.
