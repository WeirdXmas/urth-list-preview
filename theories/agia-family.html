Agia and Agilus are Severian's kin

Agia and Agilus are related to Severian, as cousins or as Dorcas's grandchildren.

24 threads · 30 posts · for 25 / against 7 · first raised 1997-08-12 by Michael Andre-Driussi
Main voices: Lee Berman (11), Michael Andre-Driussi (3), Robert Borski (3), thalassocrat (3), David Stockhoff (3), b sharp (2)

The debate

The theory that Agia and Agilus are Severian's kin was first raised on the list by Robert Borski in 1998, who proposed that Dorcas survived Ouen's birth and that the twins descend from a second child of hers [Robert Borski, 1998]. Michael Andre-Driussi followed the next day by suggesting Agia could still be Dorcas's granddaughter, since family resemblance passes only one generation [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998]. Earlier, in 1997, Andre-Driussi and C R Culver had linked Agia and Dorcas to Lilith and Eve in gnostic readings, which is a different identification [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997][C R Culver, 1997].

From 2006 the theory was pressed mainly by b sharp, thalassocrat and Lee Berman, who argued for cousin, aunt, mother or Fechin-lineage relationships using ages, looks, hair colour, Pelerine ties and weapons symbolism [b sharp, 2008][thalassocrat, 2011][Lee Berman, 2011]. Lee Berman's later posts on Agia's four weapons and on Cyriaca as her mother drew heavy pushback from David Stockhoff, who asked for textual evidence and questioned whether the bequest wording implied a dead mother [David Stockhoff, 2011][David Stockhoff, 2011]. Tim O'Donnell admitted in 2008 that he could not be sure and that the support was circumstantial, while Greg Bates asked him to justify his reading [O'Donnell, Tim (BOSI), 2008]. Jason H. and Sergei Soloviev disputed the theory on grounds of authorial intent and the shop's history [Jason H, 2011][Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011].

The debate stayed in impasse across many threads, with posters such as Jerry Friedman, Dan'l Danehy-Oakes and Roy C. Lackey repeatedly objecting that the theory explains too much. Posters agreed the key question was the evidence standard rather than any single clue [b sharp, 2008][Jason H, 2011].

For

  • Dorcas survived Ouen's birth and may have had a second child who became an ancestor of Agia and Agilus, per Borski's quotations from the Lexicon Urthus and the books [Robert Borski, 1998].
  • Agia could still be Dorcas's granddaughter, since family resemblance passes only one generation [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998].
  • Catherine, Cyriaca and Agia may be linked to Severian's mother, with matching ages and nursing-cell memories as grounds [b sharp, 2006].
  • Agia and Agilus are Severian's cousins, supported by matching ages, heights, hair, face traits, Pelerine ties and the incest motif [b sharp, 2008].
  • Agia's leg injury in the fiacre crash echoes Jolenta's, which Larry Miller takes as a link between Agia, Agilus and Severian [Larry Miller, 2011].

Against

  • Sergei Soloviev argued that the shop could not have belonged to Dorcas's parents, since the siblings were not yet born and Dorcas's family lived in an abandoned part of Nessus [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011].
  • Jason H. argued that identity puzzles such as Agia as Severian's cousin are reader-made rather than authorial, and that only intended clues are worth solving [Jason H, 2011].
  • Tim O'Donnell called the evidence circumstantial and said Wolfe was not confirming it, while he himself doubted Wolfe intended any kinship [O'Donnell, Tim (BOSI), 2008].
  • David Stockhoff argued that a bequest implies death, so Cyriaca cannot be the living mother who left Agia's weapons [David Stockhoff, 2011].
  • Roy C. Lackey withdrew his earlier Dorcas-as-mother theory because the timeline does not permit it [O'Donnell, Tim (BOSI), 2008].

Where it stands: Still disputed and never settled: the list held the kinship reading as a minority view, with most posters judging the evidence circumstantial and the theory left in impasse across many threads.
