Doris, Lupine and Mary King

In The Sorcerer's House, Lupine is linked to Doris (the woman impersonating Doris on the road, or Doris herself), and Mary King may be Lupine's soul.

28 threads · 30 posts · for 20 / against 9 · first raised 1998-05-29 by Matthew Freestone
Main voices: Marc Aramini (7), James Wynn (5), Dave Tallman (5), Roy C. Lackey (5), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (2), Robert Pirkola (2)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by Matthew Freestone in 1998 only in passing; the card's core claim (Lupine linked to Doris, Mary King possibly Lupine's soul) emerged in 2010 when James Wynn, Dave Tallman and Roy C. Lackey began reading The Sorcerer's House closely, with Wynn floating that Mary King might be Lupine's soul [James Wynn, 2010] and Tallman proposing a circle-dance pattern tying Mary King to Lupine [Dave Tallman, 2010]. In mid-April 2010 the posts multiplied: Tallman argued Doris and Lupine are one person once called Mary King who split off her soul [Dave Tallman, 2010], Wynn reversed this so that Doris's body becomes Lupine while her soul stays with Bax [James Wynn, 2010], and Lackey argued Lupine took on Doris's semblance, citing Bax's account of the musty odor and the keys [Roy C. Lackey, 2010].

The main opposition came from Wynn himself, who disputed the identity reading by citing Doris's job, eating, sex and marriage and her apparent ignorance of Faeri [James Wynn, 2010], and from Gwern Branwen, who listed inconsistencies in age, youthfulness and the drive scene against identifying Doris with Lupine [Gwern Branwen, 2010]. Tallman countered Wynn's soul-stays-with-Bax reversal by pointing to the dark thing racing near the ground as Lupine's wolf form [Dave Tallman, 2010]. Mo Holkar supplied background on the real Mary King's Close in Edinburgh, arguing Wolfe probably used an obscure source or a naming purpose [Mo Holkar / UKG, 2010].

From 2018 the theory was carried mainly by Marc Aramini, who proposed that Lupine is a double of Doris Griffin [Marc Aramini, 2018] and that Lupine is impersonating Doris, supported by her stench in Doris's disguise and her lost wolf skin [Marc Aramini, 2018]; he later said Mary King and Kiki are the same figure [Marc Aramini, 2018]. Robert Pirkola proposed that Doris is Mary King, citing Kiki's palindromic name and Bax's letter calling Doris his American girlfriend [Robert Pirkola, 2018]. Those 2018 posts drew no recorded replies.

For

  • Tallman argued that Doris and Lupine are the same person, once known as Mary King, who split off her soul to become a werewolf [Dave Tallman, 2010].
  • Wynn floated that Mary King's ghost might be Lupine's soul, citing the she-wolf meaning of the name [James Wynn, 2010][James Wynn, 2010].
  • Lackey argued Lupine took Doris's appearance, citing Bax's account of the musty odor and the keys a girl gave him [Roy C. Lackey, 2010].
  • Aramini proposed Lupine impersonates Doris, citing Bax's inconsistent accounts, her stench in Doris's disguise and her lost wolf skin [Marc Aramini, 2018].
  • Pirkola proposed Doris is Mary King, citing Bax's letter calling Doris his American girlfriend and the mirrored chapter structure [Robert Pirkola, 2018].

Against

  • Wynn argued the text shows Doris and Lupine appearing separately and Doris behaving as a corporeal person, citing her job, eating, sex and marriage [James Wynn, 2010].
  • Branwen listed inconsistencies in age, Lupine's youthfulness, Doris's attitude toward Bax and the drive scene against the identification [Gwern Branwen, 2010].
  • Wynn argued Doris seemed genuinely clueless about Faeri when she handed over Ted's ring [James Wynn, 2010][James Wynn, 2010].
  • Tallman's reading was disputed by Wynn's soul-based reversal, and Tallman in turn said a bare soul would simply vanish like the car's passenger [Dave Tallman, 2010].
  • Holkar argued the real Mary King's Close is an obscure or unrelated source, so the name need not imply a ghost link [Mo Holkar / UKG, 2010].

Where it stands: Still disputed and unresolved: the 2010 thread ended with the identity question split between Tallman, Wynn, Lackey and Branwen, and the 2018 Aramini and Pirkola posts were left without replies.
