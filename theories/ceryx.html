Ceryx is Typhon's vizier

Ceryx, the necromancer in The Urth of the New Sun, is Typhon's vizier or a version of Father Inire.

23 threads · 33 posts · for 20 / against 10 · first raised 1998-01-30 by Michael Andre-Driussi
Main voices: Lee Berman (15), b sharp (5), Sergei SOLOVIEV (3), Dan Rabin (2), David Stockhoff (2), Larry Miller (2)

The debate

The vizier wording first appears in Lee Berman's post [Lee Berman, 2010] (December 2010), which proposed that the Outsider was a Urthly friend of Typhon and that Ceryx might be Typhon's mystical vizier, built on a Horn passage about minor gods invited aboard the Whorl. Earlier posts had identified Ceryx with Father Inire rather than with Typhon: Dan Rabin in [Dan Rabin, 1999] (1999) tied the staff-carrier to Inire, and Son of Witz in [Son of Witz, 2010] suggested Ceryx possibly was Inire.

In January 2011 James Wynn in [James Wynn, 2011] agreed with Borski's Inire identification, and Berman in [Lee Berman, 2011] proposed Inire as a nexus behind Ceryx. Berman's [Lee Berman, 2011] then stated outright that Ceryx is Inire, Typhon's vizier, while Sergei Soloviev in [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011] and [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011] disputed the link, calling Ceryx a potential Antichrist figure and the Typhon connection only an ordinary devil association. Berman defended the link with Simon Magus parallels in [Lee Berman, 2011], and Dan'l Danehy-Oakes in [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2011] dismissed the time-travel explanation without citing text.

In November 2011 Berman in [Lee Berman, 2011] returned to the idea, proposing that Ceryx is Typhon's vizier scouting a new wonderworker and citing Typhon's line about being grafted to Piaton. The question resurfaced in [Lee Berman, 2011], where Berman asked whether Hethor and Ceryx are the same figure as Inire, and in [António Pedro Marques, 2011], where Stockhoff suggested Ceryx as a candidate proto-Inire. David Stockhoff later asked in [David Stockhoff, 2018] whether Inire is Ceryx from the future. Threads such as the 'Ceryx (answer to Lee)' thread ended in impasse with no agreement.

For

  • Horn's remark about minor gods invited aboard the Whorl was read as suggesting Ceryx as Typhon's mystical vizier [Lee Berman, 2010]
  • Ceryx's human-headed staff and the monkey-head imagery were linked to Inire, supporting the identification that Berman and Wynn drew on [Lee Berman, 2011] [James Wynn, 2011]
  • Typhon's line about being grafted to Piaton was used to suggest that Ceryx served Typhon as a scout [Lee Berman, 2011]
  • Severian's Yesod comparison, read celestially, was taken to show that Ceryx has been to Abaddon and served Typhon [Lee Berman, 2011]
  • Stockhoff's remark that Inire may have served on ships and that Ceryx is a candidate proto-Inire was relayed as support for a link [António Pedro Marques, 2011]

Against

  • Soloviev argued the Ceryx-Typhon link is only an ordinary evildoer-and-devil association, with Typhon a subordinate power [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011]
  • Soloviev argued Severian's arrest and Citadel visit were not an order to take Ceryx to Typhon [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011]
  • Roy Lackey objected that Berman's speculations, including that Ceryx is female, lack textual evidence [Roy C. Lackey, 2010]
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes rejected the time-travel explanation for Severian's silence to his grandfather without textual support [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2011]

Where it stands: Raised by Berman, repeatedly restated by him, and never settled, with most posters disputing or leaving it open and several threads ending in impasse.
