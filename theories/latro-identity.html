Latro is Ares (or another god)

Latro is (or is possessed by) a god such as Ares or Pleistorus, a werewolf, or a man whose name can be recovered (Lucius).

51 threads · 83 posts · for 69 / against 17 · first raised 1997-06-18 by Michael Straight
Main voices: Marc Aramini (44), James Wynn (7), James Jordan (5), Chris P (5), David Stockhoff (5), Andrew Bollen (3)

The debate

The theory that Latro is a god, or is possessed by one, was first raised on the list by Marc Aramini ([Marc Aramini, 2003] and [Marc Aramini, 2003] in May 2003), who argued Latro might be an avatar of Ares or of Pleistorus, and by James Jordan, who proposed a related avatar reading the same month [James Jordan, 2003]. Earlier posts were more tangential: Russell Wodell asked in January 2003 whether the Greek gods were demons [Russell Wodell, 2003], and Chris P floated Ares possession in June 2003 [Chris P, 2003]. Andrew Bollen proposed in June 2003 that Latro is the historical Lucius Tarquitius [Andrew Bollen, 2003], a rival naming theory.

The discussion grew in 2006, when Marc Aramini restated and expanded the Pleistorus and Ares avatar readings across several posts [Marc Aramini, 2006] [Marc Aramini, 2006]. Chris P offered alternatives, either Ares possessing Latro at the time of his wound or Latro merely resembling Pleistorus [Chris P, 2006], and Stanisław Bocian argued for Ares possession using Greek battle-madness beliefs [Stanisław Bocian, 2006]. Jeff Wilson argued that the sphinx's claim to be Latro's mother is a conventional formula of divine patronage, not a literal divinity [Marc Aramini, 2011]. The werewolf reading, a separate strand, was pushed in December 2010 by Son of Witz, who pointed to Robert Borski's book [Son of Witz, 2010], and by Jerry Friedman, who cited passages from Soldier of the Mist [Jerry Friedman, 2010].

Aramini kept returning to the idea through 2011, 2013 and 2015, and in each stage other posters pressed back, mainly Chris P, Lee Berman, Jeff Wilson and David Stockhoff, who proposed a sequence of possessing gods [David Stockhoff, 2011]. The name-pun reading (latro as thief and latreia as worship) took over from 2010 onward, with David Stockhoff and Mo Holkar disputing it [David Stockhoff, 2010] [Mo Holkar / UKG, 2015]. the list's threads end without convergence.

For

  • Latro's Greek skill and combat ability are explained by Ares or another god inhabiting him, per Marc Aramini's June 2003 argument [Marc Aramini, 2003].
  • Thamyris calls Latro Pleistorus, and Aramini takes this as a sign of divinity [Marc Aramini, 2003] [Marc Aramini, 2006].
  • Latro's injury may have let Ares possess him, a reading argued by Chris P and Stanisław Bocian [Chris P, 2006] [Stanisław Bocian, 2006].
  • The Olympic contest and the lesson of restraint are read as Ares learning compassion, per Aramini [Marc Aramini, 2006] [Marc Aramini, 2011].
  • The werewolf reading gained support from Jerry Friedman's citing of the wolf's tooth and the Spu episode, and from Son of Witz's praise of Borski [Jerry Friedman, 2010] [Son of Witz, 2010].

Against

  • Chris P argued that casting Latro as Ares would weaken the story, comparing him to Hector [Chris P, 2003].
  • Jeff Wilson argued that the sphinx's claim to be Latro's mother is a conventional formula, not a literal divinity [Marc Aramini, 2011].
  • Lee Berman quoted Wolfe's remark that Ares is the soldier god rather than the war god, against the avatar reading [Lee Berman, 2011].
  • James Wynn noted that Latro's gods work like actors in different masks, and that no single god identity fits [James Wynn, 2006].
  • Mo Holkar argued the suffix in the name is too common to carry hidden meaning [Mo Holkar / UKG, 2015].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the Ares and Pleistorus readings were argued repeatedly by Marc Aramini but never accepted by other posters, and the werewolf and name-pun readings also remained open.
