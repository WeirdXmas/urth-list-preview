Severian shaped the torturers' guild

The torturers' guild descends from an older order (the Catholic priesthood) or was founded or shaped by Severian himself in the past.

8 threads · 15 posts · for 11 / against 3 · first raised 1997-06-16 by James Jordan
Main voices: mary whalen (4), Jason Voegele (2), Matthew Malthouse (2), Marc Aramini (2), James Jordan (1), Brandon Mason (1)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by James Jordan in June 1997, who proposed that Wolfe's answer makes the Order of Seekers the Catholic Church and the torturers' guild an antichrist copy [James Jordan, 1997]. In July 1998 Mary Whalen argued the guild descends from the Roman Catholic priesthood, citing black garb, religious-sounding titles and Ymar's ban on women [mary whalen, 1998], and Jason Voegele backed this with the broken-pews passage in Shadow XI.10 [Jason Voegele, 1998]. Matthew Malthouse disputed the priesthood reading, arguing that women members, the separate Citadel chapel and the lack of theological function make the guild resemble police or intelligence [Matthew Malthouse, 1998], and that unexplained gaps in time cannot justify it [Matthew Malthouse, 1998].

From 2003 the question shifted toward whether Severian himself founded or shaped the guild. Brandon Mason and Matthew DeLuca asked whether he founded it [Brandon Mason, 2003][Matthew DeLuca, 2003], and Son of Witz later argued he may have influenced its formation through his encounter with Ymar, the end of women torturers and his fuligin cloak [Son of Witz, 2009]. Marc Aramini argued in 2003 that the torturers are corrupt priests of a distorted Catholic church [Marc Aramini, 2003], and in 2012 he tied the torturer's priestly role to Christ's carpentry [Marc Aramini, 2012].

In 2008 Craig Brewer noted list speculation that the guild's secret is that it was once the Catholic Church or the Inquisition, while observing that Severian never explains the secret [Craig Brewer, 2008]. David Stockhoff then argued the guild could be a surviving fragment of the Roman Catholic Church preserved somewhere on far-future Urth, pointing to the 20,000-plus years separating Severian from the present [David Stockhoff, 2009]. The Severian-founder and Catholic-origin strands were argued across several threads, including the 'mystery of the guild' and 'different sev's and silk as seeker' discussions, which ended without consensus.

For

  • The guild's black garb and religious-sounding initiate titles suggest a priestly origin [mary whalen, 1998].
  • Ymar's ban on women torturers mirrors the Catholic exclusion of women from the priesthood [mary whalen, 1998].
  • The broken pews in the Feast of Holy Katharine passage and the guild history in Shadow XI.10 appear too closely paired to be coincidence [Jason Voegele, 1998].
  • Palaemon and Gurloes's guild secret, which Severian never elaborates, may be that the guild was once the Church [Craig Brewer, 2008].
  • Severian's encounter with Ymar, the end of women torturers and his fuligin cloak suggest he influenced the guild's formation [Son of Witz, 2009].

Against

  • Women members, the separate Citadel chapel and the lack of theological function make the guild look more like a police or intelligence force [Matthew Malthouse, 1998].
  • Unexplained gaps in time cannot justify a claim that torturers were once Catholic-style priests [Matthew Malthouse, 1998].
  • Wolfe's 'Yes, Silk' answer plays on the phrase's ambiguity rather than proving a priesthood of the Outsider [mary whalen, 1998].
  • Stockhoff's fragment idea is speculative, and the guild always served the monarch and never seemed powerful, which argues against a surviving Church [David Stockhoff, 2009].
  • Malthouse called Palaemon's origin history a developed mythology rather than evidence of a priesthood [Matthew Malthouse, 1998].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the priesthood and Severian-founder readings were argued repeatedly from 1997 to 2012 with no consensus, and the list never settled whether the guild has a religious origin.
