Hyacinth is Fava

Hyacinth and Fava (the dream-travelling inhuma of the Short Sun) are connected, or the same.

8 threads · 19 posts · for 12 / against 2 · first raised 2010-01-17 by James Wynn
Main voices: James Wynn (9), Dave Tallman (5), Marc Aramini (3), David Stockhoff (1), António Pedro Marques (1), Larry Miller (1)

The debate

The theory that Hyacinth is Fava was first raised on the list by James Wynn in January 2010, in a reply defending Hyacinth's characterization against brunian objections [James Wynn, 2010]. Wynn restated it in August 2010 after reading The Book of the Short Sun, asking how Seawrack fit and whether the Love was their only shared element [James Wynn, 2010]. Dave Tallman said he had never seen the identification proposed and asked for evidence [Dave Tallman, 2010], and Wynn replied that he would gather references later but offered none at first [James Wynn, 2010]. Tallman then quoted Wynn contrasting Hyacinth as a crude, combative prostitute with Fava as a witty, delicate inhuma, finding little else in common [Dave Tallman, 2010].

The debate sharpened in late August 2010 when Tallman questioned the coach dream in Return to the Whorl, where Mora turns into Hyacinth and Fava becomes Orpine [Dave Tallman, 2010]. Wynn admitted he could not explain that dream but still held Fava to be Hyacinth on the strength of other clues [James Wynn, 2010], then folded the idea into a grand unified theory involving Pike's Ghost, dream-travel and Simon Magus [James Wynn, 2010]. Tallman disputed a time-looped astral-form version, citing Fava's blonde hair against Hyacinth's black and objections about eating and grief [Dave Tallman, 2010], and Wynn rejected that version too [James Wynn, 2010]. Tallman then argued that dream Fava is blond, collecting hair-colour quotations [Dave Tallman, 2010].

From late 2010 through 2011 the idea was restated and defended in new threads. Wynn argued Hyacinth is the inhuma whose body died in dream-travel and that the Rajan brought her to the Whorl [James Wynn, 2010], and in November 2011 answered objections about sprats, eating, blondeness and Hyacinth's father [James Wynn, 2011]. Marc Aramini offered a rival reading in which the dolls Mucor and Fava retrieve Hyacinth's soul [Marc Aramini, 2011], later proposing that Fava is Hyacinth merging with her original body [Marc Aramini, 2011]. Larry Miller said the idea seemed good after rereading, while admitting he lacked the familiarity needed to find flaws [Larry Miller, 2011]. The later Hyacinth and Pike threads also show the identification as a live position without consensus.

For

  • Wynn's early claim that the theory follows from weighing all rivals and a shared Love connection [James Wynn, 2010][James Wynn, 2010]
  • Wynn's argument that Fava is still Hyacinth regardless of the coach dream, citing many other clues [James Wynn, 2010]
  • Wynn's grand unified theory citing flying Oreb, Hyacinth's light weight, the green umbrella and a Calde quotation [James Wynn, 2010]
  • Wynn's November 2011 defence against objections about sprats, eating, blondeness and Hyacinth's father [James Wynn, 2011]
  • Larry Miller's agreement that the idea seemed good after rereading [Larry Miller, 2011]

Against

  • Tallman's objection that Fava's blonde hair contradicts Hyacinth's black hair and that dream spirits do not eat [Dave Tallman, 2010]
  • Tallman's claim that the coach dream turns Mora into Hyacinth rather than Fava, which Wynn could not explain [Dave Tallman, 2010][James Wynn, 2010]
  • Wynn's own rejection of the time-looped astral-form version on family and textual grounds [James Wynn, 2010]
  • Wynn's contrast of Hyacinth as crude and combative with Fava as witty and delicate, finding little shared [Dave Tallman, 2010]
  • Aramini's alternative that Mucor and Fava are separate beings retrieving Hyacinth's soul [Marc Aramini, 2011]

Where it stands: Still disputed: Wynn kept defending the identification from 2010 to 2011 and Larry Miller agreed, but Tallman, Aramini and others offered rival readings and the list never reached consensus.
