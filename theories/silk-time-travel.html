Silk and Horn travel through time

Silk and Horn travel through time (by dreaming, or near light speed), or only through space.

43 threads · 57 posts · for 27 / against 26 · first raised 1997-02-05 by Michael Andre-Driussi
Main voices: Marc Aramini (9), Gerry Quinn (9), James Wynn (7), Roy C. Lackey (6), Michael Straight (3), Lee Berman (3)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by Michael Andre-Driussi in February 1997, who argued that Silk's ghostpike sequence follows dream-like steps and that the parents' images are props from Silk's mind [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997]. In the same year he argued that Silk's dreams show non-linear, retrograde event order [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997], while Alice Turner disputed that reading, calling the page references proofing errors and saying the fever dreams prove nothing [Alice Turner, 1997]. Dave Lebling argued that the corridor episode is a dream, citing a blue tunic and the fading of a phrase in Silk's writing [Dave Lebling, 1997].

From 2002 the debate was driven mostly by Marc Aramini, who proposed that Silk travels through time by dreaming and that his dreams alter the past [Marc Aramini, 2002][Marc Aramini, 2002]. Roy C. Lackey disputed this, arguing that Silkhorn's astral travel is spatial rather than temporal and that dream trips never change earlier events [Roy C. Lackey, 2002][Roy C. Lackey, 2002][Roy C. Lackey, 2003]. James Wynn, Adam Thornton and David Duffy then argued for time travel through relativity or the astral dream of Pike and Oreb [Adam Thornton, 2006][David Duffy, 2006][James Wynn, 2006], and in 2011 James Wynn and Marc Aramini extended this to Horn's dream-travel [James Wynn, 2011][Marc Aramini, 2011].

The 2011 Lake of the Long Sun threads were the largest sustained exchange. Gerry Quinn disputed the Pike-as-older-Silk reading, arguing that Silk cannot be a time-traveller who returns as an aged Pike and preferring a ghost-projecting explanation [Gerry Quinn, 2011][Gerry Quinn, 2011], while David Stockhoff and James Wynn traded arguments about Mucor and the Pike apparition [David Stockhoff, 2011][Gerry Quinn, 2011]. Several Quinn and Lee Berman posts argued over whether the dream images carry hidden clues [Gerry Quinn, 2010][Lee Berman, 2011], and the thread overall ended without agreement.

For

  • Silk's dreams show non-linear, retrograde event order, with the Mint and Marble passage cited as support [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1997].
  • Silk's astral travel, and Horn's dream-travel, are time travel because the astral Oreb fades while the real Oreb has a broken wing [James Wynn, 2006].
  • Silk's astral appearance as Pike is read as an older Silk who travelled through time [Gerry Quinn, 2011][James Wynn, 2011].
  • Dream-travel is time travel in the Horn books, supported by Pike's ghost, Oreb's astral flight and mismatched departure dates [James Wynn, 2011].
  • Silk and Horn could travel into the future by relativistic compression of the Whorl's journey [Adam Thornton, 2006].

Against

  • Silk's dreams and fever visions prove nothing about time travel, since they merge identities and can be read as psychological [Alice Turner, 1997][Andrew Mason, 2010].
  • Silkhorn's astral travel is spatial, supported by Tartaros's thousand-year remark and the Whorl's age [Roy C. Lackey, 2003][Roy C. Lackey, 2002].
  • Silk's dream trips never change earlier events, so Salica's tale cannot show time travel [Roy C. Lackey, 2002].
  • Silk cannot be a time-traveller who returns as an aged Pike, and the apparition may be a ghost-projecting technology or god [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • Silk's dream horses and images are parts of Silk, not clues to time travel [Gerry Quinn, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the thread ended in impasse with no consensus, and posters repeatedly restated their positions across Long Sun, Short Sun and Whorl discussions.
