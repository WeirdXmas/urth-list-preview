Kevin Malone is possessed

In "Kevin Malone", Kevin is possessed by the elder Mr. Malone (or the story is a Christian parable).

13 threads · 16 posts · for 12 / against 8 · first raised 1999-02-24 in the thread ""Kevin Malone""
Main voices: Tony Ellis (7), Roy C. Lackey (7), Peter Westlake (2), Allan Lloyd (2), Nigel Price (2), Marc Aramini (2)

The debate

The possession reading was first raised on the list by Tony Ellis in February 1999, when he argued that Kevin Malone is neither a ghost nor Betty Malone's son and that the narrator's final line about possession resolves the puzzle [Tony Ellis, 1999]. Peter Westlake reported that rereading made the ending clear and credited the hints from Roy Lackey and Ellis [Peter Westlake, 1999]. Ellis then stated the specific version that the elder Mr Malone possesses the younger Kevin, citing the servant's annexe, the claim that Betty Malone was buried on the estate, and Westlake's similar reading [Tony Ellis, 1999].

The debate quickly split over whether the mechanics fit the text. Roy Lackey argued that the timeline of The Pines rules out the elder Malone having grown up on the estate whose memories Kevin has [Roy C. Lackey, 1999], and later that Kevin's parents were a separate servant pair, with the estate itself possessing him and Marcella [Roy C. Lackey, 1999]. Ellis countered that the story never says how or when possession occurred, so the question does not matter, and that it has only two Malones [Tony Ellis, 1999][Tony Ellis, 1999]. Westlake and Kieran Mullen also took part, Westlake calling Ellis's version simpler and Mullen asking for it to be posted with spoiler warnings [Peter Westlake, 1999][Kieran Mullen, 1999].

The wider thread produced rival readings. Allan Lloyd proposed a Christian parable reading built on details such as the butler Priest and the lamb and eggs Benedict meals [Allan Lloyd, 1999], which Lackey disputed [Roy C. Lackey, 1999]. Nigel Price read the story as an allegory of grace [Nigel Price, 1999]. Later, in 2014, Marc Aramini revived the family theme by arguing that the age gaps mirror the older Kevin's guilt and that the younger Kevin is the older one's cycle [Marc Aramini, 2014]. William Ansley, in the thread summary, remained confused and noted that Ellis's reading undercuts Kevin's childhood memories of the house, and the thread ended without resolution.

For

  • Ellis argued that the narrator's final line about possession resolves the puzzle and that Kevin is neither a ghost nor Betty's son [Tony Ellis, 1999].
  • Ellis argued the elder Malone possesses the younger Kevin, citing the servant's annexe and the claim that Betty was buried on the estate [Tony Ellis, 1999].
  • Westlake said rereading made the ending clear and thought the puzzle was solved [Peter Westlake, 1999].
  • Westlake argued that Ellis's version is simpler and fits the story without needing many Malones in one house [Peter Westlake, 1999].
  • Lloyd offered Christian-parable clues, such as the Priest butler and the Benedict meals, as an alternative reading [Allan Lloyd, 1999].

Against

  • Lackey argued the elder Malone was born too late to have been raised on the estate, so possession by him fails on the timeline [Roy C. Lackey, 1999].
  • Ellis argued the story never says how or when possession occurred, so the mechanism does not matter [Tony Ellis, 1999].
  • Lackey argued Kevin's parents were a separate servant pair and that the estate itself possesses him [Roy C. Lackey, 1999].
  • Ellis argued the story has only two Malones and that the shared name is by design, against Lackey's second pair [Tony Ellis, 1999].
  • Lackey disputed Lloyd's parable reading, including the Yahweh and Christ figures and the single 'rich man' phrase [Roy C. Lackey, 1999].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the possession reading had early support from Ellis and Westlake, but the thread ended unresolved with Ansley's objection left open, and later posts moved to other readings.
