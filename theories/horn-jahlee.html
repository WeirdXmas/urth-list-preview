Horn and Jahlee

Horn gives Jahlee a human soul or gives his life for her at the end.

14 threads · 15 posts · for 9 / against 5 · first raised 2000-02-19 by Allan Lloyd
Main voices: James Jordan (4), Michael Straight (3), Dave Lebling (2), Adam Stephanides (1), Alice Turner (1), Allan Lloyd (1)

The debate

The theory that Horn gives Jahlee a human soul or gives his life for her was first raised on the list by Allan Lloyd in February 2000, who argued Horn's violence resurfaces in Silk's killing of Jahlee [Allan Lloyd, 2000]. The more specific idea appeared in August 2000, when James Jordan proposed that humans must be willing to die for the inhumi and predicted Horn would give his life for Jahlee [James Jordan, 2000]. In the same month Adam Stephanides disputed the Golden Rule reading of the secret [Adam Stephanides, 2000], and Alice Turner argued the last sentence of The Book of the Long Sun shows Horn protecting Jahlee [Alice Turner, 2000].

In January 2001 Michael Straight guessed the secret involves Horn's kindness to the inhumi, such as giving Jahlee a human soul, grounding this in Horn's remark about the Vanished People enslaving the inhumi [Michael Straight, 2001]. February 2001 brought rival explanations of Jahlee's death: Dave Lebling argued she attacks Nettle out of unrequited love for Horn [Dave Lebling, 2001], while Johan Akesson argued her love for Horn dated from Gaon [Johan Akesson, 2001]. Jordan then proposed Jahlee is in love with Horn and, after reversing course, that Krait and Jahlee are Horn's children [James Jordan, 2001][James Jordan, 2001].

Later posts moved away from the soul and sacrifice readings. In 2002 allanhlloyd proposed Horn keeps sharing blood with Krait and Jahlee [allanhlloyd, 2002], and Michael Straight questioned whether Horn-in-Silk dreamed Jahlee was Krait's mother [Michael Straight, 2002]. Cliff Judge read the 'kicked her like Auk' line plainly in 2007 [Cliff Judge, 2007], and in 2013 António Pedro Marques argued killing Jahlee is the moment Silk and Horn become one person [António Pedro Marques, 2013]. the list's thread summaries add that the inhumi secret discussion in 'Inhumi Secret' ended with Jordan conceding the secret likely stays unstated [James Jordan, 2000].

For

  • Jordan proposed humans must be willing to die for the inhumi and predicted Horn would give his life for Jahlee [James Jordan, 2000].
  • Straight grounded the soul idea in Horn's remark that the Vanished People enslaved the inhumi and fell, suggesting love could work where enslavement failed [Michael Straight, 2001].
  • Turner read the book's last sentence as Horn protecting Jahlee, paralleling his failed attempt to save the cold Fava [Alice Turner, 2000].
  • Lloyd argued Horn's earlier cruelty, including his desertion and the rape of Seawrack, makes his later transformation into Silk more striking [Allan Lloyd, 2000].

Against

  • Stephanides argued the die-for-them idea fails to explain inhumi blood drinking and Krait's mother drinking from Sinew [James Jordan, 2000].
  • Stephanides argued Horn had already stated Jahlee's cruelty-and-violence point, so the unspoken secret must be something else [Adam Stephanides, 2000].
  • Lebling argued Jahlee's death is straightforward, with Horn killing her for breaking the agreement not to touch his family [Dave Lebling, 2001].
  • Akesson argued the attack on Nettle was a crime of passion rather than an act of Jahlee's inhuman nature [Johan Akesson, 2001].
  • Jordan later argued Silk lacks Horn's inhumi experience and so kills Jahlee, which fits neither a sacrifice nor a soul gift [James Jordan, 2001].

Where it stands: Still disputed and unresolved: the sacrifice and soul readings were argued in 2000 and 2001 and never settled, with the secret judged likely to stay unstated and later posters shifting to other readings.
