Casdoe's household

The people in Casdoe's house are not who Severian assumes (the old person is a woman or a relative; Casdoe is linked to Dorcas or to Severian's family).

17 threads · 30 posts · for 18 / against 14 · first raised 1998-10-18 by Robert Borski
Main voices: Lee Berman (9), thalassocrat (6), b sharp (3), Roy C. Lackey (3), António Pedro Marques (2), Son of Witz (2)

The debate

The theory was first raised in 1998, when Robert Borski [Robert Borski, 1998] disputed the links between Fechin and Severian's grandfather, and Michael Andre-Driussi [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998] proposed that the Old Man in the hut may be Becan's father rather than Casdoe's.

The gender reading arrived in 2006, when b sharp [b sharp, 2006] argued that the old person in Casdoe's house is an old woman Severian mistook for a man. Roy C. Lackey [Roy C. Lackey, 2006] supported this with passages from The Sword of the Lictor, and b sharp extended it into a family-tree reading [b sharp, 2006]. In 2010 Lee Berman [Lee Berman, 2010] took up the misgendering argument within an Inire-in-disguise reading, and Roy C. Lackey [Roy C. Lackey, 2010] and Roy C. Lackey again [Roy C. Lackey, 2010] disputed the identifications for lack of textual grounding and for conflicts with the description of Fechin.

In 2011 the debate moved to symbolism and to Dorcas and Casdoe. Son of Witz [Son of Witz, 2011] and [Son of Witz, 2011] read Casdoe's house as a symbolic family for Severian, and Gerry Quinn [Gerry Quinn, 2011] supported that reading. Lee Berman [Lee Berman, 2011] argued that the old storyteller is a woman, and [Lee Berman, 2011] that the figure signals Fechin's identity. On the name link, Andrew Mason [Andrew Mason, 2010] proposed that Casdoe is Dorcas's niece, Antonio Pedro Marques [António Pedro Marques, 2010] proposed that the two names may be one person, and David Stockhoff [David Stockhoff, 2011] proposed that they are sisters.

The discussion produced no consensus. Threads such as "Sev's family tree/gender bender" and "Dorcas and Fechin" ended in two-sided disagreement or drift, and no post on the list settled the identity of the old person.

For

  • The old person in Casdoe's house is an old woman whom Severian misgenders, supported by the self-portrait and name details in b sharp's first post [b sharp, 2006].
  • Roy C. Lackey cites passages in which the old man is addressed as Becan's father-in-law and speaks of Fechin's looks and charm as a lover would [Roy C. Lackey, 2006].
  • Lee Berman argues that the old storyteller is a woman, reading the description of Fechin's face and the line about women giving him what he wants as evidence [Lee Berman, 2011].
  • Andrew Mason proposes that Casdoe may be Dorcas's niece, named after an aunt called Cas, so the name similarity points to family [Andrew Mason, 2010].
  • Gerry Quinn supports the symbolic-family reading and adds that Severian imagines an alternative self with a family [Gerry Quinn, 2011].

Against

  • Robert Borski argues that Casdoe's father and Rudesind give conflicting ages and birthplaces for Fechin, so the grandfather link does not hold [Robert Borski, 1998].
  • Roy C. Lackey argues that the textual evidence contradicts an Inire-in-disguise identification, since Fechin is handsome and tall while Inire is described as small [Roy C. Lackey, 2010].
  • Roy C. Lackey disputes Lee Berman's claim that the old man at Casdoe's cabin is female, arguing that Berman's speculation has no textual support [Roy C. Lackey, 2010].
  • Son of Witz argues that the symbolism of Casdoe's house is evident on a plain reading and that the identity of a throwaway character is less useful to explore [Son of Witz, 2011].
  • Jeff Wilson questions why the elder in Casdoe's cabin would be Agia after forty years [Jeff Wilson, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the list never reached agreement on who the people in Casdoe's house are, and most posters did not accept the gender, family or Inire readings.
