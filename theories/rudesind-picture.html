The astronaut in Rudesind's picture

The painting Rudesind cleans (an armoured figure in a desolate landscape, read as the Apollo Moon landing) has a further meaning in the story, or Rudesind is the painter Fechin.

26 threads · 64 posts · for 40 / against 25 · first raised 1998-11-01 by Jack Lyons
Main voices: Jeff Wilson (15), David Stockhoff (11), Lee Berman (10), António Pedro Marques (5), b sharp (4), Roy C. Lackey (4)

The debate

The theory first surfaced in 1998 when Jack Lyons, in a chat-derived post, jokingly read the Armstrong-like painting as a Trinity allusion [Jack Lyons, 1998], and became a live question in 2002 when Michael Andre-Driussi compared Budrys' wounded astronaut with Wolfe's Jonas [Michael Andre-Driussi, 2002]. The main body of discussion came from 2006 onward, when b sharp proposed that Rudesind is really the painter Fechin and that Fechin, Rudesind, Dorcas' husband and Father Inire are shape-shifting versions of one figure [b sharp, 2006][b sharp, 2008]. Jeff Wilson and Dave Tallman disputed the family and identity links on grounds of resemblance, mortality and age arithmetic [Jeff Wilson, 2008][Dave Tallman, 2008].

The 2010 threads were the most intense period. David Stockhoff, Jeff Wilson, James Wynn and Ryan Dunn traced the source of the astronaut image to pulp covers, Apollo photographs and composite conventions, with Wilson arguing for a composite of Apollo photos [David Stockhoff, 2010][Jeff Wilson, 2010][Jeff Wilson, 2010]. Stockhoff disputed Lee Berman's Fechin-as-boy claim while Berman and Roy C. Lackey kept pressing Rudesind as Inire or Fechin [David Stockhoff, 2010][Lee Berman, 2010][Roy C. Lackey, 2010]. Stockhoff and John Watkins argued that the identifications were circumstantial and that Inire's lifespan and stature conflicted with them [David Stockhoff, 2010][John Watkins, 2010][Roy C. Lackey, 2010].

In 2011 Berman and Wilson argued over Fechin as the artist and self-portrait behind the boy portrait, and Wilson floated Fechin as Rudesind's father, which he undercut on age grounds [Lee Berman, 2011][Jeff Wilson, 2011][Jeff Wilson, 2011]. Later posts (2013-2014) returned to Fechin as an Inire incarnation and to Rudesind as a self-portrait, with Wilson disputing the fit [Lee Berman, 2013][Jeff Wilson, 2013][Lee Berman, 2014]. Across the list the thread ended in impasse, with the identity claims never settled by the text.

For

  • Rudesind's painting by Fechin is a self-portrait, supported by the brushes in the boy's portrait [Lee Berman, 2011][Lee Berman, 2013]
  • Fechin's red hair and self-portraits tie him to Inire, a shared red-hair trait listed by Berman and Roy Lackey [Lee Berman, 2013][Roy C. Lackey, 2008]
  • Inire's first description as old, bent and drawn to young girls matches Rudesind and the Boatman [Lee Berman, 2010][Lee Berman, 2014]
  • Rudesind's remark about cleaning the image 'again' points to an earlier cleaning, supporting a hidden history of the picture [Jeff Wilson, 2010]
  • Rudesind's possessive remark about the image suggests he is pointing it out for Severian's benefit [António Pedro Marques, 2010]

Against

  • Dead husbands cannot be immortal, so Dorcas' husband cannot be Inire [Jeff Wilson, 2008]
  • Dave Tallman argues lack of family resemblance undercuts the Fechin-as-Dorcas's-father claim [Dave Tallman, 2008]
  • Fechin and Inire's ages and lifespans cannot be reconciled with the Fechin identifications [David Stockhoff, 2010][Jeff Wilson, 2011]
  • Inire is described as small while Fechin is tall and handsome, which contradicts the identification [Roy C. Lackey, 2010]
  • Wolfe's astronaut image likely drew on memory of Apollo photos rather than a specific source, so the picture need not match [Dave Lebling, 2010]

Where it stands: Still disputed: the identification of Rudesind with Fechin and Inire was argued repeatedly across 2006-2014 without consensus, and most posters who engaged with the text came to reject or leave it open.
