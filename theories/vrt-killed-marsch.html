When and why V.R.T. killed Marsch

V.R.T. killed Marsch (in self-defence, at a datable point in the notebooks) and took his place.

14 threads · 14 posts · for 7 / against 7 · first raised 1998-06-01 by adam louis stephanides
Main voices: adam louis stephanides (3), Michael Andre-Driussi (2), Peter Cash (2), Robert Borski (2), mary whalen (1), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (1)

The debate

The earliest post on the list is Adam Stephanides's 1998-06-01 message ([adam louis stephanides, 1998]), which disputes a self-defence reading and argues against the cat following Marsch. The same day Michael Andre-Driussi proposed that V.R.T. killed Marsch on April 12, using the ballistics table and the rifles ([Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998]). On 1998-06-02 Stephanides argued instead for a killing sometime after the April 21 entries, rejecting the April 12 rifle idea ([adam louis stephanides, 1998]), and Andre-Driussi floated a rope-based method ([Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998]).

In mid-1998 the discussion branched into rival readings. Stephanides read the cat in the Puss in Boots passage as a sign of Marsch's death and V.R.T.'s planned theft of his identity ([adam louis stephanides, 1998]). Peter Cash argued that Marsch killed Maitre and forged V.R.T.'s entries ([Peter Cash, 1998]), and later defended a reading in which Marsch killed V.R.T., on the grounds that the text is deliberately ambiguous ([Peter Cash, 1998]). Mary Whalen proposed that a tiger killed Marsch so that V.R.T. could take his place ([mary whalen, 1998]).

In November 2000 Robert Borski argued that the real Marsch died before April 23 and that V.R.T. assumed his identity, citing the handwriting change, the cat-bite excuse and a Wolfe interview he read as saying V.R.T. killed Marsch ([Robert Borski, 2000]). Dan'l Danehy-Oakes proposed a name-based version in which the anthropologist was killed and V.R.T. took the name ([Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2000]), and Borski restated a false-identity version the next day, citing the missing arrival record ([Robert Borski, 2000]).

In 2007, within the thread on whether Marsch, Sandwalker and Eastwind are one person, James Wynn argued that V.R.T. killed Marsch and wrote the story as aboriginal lore, citing the cat and shared dreams with Number 5 ([James Wynn, 2007]). Tony Ellis disputed the theory by arguing that V.R.T. wrote the April 23 entry and rejecting the cat-bite explanation ([Tony Ellis, 2007]). In 2013 Gerry Quinn raised the possibility that V.R.T. ate or killed Marsch before imitating him, while attacking symbolic readings of the novel ([Gerry Quinn, 2013]).

For

  • Andre-Driussi argued that V.R.T. killed Marsch on April 12, using the ballistics table and the rifles the two carried, and explained the later bad handwriting as copying ([Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998]).
  • Borski argued from the handwriting change and the cat-bite excuse that V.R.T. had taken Marsch's identity, and cited a Wolfe interview he read as confirming it ([Robert Borski, 2000]).
  • Borski argued that the John V. Marsch of the Roncevaux story is a false identity, pointing to the lack of any official arrival record ([Robert Borski, 2000]).
  • Whalen proposed that a tiger killed Marsch and V.R.T. impersonated him using a flint-cut diary ([mary whalen, 1998]).
  • Wynn argued that the cat is an aborigine and that V.R.T.'s shared dreams with Number 5 support the killing and identity theft ([James Wynn, 2007]).

Against

  • Stephanides argued that the text lacks Cassilla evidence, that a conservation-of-matter problem arises, and that V.R.T. has an unlikely motive ([adam louis stephanides, 1998]).
  • Stephanides argued that a distant rifle killing is unlikely when V.R.T. could strike at close range, so the killing came after April 21 ([adam louis stephanides, 1998]).
  • Ellis argued that the April 23 journal entry was written by V.R.T. and rejected the cat-bite explanation for the handwriting change ([Tony Ellis, 2007]).
  • Cash argued that Marsch himself killed Maitre and forged V.R.T.'s entries, so the killing is not V.R.T.'s ([Peter Cash, 1998]).
  • Cash defended a reading in which Marsch killed V.R.T., arguing that the text leaves who killed whom deliberately ambiguous ([Peter Cash, 1998]).

Where it stands: Still disputed: the discussion never reached consensus, the competing readings (April 12, after April 21, Marsch killing V.R.T., or a cat or tiger killing) all remained standing, and the threads ended without agreement.
