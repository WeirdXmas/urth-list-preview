Isangoma is Father Inire

Isangoma, the shaman in the hut in the jungle, is Father Inire.

17 threads · 24 posts · for 19 / against 5 · first raised 2006-06-10 by b sharp
Main voices: Lee Berman (9), Ryan Dunn (4), b sharp (3), Andrew Mason (3), Jeff Wilson (2), John Watkins (2)

The debate

The theory that the jungle shaman Isangoma is Father Inire was first raised by b sharp on 2006-06-10, in a post proposing that the jungle guide, the twin assistants and Isangoma are Inire in his true, dark-skinned form [b sharp, 2006]. Over the next years b sharp repeated and elaborated it, arguing in 2009 that the Jungle Hut is a time-travel structure and that Isangoma is an incarnation of Inire [b sharp, 2009]. Agreement came in 2008 from Borsky, whose view b sharp and others relayed, that the Jungle Hut scene is set in Earth's past [b sharp, 2008].

In 2010 the theory grew more specific. Lee Berman proposed that the Jungle Hut lies on Earth and that Inire is the figure found there [Lee Berman, 2010], and later argued Inire is a fallen non-human form of Tzadkiel [Lee Berman, 2010]. John Watkins argued from a passage in The Citadel of the Autarch that Inire may be the uturuncu shaman [John Watkins, 2010], and Ryan Dunn argued the same, that Inire was disguised as the old guide [Ryan Dunn, 2010]. Jeff Wilson conceded the old shaman could be Inire in disguise, but disputed that the rescue clue proved Inire was merely watching [Jeff Wilson, 2010]. Watkins later disputed Dunn's claim that Inire is never seen, saying the tiger-shaman identification was already fairly clear [John Watkins, 2010].

The debate peaked in October 2011, when Andrew Mason proposed that Isangoma is Inire in disguise rather than Dorcas [Andrew Mason, 2011][Andrew Mason, 2011]. David Stockhoff supported this, saying Isangoma looks like an older, clothed Inire [David Stockhoff, 2011]. Lee Berman backed it with a jigsaw of clues and a James Jordan interview cited for the Earth/Urth iteration reading [Lee Berman, 2011][Lee Berman, 2011]. Jerry Friedman argued instead that Dorcas, Isangoma and the Inire guide form one shapeshifting figure [Jerry Friedman, 2011]. Berman's later posts widened the claim to a web of links with the Cumaean, Hethor and the ship [Lee Berman, 2011][Lee Berman, 2014][Lee Berman, 2014].

For

  • b sharp first proposed that Isangoma, the jungle guide and the twins are Inire in his true form, citing dark metallic skin [b sharp, 2006].
  • b sharp linked the Jungle Hut's labyrinthine vertical structure to other towers and to Ultan's Library, and cited interview clues that Urth is Earth's earlier iteration [b sharp, 2009].
  • Lee Berman argued Inire is the figure in the Jungle Hut, reached through a portal, and cited Borski's missionary identifications and the naked Isangoma glimpsed there [Lee Berman, 2010].
  • John Watkins cited a passage in The Citadel of the Autarch saying the master was in the northern jungles trying to rescue his predecessor, to argue Inire is the uturuncu shaman [John Watkins, 2010].
  • Andrew Mason argued Isangoma is a mystery with no other story roots, has a South American look, and that Inire's known disguises include one resembling Isangoma [Andrew Mason, 2011].

Against

  • Jeff Wilson argued that the text's need for an active rescuer does not show Inire was merely watching, and that aquastors and servants already cover surveillance [Jeff Wilson, 2010].
  • John Watkins's 2010 reply to Ryan Dunn stated the tiger-shaman identity was clear, questioning why Inire would need to be all the candidates [John Watkins, 2010].
  • Jerry Friedman argued the evidence linking Dorcas, Isangoma and the Inire guide points to Dorcas, turning Lee Berman's obscured-face argument against the servitor's identification [Jerry Friedman, 2011].
  • Jeff Wilson questioned the jungle guide's features and disputed Mason's reading that Isangoma can see the tokoloshe, as reported in the thread on Father Inire - Hethor.
  • Gerry Quinn, Sergei Soloviev and Andrew Mason pushed back in the Father Inire as Dionysus thread that the evidence was thin and that Hethor and Inire are distinct figures [Lee Berman, 2014].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the theory was raised repeatedly from 2006 to 2014 and gained some supporters, notably Mason and Stockhoff, but the threads ended without agreement and Berman's wider claims drew sustained skepticism.
