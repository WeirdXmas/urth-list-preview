Who grows up to be Ymar

A boy Severian meets in the past (Reechy, or Frog) grows up to be the Autarch Ymar.

7 threads · 14 posts · for 7 / against 6 · first raised 1998-01-28 by Ranjit Bhatnagar
Main voices: James Wynn (5), Lee Berman (3), Gerry Quinn (2), Ranjit Bhatnagar (1), Alice Turner (1), Michael Andre-Driussi (1)

The debate

The theory that Reechy, the boy Severian meets in the past, grows up to be Ymar was first raised by Ranjit Bhatnagar in January 1998, who offered speculation without textual evidence [Ranjit Bhatnagar, 1998]. Alice Turner then proposed a version built on the serpent and knife letters, and Michael Andre-Driussi extended it to Ymar failing the Test in Yesod, making the Old Autarch's rule a rigged game [Alice Turner, 1998][Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998]. Alice Turner's post is the one the record ties to the serpent-and-knife argument, and Michael Andre-Driussi's post is the one that tied the identity to Yesod.

The theory was largely dormant until 2011, when James Wynn revived it under a different form, arguing that Frog, not Reechy, is Ymar, the first Autarch, through name etymology, star constellations and a Noahic parallel [James Wynn, 2011][James Wynn, 2011]. Wynn pressed the case across several posts, citing Ymar's Torturer ties and a king-frog etymology, and Lee Berman asked him to explain the links to Tzadkiel and Erebus [James Wynn, 2011][James Wynn, 2011][Lee Berman, 2011]. Gerry Quinn disputed the identification, arguing that Frog is clearly the Romulus figure, and António Pedro Marques asked what the identification adds to the book [Gerry Quinn, 2011][António Pedro Marques, 2011].

The 2011 debate was extended by Lee Berman, who proposed Frog as a little Severian figure and Ymar as a parallel to him, and later argued that the identification is a contrivance [Lee Berman, 2011][Lee Berman, 2011]. Gerry Quinn replied that women were barred from the Torturers' Guild until a later edict, undermining the twin-sister idea [Gerry Quinn, 2011]. the list's threads on Frog and Fish and on the Hierogrammates show the Ymar identification being argued alongside broader Romulus and Typhon readings, without resolution.

For

  • Ranjit Bhatnagar first proposed that Reechy grew up to become Ymar, reasoning that Ymar as Conciliator would have shortened a long span of time [Ranjit Bhatnagar, 1998].
  • Alice Turner proposed the Reechy-to-Ymar reading via the serpent and knife letters [Alice Turner, 1998].
  • Michael Andre-Driussi argued that Reechy becomes Ymar who fails the Test in Yesod [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998].
  • James Wynn argued that Frog is Ymar through star-name, etymology and Noahic parallels, and that Ymar the Just is linked to 'frog' [James Wynn, 2011][James Wynn, 2011][James Wynn, 2011].
  • Lee Berman argued that Ymar parallels Frog in the Tale of the Boy Called Frog, making Frog a little Severian figure [Lee Berman, 2011].

Against

  • Gerry Quinn argued that Frog is clearly Romulus, given the mother, abandonment, wolf-rearing and twin-killing parallels [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • António Pedro Marques argued that the Frog-Ymar identification is not evident and asked what it adds to the book [António Pedro Marques, 2011].
  • Gerry Quinn noted that women were barred from the Torturers' Guild until Ymar's later edict, undermining the twin-sister argument [Gerry Quinn, 2011].
  • Lee Berman later called the Ymar identification a contrivance [Lee Berman, 2011].
  • Wynn's claim that Erebus is long dead was questioned by Lee Berman as resting on little evidence [Lee Berman, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the Reechy version was raised once in 1998 and dropped, and the Frog version drew sustained argument in 2011 that most posters, led by Quinn and Marques, did not accept.
