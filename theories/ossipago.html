Ossipago is Father Inire (or a robot)

Ossipago, one of the three hierodules at the Grange, is Father Inire, or a robot rather than a living hierodule.

21 threads · 35 posts · for 28 / against 11 · first raised 1997-07-09 by Alice Turner
Main voices: Ryan Dunn (9), b sharp (7), Roy C. Lackey (4), Lee Berman (4), Marc Aramini (3), Michael Andre-Driussi (2)

The debate

The idea that Ossipago might be a robot was first raised on the list by Daniel Fusch in 1999, who asked whether readers could deduce it from The Book of the New Sun alone and pointed to Ossipago's non-human joints [Daniel Fusch, 1999]. The identification with Father Inire came later: b sharp proposed in May 2006 that Ossipago is Inire in disguise, citing orange light blobs above Baldanders' castle and Inire's letter to the Autarch [b sharp, 2006]. Roy C. Lackey disputed that reading the same week, arguing the sparks were crewmen on the ship's hull [Roy C. Lackey, 2006], and b sharp's follow-up treated Ossipago more as a deus ex machina than as Inire [b sharp, 2006].

The identity question revived in 2010 and 2011, when Ryan Dunn, Lee Berman and Larry Miller proposed that Ossipago is one of Inire's many guises. Dunn argued Inire plays many parts, including Ossipago, the Shaman and the Boatman [Ryan Dunn, 2011]. Berman listed Ossipago among Inire's possible disguises, grounding this in Inire's monkey-like description and shared traits such as folksy speech, boats, art and red hair [Lee Berman, 2010], and later linked Inire to Pan and Dionysus while suggesting Ossipago might be Inire [Lee Berman, 2011]. Jeff Wilson disputed the single-identity view, saying Ossipago and Inire are distinct aquastors of one race and contrasting Ossipago's gruffness with Inire's kindliness [Jeff Wilson, 2010]. Marc Aramini mocked the reasoning as resting on spelling coincidences [Marc Aramini, 2011].

The robot reading gained support from James Wynn, who argued that the Hierogrammates' created beings, including Ossipago, were probably robots or androids because hierodule means slave [James Wynn, 2010]. Larry Miller later backed it, grounding it in the name's meaning of bone or calcium grower and in the fact that Severian never sees Ossipago's eyes [Larry Miller, 2011]. Dunn also said Ossipago and Palaemon seem robotic [Ryan Dunn, 2010]. Opponents of the Inire link mostly did not address the robot idea directly, so the two readings ran side by side in threads such as 'Father Inire/Paleamon' and 'Who's Right?' without resolution.

For

  • Daniel Fusch asked whether Ossipago's joints differing from a human's hint at a mechanical nature [Daniel Fusch, 1999].
  • James Wynn argued the Hierogrammates' created beings were originally robots or androids, since hierodule means slave and robot carries the same sense [James Wynn, 2010].
  • Larry Miller grounded the robot reading in the name's meaning and in Severian never seeing Ossipago's eyes [Larry Miller, 2011].
  • Ryan Dunn said Ossipago and Palaemon are of the same ilk and seem robotic [Ryan Dunn, 2010].
  • b sharp proposed Ossipago is Inire in disguise, citing orange light blobs above Baldanders' castle and Inire's letter to the Autarch [b sharp, 2006].

Against

  • Roy C. Lackey argued the orange sparks above the castle are crewmen moving on the ship's hull, not teleporting figures [Roy C. Lackey, 2006].
  • Jeff Wilson argued Ossipago and Inire are distinct aquastors of the same race, contrasting Ossipago's gruffness with Inire's kindliness [Jeff Wilson, 2010].
  • Marc Aramini mocked the Inire identification as resting on spelling coincidences [Marc Aramini, 2011].
  • David Stockhoff questioned collapsing everyone into Ossipago and Famulimus as excessive [David Stockhoff, 2018].
  • Lee Berman argued Ossipago is too mechanically unlike a chem's parts and proposed it as one of the humanity-built machines that created the hierogrammates [Lee Berman, 2010].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the robot reading and the Inire-in-disguise reading were each proposed repeatedly, with no consensus and the posters remaining divided across the threads.
