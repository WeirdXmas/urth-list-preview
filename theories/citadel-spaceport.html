The Citadel is an old spaceport

The Citadel's towers are grounded spaceships and the Citadel was once a spaceport.

11 threads · 18 posts · for 9 / against 8 · first raised 1998-06-15 by Joshua A. Solomon
Main voices: Roy C. Lackey (2), Jeff Wilson (2), Joshua A. Solomon (1), Dan Parmenter (1), Peter Stephenson (1), Michael Andre-Driussi (1)

The debate

The theory was first raised on the list by Joshua A. Solomon in 1998, who proposed that the Citadel in Nessus is a collection of rocketships, responding to Peter Westlake's quotation about the Saltus Mine machine [Joshua A. Solomon, 1998]. Within months Dan Parmenter and Peter Stephenson debated whether the missing map and the towers' flight explained the Citadel's layout, with Parmenter arguing the map predates the spaceport conversion [Dan Parmenter, 1998] and Stephenson proposing the towers flew in and out since spaceport days [Peter Stephenson, 1998]. Michael Andre-Driussi added the claim that only the towers are former zoetic ships [Michael Andre-Driussi, 1998]. Matthew Malthouse proposed in 2003 that the Citadel was once a spaceport north of Nessus, admitting he had no direct textual support [Matthew Malthouse, 2003].

The 2006 wave of posts was the main flurry. Macronaut asked whether the whole citadel could be one ship [Macronaut, 2006], and b sharp's reading of the Citadel as a Quasar-like ship drew corrections from Roy C. Lackey, who cited Urth of the New Sun page 251 for a spaceport [Roy C. Lackey, 2006] and argued most towers are individual spaceships using the map and the towers' readiness to leap [Roy C. Lackey, 2006]. Andy Robertson supported the spaceport reading from the Long Sun visionary passage [Andy Robertson, 2006], while Stanisław Bocian disputed the Citadel as a ship but offered no citation [Stanisław Bocian, 2006]. Maru Dubshinki called the textual support for a whole-citadel ship thin [Maru Dubshinki, 2006].

In 2010 to 2011 Jeff Wilson argued that the Wall's artificial construction shows Nessus was a spaceport perimeter with grounded towers [Jeff Wilson, 2010], and David Stockhoff argued the rocket-ship towers would not sit on stones [David Stockhoff, 2010]. Gerry Quinn disputed the idea by arguing the ships' gun emplacements and missing sails mark them as non-Cold-War craft [Gerry Quinn, 2010]. Lee Berman argued the towers are the ships that spread humanity, while Sergei Soloviev argued they are landers that cannot reach the stars [Lee Berman, 2011][Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011]. Dan Harris revived the ship question in 2014 but did not settle it [Dan Harris, 2014]. Across the threads, the discussion mostly drifted into related questions and never produced agreement.

For

  • Joshua A. Solomon proposed the Citadel is a collection of rocketships, grounded on the Saltus Mine machine passage [Joshua A. Solomon, 1998].
  • Roy C. Lackey cited Urth of the New Sun, chapter XXXV, page 251, as evidence the Citadel was Nessus's old space port [Roy C. Lackey, 2006].
  • Roy C. Lackey argued most towers are individual spaceships, citing towers ready to leap into the air and an old map with towers not in the Citadel [Roy C. Lackey, 2006].
  • Jeff Wilson argued the Wall cannot be as large as the Whorl because it is overtly artificial, and proposed it was a spaceport perimeter with grounded towers [Jeff Wilson, 2010].
  • David Stockhoff argued the towers, being rocket ships, would not sit on stones beneath them [David Stockhoff, 2010].

Against

  • Dan Parmenter argued the missing map may predate the citadel's conversion from spaceport, so places like the Atrium of Time are absent from it [Dan Parmenter, 1998].
  • Gerry Quinn argued the ships' gun emplacements and missing sails mark them as non-Cold-War craft [Gerry Quinn, 2010].
  • Sergei Soloviev argued the Citadel's ships are landers that cannot reach the stars, and that the Whorl is the only described starcrosser [Sergei SOLOVIEV, 2011].
  • Stanisław Bocian argued the Citadel is a starport and not a ship, with only some towers being rocket-like vessels [Stanisław Bocian, 2006].
  • Lee Berman argued the towers are the ships that spread humanity, while Tzadkiel's vessel is a different, non-Urthly ship [Lee Berman, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the spaceport and grounded-tower reading was argued repeatedly from 1998 to 2014 but never reached consensus, with several posters admitting thin or uncited support.
