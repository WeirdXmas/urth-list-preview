Green is Urth

The planet Green is Urth (or Ushas).

51 threads · 101 posts · for 45 / against 52 · first raised 1999-10-11 by Nicholas Gevers
Main voices: Marc Aramini (29), Lee Berman (17), James Wynn (13), Gerry Quinn (8), António Pedro Marques (5), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (5)

The debate

The Green-is-Urth idea first surfaced on the list when Nicholas Gevers, in October 1999, offered Green as one of three explanations for Nessus's reappearance in the Short Sun books [Nicholas Gevers, 1999]. Early posts in 2002 treated Wolfe's remark that Green, not Blue, is Urth as a central datum: Jeff Veyera argued that the remark shifted the burden of proof onto skeptics [Jeff Veyera, 2002], while Adam Stephanides called the remark probably a joke and listed statements that cannot all be true [Adam Stephanides, 2002] [Adam Stephanides, 2002]. Alice Turner separately argued that Ushas must be a blue planet [Alice Turner, 2002].

The debate grew heavily in 2010 and 2011, especially in threads such as Losing Imitation and Green is Urth Redux. Marc Aramini became the theory's main advocate, arguing that Green is literally Urth's future and that its astral or time-travel links explain the travellers' arrivals [Marc Aramini, 2011] [Marc Aramini, 2011] [Marc Aramini, 2011] [Marc Aramini, 2011]. Opponents included James Wynn, who raised astronomical and ecological obstacles [James Wynn, 2010] [James Wynn, 2011], Gerry Quinn, who called the identification a metaphor or contradicted by the text [Gerry Quinn, 2011] [Gerry Quinn, 2011], and James Jordan, who cited Wolfe's denial [James Jordan, 2011]. Several posters offered intermediate readings, such as Green as a metaphor, a symbolic future Urth, or a past Urth [Jordon Flato, 2011] [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2011] [James Wynn, 2011].

The discussion continued into 2013 and 2014 with Lee Berman's spiritual-progression reading [Lee Berman, 2013], and Dan Harris and Marc Aramini's proposal that Blue is an alternate Urth and Green the true Urth [Dan Harris, 2014] [Marc Aramini, 2014]. Some threads, such as Like a Good Neighbor and the vanished-people threads, drifted into side quarrels without resolving the question.

For

  • Nicholas Gevers first offered Green as one explanation for Nessus's reappearance, grounded in a Nova Express interview [Nicholas Gevers, 1999].
  • Jeff Veyera argued that Wolfe's answer that Green is Urth created problems for the Blue-based time-travel reading and cited a Matachin tower figure as evidence [Jeff Veyera, 2002].
  • Marc Aramini argued that Wolfe's private words and the rocket lander's mechanism make Green concretely Urth [Marc Aramini, 2011] [Marc Aramini, 2011].
  • Marc Aramini argued that the Long Sun astral-travel rules let characters reach Urth by thinking of Green [Marc Aramini, 2011].
  • Lee Berman argued that Wolfe's terse interview answer should not be treated as decisive, given the unreliability of narrators and the Planet of the Apes parallel [Lee Berman, 2010].

Against

  • Adam Stephanides argued that Wolfe's interview remark was probably a joke and listed three Wolfe statements that cannot all be true [Adam Stephanides, 2002] [Adam Stephanides, 2002].
  • Alice Turner argued that Ushas must be a blue planet because the seas rose after the ice melted [Alice Turner, 2002].
  • James Wynn argued that the planetary conditions, Lune's loss and the orbital changes clash with Green being Urth [James Wynn, 2010] [James Wynn, 2011].
  • James Jordan argued that Wolfe explicitly denied that Green is Urth, so the author's statement should settle the matter [James Jordan, 2011].
  • Gerry Quinn argued that the book's text contradicts the identification and that Green is at most a metaphor for Urth's future [Gerry Quinn, 2010] [Gerry Quinn, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the theory was never accepted by the list, and the posters remained split over Wolfe's remark and the textual and astronomical evidence, with several threads ending in drift or personal dispute.
