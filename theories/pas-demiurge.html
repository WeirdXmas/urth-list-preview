Pas is a Gnostic demiurge

Pas plays the Gnostic demiurge, a false creator-god of the Whorl, against the true God outside it.

47 threads · 79 posts · for 44 / against 33 · first raised 1997-01-25 by Derek Bell
Main voices: Lee Berman (30), James Wynn (6), David Stockhoff (5), Son of Witz (5), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (4), Alice Turner (3)

The debate

The theory that Pas is a Gnostic demiurge was first raised on the list by Derek Bell in January 1997, who proposed Pas as a demiurge in a post about the Whorl and the afterlife [Derek Bell, 1997]. The idea resurfaced in 2002, when Alice Turner, Kevin Maroney and Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued Typhon or Pas is a Demiurge against the true Outsider, while Josh Geller, Adam Stephanides and James Jordan rejected any Gnostic reading [Alice Turner, 2002] [Josh Geller, 2002] [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002] [James Jordan, 2002] [Adam Stephanides, 2002]. Jerry Friedman and Alice Turner argued the cosmology is polytheist or Hellenistic, borrowing only the Demiurge [Jerry Friedman, 2002] [Alice Turner, 2002].

From 2004 to 2008 Dan'l Danehy-Oakes was the main advocate, arguing that the Long Sun books embed a Valentinian-style cosmos with Pas as Demiurge and the Outsider as true God [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2004] [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2005] [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2007]. Chris P disputed this, warning that a Demiurge reading would turn the series into a Gnostic pamphlet and conflict with Catholic orthodoxy [Chris P, 2005] [Chris P, 2005]. In the Tzadkiel debates of December 2008, David Stockhoff argued Tzadkiel is the Demiurge parodying God, while Son of Witz held that a Demiurge only thinks it is God [David Stockhoff, 2008] [Son of Witz, 2008].

From 2010 the theory was pursued mainly by Lee Berman, who moved the demiurge role onto Father Inire, Dionysus, the Outsider and even Wolfe as author, and posted repeatedly in 2010 to 2013 [Lee Berman, 2010] [Lee Berman, 2011] [Lee Berman, 2012] [Lee Berman, 2013]. Gerry Quinn, Andrew Mason and John Watkins disputed these readings, and Watkins and James Wynn debated whether Pas is a demiurge or an antichrist [John Watkins, 2010] [James Wynn, 2010] [Gerry Quinn, 2011]. Several threads, including Father Inire Theory and Agia's Weapons, ended in impasse [Lee Berman, 2011] [Lee Berman, 2011].

For

  • Pas is credited with making the Whorl and was never overthrown, so the demiurge role fits him better than the Antichrist, according to Wynn and Maroney [James Wynn, 2010] [Kevin J. Maroney, 2002].
  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued that Long Sun literalizes Valentinian Gnosticism, with the Outsider as true God, Pas as Demiurge and Silk's enlightenment as gnosis [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2005] [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2004].
  • Son of Witz read the ending of Lake of the Long Sun as a gnostic contrast between a false creator and a real sun outside creation [Son of Witz, 2009].
  • Lee Berman argued that Father Inire and Urth are a gnostic universe ruled by an evil demiurge, citing the mirrors and Inire's disappearance replaced by Tzadkiel [Lee Berman, 2010] [Lee Berman, 2010].
  • Wolfe's own remark that Typhon and Pas are equivalent was cited by Wynn to support the demiurge reading [James Wynn, 2010].

Against

  • Josh Geller and Adam Stephanides argued Pas does not match the Demiurge and that the books have no meaningful connection to Gnosticism [Josh Geller, 2002] [Adam Stephanides, 2002].
  • Alice Turner and Jerry Friedman held the cosmology is Hellenistic or polytheist, with only the Demiurge borrowed and no textual support for Typhon making the Whorl [Jerry Friedman, 2002] [Alice Turner, 2002].
  • Chris P warned that a Gnostic Demiurge reading conflicts with Catholic orthodoxy and would reshape the series [Chris P, 2005].
  • Son of Witz argued the Demiurge only thinks it is God and that Christ stands above it, so Tzadkiel cannot simply be the Demiurge [Son of Witz, 2008].
  • Andrew Mason read the Sun cycle through Christian imagery and argued the Outsider redeems pagan gods rather than endorsing gnosticism [Andrew Mason, 2011] [Andrew Mason, 2011].

Where it stands: Still disputed: the theory was raised in 1997, argued in several threads, and has never won agreement, with most posters rejecting a straightforward Pas-as-demiurge reading while a few, notably Dan'l Danehy-Oakes and Lee Berman, kept advancing variants.
