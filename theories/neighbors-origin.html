Where the Neighbors came from

The Neighbors arose on Blue and colonized Green, and reached the Whorl by spacecraft or soul-travel.

12 threads · 14 posts · for 8 / against 4 · first raised 2000-09-06 by Kevin J. Maroney
Main voices: Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (2), Roy C. Lackey (2), James Wynn (2), Kevin J. Maroney (1), Alex David Groce (1), Jeff Veyera (1)

The debate

The idea that the Neighbors arose on Blue and colonized Green surfaces on the list first in 2000, when Kevin J. Maroney proposed that inhumi cross between Green and Blue by teleporting with Neighbor help [Kevin J. Maroney, 2000], and then in 2002 when Jeff Veyera responded to Marc Aramini's version of the Blue-origin argument by proposing speculative solutions to the Blue/Green double-planet puzzle [Jeff Veyera, 2002]. Discussion of the Blue-versus-Green origin intensified around 2010, when Roy C. Lackey argued the Neighbors were native to Blue and migrated to Green, citing Silkhorn's spaceship remarks and Quetzal's fangs [Roy C. Lackey, 2010], and James Wynn weighed the evidence and concluded the balance leaned toward Blue while calling the tree-origin construct cumbersome [James Wynn, 2010]. Lackey then moved to a roughly even balance, stressing that the inhumi's transport to the Whorl stayed open [Roy C. Lackey, 2010].

Later posts shifted toward the Long Sun and Short Sun books. Jordon Flato proposed that the Long Sun Neighbors are dream-traveling inhumi [Jordon Flato, 2011], and Dan'l Danehy-Oakes asked Wynn to confirm a three-part summary of his tree-based theory [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2011]. Wynn argued the Neighbors started on Blue and that Marc Aramini's inhumi-from-vines account was problematic [James Wynn, 2011]. In 2012 nate jarvis recalled a Green-to-Blue inhumi-sending theory and asked whether it had been proposed before [nate jarvis, 2012]. In 2013 Andrew Mason argued the Short Sun Neighbours originated on Blue, citing Windcloud's account of living in Inclito's house on Blue [Andrew Mason, 2013].

Across the threads, participants such as Lackey, Wynn, Thalassocrat, Maroney and Robert Borski argued for variants of the theory, while Danehy-Oakes, Alex David Groce, James Jordan and Kevin J. Maroney pressed objections in the Pas and Typhon threads [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2001] [Alex David Groce, 2001]. Several threads ended without consensus, and some turned personal or drifted into mythic parallels.

For

  • Kevin J. Maroney argued the inhumi cross mostly by teleporting with Neighbor help, citing the Neighbors' sense of distance and Quetzal's trip to the Whorl [Kevin J. Maroney, 2000].
  • Jeff Veyera cited Rudesind's remark that Lune is larger than the moon as a reason to pursue alternatives to doubling [Jeff Veyera, 2002].
  • Roy C. Lackey relied on Silkhorn's spaceship remarks in In Green's Jungles and argued Quetzal's fangs showed he could not have arrived by spirit travel [Roy C. Lackey, 2010].
  • James Wynn concluded the balance of evidence in the Lists of Blue and Green leaned toward a Blue origin [James Wynn, 2010].
  • Andrew Mason grounded a Short Sun version in Windcloud's account of living in Inclito's house on Blue, whose core was an old Neighbour structure [Andrew Mason, 2013].

Against

  • Dan'l Danehy-Oakes argued Typhon's choice of the Blue-Green system need not reflect the Neighbors' two heads, since the target could be arbitrary [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2001].
  • Roy C. Lackey later argued the Neighbors may not have originated on Green, weighing the evidence as roughly even [Roy C. Lackey, 2010].
  • James Wynn argued that Marc Aramini's account of inhumi arising from vines eating animal life had problems, including why only vines make inhumi [James Wynn, 2011].
  • Alex David Groce argued Pas is human and considered Scylla's human ally a point against Typhon being a Neighbor [Alex David Groce, 2001].
  • Kevin J. Maroney pressed that the second head is explicitly artificial and that the Pas-as-Neighbor theory offers no interesting answers.

Where it stands: Still disputed: the Blue-origin version was raised in 2000 and revisited through 2013, but threads ended without consensus and several posters, including Lackey, moved toward uncertainty.
