The leatherskin has twelve legs

The leatherskin creature has more limbs than an earthly insect, so it is an alien animal.

6 threads · 14 posts · for 10 / against 8 · first raised 1999-10-03 by Alex David Groce
Main voices: James Wynn (7), William H. Ansley (4), Dan'l Danehy-Oakes (3), Alex David Groce (1), David Langford (1), Don Doggett (1)

The debate

The leg-count idea was first raised by William H. Ansley on 1999-10-18, who argued that the leatherskin in On Blue's Waters has twelve legs rather than six or eight, using the passage about six legs on the starboard gunwale to show the balance would fail [William H. Ansley, 1999]. Earlier that month David Langford was responding to a post that had already corrected the creature to six legs, so the six-legs reading predates Ansley's argument in this record [David Langford, 1999]. The card's alien inference (more limbs than an earthly insect) is rarely argued directly; posters mostly debated leg count and anatomy.

The question returned in January 2003. James Wynn argued that the creature has six legs, with its tail dangling in the water to explain why only half its weight rests on the gunwale [James Wynn, 2003]. Ansley answered that Horn might call fins or flippers "legs" and asked for a usage example [William H. Ansley, 2003], and Joe Cilluffo argued for more than six submerged legs based on the narration's wording [Joe Cilluffo, 2003]. Dan'l Danehy-Oakes suggested a giant softshell crab with twice the limbs of his earlier count [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2003]. Wynn then moved to clawed hands drawn from Pausanias [James Wynn, 2003], linked the creature to the manticore [James Wynn, 2003], and finally proposed jointed forward arms with tail propulsion [James Wynn, 2003].

A separate line from 2002 concerned the creature's identity rather than its legs. Don Doggett proposed an evolved shark whose three jaws are rows of teeth that Horn misreads [Don Doggett, 2002], and Danehy-Oakes proposed a lobster-like crustacean [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2002]. Ansley disputed the teeth reading using Horn's double-door description of hinged jaws, and argued the creature is alien anyway, since a shark with at least six limbs would be unearthly too [William H. Ansley, 2002]. In 2008 Wynn tied the triple-jawed leatherskin to Pinocchio's dogfish, but that thread drifted off-topic without returning to the legs question [James Wynn, 2008].

For

  • Ansley argued the leatherskin has twelve legs, using the six-legs-on-the-gunwale passage to show that six legs would leave the balance failing [William H. Ansley, 2003]
  • Ansley proposed that the three jaws are one fixed upper jaw with two hinged lower jaws, which fits a twelve-limbed reading in his view [William H. Ansley, 1999]
  • Cilluffo argued the narration's wording implies more than six legs, since Horn would have said all six legs if there were only six [Joe Cilluffo, 2003]
  • Ansley defended the legs reading by suggesting Horn may call fins or flippers legs, and asked for a former Whorl denizen's usage to show otherwise [William H. Ansley, 2003]

Against

  • Wynn argued the creature has six legs, with the tail dangling in the water explaining the weight distribution on the gunwale [James Wynn, 2003]
  • Wynn argued the limbs are clawed hands drawn from Pausanias' triton rather than legs or flippers [James Wynn, 2003]
  • Wynn later proposed that the foremost claws sit on jointed arms and that the creature is propelled by its tail, not its claws [James Wynn, 2003]
  • Danehy-Oakes proposed a crustacean identity with a different limb count, moving the discussion away from the twelve-legs reading [Dan'l Danehy-Oakes, 2003]

Where it stands: Still disputed and unresolved: the twelve-legs reading was raised by Ansley in 1999 and contested by Wynn in 2003, and the threads ended in disagreement or drifted to other identifications without consensus.
